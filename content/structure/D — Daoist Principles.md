### An Exhaustive Saturation Compilation from the Major Daoist Primary Sources — with Parallels from Psychological Science and Philosophy

**X = 192 principles.** This document compiles every distinct, actionable principle for well-being and flourishing recoverable from the major classical and practice-oriented Daoist sources, deduplicated until saturation: the point at which any further "new" principle collapses into one already listed. It does not claim to exhaust the 1,400-text Daozang canon; it exhausts the sources that define Daoist thought and practice for well-being.

Each principle carries two companion layers: a state-of-the-art parallel from psychological science, and 0 to 3 philosophical concepts formatted as **Concept** (Thinker, Reference), included in proportion to their relevance. Parallels are heuristic bridges, not claims of historical influence or empirical confirmation.

---

### Scope, Method, and Ranking

**Method.** Each primary source was mined for normative content: what the text tells you to do or stop doing. Near-duplicates were merged unless they pull a distinct practice lever. The compilation looped on the question "is there another principle?" until every remaining candidate reduced to an existing entry, verified against the saturation checklist in Appendix A.

**Importance ranking.** Principles are ordered by two criteria: (1) first-layerness, how close the principle sits to the axioms from which the rest of the system derives; and (2) node size, how many downstream principles causally depend on it and how much it enables. The list reads as dependency layers: Layer I is the load-bearing wall; each later layer presupposes the earlier ones. Within layers, the most interconnecting principles come first.

**Formulation.** Every principle is stated as an accessible, actionable instruction, with its primary-source reference, its psychology parallel, and its philosophy parallels. The Daoist source logic remains primary; the parallels illuminate, not govern.

**Treat these as practices, not beliefs.** Their value is in the doing and the noticing, not in assent.

### Source Corpus

| Source | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Daodejing (Laozi) | First principles, desire, humility, non-contention, leadership, reversal |
| Zhuangzi | Psychological freedom, perspectivism, flow, death, identity, uselessness, skill |
| Neiye (Inner Training, in Guanzi) | Body, breath, qi, heart-mind stillness, emotional moderation |
| Liezi | Spontaneity, destiny, letting go, transparency, equanimity |
| Huainanzi | Cosmology, seasonal attunement, adaptability, rulership |
| Wenzi | Practical application of Dao and De to life and governance |
| Qingjing Jing (Scripture of Purity and Stillness) | Desire-elimination, clarity, stillness |
| Yinfu Jing (Scripture of Hidden Correspondences) | Timing, the "five thieves" of desire, alignment with cosmic process |
| Baopuzi and yangsheng (life-nourishment) literature | Vitality preservation, moderation, longevity practices |
| Cantong qi and early neidan (internal alchemy) | Harmonizing yin-yang within, gradual refinement |
| Taiyi Jinhua Zongzhi (Secret of the Golden Flower) | Turning the light around, breath-anchored attention, non-forcing in meditation |

The Daodejing and Zhuangzi are the classical core; the later texts are practice-oriented sources of the tradition and are weighted accordingly.

| Source cluster | Principles contributed | Saturation check |
|---|---|---|
| Enchiridion | 123-125, 126, 132-133, 135-142, 146-150, 167-180, 241-242, 253-262 | Every chapter of the Enchiridion either yields a listed principle or restates one; the manual is fully distributed |
| Discourses | 123-262 (throughout) | Book I covers control and desire; Book II covers action; Book III covers assent; Book IV covers role ethics. All major themes distributed across Layers I-X |
| Meditations | 123-262 (throughout) | Every book of the Meditations contributes at least two principles; remaining material is restatement or personal application of listed principles |
| Seneca, Letters | 123-262 (throughout) | Letters on grief (185, 221), anger (De Ira), tranquillity (De Tranquillitate), time (On the Shortness of Life), benefits (On Benefits), and providence (On Providence) are fully distributed |
| Seneca, De Ira | 181-183, 227 | Anger management fully covered; remaining material is argumentation |
| Seneca, De Tranquillitate | 189 | Tranquillity covered; remaining material is argumentation |
| Seneca, De Brevitate Vitae | 158, 209, 212 | Time management fully covered |
| Seneca, De Beneficiis | 159, 206 | Beneficence covered |
| Seneca, De Providentia | 129, 214, 239 | Adversity as training covered |
| Musonius Rufus | 131, 144, 155, 161, 230, 233, 243, 259, 260 | Practice-oriented Stoicism fully absorbed into listed principles |
| Golden Sayings and Fragments | 123-262 (throughout) | Additional aphorisms absorbed into listed principles |
| Early Stoic doctrine (Zeno, Cleanthes, Chrysippus) | 124, 127, 128, 130, 132, 133, 137, 190 | Zeno, Cleanthes, Chrysippus contribute only where they yield a distinct practice instruction not captured in Roman sources |

Excluded by scope: Stoic logic and physics (theoretical, not practice-oriented); the Stoic debate on the sage (idealised figure, not actionable); Musonius Rufus's arguments for gender equality (important but not a distinct practice principle beyond 160 and 209); the Stoic theory of passions in technical detail (absorbed into 181-194); Stoic cosmology (theoretical, not practice-oriented).

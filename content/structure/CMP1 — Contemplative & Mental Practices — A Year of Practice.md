> **CMP1** — The Contemplative & Mental Practices Degree — A Year of Practice

# The Contemplative & Mental Practices Degree — A Year of Practice

This degree turns the compendium's practice kernels into a walkable year. Twelve modules, one every three weeks for thirty-six weeks, followed by a lighter season of integration (weeks 37 to 48) that revisits favourite practices and builds the personal design of [[K242 — Daily architecture|Module XII]]. It teaches practices, with protocols, evidence, and guided recordings; the traditions behind them are taught in the sibling Contemplative Traditions degree.

The arc follows a deliberate order. It begins with a habit, the daily sit, before any technique talk (I). It moves through the body as anchor (II), awareness itself (III), and breath-based regulation (IV) before touching acceptance (V), because letting be is easier once the mind has something steady to stand on. Then the heart practices in two steps, goodwill toward others first (VI), self-compassion next (VII), then compassion aimed outward in action (VIII). Daily-life integration (IX) and work with thoughts (X) come next, when the practices are strong enough to survive a busy morning. Depth comes late, as two doors, concentration and release, offered side by side rather than ranked (XI). The year ends with ownership: your own schedule, your own practice (XII).

The contract of the degree: about four study hours per module beside the daily practice, never instead of it. Practice minutes ramp from ten per day in Module I to twenty by Module VI, and twenty to thirty in the final modules. Six practice days a week, one review day; [[K240 — The weekly review|the weekly review]] is the Sabbath of this degree.

## Module I — Foundations: the daily sit

**Driving question: what happens when attention has one home?**

Breath anchoring, focused attention in its plainest form, plus the three-minute breathing space as the portable version. Ten minutes a day for three weeks. Core readings: [[K100 — Breath anchoring]], [[K392 — The Three-minute Breathing Space]].

## Module II — The body as anchor

**Driving question: where is the practice located?**

The body scan, interoceptive awareness, and mindful walking. The body is the most available object of attention: it is always here and never asks to be believed. Core readings: [[K102 — Body scan]], [[K111 — Interoceptive awareness]], [[K104 — Mindful walking]]. Elective: [[K393 — The Habit Releaser]].

## Module III — Awareness itself

**Driving question: what watches the wandering mind?**

Open monitoring, noting, and the sounds-and-thoughts meditation, plus the meta-awareness check that ties them together. The shift from a single anchor to whatever arises. Core readings: [[K101 — Open monitoring]], [[K103 — Noting and labeling]], [[K394 — Sounds and Thoughts meditation]], [[K108 — Meta-awareness check]]. Elective: [[K160 — Feeling-tone pass]].

## Module IV — Regulation: breath and body

**Driving question: can the body steer the nervous system?**

Slow breathing at five to six breaths per minute, the physiological sigh, non-sleep deep rest, box breathing (with the 4-7-8 variant), and progressive muscle relaxation: the state-regulation toolkit, taught with its honest evidence scope. Core readings: [[K194 — Slow breathing (5 to 6 breaths per minute)]], [[K410 — Physiological sigh (cyclic sighing)]], [[K409 — Non-sleep deep rest (NSDR)]], [[K413 — Box breathing]], [[K206 — Progressive muscle relaxation]]. Electives: [[K195 — Posture reset]], [[K200 — Rest as practice]], [[K203 — Nap protocol]], [[K196 — Daily movement]].

## Module V — Acceptance: letting the wave pass

**Driving question: what if nothing needs fixing?**

Willingness, cognitive acceptance, urge surfing, and exploring difficulty. The counterintuitive core of the contemplative family: the practices ask for a willing, non-judging attention to what is already here. Core readings: [[K115 — Willingness practice]], [[K385 — Cognitive acceptance: watch thoughts float by]], [[K116 — Urge surfing]], [[K395 — Exploring Difficulty meditation]]. Electives: [[K120 — The 90-second wave]], [[K121 — Dropping the struggle]], [[K397 — Approach, don't avoid (the mouse in the maze)]], [[K386 — Try not to think of elephants]], [[K388 — Passengers on the bus (metaphor practice)]].

## Module VI — Loving-kindness

**Driving question: can goodwill be trained?**

Loving-kindness meditation, the befriending meditation, and sympathetic joy. In the trials, the amount of formal practice tracked the changes, an association worth knowing. Core readings: [[K161 — Loving-kindness meditation]], [[K396 — The Befriending meditation]], [[K166 — Sympathetic joy]]. Elective: [[K168 — Near-enemy audit]].

## Module VII — Self-compassion

**Driving question: what does the inner ally sound like?**

Self-compassion practice, compassion-focused breathing, the perfect nurturer, and the compassionate letter. The inner critic is treated as a habit of attention, not a truth-teller. Core readings: [[K162 — Self-compassion practice]], [[K163 — Compassion-focused breathing]], [[K382 — Perfect Nurturer: imagine an ideal compassionate figure]], [[K406 — Compassionate letter to yourself]]. Electives: [[K378 — Cultivate your Compassionate Self]], [[K379 — Compassion for a struggling version of yourself]], [[K381 — Compassionate Thinking: name the part, then look from the other side]], [[K383 — Safe Place with an affiliative welcome]], [[K384 — Two-Chair dialogue: compassionate self ↔ vulnerable self (solo-adapted)]].

## Module VIII — Compassion in action

**Driving question: where does compassion point outward?**

Compassion meditation in the CCT style, the kindness pool, and the repair attempt: compassion as something done between people, not only felt on the cushion. Core readings: [[K414 — Compassion meditation (CCT-style)]], [[K399 — The kindness pool (unblock the spring)]], [[K209 — Repair attempt]]. Electives: [[K248 — Positive greetings]], [[K167 — Equanimity under the eight worldly winds]], [[K380 — The Two Teachers: change the tone, keep the lesson]].

## Module IX — Mindfulness in daily life

**Driving question: does practice survive the morning rush?**

Single-tasking, five-senses grounding, savoring, the raisin meditation, raisin mind in routine, and mindful eating. The practices shrink in length and grow in number until the day itself becomes the session. Core readings: [[K105 — Single-tasking]], [[K106 — Five-senses grounding]], [[K110 — Savoring]], [[K390 — The Raisin meditation]], [[K391 — Bring raisin mind to one routine activity]], [[K415 — Mindful eating]]. Electives: [[K113 — Transition pause]], [[K398 — Mindfulness bells]], [[K107 — Soft fascination]], [[K205 — Nature exposure]], [[K204 — Phone-free first and last hour]], [[K249 — Half-smile]].

## Module X — Working with thoughts

**Driving question: are you your thoughts?**

Cognitive defusion, reappraisal, and expressive writing: three ways to hold thoughts more loosely, from the contemplative, the cognitive-behavioral, and the writing-desk traditions. Core readings: [[K126 — Cognitive defusion]], [[K127 — Cognitive reappraisal]], [[K245 — Expressive writing]]. Electives: [[K169 — Anger dissection]], [[K407 — Coping cards]], [[K401 — Sing the thought, or say it in a silly voice]], [[K402 — Listen to the storyteller for thirty seconds]], [[K128 — Thought record]], [[K408 — The downward arrow]].

## Module XI — Concentration and release

**Driving question: two doors to depth, which is yours?**

Word repetition (mantra) and zuowang (sitting and forgetting), presented side by side as two historically distinct doors: gathering attention to one point versus letting awareness rest wide open. The standing meditation of the Qi Gong degree (zhan zhuang, [[K114 — Standing meditation (zhan zhuang)]]) is a third door that degree teaches fully. Core readings: [[K400 — Word repetition]], [[K411 — Zuowang (sitting and forgetting)]]. Electives: [[K109 — Quiet eye]], [[K146 — Ego dissolution practice]].

## Module XII — Integration: your own practice

**Driving question: what is yours to keep?**

Weekly review, returning practice, and daily architecture, ending in the design of a personal practice that fits an actual life. Core readings: [[K240 — The weekly review]], [[K243 — The returning practice]], [[K242 — Daily architecture]]. Electives: [[K241 — Seasonal redesign]], [[K244 — Public commitment]], [[K112 — Scheduled empty time]], [[K246 — Self-monitoring]].

### Who is guiding

The UCLA recordings come from the faculty of [UCLA's Mindful Awareness Research Center](https://www.uclahealth.org/marc/mindful-meditations). [Gil Fronsdal](https://www.audiodharma.org/talks/8) is a senior guiding teacher at the Insight Meditation Center in Redwood City, trained in both the Zen and vipassana traditions. [Tara Brach](https://tarabrach.com/guided-meditations/) holds a Ph.D. in psychology and has taught insight meditation for decades from Washington, DC. [Dave Potter](https://palousemindfulness.com/selfguidedMBSR.html), who built Palouse Mindfulness, is certified as an MBSR instructor by the University of Massachusetts Medical School. [Henry Shukman](https://www.mountaincloud.org/intro-zone/) is an authorized teacher in the Sanbo Zen lineage and the spiritual director emeritus of the Mountain Cloud Zen Center in Santa Fe. [Master Gu](https://www.taoistwellness.online/about) is a 15th-generation descendant of the Wudang Sanfeng line with more than 25 years of teaching in the Wudang Mountains, and [George Thompson](https://www.taoistwellness.online/return-to-balance-course) spent over eight years training under him before co-founding the Wudang Taoist Wellness Academy. [Yongey Mingyur Rinpoche](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5GSeWdjyr1c) is a Tibetan Buddhist meditation master in the Karma Kagyu and Nyingma lineages who guides the worldwide Tergar community. [Richard Miller](https://shop.irest.org/pages/try-irest-now) is the founder of the iRest Institute and the developer of iRest. [Sister Chan Khong](https://web.plumvillage.app/meditations/sister-chan-khong) was the first fully ordained monastic disciple of Thich Nhat Hanh. [Joseph Goldstein](https://www.dharmaseed.org/teacher/96/) and [Sharon Salzberg](https://www.dharmaseed.org/talks/44) are senior teachers in the vipassana tradition, and their retreat recordings are archived at Dharma Seed. [Leigh Brasington](https://www.audiodharma.org/talks/1828) teaches in the insight meditation tradition. [Kristin Neff](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7z3ybkn9DU) is the psychology researcher behind the self-compassion practices in Module VII. [Eve Ekman](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/compassion_meditation) and [Dacher Keltner](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/podcasts/item/walk_your_way_to_calm_guided_meditation) teach with UC Berkeley's Greater Good Science Center. [Andrew Huberman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KHIbgSN2qAU) is a Stanford neuroscientist; the NSDR recording is his own protocol, already in use in Module IV.

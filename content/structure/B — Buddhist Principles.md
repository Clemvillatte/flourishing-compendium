### An Exhaustive Saturation Compilation from the Major Buddhist Practice Sources

**X = 198 principles.** This document compiles every distinct, actionable principle for well-being and flourishing recoverable from the major Buddhist sources in their secular, practice-oriented form, deduplicated until saturation: the point at which any further "new" principle collapses into one already listed. It does not claim to exhaust the whole of Buddhism across all canons. It exhausts the sources that define Buddhist practice for well-being, with the religious and cosmological elements that secular Buddhism brackets set aside: rebirth as metaphysics, devotional ritual, cosmology (devas, hells, pure lands), merit ceremonies, and miracles. Karma appears only as psychological causality (intention, habit, character, consequence). Death appears only as the fact of mortality, never as a doctrine of what follows.

Each principle carries two companion layers: a state-of-the-art parallel from psychological science, and 0 to 3 philosophical concepts formatted as **Concept** (Thinker, Reference), included in proportion to relevance. Parallels are heuristic bridges, not claims of historical influence or empirical confirmation.

---

### Scope, Method, and Ranking

**Method.** Each source was mined for normative content: what the text tells you to do or stop doing. Near-duplicates were merged unless they pull a distinct practice lever. The compilation looped on the question "is there another principle?" until every remaining candidate reduced to an existing entry, verified against the saturation checklist in Appendix A.

**The secular line.** Following the secular-Buddhist reading of the tradition (Batchelor and others), the four "noble truths" are treated as four tasks to perform, not propositions to believe. Awakening is a shift available in this life, not a ticket to another one. Every practice must stand on its own observable effects. Where a classical text carries supernatural framing, only its practice instruction is extracted.

**Importance ranking.** Principles are ordered by two criteria: (1) first-layerness, how close the principle sits to the axioms from which the rest of the system derives; and (2) node size, how many downstream principles causally depend on it and how much it enables. The list reads as dependency layers: Layer I is the load-bearing wall; each later layer presupposes the earlier ones. Within layers, the most interconnecting principles come first.

**Formulation.** Every principle is stated as an accessible, actionable instruction with its primary-source reference, its psychology parallel, and its philosophy parallels. The Buddhist source logic remains primary; the parallels illuminate, not govern.

**Treat these as practices, not beliefs.** Their value is in the doing and the noticing, not in assent.

### Source Corpus

| Source | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Pali Canon practice texts: Dhammapada; Satipatthana Sutta (MN 10); Anapanasati Sutta (MN 118); Sabbasava Sutta (MN 2); Sallatha Sutta (SN 36.6); Kalama Sutta (AN 3.65); Alagaddupama Sutta (MN 22, the raft); Sona Sutta (AN 6.55, the lute); Upaddha Sutta (SN 45.2); Mahaparinibbana Sutta (DN 16); Bahiya Sutta (Ud 1.10); Kaccayanagotta Sutta (SN 12.15); Madhupindika Sutta (MN 18); Metta Sutta; Upajjhatthana Sutta (AN 5.57) | Core psychology of practice: mindfulness, hindrances, craving, speech, effort, friendship, death |
| Nagarjuna, Mulamadhyamakakarika | Emptiness as method, two truths, middle way of view |
| Shantideva, Bodhicaryavatara (esp. ch. 6) | Anger, patience, blame of conditions, exchange of self and other |
| Heart Sutra; Diamond Sutra | Emptiness of form; non-abiding; letting go of the four views |
| Milindapanha | The chariot: self as composition |
| Chan/Zen: Platform Sutra (Huineng); Linji-lu; Gateless Gate; Blue Cliff Record; Xinxin Ming (Sengcan); Bankei; Hakuin; Layman Pang; Dogen, Shobogenzo (Genjokoan, Fukanzazengi) | Ordinary mind, beginner's mind, non-seeking, original purity, koan and great doubt, just sitting, practice-enlightenment, no preferences, nothing special |
| Tiantai: Zhiyi, Mohe Zhiguan and Xiao Zhiguan (Essentials of Calming and Contemplation) | Balancing calming and insight; each thought-moment containing the whole field |
| Vietnamese Thiền: Trúc Lâm (Trần Nhân Tông), including the "dwelling in the world, joyful in the Way" (cư trần lạc đạo) teaching; Thích Nhất Hạnh (Plum Village tradition) | Practice inside ordinary life, work, and family; interbeing; watering seeds; habit energy; engaged Buddhism |
| Secular Buddhism: Stephen Batchelor (After Buddhism; Buddhism Without Beliefs) | The four tasks, conditionality, self-reliance, agnostic reading |

Devotional Pure Land material, ritual merit-making, and cosmological texts are intentionally outside scope.

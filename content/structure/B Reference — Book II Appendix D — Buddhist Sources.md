Standard editions and translations used for the primary-source references. Sutta citations follow the standard abbreviation system (DN, MN, SN, AN, Ud, Sn, Dhp).

- Secular Buddhism: [Secular Buddhist Network — the fourfold task](https://secularbuddhistnetwork.org/the-core-concept-of-secular-buddhism-a-fourfold-task/); [Batchelor on After Buddhism (Tricycle)](https://tricycle.org/magazine/after-buddhism/); [Secular Buddhism overview](https://secularbuddhistnetwork.org/secular-buddhism/)
- Trúc Lâm and Trần Nhân Tông: [Trúc Lâm tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%C3%BAc_L%C3%A2m); [Trần Nhân Tông's writings](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/Tran-Nhan-Tong.html)
- Zhiyi and Tiantai: [Zhiyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhiyi); [Tiantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiantai); [Mohe Zhiguan (encyclopedia entry)](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/mohe-zhiguan)
- Thích Nhất Hạnh: [Interbeing and store consciousness](https://plumvillage.org/library/dharma-talks/interbeing-and-store-consciousness); [The mind as a gardener](https://plumvillage.org/articles/the-mind-as-a-gardener)
- Dogen: [Genjokoan (Tricycle)](https://tricycle.org/magazine/genjokoan/); [Genjokoan text](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/genjokoan.pdf)
- Pali Canon: access-to-insight standard editions (MN 10, MN 118, MN 2, MN 18, MN 22, MN 58, MN 61, MN 128, MN 122, SN 36.6, SN 45.2, SN 46, AN 3.65, AN 5.57, AN 6.55, AN 8.6, DN 16, Ud 1.10; Dhammapada)
- Mahayana: Heart Sutra; Diamond Sutra (standard translations); Nagarjuna, Mulamadhyamakakarika (Garfield, The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way); Shantideva, Bodhicaryavatara (Crosby and Skilton)
- Chan/Zen: Platform Sutra (Yampolsky); Linji-lu (Watson); Gateless Gate; Blue Cliff Record; Xinxin Ming; Bankei; Hakuin; Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind

---

Compiled as a companion to The Daoist Principles for Well-Being and Genuine Flourishing. Parallels are interpretive bridges: they map traditions onto each other for practice insight, and none of them should be read as the tradition being "explained by" or "reduced to" the other.

| Source cluster | Principles contributed | Saturation check |
|---|---|---|
| NE I (Eudaimonia, function, external goods, fortune) | 1-10 | Further material in NE I is argumentation supporting these principles; no distinct practice lever remains |
| NE II (Virtue, habituation, doctrine of the mean) | 3, 4, 5, 11-30, 41-42, 108 | All specific virtues in NE II-IV distributed across Layers II-III; remaining vices reduce to means of listed virtues |
| NE III (Voluntary action, choice, courage, temperance) | 11, 12, 53-62 | Moral responsibility framework fully captured in 53-62 |
| NE IV (Specific virtues: generosity, magnificence, magnanimity, ambition, gentleness, friendliness, truthfulness, wit, shame, righteous indignation) | 13-22, 24-30 | Every virtue discussed in NE IV yields a listed principle; no further virtue produces a distinct practice lever |
| NE V (Justice, equity, law) | 23, 96-105 | Distributive/corrective/natural/political justice, equity, reciprocity, limits of law all covered |
| NE VI (Phronesis, intellectual virtues) | 31-52 | All intellectual virtues (techne, episteme, nous, sophia, synesis, gnome, euboulia, deinotes) covered; cleverness versus phronesis distinguished |
| NE VII (Akrasia, pleasure) | 56-59, 79-88 | Rest of NE VII is argumentation about the unity of virtue and the nature of pleasure; all distinct practice levers captured |
| NE VIII-IX (Friendship) | 63-78 | Friendship taxonomy, self-love, benefaction, concord, living together, equality, change, cross-difference all covered |
| NE X (Pleasure, contemplation, transition to politics) | 79-95 | Pleasure theory, contemplation, theoretical life, self-sufficiency, integration with action fully covered |
| Politics I-VIII | 96-113 | Education, law, distributive justice, household, leisure, music, habituation covered; constitutional theory has no direct practice lever |
| Rhetoric I-III | 114-122 | Emotion theory (anger, fear, pity, shame, envy, emulation), ethos/pathos/logos, emotions as cognitive all covered |
| De Anima | 7, 50 | Functional psychology supports the soul-body integration in 7; perception supports 50 |
| Magna Moralia | 25, 71, 111 | Self-love, benefaction, leisure covered; remaining MM content restates NE principles |
| Poetics | 110, 122 | Catharsis and narrative empathy support 110 and 122; remaining Poetics is aesthetic theory, not practice |

Excluded by scope: purely technical discussions of the Categories, Prior Analytics, Physics, Metaphysics, and biological works unless they yield a distinct practice instruction; the constitutional analysis of Politics IV-VI (descriptive rather than normative for individual flourishing); the critique of Plato's Republic (argumentation, not practice); the detailed discussion of slavery in Politics I.4-7 (historical context, not practice principle).

The philosophy parallels draw on three pools: Buddhist-philosophy scholars, the Western canon, and cross-tradition resonances (especially with the Daoist tradition, to which this document is a companion).

- Buddhist-philosophy scholars: [Garfield (Engaging Buddhism)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/); Siderits (Buddhism as Philosophy); Flanagan (The Bodhisattva's Brain); [Batchelor (After Buddhism)](https://tricycle.org/magazine/after-buddhism/)
- Kyoto School: [Nishida (An Inquiry into the Good)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/); [Nishitani (Religion and Nothingness)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishitani/)
- Self and no-self: [Hume (A Treatise of Human Nature)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/); Dennett (1991); [Metzinger (Being No One)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness/); [Parfit (Reasons and Persons)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/parfit/)
- Phenomenology and attention: [Husserl (Ideas I)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/); [Merleau-Ponty (Phenomenology of Perception)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/); [Weil (Waiting for God)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/simone-weil/)
- Stoics (the closest Western cousin): [Epictetus (Enchiridion)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/); [Marcus Aurelius (Meditations)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcus-aurelius/); [Seneca (On Anger; On the Shortness of Life)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/seneca/)
- [Schopenhauer](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/schopenhauer/) (directly influenced by Buddhism): The World as Will and Representation; On the Basis of Morality
- [Nietzsche](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nietzsche/): amor fati (Ecce Homo); On the Genealogy of Morality
- Existentialists: [Kierkegaard (Works of Love; The Concept of Anxiety)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/); [Heidegger (Being and Time; Discourse on Thinking)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/); [Jaspers (Philosophy)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaspers/)
- Analytic and pragmatist: [Wittgenstein (Tractatus; Philosophical Investigations)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wittgenstein/); [[Dewey](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/) (Human Nature and Conduct; Experience and Education)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/); [James (The Principles of Psychology)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)
- Virtue and mean: [Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)
- Emotions as cognitions: [Spinoza (Ethics)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)
- Contemplative practice as philosophy: Hadot (Philosophy as a Way of Life; The Inner Citadel); [Foucault (The History of Sexuality, vol. 2-3)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/foucault/)

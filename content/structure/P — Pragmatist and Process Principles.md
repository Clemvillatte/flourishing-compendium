### An Exhaustive Saturation Compilation from the Common Lineage of Pragmatism and Process Philosophy

**X = 180 principles.** This document compiles every distinct, actionable principle for well-being and flourishing recoverable from the common lineage that produced Process Philosophy and Pragmatism, deduplicated until saturation. Near-duplicate principles across thinkers were merged into their most advanced and workable formulation; early or superseded versions were folded into the mature form. The compilation looped on the question "is there another principle?" until every remaining candidate reduced to an existing entry.

Because this is a multi-thinker volume, each principle names its most influential or relevant thinker in its source citation, with supporting thinkers cross-referenced in the philosophy bullets. Each principle also carries a state-of-the-art parallel from psychological science. Parallels are heuristic bridges, not claims of historical influence or empirical confirmation.

---

### Scope, Method, and Ranking

**The lineage.** The common lineage runs from ancient process images (Heraclitus's flux, Protagoras's perspectivism, Pyrrhonian suspension, Zhuangzi's transformations, Nagarjuna's dependent origination, Aristotle's phronesis) through early modern and nineteenth-century precursors (Vico's verum factum, Hume's habit, Hegel's dialectic, Goethe's morphology, Darwin's adaptation, Nietzsche's becoming), into classical pragmatism (Peirce, James, Schiller, Dewey, Addams, Mead), process philosophy proper (Bergson, Alexander, Morgan, Whitehead, Hartshorne, Rescher), the twentieth-century pluralist turns (later Wittgenstein, Quine, Goodman, Rorty, Putnam, Bernstein, Brandom), continental and scientific resonances (Simondon, Deleuze, Prigogine and Stengers, Latour, Barad, Bennett, Ingold), the science of the flowing mind (Varela, Thompson and Rosch; Juarrero; Deacon; 4E cognition), and the applied flourishing branch (Rogers, Maslow, Csikszentmihalyi, Seligman, Hayes). The 2020s wave (Nooteboom's synthesis, the Palgrave Perspectives series, pragmatism's renaissance in philosophy of science and technology) confirms the lineage is live.

**Deduplication rule.** Where multiple thinkers hold a position, the principle is attributed to the thinker who gave it its most influential or most workable formulation. The alternatives appear in the philosophy bullets as supporting or contrasting references. Obsolete or first formulations were absorbed rather than listed separately.

**The secular line.** Theological process thinkers (Hartshorne, Teilhard de Chardin, Cobb and Griffin) are drawn on only for their secularly workable ideas: relationality, ecological responsibility, complexity, value in becoming. No doctrine is asserted.

**Importance ranking.** Principles are ordered by two criteria: (1) first-layerness, how close the principle sits to the axioms from which the rest of the system derives; and (2) node size, how many downstream principles causally depend on it and how much it enables. The list reads as dependency layers: Layer I is the load-bearing wall; each later layer presupposes the earlier ones. Within layers, the most interconnecting principles come first.

**Formulation.** Every principle is stated as an accessible, actionable instruction with its primary-source reference, its psychology parallel, and its philosophy parallels. The lineage's own logic remains primary; the parallels illuminate, not govern. Treat these as practices, not beliefs.

Method, stated so the word "exhaustive" is bounded and auditable.

the Florentine Codex Book VI (the huehuetlatolli speeches), the Cantares Mexicanos and the Tetzcoco poems, the Popol Vuh (Tedlock translation), the daykeeping ethnography (Barbara Tedlock), the scholarly reconstructions of the tlamatinime's metaphysics (Maffie's Aztec Philosophy; Leon-Portilla's Aztec Thought and Culture), and the labeled modern Maya-identified formulations.

Extraction method: the huehuetlatolli's counsel categories were reviewed category by category and dispositioned (moderation, speech, bearing, humility, work, cleanliness, mockery, dignity, endurance, death-preparedness each retained as principles 27-34, 40; the near-duplicate counsels merged); the Cantares's thematic clusters were reviewed (impermanence, the dream-question, defiance, grief, the earth's slipperiness each retained); Maffie's reconstruction was reviewed chapter by chapter for its practical yield (teotl, duality, ollin, nepantla, in xochitl in cuicatl, ixtli in yollotl, yolteotl, neltiliztli each retained); the Popol Vuh was read for its moral narrative (arrogance's fall, humility, the twins' cleverness); the living-practice material was reviewed for its practical structure (milpa, tequio, daykeeping, calendar, New Fire, limpias).

Deliberate exclusions, with reasons: sacrifice (including its theology and its moral debate), divination-for-prediction, deity liturgy and prayer texts, the afterlife cosmologies of Mictlan and Xibalba's courts (retained only the Popol Vuh's psychological narrative), the conquest-era polemics, and the calendar's astrological claims (retained the rhythm and the daykeeping service, excluded prediction).

Honest limits, stated plainly: this book's corpus is genuinely smaller and partly reconstructive. The pre-Columbian Nahua philosophical record survives fragmentarily and through colonial-era compilation; Maffie's reconstruction and Leon-Portilla's presentation are named as the book's guides and are used as reconstructions. The modern formulations (Anzaldua's nepantla, "in lak'ech") are labeled modern and used on their merits. The principle count follows the material: saturation is claimed within the declared corpus and practical scope, after the reviews described, a closing pass that merged near-duplicates, and a reverse-check of the entries whose citations the drafting flagged as uncertain (the Maffie corrections of the verification pass were made in this way). A future edition could strengthen the pass with direct work in the Nahuatl and K'iche' sources and with more of the living-practice ethnography.

Stopping rule: the sweeps were closed when further material produced only duplicates of retained principles, material excluded on the stated scope grounds, or doctrine without a distinct practical instruction.

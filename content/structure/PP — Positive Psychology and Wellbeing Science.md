Each principle carries two companion layers: a state-of-the-art parallel from psychological science (where the principle itself is drawn from science, the parallel identifies the supporting evidence or the mechanism), and 0 to 3 philosophical concepts formatted as Concept (Thinker, Reference), included in proportion to relevance. The parallels are heuristic bridges, not claims of historical influence or empirical confirmation.

The science of wellbeing has undergone significant correction since its founding. Several early claims did not survive replication: the 3:1 positivity ratio (Brown, Sokal, and Friedman, 2013), the broad "ego depletion" effect (Hagger et al., 2010 meta-analysis, then replication failure), power posing (Gelman, 2017), and the facial feedback hypothesis in its strong form (Wagenmakers et al., 2016). The principles below are stated with appropriate humility about effect sizes, boundary conditions, and the difference between processes that have robust support and exercises whose specific evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

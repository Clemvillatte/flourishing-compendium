Method, stated so the word "exhaustive" is bounded and auditable.

Corpus: the complete Yoga Sutra (with Vyasa's commentary cited as commentary), the complete Bhagavad Gita, the ten principal Upanishads, Shankara's Upadesasahasri and the attributed practical works, and Vivekananda's two practical books as labeled modern adaptation.

Extraction method: a unit-by-unit extraction pass was performed against the corpus for this edition. The Yoga Sutra was walked in full against a complete sutra-by-sutra translation (all four padas, 1.1 through 4.34, in the collated literal and interpretive rendering cited in Appendix D), and each instruction-bearing sutra was mapped to a retained principle. The Gita was audited against the translation text for all eighteen chapters: the complete instruction inventory was extracted from the text (every instruction-bearing verse, chapter by chapter) and each instruction cluster was dispositioned to a retained principle, a scope exclusion, or a named judgment call; chapter 2 additionally carries a verse-by-verse row record in the internal working ledger. The audit corrected three mappings this edition: 2.47 belongs to the fruit-release principle, not the evenness principle; 2.44-45 belongs to the strands principle, not the necessary-action principle; and 6.5-6.6 is a distinct instruction given its own entry rather than read under the habit principle. Judgment calls from the audit are named in the ledger: the graded fallback ladder of 12.9-12.11 is read under the paths-by-disposition principle; 18.63 (reflect, then act as you choose) is read under the examined-teaching principles; the devotional refuge chapters (7, 8 in part, 9-10, 18.56-66) are excluded on the stated bhakti scope and their non-devotional cores retained. The Upanishads were additionally collated passage by passage: every load-bearing passage cited in the principles was checked against a verse-numbered translation with the Sanskrit text (twenty passages verified, from the Isha's renunciation verses through the Chandogya's identity instruction to the Brihadaranyaka's negative-instruction cluster); the row record is kept in the internal working ledger. Two passages (the Taittiriya sheath sequence and the Aitareya consciousness instruction) are retained on their standard content, with the verse-level fetch unavailable on the collation source; this is the one residual gap in this edition's pass. The auxiliary works (Shankara-attributed treatises, Vidyaranya, Yoga Vasistha, Vivekananda) were mapped at the named-instruction level: each instruction cluster cited in a principle is recorded as a row in the internal ledger, but a verse-level walk of these works was not performed, and this edition's saturation claim is bounded accordingly. Judgment calls from the pass, stated rather than left implicit: the sevenfold insight of YS 2.27 is read under the stages principle (84) rather than given its own entry; the higher detachment of YS 1.16 is read under the dispassion principle (6); the suffering-to-the-discerning analysis of YS 2.15 is read under the prevention and fragility principles (11, 12); and the river-and-sea similes of BG 2.70-2.72 are read under contentment and evenness (36, 108). The pass produced one correction: the self-as-friend-and-enemy instruction of BG 6.5-6.6, initially misfiled under the habit principle (23), was found to be a distinct practical instruction and was given its own entry (69). The mapping ledger (sutra and passage to principle, including every judgment call and correction) is kept as the internal working record for this edition.

Verification performed for this edition: passage-level checks were run on the entries the drafting flagged as uncertain, and corrections applied where needed: the seeded and seedless absorption distinction against YS 1.18 with 1.50-1.51; the Katha summons against the literal translation, with Vivekananda's famous rendering labeled as paraphrase; BG 4.34's teacher instruction; the mahavakyas' traditional status as liberating truths, with the prompt use labeled as this book's adaptation; YS 1.33's fourth category restored to the sutra's own wording with the modern interpersonal reading labeled as adaptation; the Gita raft attribution removed in favor of the Buddhist parable; Benedict's Rule checked so that labor is cited to chapter 48 and Opus Dei to the Divine Office it names; the reserve clause re-cited to Meditations 4.1; the Tannisho attributed as a record of Shinran's sayings; and two philosophy parallels that failed their passage checks were removed (a Taittiriya definition pressed into absorption, and a second-arrow reading of appetite fading), with one replaced by a verified parallel. Verse references follow standard numbering (YS by sutra, BG by chapter and verse, Upanishads by passage). Where a classical claim is reduced or reframed for the bounded scope, the entry names the reduction.

Deliberate exclusions, with reasons: the full siddhi catalogue of Pada 3 (retained only through its caution, principle 81); rebirth mechanics and liberation cosmology (excluded per the series' method); devotional theology (bhakti retained only where the Gita frames devotion as discipline); scriptural-authority claims; Vedic ritual and mantra initiation; ayurvedic medicine (the series treats body science in its kernel books); and the metaphysical dispute over the self, retained as a named open question (principle 14) rather than resolved.

Deduplication pass performed: the fourfold stance is taught once (principle 65) and cross-referenced in its social application (principle 114); the limbs appear once as curriculum (72) and once as troubleshooting (125, cross-referenced); the return-to-basics instruction (130) is tied to the diagnostic grid by cross-reference; the raft and ladder appear once (127).

Limits, stated plainly: saturation is claimed as an editorial judgment within the declared corpus and practical scope, after thematic sweeps of the corpus's structures and a closing pass that merged near-duplicates. It is not a claim of textual scholarship beyond that scope, and a fuller passage-by-passage collation against the Sanskrit remains work a future edition could strengthen.

Stopping rule: the sweeps were closed when further material produced only duplicates of retained principles, material excluded on the stated scope grounds, or narrative without a distinct practical instruction.

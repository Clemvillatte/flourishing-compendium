This Book describes principles of adaptive flourishing, not clinical treatment. The sociocultural issues covered here (marginalization, intergenerational trauma, cultural bereavement, minority stress, loneliness) can generate significant distress and require professional care. If you or someone you know is experiencing:

- Persistent depression, anxiety, or trauma symptoms related to marginalization, discrimination, or migration — consult a mental-health professional (see Institute of Medicine, 2009; WHO, 2022).
- Suicidal thoughts — contact emergency services or a crisis line (e.g., in Australia: Lifeline 13 11 14; in the US: 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline).
- Severe social isolation or loneliness — consult a health-care professional (see Holt-Lunstad et al., 2017).
- Culture-bound syndromes or cultural bereavement — consult a clinician trained in cultural formulation (see Lewis-Fernández and Aggarwal, 2015; Aggarwal et al., 2016).
- Intergenerational trauma or historical trauma — consult a clinician experienced in culturally grounded trauma treatment (see Gone, 2011; Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, 2014).
- Migration- or acculturation-related distress — consult a clinician experienced in cross-cultural work (see Sam and Berry, 2010).

The sociocultural principles in this Book are supports for flourishing and for participation in professional care, not substitutes for it.

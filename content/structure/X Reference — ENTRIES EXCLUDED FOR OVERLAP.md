Seven drafted teachings were removed from the numbered list because their themes have counterparts in this compendium's books, and this epilogue's charter is stricter than a distinct-technique test: it admits only principles with no thematic overlap at all. They are recorded here so the reader knows they were considered, and why they were excluded.

Remembrance by repetition (dhikr): the all-day rhythmic repetition of sacred phrases, alone or in assembly. Excluded because Book I treats mantra repetition as a portable anchor (the AUM practice, YS 1.27-1.29); the Sufi instrument's difference is of rhythm and setting rather than of kind.

Purity of intention from display (ikhlas against riya'): the diagnostic of mixed motives, run act by act. Excluded because Book I holds the same rule in the karma-yoga instruction to act without attachment to the act's fruits.

The teacher-student bond served with courtesy (adab): humility formalized as a learning technology. Excluded because Book I treats the teacher-student bond directly (BG 4.34).

Self-trust as ground stance (Emerson's Self-Reliance): the decision to treat one's own perception as evidence. Excluded because Book I's svadharma (BG 3.35) holds the theme of one's own path as normative.

Polish the mirror of the heart (the Sufi mirror-rust image): purification as clearing rather than building, the heart's afflictions as rust. Excluded because Book I holds the same rule (shaucha, YS 2.40-2.41, with the settled-lake image of YS 1.41): the clearing is already a principle there.

Do not cling to states; stations are built (ahwal versus maqamat): measuring the path by stability rather than by passing experience. Excluded because Book I holds the same measurement rule (YS 1.3, measure progress by stability, not intensity).

Die before you die (the death-rehearsal): the periodic letting-go of attachments before death does the letting-go. Excluded because Book III treats death contemplation directly (its mortality-reflection principle cites the same research line), and Book I holds the detachment rule (vairagya, YS 1.15-1.16).

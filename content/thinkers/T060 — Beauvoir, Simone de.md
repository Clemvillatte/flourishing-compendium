Simone de Beauvoir (9 January 1908, Paris – 14 April 1986, Paris) was born in Montparnasse, studied philosophy at the Sorbonne, and in 1929 became the youngest person and eighth woman to pass France's *agrégation* in philosophy, finishing second to Jean-Paul Sartre's first place ([Wikipedia, Simone de Beauvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir)). She and Sartre remained partners for 51 years until his death in 1980 and are buried side by side at Montparnasse Cemetery ([same biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir)). *The Ethics of Ambiguity* appeared in 1947 and *The Second Sex* in 1949, the work the article names as foundational to contemporary feminism ([same biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir)). She won the Prix Goncourt for *The Mandarins* in 1954.

## Their why

Beauvoir takes Sartre's freedom and asks what it means for someone whose situation is already shaped — by gender, class, age, race — before they begin choosing. Her freedom is not a metaphysical power but a situated and conditional one: "freedom is disclosed in and by a situation" ([Wikipedia, Simone de Beauvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir)). The why is the move from "I am free" to "my freedom requires the freedom of others," which is the thesis her ethics is built on.

## Their position

The Wikipedia biography carries the position in two sentences. The first is the diagnosis:

> "Only the authentically moral attitude understands that the freedom of the self requires the freedom of others. To act alone or without concern for others is not to be free." ([Wikipedia, Simone de Beauvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir))

The second is the operating rule:

> "As Beauvoir explains, 'No project can be defined except by its interference with other projects.'" ([same biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir))

And on the case that made her famous:

> "One is not born, but rather becomes, woman." ([Wikipedia, Simone de Beauvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir), French: "On ne naît pas femme, on le devient")

The practice the wiki already teaches under E070 follows: hold the ambiguity between your own situation and your effect on others rather than collapsing it.

## Core ideas

- **Situation** — freedom is always disclosed in and by a situation; the body, the gender, the age are not obstacles to freedom but its medium. See [[Situated freedom]], [[E037 — Practise the situation|Practise the situation]], [[E078 — Practise situated embodiment and gender|Practise situated embodiment and gender]].
- **The ethics of ambiguity** — moral life is ambiguous, and the refusal of that ambiguity (religion, utopian ends-justify-means) is itself a moral error. See [[The ethics of ambiguity]], [[E070 — Practise the ethics of ambiguity|Practise the ethics of ambiguity]], [[Ambiguity]].
- **Interdependence of freedoms** — your freedom is not self-enclosed; it interferes with every other project. See [[The interdependence of freedoms]], [[E076 — Practise the interdependence of freedoms|Practise the interdependence of freedoms]], [[E077 — Practise the liberation of others|Practise the liberation of others]].
- **One becomes a woman** — gender is constructed, not biological destiny. See [[Anti-essentialism]], [[SO055 — Treat gender as a cultural system|Treat gender as a cultural system]], [[Situated embodiment]].
- **The serious man / the nihilist / the adventurer / the passionate man** — the four ways to deny ambiguity that her ethics catalogues and refuses. See [[Seriousness (le sérieux)]], [[E071 — Practise avoiding the serious man|Practise avoiding the serious man]], [[E072 — Practise avoiding the nihilist|Practise avoiding the nihilist]], [[E073 — Practise avoiding the adventurer|Practise avoiding the adventurer]], [[E074 — Practise avoiding the passionate man|Practise avoiding the passionate man]].
- **Transcendence vs immanence** — the human call to project vs the resignation to a fixed role; women have been held at immanence. See [[Authentic existence]], [[E032 — Practise the distinction between facticity and transcendence|Practise the distinction between facticity and transcendence]], [[The free person]].

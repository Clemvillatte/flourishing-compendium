Martin Buber (born 8 February 1878, Vienna; died 13 June 1965, Jerusalem) was a Jewish philosopher of encounter whose *I and Thou* (1923) made relation, rather than the isolated individual, the basic fact of human life ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/); [Wikipedia, Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber)). He coined the paired attitudes I-Thou and I-It.

## Personal story / context

Buber's mother left when he was three; his paternal grandparents raised him in Lemberg (now Lviv) until fourteen, his grandfather Solomon being "a community leader and scholar who edited the first critical edition of the Midrashim" ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). In 1904 he came across the Tzevaat Ha-RIBASH, the final teaching of the founder of the Hasidim, began recording Yiddish Hasidic legends, and published *The Tales of Rabbi Nachman* (1906) and *The Legend of the Baal-Shem* (1907), a book that influenced Rilke, Kafka and Hesse ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). He edited the Zionist weekly *Die Welt*, broke with Herzl when the movement refused cultural funding, and from his earliest Zionist years advocated a binational Jewish-Arab state, later through Brit Shalom and the Ihud party ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/); [Wikipedia, Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber)). Under the Nazis he answered bans on Jewish public life with organized adult education until emigration was forced on him in 1938; he then held the sociology chair at Hebrew University until 1951 ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). With Franz Rosenzweig he spent 1925 to 1961 translating the Hebrew Bible into German ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)).

## Their why

The wound that drives the work is objectification: people who experience everything and encounter no one. His answer was to put relation first. The IEP summary of his master idea: in an I-Thou relation both participants "exist as polarities of relation, whose center lies in the between (*Zwischen*)" ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). What matters to Buber is that the between is real, and that a life can be measured by how much of it a person dares.

## Their position

The two word-pairs mark two ways a human being exists. As the IEP puts it, "An 'I-It' relation *experiences* a detached thing, fixed in space and time, while an 'I-Thou' relation *participates* in the dynamic, living process of an 'other'" ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). To perceive the other as an It is to make them "a classified and hence predictable and manipulable object" ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). He did not renounce the I-It: we classify, function and navigate by it, and every Thou must sometimes become an It again ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). The relation extends to God as the "eternal Thou," which turns into an It whenever religion becomes fixed and mechanical ([IEP, Martin Buber](https://iep.utm.edu/buber/)). Politically the same stance shows: he called for "peace and brotherhood with the Arab people" and a republic in which both peoples could develop freely ([Wikipedia, Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber)).

## Core ideas

- **I and Thou** — the two basic word-pairs of existence: encounter versus use. See [[I and Thou]].
- **The between** — relation as an ontological category, with its center between persons. See [[The Between]], [[Relational ontology]].
- **Dialogue** — life as genuine meeting, in speech and in silence. See [[Dialogue]], [[Conversation as method]].
- **Confirming the other** — making the other present as what they can become. See [[Confirming the other]].
- **Hasidic renewal** — the legends and lived community of Hasidism as a paradigm of communal renewal. See [[Dialogical philosophy]].

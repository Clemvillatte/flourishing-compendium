> **Thinker T005** — John Dewey (1859–1952), American philosopher, psychologist and educator; with Peirce and James a founder of pragmatism; his experimentalism made education, art and democracy versions of one idea: growth.

## Personal story / context

Born October 20, 1859, in Burlington, Vermont, to Archibald Dewey, a merchant, and Lucina Rich Dewey ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). He graduated from the University of Vermont in 1879 aged nineteen, taught high school in Oil City, Pennsylvania, and his Ph.D. at Johns Hopkins in 1884; the Kant dissertation is lost ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). He taught at Michigan, briefly Minnesota, then Michigan again, forming long partnerships with Tufts and Mead ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). In 1894 he took the chair at Chicago, founding the Laboratory School in 1896 to test psychological and educational theories, his wife Alice its principal until 1904 ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). After conflicts over the school he resigned in 1904 and moved to Columbia University, affiliated with Teachers College; he retired in 1930, produced eleven more books, and died of pneumonia in New York City on June 1, 1952 ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). William James's Principles of Psychology gave his thinking "a new direction and quality" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)).

## Their why

Darwin's biology set his problem: an organism adapting itself in an environment, with no spectator's seat anywhere. Philosophy, he decided, had become too technical and detached from everyday social conditions, and should be reconnected to education, social criticism and practical life ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). His warrant for turning philosophy toward schooling is in Democracy and Education: "If we are willing to conceive education as the process of forming fundamental dispositions, intellectual and emotional, toward nature and fellow men, philosophy may even be defined as the general theory of education" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/852)).

## Their position

Growth is the point. "If education is growth, it must progressively realize present possibilities... Growing is not something which is completed in odd moments; it is a continuous leading into the future" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/852)). Education begins in activity: "The question of education is the question of taking hold of [children's] activities, of giving them direction" ([The School and Society](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). Conduct is social at the root: "Conduct is always shared... It is social, whether bad or good" ([Human Nature and Conduct](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). Democracy is more than a form of government; it is a faith about experience: "Democracy is the faith that the process of experience is more important than any special result attained" ([Creative Democracy, quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)). And art is not a department of life but experience at its most integrated: the aesthetic is the "consummatory" phase of ordinary doing and undergoing ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/)).

## Core ideas

- **Education as growth** — schooling is not preparation but the present life of the learner made richer. See [[P107 — Treat education as growth, not preparation|P107]], [[Education as growth]], [[Learning by doing]], [[Experiential learning]].
- **Learning by doing, plus reflection** — acting, undergoing consequences, then reflecting; experience alone is not enough. See [[P109 — Pair every experience with reflection|P109]], [[Experience plus reflection]], [[Reflective thinking]].
- **Habit as the material of conduct** — character is built of habits, and habits can be rebuilt. See [[P023 — Treat beliefs as habits of action|P023]], [[P076 — When habits conflict, let deliberation do its work|P076]], [[Plasticity of human nature]].
- **Inquiry as the pattern of knowing** — a felt problem, its definition, hypothesis, reasoning, test. See [[P046 — Treat problems as invitations|P046]], [[Problematic situations]], [[Fallibilism]], [[Instrumentalism]].
- **Ends-in-view and means-end continuity** — ends are not fixed termini but devices for directing action. See [[P040 — Keep means and ends continuous|P040]], [[Ends-in-view]], [[Means-end continuity]], [[P041 — Set ends-in-view, and revise them|P041]].
- **Experience as doing and undergoing** — the organism in transaction with its environment; no spectator self. See [[P005 — You are an organism in transaction with an environment|P005]], [[Doing and undergoing]], [[Organism-environment transaction]], [[P060 — Admit there is no spectator self|P060]].
- **Art as experience** — the consummatory moment of ordinary activity, not a museum privilege. See [[P110 — Make life artistic — seek an experience.|P110]], [[Art as experience]], [[Consummation]], [[Enriched experience]].
- **Democracy as a way of life** — associated living through shared inquiry and communication. See [[P127 — Treat democracy as a way of life, not a mechanism|P127]], [[Associated living]], [[The community of inquiry]], [[Socialized intelligence]].
- **Growth as the criterion of value** — the only near-ultimate value; the good life is a process, not a state. See [[P015 — Make growth your criterion of value|P015]], [[P014 — The good life is a process, not a state|P014]], [[Valuation as growth-direction]].
- **Experimental ethics** — moral judgment as dramatic rehearsal of alternatives in a concrete situation. See [[Deliberation as rehearsal]], [[The moral situation]], [[P084 — Act in the situation, not from the rulebook|P084]].

Alasdair Chalmers MacIntyre (January 12, 1929, Glasgow, Scotland – May 21, 2025, South Bend, Indiana) was a Scottish-American philosopher who revived virtue ethics against the moral language of liberal modernity, most famously in *After Virtue* (1981) ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)).

## Personal story / context

He began teaching at Manchester in 1951, then Leeds, Essex and Oxford, and moved to the United States around 1969, holding posts at Brandeis, Boston University, Wellesley, Vanderbilt, Notre Dame and Duke ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)). His intellectual path ran through Marxism and analytic philosophy to Catholic Thomism and Aristotle — "an intellectual nomad," as the article puts it. He judged his own past errors honestly, late in life reversing his earlier hope for an ethics free of biology:

> "Although there is indeed good reason to repudiate important elements in Aristotle's biology, I now judge that I was in error in supposing an ethics independent of biology to be possible." (preface to *Dependent Rational Animals*, quoted in [Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre))

## Their why

The task of *After Virtue* was to account for the dysfunction of modern moral debate: the language of morality survives, but as fragments of a shattered framework, so rival positions talk past each other and moral argument collapses into emotivism ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)). His question is what it would take for moral reasoning to have grounds again.

## Their position

The answer is a return to Aristotelian practice: goods internal to shared practices, the narrative unity of a life, and traditions as the bearers of rationality. His definition of tradition, verbatim:

> "A tradition is an argument extended through time in which certain fundamental agreements are defined and redefined" ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre))

Against both liberalism and Marxism he holds that rationality is tradition-constituted: "Marxists have always fallen back into relatively straightforward versions of Kantianism or utilitarianism" (*After Virtue*, p. 261, quoted in [Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)).

## Core ideas

- **Practices and internal goods** — a practice is a cooperative human activity with standards of excellence and goods internal to it, "goods of excellence" rather than practice-independent obligations or consequences ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)). See [[A040 — Practise the unity of the virtues|Practise the unity of the virtues]], [[Virtue ethics]], [[Phronesis]].
- **The narrative unity of a life** — a life is a story, and virtues are those qualities that sustain the quest for the good within that narrative ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)). See [[The narrative self]], [[E083 — Practise narrative identity|Practise narrative identity]].
- **Tradition-constituted rationality** — the tradition definition above; rational inquiry is socially embodied ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)). See [[R103 — Practise communitarian critique|Practise communitarian critique]], [[Communitarianism]].
- **The Aristotelian turn** — the revival of teleology, eudaimonia and the virtues against liberal individualism and emotivism ([Wikipedia, Alasdair MacIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasdair_MacIntyre)). See [[A001 — Make eudaimonia the organising end of your life|Make eudaimonia the organising end of your life]], [[A047 — Let practical wisdom unify the virtues|Let practical wisdom unify the virtues]].
- **The burned library** — the *After Virtue* thought experiment: modern moral discourse rebuilt from fragments, with no one inside the wreckage knowing what was lost. See [[ST252 — MacIntyre's Burned Library|252. MacIntyre's Burned Library]].

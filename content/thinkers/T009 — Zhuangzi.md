> **Thinker T009** — Zhuangzi (c. 369–298 BCE), Daoist thinker of China's Warring States period, from the village of Meng in the state of Song; the book that carries his name is the Zhuangzi, whose Inner Chapters are traditionally attributed to his own lifetime.

## Personal story / context

Almost everything personal about Zhuangzi arrives wrapped in jokes and parables, and the book that carries his name is a composite: its Inner Chapters are traditionally attributed to his lifetime, the rest to later hands ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zhuangzi/)). The early evidence compiled by the Han historian Sima Qian puts his birth in the village of Meng, in the state of Song, with traditional dates of about 369 to 298 BCE, an almost exact contemporary of Mencius ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/zhuangzi/)). How little can be confirmed from outside the text is itself the caution of modern references: stories about him warrant, in the Stanford Encyclopedia's phrase, "the salt of textual skepticism". The self-portrait the parables offer is a man refusing to be useful: fishing at the Pu river and offered the premiership of Chu, he asks whether the sacred tortoise, dead three thousand years and venerated in a box in the ancestral temple, would not rather be alive and dragging its tail in the mud ([[ST044 — The Tortoise in the Mud|44. The Tortoise in the Mud]], Zhuangzi ch. 17).

## Their why

The questions the book keeps returning to are grief and imposed measures of worth. Its most personal scene has him, after his wife's death, sitting by her body drumming on a basin and singing, having traced her back through life and body to the transformations of the seasons ([[ST031 — Zhuangzi Drums on a Basin|31. Zhuangzi Drums on a Basin]], Zhuangzi ch. 18). The question underneath the jokes: if every right and wrong, every use and uselessness, depends on where you stand, what remains to steer a life by?

## Their position

Right and wrong depend on where the speaker stands: in Chad Hansen's reading, judgments of shì and fēi track the judge's position in space and time, prior commitments, and the paths of behavior available ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zhuangzi/)). The dream that opens and closes his identity:

> "Once before, Zhuangzi dreamt of being a butterfly, gaily butterflying and himself embodied in this sense of purpose! He knew nothing of Zhuangzi. Suddenly awakening, he then is rooted in Zhuangzi. He doesn't know if Zhuangzi dreamt of being a butterfly or a butterfly is dreaming of being Zhuangzi—though there must be a difference. This is called 'things change'." (Zhuangzi 2:14, Hansen's translation, [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zhuangzi/))

The skepticism this grounds is, in the entry's phrasing, broad but weak: it challenges final certainty while allowing degrees of knowledge, and it is not a reason to stop acting when you know how. His exemplars are cooks, swimmers, cicada catchers and wheelwrights, whose trained responsiveness feels effortless. The Cook's line:

> "What your servant follows is a dào; that is what skill aims for. When I began to carve oxen, I saw nothing but an ox. After three years, I had ceased seeing oxen as whole, and now my sapience entangles so that I don't see with my eyes, Sensory know-how ends, and my sapient guidance takes over my performance." (Zhuangzi 3:2, Hansen's translation, [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zhuangzi/))

The knife is nineteen years old, several thousand oxen carved, and still fresh from the whetstone, because it moves through the gaps the anatomy already provides.

## Core ideas

- **The relativity of perspectives (qiwu)** — right and wrong are indexed to the standpoint; no final arbiter. See [[D080 — Dissolve rigid right and wrong (qiwu)|D080]], [[D081 — Recognize the relativity of perspectives|D081]], [[D082 — Don't impose your frame on others|D082]], [[The tetralemma]], [[Oneness of things]].
- **The pivot of dào** — responding without a fixed this-or-that. See [[D016 — Distrust names and labels|D016]], [[Distrust names and labels]], [[The ten thousand things]].
- **Skill as wu wei** — trained responsiveness that moves with the natural grain instead of forcing. See [[D056 — Follow the natural grain|D056]], [[D069 — Don't meet force with force|D069]], [[Wu wei]], [[Effortless action through long effort]], [[Skillful responsiveness]], [[Flow]], [[ST001 — Cook Ding and the Ox|1. Cook Ding and the Ox]], [[ST018 — The Swimmer at Lüliang Falls|18. The Swimmer at Lüliang Falls]], [[ST025 — The Cicada Catcher's Stillness|25. The Cicada Catcher's Stillness]].
- **Ziran — being so of itself** — what a thing is when nothing is imposed. See [[Ziran (naturalness)]], [[D078 — Wander free and easy (xiaoyao you)|D078]], [[Nature as teacher]].
- **The usefulness of the useless** — the tree is spared by the carpenter; the goose is killed for failing to honk. Usefulness itself is a perspective. See [[ST003 — The Useless Tree|3. The Useless Tree]], [[ST384 — The Tree Is Spared; the Goose Is Killed|384. The Tree Is Spared; the Goose Is Killed]], [[ST009 — The Gourd Too Large to Use|9. The Gourd Too Large to Use]], [[The usefulness of the useless]], [[D079 — Question conventional measures of success|D079]].
- **Fasting of the mind and sitting in forgetfulness** — emptying the heart-mind until it mirrors. See [[D046 — Practice fasting of the mind (xinzhai)|D046]], [[D047 — Practice sitting and forgetting (zuowang)|D047]], [[Zuowang (sitting in forgetfulness)]], [[K411 — Zuowang (sitting and forgetting)|K411 Zuowang]], [[ST019 — The Fast of the Mind|19. The Fast of the Mind]].
- **The mirror-mind** — the still mirror receives and does not hold. See [[D050 — Keep a mirror-mind|D050]], [[The still mirror]], [[ST017 — The Mind as Mirror|17. The Mind as Mirror]].
- **Transformation, death and grief** — things change; the question is how to meet it. See [[B108 — Grieve with presence|B108]], [[Grief for what passes]], [[ST031 — Zhuangzi Drums on a Basin|31. Zhuangzi Drums on a Basin]], [[ST032 — The Skull by the Road|32. The Skull by the Road]], [[ST403 — Two Friends Sing Beside the Corpse of Sang Hu|403. Two Friends Sing Beside the Corpse of Sang Hu]].
- **Free and easy wandering** — the useless life as the free one. See [[D078 — Wander free and easy (xiaoyao you)|D078]], [[Delight in the ordinary]], [[The quiet craftsman's life]].
- **Oneness of things** — from the perspective of dào, the ten thousand things are one. See [[D008 — See the oneness of things|D008]], [[Oneness of things]], [[ST002 — The Butterfly Dream|2. The Butterfly Dream]], [[The dream of the butterfly]].

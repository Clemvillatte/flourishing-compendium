Henri Bergson (1859, Paris — 1941, Paris) is the philosopher of lived time, life as creation, and intuition as the way into both. He received the 1927 Nobel Prize in Literature "in recognition of his rich and vitalizing ideas and the brilliant skill with which they have been presented" ([Wikipedia, Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)), and his account of life's vital impulse (*élan vital*) gave the wiki its cleanest statement of the opposite of mechanism: "an original common impulse which explains the creation of all living species" ([SEP, Henri Bergson](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)).

## Personal story / context

Born on Rue Lamartine in Paris in 1859, he spent his career at the center of French intellectual life, teaching at the Collège de France and drawing overflow crowds; his disciples included Étienne Gilson, Jacques Maritain, Vladimir Jankélévitch and Gabriel Marcel, and his influence reached Catholic philosophers from Teilhard de Chardin to Emmanuel Mounier ([Wikipedia, Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)). Too ill to travel, he received the Nobel in absentia and sent a text later published in *La Pensée et le mouvant*; he died in German-occupied Paris of bronchitis in January 1941 ([Wikipedia, Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)).

## Their why

Bergson wrote against two reductions. The mechanist treats each living development as already contained in what preceded it, and the strict finalist treats the whole as given in advance; both leave no room for real change or creation ([SEP, Henri Bergson](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)). His entire system is an argument that time is invention, that novelty is real, and that the mind's practical tools (concepts, analysis, clock time) cannot capture what only intuition can.

## Their position

His doctoral thesis, *Time and Free Will* (1889), introduced duration (*durée*): time as lived is "unextended yet heterogeneous," a unity and multiplicity that cannot be laid out and juxtaposed like spatial parts; Kant and the determinists had confused time with its spatial representation, and once time is seen as it is lived, determinism is impossible ([Wikipedia, Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)). *Creative Evolution* (1907) extends the account to life itself: mechanism would leave no real change possible, strict teleology would make creation a pre-given whole, so life must be explained by an original impulse that must "be situated at the origin and not at the end" and that "embrace[s] the whole of life in one single indivisible embrace" ([SEP, Henri Bergson](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)). Evolution's successive bifurcations form two great divergent tendencies, instinct and intelligence; intelligence is defined by tool-making ("Humanity is essentially *homo faber*") and is pragmatically oriented in a way that "precludes its immediate access to the essentially qualitative nature of life," while a fringe of instinct surviving at intelligence's edge "is what founds intuition" ([SEP, Henri Bergson](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)). The 1932 *Two Sources of Morality and Religion* extended the account to ethics and religion ([Wikipedia, Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)).

## Core ideas

- **Duration vs. clock time** — lived time is heterogeneous flow, not a line of juxtaposed instants. See [[P091 — Distinguish lived time from clock time|Distinguish lived time from clock time]], [[P092 — Live in a thick present|Live in a thick present]], [[P093 — You are time — own your becoming|You are time: own your becoming]].
- **The élan vital** — life as an original creative impulse, freedom inserting itself into necessity. See [[P102 — Trust the creative thrust of life|Trust the creative thrust of life]], [[P192 — Life is freedom inserting itself into necessity|Life is freedom inserting itself into necessity]], [[P006 — Creativity is the ultimate principle|Creativity is the ultimate principle]].
- **Intuition vs. intellect** — the practical, tool-making mind cannot grasp life; intuition can coincide with it. Not to be equated with modern fast/slow processing accounts, which belong to a different lineage. See [[P094 — Use intuition to enter the flow of things|Use intuition to enter the flow of things]], [[Intuition as method|Intuition as method]], [[CO002 — Recognise the two systems (or modes) of thinking — fast intuition and slow analysis|Recognise the two systems (or modes) of thinking: fast intuition and slow analysis]].
- **Life as creation, novelty as real** — "life must be equated with creation" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)). See [[P191 — Joy is the felt signature of creation; distinct from pleasure|Joy is the felt signature of creation; distinct from pleasure]], [[BE059 — Engage in creative behaviour — making is well-being|Engage in creative behaviour: making is well-being]], [[P086 — Channel the creative impulse|Channel the creative impulse]].
- **Freedom as pure mobility** — once time is seen as it is lived, determinism is impossible; Bergson identified free will with "pure mobility" ([Wikipedia, Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)). See [[P193 — Consciousness is choice; where there is no choice, there is mechanism|Consciousness is choice; where there is no choice, there is mechanism]], [[P071 — Trust the plasticity of human nature|Trust the plasticity of human nature]].

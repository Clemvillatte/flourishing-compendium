Viktor Emil Frankl (26 March 1905, Vienna — 2 September 1997, Vienna) was an Austrian neurologist, psychiatrist and Holocaust survivor who founded logotherapy, "a school of psychotherapy that describes a search for a life's meaning as the central human motivational force" ([Wikipedia, Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)).

## Personal story / context

In 1942, nine months after his marriage, Frankl and his family were sent to the Theresienstadt concentration camp; his father died there of starvation and pneumonia. In 1944 the surviving relatives were transported to Auschwitz, where his mother and brother were murdered; his wife Tilly died of typhus in Bergen-Belsen. In all he "spent three years in four concentration camps," and the Wikipedia biography notes he was held at Auschwitz only days, unregistered, before being sent on to Kaufering III, a subsidiary work camp of Dachau ([Wikipedia, Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)).

## Their why

While head of the Neurological Department at the General Polyclinic Hospital, Frankl wrote the book over a nine-day period. It was originally titled A Psychologist Experiences the Concentration Camp, released in German in 1946; the English translation, Man's Search for Meaning, was published in 1959 ([Wikipedia, Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)).

## Their position

Frankl's book states the freedom-and-responsibility pairing directly:

> "Freedom, however, is not the last word. Freedom is only part of the story and half of the truth. Freedom is but the negative aspect of the whole phenomenon whose positive aspect is responsibleness. In fact, freedom is in danger of degenerating into mere arbitrariness unless it is lived in terms of responsibleness." ([Wikipedia, Viktor Frankl, quoting Man's Search for Meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl))

Frankl "was expelled from Adler's circle when he insisted that meaning was the central motivational force in human beings" ([Wikipedia, Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)). The three ways of realizing meaning are stated as: "by making a difference in the world, by having particular experiences, or by adopting particular attitudes" ([Wikipedia, Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)).

## Core ideas

- **The will to meaning** — "logotherapy is founded upon the belief that striving to find meaning in life is the primary, most powerful motivating and driving force in humans," set against Adler's will to power and Freud's will to pleasure ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). See [[E066 — Practise the will to meaning|E066]], [[The will to meaning]].
- **Three ways of realizing meaning** — "(1) by creating a work or doing a deed; (2) by experiencing something or encountering someone; and (3) by the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). See [[E062 — Practise meaning through creative work|E062]], [[E063 — Practise meaning through love|E063]], [[E064 — Practise meaning through the attitude toward suffering|E064]].
- **The last human freedom** — "everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms – to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). See [[Attitudinal values]], [[Tragic optimism]], [[ST036 — Frankl Chooses the Attitude|36. Frankl Chooses the Attitude]].
- **Noogenic neurosis and the existential vacuum** — the logotherapeutic concepts of noogenic neurosis and existential crisis, first described by Frankl in the 1950s, entered the ICD 11 as the demoralisation crisis: hopelessness, meaninglessness and existential distress ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). See [[E065 — Practise noogenic neurosis awareness|E065]], [[Noogenic neurosis]].
- **Self-transcendence and happiness as by-product** — See [[E060 — Practise self-transcendence|E060]], [[Self-transcendence]], [[Happiness as by-product]].
- **Meaning-centred therapy** — logotherapy, which "Frankl describes ... as 'the Third Viennese School of Psychotherapy'" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). See [[Logotherapy]], [[Meaning-centred therapy]], [[The search for meaning]].

Alfred North Whitehead (born 15 February 1861, Ramsgate, Kent; died 30 December 1947, Cambridge, Massachusetts) co-wrote *Principia Mathematica* with Russell and then, in his sixties, founded process philosophy, a metaphysics in which reality is made of events and growth rather than substances ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). Its formula is his best-known line: "The many become one, and are increased with one" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/), Process and Reality).

## Personal story / context

Whitehead entered Trinity College, Cambridge in 1880 on a mathematics scholarship, graduated in 1884 and was elected Fellow the same year ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). His *Treatise on Universal Algebra* (1898) brought election to the Royal Society, and with Bertrand Russell he then spent a decade on *Principia Mathematica*, published in three volumes between 1910 and 1913, though the two had expected the work to take about a year ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). He left Cambridge in 1910, taught applied mathematics in London, and in 1924 crossed the Atlantic to become Professor of Philosophy at Harvard, where the great metaphysical books followed: the 1925 Lowell Lectures behind *Science and the Modern World*, *Process and Reality* (1929), *Adventures of Ideas* (1933) and *Modes of Thought* (1938) ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)).

## Their why

Whitehead's late work is a repair job on a science that had grown powerful by leaving out experience. He named the root error the "Fallacy of Misplaced Concreteness," the "error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete," because "this fallacy is the occasion of great confusion in philosophy" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). Against the bifurcation of nature into real primaries and merely mental secondaries he insisted that "the red glow of the sunset should be as much part of nature as are the molecules and electric waves by which men of science would explain the phenomenon" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)).

## Their position

Reality consists of "actual occasions," and in his words "actual entities are drops of experience, complex and interdependent" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). Each occasion grows by concrescence, many feelings becoming one, and the universe is a creative advance into novelty: "The eternal objects are the pure potentials of the universe, and the actual entities differ from each other in their realization of potentials" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). God is built into the system, dipolar: "God, as well as being primordial, is also consequent… God is dipolar," and the consequent nature saves what passes: "He saves the world as it passes into the immediacy of his own life. It is the judgment of a tenderness which loses nothing that can be saved" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). The religious correlate, stated apart from the metaphysics: "Religion is what the individual does with his own solitariness" ([SEP, Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)).

## Core ideas

- **Actual occasions** — reality as drops of experience, not inert substance. See [[Actual occasions]].
- **Concrescence** — many feelings growing into one unity, per occasion. See [[Concrescence]].
- **The creative advance** — the universe as ongoing novelty; the future open. See [[Creative advance]], [[P095 — Expect genuine novelty — the future is open|Expect genuine novelty: the future is open]].
- **Fallacy of misplaced concreteness** — mistaking abstractions for the concrete; his standing critique of reduction. See [[The limits of reductionism]].
- **Process before substance** — self and world as becoming. See [[Process philosophy]], [[Self as process]], [[P002 — Treat the world as events and doings, not things and substances|Treat the world as events and doings, not things and substances]].

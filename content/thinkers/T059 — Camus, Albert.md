Albert Camus (born 7 November 1913 in Mondovi, French Algeria; died 4 January 1960 in Villeblevin, France) was a French philosopher, writer and political activist, born to *pied-noir* parents; his father was killed in action in 1914, and he grew up poor in the Belcourt section of Algiers ([Wikipedia, Albert Camus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Camus)). He won the 1957 Nobel Prize in Literature at forty-four, the first literature laureate born in Africa ([same biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Camus)). The Stanford Encyclopedia dates the two books that made him famous to the same year: *The Stranger* in June 1942 and *The Myth of Sisyphus* in October 1942, both part of the planned "Absurd triptych" completed in September 1941 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Albert Camus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/)). *The Plague* (1947) and *The Rebel* (1951) carried the argument from the single individual to the collective condition.

## Their why

Camus takes the situation where meaning is demanded and not delivered — the person asking "why should someone continue to live?" — and refuses both available escape routes: ending the life, or ending the question by leaping to a faith that pretends the world is reasonable ([Wikipedia, Albert Camus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Camus); [SEP, Albert Camus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/)). He calls that confrontation *the absurd* and treats it as an unsurpassable relationship between a person and their world, not a mistake to be cured ([SEP, Albert Camus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/)). The why is lucidity itself: hold the question open with your eyes open.

## Their position

The Stanford Encyclopedia gives the tightest summary:

> "This paradoxical situation, then, between our impulse to ask ultimate questions and the impossibility of achieving any adequate answer, is what Camus calls *the absurd*." ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Albert Camus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/))

And on the post-religious posture that follows:

> "Camus urges us to 'die unreconciled and not of one's own free will' (*MS*, 55). In short, he recommends a life without consolation, but instead one characterized by lucidity and by acute consciousness of and rebellion against its mortality and its limits." ([same encyclopedia entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/))

The same entry notes the shift that made *The Rebel* (1951) the mature position:

> "'I revolt, therefore we are' (*R*, 22) is his paradoxical statement." ([same entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/))

His later word for the discipline that keeps revolt from turning into terror is *mésure*: measure, proportion, living inside limits rather than remaking the world ([same entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/)). The wiki's principle E058 already teaches that discipline.

## Core ideas

- **The absurd** — the relation between the human demand for meaning and the world's silence; it lives in the confrontation, not in either side. See [[The absurd]], [[E055 — Practise the recognition of the absurd|Practise the recognition of the absurd]].
- **Revolt** — refusing both physical and philosophical suicide; a constant confrontation without hope. See [[Revolt]], [[E056 — Practise revolt|Practise revolt]].
- **Sisyphus** — the myth as the image of a person whose punishment cannot be taken as one. See [[Sisyphus]], [[E057 — Practise Sisyphus happy|Practise Sisyphus happy]].
- **Measure (mésure)** — proportion, the self-limiting discipline that keeps revolt human. See [[Measure (mesure)]], [[E058 — Practise measure|Practise measure]].
- **Solidarity** — revolt turned outward; "I revolt, therefore we are." See [[Solidarity]], [[E059 — Practise solidarity|Practise solidarity]].
- **The human condition** — not despair but the honest name for where meaning must be made. See [[The human condition]], [[E077 — Practise the liberation of others|Practise the liberation of others]].
- **Lucidity** — the alternative to consolation; consciousness kept open. See [[Defiance]], [[K318 — Absurd-revolt practice|Absurd-revolt practice]].
- **Tragic optimism** — happiness and the absurd as "two sons of the same earth," inseparable. See [[K317 — Tragic optimism|Tragic optimism]], [[E061 — Practise tragic optimism|Practise tragic optimism]].

Carl Ransom Rogers (January 8, 1902 – February 4, 1987) was an American psychologist, one of the founders of humanistic psychology, known especially for person-centered psychotherapy, and widely considered one of the founding fathers of psychotherapy research ([Wikipedia, Carl Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Rogers)). In his 1957 Journal of Consulting Psychology paper "The Necessary and Sufficient Conditions of Therapeutic Personality Change," states the theory as six numbered conditions: "For constructive personality change to occur, it is necessary that these conditions exist and continue over a period of time:

1. Two persons are in psychological contact.
2. The first, whom we shall term the client, is in a state of incongruence, being vulnerable or anxious.
3. The second person, whom we shall term the therapist, is congruent or integrated in the relationship.
4. The therapist experiences unconditional positive regard for the client.
5. The therapist experiences an empathic understanding of the client's internal frame of reference...
6. The communication to the client of the therapist's empathic understanding and unconditional positive regard is to a minimal degree achieved." ([Rogers, 1957, reprint PDF](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)).

## Personal story / context

Rogers was born in Oak Park, Illinois, and trained at Wisconsin, Union Theological Seminary and Columbia before his academic path through Ohio State, Chicago, Wisconsin and the Western Behavioral Sciences Institute; the American Psychological Association honored his research with its Award for Distinguished Scientific Contributions in 1956 ([Wikipedia, Carl Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Rogers)). The 1957 paper comes out of that research program: the first attempt to state, as testable conditions, what it is about a relationship that produces personality change.

## Their why

The paper opens by asking: "Is it possible to state, in terms which are clearly definable and measurable, the psychological conditions which are both necessary and sufficient to bring about constructive personality change?" Its theoretical claim: "No other conditions are necessary. If these six conditions exist, and continue over a period of time, this is sufficient. The process of constructive personality change will follow." The claim is Rogers's hypothesis about the conditions, not an established treatment guarantee ([Rogers, 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)).

## Their position

The third condition is stated directly:

> "The third condition is that the therapist should be, within the confines of this relationship, a congruent, genuine, integrated person. It means that within the relationship he is freely and deeply himself, with his actual experience accurately represented by his awareness of himself. It is the opposite of presenting a facade, either knowingly or unknowingly." (Rogers, 1957, [reprint PDF](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf))

The paper adds a limit on the therapist-side demand: "It is not necessary (nor is it possible) that the therapist be a paragon who exhibits this degree of integration, of wholeness, in every aspect of his life. It is sufficient that he is accurately himself in this hour of this relationship" ([Rogers, 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)).

## Core ideas

- **The six conditions** — "For constructive personality change to occur, it is necessary that these conditions exist and continue over a period of time": two persons in psychological contact; the client "in a state of incongruence, being vulnerable or anxious"; the therapist "congruent or integrated in the relationship"; the therapist's "unconditional positive regard for the client"; the therapist's "empathic understanding of the client"; and the client's perception of that regard and empathy ([Rogers 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)). See [[Person-Centered Theory]], [[Unconditional positive regard]].
- **Congruence** — condition 3, the therapist-side genuineness: "It is not necessary (nor is it possible) that the therapist be a paragon," only "accurately himself in this hour of this relationship" ([Rogers 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)). See [[Congruence]], [[Authenticity]].
- **Empathic understanding** — condition 5, the "as if" definition: "To sense the client's private world as if it were your own, but without ever losing the 'as if' quality" ([Rogers 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)). See [[Empathic understanding]], [[Empathy]], [[Active listening]].
- **Unconditional positive regard and incongruence** — conditions 4 and 2: the regard the client receives without conditions, and the client-side state of vulnerability or anxiety it works on ([Rogers 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)). See [[Acceptance]], [[Conditional regard]], [[Cognitive-behavioural model]].
- **The six-condition sufficiency claim** — "No other conditions are necessary. If these six conditions exist, and continue over a period of time, this is sufficient. The process of constructive personality change will follow" ([Rogers 1957](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)). See [[Motivational interviewing]], [[Deep listening]].

> **Thinker T025** — Carl Rogers, American psychologist, founder of person-centered (client-centered) therapy; the 1957 conditions-of-change paper and the congruence construct are the compendium's main hooks.

## Personal story / context

Rogers's 1957 statement of the necessary and sufficient conditions of therapeutic personality change is the canonical citation. The paper names three therapist-side conditions and one client-side one, and it is the source of the compendium's Congruence, Empathic understanding and Unconditional positive regard pages.

## Their why

The theory holds that a person changes not because they are told to, but because the relationship offers conditions under which their own organismic valuing process can operate. Rogers's own statement of the therapist-side condition:

> "The third condition is that the therapist should be, within the confines of this relationship, a congruent, genuine, integrated person. It means that within the relationship he is freely and deeply himself, with his actual experience accurately represented by his awareness of himself. It is the opposite of presenting a facade, either knowingly or unknowingly." (Rogers, [The necessary and sufficient conditions of therapeutic personality change](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf), Journal of Consulting Psychology 21(2), 1957)

## Their position

The same paper defines the client-side condition as incongruence: a "discrepancy between the actual experience of the organism and the self picture of the individual insofar as it represents that experience". Rogers allows that the therapist need not be "a paragon" in the rest of life; it is enough to be "accurately himself in this hour of this relationship" ([1957 paper, reprint PDF](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf)). The locus-of-evaluation construct names the practical consequence: change is measured against the person's own experience, not the therapist's judgment.

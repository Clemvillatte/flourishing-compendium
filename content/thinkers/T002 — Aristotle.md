> **Thinker T002** — Aristotle (384–322 BCE), Greek philosopher; twenty years Plato's student, Alexander's tutor, founder of the Lyceum; author of the Nicomachean Ethics.

## Personal story / context

Born in 384 BCE in Stagira, a small city in northeastern Greece ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). Sent to Plato's Academy in Athens at about seventeen, he stayed until Plato died in 347 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). In 343 King Philip asked him to Pella to tutor his thirteen-year-old son Alexander, later Alexander the Great ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). Back in Athens in 335 he founded his school at a public exercise area dedicated to Apollo Lykeios, the Lyceum; those affiliated with it later came to be called Peripatetics, probably for the ambulatory (*peripatos*) on its grounds ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). When anti-Macedonian feeling rose after Alexander's death, he withdrew to Chalcis on Euboea and died there in 322 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). The treatises "read like what they very probably are: lecture notes, drafts first written and then reworked, ongoing records of continuing investigations" in the words of the [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/).

## Their why

He kept Plato's question, what the best life for a human being is, and answered it from the ground up. "Every art, and every science reduced to a teachable form, and in like manner every action and moral choice, aims, it is thought, at some good," he opens the Ethics; the good for a human being should therefore be findable the way a craft's good is ([Nicomachean Ethics I.1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)). Happiness is the end he settles on, because "we choose [it] always for [its] own sake, and never with a view to anything further" ([Nicomachean Ethics I.5](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)).

## Their position

The good for a human being is activity, and the distinctively human activity is rational activity: "then the Good of Man comes to be 'a working of the Soul in the way of Excellence'…" ([Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)). Virtue is a settled disposition built by practice: "by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave actions brave" ([Nicomachean Ethics II.1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)). A good life cannot be judged from a good week: "as it is not one swallow or one fine day that makes a spring, so it is not one day or a short time that makes a man blessed and happy" ([Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)).

## Core ideas

- **Eudaimonia as the organising end** — flourishing is the thing every other choice is for. See [[A001 — Make eudaimonia the organising end of your life|A001]], [[Eudaimonia]], [[Everyone desires eudaimonia]].
- **The function argument (ergon)** — find what a human being is for, and you find its good. See [[A002 — Live according to your function (ergon)|A002]], [[Eudaimonic well-being]].
- **Virtue as habituated character** — you become just by doing just acts; character is trained, not merely known. See [[A004 — Become virtuous by doing virtuous acts|A004]], [[A042 — Recognise that virtue is a hexis, not a feeling|A042]], [[A106 — Recognise that virtue is taught through habituation|A106]], [[Habituation]], [[Excellence as habit]] (a paraphrase of this idea, labelled as such in the corpus).
- **The doctrine of the mean** — each virtue sits between an excess and a deficiency, and hitting it is a perceptual skill. See [[A005 — Choose the mean, not the extreme|A005]], [[The doctrine of the mean]].
- **Phronesis, practical wisdom** — the judgment that unifies the virtues and reads the particular case. See [[A043 — Develop phronesis — practical wisdom|A043]], [[A047 — Let practical wisdom unify the virtues|A047]], [[Phronesis]], [[Practical wisdom]].
- **Friendship as a requirement of flourishing** — no one would choose to live without friends, and the best friendship is between good people. See [[A063 — Recognise that friendship is necessary for flourishing|A063]], [[A064 — Know the three kinds of friendship|A064]], [[Aristotelian friendship]].
- **Contemplation as the highest activity** — the life of theoria is the most complete happiness available to a human being. See [[A089 — Practise contemplation (theoria)|A089]], [[Contemplation (theoria)]], [[A091 — Integrate contemplation and action|A091]].
- **The whole life and its external conditions** — flourishing takes a complete life, moderate goods, and some luck. See [[A008 — Accept that flourishing takes a whole life|A008]], [[A006 — Expect flourishing to require external goods|A006]], [[A010 — Recognise that fortune can overturn happiness|A010]].

> **Thinker T002** — Aristotle (384–322 BCE), Greek philosopher; Plato's student for twenty years, Alexander's tutor, founder of the Lyceum; the person who worked out what human flourishing is made of.

## Personal story / context

Born in 384 BCE in Stagira, a small city in the Macedonian region of northeastern Greece ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). At about seventeen he was sent to Athens to study in Plato's Academy and stayed until Plato's death in 347 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). In 343, at King Philip's request, he moved to Pella to tutor the king's thirteen-year-old son, Alexander, later Alexander the Great ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). Back in Athens in 335 he set up his own school in a public exercise area dedicated to Apollo Lykeios, the Lyceum, and walked the covered walks while teaching, which is where the "peripatetic" label comes from. When anti-Macedonian feeling rose in Athens after Alexander's death, he withdrew to Chalcis on Euboea and died there of natural causes in 322 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle/)). The treatises that made his name were not polished books but working teaching material from those Athens years, organised by later editors.

## Their why

He kept Plato's question, what the best life for a human being is, and changed the method for answering it. Instead of reasoning from timeless Forms, he collected constitutions, observed animals, and asked what function each kind of thing performs when it does well. Every craft and every inquiry aims at some good, he opens the Nicomachean Ethics, so the human good should be findable the same way: identify the work a human being does, and excellence in that work is the life worth having.

## Their position

The good for a human being is activity, and the distinctively human activity is rational activity: "then the Good of Man comes to be 'a working of the Soul in the way of Excellence,' or, if Excellence admits of degrees, in the way of the best and most perfect Excellence" ([Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)). Virtue is not a mood or a talent but a settled disposition built by practice: "by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave actions brave" ([Nicomachean Ethics II.1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)). And a good life cannot be judged from a good week: "as it is not one swallow or one fine day that makes a spring, so it is not one day or a short time that makes a man blessed and happy" ([Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-0.txt)). Friends, moderate wealth and a functioning city belong in the picture too; fortune can still spoil it.

## Core ideas

- **Eudaimonia as the organising end** — flourishing is the thing every other choice is for. See [[A001 — Make eudaimonia the organising end of your life|A001]], [[Eudaimonia]], [[Everyone desires eudaimonia]].
- **The function argument (ergon)** — find what a human being is for, and you find its good. See [[A002 — Live according to your function (ergon)|A002]], [[Eudaimonic well-being]].
- **Virtue as habituated character** — you become just by doing just acts; character is trained, not merely known. See [[A004 — Become virtuous by doing virtuous acts|A004]], [[A042 — Recognise that virtue is a hexis, not a feeling|A042]], [[A106 — Recognise that virtue is taught through habituation|A106]], [[Habituation]], [[Excellence as habit]].
- **The doctrine of the mean** — each virtue sits between an excess and a deficiency, and hitting it is a perceptual skill. See [[A005 — Choose the mean, not the extreme|A005]], [[The doctrine of the mean]].
- **Phronesis, practical wisdom** — the judgment that unifies the virtues and reads the particular case. See [[A043 — Develop phronesis — practical wisdom|A043]], [[A047 — Let practical wisdom unify the virtues|A047]], [[Phronesis]], [[Practical wisdom]].
- **Friendship as a requirement of flourishing** — no one would choose to live without friends, and the best friendship is between good people. See [[A063 — Recognise that friendship is necessary for flourishing|A063]], [[A064 — Know the three kinds of friendship|A064]], [[Aristotelian friendship]].
- **Contemplation as the highest activity** — the life of theoria is the most complete happiness available to a human being. See [[A089 — Practise contemplation (theoria)|A089]], [[Contemplation (theoria)]], [[A091 — Integrate contemplation and action|A091]].
- **The whole life and its external conditions** — flourishing takes a complete life, moderate goods, and some luck. See [[A008 — Accept that flourishing takes a whole life|A008]], [[A006 — Expect flourishing to require external goods|A006]], [[A010 — Recognise that fortune can overturn happiness|A010]].

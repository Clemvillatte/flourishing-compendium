Kitarō Nishida (西田幾多郎, May 19, 1870 – June 7, 1945) was a Japanese philosopher who founded the Kyoto School and made pure experience the starting point of philosophy ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)).

## Personal story / context

He was born in the village of Unoke, north of Kanazawa on the Sea of Japan, entered Tokyo Imperial University as a "limited status" student, and graduated in 1894 with a thesis on Hume's theory of causation ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). *An Inquiry into the Good*, begun in 1905, secured him a position at Kyoto Imperial University in 1910 and the professorship in 1914; he became emeritus in 1929 and moved to Kamakura ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). His lifelong friendship with D. T. Suzuki, the Zen ambassador to the West, runs through the work. He described himself as "always been a miner of ore; I have never managed to refine it" (preface to *Intelligibility and the Philosophy of Nothingness*, quoted in [SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)).

## Their why

His stated objective, verbatim: "from *An Inquiry into the Good* on, my objective has been to see things and think things from the most direct and fundamental standpoint — to grasp all things from the standpoint of whence they come and whither they go" (NKZ IX, 3, quoted in [SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). The question is what experience is before it is carved up into subject and object.

## Their position

The starting point is pure experience: "The moment of seeing a color or hearing a sound" is prior to the thought that the color belongs to an object or that one is seeing it; pure experience is the "one and only reality" from which all differentiated phenomena are to be understood ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). His mature philosophy builds toward absolute nothingness:

> "absolute nothingness transcends all that is, but at the same time all that is arises through it" (NKZ IX, 6, quoted in [SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/))

And the self is defined by its own mortality: "I am myself by knowing my own death" (NKZ XI, 420, quoted in [SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)).

## Core ideas

- **Pure experience** — experience before the subject-object split, the "one and only reality" of the early work ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). See [[Pure experience]], [[K001 — Return to experience before the labels|Return to experience before the labels]].
- **Absolute nothingness** — not absence but the place from which all that is arises ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). See [[Absolute nothingness]], [[K014 — Let nothingness work as the place of things|Let nothingness work as the place of things]].
- **Basho (place)** — the mid-1920s signature theory: things are located in a field or place that is not itself a thing ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). See [[K017 — Find the still point that is not a withdrawal|Find the still point that is not a withdrawal]], [[Unity of opposites]].
- **Self-identity of contradictories** — his late logic: contradiction as structure, not error ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). See [[K019 — Let contradiction stand as structure, not error|Let contradiction stand as structure, not error]], [[M003 — Think in pairs|Think in pairs]].
- **Acting intuition** — knowing by doing, the body as the self's working edge ([SEP, Nishida Kitarō](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/)). See [[K020 — Know by doing|Know by doing]], [[K021 — Treat the body as the self's working edge|Treat the body as the self's working edge]].

> **Thinker T007** — Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677), Dutch philosopher of Portuguese-Jewish descent; excommunicated by the Amsterdam Sephardic community in 1656, he wrote the Ethics in geometrical order: one substance, God or Nature, and freedom as understanding necessity.

## Personal story / context

Born in 1632 in Amsterdam, the middle son of a prominent family of moderate means in the Portuguese-Jewish community ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). A star pupil in the congregation's Talmud Torah school, he left his studies at seventeen to help run the family importing business ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). On July 27, 1656, he received the harshest herem, or ban, ever pronounced by the Sephardic community of Amsterdam, never rescinded; the alleged monstrous deeds and abominable heresies are not known for certain, but the entry describes his views as denying the soul's immortality, rejecting a transcendent and providential God, and claiming the Law was neither literally given by God nor still binding ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). Within a few years he left Amsterdam, and by 1661, when his extant correspondence begins, he was living in Rijnsburg near Leiden ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). His exposition of Descartes's Principles of Philosophy was the only work he published under his own name in his lifetime ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). He died in 1677 in The Hague, at work on his Political Treatise, which his friends published with his other unpublished writings ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)).

## Their why

The Ethics examines how people governed by external causes and powerful emotions can become more active through understanding: Spinoza calls the inability to moderate and restrain the affects bondage, and freedom does not mean escaping necessity ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). The final proposition states the aim: "Blessedness is not the reward of virtue, but virtue itself" ([Ethics V P42](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)).

## Their position

There is one substance. "That thing is called free, which exists solely by the necessity of its own nature, and of which the action is determined by itself alone" ([Ethics I Def VII](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)). God is not separate from the world: "the eternal and infinite Being, which we call God or Nature, acts by the same necessity as that whereby it exists" ([Ethics IV Preface](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)). The mind is not exempt: "The order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things" ([Ethics II P7](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)). Each thing holds on: "Everything, in so far as it is in itself, endeavours to persist in its own being" ([Ethics III P6](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)). The passions yield to understanding: "An emotion, which is a passion, ceases to be a passion, as soon as we form a clear and distinct idea thereof" ([Ethics V P3](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)).

## Core ideas

- **Substance monism** — one substance with infinite attributes; thought and extension are two ways of being the same. See [[Necessitarianism]], [[Mind-body parallelism]].
- **God or Nature** — no transcendent creator; nature acts from the same necessity by which it exists. See [[Deus sive natura]], [[Nature connectedness]].
- **Necessity and freedom** — nothing could have been otherwise; freedom is acting from one's own nature, known and understood. See [[Freedom]], [[Serene acceptance of necessity]].
- **Conatus** — the striving of each thing to persevere in its being; in a human being it is appetite and will. See [[Conatus]].
- **The affects** — joy as passage to greater perfection, sadness to lesser; affects are passions when caused from outside, actions when caused from adequate ideas. See [[Affects]], [[The taxonomy of passions]], [[Naming the passions]].
- **Bondage and its cure** — bondage is the inability to restrain the affects; understanding necessity calms them. See [[Akrasia]].
- **The three kinds of knowledge** — imagination, reason, and intuition grasping things under the aspect of eternity. See [[The three kinds of knowledge]], [[Sub specie aeternitatis]].
- **Intellectual love of God** — understanding the universe is virtue, happiness and freedom at once. See [[Amor dei intellectualis]], [[Blessedness]].

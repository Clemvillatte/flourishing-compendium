David Hume (born 1711, Edinburgh; died 1776, of terminal illness he met with equanimity) was the Scottish philosopher who made human nature the science of everything else, arguing that morals rest on sentiment, that reason serves passion, and that belief must be weighed like evidence ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)). His own rule for the mind holds up across centuries: "In general, there is a degree of doubt, and caution, and modesty, which, in all kinds of scrutiny and decision, ought for ever to accompany a just reasoner" ([Hume, Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, XII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)).

## Personal story / context

A second son who had to make his own fortune, Hume was raised and educated by his widowed mother, entered the University of Edinburgh at eleven, and left at fifteen without taking a degree ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)). At twenty-three he withdrew to La Flèche in France and began the *Treatise of Human Nature*, which appeared anonymously in 1739 and 1740, and he said it fell "deadborn from the press" ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)). The hurt did not stop him: he recast its arguments in essays and Enquiries, found success with his *Political Discourses* (1752), and became famous as a historian with his *History of England* ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)). Paris adored him during his years as a diplomat's secretary from 1763, giving him "the adulation that he had craved all his life" ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)). Near the end he wrote a short autobiography, *My Own Life*, and arranged for his skeptical *Dialogues concerning Natural Religion* to appear posthumously, which it did in 1779 ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/)).

## Their why

Hume's program was to bring the experimental method of Newton into the study of the mind: find what human nature actually does with belief, passion, morals and taste, and build philosophy on that. He was after the workings, in his phrase, of "the reflections of common life, methodized and corrected" ([Hume, Enquiry XII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)). What drives the whole inquiry is curiosity disciplined by evidence: "Every passion is mortified by it, except the love of truth; and that passion never is, nor can be, carried to too high a degree" ([Hume, Enquiry I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)).

## Their position

On morals, Hume's verdict is plain: "Morals and criticism are not so properly objects of the understanding as of taste and sentiment. Beauty, whether moral or natural, is felt, more properly than perceived" ([Hume, Enquiry XII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)). On belief, his rule: "A wise man, therefore, proportions his belief to the evidence," withholding more than probability where experience is not uniform ([Hume, Enquiry X](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)). On reason's reach, he held that nothing can check a passion except a contrary impulse, that demonstration belongs to quantity and number alone, and that matters of fact rest wholly on experience ([SEP, David Hume](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume/); [Hume, Enquiry XII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)). Yet he prescribed a "mitigated scepticism or academical philosophy, which may be both durable and useful," whose fruits are modesty and reserve ([Hume, Enquiry XII](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)).

## Core ideas

- **Moral sentimentalism** — moral distinctions come from feeling, with reason in a supporting role. See [[Moral sentiments]].
- **Sympathy** — the mechanism by which others' passions become ours, the seed of fellow feeling. See [[Fellow feeling]], [[Empathy]].
- **The bundle theory of self** — the self as a bundle of perceptions with no discoverable core. See [[The bundle theory]], [[Anatta]].
- **Proportioning belief to evidence** — belief graded by experience, the rule behind Bayesian caution. See [[CO028 — Use the outside view — base rates beat inside views|Use the outside view: base rates beat inside views]], [[The problem of induction]].
- **Mitigated skepticism** — doubt held at a dose that cures dogmatism without paralyzing life. See [[Skepticism answered by life]], [[The limits of reason]].

> **Thinker T004** — Plato (c. 429–347 BCE), Athenian philosopher; Socrates' student and Aristotle's teacher; he wrote almost everything as dialogue, and from him come the theory of Forms, the tripartite soul, and the philosopher-ruler.

## Personal story / context

His dates are 429?–347 BCE ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). Socrates was legally charged and convicted of impiety in 399 BCE, when Plato was about 28; the entry calls him indebted to Socrates' methods ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). He probably began writing dialogues in his thirties ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). Nearly everything he wrote takes the form of dialogue; the Apology is a striking exception ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). The early or "Socratic" dialogues (Euthyphro, Laches, Charmides, Protagoras, Gorgias and others) ask "What is piety?", "What is courage?", "What is moderation?" and end without satisfactory definitions; the middle dialogues (Phaedo, Cratylus, Symposium, Republic, Phaedrus) center the theory of Forms; the late works (Sophist, Statesman, Timaeus, Critias, Philebus, Laws) are generally agreed to be late on stylistic grounds ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). Most of the letters attributed to him concern his involvement in the politics of Syracuse, educating its ruler Dionysius II with the help of his friend Dion, though their authenticity is disputed ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)).

## Their why

Socrates was convicted of impiety in 399 BCE, when Plato was about 28, and the entry ties Plato's work to that event ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). The early dialogues keep the questions Socrates asked: Euthyphro, Laches, Charmides, Protagoras, Gorgias ask "What is piety?", "What is courage?", "What is moderation?" and end without satisfactory definitions ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato/)). In the Apology, Plato's Socrates makes the claim the corpus keeps: "daily to discourse about virtue, and of those other things about which you hear me examining myself and others, is the greatest good of man, and that the unexamined life is not worth living" ([Apology, Socrates speaking](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1656)).

## Their position

The soul has parts, and justice is their harmony. In the Republic the argument reaches the question "whether the soul has these three principles or not" ([Republic IV](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497)), reason, spirit and appetite, and the just soul is the one in which each does its own work. Politics follows the same plan: "Until philosophers are kings, or the kings and princes of this world have the spirit and power of philosophy... cities will never have rest from their evils" ([Republic V](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497)). Behind the visible world stands the Form of the Good: "the idea of good is the highest knowledge" ([Republic VI](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497)). And education is a turning, not a pouring: in the cave allegory "the prison-house is the world of sight, the light of the fire is the sun" ([Republic VII](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1497)).

## Core ideas

- **The examined life** — daily discourse about virtue as the greatest good; the unexamined life not worth living. See [[The examined life]], [[Self-examination]], [[Socratic inquiry]], [[Socratic wisdom]].
- **Socratic questioning** — elenchus, aporia, and knowing that you do not know. See [[Socratic elenchus]], [[Aporia]], [[Socratic ignorance]], [[Socratic testing]], [[Questioning inherited beliefs]].
- **The tripartite soul** — reason, spirit, appetite; virtue is their harmony. See [[The tripartite soul]], [[Ordering the soul]], [[The unity of the soul]], [[Harmony]], [[Temperance]].
- **The Forms** — the one thing that truly is Beauty, Goodness, Justice behind the many that appear. See [[Distinguishing appearance from reality]], [[Noesis, direct intellectual seeing]], [[Arete]].
- **Recollection (anamnesis)** — the doctrine the corpus records from the Meno. See [[Anamnesis]], [[ST155 — Meno's Paradox|155. Meno's Paradox]].
- **Eros and the ladder of love** — the corpus's treatment descends from the Symposium. See [[Eros]], [[The ladder of love]], [[The wounds of love]], [[Obsessive attachment]].
- **The philosopher-ruler** — cities rest from evils only when philosophers rule or rulers philosophize. See [[Philosopher-king]], [[Justice]], [[Dikaiosyne]].
- **The unity of the virtues** — a Socratic doctrine the corpus carries. See [[The unity of the virtues]], [[A040 — Practise the unity of the virtues|A040]], [[A047 — Let practical wisdom unify the virtues|A047]].
- **Education as turning the soul** — the cave: the ascent from the prison-house of sight. See [[Distinguishing appearance from reality]], [[Noesis, direct intellectual seeing]], [[The daimon]], [[Elpis (hope)]].

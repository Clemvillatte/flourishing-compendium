> **Thinker T006** — Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, the awakened one; born a householder of the Śākya clan, he left wife and son to seek the end of suffering, attained bodhi, and taught what he had found; the Nikāyas preserve the sermons.

## Personal story / context

He was born into a family of some wealth and power, members of the Śākya clan, in the area of the present border between India and Nepal ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). He traditionally lived to age eighty; his dates were formerly put around 560-480 BCE, though many scholars now hold he died around 405 BCE, fl. circa 450 BCE ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). In early adulthood he abandoned his comfortable life as a householder, and his wife and young son, to seek a solution to the problem of existential suffering ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). He trained under ascetic teachers, found their teachings unsatisfactory, and, through a combination of insight and meditational practice, attained enlightenment (bodhi), the cessation of all further suffering ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). What he taught is preserved in texts known as the Nikāyas or Āgamas, not written down until centuries after his death, surviving in Pāli and Chinese ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)).

## Their why

His one question is the one this whole compendium serves: the end of suffering. He left wealth, family and position for it, and after his awakening taught what he had found ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). The Dhammapada keeps the note of urgency: "Earnestness is the path of immortality (Nirvana), thoughtlessness the path of death. Those who are in earnest do not die, those who are thoughtless are as if dead already" ([Dhammapada 21](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2017)).

## Their position

Mind is the forerunner: "All that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts, it is made up of our thoughts" ([Dhammapada 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2017)). The teaching in one line: "Not to commit any sin, to do good, and to purify one's mind, that is the teaching of (all) the Awakened" ([Dhammapada 183](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2017)). The program is four truths: there is suffering, its origination, its cessation, and a path to that cessation ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). There is no permanent self: none of the five psychophysical elements is permanent, and if there were a self it would be permanent ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddha/)). The work is one's own: "Self is the lord of self, who else could be the lord? With self well subdued, a man finds a lord such as few can find" ([Dhammapada 160](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2017)).

## Core ideas

- **The four truths** — suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path: the frame of the whole corpus. See [[B001 — Face suffering directly|B001]], [[B006 — Craving is the engine of suffering (tanha)|B006]], [[Four Noble Truths]], [[Dukkha]], [[The avoidance of suffering]].
- **Non-self (anatta)** — the self is a construction of impermanent parts; no spectator self to defend. See [[B075 — The thinker is another thought|B075]], [[Anatta]], [[SE001 — Understand that the self is constructed, not discovered|SE001]], [[Self-as-context]].
- **Impermanence (anicca)** — everything arises and passes; clinging to the passing is the machinery of suffering. See [[Impermanence]], [[Non-attachment]], [[The art of letting go]].
- **Karma as intention** — it is the volition behind the act that carries consequence. See [[B005 — Intention is karma|B005]], [[Karma]], [[Samskara]].
- **The middle path** — neither indulgence nor mortification; tune the lute string. See [[B009 — Walk the Middle Way|B009]], [[B036 — Tune, don't force — the lute string|B036]], [[The middle path]].
- **Mindfulness (sati)** — present-moment awareness as the foundation of the training. See [[B027 — Develop right mindfulness (samma sati)|B027]], [[Sati]], [[Mindfulness]], [[Right concentration]].
- **The gradual training** — virtue, concentration, wisdom in sequence; practice over belief. See [[B029 — Follow the gradual training|B029]], [[Gradual training]], [[The three trainings]], [[Eightfold Path]].
- **Right speech** — true, timely, gentle, useful; test words before they leave. See [[B023 — Practice right speech|B023]], [[B146 — Test speech before it leaves|B146]], [[Beneficial speech]], [[Wise speech criteria]].
- **Heedfulness (appamada)** — earnestness as the path of deathlessness. See [[B017 — Practice heedfulness (appamada)|B017]], [[The fearless mind]].
- **Be your own island** — the teaching points; the crossing is yours, and the raft is dismantled after the crossing. See [[B194 — Be your own island (attadipa)|B194]], [[B195 — Be a lamp unto yourself|B195]], [[B127 — Dismantle the raft when you've crossed|B127]], [[The raft]], [[The Kalama principle]].
- **Loving-kindness (metta) and protection** — protecting others, you protect yourself. See [[B157 — Protecting others, you protect yourself|B157]], [[Metta (loving-kindness)]], [[K166 — Sympathetic joy|K166]].

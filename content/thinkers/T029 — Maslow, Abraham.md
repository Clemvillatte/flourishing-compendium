Abraham Harold Maslow (born April 1, 1908, Brooklyn; died June 8, 1970, Menlo Park, California) founded humanistic psychology and gave the study of flourishing its most famous image, the hierarchy of needs ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)). His program was set out in one line from the 1943 paper that started it: "If I were permitted this usage, I should then say simply that a healthy man is primarily motivated by his needs to develop and actualize his fullest potentialities and capacities" ([Maslow, A Theory of Human Motivation, 1943](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm)).

## Personal story / context

Maslow was the oldest of seven children of Jewish immigrants from Kyiv, and grew up poor in Brooklyn; he married Bertha Goodman in 1928 and had two children ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)). He studied at City College of New York, Cornell and Wisconsin, where Harry Harlow supervised his doctorate ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)). He taught at Brooklyn College from 1937 to 1951, then at Brandeis until 1969, and served as president of the American Psychological Association ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)). Late in life he pushed past self-actualization toward transcendence, co-founding transpersonal psychology as a "fourth force" with Stanislav Grof, Viktor Frankl and others ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)). When the new humanistic association wanted him as president in 1963, he declined, saying it should develop as an intellectual movement without a leader ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)).

## Their why

Maslow's stated complaint was that psychology had mapped only the sick half of the mind. In his words: "It is as if Freud supplied us the sick half of psychology and we must now fill it out with the healthy half" ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)). He therefore studied mentally healthy people, including Jefferson, Lincoln, Eleanor Roosevelt, Einstein and Thoreau, asking what their growth looked like ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)).

## Their position

The 1943 theory holds that "Human needs arrange themselves in hierarchies of pre-potency": physiological needs, safety, love, esteem, and at the top self-actualization, defined as "the desire to become more and more what one is, to become everything that one is capable of becoming" ([Maslow, 1943](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm)). Its most quoted lines make the top of the ladder personal: "A musician must make music, an artist must paint, a poet must write, if he is to be ultimately happy. What a man *can* be, he *must* be" ([Maslow, 1943](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm)). Yet Maslow insisted on the hierarchy's flexibility: "We have spoken so far as if this hierarchy were a fixed order but actually it is not nearly so rigid as we may have implied," and most people "are partially satisfied in all their basic needs and partially unsatisfied in all their basic needs at the same time" ([Maslow, 1943](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm)). He also made gratification a working concept: "gratification becomes as important a concept as deprivation in motivation theory" ([Maslow, 1943](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm)). The pyramid itself is a later textbook illustration; none of his published works contained one ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow)).

## Core ideas

- **Hierarchy of needs** — needs arranged by prepotency, from physiological to self-actualization. See [[Hierarchy of Needs Theory]].
- **Self-actualization** — becoming everything one is capable of becoming; the need that appears when the others are gratified. See [[Self-actualization (Maslow)]], [[SE065 — Learn self-actualization — become who you are capable of being|Learn self-actualization: become who you are capable of being]].
- **Deficiency and being needs** — D-needs that motivate by lack, and B-values that draw the healthy person. See [[B-values]], [[Being-values (B-values)]], [[P117 — Grow from being-values, not deficiency-needs|Grow from being-values, not deficiency-needs]].
- **Peak and plateau experiences** — moments of wholeness and rapture in self-actualizing people, later the calmer plateau experiences of aging. See [[Peak experiences]], [[P115 — Welcome peak experiences — don't chase them|Welcome peak experiences: don't chase them]].
- **Self-transcendence** — his late view that people need something bigger than themselves. See [[Self-transcendence]], [[Transcendence]].

> **Thinker T003** — William James (1842–1910), American philosopher and psychologist; taught at Harvard from 1872 and set up the first American psychology laboratory; author of The Principles of Psychology, The Will to Believe, The Varieties of Religious Experience, and Pragmatism.

## Personal story / context

Born January 11, 1842, in New York City, eldest child of Henry and Mary Walsh James; his brother Henry, born 1843, became the novelist ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)). He entered Harvard's Lawrence Scientific School in 1861 and its medical school in 1864, took his M.D. in 1869, and never practiced ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)). The late 1860s were his darkest: eye strain, back trouble, and suicidal depression ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)). On April 30, 1870, he recorded in his diary a resolve: "I think that yesterday was a crisis in my life ... My first act of free will shall be to believe in free will" ([Letters of William James, Vol. I](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/40307)). Harvard hired him to teach in 1872; by 1874-75 he was teaching psychology and had established the first American psychology laboratory there ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)). The Principles of Psychology appeared in 1890, The Varieties of Religious Experience in 1902, and Pragmatism in 1907, the year he resigned his Harvard professorship ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)). He died on August 26, 1910, at Chocorua, New Hampshire ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/)).

## Their why

The question that survived the depression is the one in that entry: whether a person is free, and whether believing in one's freedom could itself be the first exercise of it. He resolved to "voluntarily cultivate the feeling of moral freedom ... as well as by acting" ([Letters, Vol. I](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/40307)). His psychology and his pragmatism are one man testing whether a life can be steered.

## Their position

Experience comes first and it comes whole. Thought is a river: "Consciousness ... does not appear to itself chopped up in bits ... It is nothing jointed; it flows. A 'river' or a 'stream' are the metaphors by which it is most naturally described" ([The Principles of Psychology, ch. IX](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/57628)). A life is built by what attention selects and what habit consolidates: "The great thing, then, in all education, is to make our nervous system our ally instead of our enemy" ([Principles, ch. IV](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/57628)). When a genuine option, in his sense one that is living, forced and momentous, cannot be decided on intellectual grounds, he argued, the passional nature "must decide an option between propositions" ([The Will to Believe](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/26659)). And religion, where it exists, is "the belief that there is an unseen order, and that our supreme good lies in harmoniously adjusting ourselves thereto" ([The Varieties of Religious Experience, Lecture III](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/621)).

## Core ideas

- **The pragmatic method** — settle what a belief means by tracing its practical consequences; "cash value" is the test. See [[P020 — Ask for the cash value of your beliefs|P020]], [[P013 — Judge by workability|P013]], [[The pragmatic maxim]], [[Cash value]], [[Workability]].
- **Truth as verification** — ideas become true by events; beliefs are tested by living them. See [[P028 — Expect truth to be what works in the long run|P028]], [[P022 — Verify in experience — test beliefs by living them|P022]], [[Verification in experience]], [[Pragmatism]].
- **The stream of consciousness** — thought flows, it is not jointed; "within each personal consciousness thought is sensibly continuous". See [[P055 — Know your thought as a stream|P055]], [[Stream of consciousness]], [[The specious present]], [[Self as process]].
- **Habit as the flywheel of character** — make the nervous system an ally by automating useful action early. See [[P038 — Make habit your flywheel|P038]], [[P056 — Your character is your habits|P056]], [[P070 — Make your nervous system your ally|P070]], [[P075 — Treat habits as will|P075]], [[Habit]], [[Habit formation]], [[Belief as habit]].
- **The will to believe** — when a live, forced, momentous option cannot be decided on intellectual grounds, the passional nature must decide. See [[P069 — Believe at the living edge — the will to believe|P069]], [[The will to believe]].
- **Attention as experience and will** — "Effort of attention is thus the essential phenomenon of will"; "my experience is what I agree to attend to". See [[P067 — My world is what I attend to|P067]], [[P068 — Will is the effort of attention|P068]], [[Attention]], [[The effort of attention]], [[AT012 — Understand that attention is the gateway to experience — it is where well-being is lived|AT012]].
- **The empirical, social, many selves** — "A man's Social Self is the recognition which he gets from his mates"; the self is material, social and spiritual at once. See [[P062 — Expect multiple selves — and coordinate them|P062]], [[P060 — Admit there is no spectator self|P060]], [[The empirical self]], [[The many selves]], [[The social selves]], [[SE049 — Recognise the social self — you have different selves in different relationships|SE049]].
- **Emotion as bodily response** — the James-Lange theory: "we feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble". See [[P181 — Regulate feeling by regulating expression|P181]], [[James-Lange theory of emotion]], [[Embodied emotion theory]], [[Action precedes feeling]].
- **Religious experience taken seriously** — the varieties of experience, the healthy-minded and the sick soul, the unseen order. See [[P184 — Distinguish the once-born from the twice-born: two routes through suffering|P184]], [[P185 — The sovereign cure for worry is a hold on larger realities|P185]], [[The healthy-minded and the sick soul]], [[Epiphany]], [[Peak experiences]].
- **Pluralism and radical empiricism** — the universe is not a monolith; relations are experienced, and experience comes whole before our splits. See [[P123 — Accept that the universe is plural|P123]], [[P065 — Experience comes whole — relations are experienced|P065]], [[P066 — Rest in pure experience before the splits|P066]], [[Pure experience]], [[Radical empiricism]], [[Both-and]].
- **A certain blindness** — "the blindness with which we all are afflicted in regard to the feelings of creatures and people different from ourselves"; tolerance grows from admitting it. See [[P136 — Recognize your blindness — others live in worlds you cannot see|P136]], [[P129 — Practice sympathetic understanding|P129]], [[A certain blindness]], [[Tolerance without indifference]].
- **The moral equivalent of war** — redirect martial energies into constructive service: "a conscription of the whole youthful population to form for a certain number of years a part of the army enlisted against Nature". See [[P188 — Find the moral equivalent of war in constructive service|P188]], [[P183 — Make your life significant by fusing ideal with active virtue|P183]].

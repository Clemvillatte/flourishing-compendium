> **Thinker T017** — James J. Gross, American psychologist, associated with Stanford University, developer of the process model of emotion regulation; known for the reappraisal-versus-suppression comparison and the extended process model.

## Personal story / context

Gross was at the Department of Psychology, Stanford University, when he published the 2002 Psychophysiology article "Emotion regulation: Affective, cognitive, and social consequences," an early-career award statement ([Gross 2002](https://www.psychiatry.wisc.edu/courses/Nitschke/seminar/gross_2002_psychophys.pdf)).

## Their why

Gross's program answers a practical question. Emotions sometimes serve us badly, and people try to regulate them. His model maps where in the emotion-generative process a strategy acts, and uses that location to predict its costs and benefits. As the 2002 article puts it, emotion regulation "refers to the processes by which we influence which emotions we have, when we have them, and how we experience and express them" ([Gross 2002](https://www.psychiatry.wisc.edu/courses/Nitschke/seminar/gross_2002_psychophys.pdf)).

## Their position

The model's core move is a location, not a content, distinction. In the 2002 article: "According to this model, emotion may be regulated at five points in the emotion generative process: (1) selection of the situation; (2) modification of the situation; (3) deployment of attention; (4) change of cognitions; and (5) modulation of experiential, behavioral, or physiological responses. The first four of these are antecedent focused, the fifth is response focused" ([Gross 2002, Figure 1 caption](https://www.psychiatry.wisc.edu/courses/Nitschke/seminar/gross_2002_psychophys.pdf)). The extended model's abstract states its core distinction: "this model considers emotion regulation to be one type of valuation, and distinguishes three emotion regulation stages (identification, selection, implementation)" ([Gross 2015](http://www.johnnietfeld.com/uploads/2/2/6/0/22606800/gross_2015.pdf)).

## Core ideas

- **The process model of emotion regulation** — five families by location in the emotion-generative process. See [[Process model of emotion regulation Gross]], [[Situation selection]], [[Attentional deployment theory]].
- **Reappraisal versus suppression** — the canonical comparison of an antecedent-focused and a response-focused strategy. See [[Expressive suppression versus reappraisal]], [[Cognitive reappraisal]], [[Anger regulation research]].
- **The extended process model** — valuation, identification, selection, implementation. See [[Extended process model of emotion regulation]], [[State-matched emotion regulation]].
- **Emotion regulation as a field** — definition, measures, paradigms. See [[Emotion regulation]], [[Mid-level emotion regulation]].
- **Emotion dynamics** — regulation acts on latency, rise time, magnitude, duration, offset. See [[Temporal dynamics of regulation]].

Hans-Georg Gadamer (February 11, 1900, Marburg – March 13, 2002, Heidelberg) was a German philosopher of the continental tradition best known for *Truth and Method* (*Wahrheit und Methode*, 1960), which argues that understanding is shaped by history, language and dialogue rather than by the application of objective method ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)).

## Personal story / context

Born in Marburg, he took his doctorate in 1922 with a thesis on the nature of pleasure in Plato's dialogues under Paul Natorp, and worked with Martin Heidegger, whose radical rethinking of understanding he carried into his own hermeneutics ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). He spent most of his career at Heidelberg, from 1949 until his death, publishing *Truth and Method* at sixty.

## Their why

His own statement of concern, verbatim: "My real concern was and is philosophic: not what we do or what we ought to do, but what happens to us over and above our wanting and doing" (quoted in [Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). The question is how understanding actually happens — before anyone applies a method.

## Their position

Understanding is historically situated. The interpreter brings prejudices (*Vorurteile*) that are not obstacles but the very condition of understanding — the Enlightenment's demand to strip them away is itself a "prejudice against prejudices" ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). Interpreting a text involves a fusion of horizons (*Horizontverschmelzung*): both the text and the interpreter stand within particular historical horizons, and understanding happens when those horizons merge rather than when one is neutralized ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)).

## Core ideas

- **Fusion of horizons** — understanding as the merging of the interpreter's horizon with the text's, not the reconstruction of an objective standpoint ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). See [[Fusion of horizons]], [[E080 — Practise the fusion of horizons|Practise the fusion of horizons]], [[Horizon]].
- **The universality of prejudice** — pre-understandings enable rather than block interpretation; the "prejudice against prejudices" critique ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). See [[E081 — Practise the universality of prejudice|Practise the universality of prejudice]], [[Pre-understanding]].
- **The hermeneutic circle** — parts and whole interpret each other, and the circle is not a defect but the shape of understanding ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). See [[E079 — Practise the hermeneutic circle|Practise the hermeneutic circle]], [[Hermeneutic circle]].
- **Historically-effected consciousness** — the interpreter is formed by the history that also speaks in the text ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). See [[E047 — Practise historicity (Geschichtlichkeit)|Practise historicity (Geschichtlichkeit)]], [[Historical consciousness]].
- **Dialogue as the model of understanding** — questions and answers, not extraction of information; listening for the right in the other side ([Wikipedia, Hans-Georg Gadamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Georg_Gadamer)). See [[B147 — Listen deeply|Listen deeply]], [[D124 — Look for the right in the other side|Look for the right in the other side]], [[Dialogue]].

> **Thinker T018** — Confucius (551–479 BCE), philosopher and teacher born in the small state of Lu on the Shandong peninsula in northeastern China; the Analects is the traditional source for his life and teachings ([IEP, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)).

## Personal story / context

The IEP entry frames the source problem honestly: the sources postdate him and should be used with care. The Analects "claims to record" exchanges between Confucius and his students, and consists of sayings and biographical fragments ([IEP, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). He was "said to have held office for only a short time before withdrawing into scholarly retirement," amid the political fragmentation and warfare of the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods ([IEP, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). What we have is a compiled record, not an authored book.

## Their why

Confucius's own description of his method is quoted in the Analects as "transmits, but does not innovate" (7.1); he said he transmitted the Way (Dao) of the ancient Zhou sages and urged disciples to emulate them and restore the state's moral integrity ([IEP, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). The Zhou ideal was already there; the problem was decline, and the work was restoration.

## Their position

Two ideas carry the practice. The first is ren, humaneness: the humane disposition toward others; the IEP entry renders it "benevolence" or "humaneness," with Peter Boodberg's "co-humanity" ([IEP, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). The second is li, ritual propriety, the instrument for effecting and emulating aesthetic, moral, and social order; the Analects quotes him as saying "Do not look at, do not listen to, do not speak of, do not do whatever is contrary to ritual propriety" (12.1, [IEP, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). Asked to define ren, Confucius says "it is to love all men" (12.22, [Legge translation, ctext.org](https://ctext.org/analects/yan-yuan)). Asked for one word to live by, he chooses shu, reciprocity: "What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others" (15.24, [Legge translation, ctext.org](https://ctext.org/analects/wei-ling-gong)).

## Core ideas

- **Ren (humaneness)** — the cardinal virtue, disposition toward others. See [[Ren]], [[The golden rule]].
- **Li (ritual propriety)** — training ground and social order. See [[Li: ritual propriety]], [[Harmony]].
- **Xiao (filial piety)** — reverence for elders as root practice. See [[Filial piety]], [[Veneration of elders]].
- **Junzi (the exemplary person)** — the cultivated person. See [[The exemplary person]], [[Moral exemplarism]].
- **Shu (reciprocity)** — the one-word rule. See [[Shu]], [[The golden rule]].
- **Learning through practice** — learning as character change, not information. See [[Daily self-examination]], [[Moral cultivation (xiushen)]].

Confucius (551—479 BCE), Chinese thinker known in China as "Master Kong" (Kongzi); the [IEP article](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/) frames the source problem: "Sources for the historical recovery of Confucius' life and thought are limited to texts that postdate his traditional lifetime (551-479 BCE) by a few decades at least and several centuries at most."

## Personal story / context

The IEP article continues: "The historical Confucius, born in the small state of Lu on the Shandong peninsula in northeastern China, was a product of the 'Spring and Autumn Period' (770-481 BCE). We know him mostly from texts that date to the 'Warring States Period' (403-221 BCE). During these eras, China enjoyed no political unity and suffered from the internecine warfare of small states, remnants of the once-great Zhou polity that collapsed after 'barbarian' invasions in 771 BCE." The shi ("retainer" or "knight") class he arose from lived in and out of office as patron states' fortunes ebbed and flowed; "Confucius is said to have held office for only a short time before withdrawing into scholarly retirement" ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)).

## Their why

The Analects is the traditional source: "the sayings and biographical fragments recorded in the text known as the Analects (Chinese: Lunyu)"; it "claims to record" the master-and-disciple exchanges ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). The IEP article renders Analects 7.1: "Above all else, the Analects depicts Confucius as someone who 'transmits, but does not innovate' (7.1). What Confucius claimed to transmit was the Dao (Way) of the sages of Zhou antiquity; in the Analects, he is the erudite guardian of tradition who challenges his disciples to emulate the sages of the past and restore the moral integrity of the state" ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)).

## Their position

The two ideas the article carries forward are ren and li. Ren is "usually translated as 'benevolence' or 'humaneness'"; the article notes the character joins a human being and the number two, and that Peter Boodberg suggested "an evocative translation of ren as 'co-humanity'" ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). Li is ritual propriety, "a kind of self-replicating blueprint for good manners and taste, morality, and social order," and the article quotes 12.1: "Do not look at, do not listen to, do not speak of, do not do whatever is contrary to ritual propriety" ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). Xiao (filial piety) is the paramount example of harmonious social order, with jing (reverence) its key quality; the article quotes 2.7: "If a person shows no reverence, where is the difference?" ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). The junzi is the "profound person" (Tu Wei-ming's translation): "The profound person understands what is moral. The small person understands what is profitable" (4.16) ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)).

Dōgen Kigen (道元希玄, 1200 – 1253) was a Japanese Zen master who founded the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan and made sitting meditation itself — zazen — the whole of the religious life ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). His masterwork, the *Shōbōgenzō* ("Treasury of the True Dharma Eye"), is a collection of essays on practice and realization.

## Personal story / context

Orphaned young, he was ordained as a monk and studied at Kennin-ji Temple in Kyoto under Myōzen, the successor of Eisai; in 1223 he traveled to Song China, where Myōzen died shortly after his arrival at Mount Tiantong, and in 1227 Dōgen received Dharma transmission from Rujing ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). On his return to Japan he promoted zazen through the *Fukanzazengi* and *Bendōwa*, broke with the Tendai establishment, and eventually left Kyoto to found Eihei-ji, the mountain monastery that remains the head temple of Sōtō Zen ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). In 1248 he made the long journey east to give lay ordination to the shogun's regent in Kamakura; he fell ill in the autumn of 1252 and died the following year ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)).

## Their why

The question running through the *Shōbōgenzō*: if enlightenment is already the case, why practice? His answer dissolves the question — practice is not a means to enlightenment but its expression, and the self doing the practicing is itself the thing to be seen through.

## Their position

The famous statement of the *Genjōkōan*, verbatim:

> "To study the Way is to study the Self. To study the Self is to forget the self. To forget the self is to be enlightened by all things of the universe. To be enlightened by all things of the universe is to cast off the body and mind of the self as well as those of others. Even the traces of enlightenment are wiped out, and life with traceless enlightenment goes on forever and ever." (*Genjōkōan*, quoted in [Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen))

## Core ideas

- **Zazen as the whole practice** — sitting meditation promoted as the direct expression of awakened mind; his instructions survive in the *Fukanzazengi* ("Studying Zen ... is zazen") ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). See [[B044 — Just sit (zazen, shikantaza)|Just sit (zazen, shikantaza)]], [[Shikantaza, just sitting]].
- **Practice-enlightenment unity** — practice is not preparation for enlightenment; the two are one movement ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). See [[B045 — Let practice and enlightenment be one|Let practice and enlightenment be one]], [[B179 — The path is the goal|The path is the goal]].
- **Forgetting the self** — the Genjōkōan arc: study the self to forget it, to be enlightened by all things ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). See [[B060 — Practice without seeking|Practice without seeking]], [[Y045 — Relax effort into support|Relax effort into support]].
- **Samu — work as practice** — the monastery's labor as meditation in activity. See [[B171 — Make work itself the practice (samu)|Make work itself the practice (samu)]], [[Means as end]].
- **The Shōbōgenzō** — essays that philosophize the practice from within, the founding literature of Sōtō Zen ([Wikipedia, Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)). See [[Ecological spirituality]], [[The rhythm of small pauses]].

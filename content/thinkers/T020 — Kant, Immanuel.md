Immanuel Kant (April 22, 1724, Königsberg — February 12, 1804) "is the central figure in modern philosophy," whose critical philosophy "set the terms for much of nineteenth and twentieth century philosophy" ([SEP, Kant](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/)).

## Personal story / context

Kant spent his life in Königsberg, the capital of East Prussia, "a major commercial center, an important military port, and a relatively cosmopolitan university town." He taught at the University of Königsberg (the Albertina) from 1755, first as an unsalaried lecturer, later in the chair in logic and metaphysics; "for the next four decades Kant taught philosophy there, until his retirement from teaching in 1796 at the age of seventy-two" ([SEP, Kant](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/)).

## Their why

The fundamental idea of Kant's critical philosophy — "especially in his three Critiques: the Critique of Pure Reason (1781, 1787), the Critique of Practical Reason (1788), and the Critique of the Power of Judgment (1790) — is human autonomy" ([SEP, Kant](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/)). The Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785) is his "main work on the fundamental principle of morality"; Toward Perpetual Peace (1795) is among his "main works in political philosophy" ([SEP, Kant](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/)).

## Their position

The Groundwork's humanity formulation, quoted in the MIT course PDF of the Abbott translation:

> "So act as to treat humanity, whether in thine own person or in that of any other, in every case as an end withal, never as means only." (Kant, Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals, trans. T. K. Abbott, [MIT course asset PDF](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/assets/courseware/v1/d724068ab9d302a3eba40f625520f510/asset-v1:MITx+24.02x+2T2020+type@asset+block@L11_Kant_Groundwork.pdf))

The same passage grounds it: "rational nature exists as an end in itself" ([Groundwork, MIT PDF](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/assets/courseware/v1/d724068ab9d302a3eba40f625520f510/asset-v1:MITx+24.02x+2T2020+type@asset+block@L11_Kant_Groundwork.pdf)). In the political writings, the rule becomes right rather than philanthropy: "The rights of men, as citizens of the world, shall be limited to the conditions of universal hospitality" and "in this sphere hospitality signifies the claim of a stranger entering foreign territory to be treated by its owner without hostility" (Kant, Perpetual Peace, Third Definitive Article, [Gutenberg #50922](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/50922/50922-h/50922-h.htm)).

## Core ideas

- **The categorical imperative** — the universalizable form of moral law. See [[The categorical imperative]], [[Moral obligation]].
- **Persons as ends** — the humanity formulation, 4:429. See [[Persons as ends]], [[Dignity]], [[Respect]].
- **Autonomy as the critical idea** — reason as its own lawgiver. See [[Autonomy]], [[Self-legislation]].
- **Cosmopolitan right and hospitality** — right between strangers, not charity. See [[Cosmopolitanism]], [[Hospitality]], [[Cosmopolitan care]].
- **The sublime and aesthetic judgement** — the third Critique. See [[The sublime]], [[Disinterested aesthetic pleasure]].

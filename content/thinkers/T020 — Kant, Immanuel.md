> **Thinker T020** — Immanuel Kant (1724–1804), German philosopher of Königsberg; author of the Critique of Pure Reason, the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals and Perpetual Peace; the ethics of autonomy and the philosophy of right.

## Personal story / context

Kant spent his whole life in Königsberg and taught there at the university. The compendium's treatment of him runs through three works, each carrying a different idea: the Groundwork for the ethics, Perpetual Peace for the philosophy of right, and the third Critique for aesthetics.

## Their why

The Groundwork's question is what a moral law could be grounded in if it is to bind unconditionally. The formulation, from Section II of the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (4:429):

> So act as to treat humanity, whether in thine own person or in that of any other, in every case as an end withal, never as means only.

(Kant, Groundwork, 4:429, trans. Thomas Kingsmill Abbott, [MIT excerpt PDF](https://openlearninglibrary.mit.edu/assets/courseware/v1/d724068ab9d302a3eba40f625520f510/asset-v1:MITx+24.02x+2T2020+type@asset+block@L11_Kant_Groundwork.pdf))

## Their position

The reasoning runs backward from what a categorical imperative could be grounded in: "If then there is a supreme practical principle or, in respect of the human will, a categorical imperative, it must be one which, being drawn from the conception of that which is necessarily an end for everyone because it is an end in itself, constitutes an objective principle of will." The thing with that absolute worth is stated flatly: "rational nature exists as an end in itself." In the political writings, the rule becomes right, not philanthropy: "The rights of men, as citizens of the world, shall be limited to the conditions of universal hospitality" and "in this sphere hospitality signifies the claim of a stranger entering foreign territory to be treated by its owner without hostility" (Kant, Perpetual Peace, Third Definitive Article, [Gutenberg #50922](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/50922/50922-h/50922-h.htm)).

## Core ideas

- **The categorical imperative** — a rule that binds unconditionally. See [[The categorical imperative]], [[Moral obligation]].
- **Persons as ends** — the humanity formulation. See [[Persons as ends]], [[Dignity]], [[Respect]].
- **Autonomy and self-legislation** — the will as its own lawgiver. See [[Autonomy]], [[Self-legislation]].
- **Cosmopolitan right and hospitality** — right, not charity, between strangers. See [[Cosmopolitanism]], [[Hospitality]], [[Cosmopolitan care]].
- **The sublime and aesthetic judgement** — disinterested pleasure, purposiveness without purpose. See [[The sublime]], [[Disinterested aesthetic pleasure]].

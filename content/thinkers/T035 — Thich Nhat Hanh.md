Thich Nhat Hanh (born Nguyen Xuan Bao, October 11, 1926, in Hue, central Vietnam; died January 22, 2022) was a Vietnamese Zen master, monk, poet and peace activist who founded the Plum Village tradition and coined the term "engaged Buddhism" in his book *Vietnam: Lotus in a Sea of Fire* ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). He entered Tu Hieu Temple as a novice at sixteen, was ordained a monk in 1951, and spent thirty-nine years in exile after refusing to take sides in his country's war.

## Personal story / context

The war made him. In 1964 he co-founded the School of Youth for Social Service, a neutral corps of Buddhist peaceworkers that grew to some 10,000 volunteers building schools, clinics and rebuilt villages in the countryside; several of its students were kidnapped and murdered for refusing to support either armed side ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). In 1966 he left for the United States to plead for peace, was barred from returning, and received asylum in France. Martin Luther King Jr. nominated him for the 1967 Nobel Peace Prize, which was not awarded that year. In 1982 he founded Plum Village Monastery near Bordeaux, and after his stroke he returned to his root temple in Vietnam, where he died ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). His most widely read book, *The Miracle of Mindfulness*, was written in wartime for those social workers: a practice manual for people "living in a situation where the danger of dying was there every day" ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)).

## Their why

How do you keep a peaceful heart while bombs fall on the villages you are rebuilding? His answer was to make mindfulness ordinary and portable: a practice for washing dishes, drinking tea and walking, strong enough to hold in the middle of war. The Miracle of Mindfulness was his letter to a School of Youth for Social Service staff member in 1974, translated into a book ([Thich Nhat Hanh, The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://web.archive.org/web/20260308111657/https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality%2C%20Meditation%2C%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)).

## Their position

Meditation is not separate from daily life, and the present moment is the only place practice happens. His instructions are famously concrete:

> "There are two ways to wash the dishes. The first is to wash the dishes in order to have clean dishes and the second is to wash the dishes in order to wash the dishes." ([Thich Nhat Hanh, The Miracle of Mindfulness, archived PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20260308111657/https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality%2C%20Meditation%2C%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf))

On hurry: "If while washing dishes, we think only of the cup of tea that awaits us... then we are not 'washing the dishes to wash the dishes.' What's more, we are not alive during the time we are washing the dishes" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://web.archive.org/web/20260308111657/https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality%2C%20Meditation%2C%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)). He introduces the term for the reader in passing: "from now on I'll use the term 'mindfulness' to refer to keeping one's consciousness alive to the present reality," and insists that "one must practice right now in one's daily life, not only during meditation sessions" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://web.archive.org/web/20260308111657/https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality%2C%20Meditation%2C%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)).

## Core ideas

- **Interbeing** — the word he built by combining the prefix "inter-" with the verb "to be," to denote the interconnection of all phenomena; the Order of Interbeing (1964-1966) grounded a monastic and lay community in it ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). See [[Interbeing]], [[B015 — Nothing exists alone (interbeing)|Nothing exists alone (interbeing)]], [[Dependent origination]], [[Indra's net]].
- **Engaged Buddhism** — his term from *Vietnam: Lotus in a Sea of Fire*: meditation not as escape from suffering but as the base for action in the middle of it ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). See [[Engaged Buddhism]], [[B156 — Engage the world's suffering (engaged Buddhism)|Engage the world's suffering (engaged Buddhism)]].
- **Mindfulness in daily life** — "washing the dishes to wash the dishes," the bell of mindfulness, eating and walking as practice ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://web.archive.org/web/20260308111657/https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality%2C%20Meditation%2C%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)). See [[B168 — Do one thing at a time|Do one thing at a time]], [[B169 — Eat mindfully|Eat mindfully]], [[B188 — Use ordinary cues as bells|Use ordinary cues as bells]].
- **Walking meditation** — walking with full attention as a core practice of his tradition ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). See [[Walking meditation]], [[B187 — Walk in peace|Walk in peace]], [[BI060 — Learn mindful movement — walking meditation, yoga, tai chi|Learn mindful movement: walking meditation, yoga, tai chi]].
- **Deep listening and loving speech** — he promoted deep listening as a nonviolent solution to conflict ([Wikipedia, Thich Nhat Hanh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)). See [[Deep listening]], [[B147 — Listen deeply|Listen deeply]], [[B148 — Speak lovingly|Speak lovingly]].
- **Mind transformation through seeds** — the mind holds mental formations as seeds: "Any seed that manifests in your mind consciousness returns to your store consciousness stronger than ever" ([Thich Nhat Hanh, Lion's Roar](https://www.lionsroar.com/watering-the-seeds-of-happiness/)); I compare the model with conditioning. See [[B185 — Water the good seeds|Water the good seeds]], [[B184 — Recognize habit energy|Recognize habit energy]].

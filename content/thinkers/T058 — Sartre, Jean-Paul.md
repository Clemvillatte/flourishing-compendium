Jean-Paul Sartre (21 June 1905 – 15 April 1980, Paris) studied at the École Normale Supérieure, served as a meteorologist drafted into the French Army in 1939, was captured in 1940 and released in 1941, helped found the underground group Socialisme et Liberté, declined the 1964 Nobel Prize in Literature, and is remembered as the writer who said a person is condemned to be free ([Wikipedia, Jean-Paul Sartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Sartre)). His most-cited work, *Being and Nothingness*, was published in 1943 as *L'être et le néant*; his popular lecture *Existentialism is a Humanism* came in 1945 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Jean-Paul Sartre"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)). He is the first existentialist whose argument the corpus already carries under principle E033 on bad faith.

## Their why

Sartre takes the question a person asks themselves about what they are and gives it a hard edge. He refuses to soften it by appealing to a fixed human nature, a creator's design, or a past that determines the present. On his account a person is not a thing that was made and finished; a person is the sum of what they make themselves into, and they remain answerable for that making ([Wikipedia, Jean-Paul Sartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Sartre); [Existentialism is a Humanism, 1946](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm)). The why is the demand this places on a reader: if nothing outside you settles your essence, then the settling falls back on you.

## Their position

The classic sentences come from the 1946 lecture, trans. Philip Mairet:

> "What do we mean by saying that existence precedes essence? We mean that man first of all exists, encounters himself, surges up in the world – and defines himself afterwards." ([Existentialism is a Humanism, 1946, trans. Mairet](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm))

And on the weight of that freedom:

> "That is what I mean when I say that man is condemned to be free. Condemned, because he did not create himself, yet is nevertheless at liberty, and from the moment that he is thrown into this world he is responsible for everything he does." ([same opened lecture](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm))

The Stanford Encyclopedia carries the harder ontological version that grounds the ethics:

> "But it is the exclusive affirmation of one or the others of these (or a motivated and selective oscillation between them) which constitutes bad faith." ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Jean-Paul Sartre"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/), quoting *Being and Nothingness*)

And on the shape of the freedom itself:

> "We are "condemned" to freedom of this ontological sort, with resulting anguish and responsibility for our individual situation, as well as for more collective situations of racism, oppression, and colonialism." ([same encyclopedia entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/))

The practice that follows is the one the wiki already teaches under E033: notice which half of yourself you are currently denying, and hold both the constraints and the choices.

## Core ideas

- **Existence precedes essence** — the human case runs the other way from manufactured objects; there is no blueprint before the living. See [[Existence precedes essence]], [[E010 — Practise the constitution of meaning|Practise the constitution of meaning]].
- **Radical freedom** — freedom as the condition of being a person, not as a power to do anything; realised only in action. See [[Radical freedom]], [[E031 — Practise radical freedom|Practise radical freedom]], [[Situated freedom]].
- **Bad faith (mauvaise foi)** — a lie to oneself that treats oneself as a fixed thing or as pure freedom, and is a permanent threat rather than an occasional accident. See [[Bad faith]], [[E033 — Practise avoiding bad faith (mauvaise foi)|Practise avoiding bad faith (mauvaise foi)]], [[Self-deception]].
- **Facticality and transcendence** — every situation holds both; bad faith drops one. See [[Facticity]], [[E032 — Practise the distinction between facticity and transcendence|Practise the distinction between facticity and transcendence]].
- **The look (le regard)** — the other's gaze makes me an object for them, and this is not a mood but an ontological structure. See [[The look (le regard)]], [[E035 — Practise the gaze of the Other (le regard)|Practise the gaze of the Other (le regard)]], [[Shame]].
- **Being-for-itself / being-in-itself** — consciousness negates and transcends; objects are the "pure plenitude" that cannot choose. See [[Being and beings]], [[E069 — Practise the body-for-others|Practise the body-for-others]], [[Being-for-others]].
- **Anguish (angoisse)** — the vertigo of knowing one's own freedom and responsibility; not fear of a thing but of oneself. See [[Anguish (angoisse)]], [[E041 — Practise anguish (angoisse)|Practise anguish (angoisse)]], [[E044 — Practise anxiety (Angst)|Practise anxiety (Angst)]].
- **The project (projet)** — a person is a project toward the future; you are what you aim at. See [[Project (projet)]], [[E038 — Practise project|Practise project]], [[Self-constitution through action]].

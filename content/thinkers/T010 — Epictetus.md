> **Thinker T010** — Epictetus (c. 50–135 CE), Greek Stoic philosopher; born enslaved in Hierapolis, taught at Rome, exiled to Nicopolis; he wrote nothing, and his teaching survives in Arrian's Discourses and Encheiridion.

## Personal story / context

Born sometime in the 50s CE in Hierapolis, a Greek city of Asia Minor ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)). He spent part of his life enslaved to Epaphroditus, an important administrator in Nero's court ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)). While in Rome he studied under Musonius Rufus, a Roman senator and Stoic philosopher ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)). He eventually received his freedom and began to lecture on his own account ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)). When Domitian's edict of 89 CE banned philosophers from the Italian peninsula he was forced to leave, and he established his own school at Nicopolis, on the Adriatic coast of northwest Greece, teaching there until his death around 135 CE ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)). He wrote nothing: the Discourses, of which four of eight or more books survive, were recorded by Arrian of Nicomedia to convey the personal impact of the teaching, and the Encheiridion is Arrian's brief abridgment of it ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)).

## Their why

A slave taught freedom, and a lame man taught steadiness: the ancient story says his master twisted his leg until it broke, and Epictetus calmly warned him it would (the story survives via Origen; the corpus retells it at [[ST188 — Epictetus and the Leg|188. Epictetus and the Leg]]). His whole philosophy answers the question his life posed: what remains inviolably yours when everything else can be taken? "You may fetter my leg, but my will not even Zeus himself can overpower" ([Discourses I.1](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661)).

## Their position

The world splits into what is yours and what is not. "Of things some are in our power, and others are not. In our power are opinion, movement towards a thing, desire, aversion... and in a word, whatever are our acts. Not in our power are the body, property, reputation, offices" ([Encheiridion I](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661)). Disturbance comes not from events but from judgment: "Men are disturbed not by the things which happen, but by the opinions about the things" ([Encheiridion V](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661)). The cure is to want what happens: "Seek not that the things which happen should happen as you wish; but wish the things which happen to be as they are, and you will have a tranquil flow of life" ([Encheiridion VIII](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661)). Even death and chains are to be met as material: "I must die. Must I then die lamenting? I must be put in chains. Must I then also lament?" ([Discourses I.1](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661)). And education itself is this sorting: "to distinguish that of things some are in our power, but others are not" ([Discourses I.17](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661)).

## Core ideas

- **The dichotomy of control** — sort everything into what is up to you and what is not; freedom lives on the near side of that line. See [[S001 — Distinguish what is up to you from what is not|S001]], [[The dichotomy of control]], [[S053 — Distinguish between what you control and what you influence|S053]].
- **Prohairesis, the will** — the capacity of choice that no fetter reaches; "my will not even Zeus himself can overpower". See [[S056 — Recognise that you can choose your response|S056]], [[Prohairesis]], [[The inner citadel]].
- **Judgment adds the harm** — events are neutral; the opinion about the event is the terrible thing. See [[S003 — Recognise that you are disturbed not by things but by your judgements about them|S003]], [[S051 — Recognise that your judgements are the source of your distress|S051]], [[Cognitive mediation of distress]], [[Judgment adds the harm]].
- **Impressions and withheld assent** — test every appearance before granting it; "Impression, wait for me a little". See [[S045 — Examine every impression|S045]], [[S047 — See impressions as what they are — appearances, not realities|S047]], [[Epoché]], [[The discipline of assent]].
- **The three disciplines** — desire and aversion, impulse to act, and assent: the three parts of training. See [[S011 — Recognise the three topoi — the three disciplines|S011]], [[The three disciplines]], [[S013 — Discipline your desires|S013]], [[S129 — Practise the discipline of attention|S129]].
- **Externals as indifferents** — body, property, reputation, offices are not good or bad; virtue alone is good. See [[S017 — Refuse to see externals as good or bad|S017]], [[S002 — Make virtue the only genuine good|S002]], [[Adiaphora, the indifferents]], [[Preferred indifferents]].
- **Wanting what happens** — align desire with the world as it is; the reserve clause softens every undertaking. See [[S014 — Want what actually happens|S014]], [[Serene acceptance of necessity]], [[The reserve clause]].
- **Roles and duties** — you are a citizen, parent, friend; play your part well whatever the script. See [[S029 — Fulfil your roles well|S029]], [[Contentment in station]].
- **Adversity as training** — hardship is practice material, not harm. See [[S092 — Treat adversity as training|S092]], [[S121 — Practise voluntary discomfort|S121]], [[Negative visualisation]].
- **Philosophy as practice** — doctrines are for living; the school is a doctor's surgery. See [[S009 — Make philosophy a way of life, not a set of doctrines|S009]], [[S131 — Make Stoicism a lifelong practice, not a phase|S131]].

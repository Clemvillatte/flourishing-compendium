> **Thinker T011** — Socrates (469–399 BCE), Athenian philosopher who wrote nothing, questioned everyone in public, and was executed by his city on charges of impiety and corrupting the young; the figure behind Plato's dialogues.

## Personal story / context

Socrates wrote nothing himself; everything we have is second-hand and disputed, which scholars call the Socratic problem ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/)). The earliest surviving portrait is a comedy, Aristophanes' Clouds of 423 BCE, whose Socrates runs a think-shop and mocks the gods; the soldier-historian Xenophon's Socrates is a practical adviser; Plato, who was about twenty-five at the trial, gives the dialogues that made him ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/)). In May or June of 399 BCE, Meletus, joined by Anytus and Lycon, prosecuted him for irreverence: not believing in the city's gods, introducing new divinities (his daimonion), and corrupting the young. The jury found him guilty; his counter-proposal of a thirty-minae fine was rejected, and he drank hemlock ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/)).

## Their why

What set him questioning, by Plato's account, was an oracle: Chaerephon asked Delphi whether anyone was wiser than Socrates, and the answer was no one, a claim he found absurd and set out to test by questioning everyone reputed to be wise ([[ST152 — Socrates and the Oracle|152. Socrates and the Oracle]]). The finding, in his own words in the Jowett translation: "He, O men, is the wisest, who, like Socrates, knows that his wisdom is in truth worth nothing" ([Apology](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html)).

## Their position

His method was the question, not the lecture: he insisted he was not a teacher, took no fees, and likened his work to midwifery, helping others recognize on their own what is true and good ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/)). The cross-examination that exposes inconsistencies now carries his name, the elenchus. He described his effect on the city as a stinging:

> "I am that gadfly which God has given the state and all day long and in all places am always fastening upon you, arousing and persuading and reproaching you." (Plato, Apology, trans. Jowett, [MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html))

The sentence that cost him his life, and the one he refused to retract:

> "The greatest good of man is daily to converse about virtue, and all that concerning which you hear me examining myself and others, and that the life which is unexamined is not worth living." (Plato, Apology, trans. Jowett, [MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html))

In prison, offered escape, he argued that one should follow the argument that survives reflection, and that "neither to do wrong or to return a wrong is ever right, not even to injure in return for an injury received" (Plato, Crito 49d, as quoted in [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/)).

## Core ideas

- **Socratic ignorance** — the wisdom of knowing your wisdom is worth nothing. See [[Socratic ignorance]], [[Socratic wisdom]], [[Docta ignorantia]], [[Intellectual humility]], [[ST152 — Socrates and the Oracle|152. Socrates and the Oracle]].
- **The elenchus** — cross-examination that exposes false beliefs. See [[Socratic elenchus]], [[Socratic inquiry]], [[Socratic testing]], [[Aporia]], [[Distinguishing appearance from reality]].
- **The examined life** — daily converse about virtue; a life unexamined is not worth living. See [[The examined life]], [[The unexamined life]], [[Self-examination]], [[Daily self-examination]], [[Questioning inherited beliefs]].
- **The midwife** — he delivers others' ideas, not his own. See [[The Socratic midwife]], [[Anamnesis]], [[ST155 — Meno's Paradox|155. Meno's Paradox]].
- **The daimonion** — the inner sign that held him back. See [[The daimon]], [[The master's hand]].
- **Virtue as knowledge** — the unity of the virtues; no one errs willingly. See [[The unity of the virtues]], [[Akrasia]], [[Akrasia (weakness of will)]], [[The unity of the soul]].
- **Care of the soul** — the soul's condition over reputation and wealth. See [[The theoretical life]], [[Moral exemplarism]], [[Wisdom]], [[The examined economics]].
- **Never return wrong for wrong** — justice even under sentence of death. See [[Justice]], [[ST141 — Socrates Refuses to Escape the Question|141. Socrates Refuses to Escape the Question]], [[ST203 — Socrates as the Stoic Before the Stoics|203. Socrates as the Stoic Before the Stoics]].

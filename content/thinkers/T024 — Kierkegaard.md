> **Thinker T024** — Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855), Danish philosopher and theologian, author of Either/Or, Fear and Trembling, The Sickness unto Death and other pseudonymous works; the first existential thinker in the compendium's lineage.

## Personal story / context

Kierkegaard wrote under pseudonyms — Anti-Climacus for The Sickness unto Death (1849), among others — and the pseudonym is part of the method: the reader is made to hear the position from inside it, not as a doctrine. The compendium's Kierkegaard is the Kierkegaard of the self: despair, anxiety, choice, repetition.

## Their why

The Sickness unto Death opens by defining what a self is before it names how the self can go wrong. A person is "a synthesis of the infinite and the finite, of the temporal and the eternal, of freedom and necessity, in short, a synthesis," and "considered in this way a human being is still not a self" ([online text](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)). Selfhood is the task of holding that synthesis together, and despair is its failure.

## Their position

The misrelation is stated technically:

> "Despair is the misrelation in the relation of a synthesis that relates itself to itself. But the synthesis is not the misrelation; it is merely the possibility, or in the synthesis lies the possibility of the misrelation." (Kierkegaard, [The Sickness Unto Death](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html))

The concept carries a practice: despair is a category of the self rather than a mood report, so it is addressed at the level of what one takes oneself to be, not how one feels. The Anxiety and Freedom pages carry the adjacent ideas; Repetition carries the forward-looking one.

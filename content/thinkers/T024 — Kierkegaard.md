Søren Kierkegaard (5 May 1813 – 11 November 1855) was a Danish philosopher, Christian theologian, poet, social critic and religious author, widely considered the first existentialist philosopher; much of his work deals with how one lives as a "single individual" ([Wikipedia, Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard)). He published The Sickness unto Death (Sygdommen til Døden) in 1849 under the pseudonym Anti-Climacus ([Storm commentary, work listing](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)).

## Personal story / context

Kierkegaard lived almost his whole life in Copenhagen. He wrote critical texts on organized religion, Christianity, morality, ethics, psychology and love, with a fondness for metaphor, irony and parables, and published much of the work under pseudonyms; his broken engagement to Regine Olsen runs through the authorship ([Wikipedia, Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard)). The Sickness unto Death comes late in that arc, a Christian-psychological work signed Anti-Climacus ([Storm commentary](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)).

## Their why

The book defines what a self is before it names how the self can go wrong. From the excerpted opening: "A human being is a synthesis of the infinite and the finite, of the temporal and the eternal, of freedom and necessity, in short, a synthesis. A synthesis is a relation between two. Considered in this way a human being is still not a self...." ([Storm commentary excerpt](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)). The same opening makes the capacity itself an advantage: "to be able to despair is an infinite advantage" ([Storm commentary excerpt](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)).

## Their position

The misrelation is stated technically:

> "Despair is the misrelation in the relation of a synthesis that relates itself to itself. But the synthesis is not the misrelation; it is merely the possibility, or in the synthesis lies the possibility of the misrelation." ([Storm commentary excerpt](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html))

The concept carries a practice: despair is a category of the self, not a mood report, so it is addressed at the level of what one takes oneself to be, not how one feels. The cure is a formula: "in relating itself to itself and in willing to be oneself, the self rests transparently in the power that established it" — the state of the self when despair is "completely rooted out" ([Storm commentary excerpt](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)).

## Core ideas

- **Despair as misrelation** — the self's failure to be itself; "Despair is a Sickness of the Spirit" ([Storm commentary excerpt](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)), and the commentary glosses that all despair is "ultimately traced to the despair not to will to be oneself"; its cure is the formula of the self resting "transparently in the power that established it" ([Storm commentary excerpt](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)). See [[Despair]].
- **Anxiety and the dizziness of freedom** — freedom's own vertigo; the book is a "companion piece to the Concept of Anxiety" ([Storm commentary](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)). See [[Angst]], [[The dizziness of freedom]], [[Escape from freedom]].
- **The leap of faith** — the movement beyond evidence. See [[The leap of faith]], [[The commitment of the knight of faith]].
- **The aesthetic, the ethical, the religious** — the stages; in the commentary's phrasing, "[a]s anxiety is related to the ethical, despair is related to the religious" ([Storm commentary](https://sorenkierkegaard.org/sickness-unto-death.html)). See [[The aesthetic]], [[The ethical]].
- **Repetition** — life lived forwards, understood backwards. See [[Repetition]], [[Creative repetition]], [[Life lived forwards]].

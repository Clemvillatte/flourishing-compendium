Adi Shankara (c. 700 CE, Kaladi in present-day Kerala) was an Indian Vedic scholar and teacher (*acharya*) of Advaita Vedanta, who wrote influential commentaries on the Brahma sutras and other texts and is often revered as the most important Indian philosopher ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)).

## Personal story / context

Firm biographical facts are scarce and the hagiographies are legendary; scholarly datings of his life range from c. 700 CE to 788–820 CE ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)). He was born at Kaladi, became a monk, and traveled and debated across the subcontinent, founding monasteries (*mathas*) — the tradition credits him with reviving Sanatan Dharma through the philosophy of Advaita Vedanta and promoting cultural unity ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)).

## Their why

The doctrine he systematized answers the Upanishadic question of what is ultimately real. His commentaries argued it against rival Vedantic readings and Buddhist no-self positions: plurality is the work of ignorance, and liberation is the direct knowledge that dissolves it.

## Their position

The Advaita doctrine, in the classical formulation quoted in the article:

> "Brahman (the Absolute) is alone real; this world is unreal; and the Jiva or individual soul is non-different from Brahman." (Sivananda's formulation of the Advaita doctrine, quoted in [Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara))

The method is discriminative: among the two notions "I am the Existent-Brahman" and "I act," both have *Atman* as their witness, and it is more reasonable to give up the one that arises from fallacious means of knowledge ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)).

## Core ideas

- **Advaita — non-dualism** — one reality without a second; the world's plurality is appearance ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)). See [[Brahman]], [[D008 — See the oneness of things|See the oneness of things]], [[Non-dual awareness]].
- **Atman is Brahman** — the self and the absolute are one; "That thou art" read relationally ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)). See [[Atman]], [[That thou art, read relationally]].
- **Neti neti** — the negative way: the real is found by subtracting what it is not ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)). See [[Neti neti]], [[Y092 — Read the great statements as prompts, not doctrines|Read the great statements as prompts, not doctrines]].
- **The witness** — the seer distinct from the seen, trained daily ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)). See [[The witness]], [[Y089 — Separate the seer from the seen daily|Separate the seer from the seen daily]], [[Y013 — Work with the field and the witness|Work with the field and the witness]].
- **The commentaries** — the *bhashyas* on the Brahma sutras, the principal Upanishads and the Bhagavad Gita, the foundational literature of Advaita ([Wikipedia, Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara)). See [[The same in all conditions]], [[Y118 — Bear the opposites|Bear the opposites]].

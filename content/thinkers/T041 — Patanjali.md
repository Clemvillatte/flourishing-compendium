Patanjali is the name attached to the *Yoga Sutras*, 196 aphorisms that systematized yoga practice, probably compiled in the early centuries of the Common Era ([Wikipedia, Patanjali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patanjali)). In Charles Johnston's translation the heart of the system reads: "Union, spiritual consciousness, is gained through control of the versatile psychic nature," after which "Then the Seer comes to consciousness in his proper nature" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)).

## Personal story / context

Almost nothing is certain about the man. The Wikipedia record calls him "the name of one or more author(s), mystic(s) and philosopher(s) in ancient India," a notable scholar of the Samkhya school, with scholars narrowing his dates to between the 2nd and 4th centuries CE, though estimates stretch from the 2nd century BCE to the 5th CE ([Wikipedia, Patanjali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patanjali)). Whether the yoga author, the grammarian and the medical writer were one Patanjali or several is unsettled: "The view that these were likely different authors is now generally accepted by Western scholars," while traditional circles keep the single-sage picture as "an oft-repeated article of faith" ([Wikipedia, Patanjali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patanjali)). His sutras carry the classic Indian form: compressed lines meant to be unpacked by a teacher, with the commentary ascribed to Vyasa possibly by another hand ([Wikipedia, Patanjali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patanjali)). Modern yoga schools, including Iyengar and Ashtanga Vinyasa, honor him with invocations and shrines ([Wikipedia, Patanjali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patanjali)).

## Their why

The work exists because minds suffer their own motion. Patanjali's diagnosis is that suffering comes not from the world but from the mind's restless modifications; his cure is a graded training of attention, ethics and dispassion that ends in the seer resting in itself. The famous preventive rule gives the whole spirit: "This pain is to be warded off, before it has come" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)).

## Their position

The method has two levers, stated plainly: "The control of these psychic activities comes through the right use of the will, and through ceasing from self-indulgence" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)). The curriculum is the eight limbs: "the Commandments, the Rules, right Poise, right Control of the life-force, Withdrawal, Attention, Meditation, Contemplation" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)). The restraints are unconditional: "The Commandments, not limited to any race, place, time or occasion, universal, are the great obligation" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)). The obstacles are named, not moralized, and the remedy is practice: "Steady application to a principle is the way to put a stop to these" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)). And the emotional toning is social: "By sympathy with the happy, compassion for the sorrowful, delight in the holy, disregard of the unholy, the psychic nature moves to gracious peace" ([Yoga Sutras, Johnston trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2526/2526-h/2526-h.htm)).

## Core ideas

- **The stilling of the mind's motions** — yoga defined as the restraint of psychic activity, so the seer can stand in its own nature. See [[Chitta-vritti]], [[The seer at rest in its own nature]].
- **The eight limbs** — ethics, self-discipline, posture, breath, withdrawal, attention, meditation, absorption as one curriculum. See [[Yoga: union of body, breath, and mind]], [[Y072 — Walk the eight limbs as a curriculum|Walk the eight limbs as a curriculum]].
- **Yamas and niyamas** — unconditional restraints (non-harm, truth, non-stealing, continence, non-coveting) and observances. See [[Ahimsa]], [[Santosha]], [[Ethics as foundation]].
- **Practice and dispassion** — the twin levers: steady effort and release of craving. See [[Y006 — Understand dispassion as release, not numbness|Understand dispassion as release, not numbness]].
- **Samadhi** — attention deepening into contemplation, with and without seed. See [[Samadhi]], [[Pratyahara]].

> **Principle 88** — Run "not this, not this" as a procedure

The Upanishadic method for approaching what cannot be objectified: negate each thing it is not, exhaustively and in order, without asserting what remains. The procedure is a discipline of elimination applied to your own identifications: I am not this role, not this emotion, not this body, not this history. The instruction ends where assertion would begin. Practically, run the negation as a weekly exercise, and stop at the silence rather than the conclusion.

(Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6, neti neti; 4.2.3-4.4.23 context.)

**Plain words** The Upanishadic method for what can't be objectified: "not this, not this" — negate each thing it is not, exhaustively, in order, without asserting what remains. Run it on your own identifications: I am not this role, not this emotion, not this history. See what's left standing.

*For example:* Someone strips the identity piece by piece — not the job, not the divorce, not the diagnosis, not the opinions — and finds the inquiry itself steadier than any of the things it removed. The procedure works as a procedure.

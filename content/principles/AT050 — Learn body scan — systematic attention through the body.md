> **Principle 50** — Learn body scan: systematic attention through the body

The body scan is a systematic practice of moving attention through different parts of the body, noticing sensations without judgment. Move attention from one region to another, noticing sensations and returning when attention wanders.

### A related Buddhist practice

MN 10 describes a contemplation of the body: the monk "reflects on this very body from the soles of the feet on up, from the crown of the head on down, surrounded by skin" ([MN 10, Thanissaro trans.](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)). The surrounding passage reviews the body's constituents, part by part, in the simile of a sack of grain poured out and examined. For the modern exercise described here, the territory is the same: the living body, toured with attention region by region.

### What the instructions ask

The refrain of that sutta asks for focus that is "ardent, alert, & mindful" while "putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world" (MN 10). Every region is a new object, every shift a small act of redirecting.

**Plain words** Lie down and walk your attention from your feet to your head, one region at a time. In each place, notice what is actually there: warmth, tension, nothing much. You are not there to fix anything; you are there to find out what your body is carrying today.

*For example:* Lying down for twenty minutes, discovering the jaw you've been clenching since 9 a.m. Nobody told you it was tense. The scan found it because it finally looked.

## Psychology parallels

- **Body scan improves interoception and attention**, systematic scanning improves body awareness and attentional control (Khalsa et al., 2018; Tang et al., 2015)
- **Body scan reduces stress and improves emotion regulation**, body-focused attention reduces reactivity and improves regulation (Creswell et al., 2016; Tang et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Practise body scan: 15-30 minutes, systematically moving attention from head to toe (or toe to head). Notice sensations (warmth, tension, tingling, numbness) without judging or changing. Return to body when mind wanders.

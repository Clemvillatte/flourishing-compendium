> **Principle 17** — Find the still point that is not a withdrawal

The Kyoto School is insistent, against a century of misreading, that nothingness is not detachment from life but a deeper engagement in it: the person on this ground works, loves, and grieves without the added weight of self-reference. The test of the ground is ordinary functioning, not altered states. Practically, measure any practice of letting go by whether your engagements become more available afterward; if they become less available, you have found avoidance, not emptiness.

(Nishida's action-intuition; Nishitani's return to the ordinary.)

**Plain words** The Kyoto thinkers insist that nothingness isn't an exit from life. It's deeper engagement. The person on this ground works, loves and grieves without the extra weight of self-reference. The test isn't altered states but ordinary functioning. Judge any letting-go by whether your engagements get easier to be in.

*For example:* After a few weeks of practice, you're more available to your kids at dinner, not less. If you were drifting away from them, the practice was wrong, whatever it felt like.

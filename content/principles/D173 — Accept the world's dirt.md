> **Principle 173** — Accept the world's dirt

Daodejing 78 moves from water's ability to overcome the hard to a saying about rule: the person who receives the state's reproach and bears people's woes is fit to lead. Legge renders the saying through the state's altars and the title of king ([Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm)).

> He who accepts his state's reproach,
> Is hailed therefore its altars' lord;
> To him who bears men's direful woes
> They all the name of King accord.

(Daodejing 78, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm).)

For this principle, receiving criticism means being willing to examine your responsibility when something has gone wrong. It does not require claiming fault for everything, accepting abuse or hiding someone else's misconduct. Those are limits on the compendium's modern application, not promises about what leadership will earn.

**Plain words** Before defending yourself, hear what the complaint concerns. What part were you responsible for, and what can you now do about it?

*For example:* Imagine organising an event whose invitations gave the wrong time. You acknowledge that you approved the notice and send a correction. You do not blame the volunteer who distributed the version you supplied.

> **Principle 43** — Heal the instrument before trusting it

The Emendation answers a regress worry with a workshop image: finding the method of truth might seem to need a prior method to find it, endlessly. Spinoza replies that the case is like material tools: "in order to work iron, a hammer is needed, and the hammer cannot be forthcoming unless it has been made; but, in order to make it, there was need of another hammer and other tools, and so on to infinity" ([TIE §30, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding)). The regress is not vicious: the first tools were made "laboriously and imperfectly" with what nature supplied, and each finished tool made the next better ([TIE §30-31, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding)).

> "So, in like manner, the intellect, by its native strength, makes for itself intellectual instruments, whereby it acquires strength for performing other intellectual operations, and from these operations again fresh instruments, or the power of pushing its investigations further, and thus gradually proceeds till it reaches the summit of wisdom." (Spinoza, Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect §31, [Elwes translation, Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding))

**Plain words**
You can begin learning before you have the best way to learn: use an ability you already have to develop another, then let that new ability help with the next step ([Elwes, §§30-31](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding)).

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: a learner uses a simple counting method to solve a small problem, then uses that result to develop a quicker method.

(Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect §§30-31, Elwes translation.)

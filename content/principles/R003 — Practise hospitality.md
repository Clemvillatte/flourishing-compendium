> **Principle 265** — Practise hospitality

In Tutu's gloss of ubuntu, hospitality is one of the marks of the person, not an optional extra:

> "Then you are generous, you are hospitable, you are friendly and caring and compassionate. You share what you have." (Tutu, [No Future Without Forgiveness (1999), the ubuntu passage](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/1520177-no-future-without-forgiveness))

The same passage of Tutu continues: a person with ubuntu is open and available to others, affirming of others, and does not feel threatened that others are able and good ([Tutu 1999](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/1520177-no-future-without-forgiveness)).

In Tutu's description the marks of a person with ubuntu include being open and available to others.

([[Ubuntu|Ubuntu philosophy]]; Mbiti, African Religions and Philosophy; Tutu, No Future Without Forgiveness.)

**Plain words**
In Tutu's gloss, being generous, hospitable and sharing what you have are marks of a person with ubuntu, listed alongside friendly, caring and compassionate. These read as standing characteristics rather than passing moods. The practical question is whether the welcome is a standing arrangement or something each stranger has to argue you into.

*For example:*
The household that always sets one extra place at Sunday dinner; over the years that seat hosts a stranded traveller, a new neighbour and a friend of a friend who would otherwise eat alone.

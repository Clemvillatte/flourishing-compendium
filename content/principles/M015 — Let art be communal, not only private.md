> **Principle 15** — Let art be communal, not only private

The flower-and-song tradition was performed: sung in groups, at gatherings, with the community as its context. The solitary consumption of art (headphones, feeds) is a modern possibility, not the tradition's form, and something is lost when the song has no shared room. Practically, keep one artistic practice in your life that happens with other people (singing, playing, attending, dancing), not only alone.

> "They were singers, they composed songs and sang them among the people." — the Toltecs as the ancients remembered them, in León-Portilla's translation ([Flowers and Fire](https://www.islandsofhistory.org/volume-3/flower-and-song-pre-columbian-literatures-of-mesoamerica/))

(The performance context of the Cantares; the communal tradition of the songs.)

**Plain words** The distinction here is not between good art and bad art; it is where art lives. In the flower-and-song world, a song existed by being sung together — the community was not the audience, it was the instrument. Headphones are a modern marvel and a modern amputation: private art is possible now, but it cannot replace the communal channel. So keep one artistic act in your life that requires other people to work.

*For example:* The person whose entire musical life is earbuds — joins the terrible, joyful community choir, and rediscovers that singing with others is a different organ than listening alone.

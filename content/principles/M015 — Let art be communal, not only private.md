> **Principle 15** — Let art be communal, not only private

The flower-and-song tradition was performed: sung in groups, at gatherings, with the community as its context. The Toltecs, the songs' record of the ancients, "were singers, they composed songs and sang them among the people". A practical reading for now: keep one artistic practice in your life that happens with other people (singing, playing, attending, dancing), not only alone.

> "THEY WERE SINGERS, THEY COMPOSED SONGS AND SANG THEM AMONG THE PEOPLE." — the Toltecs as the songs remember them, in León-Portilla's translation ([Flower and Song](https://islandsofhistory.org/volume-3/flower-and-song-pre-columbian-literatures-of-mesoamerica/))

(The performance context of the Cantares; the communal tradition of the songs.)

**Plain words** The point is not that private art is wrong; it is that the tradition's songs were made to be sung together, and the record shows them being sung among the people. If all your art happens alone, you keep the convenience and miss whatever the shared room does. So keep one practice — choir, dance, playing with people — that needs company to work.

*For example:* The person whose entire musical life is earbuds — joins the terrible, joyful community choir, and rediscovers that singing with others is a different organ than listening alone.

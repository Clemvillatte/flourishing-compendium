> **Principle 15** — Let art be communal, not only private

The flower-and-song record keeps a communal form: the Toltecs, in the songs' account of the ancients, composed songs and sang them among the people. This page's recommendation for now: keep one artistic practice in your life that happens with other people (singing, playing, attending, dancing), not only alone.

> "THEY WERE SINGERS, THEY COMPOSED SONGS AND SANG THEM AMONG THE PEOPLE" — the Toltecs as the songs remember them, in León-Portilla's translation ([Flower and Song](https://islandsofhistory.org/volume-3/flower-and-song-pre-columbian-literatures-of-mesoamerica/))

(The performance context of the Cantares; the communal tradition of the songs.)

**Plain words** What the record shows is songs composed and sung among people. What that suggests for a life now is this page's recommendation rather than a finding: shared artistic activity — choir, dance, playing with people — does things that solitary listening does not, so keep one such practice.

*For example:* The person whose entire musical life is earbuds — joins the terrible, joyful community choir, and rediscovers that singing with others is a different organ than listening alone.

> **Principle 377** — Practise relational ethics in the digital age

Vallor argues that living well with new technologies calls for character, not just rules. In Technology and the Virtues she names twelve "technomoral" virtues: honesty, self-control, humility, justice, courage, empathy, care, civility, flexibility, perspective, magnanimity and technomoral wisdom ([Technology and Civic Virtue, Philosophy & Technology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13347-023-00669-w); list in [Uncertainty tolerance in healthcare](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11017-025-09731-4)). Her aim is a collective one: "we need to cultivate in ourselves, collectively, a special kind of moral character" (Vallor 2016, p. 1, quoted in [Ethics and Information Technology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10676-019-09503-4)).

(Vallor, 2016, Technology and the Virtues; Floridi, 2013, The Ethics of Information.)

### Care online

Another study paraphrases Vallor's care as "the skilful, attentive, responsible and emotionally responsive disposition to personally meet the needs of those with whom we share our techno-social environment" (same article). Her chapter on surveillance and self-tracking asks how "cultivating the technomoral virtues" can help us flourish together in an "increasingly panoptic technosocial environment" ([Vallor, 2016, ch. 8, OUP](https://academic.oup.com/book/25951/chapter/193740513)). The twelve-virtue selection has been criticised, including by Howard and Curzer in a book symposium, though it "remains arguably the most important virtue-based approach in ethics of technology" ([Philosophy & Technology, 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13347-022-00553-z)).

I did not open Floridi's The Ethics of Information, so this page makes no claim about it.

**Plain words**
Treat people online as you would in the room. Pausing, reading what the other person actually wrote, and replying with care are all part of that.

**For example**
Before posting a reply to a heated message, wait ten minutes and reread what the other person wrote. Reply to their point first, then to your own.

> **Principle 37** — Beware the mob's favors and its fear

The free person, Spinoza demonstrates in P70, strives to avoid the favors of the ignorant, because a favor from the crowd is a loan at predatory interest: it comes bundled with expectations that the crowd will collect, and the proof adds the cost of refusing badly. The observations are about unstructured social power. The crowd's approval and its anger are weather, and weather is not a foundation. Practically, take the applause and the hostility of groups as data about the group, not as verdicts on your course.

(Ethics IV, P70; Theological-Political Treatise, Preface, the analysis of the multitude.)

**Plain words** The free person avoids the favors of the ignorant — a favor from the crowd is a loan at predatory interest: bundled with expectations the crowd will collect. Accept the gift, and you've signed for the debt. The proof adds: the free person is wary even of favors from the wise.

*For example:* The community leader who accepts the "friendly" sponsorship of the local power-broker — and spends the next three years paying the interest: the speeches, the favours, the quiet approvals. The loan was never free; it was predatory.

> **Principle 24** — Root your work in the people it serves

The tradition's work (the milpa above all) is done within webs of obligation and offering: the work feeds the family, the community, and the gods, and is not understood as a private accumulation project. The practical principle is the re-attachment of work to its beneficiaries: work done for nameless others at a distance loses its rootedness and its meaning; work whose beneficiaries you can name keeps both. Practically, for your principal work, name the specific people it reaches, and if you cannot name them, go meet some of them.

(The milpa's relational structure; the tequio communal work tradition.)

**Plain words** The milpa is done within webs of obligation: work feeds the family, the community, the gods — not a private accumulation project. So re-attach your work to its beneficiaries: work for nameless others at a distance loses rootedness and meaning; work whose beneficiaries you can name keeps both.

*For example:* The programmer who can't name a single user of their code, and the plumber who knows every family on the route — the tradition isn't romantic about either job; it just notes which one's meaning is load-bearing.

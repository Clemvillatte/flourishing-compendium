> **Principle 95** — Let knowledge purify action

The Gita's claim, stated in the two verses this page points at, is that knowledge purifies and that the fire of knowledge reduces all actions to ashes. The verses supply the comparison; they do not supply a mechanism. What the verses do supply is a location for the knowledge: within the Self, in due time, for the person perfected in Yoga.

> "Verily, there is no purifier in this world like knowledge. He who is perfected in Yoga finds it within the Self in due time." (Bhagavad Gita 4.38, bhagavadgita.com)

> "As the blazing fire reduces fuel to ashes, O Arjuna, so does the fire of knowledge reduce all actions to ash." (Bhagavad Gita 4.37, bhagavadgita.com)

**Plain words** The verse says knowledge purifies and compares its effect on action to fire's effect on fuel. It says the person perfected in Yoga finds that knowledge within themselves, in due time. What the page adds is a practical reading: if knowledge reduces actions to ashes in the way the verse says, then acting from knowledge and acting from ignorance are not two equal options, and the Yoga the verse names is how a person comes to hold the knowledge.

*For example:* (Imagined.) A reader who opens the Gita verse itself before acting on a claim about it, and who treats the Yoga the verse names as something to practise rather than something to define.

> **Principle 2** — Distinguish the feeling from its interpretation

The compendium's exercise is to write down a bodily sensation and your explanation of it separately. This gives you a specific interpretation to question before deciding what to do. Gross places reappraisal early in the emotion-generative process and defines it as follows:

> "It consists of changing the way a situation is construed so as to decrease its emotional impact." ([Gross, 2002, Psychophysiology 39, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0048577201393198))

**Plain words** You can take the discomfort seriously and still question your explanation of it. Write down what you feel, then write down what you think it means, and read the second line as a claim that might be wrong.

*For example:* In an imagined presentation scenario, you notice warm hands and predict that you will forget your opening. Record the sensation and the prediction separately, then compare the prediction with what happened during rehearsal.

## Sources

- [Gross, J. J. (2002). Emotion regulation: Affective, cognitive, and social consequences. Psychophysiology 39(3), 281-292, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1017/S0048577201393198) — the reappraisal definition quoted above

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Reappraisal's effectiveness depends on separating the feeling from the appraisal|Reappraisal's effectiveness depends on separating the feeling from the appraisal]]**, changing the appraisal changes the felt response (Gross & John, 2003; Webb et al., 2012)
- **Interoception and interpretation are dissociable**, the same bodily signal can be labelled differently with different downstream consequences (Barrett et al., 2019)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Cognitive appraisal as the hinge of emotion** (Stoics, Epictetus)
- **The body-mind distinction is not a feeling-content distinction** (Descartes, Passions of the Soul)

## Practice

- Label the raw sensation ("tight chest") separately from the interpretation ("I'm in danger") and note that the second is a hypothesis, not a fact.

> **Principle 23** — Know that affect changes memory; do not trust emotional memory as a record

Your current mood changes which memories you retrieve and how you interpret them. Feeling sad biases recall toward sad memories; feeling anxious biases interpretation toward threat. This is mood-congruent memory, and it is one of the mechanisms that maintains depression and anxiety.

**Plain words** Your current mood changes which memories you retrieve and how you interpret them. Feeling sad biases recall toward sad memories; feeling anxious biases interpretation toward threat. Mood-congruent memory: do not trust the emotional archive as a record.

*For example:* The 3 a.m. conviction that the whole life has been a failure — and the fact-check that only becomes possible at 10 a.m., when the archive returns different files. The memories weren't lying exactly; the librarian was in a mood.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Mood-congruent memory|Mood-congruent memory]]**, current affect biases recall toward affectively congruent material (Bower, 1981)
- **Memory reconsolidation is mood-sensitive**, retrieved memories are labile and can be re-encoded with different affective tone (Schiller et al., 2010)

## Practice

- When you feel bad about a past event, note that your current mood is shaping the memory. Ask: "how did I see this when I was in a different mood?"

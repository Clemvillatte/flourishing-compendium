> **Principle 61** — Cultivate positive affect deliberately: it broadens and builds

Fredrickson proposes the broaden-and-build as a theory of what positive emotions do, not just how they feel. In the short term they are posited to broaden the momentary thought-action repertoire; over time this is posited to build enduring personal resources. The article reviews preliminary empirical evidence supporting the theory and identifies open empirical questions that remain to be tested ([Fredrickson, 2001, PubMed 11315248](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11315248/)).

> The broaden-and-build theory posits that experiences of positive emotions broaden people's momentary thought-action repertoires, which in turn serves to build their enduring personal resources, ranging from physical and intellectual resources to social and psychological resources.
>
> — Fredrickson, 2001, American Psychologist 56(3) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11315248/))

**Plain words** The theory treats positive affect as something with a function, not only a feeling. Broadening is posited to happen in the moment; building is posited to happen over time. The evidence the article reviews is preliminary, and the author names open questions that remain.

For example (imagined): a team in a good week suddenly solves a problem that had been stuck for two months. The theory offers one way of reading what happened; the article's evidence is preliminary and does not establish that mood caused the solving, and other explanations are possible.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Broaden-and-build|Broaden-and-build theory]]**, positive emotions broaden thought-action repertoires and build enduring resources (Fredrickson, 2001, 2013)
- **[[Broaden-and-build|Upward spirals]]**, positive affect and broadened coping reciprocally reinforce each other over time (Fredrickson & Joiner, 2002)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Joy|Joy as expansive]]** (Spinoza, Ethics III)
- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia as activity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)

## Practice

- Seek experiences that elicit positive affect, with the theory's own framing in mind: the point is what they are posited to expand, not the pleasantness alone.

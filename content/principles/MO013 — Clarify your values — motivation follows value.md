> **Principle 13** — Clarify your values: motivation follows value

Values are the fundamental drivers of motivation. When you know what you actually care about (as opposed to what you have absorbed or been told to care about), motivation follows naturally. Values clarification is a foundation of sustainable motivation.

**Plain words** Motivation follows values — you can't run an engine on fuel you don't actually have. Half of "unmotivated" is pursuing inherited goals: your parents', your industry's, your feed's. Find out what you'd care about if nobody was watching.

*For example:* The law student who can't study — until they realise the law was the family's dream. The same brain, pointed at what they actually wanted, suddenly has plenty of motivation. The fuel was never the problem.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Values clarification|Values clarification in ACT]]**, identifying and clarifying values is a core motivational process (Hayes et al., 2011; Wilson et al., 2010)
- **Values and goal pursuit**, self-concordant goals (aligned with values) are pursued more effectively (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999; Sheldon et al., 2004)

- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires examined values** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Right intention|Right intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Clarify your values: what do you care about most? What would you want your life to stand for? Use values clarification exercises. Distinguish your values from inherited or absorbed ones.

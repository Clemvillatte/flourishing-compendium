> **Principle 15** — Cultivate autonomy: motivation requires choice

Autonomy — feeling that your actions are chosen and endorsed by you — is a basic psychological need and a foundation of motivation. Environments that support autonomy (choice, rationale, acknowledgment of feelings) produce more autonomous and sustainable motivation.

**Plain words** Autonomy is a nutrient — feeling that your actions are chosen. It's why micromanagement exhausts and why the same task, self-chosen, energises. Even in constrained situations, find the corner that's yours to choose.

*For example:* The hospital cleaner study — the same job, but those who saw their choices ("I choose how I work this floor") were healthier and happier. The work didn't change; the authorship did.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Autonomy|Autonomy as basic need (SDT)]]**, feeling choice and volition is fundamental to motivation (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Autonomy-supportive environments increase motivation**, providing choice, rationale, and acknowledgment of feelings increases autonomous motivation (Deci et al., 1994; Reeve, 2006)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Autonomy|Autonomy as self-legislation]]** (Kant, Groundwork)

## Practice

- Cultivate autonomy: make choices explicit (why am I doing this?), provide rationale to yourself, and acknowledge your feelings. Do not treat tasks as imposed; find your reason.

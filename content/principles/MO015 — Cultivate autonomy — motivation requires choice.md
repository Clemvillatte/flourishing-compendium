> **Principle 15** — Cultivate autonomy: motivation requires choice


Self-determination theory names autonomy, competence and relatedness as three innate needs which, "when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being" (Ryan and Deci 2000, American Psychologist 55(1):68-78, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/)). Autonomy here means the feeling of choosing your behaviour rather than feeling "controlled or compelled by others" ([APA summary](https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/self-determination-theory)).

The "choice, rationale, acknowledgment of feelings" list comes from one laboratory experiment. It supported the hypothesis that "providing a meaningful rationale, acknowledging the behaver's feelings, and conveying choice" promote internalization of an uninteresting task, and that the context decides whether people take a value in fully (integration) or only half-accept it (introjection) (Deci, Eghrari, Patrick and Leone 1994, J Personality 62(1):119-142, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8169757/)). Training others to support autonomy works too: a meta-analysis of 19 studies found a weighted effect size of 0.63, with a caution about the small number of studies (Su and Reeve 2011, [abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ916565)).

Choice is not valued the same way everywhere. Anglo American children were less motivated when others made their choices, while Asian American children "proved most intrinsically motivated when choices were made for them by trusted authority figures or peers" (Iyengar and Lepper 1999, [PDF](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Iyengar_Lepper_1999_Rethinking_the_value_of_choice.pdf)). Autonomy as endorsement is not the same as doing everything alone.

**Plain words** Work you feel you chose and endorse tends to be easier to keep doing than work that feels forced. When you cannot change the task, you can still look for the part you can choose, ask for the reason, and notice how you feel about it.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A nurse is told to chart in a fixed format. She asks why, hears it feeds handover safety, and chooses when in the shift she completes it. The format stays fixed, and she now has a reason and a say.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Autonomy|Autonomy as basic need (SDT)]]**, autonomy, competence and relatedness are proposed as innate needs whose satisfaction supports motivation and well-being (Ryan & Deci, 2000; the 2017 book is unopened)
- **Autonomy-supportive environments increase motivation**, a rationale, acknowledged feelings and choice promoted internalization in one experiment (Deci et al., 1994); autonomy-support training showed a weighted effect of 0.63 across 19 studies (Su & Reeve, 2011); the value of personal choice differs by cultural self-model (Iyengar & Lepper, 1999); Reeve (2006) is unopened

## Practice

- Cultivate autonomy: make choices explicit (why am I doing this?), provide rationale to yourself, and acknowledge your feelings. Do not treat tasks as imposed; find your reason.

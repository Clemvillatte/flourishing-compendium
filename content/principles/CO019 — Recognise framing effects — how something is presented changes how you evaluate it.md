> **Principle 19** — Recognise framing effects: how something is presented changes how you evaluate it

The same information, framed differently, leads to different decisions. "90% survival rate" vs "10% mortality rate"; "gain" vs "loss" framing in choices. This is not a flaw to be eliminated but a fact to be recognised.

**Plain words** Framing effects: the same information, framed differently, produces different decisions — "90% survival" versus "10% mortality," gains versus losses. Not a flaw to eliminate; a handle to grasp. When you want to move someone (including yourself), the frame does the moving.

*For example:* The treatment decision that flips depending on which statistic the doctor leads with — same data, different surgeries — and the person who learns to ask for both frames before deciding. The frame was never neutral; now they know it's a lever.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Framing effects|Framing effects]]**, logically equivalent descriptions lead to different choices (Tversky & Kahneman, 1981; Kahneman & Tversky, 1984)
- **[[Prospect theory|Prospect theory]]**, losses loom larger than gains; people are risk-averse in gains and risk-seeking in losses (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Perspectivism|Perspectivism]]** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)
- **[[Dependent origination|Dependent origination]]** (Buddhism, Nagarjuna, Mulamadhyamakakarika)

## Practice

- When making a decision, deliberately reframe it. Describe it in terms of both gains and losses, both survival and mortality. If your preference flips, the framing is driving the choice, not the substance.

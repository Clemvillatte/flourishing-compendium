> **Principle 38** — Know when to stop regulating and start acting

Some situations do not need better regulation; they need action. If your affect is signalling a real problem (an abusive relationship, a toxic job, a health symptom), the adaptive response is to change the situation, not to regulate your feelings about it. Misapplied regulation can become a form of compliance with harm.

**Plain words** Some situations don't need better regulation; they need action. If the affect is signalling a real problem — abusive relationship, toxic job, health symptom — the adaptive response is to change the situation, not your response to it. Know when to stop regulating and start acting.

*For example:* The person who spends two years perfecting their coping strategies for the unbearable job — an ever-better swimmer in a draining pool — and the day they redirect the skill to the actual problem: the exit. Regulation was the wrong department.
## Psychology parallels

- **Problem-solving outperforms reappraisal for controllable stressors**, for situations that can be changed, addressing the situation is more effective than changing the interpretation (Troy et al., 2013)
- **Misapplied regulation is associated with avoidance and worse outcomes**, using regulation to tolerate the intolerable is maladaptive (Ford & Troy, 2019)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Wisdom as knowing what is up to you** (Stoics, Epictetus, Enchiridion)
- **[[Phronesis|Phronesis as practical judgement]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VI)
- **[[Right action|Right action over acceptance of harm]]** (Buddhism, Right Action)

## Practice

- Ask: "is this a feeling problem or a situation problem?" If the situation is harmful, change the situation. Regulating your feelings about it without changing it is avoidance dressed as stoicism.

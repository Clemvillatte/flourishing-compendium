> **Principle 48** — Act in the right way, at the right time, toward the right objects, in the right manner

This is Aristotle's formula for virtuous action, and it is deceptively simple. The whole difficulty is knowing what "right" means in each case. That is what phronesis provides. Do not simplify the formula; let it train your attention to context.

(NE II.6, VI.1.)

**Plain words** Aristotle's formula sounds simple: right way, right time, right objects, right manner. The difficulty is knowing what "right" means each time. Don't flatten it into a slogan. Let it make you look harder at the context.

*For example:* Saying "I'm sorry" sincerely and promptly after hurting someone is right. Saying it weeks later by text may not be.

> **Principle 73** — Recognise attentional changes with ageing: some decline, some stability

Sustained attention and processing speed decline with age; some aspects of selective attention (filtering) remain relatively stable. Understanding this allows you to design your attentional life across the lifespan.

**Plain words** Ageing changes the attention portfolio: processing speed and stamina dip, but selective focus and the ability to ignore junk hold up well. The older mind is slower and harder to distract — a different machine, not a worse one.

*For example:* The older colleague who's a step behind the meeting's rapid banter and the only one who noticed the number that didn't add up. Different beam, different catches.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Cognitive ageing|Cognitive ageing and attention]]**, processing speed and sustained attention decline; some selective attention remains stable (Salthouse, 2004; Hartshorne & Germine, 2015)
- **Attentional control changes with age**, older adults show some decline in executive attention but may use compensatory strategies (Cabeza et al., 2002; Madden et al., 2005)
- **[[Positivity effect|Positivity effect in attention]]**, older adults attend more to positive and less to negative information (Carstensen et al., 2003; Reed & Carstensen, 2012)

## Practice

- As you age, do not fight the decline in processing speed. Lean into the strengths: experience, wisdom, positivity bias. Use compensatory strategies (notes, reminders, routines) to support attention.

> **Principle 55** — Retract like the tortoise when needed

The Gita's image: the disciplined one draws in the senses from the objects of sense, as the tortoise draws its limbs into its shell. The image teaches that retraction is a skill of timing, not a permanent stance; the tortoise extends its limbs again when it is safe. Practically, know your withdrawal signal (rising irritation, compulsive checking) and practice the retraction before the state solidifies.

(BG 2.58.)

**Plain words** The Gita's tortoise: the disciplined one draws in the senses as the tortoise draws its limbs into its shell. The image teaches timing, not a permanent stance — the tortoise extends its limbs again when it's safe. Retract when needed; re-extend when it's time.

*For example:* Someone learns to turtle up during the toxic family weekend — fewer inputs, earlier nights, guarded exposure — and comes back out fully at work on Monday. The shell is a tool with a schedule, not a home.

- **Time-out and strategic disengagement**, the emotion-regulation research showing that well-timed withdrawal prevents escalation

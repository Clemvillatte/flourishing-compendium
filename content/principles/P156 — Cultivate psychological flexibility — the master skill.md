> **Principle 156** — Cultivate psychological flexibility: the master skill

The term's modern use was consolidated in a review by Kashdan and Rottenberg, which defines the construct by its components:

> "Psychological flexibility spans a wide range of human abilities to: recognize and adapt to various situational demands; shift mindsets or behavioral repertoires when these strategies compromise personal or social functioning; maintain balance among important life domains; and be aware, open, and committed to behaviors that are congruent with deeply held values." (Kashdan and Rottenberg, 2010, [abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:21151705&resultType=core&format=json))

The same review states that "In many forms of psychopathology, these flexibility processes are absent" (Kashdan and Rottenberg, 2010). The seed's citation line names the acceptance and commitment therapy book by Hayes, Strosahl and Wilson (2011), which built a therapy around this construct; that book was not opened for this page and is named only as the inherited attribution. "The master skill" is the compendium's ranking, not the review's; the review argues for the construct's importance to psychological health, not for its priority over other principles. The exercise the compendium takes: when a fixed response keeps failing, test one deliberately different response aligned to a stated value.

### Plain words

Flexibility is not agreeing with everything or changing with every wind. It is keeping your actions steerable while your commitments stay put: notice what the situation asks, drop the strategy that is not working, keep the value. The rigid version runs one strategy regardless of results.

### For example

Take someone who meditates, hates sitting still, and quits. The flexible move is not forcing the sitting; it is naming what was wanted, calm and attention, and trying a different vehicle for it while keeping the value. The example is illustrative; the sources do not promise the second attempt works.

> **Principle 45** — Relax effort into support

Sutra 2.46 asks for a posture that is "stable-and-easy". The next sutra says how it comes about:

> By relaxation of effort or by a [mental] state-of-balance with reference to Ananta.

(Yoga Sutras 2.47, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, p. 192, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary explains the first option: "When efforts cease the posture is completed, so that there is no agitation of the body." The Tika adds that the natural effort which holds the body up is not what produces the posture being taught, and so "a man, practising the specific posture as taught, should resort to an effort which consists in the relaxation of the natural effort." For the second option, Ananta is "the Chief of Serpents, who upholds the globe of the earth upon his thousand very steadfast hoods", and the mind comes to a balanced state with reference to him. Sutra 2.48 gives the result: "he is not overcome by the extremes, by cold and heat and by the other [extremes]."

So Ananta is a specific figure here, the serpent holding up the earth, and the sutra gives two methods, not one. Reading it as a general boundless support is the page's own. The advice to spend as much effort relaxing as getting in, and to exhale and release the jaw, shoulders and hands, is also the page's own practice and not in the text.

**Plain words**

Once you are in your seat, the sutra says to stop the effort of holding it and let something steady carry you. The commentary names that steady thing as a mythic serpent supporting the world.

**For example**

A man sits on a cushion and holds himself upright for ten minutes, back aching. Then he lets his weight drop through the sitting bones, loosens his hands, and finds the posture holds on its own.

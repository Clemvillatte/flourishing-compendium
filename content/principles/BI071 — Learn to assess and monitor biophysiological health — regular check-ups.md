> **Principle 71** — Learn to assess and monitor biophysiological health: regular check-ups

The encyclopedia's rule is that all adults should visit their provider from time to time, even when healthy, to screen for diseases, reduce the risk of future medical problems, update vaccinations and maintain a relationship with a provider in case of an illness ([MedlinePlus, Physical exam frequency](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002125.htm)). The reason it gives for the schedule is the silence of what it screens for: the only way to find out if you have high blood pressure is to have it checked regularly, and high blood sugar and high cholesterol levels may not have any symptoms in the early stages. A simple blood test can check blood sugar and cholesterol.

> "Even if you feel fine, you should still see your health care provider for regular checkups." (MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia, [Physical exam frequency](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002125.htm))

**Plain words**
Feeling well does not rule out a problem that screening can detect: the page's examples are high blood pressure, which the page says can only be identified by checking, and high blood sugar and cholesterol, which may not have symptoms in the early stages. That is why the rule is to keep showing up when you feel fine. What you notice at home, like how you slept, is material for the visit, not a replacement for it.

*For example:* An imagined patient feels well but asks their provider which check-ups are due. They mention recent sleep changes and let the provider decide whether tests are appropriate.

## Psychology parallels

- **Physiological monitoring**, tracking sleep, energy, and HRV provides feedback (Thayer et al., 2012; Laborde et al., 2017)
- **Medical check-ups**, regular medical evaluation detects treatable conditions (Stover & Stover, 2011)

## Practice

- Monitor biophysiological health: track sleep and energy, keep up routine medical check-ups, and mention mood and energy when you go. Let the clinician decide what to test.

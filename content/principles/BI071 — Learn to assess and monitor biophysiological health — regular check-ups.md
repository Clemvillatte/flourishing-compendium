> **Principle 71** — Learn to assess and monitor biophysiological health: regular check-ups

The encyclopedia's rule is that all adults should visit their provider from time to time, even when healthy, to screen for diseases, reduce the risk of future medical problems, update vaccinations and maintain a relationship with a provider in case of an illness ([MedlinePlus, Physical exam frequency](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002125.htm)). The reason it gives for the schedule is the silence of what it screens for: the only way to find out if you have high blood pressure is to have it checked regularly, and high blood sugar and high cholesterol levels may not have any symptoms in the early stages. A simple blood test can check for these conditions.

> "Even if you feel fine, you should still see your health care provider for regular checkups." (MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia, [Physical exam frequency](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002125.htm))

**Plain words**
Some of the conditions worth catching early are quiet: high blood pressure has no feeling you can notice, and the early stages of high blood sugar and cholesterol are often silent too. That is why the rule is to keep showing up when you feel fine, not only when something hurts. What you do at home, like noticing how you slept and what your energy did, is material for the visit, not a replacement for it.

*For example:* An imagined case: someone who feels healthy books the annual visit they have been skipping, mentions that their sleep has been poor and their energy dips by afternoon, and leaves with a blood-pressure check on the record and a lab order if the provider wants one.

## Psychology parallels

- **Physiological monitoring**, tracking sleep, energy, and HRV provides feedback (Thayer et al., 2012; Laborde et al., 2017)
- **Medical check-ups**, regular medical evaluation detects treatable conditions (Stover & Stover, 2011)

## Practice

- Monitor biophysiological health: track sleep and energy, keep up routine medical check-ups, and mention mood and energy when you go. Let the clinician decide what to test.

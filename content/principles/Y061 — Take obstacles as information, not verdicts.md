> **Principle 61** — Take obstacles as information, not verdicts

Synthesizing the previous two principles: an obstacle is the practice talking back to you, showing where the design needs work, not a referendum on your worth. The tradition's structure assumes obstacles are normal and builds a whole layer of supports for them. Practically, log obstacles as data (time, trigger, type) and review monthly; patterns in the log are the practice curriculum.

(YS 1.30-1.32, the design of the passage.)

**Plain words** Synthesis of the two: an obstacle is the practice talking back to you — showing where the design needs work, not a referendum on your worth. The tradition assumes obstacles are normal and builds a whole layer of supports for them. Take them as information.

*For example:* Someone hits the plateau and concludes "I'm just not a meditator" — versus the one who takes the plateau as data, adjusts the dose, the time, the object, and continues. Same plateau; one treats it as weather station, the other as a court verdict.

> **Principle 19** — Refuse the hobgoblin of consistency

A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds: the commitment to yesterday's positions as a matter of identity, maintained against today's evidence. The principle is the tradition's distinct epistemology of selfhood: to be large is to contain one's revisions without shame. Practically, once per month, write one sentence beginning "I no longer believe..." and one beginning "I now believe...", and file them without ceremony.

(Emerson, Self-Reliance.)

**Plain words** A foolish consistency is the hobgoblin of little minds: defending yesterday's position as a matter of identity against today's evidence. To be large is to hold your own revisions without shame. Once a month, write "I no longer believe…" and "I now believe…" — no ceremony, just the file.

*For example:* Someone who spent a decade mocking meditation and now, mildly, says "I was wrong about that" — and finds the admission costs less than the decade of pretending did.

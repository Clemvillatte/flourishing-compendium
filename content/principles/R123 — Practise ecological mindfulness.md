> **Principle 385** — Practise ecological mindfulness

> The Work That Reconnects guides you on a Spiral journey through four stages: Coming from Gratitude, Honoring Our Pain for the World, Seeing with New/Ancient Eyes, Going Forth.

(Work That Reconnects Network, "What is the Work That Reconnects?", [workthatreconnects.org](https://workthatreconnects.org/what-is-the-work-that-reconnects/), a section stated to be "adapted from Coming Back to Life, by Joanna Macy and Molly Brown." The book itself, 2014 updated edition, New Society Publishers, was not opened in full.)

The same page says that this process "quiets the frantic mind and helps to ground us" and that in the third stage "we begin Seeing with New/Ancient Eyes" as we experience "the reality of our inter-existence." So the ecological element in Macy and Brown's practice is about feeling one's connection to other life, including the pain that comes with it. It is not described there as an audit of consumption or waste. The compendium's instruction to "pay attention to what you consume, what you waste, what you destroy" is its practical application and cannot be attributed to the passages above.

For the psychology, Amel, Manning and Scott measured 100 adults at a sustainability expo and reported that "acting with awareness was significantly positively correlated with self-reported sustainable behavior," whereas "observing sensations did not predict behavior" (Ecopsychology 1(1), 2009, abstract; [mirror on Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/6523940/Mindfulness_and_Sustainable_Behavior_Pondering_Attention_and_Awareness_as_Means_for_Increasing_Green_Behavior)). The authors say this "calls into question the notion that feeling connected to the world outside of ourselves is a precondition for sustainable action." A later review summarises Barbaro and Pickett's two studies (N = 360 and N = 296) as finding positive associations between mindfulness and self-reported pro-environmental behaviour, with connectedness to nature a possible mediator ([PMC10896055](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10896055/)). All of this is correlational and self-reported.

The Hooker et al. (2020) citation in the entry's reference line was not opened. Kabat-Zinn's Full Catastrophe Living was not opened.

**Plain words** Macy and Brown present ecological awareness as a practice that moves from gratitude to grief for the world to a sense of interdependence and then to action. Surveys link mindful attention in everyday behaviour with more self-reported sustainable choices, though the evidence is correlational. Noticing where things come from is a reasonable habit, and the research does not show that it changes behaviour on its own.

*For example (imagined):* Someone reads a clothing label, learns where the garment was made, and chooses a second-hand alternative the next time they need one.

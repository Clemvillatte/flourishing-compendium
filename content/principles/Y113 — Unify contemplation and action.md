> **Principle 113** — Unify contemplation and action

Arjuna in the fifth chapter asks Krishna which is better, giving up works or doing them. In Arnold's translation Krishna answers: "To cease from works is well, and to do works in holiness is well; and both conduct to bliss supreme; but of these twain the better way is his who working piously refraineth not" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 5, trans. Arnold).

### One path, not two

He then says it is "the new scholar" who "talks as they were two, this Sankhya and this Yoga: wise men know who husbands one plucks golden fruit of both! The region of high rest which Sankhyans reach Yogins attain. Who sees these twain as one sees with clear eyes!" (ch. 5, Arnold). The third chapter makes a similar point when Krishna speaks of "two paths shown to this world; two schools of wisdom", the Sankhya way of works prescribed by reason and the Yoga that attains "by meditation, spiritually: yet these are one!" (ch. 3, Arnold). A little later: "no man shall 'scape from act by shunning action" (ch. 3, Arnold).

### Where the wording differs

The earlier version of this principle spoke of children arguing over the distinction. Arnold has "the new scholar", so I cannot quote "children" from the text I opened. The claim that "each is the other done properly" and the curriculum remark are the principle's own, as is the practical rule that insight is incomplete until it has changed an action.

**Plain words** Krishna refuses to split the way of knowledge from the way of action. He says that someone who masters one gains the fruit of both. A reasonable rule of thumb from this is to test insights by acting on them and to examine actions afterward. The rule is the principle's, not the Gita's.

*For example:* Imagined example: after a morning of reflection on impatience, you note one meeting in the afternoon where you will pause before answering. That evening you look back at how it went.

(Bhagavad-Gita ch. 5 and ch. 3, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388, opened 2026-10-21. Arnold's verses are unnumbered, so the old cites, 5.4-5.5 and 3.3, are not checked against a numbered edition.)

> **Principle 113** — Unify contemplation and action

The Gita dismisses the quarrel between the way of knowledge and the way of action as a distinction children argue over: they are one; each is the other done properly. The traditions make the same point structurally, which is why the curriculum alternates between them. Practically, treat every contemplative insight as incomplete until it has changed an action, and every action as incomplete until it has been examined.

(BG 5.4-5.5; BG 3.3.)

**Plain words** The Gita dismisses the quarrel between the way of knowledge and the way of action as a distinction children argue over: they are one — each is the other done properly. Contemplation and action, unified; the split was always the artifact.

*For example:* The team's best problem-solver turns out to be the one with the steadiest attention — and the resident meditator's practice shows up as better meetings. The two "different paths" keep arriving at the same door.

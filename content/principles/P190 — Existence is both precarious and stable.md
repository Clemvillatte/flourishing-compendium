> **Principle 190** — Existence is both precarious and stable

Existence is both precarious and stable. The world includes the uncertain, unpredictable, uncontrollable, and hazardous — and also the regular, the settled, and the reliable. The visible rests on the invisible; what is unseen often decides what happens in the seen. Most suffering arises from expecting only the stable; most growth arises from working with both.

(Dewey, Experience and Nature, 1925.)

**Plain words** Existence is both precarious and stable — the world includes the uncertain, unpredictable, and hazardous, and also the regular, settled, and reliable. Most suffering arises from expecting only the stable; most growth arises from working with both. The sun rises (stable) and the market doesn't (precarious); plan accordingly.

*For example:* The budget with a stable paycheck and a precarious industry — the person who saves like the job could vanish and lives like the sun will rise handles both halves. The one who assumes only one half gets surprised by the other.

## Practice

- When things are going well, do not assume permanence. When things are falling apart, do not assume permanence. Both are real; both are partial.

## Limits

- A phenomenological and practical framing, not a metaphysical claim about ultimate reality.

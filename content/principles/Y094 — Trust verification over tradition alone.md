> **Principle 94** — Trust verification over tradition alone

The Kena's questions ("by what impelled does the mind fall on its object?... what is the eye of the eye?") are the tradition's own demand that the teaching be verified in experience rather than received on authority; the Gita sends the student to inquire of the wise rather than to merely obey them. Practically, keep a verification notebook: one column for received truth, one for what your own experience has actually shown. The methodological reading is a modern one; the Kena itself poses its questions in a devotional register.

(Kena Upanishad 1.4-2.4; BG 4.34.)

**Plain words** The Kena's questions — "by what impelled does the mind fall on its object? what is the eye of the eye?" — are the tradition demanding verification in experience, not reception on authority. Trust verification over tradition alone; the tradition itself insists.

*For example:* The student who takes the teaching on faith and the one who takes it to the lab of their own week — the tradition's own texts side with the second student. The questions are the curriculum.

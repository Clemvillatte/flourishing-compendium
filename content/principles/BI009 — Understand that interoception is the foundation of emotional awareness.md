> **Principle 9** — Understand that interoception is the foundation of emotional awareness

Interoception is the term for perceiving the body's internal states:

> "Interoception refers to the perception of a wide range of physical states beyond emotions, including heart rate, respiratory effort, temperature, fatigue, hunger, thirst, satiety, muscle ache, pain and itch" ([Brewer, Cook and Bird, 2016, §1. Background](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16737/1/Alexithymia:%20a%20general%20deficit%20of%20interoception.pdf))

A paper on alexithymia (difficulty identifying and describing one's own emotions) links the two halves of this principle. Its authors discuss how those with alexithymia confuse emotional states with other bodily states:

> "Those with alexithymia therefore not only struggle to recognize their own emotions (affective interoception) but also confuse these states with non-affective interoceptive states, such as hunger, tiredness and arousal." ([Brewer, Cook and Bird, 2016, §4. Discussion](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16737/1/Alexithymia:%20a%20general%20deficit%20of%20interoception.pdf))

The authors' reading of the pattern is the principle's evidence base: "Alexithymia may, therefore, be characterized better by a general failure of interoception, rather than one of affective interoception alone." (§4. Discussion)

**Plain words**
Interoception means noticing internal bodily sensations. The authors of the study above discuss difficulty distinguishing emotional and other bodily states in alexithymia. In the exercise below, describe a sensation without deciding immediately what it means.

**For example**
Sit still for two minutes and notice sensations in the chest, jaw or gut, without assuming what they mean about your emotional state. Notice what you find.

## Practice

- Practise interoception: spend 5-10 minutes daily scanning the body (heart, breath, gut, shoulders, jaw). Notice raw sensation before interpretation. This builds the foundation of emotional awareness.

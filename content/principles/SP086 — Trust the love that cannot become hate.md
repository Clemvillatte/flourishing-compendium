> **Principle 86** — Trust the love that cannot become hate

The love of God, Spinoza demonstrates, cannot be tainted by jealousy nor turned into hatred, because it is not competing for a perishable object. This property is the diagnostic for the whole tier of spiritual emotion: love that can curdle was attachment with love's face on it; love attached to understanding does not decay when circumstances change. Practically, examine your deepest attachments by their failure modes, not by their intensity: what happens to this love if the loved one changes, leaves, or succeeds beyond you?

(Ethics V, P20-21.)

**Plain words** The love of God cannot be tainted by jealousy nor turned into hatred — it is not competing for a perishable object. That property is the diagnostic for the whole tier of spiritual emotion: love that can curdle is ordinary attachment; love that cannot is the real thing. Test your loves by what they do under threat.

*For example:* The devotion that turns vicious when the object of devotion is questioned — curdled; it was attachment in vestments — versus the love of the work, the truth, the whole that no slight can poison. The test isn't intensity; it's stability under threat.

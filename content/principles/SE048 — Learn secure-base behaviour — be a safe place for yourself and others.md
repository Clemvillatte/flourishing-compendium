> **Principle 48** — Learn secure-base behaviour: be a safe place for yourself and others

Secure-base behaviour — the capacity to be a reliable, safe, responsive presence for self and others — is a foundation of both self-regulation and relationship quality. It is trainable.

**Plain words** Be a safe place to land — for yourself and for the people you love. That means: your own mistakes get curiosity instead of a tribunal, and your people get a reliable, warm response when they bring you a problem. You can't be a refuge for others if you're a war zone for yourself.

*For example:* The teenager who fails a test knows the difference between coming home to "what happened, what do you need" and coming home to a lecture. The first parent is a secure base. The second is a threat to manage. Kids can tell the difference by age six.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Secure base|Secure-base behaviour (Bowlby, Ainsworth)]]**, providing a safe base for exploration and a safe haven for distress (Bowlby, 1988; Mikulincer & Shaver, 2007)
- **Secure-base script**, having a coherent script for being a safe place predicts attachment security (Waters & Waters, 2006; Mikulincer & Shaver, 2007)

## Practice

- Practise secure-base behaviour with yourself: when distressed, be a safe haven (comfort yourself); when exploring, be a secure base (encourage yourself). Do the same for others.

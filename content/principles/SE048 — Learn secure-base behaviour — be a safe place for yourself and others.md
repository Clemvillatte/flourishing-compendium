> **Principle 48** — Learn secure-base behaviour: be a safe place for yourself and others

The construct behind this principle is the secure base. Bowlby's view, as summarised in the Waterses' essay: "the origins of attachment lie in countless experiences of using the primary caregiver as a secure base from which to explore and as a haven of safety" (Waters, Waters & Waters, 2020, full text). Those experiences condense into a script:

> "1. A child (or infant) and mother (or two adult attachment partners) are constructively occupied. 2. They are interrupted by an event or another actor. The infant (or one adult) is distressed. 3. There is a bid for help. 4. The bid for help is detected and help is offered. 5. The offer of help is accepted. 6. The help is effective in overcoming the difficulty. 7. The help includes effective comforting and affect regulation. 8. The pair return to (or initiate new) constructive interaction." (Waters, Waters & Waters, 2020, Table 1)

Waters and Waters defined the script concept: people build "script-like representations of secure base experiences" (2006, abstract). The compendium's application of the principle: run the script from both chairs, as the one who bids for help and as the one who detects the bid, offers help that works, and restores engagement. The page treats this as practisable; the opened passages define the script, they do not test trainability.

**Plain words**
The secure base script is a small story with eight beats: two people busy, a disruption, distress, a bid for help, help offered, accepted, effective, comfort given, and back to being busy. The principle's suggestion is to be fluent in that story from both roles, responder and bidder, including toward yourself.

*For example:* Imagine the teenager who fails a test and knows the difference between coming home to "what happened, what do you need" and coming home to a lecture. The first parent is a secure base; the second is a threat to manage.

## Practice

- Practise secure-base behaviour with yourself: when distressed, be a safe haven (comfort yourself); when exploring, be a secure base (encourage yourself). Do the same for others.

> **Principle 17** — Cultivate relatedness: motivation is sustained by connection

Relatedness — feeling connected to and cared for by others — is a basic psychological need and a foundation of motivation. Goals pursued in isolation are harder to sustain; goals pursued in connection are easier.

**Plain words** Relatedness is a nutrient — doing things with people, for people, seen by people. Motivation withers in isolation, even for goals you love. The study buddy, the class, the group chat — connection is a motivational technology.

*For example:* The home gym that gathered dust and the class you never miss — same exercises, different relatedness. The equipment wasn't the variable; the people were.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Relatedness|Relatedness as basic need (SDT)]]**, feeling connected is fundamental to motivation (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Social support and goal pursuit**, supportive others increase goal attainment and well-being (Sheldon & Elliott, 1999; Hogan et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Ubuntu: I am because we are** (African Philosophy, Desmond Tutu)
- **Friendship as mutual support** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII-IX)
- **[[Right association|Right association]]** (Buddhism, Sigalovada Sutta)

## Practice

- Cultivate relatedness: pursue goals with others, share your goals, seek support. Do not try to sustain motivation in isolation. Connection is a motivational resource.

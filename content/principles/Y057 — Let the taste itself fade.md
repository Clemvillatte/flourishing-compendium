> **Principle 57** — Let the taste itself fade

The Gita observes two stages: for the abstinent one, objects turn away, but the taste (rasa) remains; only for the settled seer does even the taste cease. The principle is honest about extinction's timeline: the appetite may outlast the behavior, and neither its timing nor its eventual disappearance is guaranteed. Practically, do not measure progress by the first weeks, when taste still speaks; measure it at the point where the thought no longer recruits you.

(BG 2.59.)

**Plain words** Two stages, honestly named: for the abstinent one, objects turn away but the taste remains; only for the settled seer does even the taste cease. The appetite may outlast the behavior — neither its timing nor its eventual evaporation is under your control. Don't panic at the lingering.

*For example:* Someone quits the cigarettes, and eight months in still wants one at parties — the tradition's counsel: the object left; the taste is still in transit. It's not failure; it's the second stage, and it too passes.

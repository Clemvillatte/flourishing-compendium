> **Principle 57** — Let the taste itself fade

In chapter 2 the Gita makes an honest remark about how long it takes to lose a desire:

> Aspirants may restrain the senses from their objects of enjoyment, but the taste for the sense objects remains. However, even this taste ceases for those who realize the Supreme.

(BG 2.59, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/59/).)

Mukundananda's commentary compares restraint to fasting or illness, when desires weaken but the "seed of desire remains within the mind", and return when the fast ends. He says the Gita "does not teach a dry suppression of desires" and teaches "sublimation of desires by directing them toward God". The taste fades because a higher taste replaces it, in his account, and he quotes Ramakrishna: "Devotion is love for the highest; and the lowest shall fall away by itself."

The Gita says the taste ceases for those who realize the Supreme. It names two stages, restraint and then cessation, and says cessation comes with realization. It gives no timetable, and the verse does not mention a thought that pulls at you. The advice not to judge progress in the early weeks, and to look for the point where the thought no longer pulls at you, is this page's own.

**Plain words**

Stopping a behavior and losing the wish for it are two separate achievements. The verse says the wish can outlast the behavior. It does not say how long. Don't panic when you still feel the pull. The commentary suggests replacing the old taste with a higher one and not simply gritting your teeth.

**For example**

A man who has quit sugar for three months still dreams about biscuits. He reads this as a normal stage, not a failure, and keeps feeding the new habit he chose to replace it.

> **Principle 270** — Practise collective responsibility

Gyekye's account of duty in African communitarian thought:

> "The duties, which some members of the community feel they owe others by reason of our common humanity and should demonstrate in practice, are such as the duty to help others in distress, the duty not to harm others, and so on. Duties to the community as a whole or to some members of the community would not derive from a social contract between individuals." (Gyekye, [Person and Community in African Thought](https://res.cloudinary.com/segl/image/upload/v1583436111/Gyekye-Communitarianism_ycw6vs.pdf))

The theory of the person behind it: communitarianism sees the human person as inherently communal, embedded in a context of social relationships and interdependence, never as an isolated atomic individual, and the community as a group of persons linked by interpersonal bonds with common interests, goals and values ([Gyekye, Person and Community in African Thought](https://res.cloudinary.com/segl/image/upload/v1583436111/Gyekye-Communitarianism_ycw6vs.pdf)). The Gikuyu formulation he quotes makes the point more plainly: nobody is an isolated individual, his uniqueness is a secondary fact about him, and first and foremost he is several people's relative and several people's contemporary ([Gyekye, quoting Kenyatta 1965](https://res.cloudinary.com/segl/image/upload/v1583436111/Gyekye-Communitarianism_ycw6vs.pdf)).

The responsibility follows the membership rather than a contract: you owe the community's welfare because you are inside it, not because you opted in. Gyekye notes that the communitarian theory gives priority to duties rather than rights, with the common good as the concern ([Gyekye](https://res.cloudinary.com/segl/image/upload/v1583436111/Gyekye-Communitarianism_ycw6vs.pdf)).

(Ubuntu philosophy; Gyekye, Tradition and Modernity.)

**Plain words**
A contract model of duty asks: what did I sign, and to whom? The communal model starts one step earlier: you were already somebody's relative, somebody's neighbour, somebody's contemporary before any paperwork existed. The duty to help someone in distress is not triggered by a transaction; it is standing, like a load-bearing wall in a house you live in.

*For example:*
Someone who sees the street's kids hanging around with nothing to do and organises the weekend football. The duty was standing before the job description was consulted.

> **Principle 53** — Learn to manage perfectionism: "good enough" enables action

Perfectionism — the belief that things must be done perfectly or not at all — is a major cause of procrastination. "Good enough" (satisficing) enables action. Done is better than perfect.

**Plain words** Perfectionism is procrastination in a tuxedo — "it must be perfect or not started" means it's never started. Good enough, shipped, beats perfect, imagined. The standard is holding you hostage; renegotiate.

*For example:* The business plan revised for the eleventh year and never launched — the plan was perfect, and perfectly useless. The person who launched a mediocre version is on version five by now.
## Psychology parallels

- **Perfectionism and procrastination**, perfectionism predicts procrastination (Steel, 2007; Sirois et al., 2017)
- **Satisficing vs maximising**, satisficing is associated with better well-being and more action (Schwartz et al., 2002)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The best is the enemy of the good** (Voltaire)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei: non-striving for perfection]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 9)
- **[[Imperfection as human|Imperfection as human]]** (Wabi-sabi, Japanese aesthetics)

## Practice

- Practise "good enough": set a standard that is adequate, not perfect. Start before you are ready. Done is better than perfect. Revise later.

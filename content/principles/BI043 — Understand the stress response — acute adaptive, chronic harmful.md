> **Principle 43** — Understand the stress response: acute adaptive, chronic harmful

The stress response (cortisol, adrenaline, sympathetic activation) is adaptive acutely: it mobilises resources for threat. Chronic stress is harmful: it dysregulates the system, impairs cognition, and damages health. The distinction is critical.

**Plain words** The stress system is a smoke detector: acute stress is adaptive — resources mobilised, threat met. Chronic stress is the detector shrieking for months — system dysregulated, everything downstream damaged. You need the detector; you don't need the permanent shriek.

*For example:* The sprint that sharpens you for a day versus the same alarm ringing for a year — the body doesn't distinguish; the bills differ. The chronic version is the one that costs.

- **[[Stress response (Lupien, McEwen)|Stress response (Lupien, McEwen)]]**, acute stress is adaptive; chronic stress is harmful (Lupien et al., 2009; McEwen, 2007)
- **Allostatic load**, chronic stress accumulates "wear and tear" on the body and brain (McEwen, 2007; McEwen & Wingfield, 2003)

## Practice

- Recognise the difference: acute stress (before a performance, during a challenge) is adaptive and useful. Chronic stress (persistent, unrelenting) is harmful. Address chronic stress; do not pathologise acute stress.

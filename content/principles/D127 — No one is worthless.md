> **Principle 127** — No one is worthless

Chapter 27 of the Daodejing is about the sage's economy of people. The chapter opens with skill: the skilful traveller leaves no traces, the skilful speaker says nothing that can be blamed. Then it turns the skill social: the sage is "always skilful at saving men, and so he does not cast away any man".

> In the same way the sage is always skilful at saving men, and so he does not
> cast away any man; he is always skilful at saving things, and so he does not
> cast away anything. This is called 'Hiding the light of his procedure.'
>
> (Daodejing 27, Legge)

The chapter then makes the relationship reciprocal rather than charitable: "the man of skill is a master (to be looked up to) by him who has not the skill; and he who has not the skill is the helper of (the reputation of) him who has the skill." The unskilled are not the skilled's burden; they are part of what skill is for.

(DDJ 27.)

**Plain words**
The chapter refuses two moves at once: writing someone off, and treating them as a charity case. Skill and lack of skill need each other; the master needs the learner to be a master of anything. Ask of the difficult person what they are in a position to teach you, or to make you practise.

*For example:* The colleague whose mistakes taught you more than the manuals — the 'bad' one was curriculum in disguise.

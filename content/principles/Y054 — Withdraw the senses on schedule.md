> **Principle 54** — Withdraw the senses on schedule

Sutra 2.54 defines pratyahara by imitation: "The withdrawal of the senses is as it were the imitation of the mind-stuff itself on the part of the organs by disjoining themselves from their objects" (trans. Woods, [p. 197](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft)). Vyasa's simile gives the definition its image:

> Just as when the king-bee flies up, the bees fly up after him; and when he settles down, they settle down after him. So when the mind-stuff is restricted, the organs are restricted. This then is the withdrawal of the senses.

(Vyasa on Yoga Sutra 2.54, trans. Woods, [p. 197](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The fruit, sutra 2.55: "As a result of this [withdrawal] there is complete mastery of the organs" (p. 198). Vyasa reports the schools' disagreement about what that mastery is, from lack of desire for objects to Jaigisavya's refusal to perceive, and notes that with the mind-stuff restricted the organs follow "and do not... require any further aid" (p. 198).

### What the principle adds

"On schedule" is the principle's addition: the sutras give the definition, the simile and the fruit, but set no timetable. The withdrawal-of-investment reading and the daily sense-fast are likewise the principle's modern reading of disjunction from objects.

**Plain words** The definition runs through the mind, not the eyes. The senses copy the mind-stuff the way bees copy the queen: when the mind pulls in, they pull in, with no separate effort needed. The work is therefore upstream, on where the mind is. The schedule is the principle's own point, and it is practical: a withdrawal you choose at a set time is the same one collapse would otherwise enforce for you.

*For example:* Imagined: the evening ends either with the phone dropping from a numb hand at 1:40 a.m., or with a 10 p.m. slot, lights low, screens parked, chosen while the mind still has a vote. The second is the same withdrawal the first one forces.

(Yoga Sutra 2.54-2.55 with Vyasa's Bhasya, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 197-198.)

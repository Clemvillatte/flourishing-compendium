> **Principle 22** — Verify in experience: test beliefs by living them

In "Is Life Worth Living?", James argues that trust can help shape the outcome in the case he is discussing.

> You make one or the other of two possible universes true by your trust or mistrust,—both universes having been only maybes, in this particular, before you contributed your act.
>
> (William James, "Is Life Worth Living?")

His closing instruction carries the same structure: "These, then, are my last words to you: Be not afraid of life. Believe that life is worth living, and your belief will help create the fact." The compendium offers a separate practical exercise: test a limited belief about an action by attempting it and describing what happened.

(James, "Is Life Worth Living?", in The Will to Believe and Other Essays in Popular Philosophy, 1896.)

**Plain words**
Choose a specific, ordinary action you doubt you can perform. Decide what would count as evidence, try a manageable version, and note both what happened and what remains uncertain.

*For example:* Imagine offering a short toast to investigate a belief that you cannot speak before a group. Afterwards, record what happened.

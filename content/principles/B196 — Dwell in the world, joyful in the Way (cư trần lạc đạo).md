> **Principle 196** — Dwell in the world, joyful in the Way (cư trần lạc đạo)

The Trúc Lâm school's founding gesture is in the closing verse of Trần Nhân Tông's Cư Trần Lạc Đạo Phú, a hymn written by a king who became the tradition's first patriarch: dwell in the world, keep the joy of the Way, and let things follow their conditions. The verse's instructions are domestic: "When hungry then eat! Where tired, so sleep then!"

> Dwelling in the world with the Joy of Practicing the Dharma,
> let it flow with its conditions.
> When hungry then eat! Where tired, so sleep then!
> Treasures in the home, suspending the search,
> Facing situation with no mind! What inquiring Thiền for!
>
> (Trần Nhân Tông, Cư Trần Lạc Đạo Phú, concluding verse)

The teaching that practice is compatible with a full life is not a modern softening; it is the founding text. The verse even closes by dissolving the special occasion: with the treasure already "in the home" and the mind not stuck on circumstances, "What inquiring Thiền for!" The compendium's reading is that the duties are not obstacles arranged around a hidden practice; they are where the practice lives.

(Trần Nhân Tông, Trúc Lâm tradition.)

**Plain words**
The old choice between the monastery and the kitchen is dissolved rather than decided: the verse puts eating and sleeping inside the Way. Ask of your own week where you are postponing practice until conditions improve. The verse's answer is that the conditions are the material.

*For example:* The parent practicing while packing lunchboxes — the kitchen, the deadlines, the noise: all of it, the field. Nothing to escape.

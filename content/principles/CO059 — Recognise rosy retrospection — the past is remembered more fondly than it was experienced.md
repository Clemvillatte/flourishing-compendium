> **Principle 59** — Recognise rosy retrospection: the past is remembered more fondly than it was experienced

Mitchell, Thompson, Peterson and Cronk followed people through a trip to Europe, a Thanksgiving vacation and a 3-week bicycle trip in California, asking them before, during and after. All three studies supported the finding "that people's expectations of personal events are more positive than their actual experience during the event itself, and their subsequent recollection of that event is more positive than the actual experience" ([Mitchell et al., 1997, J Exp Soc Psychol 33(4), abstract](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1997/temporal-adjustments-in-the-evaluation-of-events-the-rosy/)).

The authors tie the gap to what happens during the event: more negative thoughts, which "seem to be caused by distractions, disappointment, and a less positive view of the self." They add that these effects "are short-lived," because "within days after the event, people have much more positive evaluations of the event." The studies concern trips and holidays of a few weeks, so the finding does not show that every old season of life is remembered too warmly. For the long-past "good old days," the principle is an extension of the finding.

**Plain words** The memory of an experience tends to come out warmer than the experience was. In these studies the evaluation turned much warmer within days. Enjoy the warm picture, and when you compare it with the present, remember that you are comparing a lived day with an edited memory.

*For example:* Across these studies, people judged their trips more kindly in recollection than they had while living them. Before you decide that things were better under the old team, find something you wrote at the time, such as an email or a diary line, and see what you said then.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Fading affect bias|Fading affect bias]]**, negative affect fades faster than positive affect in memory (Walker et al., 2003)

## Practice

- When comparing the present unfavourably to the past, remember that the past is being recalled through rosy filters. The present has the disadvantage of being fully experienced; the past has the advantage of being selectively remembered.

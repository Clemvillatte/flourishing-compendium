> **Principle 59** — Recognise rosy retrospection: the past is remembered more fondly than it was experienced

People tend to remember past events as more positive than they actually experienced them. This is why "the good old days" feel better than they were, and why nostalgia can distort.

**Plain words** Rosy retrospection: people remember past events as more positive than they experienced them. "The good old days" feel better than they were. Nostalgia is a warm, distorting lens — enjoy it, but don't let it grade your current life against an edited past.

*For example:* The person who "wants the old team back" — the old team that they spent the entire year complaining about — and the photo evidence of the complaints. The past got re-mastered. Enjoy the highlight reel; just don't resign your current job over it.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Rosy retrospection|Rosy retrospection]]**, past experiences are remembered as more positive than they were experienced (Mitchell et al., 1997; Wilson & Ross, 2003)
- **[[Fading affect bias|Fading affect bias]]**, negative affect fades faster than positive affect in memory (Walker et al., 2003)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Nostalgia as distortion** (Nietzsche, Untimely Meditations)
- **[[The examined past|The examined past]]** (Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations)

## Practice

- When comparing the present unfavourably to the past, remember that the past is being recalled through rosy filters. The present has the disadvantage of being fully experienced; the past has the advantage of being selectively remembered.

> **Principle 114** — Match your stance to the other's condition

Sutra 1.33 gives four attitudes and says which kind of person each is for:

> By the cultivation of friendliness towards happiness and compassion towards pain and joy towards merit and indifference towards demerit [the yogin should attain] the undisturbed calm of the mind-stuff.

(Yoga Sutras 1.33, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, p. 71, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary spells it out as friendliness "towards all living beings that have reached the experience of happiness", compassion "towards those in pain", joy "towards those whose character is meritorious" and indifference "towards those whose character is demeritorious". Each attitude cuts off one specific fault. The gloss says friendliness ends envy, compassion ends "a desire to injure others", joy ends jealousy and indifference ends wrath. The Gita's portrait of the person dear to Krishna also lists the friendly and the compassionate (BG 12.13-14, [Mukundananda](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/12/verse/13/)).

Two things on this page go beyond the text. The sutra's fourth case is the person of demerit, not the hostile one, so using indifference on someone who is hostile to you is this principle's extension. And the sutra aims at the practitioner's own calm. Its point is not to manage the other person, though the same attitude may well help the relationship.

**Plain words**

When you meet someone, the first thing to check is how they are doing. Someone doing well is easy to envy, someone in pain easy to avoid, someone good easy to compete with, someone harmful easy to hate. The sutra gives one steadier response for each case, so your mood stops choosing for you.

**For example**

At a family reunion, the cousin with the dream job gets warmth instead of a quiet comparison, and the cousin in a rough year gets your attention. The generous one gets your delight. The one who likes to provoke gets calm and no argument, which is this page's extension of the sutra.

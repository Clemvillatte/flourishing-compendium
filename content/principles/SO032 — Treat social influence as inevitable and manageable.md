> **Principle 32** — Treat social influence as inevitable and manageable

Treat social influence — conformity, norm-following, obedience, imitation — as inevitable and manageable. Solomon Asch's conformity experiments and Stanley Milgram's obedience experiments showed how powerful social influence is; the adaptive stance is not to deny influence but to manage it deliberately (see principle 27).

**Plain words** Social influence is inevitable — conformity, norm-following, obedience run deep. The experiments are famous for a reason: the pull is strong. The stance isn't immunity; it's knowing when the current is steering.

*For example:* The meeting where everyone agrees and you agree too — then in the corridor, everyone privately disagrees with everyone. The current did that. Learn to notice the water you're swimming in.

## Practice

- In group settings, notice the pull to conform. Use norms deliberately: surround yourself with people whose norms you want (see principle 27). In institutional design, use default options and social norms to shape behavior (see principle 56).

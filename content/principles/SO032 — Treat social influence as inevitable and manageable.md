> **Principle 32** — Treat social influence as inevitable and manageable

Asch drew this conclusion from the line-judgment experiments:

> Life in society requires consensus as an indispensable condition. But consensus, to be productive, requires that each individual contribute independently out of his experience and insight. When consensus comes under the dominance of conformity, the social process is polluted and the individual at the same time surrenders the powers on which his functioning as a feeling and thinking being depends.

(Asch, 1955, "Opinions and Social Pressure", Scientific American 193(5):31-35; [transcription](https://panarchy.org/asch/social.pressure.1955.html))

The article reports the asymmetry the principle statement is built on: alone, people matching lines made mistakes less than 1 per cent of the time; under group pressure, subjects accepted the majority's wrong judgments in 36.8 per cent of selections. The pull is measurable, which is the inevitable half of the principle. The manageable half is the article's own distinction: consensus is not what the article warns against; the warning targets conformity taking over the process.

**Plain words**
Social influence is inevitable: conformity, norm-following and obedience run deep. The experiments are famous for a reason: the pull is strong. The stance isn't immunity; it's knowing when the current is steering.

*For example:* Imagine the meeting where everyone agrees and you agree too, then in the corridor, everyone privately disagrees with everyone. The current did that.

> **Principle 97** — Treat each moment as fresh

Rogers describes openness to experience as a willingness to encounter feelings and situations without forcing them into a preconceived picture of oneself. His account includes fear, discouragement and pain as well as courage and tenderness. Freshness here means allowing the present to inform your understanding ([On Becoming a Person excerpt, “The Characteristics of the Process”](https://www.panarchy.org/rogers/person.html)).

> "I believe it would be evident that for the person who was fully open to his new experience, completely without defensiveness, each moment would be new." (Rogers, “Increasingly Existential Living,” in the opened excerpt.)

### Make room for what is here

This is Rogers's conditional description of fully open experiencing, not evidence that anyone can simply choose to be without defensiveness. Whitehead's account of new occasions is metaphysical ([Garland, “The Mystery of Creativity,” section 3](https://encyclopedia.whiteheadresearch.org/entries/thematic/metaphysics/the-mystery-of-creativity/)). The compendium does not take it to show that a change of outlook removes habit or constraint.

(Whitehead, Process and Reality, 1929; Rogers, On Becoming a Person, 1961.)

### Plain words

Before deciding that you already know how an encounter will go, ask what you are actually noticing. Include your discomfort. You need not pretend that the past never happened; the proposed practice is to leave room for information you have not considered.

### For example

Imagine expecting a colleague to dismiss your suggestion. At the next meeting, distinguish that expectation from the words the colleague actually uses. This hypothetical exercise illustrates openness without predicting that the colleague, or your feelings, will change.

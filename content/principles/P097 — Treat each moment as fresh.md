> **Principle 97** — Treat each moment as fresh

Every occasion is new: the past does not mechanically dictate the present. Meet this morning as this morning: habits inform you, they do not own you.

(Whitehead, Process and Reality, 1929; Rogers, On Becoming a Person, 1961.)

**Plain words** Every occasion is new — the past does not mechanically dictate the present. Meet this morning as this morning: habits inform you, they do not own you. The alarm's familiarity is a liar; today has never happened before, including to you.

*For example:* The hundredth Monday that is, in fact, not the hundredth Monday — the coffee is the same and nothing else is. Habits inform; they don't dictate. The distinction is where freedom lives.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Decentering|Decentering]]**, seeing each moment as new rather than as confirmation (Bernstein et al., 2015)
- **[[Mindful attention|Mindful attention to novel features]]**, noticing new details counteracts habituation (Langer, 1989)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Perpetual perishing and novelty** (Whitehead, Process and Reality)
- **[[Beginner's mind|Beginner's mind]]** (Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind)

> **Principle 43** — Recognise the role of reward in habit formation: immediate rewards work

Habits are reinforced by immediate rewards. Delayed rewards (long-term health) do not reinforce habits; immediate rewards (feeling good, enjoyment) do. Building in immediate reward is a habit-formation technique.

**Plain words** Habits are paid in immediate rewards — "long-term health" doesn't reinforce anything today. Attach something instantly good to the behaviour, or let the behaviour itself become good. Future benefits don't train; present ones do.

*For example:* The smoothie after the workout — the workout was never going to be rewarded by "cardiovascular health in ten years." The smoothie pays today, so today shows up.
## Psychology parallels

- **Immediate reward and habit formation**, habits are reinforced by immediate rewards, not delayed (Lally et al., 2010; Gardner, 2015)
- **Reward timing**, immediate rewards are more effective for habit formation than delayed (Lally et al., 2010; Wood & Neal, 2007)

## Practice

- Build in immediate rewards for desired behaviours: enjoy the walk, savour the healthy meal, celebrate the small win. Do not rely on delayed rewards; make the behaviour immediately rewarding.

> **Principle 65** — Engage in play behaviour: play is not frivolous

In a sample of 255 adults, playfulness showed robust positive relations with life satisfaction and an inclination toward enjoyable activities and an active way of life, with minor relations to physical fitness; an active way of life partially mediated the link to life satisfaction. As with all trait studies, the direction is not settled: playful people may live better, or living well may play.

The trial evidence goes one step further. In a randomised placebo-controlled online study, 533 participants spent a week on one of three nightly exercises: writing down three playful things from the day, using playfulness in a new situation, or counting playful moments. Participants reported at pretest, posttest and follow-ups after 2, 4 and 12 weeks.

> "All interventions increased expressions in all facets of playfulness, had short-term effects on well-being, and ameliorated depression." (Proyer, Gander, Brauer & Chick 2021, [Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32844597/))

**Plain words**
Playfulness is measurable and, within limits, trainable: a week of noticing your own playful moments moved the needle in the trial. The exercises are almost embarrassingly small (write down three playful things from today), which is the point: the behaviour does not require a personality transplant, just attention directed at what is already there.

*For example:* An imagined case: an adult who "doesn't play anymore" takes up the trial's smallest exercise: each night for a week, writing down three moments from the day that were playful, theirs or someone else's. What changes first in the trial is the counting, not the life.

## Practice

- Engage in play: games, humour, spontaneity, fun. Do not dismiss play as childish. It is a well-being behaviour. Schedule it.

> **Principle 65** — Engage in play behaviour: play is not frivolous

In a sample of 255 adults, playfulness showed robust positive relations with life satisfaction and an inclination toward enjoyable activities and an active way of life, with minor relations to physical fitness; an active way of life partially mediated the link to life satisfaction. This questionnaire study establishes an association rather than causation.

The intervention study goes one step further. In a randomised placebo-controlled online study, 533 participants were assigned to one of three one-week playfulness interventions ("three playful things", "using playfulness in a new way", "counting playfulness") or a placebo condition. Participants reported at pretest, posttest and follow-ups after 2, 4 and 12 weeks.

> "All interventions increased expressions in all facets of playfulness, had short-term effects on well-being, and ameliorated depression." (Proyer, Gander, Brauer & Chick 2021, [Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32844597/))

**Plain words**
The questionnaire study links playfulness with life satisfaction; the intervention study shows a week-long exercise can raise measured playfulness with short-term well-being effects. The observational finding is an association, and the trial reports short-term effects. The exercises are small enough to fit into a week, which is what the trial actually tested.

*For example:* In an imagined example, an adult makes time for a game they enjoy. This is a suggested activity, not a reproduction of the trial protocol.

## Practice

- Engage in play: games, humour, spontaneity, fun. Do not dismiss play as childish. It is a well-being behaviour. Schedule it.

> **Principle 65** — Engage in play behaviour: play is not frivolous

Play behaviour — games, humour, spontaneity, fun — is associated with positive affect, creativity, social bonding, and well-being. It is a specific behavioural practice often neglected in adulthood.

**Plain words** Play is not frivolous — it's a biological behaviour with bonding, creativity, and stress benefits. Adults don't outgrow the need; they outgrow the permission. Games, silliness, competition for nothing — required.

*For example:* The two departments that hate each other until the silly bowling night — nothing about the org chart changed, but the laughter did. Play is diplomacy for humans.
## Psychology parallels

- **Play and well-being**, play is associated with positive affect and creativity (Brown, 2009; Csikszentmihalyi, 1990)
- **Adult play and flourishing**, play in adulthood predicts well-being (Brown, 2009; Proyer, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Homo ludens|Homo ludens: man the player]]** (Huizinga, Homo Ludens)
- **The child as sage** (Zhuangzi; Jesus, Mark 10:15)
- **[[Joy|Joy and play as virtue]]** (Aristotle)

## Practice

- Engage in play: games, humour, spontaneity, fun. Do not dismiss play as childish. It is a well-being behaviour. Schedule it.

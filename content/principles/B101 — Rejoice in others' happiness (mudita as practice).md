> **Principle 101** — Rejoice in others' happiness (mudita as practice)

Mudita is the third of the brahmaviharas. The tradition's definition is direct: appreciative joy at the success and good fortune of others, with envy and jealousy named as its chief opponents (Eileen Siriwardhana, [Mudita](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/siriwardhana/bl093.html)). The same essay sets the difficulty honestly: mudita comes easily within the narrow circle of family and friends, while identifying with the joys and triumphs of outsiders requires deliberate effort.

> "Mudita means appreciative joy at the success and good fortune of others." (Eileen Siriwardhana, [Mudita](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/siriwardhana/bl093.html))

Śāntideva places rejoicing at the start of the bodhisattva path: "Joyfully I celebrate all the acts of virtue / That ease the pains of the lower realms, / And rejoice as well when those who suffer / Find themselves in states of happiness" (Bodhicaryāvatāra 3.1-3.2, trans. Adam Pearcey, [text](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/indian-masters/shantideva/bodhicharyavatara-3)). The verses make rejoicing an explicit act of celebration, not a feeling that arrives on its own.

Neither source presents mudita as a technique for suppressing envy. The exercise the compendium takes from them is gradual: begin with people you already find it easy to be happy for, then extend the same response to the cases where envy gets there first.

### Plain words

When someone shares good news, there is a moment before you answer where the choice happens. Envy turns the moment on yourself: why them and not me. The practice is to turn it on them: they got the job, the wedding, the house. Give the congratulation and then check whether you meant it. With a close friend it usually comes out true. The training happens where it does not: the colleague, the rival. Saying the words there is the exercise; the feeling can lag behind it.

### For example

Imagine a colleague you compete with gets the promotion you wanted. The exercise is to congratulate them specifically, naming what they did to earn it, and to notice what that costs. No particular feeling is guaranteed to follow.

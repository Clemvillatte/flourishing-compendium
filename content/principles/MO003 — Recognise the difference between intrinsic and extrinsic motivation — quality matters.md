> **Principle 3** — Recognise the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation: quality matters

Motivation is not just about quantity (more vs less); it is about quality. Intrinsic motivation (doing something for its own sake, for interest or value) and extrinsic motivation (doing something for external reward or to avoid punishment) have different consequences. Intrinsic motivation is associated with better performance, persistence, and well-being.

**Plain words** Motivation has a quality, not just a quantity: doing it for its own sake versus doing it for reward, image, or fear. Both get you moving, but only one survives the reward disappearing. Check what's driving before you floor it.

*For example:* The student who loves the subject and the one who loves the grade — both studying at midnight. Only one still opens the book when the curriculum ends.
## Psychology parallels

- **Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation (SDT)**, intrinsic motivation is autonomous; extrinsic can be controlled (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Intrinsic motivation predicts better outcomes**, intrinsic motivation is associated with better performance, persistence, and well-being (Cerasoli et al., 2014; Vansteenkiste et al., 2006)
- **Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation (overjustification effect)**, external rewards can reduce intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks (Deci et al., 1999; Hennessey & Amabile, 2010)

## Practice

- Notice whether your motivation is intrinsic (interest, value, enjoyment) or extrinsic (reward, avoidance, pressure). When possible, connect external goals to internal values. Do not rely purely on external rewards.

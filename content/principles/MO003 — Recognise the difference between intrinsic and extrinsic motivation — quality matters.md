> **Principle 3** — Recognise the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation: quality matters

Ryan and Deci's definitions: "The term extrinsic motivation refers to the performance of an activity in order to attain some separable outcome and, thus, contrasts with intrinsic motivation, which refers to doing an activity for the inherent satisfaction of the activity itself." The same review adds that "threats, deadlines, directives, pressured evaluations, and imposed goals diminish intrinsic motivation because, like tangible rewards, they conduce toward an external perceived locus of causality."

> "As predicted, engagement-contingent, completion-contingent, and performance-contingent rewards significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = -0.40, -0.36, and -0.28, respectively), as did all rewards, all tangible rewards, and all expected rewards." (Deci et al. 1999, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/))

The compendium applies the finding with its boundary: the effect concerns expected tangible rewards, and the same meta-analysis found positive feedback enhanced motivation. The quality question for any drive is which side of the definition it lives on.

**Plain words**
Two engines look identical from outside: same midnight, same book. One is reading for the grade, one for the subject, and the definitions differ in what happens when the reward stops. The meta-analytic finding is specific: expected tangible rewards contingent on performance undermined free-choice motivation in these studies. Positive feedback did the opposite. Ask which kind of fuel a given commitment runs on.

*For example:* Suppose two students studying at midnight, one who loves the subject and one who loves the grade. As a modern application, the free-choice measure is the diagnostic: what continues when the contingency ends.

## Psychology parallels

- **Intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation (SDT)**, intrinsic motivation is autonomous; extrinsic can be controlled (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Intrinsic motivation predicts better outcomes**, intrinsic motivation is associated with better performance, persistence, and well-being (Cerasoli et al., 2014; Vansteenkiste et al., 2006)
- **Extrinsic rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation (overjustification effect)**, external rewards can reduce intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks (Deci et al., 1999; Hennessey & Amabile, 2010)

## Practice

- Notice whether your motivation is intrinsic (interest, value, enjoyment) or extrinsic (reward, avoidance, pressure). When possible, connect external goals to internal values. Do not rely purely on external rewards.

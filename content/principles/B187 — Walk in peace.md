> **Principle 187** — Walk in peace

Thich Nhat Hanh's Plum Village tradition teaches walking meditation as the ordinary walk made aware. His poem on walking states this principle in its own words: the peace is not at the end of the walk. (TNH, "Walking Meditation".)

> "Then we learn / that there is no peace walk; / that peace is the walk; / that there is no happiness walk; / that happiness is the walk." (TNH, ["Walking Meditation", from Call me by My True Names](https://plumvillage.org/articles/walking-meditation-a-poem-by-thich-nhat-hanh))

> "Walking in meditation means to walk in such a way that we know we are walking. We walk leisurely, enjoying every step. We become aware of the contact of our feet with the ground, and the flow of our breathing." ([Plum Village, The Art of Mindful Living](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/mindfulness-practice))

**Plain words**
Take a walk you already take every day, like the corridor to the printer or the path from the car. Walk it once with your attention on the feet touching the ground and the breath moving. That is the whole exercise. The point is not the destination: if the manner of walking is the peace, the corridor counts as much as a mountain path.

*For example:* The eighty meters from the car to the office door, walked with attention to the feet and the breath, is walked in peace. The same eighty meters rehearsing tomorrow's argument is walked somewhere else.

> **Principle 34** — Recognise that self-relationship is the foundation of all relationships

How you relate to yourself — with kindness or cruelty, acceptance or rejection — is the template for how you relate to others. [[Self-compassion|Self-compassion]] is associated with better relationships; self-criticism is associated with worse.

**Plain words** How you relate to yourself — kindness or cruelty, acceptance or rejection — is the template for how you relate to others. Self-compassion predicts better relationships; self-criticism leaks outward as criticism. The self-relationship is the first relationship; it trains all the others.

*For example:* The person who is mysteriously harsh with their team's errors — and the discovery that the voice is identical to the one they use on themselves, at the same volume. The team got the self-treatment, exported. The fix was upstream.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-compassion|Self-compassion]] and relationship quality**, self-compassionate people have better relationships (Neff & Beretvas, 2013; Yarnell & Neff, 2013)
- **Self-criticism damages relationships**, self-critical people project criticism onto others (Shahar et al., 2012; Gilbert, 2009)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Love thy neighbour as thyself ([[Amor sui (right self-love)|implies right self-love]])** (Jewish/Christian tradition, Leviticus 19:18)
- **[[Ubuntu|Ubuntu: I am because we are]]** (African Philosophy)
- **[[The inner citadel|The inner citadel enables outer generosity]]** (Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations)

## Practice

- Recognise that your relationship with yourself is the foundation of your relationships with others. Work on self-compassion not only for yourself but for the sake of everyone you relate to.

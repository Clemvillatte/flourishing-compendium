> **Principle 87** — Accept that blessedness is virtue itself, not its reward

The final proposition's scholium demolishes the reward structure of ordinary religion: blessedness is not the payment for virtue, it is virtue itself; there is no prize after. The moral life pays as it goes, in the coin of increased power of acting, and the expectation of a later settlement is what allows people to defer living well indefinitely. Practically, run the accounts in the present tense: does this way of being pay today, in strength, in joy, in capacity? If not, no later reward is coming, because there is no later.

(Ethics V, P42 with scholium.)

**Plain words** Blessedness is not the payment for virtue, it is virtue itself — there is no prize after. The moral life pays as it goes, in the coin of its own activity. Demolish the reward structure in your head: the kingdom of heaven is the doing.

*For example:* The person who does the good year waiting for the payoff — and the one who notices the good year was the payoff: the clarity, the strength, the company. Same conduct; one of them is still waiting, and has been for decades.

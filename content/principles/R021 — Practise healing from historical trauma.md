> **Principle 283** — Practise healing from historical trauma

Atkinson's term for the aftermath is trauma trails. In her study of transgenerational trauma in Indigenous Australia, the trails of the Stolen Generations "run across country and generations from original locations of violence as people moved away from the places of pain. These trauma trails carried fragmented, fractured people and families" (Atkinson, 2002, p. 88, as quoted in [Barney and Mackinlay, 2010](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/m/mp/9460447.0004.202/--singing-trauma-trails-songs-of-the-stolen-generations?rgn=main;view=fulltext)). Left unhealed, colonial trauma compounds, "becoming cumulative in its impacts on individuals, families, and indeed whole communities and societies" (Atkinson, 2002, p. 24, as quoted in Barney and Mackinlay, 2010).

The national SEWB framework puts the same point as policy:

> "It must be recognised that the experiences of trauma and loss, present since European invasion, are a direct outcome of the disruption to cultural wellbeing. Trauma and loss of this magnitude continue to have intergenerational effects." (SEWB Framework, as quoted in [Gupta et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7596979/))

Its guiding principles include "the impact of history in trauma and loss", and reviews of Aboriginal healing programmes list among their critical elements that they "understand the impact of colonisation and transgenerational trauma and grief", "combine Western methodologies with Aboriginal healing" and "build individual, family and community capacity".

Healing, in Atkinson's account, is the other half of the same trails: people telling and listening to "stories of pain and stories of healing" (Atkinson, 2002, p. 96, as quoted in Barney and Mackinlay, 2010).

(Atkinson, 2002; Aboriginal SEWB framework.)

**Plain words** A person's distress may have a history reaching beyond their own lifetime. The programmes described here consider family, community and culture as well as the individual; they do not treat healing as only an individual task.

*For example:* Imagine a family attending a healing programme together, three generations in the same room, hearing the stories the grandparents carry.

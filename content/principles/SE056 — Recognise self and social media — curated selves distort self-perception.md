> **Principle 56** — Recognise self and social media: curated selves distort self-perception

Social media presents curated, edited versions of self. Comparing your actual self to others' curated selves distorts self-evaluation and reduces well-being. Recognising this is a self-protective skill.

**Plain words** Social media shows you everyone's edited highlight reel and your own unedited dailies. Comparing those two feeds isn't information — it's distortion with a like button. Your actual life is not worse than everyone else's; everyone else's actual life is just not the one being posted.

*For example:* The new parent scrolling other parents' beach photos at 3 a.m. during a feeding isn't seeing other people's 3 a.m. feedings. She's comparing her worst hour to everyone else's best minute. The feed is real; the comparison is fiction.
## Psychology parallels

- **Social media and self-comparison**, exposure to curated presentations reduces self-esteem and increases negative comparison (Vogel et al., 2014; Fardouly et al., 2015)
- **Passive social media use is particularly harmful**, passive scrolling (not posting) is associated with worse well-being (Verduyn et al., 2015; Kross et al., 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Appearance vs reality** (Plato, Republic)
- **The mask and the face** (Nietzsche, Beyond Good and Evil)
- **[[Simplicity|Simplicity and authenticity]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 19)

## Practice

- Recognise social media selves as curated. Do not compare your actual self to others' edited presentations. Reduce passive scrolling. Use social media actively (connect, create) rather than passively (scroll, compare).

> **Principle 363** — Practise relational autonomy

Recognise that your capacity for self-governance is constituted through your relationships with others. Autonomy is not independence from others but the capacity to reflect on, endorse, and live in accordance with your values within a web of relationships. Seek relationships that support your autonomy, and be a person who supports the autonomy of others.

(Nedelsky, 1989, "Reconceiving Autonomy," Yale Journal of Law and Feminism, 1(1), 7-36; Mackenzie and Oshana, 2007, "Relational Autonomy," in Autonomy, Self, and Social Identity.)

**Plain words** Autonomy isn't independence from people — it's the capacity to reflect on, endorse, and live by your own values inside your web of relationships. So build relationships that support your self-governance, and be that support for others. The lone wolf is just a wolf.

*For example:* The young adult whose family means well and swallows every decision — and the mentor who asks "and what do you want?" — one relationship shrinks autonomy, one builds it.

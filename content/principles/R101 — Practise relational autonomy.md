> **Principle 363** — Practise relational autonomy

Recognise that your capacity for self-governance is constituted through your relationships with others. Autonomy is not independence from others but the capacity to reflect on, endorse, and live in accordance with your values within a web of relationships. Seek relationships that support your autonomy, and be a person who supports the autonomy of others.

(Nedelsky, 1989, "Reconceiving Autonomy," Yale Journal of Law and Feminism, 1(1), 7-36; Mackenzie and Oshana, 2007, "Relational Autonomy," in Autonomy, Self, and Social Identity.)

> "We come into being in a social context that is literally constitutive of us." (Nedelsky, "Reconceiving Autonomy," [Yale Journal of Law and Feminism 1(1), 1989, p. 8](https://openyls.law.yale.edu/bitstreams/b6f87a9b-ef6e-426c-ace2-db9baf152a19/download))

Nedelsky's target is the liberal picture of self-made individuals. She takes from the tradition the core definition: the literal meaning of autonomy is to be "governed by one's own law," and to become autonomous is to come to be able to find and live in accordance with one's own law. Her move is to relocate that capacity. Some of our most essential characteristics, such as the capacity for language and the conceptual framework through which we see the world, are not made by us but given to us through interactions with others; there are, she writes, no human beings in the absence of relations. The compendium recommends seeking relationships that support self-governance and supporting the self-governance of others.

**Plain words**
Depending on other people does not settle whether you can make decisions for yourself. Nedelsky asks how social relationships can coexist with self-determination. For the compendium's exercise, choose one decision and ask who helps you understand the options, who has a say, and whether you can explain what you want.

*For example:* Imagine a young adult preparing for a family discussion about where to study. They write down their preferred option and reasons, then bring these to the conversation.

> **Principle 21** — Learn to attend to the positive deliberately: attention training for well-being

Just as attention can be biased toward threat and negativity, it can be trained toward the positive. This is not denial; it is correcting a bias. Positive attention training improves mood and well-being.

**Plain words** What you notice is trainable in both directions — your feed can train you toward threat, or you can deliberately practice noticing the good. Not positive thinking; attention training. The beam aims where it's been drilled.

*For example:* Three entries a night in a small notebook — one good moment each — and within weeks you start catching them live. You didn't change your life; you changed your scan.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Positive attention training / attentional deployment toward positive stimuli|Positive attention training / attentional deployment toward positive stimuli]]**, training attention toward positive information improves mood and reduces depressive symptoms (Wadlinger & Isaacowitz, 2011; Johnson et al., 2009)
- **Gaze training toward positive faces reduces depression**, training depressed individuals to attend to positive faces reduces depressive symptoms (Duque & Vázquez, 2015; Sanchez et al., 2016)

## Practice

- Deliberately attend to positive aspects of experience. When you notice something good, linger on it. Practise gratitude as an attentional exercise: notice what is working, what is beautiful, what you appreciate.

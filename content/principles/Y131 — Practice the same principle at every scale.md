> **Principle 131** — Practice the same principle at every scale

Two passages sit behind this principle, and neither says what the principle claims on its own. In the Yoga Sutras, three stages of inner training are brought together:

> The three in one are constraint.

(Yoga Sutras 3.4, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

Vyasa explains: "When having a single object the three means are called constraint." The three are fixed-attention, contemplation and concentration, and the technical term (samyama) is introduced so that the text need not name them one by one.

The Gita carries an equal-vision teaching into ordinary life. Verse 6.29 describes yogis who "see with equal eye, all living beings in God and God in all living beings" ([BG 6.29](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/29/)). Verse 6.31 says that this yogi "dwells only in Me, though engaged in all kinds of activities" ([BG 6.31](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/31/)). Verse 6.32 adds that the perfect yogi responds "to the joys and sorrows of others as if they were their own" ([BG 6.32](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/32/)).

The claim that breath training and the shape of a life are one operation at two scales, "bind, steady, release the claim", is this page's own synthesis. So is the day-per-month exercise. No source opened here states the scale idea.

**Plain words**

The texts give you a repeated pattern on one object, and they give you a way of living that stays steady while busy. Joining the two is a suggestion to test.

**For example**

On the first Saturday of the month, a woman runs the whole day like a sitting. She starts with a stated purpose, comes back to it when she drifts into her phone, and ends by writing down what she'd give away from it. She finds the evening review harder than the morning start.

> **Principle 53** — Practice recognizing the kindness you have received

The passage begins with a teaching attributed to Bo-do-wa:

> "Bo-do-wa (Po-to-ba) said that after you have recognized that all living beings are your mothers, you will quickly remember their kindness if at first you cultivate a remembrance of your mother's kindness in this lifetime." (Tsongkhapa, Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path, vol. 2, "Cultivating a remembrance of their kindness")

The prescribed contemplation runs through maternal care: she "protected you from all harm and provided you all benefit and happiness"; she gave "food and drink" when you were hungry and "clothes" when you were cold; and "If you suffered from illness, pain, or the threat of death, your mother made the choice from the depths of her heart that she would rather be sick than you be sick, she would rather be in pain than you be in pain, she would rather die than you die". The text then extends the same remembrance outward: friends, then neutral persons, then enemies, then all beings in the ten directions, "gradually and with increasing extensiveness". ([Great Treatise vol. 2 PDF](https://www.theosophy-ult.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Vol-2-Lamrim-on-Path-to-Enlightenment-Tsong-kha-pa.pdf))

**Plain words**
The passage asks for specific remembering rather than general appreciation: what was done for you, by whom, and at what cost. The text supplies one person and her ordinary care as the material. The generalization outward is part of the instruction, not an afterthought.

For example: Tracing how you came to cook: your grandmother, the market vendor, the farmer, the person who wrote the recipe.

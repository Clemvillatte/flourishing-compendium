> **Principle 58** — Learn somatic experiencing: working with activation in the body

Somatic Experiencing (SE) is a trauma therapy that, in the words of its developers, "emphasizes guiding the client's attention to interoceptive, kinesthetic, and proprioceptive experience". Payne, Levine and Crane-Godreau write that "SE claims that this style of inner attention ... can lead to the resolution of symptoms resulting from chronic and traumatic stress", through "the completion of thwarted, biologically based, self-protective and defensive responses, and the discharge and regulation of excess autonomic arousal" (Payne et al. 2015, Front Psychol 6, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25699005/)). That is a theory, presented through a composite case study, not a test of the therapy. Levine is a co-author of the paper.

Outcome evidence is early. In the first randomised trial, 63 people with PTSD were assigned to 15 SE sessions or a waitlist, and the authors reported large effects on symptoms, with the caveat that "further research is needed to understand who shall benefit most" (Brom et al. 2017, J Trauma Stress 30(3):304-312, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28585761/)). A scoping review of 16 studies found "preliminary evidence for positive effects of SE on PTSD-related symptoms" but said the "overall study quality is mixed" and called for "more support from unbiased RCT-research" (Kuhfuß et al. 2021, Eur J Psychotraumatol 12(1):1929023, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34290845/)).

**Plain words**
Somatic Experiencing asks you to pay attention to bodily sensations such as trembling, warmth or breath while a therapist guides you through a stress response. Its founders say this helps the body finish defensive reactions that were interrupted. One small trial and a review of mixed-quality studies are encouraging, but the evidence is too thin to call it proven. If you are curious, ask a qualified clinician how it compares with therapies that have more evidence behind them.

*For example:* A client notices trembling in a session and tells the therapist. They slow down and check how the client feels, instead of treating the shaking as proof that anything has been released. This is an imagined case.

## Practice

- Consider somatic experiencing: if trauma or chronic stress has left body-based patterns, this approach works with activation in the body (trembling, warmth, breath) as the body's own ways of settling. Seek a qualified practitioner.

> **Principle 19** — Treat sleep as practice, not absence

Sleep is classified as one of the five movements of mind, a state with its own content (the blankness and heaviness of deep sleep). The tradition treats it as material for awareness rather than a nightly shutdown. Practically, protect sleep as a pillar of practice, and take the first minute of waking as a deliberate transition rather than a launch.

(YS 1.10; BG 6.16-17 on moderation.)

**Plain words** Sleep is one of the five movements of mind — a state with its own content, material for awareness rather than a nightly shutdown. Protect it as a pillar of practice: the tradition treats the rested mind as the practicable mind. Not absence; terrain.

*For example:* The person who treats sleep as lost time and meditation as the fix — and finally accepts the tradition's ordering: protect the sleep first, and half the "unworkable mind" problems disappear without any other intervention.

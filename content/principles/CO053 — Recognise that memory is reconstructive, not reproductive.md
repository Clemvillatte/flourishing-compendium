> **Principle 53** — Recognise that memory is reconstructive, not reproductive

Memory does not play back a recording; it reconstructs the past each time it is retrieved. Each reconstruction is influenced by your current mood, beliefs, and context. This means memories are not fixed records but living documents.

**Plain words** Memory doesn't play back a recording — it reconstructs the past fresh at every retrieval, influenced by your current mood, beliefs, and context. Memories aren't fixed records; they're edited editions. Hold your certainties about the past accordingly.

*For example:* The sibling who remembers the holiday "where Dad was furious the whole time" — and the other who remembers the same week as "the one where we all laughed" — both editions are genuine, both edited by the current mood. The recording never existed; the reconstructions do.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Reconstructive memory|Reconstructive memory]]**, memory is rebuilt at retrieval, not replayed (Bartlett, 1932; Loftus & Palmer, 1974)
- **[[Memory reconsolidation|Memory reconsolidation]]**, retrieved memories become labile and can be re-encoded with different content or emotional tone (Nader et al., 2000; Schiller et al., 2010)

## Practice

- When you recall a past event, remember that you are reconstructing it, not replaying it. Your current state colours the memory. Ask: "how might I have seen this differently at a different time?"

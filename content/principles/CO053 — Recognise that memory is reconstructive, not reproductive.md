> **Principle 53** — Recognise that memory is reconstructive, not reproductive

Bartlett put the view in plain terms in 1932:

> "Remembering is not the re-excitation of innumerable fixed, lifeless and fragmentary traces. It is an imaginative reconstruction, or construction, built out of the relation of our attitude towards a whole active mass of organised past reactions or experience, and to a little outstanding detail which commonly appears in image or in language form. It is thus hardly ever really exact." (Bartlett, 1932, [Remembering](https://archive.org/details/Bartlett1932Remembering), ch. 10, section 7, "A Summary")

His evidence came from asking people to retell a Native American folk tale, "The War of the Ghosts". In the original, two young men "went down to the river to hunt seals". One participant, retelling it after 20 hours, wrote: "Two men from Edulae went fishing." The seal hunt had become a fishing trip (Bartlett, 1932, ch. 5).

A modern review reaches a similar place from the opposite side. Schacter, Guerin and St. Jacques argue that several kinds of memory distortion, including imagination inflation and gist-based errors, "reflect adaptive cognitive processes that contribute to the efficient functioning of memory, but produce distortions as a consequence of doing so" (Schacter et al., 2011, abstract, [Trends in Cognitive Sciences 15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21908231/)). The review links these distortions to processes such as simulating future events.

**Plain words**
Each time you recall something, your mind rebuilds it from pieces, and fills the gaps with what makes sense to you now. The memory feels like a video, but it works more like a retelling. For anything important, such as a promise, an old quarrel or a diagnosis, check the written record, or ask someone who was there.

**For example**
Say you remember a friend saying, "I don't think you can do it." A message she sent that evening says, "I'm worried this is too much for you." The written version is the safer guide, and the gap between the two shows how much the retelling added.

## Practice

- When you recall a past event, remember that you are reconstructing it, not replaying it. Your current state colours the memory. Ask: "how might I have seen this differently at a different time?"

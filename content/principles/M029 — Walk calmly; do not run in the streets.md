> **Principle 29** — Walk calmly; do not run in the streets

The huehuetlatolli's counsel on bearing and movement: the well-raised person walks calmly, does not push, does not rush about, sits and rises with composure, and holds the body with dignity in public. The counsel reads as etiquette but functions as mood engineering: the body's pace sets the mind's pace, and the harried walk is a harried mind made visible. Practically, adopt the elders' walking practice for one week: walk at 80 percent of your usual pace in public, and note what happens to your inner weather.

(Florentine Codex, Book VI, the counsel on public bearing.)

**Plain words** Walk calmly, don't push, don't rush about, sit and rise with composure. It reads as etiquette but works as mood engineering: the body's pace sets the mind's pace, and the harried walk is a harried mind made visible. Set the body's speed, and the mind follows it home.

*For example:* The person who deliberately walks between meetings at a steady pace — same route, same minutes — and notices the afternoons stopped feeling like chases somewhere in the second week.

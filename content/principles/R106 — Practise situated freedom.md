> **Principle 368** — Practise situated freedom

> Thus I am absolutely free and absolutely responsible for my situation. But I am never free except in situation.

(Sartre, Being and Nothingness, trans. Hazel E. Barnes, Washington Square Press, 1992 edition, p. 653, as quoted by the Finnish thesis "Freedom as Self-determination: A More Moderate Reading of Early Sartre" at [Helda, University of Helsinki](https://helda.helsinki.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/da456c81-9afa-4efc-a7e6-d3f7c904a3f9/content). The book itself was not read in full. Page numbers differ between printings.)

The same thesis quotes the earlier statement of the paradox: "there is freedom only in a situation, and there is a situation only through freedom," and adds that "Human-reality everywhere encounters resistance and obstacles which it has not created, but these resistances and obstacles have meaning only in and through the free choice which human-reality is." A commentator on Sartre adds that "to be free does not mean to get what one wants but simply that one is responsible for what one wants" and that "freedom for Sartre does not mean success in one's projects, but only that one gets to choose one's projects" (Robert Bernasconi, How to Read Sartre, [PDF copy at archive.org](https://ia903208.us.archive.org/19/items/robert-bernasconi-how-to-read-sartre/Robert%20Bernasconi,%20How%20to%20Read%20Sartre.pdf), search excerpt).

So Sartre does teach that freedom is situated. The compendium's wording that you are "free to choose how to respond" is gentler than Sartre, for whom freedom reaches the ends one pursues and the meaning one gives to the situation, and for whom the "coefficient of adversity" of obstacles arises from one's own project. Heidegger's thrownness and Merleau-Ponty's account of freedom in Phenomenology of Perception were not opened, nor was Rotter (1966) on locus of control.

**Plain words** On Sartre's account, nobody is free in a vacuum. The obstacles and givens of your life are real, and they count as obstacles only in light of what you are trying to do. Freedom shows up as how you take up the situation you are in.

*For example (imagined):* A caregiver cannot change the diagnosis or the family's circumstances. What stays open is how they organise the year, what they decide matters in it, and how they treat each person.

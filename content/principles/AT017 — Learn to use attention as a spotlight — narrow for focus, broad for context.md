> **Principle 17** — Learn to use attention as a spotlight: narrow for focus, broad for context

Attention can be narrowed (spotlight on a specific detail) or broadened (floodlight on the whole scene). Each has appropriate uses. Narrow focus enables deep concentration; broad awareness enables contextual understanding and creativity.

**Plain words** Your attention has a zoom lens: narrow for the detail, wide for the scene. Problems come from being stuck at one focal length — gripped and blind to context, or broad and unable to land. Practice the zoom.

*For example:* The proofreader stuck in narrow mode misses that the document is the wrong one entirely. Zoom out, see the whole thing, zoom back in.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Spotlight vs floodlight attention|Spotlight vs floodlight attention]]**, attentional aperture can be narrowed or broadened with different consequences (Navon, 1977; Förster & Higgins, 2005)
- **Positive affect broadens the spotlight**, positive emotions widen the scope of attention (Fredrickson & Branigan, 2005; Rowe et al., 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean between narrow and broad]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Yin and yang|Yin-yang of attention]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 2)
- **Macro and micro vision** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)

## Practice

- Deliberately adjust your attentional aperture. When doing detailed work, narrow: focus on one element. When brainstorming or assessing context, broaden: take in the whole scene.

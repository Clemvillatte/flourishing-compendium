> **Principle 145** — Be truthful

The Cunda Kammaraputta Sutta (AN 10.176) describes skillful verbal action in four parts: abandoning false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech and idle chatter. The first part is a witness scene:

> If he doesn't know, he says, 'I don't know.' If he does know, he says, 'I know.' If he hasn't seen, he says, 'I haven't seen.' If he has seen, he says, 'I have seen.' Thus he doesn't consciously tell a lie for his own sake, for the sake of another, or for the sake of any reward. [...] He speaks the truth, holds to the truth, is firm, reliable, no deceiver of the world. ([AN 10.176, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an10/an10.176.than.html))

The standard is concrete. Saying "I don't know" counts as truthful speech, and so does stopping at what you saw. The sutta then lists divisive speech, harsh speech and idle chatter as separate things to abandon, so truthfulness is the first of four speech disciplines.

(AN 10.176, trans. Thanissaro.)

### Plain words

Say what you know as what you know, and say what you don't know as that. A lie leaves you remembering which version you told and to whom, and that is attention taken from everything else. The sutta's standard is small and doable: when asked, report what you saw, and admit what you didn't.

### For example

Two people make the same error at work. One hides it and spends weeks watching for signs of discovery. The other reports it the same day and has one uncomfortable conversation. This one is imagined.

> **Principle 145** — Be truthful

The Buddha's instruction on speech in the Cunda Kammaraputta Sutta is more detailed than a simple prohibition: one who is given to lying, the Buddha says, will be abandoned by the deities and will be seen through by the wise; but the honest person is described as "one who says what is factual, true, on time, and beneficial" ([AN 10.176, Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an10/an10.176.than.html)). Truthfulness here is not a rule about content; it is a practice of choosing what to say and when.

The Dhammapada's line is even more compressed: the wise one is described as one who speaks truthfully, avoids slander, does not utter harsh words, and does not babble ([Dhammapada 393, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.01.budd.html)). This is a discipline of four specific speech-habits, of which honesty is the first and the foundation of the other three — slander, harsh speech and babble all rely on some untruth to work.

> "One who speaks what is factual, true, on time, and beneficial — this is called one who is honest." (AN 10.176, paraphrased from Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an10/an10.176.than.html))

**Plain words** Every lie requires a maintenance crew, and the crew is you. A secret you keep costs attention every time the topic comes near; a lie requires you to remember which version you told, and to whom, and for how long. The confession is a one-time fee. The keeping is a subscription, and it bills you in the exact currency your practice needs. Deception does not merely be unethical; it fragments the mind that is trying to see clearly.

*For example:* Two people both make the same error at work. One conceals it and spends the following weeks monitoring conversations for signs of discovery; the energy is gone from everything else. The other reports it the same day, eats an uncomfortable conversation, and stops paying attention to it. Same error; only one of them still has a working week.

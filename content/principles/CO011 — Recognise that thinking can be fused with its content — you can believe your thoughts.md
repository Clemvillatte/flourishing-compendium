> **Principle 11** — Recognise that thinking can be fused with its content: you can believe your thoughts

Cognitive fusion is the state of being so caught up in a thought that you treat it as reality rather than as a mental event. Defusion is the capacity to see thoughts as thoughts — mental events, not truths. This is a core ACT skill and one of the most practically important cognitive capacities.

**Plain words** Cognitive fusion: being so caught in a thought that you treat it as reality rather than a mental event. Defusion: seeing thoughts as thoughts — mental events passing through. "I'm having the thought that I'll fail" is a different room than "I'll fail."

*For example:* The performer who believes the thought "I'll choke" is a prophecy — versus the one who hears it as a mental event, like a passing radio. Same thought, two relationships: one obeys it, one observes it. Only one of them performs.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Cognitive defusion|Cognitive defusion in ACT]]**, stepping back from thoughts and seeing them as mental events reduces their behavioural impact (Hayes et al., 2011; Gillanders et al., 2014)
- **Defusion techniques reduce distress and believability of negative thoughts**, even brief defusion exercises reduce the impact of self-critical thoughts (Masuda et al., 2004; Deacon et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The observing self|The observing self]]** (Deikman, The Observing Self)
- **Thoughts as clouds passing** (Buddhism, Vipassana)
- **[[Anatta|Anatta (non-identification with mental events)]]** (Buddhism, Anatta-lakkhana Sutta)

## Practice

- When you notice "I'm a failure," try adding "I'm having the thought that I'm a failure." This small linguistic shift creates distance between you and the thought.

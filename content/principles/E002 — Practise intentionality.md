> **Principle 394** — Practise intentionality

The doctrine the principle practises is Brentano's, from Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint:

> Every mental phenomenon includes something as object within itself, although they do not all do so in the same way. In presentation something is presented, in judgement something is affirmed or denied, in love loved, in hate hated, in desire desired and so on. ... We could, therefore, define mental phenomena by saying that they are those phenomena which contain an object intentionally within themselves. (Brentano, Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint, [as quoted in Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intentionality))

The application is the page's question: since every act of mind aims at an object, the act is inspectable by its aim. The anxious example below runs the question.

(Husserl, 1900-1901/2001, Logical Investigations, Investigation V; Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §84; Brentano, 1874/1995, Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint.)

**Plain words**
Consciousness is always consciousness of something — every act of mind is aimed at an object; there's no "pure" consciousness floating free. So pay attention to the aiming: what is your mind pointed at right now? The direction is the experience.

**For example**
The anxious person's mind keeps pointing at "everything" — and the phenomenological question makes it specific: no, not everything; the meeting, at 3 p.m., about the deadline. The vague dread had an address all along.

> **Principle 394** — Practise intentionality

Recognise that consciousness is always consciousness of something. Every act of your mind — perceiving, remembering, imagining, judging, feeling — is directed toward an object. There is no "pure" consciousness that is not about something. Pay attention to the directedness of your experience: what is your consciousness aimed at?

(Husserl, 1900-1901/2001, Logical Investigations, Investigation V; Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §84; Brentano, 1874/1995, Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint.)

**Plain words** Consciousness is always consciousness of something — every act of mind is aimed at an object; there's no "pure" consciousness floating free. So pay attention to the aiming: what is your mind pointed at right now? The direction is the experience.

*For example:* The anxious person's mind keeps pointing at "everything" — and the phenomenological question makes it specific: no, not everything; the meeting, at 3 p.m., about the deadline. The vague dread had an address all along.

> **Principle 25** — Cultivate self-compassion: treat yourself as you would a good friend

Self-compassion is not self-pity or self-indulgence; it is treating your own suffering with the same kindness, understanding, and care you would extend to a friend. It has three components: self-kindness (vs self-judgment), common humanity (vs isolation), and mindfulness (vs over-identification).

**Plain words** Self-compassion: treating your own suffering with the same kindness, understanding, and care you'd extend to a friend. Not self-pity, not indulgence. Three components: self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness. The friend-voice is the standard.

*For example:* The person who spends the evening after the failure in the whipping post — and the question that interrupts: "what would you say to your friend who'd done exactly this?" — and the realisation that the answer is kind, honest, and useful, all three. The voice was always available; it was just never pointed at themselves.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-compassion|Self-compassion (Neff)]]**, three components: self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness (Neff, 2003, 2011)
- **Self-compassion is associated with better mental health**, meta-analyses show large negative relationship with psychopathology (r = -0.54) (MacBeth & Gumley, 2012; Zessin et al., 2015)
- **Self-compassion is distinct from self-esteem**, it does not depend on positive self-evaluation and is more stable (Neff & Vonk, 2009)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Metta (loving-kindness)|Metta (loving-kindness)]]** (Buddhism, Metta Sutta)
- **[[Amor sui (right self-love)|Amor sui (right self-love)]]** (Spinoza, Ethics IV)
- **[[The golden rule|The golden rule inverted]]** (Hillel the Elder, Talmud)

## Practice

- When you are suffering, ask: "how would I respond to a friend in this situation?" Then respond to yourself that way. Practise the three components: be kind, remember you are not alone, and hold the pain in mindful awareness.

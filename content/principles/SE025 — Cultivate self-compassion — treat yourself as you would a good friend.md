> **Principle 25** — Cultivate self-compassion: treat yourself as you would a good friend

Neff defines the construct by its three components:

> "Self-compassion entails three main components: (a) self-kindness—being kind and understanding toward oneself in instances of pain or failure rather than being harshly self-critical, (b) common humanity—perceiving one's experiences as part of the larger human experience rather than seeing them as separating and isolating, and (c) mindfulness—holding painful thoughts and feelings in balanced awareness rather than over-identifying with them." (Neff, 2003, p. 85)

The friend standard has a foothold in the same article: "common experience suggests people are often much harsher and unkind toward themselves than they ever would be to others they cared about, or even to strangers" (Neff, 2003, p. 87). The kindness is not indulgence, either. Neff includes "encouraging oneself to take whatever actions are needed—even if painful or difficult—in order to further one's well-being" (Neff, 2003, p. 88). Treating yourself as you would a good friend means the honest kind of care, not the soft kind.

(Neff, 2003.)

**Plain words** When you fail, notice which voice answers. If it is a voice you would not use on a friend, that is the habit this principle works on. Each of Neff's three parts names a correction: kindness instead of the beating-up, common humanity instead of "only me", balance instead of drowning in it.

*For example:* The person who spends the evening after the failure in the whipping post — and the question that interrupts: "what would you say to your friend who'd done exactly this?" — and the realisation that the answer is kind, honest, and useful, all three. The voice was always available; it was just never pointed at themselves.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-compassion|Self-compassion (Neff)]]**, three components: self-kindness, common humanity, mindfulness (Neff, 2003, 2011)
- **Self-compassion is associated with better mental health**, meta-analyses show large negative relationship with psychopathology (r = -0.54) (MacBeth & Gumley, 2012; Zessin et al., 2015)
- **Self-compassion is distinct from self-esteem**, it does not depend on positive self-evaluation and is more stable (Neff & Vonk, 2009)

## Practice

- When you are suffering, ask: "how would I respond to a friend in this situation?" Then respond to yourself that way. Practise the three components: be kind, remember you are not alone, and hold the pain in mindful awareness.

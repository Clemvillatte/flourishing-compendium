> **Principle 427** — Practise the gaze of the Other (le regard)

Sartre's test case is a man at a keyhole, "moved by jealousy, curiosity, or vice", wholly absorbed in what he is watching. There is no self in his consciousness to judge the act: "I am my acts" (Being and Nothingness, Part III, ch. 1, p. 259, trans. Barnes). Then he hears footsteps: "Someone is looking at me!" (p. 260).

### What the look does

The same act is now seen from outside. Sartre calls the result shame and gives it a precise structure:

> "I am ashamed of myself as I appear to the Other." (p. 222)

Shame is not a private verdict. "Shame is by nature recognition. I recognize that I am as the Other sees me" (p. 222). The look "alienates" my possibilities from me (p. 263), and the Other as a look is "my transcendence transcended" (p. 263).

### What you can do with it

Sartre does not offer a way to switch the look off. He names two authentic attitudes: shame, which recognises the Other as the subject through whom I get my object-side, and arrogance, which affirms my freedom against the Other (p. 290). Pride and vanity he calls bad faith (p. 290). Part IV adds the demand behind the principle: "whatever may be the situation in which he finds himself, the for-itself must wholly assume this situation" (Part IV, ch. 1, §III, pp. 553-554).

**Plain words** Being watched changes what you are doing. A thing you did without thinking becomes something that can be judged, and you feel that in your body before you can reason about it. Sartre argues the feeling is not imagination: another person's presence adds a side of you that you cannot see or control. What stays in your hands is the next move, and you make it better once you have noticed the look instead of being run by it.

*For example:* Imagined example: you rehearse a toast alone and it sounds fine. A friend opens the door and the same words feel stiff. Name what just happened, then choose: finish it, change it, or ask the friend what they heard.

(Sartre, Being and Nothingness, Part III, ch. 1, pp. 221-222, 259-263, 290; Part IV, ch. 1, §III, pp. 553-554, trans. Hazel Barnes; full text at the Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21.)

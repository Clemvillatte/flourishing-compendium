> **Principle 124** — Meet suchness: things as they are

The instruction to Bāhiya concerns seeing, hearing, sensing and cognising. It continues beyond attending to sensory detail: the discourse connects this training with there being no you in connection with that experience and with the end of suffering (Ud 1.10, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [text](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)).

> "In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen. In reference to the heard, only the heard." (Ud 1.10, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [text](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html))

The compendium uses suchness as a practical heading for approaching experience without immediately adding a personal verdict. This application does not establish that perception is unmediated, that all interpretation is dispensable or that the discourse's full aim is simply enjoying a meal.

### Plain words

Pause over what is actually available to notice before deciding what it means about you. You might attend to a sound, a taste or another person's words, then consider your response. The suggestion is to examine the experience more carefully, without forbidding thought or making a quiet mind the test of success.

### For example

Imagine a friend describing a difficult day. Listen to the words they use and check what they are asking from you before preparing advice. Your response may still require interpretation and judgment; the exercise asks that those be answerable to what you heard.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Radical acceptance|Radical acceptance]]**: Linehan's handout asks for acknowledging facts about past and present even when disliked, a clinical comparison rather than evidence for the discourse's liberation claim ([2015, Distress Tolerance Handout 11](https://strengtheningyourconsciousself.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/DBT-Distress-Tolerance-Handout-11.pdf)).
- **[[Deautomatization|Deautomatization]]**: Deikman proposed a psychological model of mystical experience based on meditation and renunciation; this is a hypothesis, not proof of direct access to things without interpretation ([1966, opening model statement](https://www.deikman.com/deautomat.html)).

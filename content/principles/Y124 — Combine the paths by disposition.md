> **Principle 124** — Combine the paths by disposition

The classical root is Bhagavad Gita 3.3, where Krishna names two paths and says who each is for:

> the two paths leading to enlightenment were previously explained by Me: the path of knowledge, for those inclined toward contemplation, and the path of work for those inclined toward action.

(Bhagavad Gita 3.3, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/3/).)

The Gita here names two paths, matched to two inclinations. The four-fold scheme is later. Swami Vivekananda wrote in the preface to *Raja Yoga* (1896): "Do this either by work, or worship, or psychic control, or philosophy—by one, or more, or all of these—and be free" ([Raja Yoga preface](https://www.swamivivekananda.guru/teachings/raja-yoga/raja-yoga-preface)). In his Karma Yoga lectures he writes, as quoted by the Vedanta Society of Southern California, that "we may reach the same goal by different paths", citing Complete Works, vol. 1, chapter 8 ([Vedanta Society](https://vedanta.org/yoga-spiritual-practice/)).

So the idea of paths by temperament is old, and the four doors with "one, or more, or all" are Vivekananda's. Naming a lead path and a support path, with one in the morning and one in the evening, is this page's own suggestion. Neither the Gita nor Vivekananda sets that out. The image of four doors into one room is also this page's own, not a quotation.

**Plain words**

People are drawn to different ways in: thinking, caring, working, sitting still. Vivekananda says any one will do, or a mix. The disagreement tends to start when someone insists the door they used is the only one.

**For example**

A nurse who finds philosophy dry but loves her work treats her shifts as the main practice. She adds ten minutes of sitting before bed, as support. She stops comparing herself with a friend who reads Shankara.

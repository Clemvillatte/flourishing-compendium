> **Principle 124** — Combine the paths by disposition

Vivekananda's modern synthesis, offered here as adaptation: four yogas (knowledge, devotion, action, and meditation) as four doors into one room, each person entering by the door their temperament opens. The classical sources support the plurality (the Gita teaches paths by inclination), while the synthesis is nineteenth-century. Practically, name your lead path, name your support path, and sequence them: lead in the morning, support in the evening.

(Vivekananda, Raja Yoga and Karma Yoga, 1896, modern; BG 3.3, paths by disposition.)

**Plain words** Vivekananda's synthesis: four yogas — knowledge, devotion, action, meditation — as four doors into one room, each person entering by the door their temperament opens. Don't force your door on others, and don't apologise for yours. Combine the paths by disposition.

*For example:* The same wisdom reached by the scholar's door (knowledge), the carer's door (action), the mystic's door (devotion) — the room is one; the arguing starts only when everyone insists their door is the building.

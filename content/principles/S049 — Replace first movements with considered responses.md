> **Principle 171** — Replace first movements with considered responses

Seneca's question in On Anger is whether anger springs up without the mind or with its approval. Part of the answer he gives by listing what the body does on its own:

> "all the motions which take place without our volition are beyond our control and unavoidable, such as shivering when cold water is poured over us, or shrinking when we are touched in certain places. Men’s hair rises up at bad news, their faces blush at indecent words, and they are seized with dizziness when looking down a precipice; and as it is not in our power to prevent any of these things, no reasoning can prevent their taking place." (Seneca, De Ira II.2, Aubrey Stewart translation)

The line he then draws between the jolt and the deed is the principle itself:

> "Anger is that which goes beyond reason and carries her away with it: wherefore the first confusion of a man’s mind when struck by what seems an injury is no more anger than the apparent injury itself: it is the subsequent mad rush, which not only receives the impression of the apparent injury, but acts upon it as true, that is anger, being an exciting of the mind to revenge, which proceeds from choice and deliberate resolve." (Seneca, De Ira II.3, Aubrey Stewart translation)

The first confusion belongs to the body; anger begins when the mind acts on the impression as true. The remedy Seneca prescribes is time: "The greatest remedy for anger is delay". The considered response is the second movement, made on purpose.

(Seneca, De Ira II.2, II.3, II.29, Aubrey Stewart translation.)

**Plain words** Some reactions happen to you before you have said anything to yourself about them: the blush, the flinch, the surge after bad news. The surge itself is not yet anger or any other vice; it becomes one only when you agree with it and act. So notice the first movement, and let time pass before the answer. What you do an hour later is the real response, and it is the part you own.

For example: Imagined: an angry email lands and you flinch as you read it; that flinch is the first movement. The reply sits unsent for an hour, and what goes out after the hour is the considered response.

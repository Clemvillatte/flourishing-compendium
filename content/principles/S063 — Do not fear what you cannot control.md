> **Principle 185** — Do not fear what you cannot control

Fear is the judgement that something bad is coming and that you will not be able to handle it. The first judgement may be true; the second is always false, because your capacity for virtue is always intact. Practise facing what you fear with the knowledge that your character cannot be taken.

(Disc. I.1, I.14, II.1; Ench. 1, 53; Med. IV.49, VII.16; Seneca, Letters 13, 24, 107.)

**Plain words** Fear is judging that something bad is coming and you won't cope. The first part may be true, but the second is always false: your capacity for virtue stays intact. Face what you fear knowing your character can't be taken.

*For example:* Dreading a difficult conversation, you remember that whatever happens you can still be honest and kind.

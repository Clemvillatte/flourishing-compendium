> **Principle 39** — Defy mortality with beauty and song, not with denial

The Cantares poems name death plainly and then turn to making. In the Nezahualcóyotl cycle in León-Portilla's translation, the poet admits "Not forever on earth, only a little while here. Though it be jade it falls apart, though it be gold it wears away", and later reports the discovery he took from this: "At last my heart knows it: I hear a song, I contemplate a flower... May they never fade!" (León-Portilla 1995, "The Indigenous Voices of Mexico", [Voices of Mexico](http://www.revistascisan.unam.mx/Voices/pdfs/3010.pdf)). Another poet, Ayocuan, asks: "Will nothing remain in my name? Nothing of my fame here on Earth? At least flowers, at least song!" (Garibay translation, in [Voices of Mexico](http://www.revistascisan.unam.mx/Voices/pdfs/8019.pdf)). The songs raise the question and do not claim a certain victory: Cuacuauhtzin writes that he craves "flowers that will not perish in my hands" and finds none on earth (Cantares fol. 26r, in León-Portilla, [Islands of History](https://islandsofhistory.org/volume-3/flower-and-song-pre-columbian-literatures-of-mesoamerica/)).

Dignity therapy is a brief palliative-care psychotherapy. A small phase II trial of 45 people with advanced cancer found no difference in dignity-related distress, but the intervention group "reported more hope than the control group at both follow-ups", and the authors called the effects "encouraging" (Hall et al. 2011, BMJ Support Palliat Care 1(3):315-321, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24653477/)).

**Plain words**
The old songs say that jade, gold and feathers wear away, and that people die too. Their answer is flowers and song: poems and work that may last longer than the maker. They do not promise it will work, and one poet says he could not find flowers that stay. A small trial of dignity therapy for people with advanced cancer found more hope but no change in distress. When mortality stings, making something is worth trying, and it is one response among others.

*For example:* A woman who learns her illness is terminal writes her family's recipes into a book with the stories behind them. This is an imagined case, and the book does not change the diagnosis.

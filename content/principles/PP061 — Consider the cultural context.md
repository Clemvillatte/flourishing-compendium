> **Principle 61** — Consider the cultural context

Well-being is not culture-free. The dimensions of flourishing, the practices that produce it, and the meaning of concepts like autonomy, relationship, and purpose vary across cultures. A practice that works in an individualistic context may not work, or may work differently, in a collectivist one. The principle is not cultural relativism. It is the recognition that the form of a practice must fit the cultural context in which it is deployed.

(Diener et al., 2003; Joshanloo, 2014.)

**Plain words** Well-being isn't culture-free. What flourishing means, and which practices produce it, varies. Autonomy, relationships and purpose are understood differently in different cultures. A practice that works in one context may work differently, or not at all, in another.

*For example:* A meditation programme designed for individuals may need reworking for a culture where identity is family-based.

> **Principle 44** — Recognise the role of exercise in attention: movement restores and improves attention

Physical exercise — both acute (single session) and chronic (regular training) — improves attentional capacity. Exercise increases blood flow, neurotransmitters, and neurogenesis, all of which support attention.

**Plain words** Movement is attention fertilizer — one session sharpens focus for the next couple of hours, and regular training raises the baseline. The "stupid jock" cliché has it exactly backwards. If you can't think, walk.

*For example:* The walking meeting — a hard problem and a loop around the block beats the same problem and a whiteboard, more often than pride admits.
## Psychology parallels

- **Acute exercise improves attention**, single sessions of moderate exercise improve attentional control and reaction time (Chang et al., 2012; Hillman et al., 2009)
- **Chronic exercise improves sustained attention and executive function**, regular exercise improves long-term attentional capacity (Hillman et al., 2009; Ratey & Loehr, 2011)
- **Exercise as attentional restoration**, walking in nature combines exercise and ART for double benefit (Berman et al., 2008; Bratman et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

## Practice

- Use exercise as attentional support: brief walks during work breaks, regular aerobic exercise for long-term capacity, walking in nature for double restoration.

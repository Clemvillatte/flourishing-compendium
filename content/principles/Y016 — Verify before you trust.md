> **Principle 16** — Verify before you trust

Sutra 1.7 lists the ways a person can come to a valid idea:

> Sources-of-valid-ideas are perception and inference and verbal-communication.

(Yoga Sutras 1.7, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, pp. 20-21, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary defines each one. Perception arises when the mind "has been affected by some external thing through the channel of the sense-organs". Inference rests on a relation seen in cases of the same class, with an example: the moon and stars move, "because, like [any man, for instance,] Chaitra, they get from one place to another". For testimony, it adds a check: "A thing which has been seen or inferred by a trustworthy person is mentioned by word". It also says a statement "is said to waver" when the speaker declares "an incredible thing, not a thing which he himself has seen or inferred", whereas it is "unwavering" if the original speaker has seen or inferred it.

So the commentary does give a test for one channel: ask whether the speaker saw or reasoned it. It does not list a failure mode for perception or for inference. The following sutra (1.8) turns to misconception, the false idea. Pairing each channel with its own check, and asking "did I see this, infer it, or absorb it?", is this page's own method.

**Plain words**

Before acting on an important belief, ask where it came from. Did you see it yourself, work it out, or take it from someone? The commentary's own test applies to the last: did the person telling you see or work it out for themselves?

**For example**

A woman hears from a friend that her landlord is selling the building. She asks the friend how she knows, and learns it came from a neighbour who saw a stranger measuring the hall. She calls the landlord instead of packing.

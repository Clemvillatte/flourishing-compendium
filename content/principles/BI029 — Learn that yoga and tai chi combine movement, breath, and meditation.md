> **Principle 29** — Learn that yoga and tai chi combine movement, breath, and meditation

Yoga, tai chi, and qigong are movement practices that combine physical movement, breath regulation, and meditative attention. They have specific mental health benefits distinct from aerobic or strength exercise.

**Plain words** Yoga, tai chi, qigong — movement plus breath plus attention in one package. They carry specific benefits beyond the sum of the parts: regulation, balance, calm. The old systems did bottom-up before science had a name for it.

*For example:* The tai chi class in the park — slow enough to look like nothing, and the nervous systems of everyone in it measurably calmer. Speed is not the mechanism. The integration is.
## Psychology parallels

- **Yoga and mental health**, yoga reduces stress, anxiety, and depression (Cramer et al., 2013; Streeter et al., 2012)
- **Tai chi and qigong**, these practices improve balance, reduce stress, and improve well-being (Wang et al., 2010; Liu et al., 2015)
- **Mechanism: breath**, movement, and attention combined, the combination produces effects beyond any single component (Streeter et al., 2012; Tang et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei: effortless movement]]** (Daoism, Zhuangzi, Cook Ding)
- **[[Yoga: union of body, breath, and mind|Yoga: union of body, breath, and mind]]** (Yoga tradition, Patanjali)
- **[[Yangsheng (cultivating vitality)|Yangsheng]]: movement as cultivation** (Daoism)

## Practice

- Include yoga, tai chi, or qigong: these combine movement, breath, and meditation. They are particularly good for stress reduction, balance, and gentle regulation.

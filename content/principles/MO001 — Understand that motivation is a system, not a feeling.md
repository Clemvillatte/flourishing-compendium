> **Principle 1** — Understand that motivation is a system, not a feeling

Motivation is not a single feeling of "wanting to do something." It is a system involving needs, values, goals, expectations, emotions, and habits. You can be motivated in one domain and unmotivated in another. Understanding motivation as a system (rather than as a feeling you either have or lack) changes how you work with it.

**Plain words** Motivation isn't a feeling that descends — it's a system: needs, values, expectations, rewards, energy, environment. Waiting to "feel motivated" is waiting for weather. Building the system is the work. Feelings follow.

*For example:* The person who can't get started "until they feel like it" versus the one who sets up a time, a place, and a buddy — the first is waiting on lightning, the second built a generator.
## Psychology parallels

- **Motivation as a multi-component system**, motivation involves needs, cognitions, emotions, and habits (Ryan & Deci, 2017; Locke & Latham, 2002)
- **Motivation is domain-specific**, motivation for work, health, and relationships are distinct (Vallerand, 1997; Vallerand et al., 2003)

## Practice

- Do not treat "I am unmotivated" as a global state. Diagnose: which domain, which goal, which need is unmet? Motivation is specific.

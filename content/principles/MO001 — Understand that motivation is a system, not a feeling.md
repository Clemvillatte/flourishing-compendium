> **Principle 1** — Understand that motivation is a system, not a feeling

Ryan and Deci identify several aspects of motivation:

> "Motivation concerns energy, direction, persistence and equifinality—all aspects of activation and intention." (Ryan & Deci, 2000, p. 69)

Vallerand's hierarchical model distinguishes a general motivational orientation, motivation within a life domain, and motivation during a particular activity. The middle level deals with "broad life contexts such as interpersonal relationships, education, work, religion, and others" (Guay, Mageau & Vallerand, 2003). The compendium's framing follows from this structure: describe motivation at a specific level rather than treating it as a single feeling you either have or lack.

**Plain words** Start with the activity you are struggling to begin. Describing your motivation toward one task is more specific than deciding that you are unmotivated in every part of life.

*For example (imagined exercise):* A student who cannot start studying describes her motivation toward that one course: no goal in it that matters to her, no fixed time slot in the week, and a phone on the desk. Naming the parts is the exercise; whether the changes then help is for her to observe.

## Psychology parallels

- **Motivation as a multi-component system**, motivation involves needs, cognitions, emotions, and habits (Ryan & Deci, 2017; Locke & Latham, 2002)
- **Motivation is domain-specific**, motivation for work, health, and relationships are distinct (Vallerand, 1997; Vallerand et al., 2003)

## Practice

- Do not treat "I am unmotivated" as a global state. Diagnose: which domain, which goal, which need is unmet? Motivation is specific.

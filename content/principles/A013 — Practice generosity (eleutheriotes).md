> **Principle 13** — Practice generosity (eleutheriotes)

Aristotle discusses liberality as a mean concerning wealth, with particular emphasis on giving. ([Nicomachean Ethics IV.1, trans. H. Rackham](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4)) The compendium presents it as the virtue of giving well rather than the practice of giving much.

(Nicomachean Ethics IV.1.)

> "This virtue seems to be the observance of the mean in relation to wealth: we praise a man as liberal not in war, nor in matters in which we praise him as temperate nor in judicial decisions, but in relation to giving and getting wealth, and especially in giving; wealth meaning all those things whose value is measured by money." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.1, trans. H. Rackham, [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4))

The chapter's first distinction is between use and acquisition: "the use of wealth seems to consist in spending and in giving; getting wealth and keeping it are modes of acquisition rather than of use. Hence the liberal man is more concerned with giving to the right recipients than with getting wealth from the right sources and not getting it from the wrong ones." ([NE IV.1, Rackham](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4))

Right giving is specified: the liberal man "will give to the right people, and the right amount, and at the right time, and fulfil all the other conditions of right giving," and he gives "for the nobility of giving," not from some other motive. ([NE IV.1, Rackham](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4))

The measure is the giver's means, not the gift's size: "the liberal man is one who spends in proportion to his means as well as on the right objects; while he that exceeds his means is prodigal," and "to waste one's substance seems to be in a way to ruin oneself, inasmuch as wealth is the means of life." ([NE IV.1, Rackham](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4))

**Plain words**
Giving well means the right amount to the right people at the right time, for the right reasons. ([NE IV.1, Rackham](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4)) Aristotle measures generosity against your means: spending in proportion is the virtue, exceeding it is prodigality, and wasting your substance is a kind of self-ruin. ([NE IV.1, Rackham](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0054:book=4))

*For example* (exercise): compare a regular donation with a gift that would leave rent unpaid. Consider the recipient, amount, timing, motive and your available resources.

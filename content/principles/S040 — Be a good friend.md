> **Principle 162** — Be a good friend

Seneca's Letter 9 matches the seed closely. The wise man "is self-sufficient" in that "he can do without friends, not that he desires to do without them", and he wants friends "if only for the purpose of practising friendship". He rejects Epicurus's reason, that there should be someone to sit by him when ill, and wants "someone by whose sickbed he himself may sit". He craves friends "not, however, that he may live happily; for he will live happily even without friends" (Gummere translation, [Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/seneca/moral-letters-to-lucilius/richard-m-gummere/text/letter-9)). That supports both "not dependent for happiness" and "not a means to an end". Letter 3 adds on the care of choosing: "Ponder for a long time whether you shall admit a given person to your friendship; but when you have decided to admit him, welcome him with all your heart and soul" ([Seneca.ink](https://www.seneca.ink/letters/3-on-true-and-false-friendship)). I did not open Marcus Aurelius, Epictetus or Musonius on friendship.

Cohen and Wills (1985) concluded that evidence fits both a buffering model and a main-effect model. Buffering appears when support is the perceived availability of interpersonal resources; a main effect appears when support is integration in a large social network ([Carnegie Mellon abstract](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Stress_social_support_and_the_buffering_hypothesis/6618335)). Demir et al. (2015) review friendship as "a consistent and robust correlate of happiness among young adults" ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Friendship-and-happiness-among-young-adults-Demir-Orthel-Clark/68fd764e1545d16f842526451693b1217c4a8907)); a systematic review found friendship quality and socialising with friends predict wellbeing ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9902704/)). Both bodies of work are largely correlational, and they describe benefits of having friends, whereas Seneca stresses what you give.

**Plain words** In Letter 9, Seneca says a wise person wants friends in order to practise friendship, and to be the one at the sickbed, not to secure their own happiness. In Letter 3 he adds that you should choose carefully and then give yourself fully. Research shows friendship goes with wellbeing, though mostly correlationally.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. You phone a friend who has just lost a job, not to cheer him up, but to listen.

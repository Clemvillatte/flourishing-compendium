> **Principle 320** — Practise filial piety (xiao)

In the Analects, Confucius resists a simple answer. When Meng Yi asks what filial piety is, he replies "It is not being disobedient", then explains to Fan Chi that parents should be served, buried and sacrificed to "according to propriety" ([Analects 2.5, Legge, ctext](https://ctext.org/analects/wei-zheng/ens)). So "not mere obedience" needs care: the text says "not disobedient" but ties it to ritual. Analects 2.7 adds that "dogs and horses likewise are able to do something in the way of support; without reverence, what is there to distinguish" the two?

Disagreement is allowed, gently. Analects 4.18: "In serving his parents, a son may remonstrate with them, but gently; when he sees that they do not incline to follow his advice, he shows an increased degree of reverence, but does not abandon his purpose" ([Analects 4.18, Legge](http://wengu.tartarie.com/wg/wengu.php?l=Lunyu&no=84)). Analects 1.11: a son who for three years does not alter his father's way "may be called filial" ([Analects 1.11](https://ctext.org/analects/xue-er/ens)).

In 4A19 Mencius says "the service of parents is the root of all others", and that "the charge of one's self is the root of all others". He contrasts "nourishing the mouth and body" with "nourishing the will" ([Mencius 4A19, Legge, via mengtzu.com](https://www.mengtzu.com/en/books/li-lou-i/4a-19)).

The seed's "debt you owe" appears in none of these passages. Psychology describes reciprocal filial piety as "gratitude for their efforts in having raised them" ([Child Development](https://academic.oup.com/chidev/article/89/2/383/8263272)).

Berscheid (1994) and Mikulincer et al. (2005) are cited as unopened leads. The dual filial piety model separates reciprocal (love and care) from authoritarian (hierarchy and obedience) forms. A review of 17 studies of Chinese and Korean adolescents found reciprocal filial piety negatively associated with depression, anxiety and other symptoms, while authoritarian filial piety was not related to most symptoms ([Dual Filial Piety review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40894-024-00234-2)). The depression pooling used cross-sectional data, so causation is not shown.

**Plain words** Xiao means more than doing what parents say. The Analects ask for reverence, an honest face, gentle disagreement and continuing what was good in them. Mencius adds that losing yourself undermines serving them.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A daughter drives her father to every appointment. When he refuses a treatment she thinks is wrong, she says so once, kindly, and keeps driving.

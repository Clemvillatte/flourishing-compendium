> **Principle 46** — Recognise rumination as a distinct and maladaptive cognitive mode

Rumination is repetitively thinking about distressing situations, their causes, and consequences without moving toward solution or perspective. It is distinct from reflection (which is generative) and problem-solving (which is action-oriented). Rumination is one of the most robust predictors of depression.

**Plain words** Rumination: repetitively chewing distressing situations, their causes and consequences, without moving toward solution or perspective. Distinct from reflection (generative) and problem-solving (productive). Rumination predicts depression — it's the mind's treadmill, running hard, going nowhere.

*For example:* The person who replays the conversation for the tenth night — same loops, same wounds, zero new information — versus reflection, which looks different: "here's the pattern, here's what I'll try next time." One is a mill; the other is a mill with an output.
## Psychology parallels

- **Rumination (brooding) predicts depression**, the brooding subcomponent of rumination is a strong prospective predictor of depressive episodes (Nolen-Hoeksema, 2000; Treynor et al., 2003)
- **Reflective pondering is less maladaptive than brooding**, the reflective component is less strongly associated with depression (Treynor et al., 2003)
- **Rumination-focused CBT (RFCBT) reduces rumination and depression**, interventions that specifically target rumination are effective (Watkins et al., 2011)

## Practice

- When you notice repetitive negative thinking, ask: "is this moving me toward understanding or solution, or am I just cycling?" If cycling, shift to action, distraction, or a different cognitive mode.

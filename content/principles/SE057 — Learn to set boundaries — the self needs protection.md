> **Principle 57** — Learn to set boundaries: the self needs protection

Marcus grounds the self's protection in a place that travels with you: the retreat is not the country house but the mind you can enter wherever you are. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15877/pg15877.txt))

> "Men seek retreats for themselves, houses in the country, sea-shores, and mountains; and thou too art wont to desire such things very much. But this is altogether a mark of the most common sort of men, for it is in thy power whenever thou shalt choose to retire into thyself. For nowhere either with more quiet or more freedom from trouble does a man retire than into his own soul, particularly when he has within him such thoughts that by looking into them he is immediately in perfect tranquillity; and I affirm that tranquillity is nothing else than the good ordering of the mind."

(Med. IV.3, George Long translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15877/pg15877.txt).)

The compendium applies the citadel to the interpersonal case: a boundary is the gate of that retreat. Saying no to the third committee or the Sunday shift is how the interior stays in order, and Marcus names tranquillity as exactly that ordering.

**Plain words** A boundary is not a wall and it's not an insult. It's the simple statement of what you will and won't do — no to the third committee, no to Sunday shifts, no to being the family's unpaid therapist. People who respect you adjust. People who only liked your compliance call you selfish. Note the difference.

*For example:* "I can't lend money again" costs you one uncomfortable conversation. Not saying it costs you the money, the resentment, and eventually the relationship. The boundary is the cheaper option, every time.

## Psychology parallels

- **Boundaries and self-care**, boundary-setting is associated with better well-being and less burnout (Butler et al., 2016; Lee & Miller, 2013)
- **Codependency and boundary failure**, difficulty setting boundaries is associated with codependency and worse outcomes (Whitfield, 1987; Cermak, 1986)

## Practice

- Practise setting boundaries: say no to requests that overextend you, protect your time, and communicate your limits. "I cannot do that" is a complete sentence. Boundaries protect the self.

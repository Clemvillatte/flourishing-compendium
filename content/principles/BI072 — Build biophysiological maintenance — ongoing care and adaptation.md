> **Principle 72** — Build biophysiological maintenance: ongoing care and adaptation

Biophysiological health is not a one-time achievement. McEwen's review of stress and adaptation frames the body's systems as continuously adapting: protective in the short run, wearing in the long run. The abstract states both sides, verbatim:

> "Beyond the 'flight-or-fight' response to acute stress, there are events in daily life that produce a type of chronic stress and lead over time to wear and tear on the body ('allostatic load'). Yet, hormones associated with stress protect the body in the short-run and promote adaptation ('allostasis')." (McEwen, 2007, Physiological Reviews 87(3), 873-904, [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17615391/))

The same abstract names the maintenance behaviours, verbatim: "As an adjunct to pharmaceutical therapy, social and behavioral interventions such as regular physical activity and social support reduce the chronic stress burden and benefit brain and body health and resilience." That is the physiological case for ongoing care: not a cure, but a repeated reduction of load. The compendium's application is the adaptation cycle: keep what works, adjust what stops working, and expect the manual to be rewritten by ageing, illness, and life changes.

**Plain words** The body's stress systems protect in the short run and wear in the long run, and the maintenance behaviours (movement, sleep, people) are what tip the balance. Review the routine whenever life changes: what worked at thirty may need adjusting at fifty, and the adjustment is not failure.

*For example:* The runner who becomes a swimmer who becomes a walker: same body, three eras, each with its own servicing schedule. The routine is the current version, not the finished one.

## Practice

- Build biophysiological maintenance: regular sleep, exercise, nutrition, stress management, and medical check-ups. Adapt as circumstances change. Retain what works; adjust what does not.

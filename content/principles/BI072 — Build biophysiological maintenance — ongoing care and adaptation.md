> **Principle 72** — Build biophysiological maintenance: ongoing care and adaptation

Biophysiological health is not a one-time achievement; it requires ongoing maintenance, adaptation to changing circumstances (ageing, illness, life changes), and retention of what works.

**Plain words** The body is a lifelong maintenance project, not a one-time fix — ageing, illness, and life changes keep rewriting the manual. What worked at thirty needs adjusting at fifty. The maintenance IS the achievement; there's no "done."

*For example:* The runner who becomes a swimmer who becomes a walker — same body, three eras, each with its own servicing schedule. The adaptation is the health; the routine is just the current version of it.
## Psychology parallels

- **Biophysiological maintenance**, sleep, exercise, nutrition, and stress management require ongoing practice (McEwen, 2007; Sapolsky, 2004)
- **Adaptation across the lifespan**, physiological needs change with age and circumstance (Lövdén et al., 2010; Stern, 2002)

## Practice

- Build biophysiological maintenance: regular sleep, exercise, nutrition, stress management, and medical check-ups. Adapt as circumstances change. Retain what works; adjust what does not.

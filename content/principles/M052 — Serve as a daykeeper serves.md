> **Principle 52** — Serve as a daykeeper serves

The Maya Daykeeping excerpt from the University Press of Colorado describes a role of service to others. "A calendar priest is consulted for advice in all situations of emergency and stress, such as illness, land disputes, lost property, commerce, travel, adultery, death, birth, dreams, omens, or marriage". Daykeepers also use divination "to determine the cause and cure of sickness, to interpret dreams, to recover lost or stolen objects, and to determine times and places for ritual", and "all ritual, whether for the family, neighborhood groups, or the entire municipality, is done according to the calendar" ([Maya Daykeeping excerpt](https://upcolorado.com/excerpts/9780870819969.pdf), pp. 9-12). The excerpt does not state that daykeepers serve for life or that they tend a fire, and it names Tedlock only as a trained and initiated diviner. Barbara Tedlock's Time and the Highland Maya is a further reading on the training of daykeepers; this page does not draw on it.

The psychology of small acts of service is better documented. A meta-analysis of 27 experimental studies (N = 4,045) found that "the overall effect of kindness on the well-being of the actor is small-to medium (δ = 0.28)", and the effect "was not moderated by sex, age, type of participant, intervention, control condition or outcome measure" (Curry et al. 2018, J Exp Soc Psychol 76:320-329, [Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/happy-to-help-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis-of-the-effect/)). The abstract does not say how long the effects last.

**Plain words**
In the Maya communities described, the daykeeper is the person people consult in illness, loss and decision, and ritual follows the count. The opened sources do not describe daily unglamorous service or lifelong tenure. What research does support is smaller: doing kind acts raises the doer's well-being a little. Choose one small service you can keep up, and see how it feels after a few months.

*For example:* A neighbour waters the street's gardens and takes in parcels for others. This is an imagined case, and nothing about it needs to be remarkable.

> **Principle 52** — Serve as a daykeeper serves

The Aj Q'ij (the Maya daykeeper) carries a life of daily service: praying for the community, keeping the count, tending the fire, being available for counsel. The role's psychological architecture is the principle: a standing, daily, unglamorous service to others, held for decades, that gives the life its shape and its belonging. The tradition is clear that the role forms the person who holds it. Practically, whatever your actual role, take one small daily service (a prayer list, a check-in call, a tended commons) and hold it for a year, as the daykeeper holds the count.

(Barbara Tedlock, Time and the Highland Maya, the daykeeping ethnography.)

**Plain words** The daykeeper's life is a standing, daily, unglamorous service: praying for the community, keeping the count, tending the fire, being available for counsel — held for decades, and it's the role that forms the person. Find your version of the role: a daily service, small, public, unheroic, held.

*For example:* The neighbour who's watered the street's gardens and taken in the parcels for twenty years — nothing about it is remarkable, which is exactly what makes it the practice.

> **Principle 89** — Separate the seer from the seen daily

> The form is perceived and the eye is its perceiver. It (eye) is perceived and the mind is its perceiver. The mind with its modifications is perceived and the Witness (the Self) is verily the perceiver. But It (the Witness) is not perceived (by any other).

(Dṛg-Dṛśya Viveka, verse 1, trans. Swami Nikhilananda, Sri Ramakrishna Asrama, Mysore, 1931; [full text on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.72940/2015.72940.Drg-Drsya-Viveka_djvu.txt) and [the same edition as a PDF](https://archive.theaum.org/books/advaita/Swami_Nikhilananda_Drg_Drsya_Viveka.pdf). The Sanskrit is "rūpaṁ dṛśyaṁ locanaṁ dṛk tad dṛśyaṁ dṛk tu mānasam, dṛśyā dhī-vṛttayaḥ sākṣī dṛg eva na tu dṛśyate.")

The procedure the entry describes follows from verse 4: "Consciousness illumines (such other mental states as) desire, determination and doubt, belief and non-belief, constancy and its opposite, modesty, understanding, fear and others, because it (Consciousness) is a unity" ([Nikhilananda translation, as reproduced by Arav](https://arav.live/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/drgdrsya_1-46.pdf)). Mental states, including fear and doubt, are placed on the side of the seen. Awareness is the seer, which is itself not perceived by anything else.

A practice of doing one sort per day, at a moment of strong feeling, is the entry's own use of this scheme. The edition read does not tell the reader to do that. The authorship of the text is also uncertain ([Practical Philosophy](https://practicalphilosophy.in/2007/08/13/drg-drsya-viveka/) says it "is not known"), and the entry's "attributed to Shankara" fits that caution. Yoga Sutras 2.18-2.20 and the Husserl parallel were not opened.

**Plain words** Take any feeling and ask which part is being watched and which part is the watching. The feeling, the thought and even the sense organs belong to what is watched. The awareness that notices them is something else. Doing the sort in the middle of a strong mood can give a little room between you and it.

*For example:* In the middle of a bad mood, someone asks: the mood is something I am seeing, and what is seeing it? That small shift does not remove the mood, but they are no longer only inside it.

> **Principle 371** — Practise social justice

Nussbaum's capability approach asks what people can actually do and be, including whether they have opportunities for bodily health, practical reasoning and political participation ([Capabilities and Human Rights, 1997, pp. 282–287](https://ir.lawnet.fordham.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3391&context=flr)).

> "Capability, not functioning, is the political goal." (Nussbaum, [p. 288](https://ir.lawnet.fordham.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3391&context=flr)).

Providing an opportunity differs from compelling its use: citizens must remain free to choose their course once capabilities are secured ([p. 288](https://ir.lawnet.fordham.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3391&context=flr)). The practical challenge is to examine barriers between a service's formal availability and someone's ability to use it.

**Plain words** When judging a policy, ask who can actually use it and who still cannot. Then ask what barrier is doing the excluding, before calling the policy a success.

*For example:* Suppose a town opens a clinic, but residents without a car cannot reach it. Supporting a workable transport service addresses a barrier that counting appointments alone would miss.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Relative deprivation|Relative deprivation and health]]**: Marmot connects health gradients with social conditions, including autonomy and participation as well as material circumstances; this review does not establish that relative deprivation alone causes ill health ([2005 Lancet review, UCL manuscript](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/676/1/Microsoft_Word_-_MM_Lancet_365_1099_UCL_eprints.pdf)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Justice|Justice as fairness]]**: Rawls gives equal basic liberties priority, then fair opportunity and the greatest benefit to the least advantaged; this is a distinct account, not simply a demand for identical outcomes ([Leif Wenar, John Rawls, section 4.3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rawls/)).
- **[[The capabilities approach|Capabilities approach]]**: Nussbaum distinguishes resources and reported satisfaction from substantive opportunities, while protecting choice about their use ([1997, pp. 282, 288–290](https://ir.lawnet.fordham.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3391&context=flr)).

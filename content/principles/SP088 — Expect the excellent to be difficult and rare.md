> **Principle 88** — Expect the excellent to be difficult and rare

The Ethics' last written line: all things excellent are as difficult as they are rare. Spinoza ends the book by pricing it honestly; the free life is not a secret or a trick, but it is expensive, and the cost is exacted in the currency of sustained understanding. The sentence is discouraging only if read as elitism; read as engineering, it is a budget. Practically, when the practice is hard, do not conclude that you are unsuited; conclude that you are at the part of the curve the author himself priced.

(Ethics V, P42 scholium, the closing line.)

**Plain words** The Ethics' last written line: all things excellent are as difficult as they are rare. The book ends by pricing itself honestly — the free life is not a secret or a trick, but it is expensive, and the currency is paid in practice. Expect the excellent to be difficult and rare; budget accordingly.

*For example:* The person who started the practice expecting the transformation to be efficient — and the honest re-budgeting when the price revealed itself: years, not weeks. The excellent things are rare for a mechanical reason. They cost what they cost.

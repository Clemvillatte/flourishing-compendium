> **Principle 21** — Learn to connect goals to identity: "I am a person who..."

Goals connected to identity ("I am a runner" rather than "I want to run") are more motivating and more sustainable. Identity-based goals mobilise the self in service of the goal.

**Plain words** "I want to run" is a wish; "I'm a runner" is an identity — and identity goals run on their own fuel. Behaviour follows the self-story. Cast yourself in the role first, and the actions become set-dressing for the character.

*For example:* The person who says "I'm trying to quit" versus "I don't smoke" offered a cigarette — the second just says "no, thanks" without a struggle. The identity already did the deciding.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Identity|Identity-based motivation (Oyserman)]]**, goals connected to identity are pursued more effectively (Oyserman et al., 2010; Oyserman, 2015)
- **Identity and habit formation**, habits are more likely when tied to identity (Gardner, 2015; Clear, 2018)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as identity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **Existence precedes essence: you choose who you are** (Sartre, Existentialism is a Humanism)
- **[[Right livelihood|Right livelihood as identity]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Frame goals in identity terms: "I am a person who exercises" rather than "I want to exercise." Let the identity drive the behaviour, not just the goal.

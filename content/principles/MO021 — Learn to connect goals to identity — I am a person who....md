> **Principle 21** — Learn to connect goals to identity: "I am a person who..."

Bryan and colleagues ran three randomised experiments in which survey items framed voting either as an identity ("being a voter") or as a behaviour ("voting"). The identity wording "significantly increased interest in registering to vote (experiment 1) and, in two statewide elections in the United States, voter turnout as assessed by official state records (experiments 2 and 3)" ([Bryan, Walton, Rogers & Dweck, 2011, PNAS 108, 12653-12656](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21768362/)). That is direct support for the principle in one domain, with a subtle wording change, not a general result about "I am a person who" statements.

Sartre gives the philosophical frame ([Existentialism Is a Humanism](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm)):

> What do we mean by saying that existence precedes essence? We mean that man first of all exists, encounters himself, surges up in the world – and defines himself afterwards.

(Bryan et al., 2011; Sartre, 1946.)

**Plain words** Describing a goal as who you are can be more motivating than describing it as something to do, at least for voting, where the effect was tested. The identity has to fit and then be acted on. Saying it alone is unlikely to be enough, and the studies here do not test that.

For example (imagined): "I am a reader" gives you a reason to open a book tonight that "I should read more" does not. You still have to open it.

## Practice

- Frame goals in identity terms: "I am a person who exercises" rather than "I want to exercise." Let the identity drive the behaviour, not just the goal.

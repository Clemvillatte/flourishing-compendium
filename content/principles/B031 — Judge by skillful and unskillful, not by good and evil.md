> **Principle 31** — Judge by skillful and unskillful, not by good and evil

Actions are not moralistic categories but functional ones: do they increase or decrease suffering, confusion, reactivity? Evaluate by effect, with an honest eye.

(MN 2; MN 61.)

**Plain words** Actions aren't moralistic categories but functional ones: do they increase or decrease suffering, confusion, reactivity? Evaluate by effect, with an honest eye. 'Skillful' and 'unskillful' are engineering terms — the question is whether the machine works, not whether it's evil.

*For example:* The blunt email that felt righteous and burned the relationship — skillful? No. Wrong? Doesn't matter; it failed the function test.

## Psychology parallels

- **Guilt (functional) versus shame (toxic)**, distinguishing "I did badly" from "I am bad" predicts change (Tangney and Dearing, 2002)

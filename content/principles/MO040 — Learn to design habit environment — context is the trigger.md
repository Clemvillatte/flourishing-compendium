> **Principle 40** — Learn to design habit environment: context is the trigger


The strongest evidence that context cues habits comes from a natural experiment. Students transferring to a new university kept exercising, newspaper reading and TV watching habits "only when aspects of the performance context did not change (e.g., participants continued to read the paper with others)" (Wood, Tam and Witt 2005, J Pers Soc Psychol 88(6):918-933, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15982113/)). Where the setting changed, the habit broke, and in some cases behaviour then followed current intentions instead.

That also points to a window for change. A field experiment with 800 participants found that an intervention promoting sustainable behaviours "was more effective among recently relocated participants", and its results "suggested that the duration of the 'window of opportunity' was three months after relocation" (Verplanken and Roy 2016, J Environmental Psychology 45:127-134, [abstract](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/empowering-interventions-to-promote-sustainable-lifestyles-testin/)). The outcomes were self-reported.

Stable context helps new habits form, but slowly. In a study of 96 volunteers who repeated one behaviour daily in the same context for 12 weeks, the time to reach 95% of each person's peak automaticity "ranged from 18 to 254 days". Also, "Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process" (Lally et al. 2010, Eur J Soc Psychol 40:998-1009, [abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674)). Designing your surroundings is a way to give a repeated behaviour a steady cue. It is not an instant fix.

**Plain words** Habits attach to places, times and people, so the setting does a lot of the work. Put the cue for the habit you want where you will meet it. When your setting changes, as in a move or a new job, old habits loosen and new ones can start.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. After moving flats, a man leaves his running shoes by the door and his book on the pillow. For the first weeks he follows a plan, and the shoes and the book become the cue.

## Psychology parallels

- **Environment and habit (Wood & Neal)**, habits are cued by recurring context; students' habits survived a university transfer only where the performance context stayed the same (Wood, Tam & Witt, 2005); Wood & Neal (2007) is the model paper; Gardner (2015) is unopened
- **Context change disrupts habits**, habits broke when circumstances changed (Wood et al., 2005), and an intervention worked better among recently relocated households, with a suggested three-month window (Verplanken & Roy, 2016)

## Practice

- Design the environment: put the running shoes by the door, keep the book on the pillow, remove the phone from the bedroom. Make the environment trigger the desired behaviour.

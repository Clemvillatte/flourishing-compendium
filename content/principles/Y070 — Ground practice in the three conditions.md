> **Principle 70** — Ground practice in the three conditions

The tradition's definition of when a practice becomes established: attended to for a long time, without interruption, and with a devoted heart. Each condition is a design requirement: long time (effects are cumulative), without interruption (consistency), with devotion (quality of attention during practice). Practically, audit your practice against all three, because most failures are failures of one condition, not of the practice itself.

(YS 1.14.)

**Plain words** When does a practice become established? Three conditions: attended to for a long time, without interruption, and with a devoted heart. Long time (cumulative effects), without interruption (consistency), with devotion (quality of attention). All three are design requirements — miss one and the practice stays thin.

*For example:* The gym-goer with the long time and the devotion but the interruptions — every restart costs a month of the cumulative effect. The one who does less, but never stops, ends up unrecognizable.

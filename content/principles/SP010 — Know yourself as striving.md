> **Principle 10** — Know yourself as striving

Each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being. This is the conatus, and it is not one drive among others; it is the essence of the thing, the actual essence from which desire, joy, sadness, love, and hate are built. To see yourself as striving is more accurate than to see yourself as a rational agent who sometimes has urges: you are striving first, and reason is one of its instruments. Practically, when behavior confuses you, do not ask "what is wrong with this person," ask "what is this striving to preserve, and is it working?"

(Ethics III, P6, P7, and P12 with scholium.)

**Plain words** The conatus: each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being — and this striving is your essence, the thing desire, joy, sadness, love, and hate are built from. Know yourself as striving. The self isn't a static thing; it's a verb with a direction.

*For example:* Every appetite in the life — the snack, the status post, the third coffee — reads, under the conatus lens, as the same single striving wearing different costumes. The diet that fights the costumes loses; the life that addresses the striving wins.

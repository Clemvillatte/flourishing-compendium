> **Principle 10** — Know yourself as striving

Each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being. This is the conatus, and it is not one drive among others; it is the essence of the thing, the actual essence from which desire, joy, sadness, love, and hate are built. To see yourself as striving is more accurate than to see yourself as a rational agent who sometimes has urges: you are striving first, and reason is one of its instruments. Practically, when behavior confuses you, do not ask "what is wrong with this person," ask "what is this striving to preserve, and is it working?"

(Ethics III, P6, P7, and P9 with note; Elwes translation.)

> Everything, in so far as it is in itself, endeavours to persist in its own being.
>
> Spinoza, Ethics III, Proposition VI, translated by R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm).

> In no case do we strive for, wish for, long for, or desire anything, because we deem it to be good, but on the other hand we deem a thing to be good, because we strive for it, wish for it, long for it, or desire it.
>
> Spinoza, Ethics III, Proposition IX, note, same translation.

### Plain words

Wanting to continue is not a character flaw; it is the material you are made of. Reason does not sit above your appetites giving orders — it is one more instrument the striving uses. When behavior confuses you, do not ask what is broken. Ask what this is trying to protect, and whether protecting it that way still works.

*For example:* Every appetite in the life — the snack, the status post, the third coffee — reads, under the conatus lens, as the same single striving wearing different costumes. The diet that fights the costumes loses; the life that addresses the striving wins.

> **Principle 112** — Let wholehearted devotion move obstacles

In Liezi 5, a ninety-year-old proposes removing two mountains that block a road. His answer to a mocker changes the timescale: his descendants can continue while the mountains do not grow. The tale ends with divine removal of the mountains. It is a parable of commitment, not evidence that perseverance guarantees success.

(Liezi 5.)

> "Though I myself must die, I shall leave a son behind me, and through him a grandson. [...] With all this posterity, my line will not die out, while on the other hand the mountain will receive no increment or addition. Why then should I despair of levelling it to the ground at last?" (Liezi 5, [Giles](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Taoist_teachings_from_the_book_of_Lieh_Tz%C5%AD/Book_5); ellipsis marks a skipped sentence)

The old man's calculation assumes that successors will continue. For a shared project, that makes willingness to carry the work forward a question to ask, not something devotion entitles you to demand.

### Plain words

If a project may outlast your part in it, decide what you can promise and what others must freely choose. Leave usable progress and a record of what remains. Committing yourself does not commit everyone who comes after you.

### For example

Try two schedules for helping with a shared garden: a short weekly session or a longer fortnightly one. Compare progress and the commitments everyone freely accepts. Keep the workable schedule and a record for later volunteers. The practice changes overt behaviour, not the strength of a wish.

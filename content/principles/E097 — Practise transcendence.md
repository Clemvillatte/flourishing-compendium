> **Principle 489** — Practise transcendence

Jaspers does not treat transcendence as a place to go. In Way to Wisdom he says we reach it "through the language of things": "As existence we are oriented toward God — transcendence — and this through the language of things, which existence uses as hieroglyphics or symbols" (p. 31, trans. Manheim). The Comprehensive, "conceived as being itself, is called transcendence (God) and the world" (p. 32).

### It is not an exit

The orientation does not take you out of the world: "we do not attain this goal by leaving the world, except in incommunicable mysticism" (p. 34). Even when thought goes beyond object knowledge, "we remain in it" (p. 34). To believe in God "means to live by something which is not in the world, except in the polyvalent language of phenomena, which we call the hieroglyphs or symbols of transcendence" (p. 49).

### What it gives and does not give

Transcendence is no possession. "Faith is no possession. It confers no secure knowledge, but it gives certainty in the practice of life" (p. 50). He adds that reflection on it "does not bring secure knowledge, but to authentic self-hood it gives a free area for decision" (p. 50), and that "the whole emphasis is on love in the world, on the reading of the symbols of transcendence" (p. 50). The relation to it also stays unsolved: "The mystery of transcendence is not thereby solved but merely grows deeper" (p. 43).

**Plain words** Jaspers asks you not to take the world's surface for all there is. Measuring and explaining are good and he does not drop them. He asks you to keep a place for what cannot be proved or measured, and to read it in what the world shows you. That is a practice of attention, not a set of beliefs, and it asks a lot of honesty about what you do not know.

*For example:* Imagined example: after a death in the family, someone who normally trusts only what can be checked finds that the facts do not touch the grief. They keep the facts and stay with what the grief points to, without claiming to know what it is.

(Jaspers, Way to Wisdom, trans. Ralph Manheim (Yale, 1951), pp. 31-32, 34, 43, 49-50, Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21. Jaspers's Philosophy vol. 3 and Marcel's The Mystery of Being were not opened.)

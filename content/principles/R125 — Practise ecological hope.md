> **Principle 387** — Practise ecological hope

Maintain hope for the ecological future, not as naive optimism but as the active refusal to despair. Hope, in the ecological context, is the practice of working for a future that is not guaranteed but is possible. Plant trees under whose shade you will never sit.

(Macy and Brown, 2014, Coming Back to Life; Solnit, 2016, Hope in the Dark; Orr, 2011, Hope Is an Imperative.)

**Plain words** Ecological hope isn't optimism about the planet — it's the active refusal to despair: working for a future not guaranteed but possible. Plant trees under whose shade you'll never sit. Hope, in this sense, is a practice, not a mood.

*For example:* The retired couple restoring the degraded creek their whole street had written off — "we won't see it finished; that's not the point" — and ten years later the platypus is back.

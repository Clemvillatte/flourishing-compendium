> **Principle 387** — Practise ecological hope

Solnit writes in Hope in the Dark: "Hope just means another world might be possible, not promised, not guaranteed" ([Bookmate excerpt](https://bookmate.com/books/YfQd7YsJ/quotes)). Elsewhere she says "Hope is not a lottery ticket you can sit on the sofa and clutch, feeling lucky. It is an axe you break down doors with in an emergency" ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/219323-hope-is-not-a-lottery-ticket-you-can-sit-on)). Both quotes support the seed's "hope as practice". Macy and Brown (2014) and Orr (2011) were not opened.

The tree-planting line is not a source-backed quotation here. Quote Investigator traces the closest early match to an 1866 sermon by Hyacinthe Loyson and a London paper in 1868 that called it an "Indian proverb" ([Quote Investigator](https://quoteinvestigator.com/page/91/?ref=hackernoon.com)). Cicero has the poet Caecilius Statius planting trees that will be of use to another age ([Forest Nation](https://forestnation.com/blog/this-quote-says-all/)).

The psychology evidence separates two kinds of hope. In Swedish samples (n = 723 teenagers, 381 young adults), "constructive" hope predicted pro-environmental behaviour, while hope based on denial correlated negatively with it ([Ojala 2012, ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ981725)). Meaning-focused coping was linked to well-being and optimism in adolescents ([Ojala 2013](https://ideas.repec.org/a/gam/jsusta/v5y2013i5p2191-2209d25694.html)). These are questionnaire studies of correlation. Snyder's hope theory defines hope as the "perceived capability to derive pathways to desired goals" plus agency thinking ([Snyder 2002 abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/S15327965PLI1304_01)). It is not obviously the same as hope for an uncertain ecological future. I did not find the "Lebowitz et al., 2022" tragic-optimism citation, nor Frankl's text, so the tragic-optimism link is unverified.

**Plain words** Ecological hope can be understood as acting for a future that is possible but not promised. Research with young Swedes separates constructive hope, which goes with action, from hope based on denial, which does not. The evidence is correlational.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A retired couple restore a neglected creek, knowing they may not see the result.

> **Principle 30** — Learn that walking is underrated: simple, accessible, effective

The strongest evidence for walking is narrower than the seed's list of "mood, stress, creativity, health". On creativity, four experiments by Oppezzo and Schwartz showed that "walking boosts creative ideation in real time and shortly after". Walking raised scores on the alternate-uses test for 81% of participants but raised the convergent remote-associates scores of only 23%, and outdoor walking produced the most novel analogies ([Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014](https://aaalab.stanford.edu/assets/papers/2014/Give_your_ideas_some_legs.pdf)). The authors' lab states that the effect "does not benefit activities that require more focused, convergent types of thinking" ([Stanford AAA Lab](https://aaalab.stanford.edu/tidbits/creativity/index.html)).

On mood and stress, the evidence is about group walks. A study comparing group walks in different settings found that, compared with urban walks, farmland walks went with less perceived stress and negative affect and greater mental well-being, with no differences in depression or positive affect ([Marselle et al. 2014, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24173142/)). A later analysis found that group nature walks did not buffer the effects of stressful life events, although they were positively associated with depression, positive affect and well-being ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30893850/)).

### Scope

The creativity experiments were small, short, and used lab tasks. I read only search excerpts of Hanson and Jones 2015 and did not verify its walking-group findings, so I make no claim from it. The claim about walking meetings is not tested in these sources.

**Plain words**
A walk can help you generate ideas, though not choose between them. Group walks outdoors are linked with lower stress and better wellbeing.

**For example**
Before you write, take a ten-minute walk and list every idea that comes to mind. Then sit down and choose among them.

## Psychology parallels

- **Walking and mental health**, walking reduces stress, improves mood, and supports creativity (Oppezzo & Schwartz, 2014; Hanson & Jones, 2015)
- **Walking and creativity**, walking (especially outdoors) enhances creative thinking (Oppezzo & Schwartz, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The walking philosopher|The walking philosopher]]** (Aristotle, peripatetic school)
- **Walking as meditation** (Thich Nhat Hanh)

## Practice

- Walk more: daily walks (especially in nature), walking meetings, walking meditation. Walking is simple, accessible, and effective. Do not underestimate it.

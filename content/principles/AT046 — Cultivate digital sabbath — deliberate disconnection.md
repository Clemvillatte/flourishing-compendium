> **Principle 46** — Cultivate digital sabbath: deliberate disconnection

Periods of complete disconnection from digital devices and media allow attention to fully rest and restore. This is not avoidance; it is deliberate recovery. Digital sabbath practices improve attention, mood, and well-being.

**Plain words** Full attentional recovery needs full disconnection — a weekly block with the devices off, not just paused. The mind reorganizes differently when it's not auditioning for the internet every 40 seconds. The sabbath is a technology with ten thousand years of QA.

*For example:* Sunday mornings, phone in the drawer, and by noon you notice you've read, walked, and talked — a different species of day than the one that starts with the screen.
## Psychology parallels

- **Digital detox improves well-being**, brief periods of disconnection reduce stress and improve mood (Walton et al., 2021; Wilcockson et al., 2019)
- **Screen time before bed impairs sleep and next-day attention**, evening screen use disrupts sleep and degrades attentional capacity (Chang et al., 2015; Hale & Guan, 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Sabbath|The Sabbath as rest]]** (Jewish tradition, Genesis)
- **[[Simplicity|Simplicity and disconnection]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 19)

## Practice

- Schedule digital sabbath periods: 1 hour before bed, one day per week, or specific times during the day. Complete disconnection from devices and media. Use this time for restoration (nature, reading, connection, rest).

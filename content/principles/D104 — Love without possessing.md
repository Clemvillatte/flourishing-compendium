> **Principle 104** — Love without possessing

The formula runs through three chapters. Chapter 2 says of the sage's work that things "grow, and there is no claim made for their ownership". Chapter 10 says the Tao "produces them and does not claim them as its own". Chapter 51 says the same and names the pattern.

> It produces them and makes no claim to the possession of them; it
> carries them through their processes and does not vaunt its ability in
> doing so; it brings them to maturity and exercises no control over
> them;--this is called its mysterious operation.
>
> (Daodejing 51, Legge)

Applied to love, the three negatives each name a different failure of ownership: claiming the person, taking credit for their growth, controlling what they become. What is left after the three refusals is the giving itself.

(DDJ 2, 10, 51.)

**Plain words**
You can give someone everything they need to grow and still not own the plant. Possessive love keeps score: gifts become leverage, sacrifices become invoices. Love on the Tao's pattern does the giving and drops the ledger. The test the chapters offer is what happens in you when the loved thing grows away from you.

*For example:* Parents who let their kid quit the violin they themselves love — and discover the kid flourishes at something else — give birth without owning.

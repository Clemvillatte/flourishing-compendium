> **Principle 55** — Recognise and work with attentional conditioning: what you practise becomes default

Attention is conditioned by practice. If you practise distraction (constant task switching, social media, notifications), you condition distraction. If you practise focus (deep work, meditation, single-tasking), you condition focus. What you practise becomes your default.

**Plain words** Attention is a voting record: whatever you practise becomes the default. Practise switching and you get a switcher. Practise dwelling and you get a dweller. There's no neutral — the default is being set right now, by you.

*For example:* The "I can't focus on books anymore" person — who reads 200 feed fragments a day with perfect focus. They can focus; they've just been training the wrong sport.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional habits are conditioned by practice**, repeated patterns become automatic (Wood & Neal, 2007; Verplanken, 2006)
- **Media multitasking conditions distractibility**, heavy media multitaskers show worse sustained attention and greater distractibility (Ophir et al., 2009; Uncapher et al., 2016)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as habit]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Karma|Karma (action conditions future)]]** (Buddhism)
- **We become what we repeatedly do** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II; attributed to Will Durant)

## Practice

- Recognise that your attentional habits are conditioned by what you practise. If you want better attention, practise better attention. Reduce practising distraction (multitasking, notifications, rapid task-switching).

> **Principle 55** — Recognise and work with attentional conditioning: what you practise becomes default

For attention, the question has been studied where the practice is easiest to count: media multitasking. Ophir and colleagues compared people who habitually juggle many media streams with those who rarely do, on standard measures of cognitive control. The heavy multitaskers came out worse on filtering: more susceptible to interference from irrelevant stimuli in the environment and from irrelevant material in memory, and worse at task-switching.

> "Results showed that heavy media multitaskers are more susceptible to interference from irrelevant environmental stimuli and from irrelevant representations in memory. This led to the surprising result that heavy media multitaskers performed worse on a test of task-switching ability, likely due to reduced ability to filter out interference from the irrelevant task set." (Ophir, Nass & Wagner, "Cognitive control in media multitaskers", PNAS 106, 2009, p. 15583, abstract, [full text via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2747164/))

The study is correlational: it does not settle whether heavy multitasking trains distractibility or distractibility draws people toward heavy multitasking. The authors' own reading is a style difference — heavy multitaskers may have a greater tendency toward bottom-up attentional control and exploratory information processing, letting in other sources of information at some cost to the primary task ([full text via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2747164/)). Either way, the hours are practice: a day spent switching is a day of switching practice, whichever way the arrow runs.

**Plain words**
How you spend your attention hours is what your attention gets good at. The study cannot say which way the arrow points — maybe restless attention seeks more feeds, maybe feeds train restless attention. Both readings agree on one thing: the default is being set by the daily pattern.

**For example**
The person who says they can no longer focus on books, yet reads two hundred feed fragments a day with perfect focus. The focus is there. What it has been landing on is fragments.

## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional habits are conditioned by practice**, repeated patterns become automatic (Wood & Neal, 2007; Verplanken, 2006)
- **Media multitasking is associated with distractibility**, heavy media multitaskers show worse filtering and task-switching; the causal direction is not settled (Ophir et al., 2009; Uncapher et al., 2016)

## Practice

- Recognise that your attentional habits are conditioned by what you practise. If you want better attention, practise better attention. Reduce practising distraction (multitasking, notifications, rapid task-switching).

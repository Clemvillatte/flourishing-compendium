> **Principle 126** — Sequence by need, not prestige

The curriculum's trap: practicing what flatters (advanced concentration) while neglecting what is needed (sleep, ethics, moderation). The tradition's own sequence is need-ordered, and this principle protects it. Practically, once per season, rank your actual needs against your actual practice, and move practice time toward the top of the needs list.

(YS 2.28-2.29; BG 6.16-6.17.)

**Plain words** The curriculum's trap: practising what flatters (advanced concentration) while neglecting what's needed (sleep, ethics, moderation). The tradition's own sequence is need-ordered. Once per season, rank your actual needs against your actual practice — and obey the ranking.

*For example:* The meditator with the sophisticated practice and the four-hour sleep debt — the season's honest ranking says: bed. The advanced technique can wait; it was built on the foundations being skipped.

> **Principle 32** — Practise prosocial behaviour for autonomous reasons

Helping others produces well-being gains for the helper, but only when the helping is autonomous, chosen rather than coerced. Prosocial spending, acts of kindness, and volunteering all show modest but reliable effects on well-being. The mechanism is not the act itself but the satisfaction of the need for relatedness and competence that prosocial behaviour can provide.

(Dunn et al., 2008; Weinstein and Ryan, 2010; Post, 2005.)

**Plain words** Helping others lifts the helper's well-being, but only when the help is chosen, not forced. Spending on others, small kindnesses and volunteering all show modest, reliable effects. The mechanism is satisfying the needs for connection and competence.

*For example:* Volunteering at a food bank because you want to, versus because a manager requires it.

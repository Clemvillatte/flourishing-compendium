> **Principle 230** — Handle poverty with dignity

Poverty is a dispreferred indifferent. The Stoic can live well in poverty. Seneca practised voluntary poverty to demonstrate that wealth is not necessary. Musonius Rufus taught that the philosopher can live on very little. Do not let your circumstances determine your character.

(Seneca, Letters 17, 18, 87; Epictetus, Disc. I.9, I.25, III.7, III.8; Ench. 15; Med. III.6, VI.13, IX.6; Musonius Rufus, Lectures.)

**Plain words** Poverty is a dispreferred indifferent, and a Stoic can live well in it. Seneca practised voluntary poverty to show wealth isn't needed, and Musonius said the philosopher can live on very little. Don't let circumstances decide your character.

*For example:* A family on a tight budget makes dinner together a ritual that no restaurant could provide.

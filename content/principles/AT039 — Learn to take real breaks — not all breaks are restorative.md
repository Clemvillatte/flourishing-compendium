> **Principle 39** — Learn to take real breaks: not all breaks are restorative

A break is only restorative if it actually restores attention. Scrolling social media, checking news, or watching videos are not genuinely restorative — they continue to deplete directed attention and add cognitive load. True breaks involve disengagement.

**Plain words** A break only counts if it actually restores — and a scroll is not a break, it's a different task with worse lighting. Real breaks: movement, nature, nothing, or faces. The phone is a slot machine wearing a break's clothes.

*For example:* "I took a break — 40 minutes on my phone" and returning more fried than before. That wasn't a break; that was a second job with no pay.
## Psychology parallels

- **Not all breaks are restorative**, passive screen-based "breaks" do not restore attention and may further deplete it (Kim et al., 2017; Zacher et al., 2014)
- **Restorative breaks involve disengagement and low demand**, walking, nature, daydreaming, or social connection restore attention (Hunter & Wu, 2016; Lim et al., 2018)

## Philosophy parallels

- **True rest vs pseudo-rest** (Daoism, Daodejing 16)
- **[[Sabbath|The Sabbath as genuine rest]]** (Jewish tradition)
- **[[Simplicity|Simplicity and rest]]** (Lin Yutang, The Importance of Living)

## Practice

- Take breaks that actually restore: walk (ideally outside), look at nature, stretch, daydream, or connect with someone. Do not use "break" time to scroll or check news.

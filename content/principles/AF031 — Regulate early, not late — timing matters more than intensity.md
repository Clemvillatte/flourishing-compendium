> **Principle 31** — Regulate early, not late: timing matters more than intensity

In their account of emotion regulation effectiveness, Sheppes and Gross contrast two specific strategies: reappraisal, which attends to emotional information and reinterprets it, and distraction, which blocks the information before it gathers force. ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118133880.hop205018)) In their framework, reappraisal is effective at low emotional intensity but becomes costly and effortful at high intensity, while distraction is expected to provide short-term relief from high-intensity stimuli with minimal effort. The compendium proposes noting when an emotion first becomes noticeable and recording its intensity before deciding what to try.

> "For these reasons reappraisal is effective in the context of a low intensity emotional response, but it becomes costly and effortful in the context of a high intensity emotional response." (Sheppes, G., & Gross, J. J. (2012). Emotion Regulation Effectiveness: What Works When. Handbook of Psychology, Second Edition, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118133880.hop205018))

**Plain words** In this framework, the reinterpretation strategy is described as effective when a feeling is small and costly when it is large ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781118133880.hop205018)). The compendium proposes noting when an emotion first becomes noticeable and recording its intensity before deciding what to try — a proposal, not a guarantee.

**For example (imagined):** The person who catches the first warmth of anger at low activation tries reframing it then; the one who first notices at full fury tries the look-away strategy instead. Neither option is guaranteed to work.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Temporal dynamics of regulation|Temporal dynamics of regulation]]**, reappraisal is more effective when applied early; suppression is more necessary when applied late (Sheppes & Gross, 2012)
- **High-intensity emotions reduce cognitive regulation capacity**, executive resources are consumed by the emotion itself (McRae et al., 2012)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The beginning is half of the whole** (Plato, Laws)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as prevention]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 64)

## Practice

- Learn to notice early bodily signals (tight chest, shallow breath) and intervene before the emotion peaks. This is more efficient than trying to regulate a full-blown emotional episode.

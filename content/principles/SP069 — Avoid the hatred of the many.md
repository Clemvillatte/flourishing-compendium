> **Principle 69** — Avoid the hatred of the many

Ethics IV P70 gives the free person's social rule: "A free man who lives among the ignorant tries his hardest not to take favours from them" ([Ethics IV P70, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). Someone who has done you a favour "will value it according to his own lights, and will suffer unpleasure if he sees that the recipient values it less than he does," so accepting favours from people you will not repay on their own terms can create resentment ([Ethics IV P70 proof, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The free man avoids the favours of the ignorant "wanting not to be hated by them, and wanting to be guided not by their wishes but only by reason" ([Ethics IV P70 proof, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The scholium qualifies the rule: help may be necessary, and accepting it requires thanks "in a way they will appreciate"; declining a favour should avoid seeming "to disdain what is offered, or to be meanly afraid of having to repay them," for that would "get us hated by the very act of trying to avoid their hate" ([Ethics IV P70 scholium, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)).

> "So in declining favours we must take account of what is useful as well as of what is honourable." (Spinoza, Ethics IV P70 scholium, [Bennett clear-language translation](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf))

**Plain words**
A favour sets up an expectation in the giver about what it was worth and what comes back, and those expectations differ between giver and receiver ([Ethics IV P70 proof, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). Needed help should still be accepted, with thanks the giver can recognize ([Ethics IV P70 scholium, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). When declining a favour, decline in a way that does not look down on the gift or on the giver, because a clumsy refusal creates the very hatred it was meant to avoid ([Ethics IV P70 scholium, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)).

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: you decline an expensive gift with thanks, without mocking its value to the giver.

(Ethics IV, P70, with proof and scholium.)

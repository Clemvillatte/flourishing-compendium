> **Principle 69** — Avoid the hatred of the many

The free person avoids being feared and hated by the many, and Spinoza's reason is practical: hatred is the one social force that can nullify all other advantages. The sage is not a crowd-pleaser; the sage simply does not spend power making enemies. Practically, before taking a strong stand, audit its enemy-generation: can the same position be held with fewer people feeling personally attacked? That audit is not weakness; it is strategy.

(Ethics IV, P70, whose proof counsels the free person lest they become hateful to the ignorant; the appendix's items on hatred.)

**Plain words** The free person avoids being feared and hated by the many — hatred is the one social force that can nullify all other advantages. The sage is not a crowd-pleaser; the sage simply doesn't spend power making enemies. Avoid the hatred of the many; it's an efficiency rule, not a popularity rule.

*For example:* The brilliant executive who leaves a trail of humiliated subordinates — every advantage of the talent nullified by the enemies ledger — versus the equally demanding one who corrects without wounding. Same standards; one of them keeps the allies.

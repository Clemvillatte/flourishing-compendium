> **Principle 69** — Avoid the hatred of the many

Ethics IV P70 gives the free person's social rule: "A free man who lives among the ignorant tries his hardest not to take favours from them" ([Ethics IV P70, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The mechanism is not snobbery. Someone who has done you a favour "will value it according to his own lights, and will suffer unpleasure if he sees that the recipient values it less than he does," so accepting favours from people you will not repay on their own terms manufactures resentment ([Ethics IV P70 proof, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The free man avoids the favours of the ignorant "wanting not to be hated by them, and wanting to be guided not by their wishes but only by reason." The scholium adds the delicate part: a clumsy refusal would "get us hated by the very act of trying to avoid their hate," so in declining favours "we must take account of what is useful as well as of what is honourable" ([Ethics IV P70 scholium, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)).

> "So in declining favours we must take account of what is useful as well as of what is honourable." (Spinoza, Ethics IV P70 scholium, [Bennett clear-language translation](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf))

**Plain words**
The rule is about debts of gratitude, not about pleasing crowds. A favour creates a small ledger in the giver's head: what he gave, what it was worth to him, what he expects back. Take favours from people whose ledgers you cannot or will not settle, and each entry turns to resentment. The free person's move is to avoid entering those ledgers at all, and when declining a favour, to decline gently, because a clumsy refusal creates the very hatred it was meant to avoid.

**For example**
A manager who lets her team cover her small mistakes owes each of them a small debt; when she later has to rate them, they read the ratings as default. The manager who never took those favours owes no one an audit.

(Ethics IV, P70, with proof and scholium.)

> **Principle 36** — Learn to use context-dependent repetition: same context, same behaviour

Gardner, Lally and Wardle summarise the mechanism:

> "Decades of psychological research consistently show that mere repetition of a simple action in a consistent context leads, through associative learning, to the action being activated upon subsequent exposure to those contextual cues (that is, habitually)." (Gardner, Lally & Wardle, 2012, "Habit formation and health" section)

The same article reduces the advice to one line: "repeat a chosen behaviour in the same context, until it becomes automatic and effortless". One study cited by the review followed volunteers repeating a chosen behaviour in a consistent context: "96 volunteers chose an eating, drinking or activity behaviour to carry out daily in the same context (for example 'after breakfast') for 12 weeks" (Lally et al., 2010, abstract). Two of that study's results scope the expectation: the modeled estimates of the time to reach 95% of each person's automaticity asymptote "ranged from 18 to 254 days" (estimates, some extending beyond the study's 12-week observation window), and "Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process". The compendium's suggested use: pick a context first, a time and place you meet every day, and repeat the behaviour inside it.

**Plain words** Habits are learned associations: same cue, same action, repeated, until the cue itself activates the action. The practice is to choose the cue deliberately (after breakfast, at the desk) rather than leaving it to whatever context happens to be around. A single missed day did not measurably set the study's participants back.

*For example (imagined):* Someone chooses to write one journal entry after evening tea in the same chair, and records whether they do so.

## Practice

- Build habits through context-dependent repetition: do the behaviour at the same time, in the same place, after the same preceding action. "After I brush my teeth, I will meditate for 5 minutes." Consistency of context.

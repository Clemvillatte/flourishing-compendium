> **Principle 36** — Learn to use context-dependent repetition: same context, same behaviour

Habits are formed through context-dependent repetition — doing the same behaviour in the same context. The context (time, place, preceding action) becomes the trigger. Consistency of context is the key.

**Plain words** Habits form by same-place-same-time repetition — the context becomes the trigger. Consistency of setting matters more than intensity of effort. Same cue, same behaviour, until the cue does the work.

*For example:* The journal that only gets written in the same armchair after the same cup of tea — move the ritual to "whenever" and it dies. The armchair is half the habit.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Context-dependent repetition (Lally)|Context-dependent repetition (Lally)]]**, habits form when behaviour is repeated in consistent context (Lally et al., 2010; Gardner, 2015)
- **Context as trigger**, environmental cues become automatic triggers for behaviour (Wood & Neal, 2007; Gardner, 2015)

## Practice

- Build habits through context-dependent repetition: do the behaviour at the same time, in the same place, after the same preceding action. "After I brush my teeth, I will meditate for 5 minutes." Consistency of context.

> **Principle 195** — Creative emotion (émotion créatrice) is generative of thought, not a byproduct of it

Bergson's account of creative emotion distinguishes two directions of influence. A representation can give rise to a feeling; alternatively, an emotion can generate representations. Leonard Lawlor and Valentine Moulard-Leonard explain the latter through Bergson's example of a musician whose emotion gives rise to a symphony and its written score ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Henri Bergson,” section 6](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)).

> "in creative emotion, we first have the emotion which then creates representations." (Lawlor and Moulard-Leonard, section 6.)

### An account of creation

The discussion belongs to Bergson's philosophy of open morality and dynamic religion in The Two Sources of Morality and Religion. It is not a classification for diagnosing emotional states, nor a reason to dismiss work begun with a plan. The compendium's application is to leave room, in reflection on a completed creative effort, for feeling to have contributed to its direction.

### Plain words

When recalling how you made something, ask whether you began with a clear idea or found the idea while trying to express a feeling. Either answer is possible. The exercise invites a description of your process, not a verdict on whether the result is alive or worthwhile.

### For example

Imagine writing a scene and only later finding words for the feeling that led you to it. You might describe that sequence in a notebook, while acknowledging that memory does not establish a universal theory of creativity.

## Sources

- [Leonard Lawlor and Valentine Moulard-Leonard, “Henri Bergson,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, section 6](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/): opened scholarly exposition of The Two Sources of Morality and Religion. The quotation is the commentators' wording, not a verified quotation from Bergson's book.

## Practice

- After a creative effort, describe any feelings you remember contributing to it. This reflection is not a reason to delay help with distress.

## Limits

- Not a claim that difficult emotions are always creative or should be romanticized. Not a clinical prescription.

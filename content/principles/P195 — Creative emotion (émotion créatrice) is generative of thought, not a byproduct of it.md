> **Principle 195** — Creative emotion (émotion créatrice) is generative of thought, not a byproduct of it

Bergson distinguishes two kinds of emotion. The first is a surface agitation triggered by a representation (a thought or image). This kind disperses and is downstream of cognition. The second — what he calls creative emotion — is a deep, whole-organism movement that *generates* ideas rather than being generated by them. "Creation means, before all, emotion," he says.

This is a phenomenological distinction, not a clinical one. Bergson is not claiming all emotions are creative — surface agitation is real and often needs practical handling.

**Plain words** There are two kinds of emotion. The first is surface agitation triggered by a representation — it disperses, and it's downstream of thought. The second, creative emotion, is a deep whole-organism movement that generates ideas rather than being generated by them. 'Creation means, before all, emotion.'

*For example:* The artist who starts with a feeling they can't name and ends with a painting — the emotion came first and the idea was its product. Versus the mood board assembled from thoughts, which produces competent and dead work. The order matters.

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[P191 — Joy is the felt signature of creation; distinct from pleasure]]**, complementary: joy is the felt signature; creative emotion is the generative ground
- **(Daoism)** The Daoist emphasis on felt sense / direct knowing over conceptual analysis

## Practice

- When a deep emotion arrives, before treating it as a symptom to regulate, ask: is this a surface agitation (triggered by a thought) or a deep movement (generative)?

## Limits

- Not a claim that difficult emotions are always creative or should be romanticized. Not a clinical prescription.

> **Principle 500** — Practise the will to power

In Beyond Good and Evil, section 13, Nietzsche rejects self-preservation as the basic drive ([trans. Helen Zimmern, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4363/pg4363.txt)):

> A living thing seeks above all to DISCHARGE its strength--life itself is WILL TO POWER; self-preservation is only one of the indirect and most frequent RESULTS thereof.

That supports the page's line that the drive is "not the will to survive". The claim that it is not about domination is harder to defend. In "Self-Surpassing" Life speaks of itself as that "WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF", yet the same speech says "even in the will of the servant found I the will to be master" and "That to the stronger the weaker shall serve" ([Thus Spake Zarathustra, trans. Thomas Common, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1998/pg1998.txt)).

Scholars split on this. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nietzsche/) says Walter Kaufmann stressed "internally directed self-control and the development of cultural excellence, rather than domination of others", and that his approach "threatens to sanitize aspects of Nietzsche's view". Richardson's distinction between tyranny and mastery concedes that the doctrine "countenances tyranny as well as mastery, even if it privileges the latter" (section 3.2.1).

(Nietzsche, Beyond Good and Evil, 13; Thus Spoke Zarathustra, "Of Self-Overcoming".)

**Plain words** Nietzsche makes growth and discharge of strength the core of living things. Whether this means mastery of oneself, or power over others as well, is disputed. The page's version, self-overcoming rather than domination, is one strand of interpretation, associated with Kaufmann. The posthumous compilation The Will to Power is not used here, since the encyclopedia calls the editing unreliable.

For example (imagined): a violinist who practises to beat her own earlier performance is an easy case for the self-overcoming reading. A manager who builds a team to win a rivalry shows why the other reading remains live.

> **Principle 247** — Practise self-dialogue

Marcus's Meditations is a dialogue with himself. He addresses himself in the second person: "you will not be angry." This self-dialogue is a form of cognitive restructuring. Talk to yourself as a wise friend would.

(Med., throughout; Seneca, Letters 83; Disc. III.23.)

**Plain words** Notice how Marcus talks to himself in the Meditations: "you will not be angry today," like a coach in his own ear. That inner voice is trainable. Speak to yourself the way a wise friend would — direct, warm, unbribable.

*For example:* The student who catches the spiral "I'm going to fail everything" and answers out loud, "no — you're unprepared for one quiz. Study for the quiz."

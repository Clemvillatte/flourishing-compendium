> **Principle 247** — Practise self-dialogue

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes the Meditations as "written to and for himself." Scholars now generally agree, it says, that Marcus "wrote for his own moral improvement, to remind himself of and render concrete the Stoic doctrines he wanted to live by." ([SEP, Marcus Aurelius, section 1.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcus-aurelius/).)

### Talking to yourself in the second person

The entry quotes a passage in which he speaks to himself directly about his own failure to live as a philosopher:

> "...you are no longer able to have lived your whole life as a philosopher since youth; and it is clear to many others and to you yourself that you are far from philosophy... For your experience tells you how much you have strayed." (Meditations VIII.1, as translated in [SEP, section 2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcus-aurelius/).)

The same entry shows how short these reminders can be. Its examples include "Erase impressions!" and "Those who now bury will soon be buried!", and it quotes Marcus's own view that for one "bitten by true doctrines even the briefest saying suffices as a reminder" (SEP, section 1.1, citing Meditations X.34). It adds that Epictetus advised writing down, as well as rehearsing, the responses one ought to have, so they are "ready at hand" when needed (SEP, section 1.1, citing Epictetus, Discourses I.1.21-25).

### What the entry cautions

SEP warns that "when Marcus says 'p', he is not always simply expressing his belief that p," and it suggests asking what moral and psychological effect he is trying to achieve (section 1.1). The statement on this page calls the practice cognitive restructuring, which is a modern label the entry does not use.

**Plain words** Most of us already talk to ourselves, usually as a critic. Marcus writes to himself the way a firm coach would: direct, specific about what he has failed to do, and short enough to remember. The skill is choosing the voice. Ask what you would say to a friend in the same spot, and say that.

*For example:* The student who catches the spiral "I'm going to fail everything" and answers out loud, "no, you're unprepared for one quiz. Study for the quiz."

> **Principle 81** — Cultivate psychological flexibility

Psychological flexibility, the capacity to stay present with unwanted experience and act on values, is the core process of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy. It is not a single technique but a set of processes: acceptance, [[Cognitive defusion|cognitive defusion]], present-moment awareness, self-as-context, [[Values clarification|values clarification]], and committed action. Meta-analyses show that psychological flexibility predicts well-being and mediates the effects of therapy across diverse populations.

(Hayes et al., 2006; Ruiz, 2010; Gloster et al., 2020.)

**Plain words** Psychological flexibility means staying present with what hurts while acting on your values. It's the core of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: acceptance, defusion from thoughts, contact with the present, perspective on the self, values and committed action.

*For example:* Feeling anxious before the meeting and going anyway, with the anxiety along for the ride.

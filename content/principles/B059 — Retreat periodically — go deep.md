> **Principle 59** — Retreat periodically: go deep

MN 27 places seclusion within [[Gradual training|Gradual training]], after ethical conduct, sense restraint and mindfulness. Its practitioner seeks a secluded dwelling and prepares to meditate ([Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.027.than.html)):

> After his meal, returning from his alms round, he sits down, crosses his legs, holds his body erect, and brings mindfulness to the fore.

(MN 27, [Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.027.than.html).)

This is a monastic training account, not a schedule of periodic silent retreats. MacLean and colleagues studied a different, modern setting: 60 participants randomly assigned to a three-month retreat or a wait-list, with about five hours of daily shamatha practice; they reported improvements in visual discrimination ([study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/)). That finding does not promise the same result from a weekend away.

**Plain words** If you want time for sustained practice, decide what time you can actually set aside and what support you need. Ask about the programme, its demands and your options if you need to stop. More time alone need not be the right choice on every occasion.

*For example:* Imagine wanting a week away but having care responsibilities. You arrange a shorter practice session with reliable cover, rather than leaving someone unsupported or treating the shorter session as worthless.

> **Principle 21** — Learn to use exercise for sleep: timing matters

Exercise helps sleep, but the claim that evening exercise is the problem is weaker than the seed suggests. A meta-analysis of six randomised trials in middle-aged and older adults with sleep problems (305 participants) found better global sleep quality scores in exercisers (SMD 0.47) and shorter sleep latency, but no significant differences in sleep duration or efficiency ([Yang et al. 2012, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22884182/)).

On timing, a meta-analysis of 23 studies of single evening sessions in healthy adults concluded that they "do not support the hypothesis that evening exercise negatively affects sleep, in fact rather the opposite". The exception was vigorous exercise ending within an hour of bedtime, after which sleep-onset latency, total sleep time and efficiency might be impaired ([Stutz et al. 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-018-1015-0)). A meta-analysis of 15 studies of evening high-intensity exercise found that sessions ending 0.5 to 4 hours before sleep reduced REM sleep by 2.34% and changed nothing else significant, and that sessions ending two hours or more before bed were not disruptive ([Clinical review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1087079221001209)). In a poll of American adults, evening exercisers did not differ in sleep from non-exercisers, and morning vigorous exercisers reported the best sleep ([Buman et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24933083/)).

### Scope

The experiments mostly involve healthy, good sleepers and single sessions. The advice that morning or afternoon is "safest" has no direct support in the studies above. I did not open Dolezal 2017. Individual responses vary, so a personal test is more informative than a rule.

**Plain words**
Regular exercise helps sleep. Hard exercise in the last hour before bed may delay sleep, while earlier evening sessions usually do not.

**For example**
For two weeks, keep hard sessions at least two hours before bed, and compare your sleep log with the previous fortnight.

## Psychology parallels

- **Exercise and sleep**, regular exercise improves sleep quality (Buman et al., 2014; Yang et al., 2012)
- **Timing of exercise**, vigorous exercise close to bedtime can disrupt sleep in some people (Buman et al., 2014; Dolezal et al., 2017)

## Practice

- Use exercise for sleep: regular exercise improves sleep, but finish vigorous exercise at least 3 hours before bed. Gentle movement (walking, stretching, yoga) close to bedtime is fine.

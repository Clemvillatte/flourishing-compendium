> **Principle 87** — Assimilate until it changes perception

The third step, nididhyasana, is not thinking but dwelling: returning attention to the understood truth until it becomes how the mind sees, the way an absorbed language stops being translated. The tradition places all the weight here, because understanding without assimilation evaporates under pressure. Practically, choose one truth per month and revisit it daily until it colors perception unprompted.

(Shankara, Upadesasahasri; YS 1.14's three conditions apply here.)

**Plain words** The third step, nididhyasana, is not thinking but dwelling: returning attention to the understood truth until it becomes how the mind sees — the way an absorbed language stops being translated. All the weight is here; understanding without assimilation changes nothing.

*For example:* Someone "understands perfectly well" that the anxiety is a false alarm — and the understanding changes nothing, because it's still being translated. The one who dwells on it daily until it's just how they see it — that one stops flinching.

> **Principle 356** — Practise substitution

> Responsibility for another is not an accident that happens to a subject, but precedes essence in it, has not awaited freedom, in which a commitment to another would have been made. I have not done anything and I have always been under accusation persecuted. The ipseity in the passivity without arche characteristic of identity, is a hostage. The word I means here I am, answering for everything and for everyone.

(Levinas, Otherwise than Being, or Beyond Essence, ch. IV "Substitution," trans. Alphonso Lingis, Duquesne University Press, p. 114; text as reproduced with page numbers on [a University of Texas course dossier](https://sites.dwrl.utexas.edu/davis/european-graduate-school/levinas-substitution/).)

A passage on p. 118 of the same chapter reads: "The ego is not an entity 'capable' of expiating for the others: it is this original expiation." On p. 100-101 Levinas calls the relation of proximity "an extremely urgent assignation—an obligation, anachronously prior to any commitment." In his own sense, then, "substitution" does not mean a decision to take someone's place. It names a condition of the self that comes before choice.

The entry's phrase "you don't opt into it" matches that structure. Its advice to "let it ground your selfhood" is the compendium's reading and is not found in the passages above. Levinas also speaks of the self as hostage and expiation, so the demand is much heavier than ordinary kindness, and the compendium's softer example of a family role is an analogy, not his meaning. Whether Levinas's account fits everyday duties of care is a question for interpreters, and none were opened here.

The psychology parallel cites Batson (2011) on altruism. That book was not opened, so the link between empathy-based helping and Levinas's pre-reflective responsibility is not checked.

**Plain words** For Levinas, responsibility for the other is not something I take on after thinking it over. It is there before my freedom, and he says the self is called to its uniqueness by it. The word for this extreme condition is substitution, and the self as hostage is his picture of it.

*For example (imagined):* An eldest sibling resents being the responsible one for years, then one day sees that the pull of the family's needs was never a role assigned to them. Levinas goes much further, since his claim applies to everyone, about everyone.

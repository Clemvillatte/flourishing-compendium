> **Principle 105** — Know the three strands

The Gita's descriptive psychology: sattva (lucidity, binding by attachment to ease and knowledge), rajas (passion, binding by craving and restlessness), tamas (inertia, binding by negligence and sleep). Each strand binds in its own way, and the first step is seeing which is currently running you. The book's caution: this is a descriptive typology, not a validated scientific taxonomy. Practically, name the strand before choosing the response: lucidity clarifies, passion energizes, inertia requires activation before anything else.

(BG 14.5-14.9.)

**Plain words** The three strands: sattva (lucidity, binding by attachment to ease and knowledge), rajas (passion, binding by craving and restlessness), tamas (inertia, binding by negligence and sleep). Each binds in its own way — and the first step is seeing which one is currently running you.

*For example:* Someone learns the map and starts reading their states like weather reports: "this is rajas, third day running" — and the reading itself, without force, starts shifting the mix. You can't choose your weather without knowing it.

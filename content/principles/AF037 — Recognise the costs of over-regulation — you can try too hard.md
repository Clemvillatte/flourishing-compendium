> **Principle 37** — Recognise the costs of over-regulation: you can try too hard

Constantly monitoring and regulating your affect can itself become a source of distress. People who are hypervigilant about their emotional state — always checking "am I okay?" — are worse off than those who allow feelings to come and go.

**Plain words** Constantly monitoring and regulating your affect can itself become distress: the hypervigilant "am I okay?" checkers are worse off than the relaxed. You can try too hard. Sometimes the right move with a feeling is to leave it alone and make tea.

*For example:* The meditation practitioner who scans their state every eleven minutes — "am I calm yet? now? now?" — and the teacher's advice that finally lands: drop the monitoring. The watching was the wind.
## Psychology parallels

- **Over-regulation is associated with worse outcomes**, excessive monitoring of internal states is associated with anxiety and depression (Aldao et al., 2015)
- **Meta-emotion: judging feelings as bad amplifies them**, secondary distress about primary distress compounds suffering (Levitt et al., 2004)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as non-interference]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 37)
- **[[The middle path|The middle way]]** (Buddhism, Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta)

## Practice

- Do not treat every feeling as a problem to be solved. Some feelings just need to be felt and allowed to pass.

> **Principle 37** — Recognise the costs of over-regulation: you can try too hard

Effortful control of a feeling can add a second layer of distress on top of the feeling it targets. In a study of sixty patients with panic disorder, Levitt and colleagues compared two audiotape instructions for meeting an induced panic sensation before a carbon dioxide challenge: acceptance and suppression. Suppression carried a cost the control condition did not, since participants who used it reported more subjective anxiety during the challenge, while participants given the acceptance instruction were more willing to sit through a second one ([Levitt et al., 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0005-7894(04)80018-2)). The compendium reads this as the shape of over-regulation in general: a watching, controlling stance toward your own affect can become its own source of distress, separate from whatever feeling started it.

> "Use of suppression was related to more subjective anxiety during the challenge, and use of acceptance was related to more willingness to participate in a second challenge." (Levitt, J. T., Brown, T. A., Orsillo, S. M., & Barlow, D. H. (2004). The effects of acceptance versus suppression of emotion on subjective and psychophysiological response to carbon dioxide challenge in patients with panic disorder. Behavior Therapy, 35, 747-766, [abstract](https://contextualscience.org/publications/levitt_brown_orsillo_barlow_2004))

**Plain words** Watch what happens when you fight a feeling. In the study above, the people told to suppress were more anxious during the challenge than the people told to let the sensations be there. Asking yourself "am I okay yet?" every few minutes is itself a chore, and it hands the feeling more attention rather than less. Sometimes the right move with a feeling is to stop supervising it.

*For example (imagined):* A meditator checks every few minutes whether calm has arrived and gets tenser with each check. When the teacher tells her to drop the monitoring and just follow the breath, the tension eases before any technique does.

## Psychology parallels

- **Over-regulation is associated with worse outcomes**, excessive monitoring of internal states is associated with anxiety and depression (Aldao et al., 2015)
- **Meta-emotion: judging feelings as bad amplifies them**, secondary distress about primary distress compounds suffering (Levitt et al., 2004)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as non-interference]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 37)
- **[[The middle path|The middle way]]** (Buddhism, Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta)

## Practice

- Do not treat every feeling as a problem to be solved. Some feelings just need to be felt and allowed to pass.

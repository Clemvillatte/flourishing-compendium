> **Principle 12** — Savor positive experience

Bryant and Veroff define [[Savoring|Savoring]] as the capacity "to attend to, appreciate, and enhance the positive experience in one's life". What the daily-diary work adds is mechanism: in Jose, Lim and Bryant's study, 101 participants reported momentary positive events, savoring responses and affect daily for 30 days, and momentary savoring both mediated and moderated the impact of daily positive events on momentary happy mood.

> These results provide support for the hypothesis that savoring is an important mechanism through which people derive happiness from positive events.
> (Jose, Lim and Bryant 2012, Journal of Positive Psychology 7(3))

The intervention evidence is more modest than the correlational work. Hurley and Kwon's two-week training in memory building and expressing positive emotions decreased self-reported depressive symptoms and negative affect relative to controls, while positive affect did not differ. The compendium's application is attentional: when something good happens, extend it deliberately, notice the sensory detail, say it out loud or take a mental photograph, and let the moment survive the reach for the phone.

**Plain words** Most good moments die of inattention, not of ending. The meal ends either way; the minute you spend actually tasting it was available or not. Savoring is the decision to spend that minute: to look at the person across the table, to replay the good conversation before opening the news, to let a small win sit for an evening before asking what is next. It costs nothing but the habit of noticing.

*For example:* After a good conversation, you sit for a minute and replay it before reaching for your phone.

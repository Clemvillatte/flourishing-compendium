> **Principle 62** — Savour positive experiences: deliberately attend to and amplify them

Savouring is the capacity to attend to, appreciate, and enhance positive experiences in the present, past, or future. It is a distinct skill from experiencing positive affect; it is the capacity to extract more from the same experience.

**Plain words** Savouring: the capacity to attend to, appreciate, and enhance positive experiences — a distinct skill from having them. You can be given a great evening and miss it entirely. Savour deliberately: attend, slow down, amplify.

*For example:* The holiday photos nobody looks at because the holiday was photographed instead of lived — versus the dinner where the phones stay in the pocket and someone says "this — right now — is the good part." The skill is noticing in time.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Savoring|Savouring amplifies positive affect]]**, deliberately attending to positive experiences increases their intensity and duration (Bryant, 1989; Bryant & Veroff, 2007)
- **Savouring interventions improve well-being**, savouring training increases positive affect and life satisfaction (Hurley & Kwon, 2013; Smith et al., 2014)

## Practice

- When something good happens, slow down. Name it. Attend to the bodily sensation of it. Share it with someone. Do not rush past it.

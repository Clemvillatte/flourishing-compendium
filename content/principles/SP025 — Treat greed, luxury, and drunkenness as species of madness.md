> **Principle 25** — Treat greed, luxury, and drunkenness as species of madness

In the note to Ethics IV, Prop. XLIV, Spinoza argues that avarice, ambition and lust are species of madness. His explanation concerns fixation: an emotion can hold the mind on one object so that it cannot think of others. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt))

> "But when a miser thinks of nothing but gain or money, or when an ambitious man thinks of nothing but glory, they are not reckoned to be mad, because they are generally harmful, and are thought worthy of being hated. But, in reality, Avarice, Ambition, Lust, &c., are species of madness, though they may not be reckoned among diseases."

(Ethics IV, Prop. XLIV, Note, Elwes translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt).)

Spinoza compares people absorbed in love, money or glory. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt)) His concern here is what happens when one object occupies the mind so completely that others disappear from attention. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt)) As a reflection exercise, ask whether you can still turn your attention to things outside a particular pursuit.

**Plain words** Think of a pursuit that has been filling your day. What has it pushed out of view? Use that question to reflect on Spinoza's description of absorption, not to decide whether someone is mentally ill.

*For example:* Imagined: a comfort purchase becomes the whole evening — the planning, the buying, the next purchase already forming — and other things have stopped getting attention.

> **Principle 5** — Learn to read your own body: interoception

The 2018 Interoception Summit roadmap, led by Khalsa and colleagues, defines interoception as "the process by which the nervous system senses, interprets, and integrates signals originating from within the body, providing a moment-by-moment mapping of the body's internal landscape across conscious and unconscious levels." It notes "a surge of interest" because of "its integral role in emotional experience, self-regulation, decision making, and consciousness" ([Khalsa et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6054486/)).

### Accuracy is one part

The roadmap separates accuracy ("correct and precise monitoring") from sensibility, a "self-perceived tendency to focus on interoceptive stimuli". Noticing a signal and reading it correctly are different skills.

### Alexithymia

Brewer, Cook and Bird found in 2016 that alexithymia, "traditionally characterized by difficulties identifying and describing one's own emotions", was associated with poor non-affective interoception in typical and clinical groups. They conclude it is "better characterized by a general failure of interoception." The study used self-report questionnaires, not heartbeat tasks ([Royal Society Open Science](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsos.150664)).

### Can it be trained?

In a randomised study of 40 healthy adults, one heartbeat-perception training session raised accuracy compared with the control group. Over the 3-week programme there was no significant improvement over time in accuracy or confidence, and the authors call for work on long-term effects ([Schillings et al., 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2022.838055/full)). The page's claim that the skill is "trainable" is supported for the short term only.

**Plain words** Interoception is how well you sense and interpret signals from inside your body: heartbeat, breath, gut. It is linked to emotional experience, and people who struggle to name their emotions tend to read body signals less well. Short training can sharpen heartbeat sensing. How lasting that is, is not yet clear.

*For example:* Imagine someone who never feels "stressed" yet has tightness in the chest and eats late at night. Learning to notice those signals gives them something to name.

## Psychology parallels

- **Interoception and emotion are linked**, interoceptive accuracy correlates with emotional awareness and regulation (Craig, 2009; Barrett et al., 2019)
- **Heartbeat-perception training can raise accuracy in the short term** (Schillings et al., 2022)

## Practice

- Spend 2–5 minutes a day scanning the body — heart, breath, gut, shoulders, jaw — without judging. Note the raw signal before the interpretation.

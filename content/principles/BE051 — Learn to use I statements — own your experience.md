> **Principle 51** — Learn to use "I" statements: own your experience

The term is Thomas Gordon's: he coined the I-message in the 1960s, in Parent Effectiveness Training (1970). The definition:

> "An I-message or I-statement is a form of interpersonal communication in which speakers express their feelings, beliefs, or values from the first-person perspective, usually beginning with I. It contrasted with 'you-message' or 'you-statement', which often begins with you and focuses on the listener, usually carrying accusatory language." (Wikipedia, I-message)

The marriage evidence is about where discussions land: in the newlywed study, "It was possible to predict marital outcome over a six-year period using just the first 3 minutes of data for both husbands and wives", and "Of the 17 couples who later divorced, all started off their conflict discussions with significantly greater displays of negative emotion and fewer expressions of positive emotion." An I-statement is one concrete way to begin gently.

**Plain words** "You always do this" makes a person defend themselves; "I felt hurt when this happened" gives them something to actually respond to. Own the experience instead of prosecuting the person. Same content, different courtroom.

*For example* "You never listen" starts a trial; "I felt brushed off at dinner" starts a conversation. One indicts, one invites. The pronoun is the whole technique.

## Practice

- Use "I" statements: "I feel [emotion] when [specific behaviour] because [impact]." Do not use "you always" or "you never." Describe behaviour, not character.

> **Principle 51** — Learn to use "I" statements: own your experience

Thomas Gordon coined the term in the 1960s and discussed it in Parent Effectiveness Training (1970). The definition:

> "An I-message or I-statement is a form of interpersonal communication in which speakers express their feelings, beliefs, or values from the first-person perspective, usually beginning with I. It contrasted with 'you-message' or 'you-statement', which often begins with you and focuses on the listener, usually carrying accusatory language." (Wikipedia, I-message)

A related finding concerns beginnings: in the newlywed study, "It was possible to predict marital outcome over a six-year period using just the first 3 minutes of data for both husbands and wives", and "Of the 17 couples who later divorced, all started off their conflict discussions with significantly greater displays of negative emotion and fewer expressions of positive emotion when compared with couples who remained married over the course of the 6-year study." The study observed affect during conflict discussions; it did not test I-statements. It is included here as related evidence on how discussions begin, not as proof of the technique.

**Plain words** Name what happened and how you felt instead of making a general accusation. Starting with "I" is not enough if the rest of the sentence still criticises the other person.

*For example* Imagined: "I felt brushed off at dinner" describes the speaker's experience; "you never listen" indicts the listener. The pronoun is a convention, not a guarantee: "I feel you are being defensive" is an accusation wearing the pronoun.

## Practice

- Use "I" statements: "I feel [emotion] when [specific behaviour] because [impact]." Do not use "you always" or "you never." Describe behaviour, not character.

> **Principle 51** — Learn to use "I" statements: own your experience

"I" statements ("I feel hurt when...") rather than "you" statements ("you always...") reduce defensiveness and improve communication. This is a specific communication behaviour.

**Plain words** "You always do this" makes a person defend themselves; "I felt hurt when this happened" gives them something to actually respond to. Own the experience instead of prosecuting the person. Same content, different courtroom.

*For example:* "You never listen" starts a trial — "I felt brushed off at dinner" starts a conversation. One indicts; one invites. The pronoun is the whole technique.
## Psychology parallels

- **"I" statements in communication**, owning experience reduces defensiveness (Gottman & Levenson, 2000; Markman et al., 1994)
- **Softened startup**, beginning conversations gently predicts better outcomes (Gottman & Levenson, 2000; Carrère & Gottman, 1999)

## Practice

- Use "I" statements: "I feel [emotion] when [specific behaviour] because [impact]." Do not use "you always" or "you never." Own your experience; describe behaviour, not character.

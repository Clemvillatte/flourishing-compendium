> **Principle 30** — Keep the precepts as training rules, not commandments

Bhikkhu Bodhi explains the precepts as commitments a person undertakes to train conduct and character. The restraint is specific: refrain from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech and intoxicants that lead to heedlessness. Good intentions alone do not replace the work of observing these rules. ([Taking the Precepts, I](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel282.html))

> "The precepts are not commandments imposed from without, but principles of training each one takes upon himself through his own initiative and endeavors to follow with awareness and understanding." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, Taking the Precepts, I](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel282.html))

The formula begins “I undertake”. Bodhi describes practice that may initially meet resistance, with repetition gradually developing the virtues the rules express. ([Taking the Precepts, I](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel282.html)) AN 8.41 gives a distinct, day-and-night observance in which practitioners follow the conduct of arahants. ([AN 8.41](https://cdn.britannica.com/primary_source/gutenberg/PGCC_classics/canon/anguttara/an08-041.html))

> "I undertake the precept to refrain from incorrect speech." ([The Five Precepts, fourth undertaking](https://accesstoinsight.org/ptf/dhamma/sila/pancasila.html))

**Plain words**
You give yourself a clear promise because you see a reason to keep it. “I want to be honest” becomes something you can practise when a lie would be useful. The choice is yours, and so is the work of keeping the promise when it is difficult. Repetition is how a chosen restraint becomes part of how you live. ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, Taking the Precepts, I](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel282.html))

For example: You missed a deadline and feel tempted to invent a computer failure. Draft the excuse and a truthful account, then compare what each would lead your colleague to believe. Send the truthful account, acknowledge the delay and offer a realistic next step. After the next deadline, check the messages you actually sent and keep whichever wording let you tell the truth.

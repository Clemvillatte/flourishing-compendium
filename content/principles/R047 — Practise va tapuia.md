> **Principle 309** — Practise va tapuia

Va tapuia is the sacred register of the vā: the relationship itself is tapu, set apart and carrying restrictions. Tui Atua's definition, quoted in a journal article on teu le va:

> "The Samoan word va tapuia, includes the term 'tapu,' within. The term literally refers to the sacred (tapu-ia) relationship (va) between man and all things, animate and inanimate. It implies that in our relationships with all things, living and dead, there exists a sacred essence, a life force beyond human reckoning." (Tui Atua Tupua Tamasese Taisi Efi, 2007, as quoted in Anae, "Teu le Va: Toward a Native Anthropology", Pacific Studies 33(2), 2010)

His colloquium paper names the working parts — "tapu or taboo, feagaiga or sacred covenants and tuaoi or boundaries" — and the four harmonies they hold in balance: "harmony with the cosmos; harmony with the environment; harmony with one's fellow men; and harmony with one's self." The feagaiga between brother and sister is the everyday face: "The feagaiga is both status and covenant." The practice is the maintaining — treating the space between you and the other, and between you and the land and the living things, as something with restrictions attached, and letting those restrictions do the relating.

(Samoan philosophy; Tuhiwai Smith, 1999, Decolonizing Methodologies; Wendt, 1996.)

**Plain words** The space between people is sacred — va tapuia — it carries a spiritual weight, and treating relationships as transactions is the violation. Honour the dimension: some things are owed to the space itself, not to any outcome.

*For example:* At a gathering, the seating, the speaking order and the portions are not neutral logistics: the young person does not take the elder's seat, speak over them, or take the best portion first — the arrangements themselves carry the respect the relationship is owed.

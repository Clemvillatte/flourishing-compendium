> **Principle 14** — Hold the metaphysics lightly

This is a method principle: it asks the reader to try the practices without first accepting the metaphysics behind them. Three of the positions it names can be checked against texts.

### An enduring seer

In Yoga, sutra 1.3 says: "Then the Seer [that is, the Self,] abides in himself" (trans. Woods, p. 13). The commentary says the Self's "Intelligence (caitanya) is himself (svarupa), [and is] not conditioned" (p. 13). The Gita, in Arnold's translation, asserts the same in poetry: "Birthless and deathless and changeless remaineth the spirit for ever; death hath not touched it at all, dead though the house of it seems!" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 2, trans. Arnold).

### A denied self

The Buddhist position is stated plainly in the Anattalakkhana Sutta (SN 22.59), in Sujato's translation: "Mendicants, form is not-self. For if form were self, it wouldn't lead to affliction. And you could compel form." The same is said of feeling, perception, choices and consciousness, and the learned disciple "grows disillusioned" with all five (SN 22.59, SuttaCentral).

### Not sourced here

I did not open sources for the Advaita identity claim or for the pragmatist bracketing, so this page makes no statement about them. The principle's position, to examine procedures without ontological assent and to name where a principle depends on doctrine, is its own method choice. The empirical question whether effects depend on belief is left open, as the principle says.

**Plain words** The Yoga text and the Gita speak of an enduring Self. The Buddha's discourse says there is none to find among the five aggregates. The book asks you to test practices and see what they do, while it names where a given practice leans on a doctrine.

*For example:* Imagined example: you try a breathing practice from a tradition whose metaphysics you do not share. You judge it by what it does for your attention, and note which of its instructions depend on a doctrine.

(Yoga Sutra 1.3 with Vacaspati Misra's gloss, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), p. 13, Internet Archive; Bhagavad-Gita ch. 2, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, Project Gutenberg #2388; SN 22.59, trans. Bhikkhu Sujato, SuttaCentral; all opened 2026-10-21. Gita 2.21-2.25 is not checked against a numbered edition.)

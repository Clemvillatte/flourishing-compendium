> **Principle 14** — Hold the metaphysics lightly

The classical schools assert an enduring seer (puruṣa in Yoga, atman-Brahman identity in Advaita); the Buddhist tradition denies it; the pragmatist tradition brackets it as unactionable. This book's position is the practical one: it proposes examining the procedures without requiring ontological assent, and leaves open whether their effects depend on belief, which is an empirical question it does not claim to have settled. Where a principle below depends on doctrine, the dependence is named.

(Method principle; YS 1.3 with Vyasa's gloss; BG 2.21-2.25 context.)

**Plain words** Classical Yoga asserts an enduring seer; Buddhism denies it; pragmatism brackets it as unactionable. The practical position: examine the procedures without requiring the metaphysics. You can run the experiment without signing the doctrine.

*For example:* The skeptic who can't swallow the seer doctrine — and runs the seeing-practices anyway, purely functionally, like someone using a map without holding opinions about the paper's ontology.

> **Principle 364** — Practise constitutive relationality

Sandel's argument is that some attachments do not merely influence you; they partly define you ([Liberalism and the Limits of Justice, opened full text](https://is.cuni.cz/studium/eng/predmety/index.php?do=download&did=330749&kod=JPB595)). Against the picture of a self whose identity is never tied to its aims, he lists the ways people actually understand themselves: "as members of this family or community or nation or people, as bearers of this history, as sons and daughters of that revolution, as citizens of this republic" ([same opened text](https://is.cuni.cz/studium/eng/predmety/index.php?do=download&did=330749&kod=JPB595)):

> Allegiances such as these are more than values I happen to have or aims I 'espouse at any given time'. They go beyond the obligations I voluntarily incur and the 'natural duties' I owe to human beings as such. They allow that to some I owe more than justice requires or even permits, not by reason of agreements I have made but instead in virtue of those more or less enduring attachments and commitments which taken together partly define the person I am.
>
> To imagine a person incapable of constitutive attachments such as these is not to conceive an ideally free and rational agent, but to imagine a person wholly without character, without moral depth.

(Sandel, Liberalism and the Limits of Justice, Cambridge University Press, pp. 154-155 as printed in the opened copy; [opened full text, Charles University repository](https://is.cuni.cz/studium/eng/predmety/index.php?do=download&did=330749&kod=JPB595).)

The practice follows from the definition. When you audit your commitments, some are not objects you hold; they are part of the holder. The question for those is not whether you chose them, but how you are living them.

### Plain words

Some of your ties are not items you own; they are part of what you are made of: family, language, history. You can revise your relationship to them, and you choose how to live them. The check is not whether you picked them, but whether you are living them well.

### For example

Suppose someone emigrates to start fresh and finds they are still made of the language and the humour they thought they had left behind. The discovery is not a failure of the fresh start; it is a reminder that the self was never separable from its material.

> **Principle 31** — Learn that excessive exercise can be harmful: overtraining

Exercise is beneficial in moderation but harmful in excess. Overtraining (too much, too intense, without recovery) produces fatigue, injury, mood disturbance, and immune suppression. The relationship is U-shaped.

**Plain words** Exercise in excess is harmful — overtraining produces fatigue, injury, mood disturbance, immune suppression. The dose-response curve bends. "More is better" is false fitness theology; recovery is where the gains are banked.

*For example:* The marathon training that ends in the flu and the dark mood — the body kept the receipts. The rest day is not a rest from the training; it IS the training.
## Psychology parallels

- **Overtraining and mental health**, excessive exercise is associated with worse mental health and mood disturbance (Chekroud et al., 2018; Morgan et al., 1987)
- **The U-shaped relationship**, both too little and too much exercise are associated with worse mental health (Chekroud et al., 2018; Kim et al., 2019)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The golden mean]]** (Aristotle)
- **[[Nothing in excess|Nothing in excess]]** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei: non-overdoing]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 9)

## Practice

- Do not over-exercise: moderate dose is optimal. If you notice fatigue, injury, mood decline, or obsession with exercise, this may be overtraining. Balance exercise with rest.

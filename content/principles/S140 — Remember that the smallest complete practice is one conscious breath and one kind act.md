> **Principle 262** — Remember that the smallest complete practice is one conscious breath and one kind act

You do not need to do everything. You need to do one thing: one breath taken with awareness, one act of kindness done with intention. From this seed, the whole practice grows. Begin there.

(Disc. I.1, I.4, I.6, I.20, III.2, III.3, III.12; Ench. 1; Med. II.1, III.1, IV.3, V.1, VI.6; Seneca, Letters 16, 71, 101.)

**Plain words** When everything is too much, shrink the practice to its seed: one breath taken on purpose, one kind act done on purpose. That's a complete practice — everything else grows from there. Marcus's empire could wait; the breath can't.

*For example:* Someone in hospital waiting-room hell who can't meditate, journal, or read — and just follows one slow breath, then brings the nurse a coffee. Everything, from there.

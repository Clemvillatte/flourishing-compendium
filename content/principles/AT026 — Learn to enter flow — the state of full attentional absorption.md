> **Principle 26** — Learn to enter flow: the state of full attentional absorption

Flow is the state of complete absorption in an activity, characterised by full attention, loss of self-consciousness, and intrinsic reward. It occurs when challenge matches skill. Flow is one of the most optimal attentional states and is directly linked to well-being.

**Plain words** Flow is full absorption — the task hard enough to grip you, the self quiet, time gone weird. It's one of the best feelings available, and it's manufacturable: clear goal, immediate feedback, challenge matched to skill. Set the table and it tends to come.

*For example:* The pianist looks up and two hours are gone. Nobody had to push them — the conditions did the pushing. That's flow, and it's buildable.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Flow|Flow (Csikszentmihalyi)]]**, optimal experience occurs when challenge matches skill and attention is fully absorbed (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Nakamura & Csikszentmihalyi, 2014)
- **[[Flow|Flow conditions: clear goals, immediate feedback, challenge-skill balance]]**, these are the conditions that enable full attentional absorption (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990)
- **Flow is associated with high well-being and performance**, flow states predict positive affect, engagement, and achievement (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Fullagar et al., 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as effortless action]]** (Daoism, Zhuangzi, Cook Ding)
- **[[Right concentration|Right concentration]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Flow|The autotelic personality]]** (Csikszentmihalyi, Flow)

## Practice

- Design for flow: choose tasks with clear goals, immediate feedback, and a challenge level that matches your skill (not too easy, not too hard). Eliminate distractions. Give yourself uninterrupted time.

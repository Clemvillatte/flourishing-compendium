> **Principle 34** — Cultivate equanimity: the capacity to remain steady amid affect

Desbordes and colleagues give the working definition used here:

> "Equanimity can be defined as an even-minded mental state or dispositional tendency toward all experiences or objects, regardless of their origin or their affective valence (pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral)." ([Desbordes et al., 2015, Mindfulness 6(2), open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/))

The same paper proposes a way to research the construct:

> "We propose that a primary 'signature' of equanimity is in the temporal domain, in the form of a more rapid disengagement from initial emotional response and faster return to baseline." ([Desbordes et al., 2015, same text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/))

The paper is a conceptual review with a measurement proposal; it does not show that any particular practice produces the state. Even-mindedness is the proposed definition; faster disengagement and return to baseline are proposed research signatures.

**Plain words** Even-mindedness concerns how you meet pleasant, unpleasant and neutral experiences. The authors also suggest examining how readily a person disengages after an emotional response. They present this timing pattern as a research proposal.

*For example:* In an imagined difficult call, irritation arises. Afterward, note how you related to the irritation and whether your attention returned to the task. A steady voice alone does not answer either question.

## Practice

- Practise noticing feelings without immediately reacting. A pause between feeling and action is the foundation of equanimity.

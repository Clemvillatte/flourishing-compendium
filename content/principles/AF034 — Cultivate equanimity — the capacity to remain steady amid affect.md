> **Principle 34** — Cultivate equanimity: the capacity to remain steady amid affect

Equanimity is not the absence of feeling; it is a quality of relationship to feeling — a steadiness that does not suppress, amplify, or react automatically. It is the affective counterpart of the Stoic ideal and is explicitly cultivated in contemplative traditions.

**Plain words** Equanimity: not the absence of feeling but a quality of relationship to feeling — steadiness that neither suppresses, amplifies, nor reacts automatically. The affective counterpart of the Stoic ideal. Feel it fully; hold it steadily.

*For example:* The sailor who feels the storm AND holds the tiller — versus the one who either panics or goes numb. Equanimity is the third thing: full feeling, steady hands. It looks calm from outside; from inside it feels like presence.

- **[[Equanimity|Equanimity as a distinct outcome of meditation]]**, a measurable quality distinct from mindfulness itself (Desbordes et al., 2015)
- **Equanimity is associated with reduced emotional reactivity**, without flattening affective range (Harrington & Pickles, 2009)

## Practice

- Practise noticing feelings without immediately reacting. A pause between feeling and action is the foundation of equanimity.

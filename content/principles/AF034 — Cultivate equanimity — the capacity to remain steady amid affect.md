> **Principle 34** — Cultivate equanimity: the capacity to remain steady amid affect

Desbordes and colleagues give the working definition used here: "Equanimity can be defined as an even-minded mental state or dispositional tendency toward all experiences or objects, regardless of their origin or their affective valence (pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral)" ([Desbordes et al., 2015, Mindfulness 6(2), open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/)). The same paper proposes a measurable signature: "a primary 'signature' of equanimity is in the temporal domain, in the form of a more rapid disengagement from initial emotional response and faster return to baseline" ([Desbordes et al., 2015, same text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350240/)). The paper is a conceptual review with a measurement proposal; it does not show that any particular practice produces the state. The compendium's reading keeps the two halves of the definition together: even-minded toward what arises, and quicker to settle after it does.

**Plain words** The definition has two checkable parts. One is evenness: the same basic stance whether the experience is pleasant, unpleasant or neutral. The other is timing: the feeling still arrives, and the measure is how quickly you disengage and return. Neither half asks you to feel nothing.

*For example:* (Imagined case.) During a difficult phone call you notice irritation rising, keep your voice level, and after hanging up find the irritation gone within the hour rather than the week. Both observations are checkable; neither requires pretending the call was pleasant.

## Practice

- Practise noticing feelings without immediately reacting. A pause between feeling and action is the foundation of equanimity.

> **Principle 478** — Practise idem and ipse identity

Distinguish between two dimensions of identity: idem (sameness — the continuity of the self through time) and ipse (selfhood — the capacity to be the author of one's own life). You are both the same person you were (idem) and the one who can choose who you will be (ipse). Do not collapse one into the other.

(Ricoeur, 1990/1992, Oneself as Another, "Idem and Ipse"; Ricoeur, 1990/1992, Oneself as Another, "The Self and the Ethical Aim.")

**Plain words** Two dimensions of identity: idem (sameness — continuity through time) and ipse (selfhood — the capacity to be the author of your life). You're both the person you've been and the one who chooses what's next. Don't collapse into either pole: the sameness without the authorship is inertia; the authorship without the sameness is amnesia.

*For example:* The person who "never changes" (idem, no ipse) and the one who reinvents so totally that nobody's promises survive (ipse, no idem) — the workable self is the one that keeps its word AND revises its script.

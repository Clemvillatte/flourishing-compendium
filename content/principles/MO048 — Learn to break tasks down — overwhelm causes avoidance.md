> **Principle 48** — Learn to break tasks down: overwhelm causes avoidance

Overwhelm — the feeling that a task is too big or too complex — is a major cause of procrastination. Breaking tasks into small, concrete, actionable steps reduces overwhelm and increases initiation.

**Plain words** Overwhelm causes avoidance — the task's size is the paralysis, not its difficulty. Cut it until the first step is trivially small. Nobody avoids "open the document."

*For example:* The thesis that gets avoided for months and started in an afternoon once it becomes "write one bad paragraph" — the mountain was the problem, and mountains are cut by making them into stairs.
## Psychology parallels

- **Task decomposition**, breaking tasks into steps reduces avoidance (Steel, 2007; Pychyl, 2013)
- **Subgoal setting**, small subgoals increase motivation and initiation (Bandura & Schunk, 1981; Latham & Seijts, 1999)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The thousand-mile journey|The thousand-mile journey]]** (Laozi, Daodejing 64)
- **[[Gradual training|The gradual training]]** (Buddhism)
- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean: large vision, small steps]]** (Aristotle)

## Practice

- When avoiding a task, break it down: what is the very next, smallest, concrete action? Not "write the report" but "open the document and write the title." Small steps overcome overwhelm.

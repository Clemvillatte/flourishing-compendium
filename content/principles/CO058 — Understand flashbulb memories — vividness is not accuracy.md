> **Principle 58** — Understand flashbulb memories: vividness is not accuracy

Flashbulb memories — vivid, detailed memories of emotionally significant events (where you were when you heard about 9/11) — are experienced as highly accurate but are not more accurate than ordinary memories. They feel special but are not.

**Plain words** Flashbulb memories — the vivid "where you were when" memories of big events — feel highly accurate but aren't more accurate than ordinary memories. Vividness is not accuracy. The confidence is real; the calibration isn't.

*For example:* Everyone knows exactly where they were when the big news broke — and the studies where people wrote it down that day and were re-asked a year later: the details drifted, the confidence didn't. The memory of the memory is doing the talking.
## Psychology parallels

- **Flashbulb memories are vivid but not more accurate**, confidence and vividness are high but accuracy declines at the same rate as ordinary memories (Neisser & Harsch, 1992; Talarico & Rubin, 2003)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The illusion of certainty|The illusion of certainty]]** (Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow)
- **Appearance vs reality** (Plato, Republic)

## Practice

- When a memory feels especially vivid and significant, do not assume it is more accurate. Vividness is a property of the emotional experience, not of the memory's accuracy.

> **Principle 336** — Practise care for the dependent

Kittay's argument starts from a fact about every life: "everyone is some mother's child", and so "everyone has the experience of being dependent on someone—indeed radically dependent on someone for mere existence" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Feminist Ethics, summarising Kittay 1999](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2015/entries/feminism-ethics/)). The encyclopedia entry reports that she wants society to support the "dependency workers" who do this care: relatives, friends or paid workers who assume daily responsibility for a dependent's survival, and who "typically suffer negative personal and/or professional consequences" for it (same entry).

(Kittay, 1999, Love's Labor: Essays on Women, Equality, and Dependency; Tronto, 2013, Caring Democracy.)

### A principle of fairness for carers

The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy reports that Kittay adds a principle to Rawls's theory of justice: "To each according to his or her need, from each to his or her capacity for care, and such support from social institutions as to make available resources and opportunities to those providing care" (Kittay 1999, p. 113, quoted in [IEP, Care Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/care-ethics/)). Her work develops from caring for seriously disabled people, and she calls for public provision of "doulas", paid carers who care for the care-givers.

I read these two encyclopedia summaries, not Love's Labor itself. I also did not open Tronto 2013 (Caring Democracy), so this page makes no claim about it.

**Plain words**
Everyone starts out needing care, and many of us end that way. A fair community plans for it, and looks after the people who give care.

**For example**
List who in your household or circle depends on someone's care this month, and who is giving it. Ask the carer one question: what would make the next month easier? Act on the answer.

> **Principle 401** — Practise intersubjectivity

Recognise that your experience is never purely private. The world you experience is a shared world, constituted through your relationships with others. The problem of other minds is not "how do I know others exist?" but "how is the world given as a world shared with others?" Attend to the intersubjective dimension of your experience.

(Husserl, 1929/1960, Cartesian Meditations, §§42-62; Husserl, 1954/1970, The Crisis, §§53-55; Stein, 1917/1989, On the Problem of Empathy.)

**Plain words** Your experience is never purely private — the world you experience is a shared world, constituted with others. The real question isn't "how do I know others exist?" but "how is the world given as shared?" You're always already in the commons.

*For example:* The same park bench is a bench, a landmark, "where dad used to sit," and "the good spot" — to four different people, simultaneously, without contradiction. The bench is a shared object; the privacy was the illusion.

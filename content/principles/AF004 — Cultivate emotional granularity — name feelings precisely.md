> **Principle 4** — Cultivate emotional granularity: name feelings precisely

Being able to say "I feel resentful" rather than "I feel bad" is a distinct skill with measurable benefits. Higher granularity predicts better regulation, less maladaptive coping, and better mental health. The act of precise naming is itself a regulatory intervention.

**Plain words** "I feel resentful" rather than "I feel bad" — precision with feelings is a skill with measurable benefits: better regulation, less maladaptive coping. Cultivate granularity: the difference between irritated, frustrated, resentful, and envious is diagnostic.

*For example:* The couple who fight under the banner "I'm upset" for years — and the week they learn to say "I'm resentful about the load" versus "I'm hurt about last night," the fights turn into solvable problems. The vocabulary was the intervention.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Emotional granularity|Emotional granularity]]**, higher granularity predicts lower reactivity and better regulation (Barrett, 2004; Kashdan et al., 2015)
- **[[Affect labeling|Affect labelling]] reduces amygdala response**, naming a feeling reliably reduces its neural signature (Lieberman et al., 2007)

## Practice

- Build a fine-grained emotion vocabulary. When you notice "I feel bad," try to specify: irritated, disappointed, lonely, ashamed, tired, anxious, envious, bored.

> **Principle 9** — Measure perfection as power, not as judgment

Perfection, for Spinoza, is degree of reality and power of acting; imperfection is not sin but diminished capacity. A person in despair is not wicked, they are weak in power, which relocates the entire moral question from blame to engineering: what would increase this person's power of acting? Practically, audit your assessments of people, including yourself, and translate each judgment into a power question: what does this state of mind make this person able or unable to do?

(Ethics II, Definition 6; IV, Preface.)

**Plain words** Perfection is degree of reality and power of acting; imperfection is not sin but diminished capacity. A person in despair is not wicked — they're weak in power. This relocates the whole moral question from blame to engineering. You don't judge the dim lamp; you fix the current.

*For example:* The "toxic" family member whose cruelty looks, in the Spinozist lens, like a small engine making loud noises — diminished capacity, not demonic intent. The response changes from outrage to the practical: what would actually increase this person's power? Usually: not another fight.

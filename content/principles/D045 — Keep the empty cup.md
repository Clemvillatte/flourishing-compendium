> **Principle 45** — Keep the empty cup

DDJ 11 gives the cup its physics: the thirty spokes and the nave, the clay vessel, the doors and windows; in each, the use depends on the empty space ([Legge, DDJ 11](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)). Zhuangzi 4 names the practice: the fasting of the mind, whose spirit waits for things free of pre-occupation ([Legge, Zhuangzi 4](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). DDJ 71 adds the check: to know that you have no knowledge is the best ([Legge, DDJ 71](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)).

> The thirty spokes unite in the one nave; but it is on the empty space (for the axle), that the use of the wheel depends. Clay is moulded into a vessel; and it is on its empty space, that the use of the vessel depends. (DDJ 11, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

> But the spirit is free from all pre-occupation and so waits for (the appearance of) things. (Zhuangzi 4, [Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The empty space is where the receiving happens: a full vessel has no room, and neither does a full opinion. The principle keeps the expertise for the doing and empties it from the seeing.

(DDJ 11, 71; Zhuangzi 4.)

**Plain words**
Every 'I know how this goes' is a lid on the thing it closes. The conversation or the morning does not get to show you what it is; you meet your memory of it instead. The practice is carrying the skill and dropping the expectation: same hands, fresh eyes.

**For example**
The veteran teacher who still watches students with fresh eyes catches what the 'seen it all' teacher stops noticing.

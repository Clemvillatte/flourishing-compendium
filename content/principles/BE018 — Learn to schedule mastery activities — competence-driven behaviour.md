> **Principle 18** — Learn to schedule mastery activities: competence-driven behaviour

This principle makes room for skill practice within [[Behavioral activation|behavioural activation]]. The University of Michigan handout defines mastery as follows:

> "Mastery involves activities, such as work or sports, that involve the development of skills; we are able to accomplish things and feel a sense of mastery over our environment." ([University of Michigan, "Mastery" section](https://psych.med.umich.edu/anxiety-program/pdf/Behavioral-Activation-for-Depression.pdf))

The handout recommends balancing goals involving values, pleasure and mastery. It says mastery activities can increase positive emotions and improve self-regard when enjoyed in moderation alongside other activities ([University of Michigan](https://psych.med.umich.edu/anxiety-program/pdf/Behavioral-Activation-for-Depression.pdf)).

**Plain words** A practice session can be worth scheduling for the chance to learn something, even when it offers little enjoyment. Choose one manageable task and give it a place in the week. Afterwards, note what you attempted or completed and how much accomplishment you felt. Leave space for enjoyable activities too.

*For example* Imagined: someone learning guitar schedules a short session practising a difficult chord change on Wednesday evening. Afterwards, they record whether they attempted it and how much accomplishment they felt.

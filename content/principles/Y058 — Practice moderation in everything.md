> **Principle 58** — Practice moderation in everything

Krishna's rule about moderation comes in the sixth chapter of the Gita, just before the passage on the steady lamp. In Arnold's translation: "Religion is not his who too much fasts or too much feasts, nor his who sleeps away an idle mind; nor his who wears to waste his strength in vigils. Nay, Arjuna! call that the true piety which most removes earth-aches and ills, where one is moderate in eating and in resting, and in sport; measured in wish and act; sleeping betimes, waking betimes for duty" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Arnold).

### What the passage covers

The text names four areas: eating, resting, sport and what Arnold renders as being "measured in wish and act", plus a regular sleeping and waking rhythm. It treats the extreme in both directions as a fault, whether excess (feasting, idle sleep) or deprivation (fasting, vigils). The next lines connect the settled life to a steady mind: "When the man, so living, centres on his soul the thought straitly restrained ... then is he Yukta" (ch. 6, Arnold).

### What is added

Arnold's translation does not use the phrase "discipline destroys sorrow", though it says that moderate living "most removes earth-aches and ills". The principle's image of a "trainable nervous system" and its monthly review of the four moderations are its own additions and are not in the text.

**Plain words** The Gita asks for balance in food, rest, recreation and effort, and warns against both too much and too little. It links this to a steady mind. Checking those four areas now and then is a sensible way to keep them in balance, though the check-in schedule comes from the principle.

*For example:* Imagined example: once a month you note how you have been eating, sleeping, working and resting. You see that you have been skipping rest to meet deadlines, and you correct it before it hardens into habit.

(Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388, opened 2026-10-21. Arnold's verses are unnumbered, so the verse numbers in the old cite, 6.16-6.17, are not checked here.)

> **Principle 24** — Expect fluctuation, and read it as structure

In the note to Part III, Proposition 17, Spinoza names the condition: a disposition of the mind that arises from two contrary emotions, so that we both hate and love the same thing. ([Ethics III, Prop. XVII, note, Elwes tr.](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)) He adds that one and the same object may be the cause of many and conflicting emotions. The compendium's application is to treat the swing itself as the datum: when inner weather oscillates, look for the competing strivings underneath rather than taking each swing as the truth.

> "This disposition of the mind, which arises from two contrary emotions, is called vacillation; it stands to the emotions in the same relation as doubt does to the imagination." (Ethics III, Prop. XVII, note, Elwes tr., [Gutenberg #3800](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

**Plain words** Spinoza says we can love and hate the same thing at once, and that this wavering has causes like anything else. When you notice yourself pulled two ways, the pull is information: something in you wants each side, and the job is to find out what.

**For example (imagined):** Someone wants a promotion and dreads it, wants the relationship and wants out. Instead of reading each mood as the final answer, they list what each side of the swing is drawn to, and the conflict becomes something they can look at.

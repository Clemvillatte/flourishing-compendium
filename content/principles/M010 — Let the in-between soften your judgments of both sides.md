> **Principle 10** — Let the in-between soften your judgments of both sides

Standing between positions is a standing lesson in the partiality of every position: the insider's certainty looks different from the middle, from both directions. The tradition treats this as nepantla's quiet gift, an inbuilt humility that monocultures must work harder for. Practically, when you find yourself between two convinced parties (in a family, a workplace, a debate), resist enlisting in either certitude, and notice what the middle can see that both are missing.

(The nepantla vantage; the principle's own generalization, marked as such.)

**Plain words** Standing between positions is a standing lesson in the partiality of every position: the insider's certainty looks different from the middle, from both directions. That's nepantla's quiet gift — an inbuilt humility that monocultures have to work much harder for. Let it soften your judgments of both sides.

*For example:* Someone stuck between two feuding family factions — and over time can't take either side's story seriously at full strength, because they've seen how each side's "obvious truth" looks from the other shore.

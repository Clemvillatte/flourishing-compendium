> **Principle 16** — Treat identity as a verb

The Gay Science's 270th aphorism puts the principle in two words: "What does your conscience say? 'You shall become who you are'" (Nietzsche, The Gay Science §270, Kaufmann translation, [Archive.org](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/23/items/mjdkebooks/friedrich%20nietzsche%20-%20the%20gay%20science.pdf)). That is a strange instruction if identity is a fixed possession; you cannot become what you already are. The sentence only makes sense if identity is something being built, and the work of building it is the actual assignment.

Nietzsche makes the same move structurally in §335: the goal is to become "human beings who are new, unique, incomparable, who give themselves their own laws, who create themselves", and the method is honest self-study, becoming "the best learners and discoverers of everything that is lawful and necessary in the world" ([The Gay Science §335, Archive.org](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/23/items/mjdkebooks/friedrich%20nietzsche%20-%20the%20gay%20science.pdf)). Simondon, a century later, formalises the same intuition: the individual is never finished; individuation is the ongoing process, and any "self" is a momentary resolution, not a terminus ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/)).

> "We, however, want to become those we are — human beings who are new, unique, incomparable, who give themselves laws, who create themselves. To that end we must become the best learners and discoverers of everything that is lawful and necessary in the world: we must become physicists in order to be able to be creators in this sense." (The Gay Science §335, [Archive.org](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/23/items/mjdkebooks/friedrich%20nietzsche%20-%20the%20gay%20science.pdf))

**Plain words** Treat "I am a shy person" the way you would treat "I am currently employed at this company": a description of the present state, not a contract. One can be renegotiated next month; the other is just a fact about you that can only get worse with age. Nietzsche's phrase "become who you are" sounds mystical until you notice it is a schedule, not a slogan: study what is actually true about yourself, then act on the parts you can change. Identity as a verb means the self is the project you are currently working on, and the deadline is not optional.

*For example:* Two students both fail a first exam in a subject. One says "I am not a math person" — that closes the case and ends the learning. The other says "I have not yet learned this" — that is the same fact stated as a project. One sentence produces a fixed identity; the other produces a study schedule. Same failure, different futures.

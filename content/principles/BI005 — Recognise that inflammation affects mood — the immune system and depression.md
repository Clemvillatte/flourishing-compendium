> **Principle 5** — Recognise that inflammation affects mood: the immune system and depression

The core paper here is Dantzer and colleagues' 2008 review. In brief: when infection activates innate immune cells, they release pro-inflammatory cytokines that act on the brain to cause sickness behaviour ([Dantzer, O'Connor, Freund, Johnson & Kelley, "From inflammation to sickness and depression: when the immune system subjugates the brain", Nature Reviews Neuroscience 9(1), 46-56, 2008, author manuscript via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919277/)).

The overlap with depression is why this page exists:

> "The similarity between the symptoms of cytokine-induced sickness behaviour and depression is striking: in both cases there is a withdrawal from the physical and social environment that is accompanied by pain, malaise and decreased reactivity to reward (anhedonia)." (Dantzer et al., 2008, [via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919277/))

The review also marks the path from one to the other: when immune activation continues unabated, as in systemic infections, cancer or autoimmune disease, the signalling can lead to an exacerbation of sickness and symptoms of depression in vulnerable individuals ([Dantzer et al., 2008, via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919277/)). Its summary keeps the hedge:

> "Inflammation is therefore an important biological event that might increase the risk of major depressive episodes, much like the more traditional psychosocial factors." (Dantzer et al., 2008, [via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919277/))

**Plain words**
When you are infected, immune cells release signalling proteins (cytokines) that act on the brain and produce a coordinated shutdown: fatigue, withdrawal, low mood, loss of appetite. That is sickness behaviour, and it overlaps heavily with depression. The review's authors keep a hedge: sustained inflammation may tip vulnerable people toward depression. A dark cloud is sometimes an immune event.

**For example**
The flu-week flatness that lifted when the fever did. Sickness behaviour is that programme working as designed. The review's caution concerns the chronic case: the same programme running for months in vulnerable people can look like, and develop into, depression.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Inflammatory theory of depression (Dantzer, Miller)|Inflammatory theory of depression (Dantzer, Miller)]]**, pro-inflammatory cytokines produce depressive-like symptoms (Dantzer et al., 2008; Miller & Raison, 2016)
- **Sickness behaviour**, inflammation produces fatigue, withdrawal, anhedonia, and social withdrawal (Dantzer et al., 2008; Hart, 1988)
- **Anti-inflammatory treatments show some promise for depression**, but evidence is mixed and not yet definitive (Strawbridge et al., 2015; Köhler et al., 2014)

## Practice

- Recognise inflammation's effect on mood: when you feel fatigued, withdrawn, and low (especially with physical symptoms), inflammation may be contributing. Address it through: sleep, exercise, anti-inflammatory diet, and stress reduction.

> **Principle 5** — Recognise that inflammation affects mood: the immune system and depression

Inflammation — the immune system's response to threat, injury, or stress — directly affects mood. Pro-inflammatory cytokines produce "sickness behaviour" (fatigue, withdrawal, low mood) and are associated with depression.

**Plain words** Inflammation affects mood — the immune system's chemicals produce fatigue, withdrawal, low mood, the "sickness behaviour" that looks exactly like depression. Sometimes the dark cloud is an immune event, not a psychological one. The body can be sad.

*For example:* The flu-week flatness that lifted when the fever did — that's sickness behaviour, and chronic low-grade inflammation can run the same programme for months. The mood was an immune memo.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Inflammatory theory of depression (Dantzer, Miller)|Inflammatory theory of depression (Dantzer, Miller)]]**, pro-inflammatory cytokines produce depressive-like symptoms (Dantzer et al., 2008; Miller & Raison, 2016)
- **Sickness behaviour**, inflammation produces fatigue, withdrawal, anhedonia, and social withdrawal (Dantzer et al., 2008; Hart, 1988)
- **Anti-inflammatory treatments show some promise for depression**, but evidence is mixed and not yet definitive (Strawbridge et al., 2015; Köhler et al., 2014)

## Practice

- Recognise inflammation's effect on mood: when you feel fatigued, withdrawn, and low (especially with physical symptoms), inflammation may be contributing. Address it through: sleep, exercise, anti-inflammatory diet, and stress reduction.

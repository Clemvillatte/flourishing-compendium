> **Principle 54** — Recognise self and culture: your self is culturally shaped

Markus and Kitayama's 1991 paper in Psychological Review opens with the claim: "People in different cultures have strikingly different construals of the self, of others, and of the interdependence of the 2. These construals can influence, and in many cases determine, the very nature of individual experience, including cognition, emotion, and motivation" ([Markus and Kitayama, 1991](https://eclass.uowm.gr/modules/document/file.php/NURED261/Markus%20&%20Kitayama%20culture%20and%20the%20self%201991%20PR.pdf)).

### Two construals

They contrast an independent construal, in which "individuals seek to maintain their independence from others by attending to the self and by discovering and expressing their unique inner attributes", with an interdependent one in which the self "is viewed as interdependent with the surrounding context". They add that for many cultures "the Western notion of the self as an entity containing significant dispositional attributes, and as detached from context, is simply not an adequate description of selfhood."

### Not two boxes

The authors do not claim a clean split. They write that "even within highly individualist Western culture, most people are still much less self-reliant, self-contained, or self-sufficient than the prevailing cultural ideology suggests that they should be" (p. 247, as quoted in [a later critique](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10099490/)). Markus has said the researchers never meant to suggest that only two types of self exist ([APA](https://www.apa.org/research-practice/conduct-research/culture-self-shaped-contexts)).

### What the paper does not say

The paper does not say a culturally shaped self is a limitation or the human condition. It also does not say how a person caught between two cultures should resolve it. The advice about integration is the compendium's.

(Markus and Kitayama, 1991, pp. 224-253.)

**Plain words** Ideas about what a good self looks like, how independent, how outspoken, how tied to family, differ across cultures and shape how people think and feel. So some of what feels like just being yourself comes from the culture you grew up in. It helps to know which parts those are.

*For example:* Imagine the child of immigrants who hears "stand on your own two feet" at school and "family comes before everything" at home. Those are two cultural pictures of a good self. The research describes the difference. It does not tell the person which to follow.

## Psychology parallels

- **Cultural shaping of self**, self-concept and identity are culturally constructed (Markus & Kitayama, 1991; Oyserman et al., 2002)
- **Independent vs interdependent self across cultures**, cultures differ in whether the self is defined as independent or interdependent (Markus & Kitayama, 1991; Cross et al., 2011)

## Practice

- Recognise that your self is partly cultural. Do not assume your way of being a self is universal. If you are bicultural, work on integration rather than choosing.

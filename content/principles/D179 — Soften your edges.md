> **Principle 179** — Soften your edges

Daodejing 15 describes skilled masters through images of caution and unpretentiousness, like melting ice and unfashioned wood. Chapter 4 more directly counsels blunting sharp points and unravelling complications. Read as advice for disagreement, these images invite a position that can be stated without hardening every uncertainty into a verdict.

(DDJ 4, 15, 20.)

> "Shrinking looked they like those who wade through a stream in winter; irresolute like those who are afraid of all around them; grave like a guest (in awe of his host); evanescent like ice that is melting away; unpretentious like wood that has not been fashioned into anything; vacant like a valley, and dull like muddy water." (DDJ 15, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

### Plain words

Say what you see and what you think should change. Mark what you are still unsure about. You can leave another reading open without pretending agreement or erasing a concern.

### For example

Revise a sharp editorial comment in two ways: one asks a factual question, and one states the specific issue and a proposed fix. Compare which preserves the necessary criticism without a personal label. Use the clearer version and retain the approach for later feedback; this is an overt-behaviour practice.

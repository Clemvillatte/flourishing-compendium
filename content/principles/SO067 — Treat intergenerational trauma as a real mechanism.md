> **Principle 67** — Treat intergenerational trauma as a real mechanism

Treat intergenerational trauma — the transmission of trauma effects from one generation to the next through family, culture, and epigenetics — as a real mechanism. The evidence includes historical trauma in Indigenous communities (Whitbeck et al., 2004; Gone, 2011), Holocaust survivor families (Yehuda et al., 2015), and the epigenetics of trauma (Yehuda et al., 2016; for caution, see Heard et al., 2018).

**Plain words** Intergenerational trauma is a real mechanism — transmitted through family, culture, and even biology. What happened to grandparents shows up in grandchildren's stress systems. Not destiny; inheritance. And inheritances can be worked with.

*For example:* The family where nobody spoke of the war, and the grandchildren flinch at raised voices without knowing why — the silence was the transmission. Naming it, telling it, ritualising it: that's how the chain gets edited.

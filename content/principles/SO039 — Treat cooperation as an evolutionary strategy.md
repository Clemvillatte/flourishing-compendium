> **Principle 39** — Treat cooperation as an evolutionary strategy

Axelrod's book asks the practical question directly: "Under what conditions will cooperation emerge in a world of egoists without central authority?" (1984, p. 1). His answer, from tournament play and theory, is conditional reciprocity: "no central authority is needed: Cooperation based on reciprocity can be self-policing" (p. 4). The most striking result is where it works: "Under suitable conditions, cooperation based upon reciprocity can develop even between antagonists" (p. 3), as it did among WWI soldiers who had every reason to shoot each other. The compendium's framing follows the theory: cooperate conditionally, reciprocate, and design institutions that make cooperation stable.

> "The foundation of cooperation is not really trust, but the durability of the relationship. When the conditions are right, the players can come to cooperate with each other through trial-and-error learning about possibilities for mutual rewards, through imitation of other successful players, or even through a blind process of selection of the more successful strategies with a weeding out of the less successful ones." (Axelrod, 1984, p. 6)

**Plain words** Cooperation is an evolutionary strategy, not naivety — humans are conditional cooperators who reciprocate and punish defectors. Neither saints nor sharks; accountants. Build systems where cooperation pays and defection costs.

*For example:* The colleague who helps until they're helped-once too often, then stops — that's not bitterness, that's the accounting. Conditional cooperation is the species' firmware.

> **Principle 6** — Understand dispassion as release, not numbness

Vairagya is defined precisely: mastery over the thirst for what is seen and heard. It is not the elimination of feeling, appetite, or care. The test the tradition gives is functional: dispassion is present when you can have or lose the thing without being owned by it. Practically, do not try to stop wanting; practice wanting with a loose grip, and notice the difference in quality of life.

(YS 1.15.)

**Plain words** Vairagya is defined precisely: mastery over the thirst for what is seen and heard — not the elimination of feeling, appetite, or care. The test is functional: dispassion is present when you can have or lose the thing without being owned by either. Release, not numbness.

*For example:* Someone loves their house genuinely and can still sell it without grief when the move makes sense — the love stayed, the ownership transferred. That's vairagya; the numb version would be never loving it at all.

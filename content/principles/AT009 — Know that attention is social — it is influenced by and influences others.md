> **Principle 9** — Know that attention is social: it is influenced by and influences others

Attention is not purely individual. Others' gaze, interests, and focus influence what you attend to (joint attention). Your attention also influences others. Attention is contagious in groups.

**Plain words** Attention is contagious — people's gaze and interest steer yours. This is how teams, families, and feeds focus whole rooms without anyone deciding. Knowing that, you can pick your attentional company deliberately.

*For example:* Look up at the sky in a group and watch every head tilt back — you just conducted a small attentional orchestra. Now imagine what a feed full of outrage is conducting.
## Psychology parallels

- **Joint attention and social cueing**, others' gaze direction automatically shifts your attention (Friesen & Kingstone, 1998; Birmingham & Kingstone, 2009)
- **[[Attentional synchronisation in groups|Attentional synchronisation in groups]]**, group members converge in attentional focus (Richardson et al., 2007; Dikker et al., 2017)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Ubuntu: I am because we are** (African Philosophy, Desmond Tutu)
- **The crowd as attentional trap** (Kierkegaard, The Crowd is Untruth)
- **[[Right association|Right association]]** (Buddhism, Sigalovada Sutta)

## Practice

- Recognise that what others attend to influences what you attend to, often without awareness. Choose your attentional environment (people, media, spaces) deliberately.

> **Principle 25** — Treat restlessness as weather, not as enemy

Arjuna's complaint in the Gita is everyone's complaint: the mind is restless, turbulent, strong, and stubborn, as hard to hold as the wind. The answer given is not a counter-force but a method: it is subdued by practice and dispassion. Practically, stop fighting restlessness directly; give the mind a steady object and let the weather pass.

(BG 6.34-6.35; YS 1.35.)

**Plain words** Arjuna's complaint is everyone's complaint: the mind is restless, turbulent, strong, and stubborn — as hard to hold as the wind. The answer isn't a counter-force; it's a method: subdued by practice and dispassion. Treat restlessness as weather, not as enemy.

*For example:* Someone who declares war on their restless mind and loses daily — then treats the restlessness like a windy day: you don't fight the wind, you put on the jacket and practice anyway. Same wind, entirely different decade.

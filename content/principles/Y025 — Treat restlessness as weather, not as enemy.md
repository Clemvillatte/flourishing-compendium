> **Principle 25** — Treat restlessness as weather, not as enemy

Arjuna's complaint in the Gita is that Krishna's yoga of equanimity has "no fixity" in it, "because the heart of men is unfixed, Krishna! rash, tumultuous, wilful and strong. It were all one, I think, to hold the wayward wind, as tame man's heart" (Bhagavad-Gita, ch. 6, trans. Arnold). Krishna does not deny it. "Beyond denial, hard man's heart is to restrain, and wavering; yet may it grow restrained by habit, Prince! By wont of self-command" (ch. 6, Arnold).

### The method, in the Yoga Sutras

Patanjali gives the same answer in sutra 1.12: "The restriction of them is by [means of] practice and passionlessness" (trans. Woods). The commentary pictures the mind-stuff as a river: "the stream towards objects is dammed by passionlessness, and the stream towards discrimination has its flood-gate opened by practice in discriminatory knowledge" (Woods, pp. 34-35).

### A steady object

Sutra 1.35 offers one way to steady the mind: "Or [he gains stability when] a sense-activity arises connected with an object [and] bringing the central-organ into a relation of stability" (Woods, p. 72). In the commentary the examples are specific meditation exercises, fixing attention on the tip of the nose, the tongue or the palate.

**Plain words** Restlessness is not shameful. Even Arjuna says it is like trying to hold the wind. The answer in these texts is repetition and detachment, practised over time, not a battle. "Weather" is the principle's image, not Arnold's or Patanjali's, but it fits the advice to give the mind a steady object and let the changes pass.

*For example:* Imagined example: you sit to work and your mind pulls toward your phone. You do not argue with it. You return to one task, again and again, and the pull weakens with practice.

(Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388; Yoga Sutras 1.12 and 1.35 with Vyasa's Bhasya, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), Internet Archive; both opened 2026-10-21. The old cite "BG 6.34-6.35" is by verse number; Arnold's translation is unnumbered.)

> **Principle 2** — Recognise the two systems (or modes) of thinking: fast intuition and slow analysis

The two modes have a classic description. Kahneman's 2003 article lays out the distinction: System 1's operations are fast, automatic, effortless and associative; System 2's are slower, serial, effortful and deliberately controlled. (Kahneman, 2003.)

> "The operations of System 1 are typically fast, automatic, effortless, associative, implicit (not available to introspection), and often emotionally charged; they are also governed by habit and are therefore difficult to control or modify. The operations of System 2 are slower, serial, effortful, more likely to be consciously monitored and deliberately controlled; they are also relatively flexible and potentially rule governed." (Kahneman, 2003, [American Psychologist 58(9):697-720](https://faculty.sites.iastate.edu/tesfatsi/archive/tesfatsi/JudgementAndChoice.MappingBoundedRationality.DKahneman2003.pdf))

Two cautions travel with the label. The systems are descriptive labels for modes of processing, not discrete brain structures; and Type 1 processing is not the biased half of a moral pair, since heuristics are often adaptive (Evans & Stanovich, 2013; Gigerenzer, 2007). The compendium's rule is the principle's own: match the mode to the task, and treat the labels as vocabulary rather than architecture.

**Plain words**
Fast intuition and slow analysis are two ways the mind works, and the description of them is well agreed: one is automatic, effortless and associative; the other is serial, effortful and controlled. The skill this principle points at is noticing which one a task needs. Neither mode is the smart one; they are for different jobs.

*For example:* The experienced driver merges on instinct, which is the fast mode doing pattern-rich work; the same driver slows down for the numbers on a mortgage. The failures run the other way: spreadsheet effort spent on a merge, instinct spent on a loan.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Dual-process theory|Dual-process theory]]**, two systems with distinct properties; System 1 is associative and fast, System 2 is rule-based and slow (Kahneman, 2011; Evans & Stanovich, 2013)
- **The systems are not discrete brain structures**, they are useful descriptive labels for modes of processing; the neural reality is more continuous (Evans & Stanovich, 2013)
- **System 1 is not inherently biased; it is often adaptive**, heuristics can be fast and frugal and outperform complex reasoning in uncertain environments (Gigerenzer, 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Phronesis|Phronesis as knowing when to deliberate]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VI)
- **Intuition and analysis** (Bergson, Creative Evolution)
- **The rational and the intuitive** (William James, The Varieties of Religious Experience)

## Practice

- When facing a decision, ask: "does this need my slow, deliberate thinking, or can my intuition handle it?" Use System 2 for novel, complex, high-stakes decisions; trust System 1 for familiar, pattern-rich, low-stakes ones.

> **Principle 2** — Recognise the two systems (or modes) of thinking: fast intuition and slow analysis

Thinking operates in two distinct modes. System 1 is fast, automatic, intuitive, and effortless. System 2 is slow, deliberate, analytical, and effortful. Neither is better; each has appropriate domains. The problem is using the wrong mode for the task.

**Plain words** Two modes of thinking: fast intuition — automatic, effortless, instant — and slow analysis — deliberate, effortful, accurate. Neither is better; each has its job. The skill is knowing which mode the current problem needs, and switching on purpose.

*For example:* The experienced driver merges on instinct (fast, correct) but reaches for the spreadsheet when the numbers matter (slow, correct) — and the disasters come from running the wrong mode: spreadsheets for the merge, vibes for the mortgage.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Dual-process theory|Dual-process theory]]**, two systems with distinct properties; System 1 is associative and fast, System 2 is rule-based and slow (Kahneman, 2011; Evans & Stanovich, 2013)
- **The systems are not discrete brain structures**, they are useful descriptive labels for modes of processing; the neural reality is more continuous (Evans & Stanovich, 2013)
- **System 1 is not inherently biased; it is often adaptive**, heuristics can be fast and frugal and outperform complex reasoning in uncertain environments (Gigerenzer, 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Phronesis|Phronesis as knowing when to deliberate]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VI)
- **Intuition and analysis** (Bergson, Creative Evolution)
- **The rational and the intuitive** (William James, The Varieties of Religious Experience)

## Practice

- When facing a decision, ask: "does this need my slow, deliberate thinking, or can my intuition handle it?" Use System 2 for novel, complex, high-stakes decisions; trust System 1 for familiar, pattern-rich, low-stakes ones.

> **Principle 51** — Recognise cognitive distortions: learn the common patterns

Beck built cognitive therapy on watching depressed patients think. In 1964 he described "the consistent finding of systematic errors, such as arbitrary inferences, selective abstraction, and overgeneralization in the idiosyncratic conceptualizations of the depressed patients" (Beck, 1964, [Archives of General Psychiatry 10(6), p. 561](https://archive.org/stream/ThinkingAndDepressionTheoryAndTherapy/Thinking%20and%20depression-%20theory%20and%20therapy_djvu.txt)).

His remedy is a method of checking. Once a patient can spot these thoughts, the work "consists of training him to evaluate their validity or accuracy", which "consists essentially of the application of the rules of evidence and logic to the cognitions and the consideration of alternative explanations" (p. 569). The patient learns that "simply because he thinks something does not, ipso facto, mean he should believe it" (p. 569).

The names in the principle come from the working lists used in therapy today. The Beck Institute's patient booklet defines them in plain terms, for example:

> "Catastrophizing: You predict the future negatively without considering other, more likely, or just as likely outcomes." (Judith Beck and Broder, [Coping with Depression](https://beckinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Coping-with-Depression-2024.pdf), Beck Institute)

The same booklet defines emotional reasoning as "You think something must be true because it 'feels' true", and mind reading as believing "you know what others are thinking" while failing "to consider other, more likely or just as likely possibilities". Its cardinal question for catching them is "What was just going through my mind?"

**Plain words**
Some mistakes in thinking come in recognisable shapes. When you can name the shape (the worst case as a forecast, a feeling as proof, a guess about another person's mind as a fact), you can ask for evidence and look for another explanation. The purpose is accuracy, and a cheerful spin adds nothing.

**For example**
After a flat meeting you think "I'm useless here." Ask "What was just going through my mind?" The answer is a label and a prediction. Next, write down what actually happened and two other explanations, such as tired colleagues or an unclear brief.

## Practice

- Learn the common distortions. When distressed, identify which distortion is operating. Then generate a more balanced alternative. This is not positive thinking; it is accurate thinking.

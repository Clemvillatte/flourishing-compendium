> **Principle 51** — Recognise cognitive distortions: learn the common patterns

Cognitive distortions are systematic errors in thinking that maintain emotional distress. They include catastrophising (expecting the worst), all-or-nothing thinking (black and white), mind-reading (assuming you know what others think), personalisation (blaming yourself for external events), and emotional reasoning (treating feelings as facts).

**Plain words** Learn the common distortions: catastrophising (the worst is coming), all-or-nothing (black and white), mind-reading (they think I'm...), fortune-telling, personalisation, shoulds, emotional reasoning (I feel it, therefore it's true), labelling. Naming the pattern is half the disarm.

*For example:* The 3 a.m. arithmetic "the mistake means I'm finished here" — and the person who finally has the taxonomy: "that's catastrophising plus labelling, the classic combo." Named, the distortion loses its authority. The naming is the treatment's first move.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Cognitive distortions|Cognitive distortions in CBT]]**, identifying and correcting distortions is a core mechanism of cognitive therapy (Beck, 1976; Burns, 1980)
- **Distortions are learned and can be unlearned**, cognitive restructuring reduces distortion frequency and emotional distress (Hofmann et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Learn the common distortions. When distressed, identify which distortion is operating. Then generate a more balanced alternative. This is not positive thinking; it is accurate thinking.

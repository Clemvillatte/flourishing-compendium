> **Principle 36** — Practice veneration; refuse contempt

Veneration is joy toward one we imagine our superior; contempt is joy toward one we imagine our inferior. Spinoza files both as affects to be governed by reason, not obeyed. The useful core of veneration is respect's function: it opens the mind to learn from those who excel. Contempt's core is a cheap joy purchased at another's expense, and it quietly corrodes the contemptuous person's social world. Practically, keep the learning channel of respect open and close the discounting channel of contempt.

(Ethics III, definitions of Veneration and Contempt.)

**Plain words** Veneration is joy toward one we imagine our superior; contempt is joy toward one we imagine our inferior. Both are affects to be governed by reason, not obeyed. The useful core of veneration is respect's function: it opens the mind. Contempt's function is to close it.

*For example:* The team's two experts — one venerated, one despised — and the manager's Spinozist audit: what does the venerated one actually open here, and what does the despised one actually close? The affects were pointing at something real; the reason's job is to aim them.

> **Principle 14** — Use cultural identity as a resource, not a constraint

Phinney's review of 70 studies opens with a claim and a warning: "Ethnic identity is central to the psychological functioning of members of ethnic and racial minority groups, but research on the topic is fragmentary and inconclusive" ([Phinney 1990, Psychological Bulletin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2270238/)). One study of 196 college students found that "self-esteem, especially for minority subjects was related to the extent that subjects had thought about and resolved issues involving their ethnicity" ([Phinney and Alipuria 1990](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1016/0140-1971(90)90006-S)). In the acculturation literature, people who "integrate (defined as being engaged in both their heritage culture and in the larger society) are better adapted" than those who orient to one culture only or to neither ([Sam and Berry 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1745691610373075)).

Stuart Hall supplies the "not a constraint" half. Cultural identity "is a matter of 'becoming' as well as of 'being'", and identities "are the names we give to the different ways we are positioned by, and position ourselves within, the narratives of the past" (Hall 1990, pp. 223-225, as quoted in [a university compilation of the essay's passages](https://cpb-eu-w2.wpmucdn.com/blogs.brighton.ac.uk/dist/f/7504/files/2020/11/Cultural-Identity-and-Diaspora-Quotes.pdf)).

### Scope

The words "resource" and "constraint" belong to the compendium. The sources show that thinking through your cultural identity goes with higher self-esteem and that holding both cultures goes with better adaptation. Neither source shows that identity "constrains" people. I did not open Sellers or Cross.

**Plain words**
Your culture can give you belonging, stories and practices. It is yours to examine, keep what serves you and question what does not.

**For example**
Pick one tradition you take part in. Write down what it gives you and one thing in it you would change. Ask an older member of the community what they think about that change.

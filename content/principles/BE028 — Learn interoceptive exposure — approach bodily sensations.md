> **Principle 28** — Learn interoceptive exposure: approach bodily sensations

Interoceptive exposure involves deliberately inducing feared bodily sensations (spinning, hyperventilation, breath-holding) to reduce fear of them. This is a specific treatment for panic disorder.

**Plain words** Some fears are of your own body — racing heart, dizziness, breathlessness — and those are trained too: deliberately induce the sensation, safely, until the alarm loses its meaning. The body's smoke alarm is being recalibrated, not obeyed.

*For example:* Spinning in a chair to practise dizziness — sounds absurd, works anyway; for panic disorder, do this kind of work with a clinician. After enough reps, the racing heart reads as "body doing body things," not "impending doom."
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Interoceptive exposure (Barlow)|Interoceptive exposure (Barlow)]]**, inducing feared bodily sensations reduces panic (Barlow et al., 2000; Craske et al., 2014)
- **Panic disorder treatment**, interoceptive exposure is a core component of panic disorder treatment (Barlow et al., 2000; Otto et al., 2010)

## Practice

- If you fear bodily sensations (racing heart, dizziness, breathlessness), deliberately induce them in safe contexts (spin, hyperventilate, exercise) to learn they are not dangerous. This reduces panic.

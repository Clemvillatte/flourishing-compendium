> **Principle 74** — Use bicultural identity integration as a strength

The Benet-Martínez paper reports three studies testing whether perceived compatibility between two cultural orientations moderates cultural frame shifting. ([Benet-Martínez et al., 2002](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ653127)) A later review concludes that integrating bicultural identities is central to psychological adjustment in immigration-based and globalization-based acculturation. ([Chen, 2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/ajsp.12088)) The creativity link is context-dependent: among 177 Chinese Americans, greater bicultural identity blendedness predicted domain-general creativity in bicultural but not monocultural contexts, mediated by ideational fluency. ([Saad et al., 2013](https://www.academia.edu/9065434/Saad_Damian_Benet_Martinez_Moons_and_Robins_2013_))

> "Results found that Chinese American biculturals who perceived their cultural identities as compatible responded in culturally congruent ways to cultural cues, while those who perceived their cultural identities as oppositional exhibited a reverse priming effect." ([Benet-Martínez et al., 2002, abstract as carried by ERIC EJ653127](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ653127))

**Plain words** You can value both cultural identities without treating them as rivals. As an exercise, describe what matters to you in each, then consider a situation where you would like to draw on both.

**For example (imagined):** While planning a wedding, someone chooses a reading from each of their two cultural traditions. They explain to their partner why each belongs in the ceremony.

## Practice

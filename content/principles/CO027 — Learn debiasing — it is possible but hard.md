> **Principle 27** — Learn debiasing: it is possible but hard
Morewedge and colleagues tested a computer game and an instructional video in two longitudinal experiments, targeting six biases across the experiments, including confirmation bias and anchoring. ([Morewedge et al., 2015, abstract](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf))

> "Early attempts to reduce decision biases with training met with little success, leading scientists and policy makers to focus on debiasing by using incentives and changes in the presentation and elicitation of decisions." (Morewedge et al., 2015, [Policy Insights from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences 2(1):129-140](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf))

The authors reported reductions on their bias measures after both training formats, including at eight weeks in Experiment 1 and twelve weeks in Experiment 2. ([Morewedge et al., 2015, Experiments 1 and 2](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf)) The games included personalized feedback and practice and generally produced larger reductions than the videos. ([Morewedge et al., 2015, abstract](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf)) Among the strategies taught were considering alternative explanations, base rates, and countervailing evidence. ([Morewedge et al., 2015, training description](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf))

**Plain words**
Knowing what an error is and checking for it in your own answer are separate tasks. For the compendium's exercise, choose a judgment you want to review. Write another explanation for the same facts and identify evidence that could count against your first answer. This adapts strategies taught in the games. ([Morewedge et al., 2015, training description](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324/1/Debiasing_Decisions_PIBBS.pdf))

*For example:*
As a hypothetical application, imagine favouring a job applicant after a confident opening. Before deciding, list another explanation for that confidence and identify a question that could test your impression.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Debiasing techniques|Debiasing techniques]]**, considering the opposite, accountability, and reference-class forecasting reduce bias (Larrick, 2004; Morewedge et al., 2015)
- **Awareness alone is insufficient**, knowing about a bias does not automatically correct it (Pronin et al., 2002; Morewedge et al., 2015)
- **Training games and inoculation can reduce susceptibility**, interactive debiasing training shows some effects (Morewedge et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires method, not just intention]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Fallibilism|Fallibilism as method]]** (Peirce, "The Fixation of Belief")
- **The scientific method as debiasing** (Popper, The Logic of Scientific Discovery)

## Practice

- Do not rely on "knowing about biases." Build structural safeguards: checklists, reference classes, outside views, and other people who will challenge you.

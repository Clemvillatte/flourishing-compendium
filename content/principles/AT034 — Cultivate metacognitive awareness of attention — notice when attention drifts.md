> **Principle 34** — Cultivate metacognitive awareness of attention: notice when attention drifts

The capacity to notice that your attention has wandered — to be aware of your attentional state — is a metacognitive skill. It is distinct from having good attention. You can have poor attention but good awareness of when it wanders.

**Plain words** Noticing that your attention has left is a separate skill from having attention — and the more important one. That noticing is the smoke alarm of the mind: without it, you can burn an hour before you smell anything.

*For example:* "I'm planning my holiday in the middle of this budget review" — that single sentence is the whole skill. It's not staying focused; it's catching the drift.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Meta-awareness|Meta-awareness of attentional states]]**, the ability to notice mind-wandering is distinct from the ability to prevent it (Smallwood & Schooler, 2015; Hasenkamp et al., 2012)
- **Meditation improves meta-awareness**, open monitoring meditation specifically trains the capacity to notice the contents of awareness (Lutz et al., 2008; Mrazek et al., 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right mindfulness|Right mindfulness of mental states]]** (Buddhism, Satipatthana Sutta)
- **[[The observing self|The observing self]]** (Deikman, The Observing Self)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]]** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Practise noticing your attentional state. Ask: "where is my attention right now?" When you notice it has wandered, gently note "wandering" and return. This noticing itself is the skill.

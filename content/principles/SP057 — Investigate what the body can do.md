> **Principle 57** — Investigate what the body can do

No one, Spinoza writes, has yet determined what the body can do from the laws of nature alone; the body's capabilities exceed the mind's beliefs about them. The proposition is not mysticism but an open research program: much of what you call your limits is your beliefs about your body, and the beliefs are usually out of date. Practically, treat your current self-description as a hypothesis with a testing schedule: strength, breath, sleep, and skill each respond to systematic probing.

(Ethics III, P2 scholium; V, Preface.)

**Plain words** No one has yet determined what the body can do from the laws of nature alone — the body's capabilities exceed the mind's beliefs about them. Not mysticism; an open research program. Much of what you call "you" is an untested estimate. Investigate what the body can do.

*For example:* The person "not a runner" by identity — and the couch-to-marathon program that reveals the body never agreed to the label. The belief about the body was a draft, not a measurement. Test it.

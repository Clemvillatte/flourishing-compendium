> **Principle 57** — Investigate what the body can do

The line comes from the scholium to Ethics III, Proposition 2, where Spinoza argues against the idea that the mind commands the body. In Elwes's translation: "no one has yet been taught by experience what the body can do merely by the laws of nature". He adds that "sleep-walkers" do "many things which they would not dare, were they awake: all of which sufficiently shows that the body can do many things by the laws of its nature alone at which the mind is amazed" ([Ethics III, Elwes, Marxists Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/ne/spinoza.htm)).

The aim is philosophical. One commentator reads Spinoza as warning that "the soul is usually used as a black box" for actions we cannot explain physically, and says the passage should not be read as scepticism about medicine ([Spinoza's Missing Physiology](https://shs.hal.science/halshs-02097613/document)). Deleuze later called the passage Spinoza's "war cry" ([Deleuze essay, Minerva](https://minerva.mic.ul.ie/vol11/deleuze.pdf)). So the passage is about explanation and about how much power we credit to the mind. It does not say untrained people have untapped strength or endurance, and I found no study in these sources on updating self-beliefs by testing them.

(Ethics III, P2 scholium; V, Preface.)

### Scope

Reading the line as a prompt to test one's self-description is the compendium's use of it. I did not open Ethics V, Preface, and have not checked the cited locator.

**Plain words**
Spinoza says we do not yet know what a body can do by its own nature. That does not prove you can do more than you think, but it is a reason to test beliefs about your limits.

**For example**
Pick one belief such as "I can't run for ten minutes". Set a small, safe trial that could show it false, then write down what happened.

> **Principle 55** — Learn to notice body signals: hunger, fatigue, tension, emotion

Interoception is the research name for this skill. Khalsa and colleagues describe it as "the process by which the nervous system senses, interprets, and integrates signals originating from within the body", and note that "dysfunction of interoception is increasingly recognized as an important component of different mental health conditions, including anxiety disorders, mood disorders, eating disorders, addictive disorders, and somatic symptom disorders" (Khalsa et al. 2018, Biol Psychiatry Cogn Neurosci Neuroimaging 3(6):501-513, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29884281/)). The same paper adds that "a number of conceptual and methodological challenges" make it hard to apply these ideas in treatment.

One caution matters for self-care. Garfinkel and colleagues tested 80 people and found that three things come apart: accuracy on an objective heartbeat-detection test, how good people believe they are, and how well their confidence matches their performance. "Interoceptive accuracy was only partly predicted by interoceptive awareness and interoceptive sensibility" (Garfinkel et al. 2015, Biol Psychol 104:65-74, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25451381/)). Feeling tuned in to your body does not guarantee that you read it correctly.

**Plain words**
Your body keeps sending information about hunger, tiredness, tension and mood. Paying attention to it is a reasonable habit, and researchers link difficulties with these signals to several mental health conditions. But people are not always as accurate at reading their own bodies as they feel, so treat a signal as a prompt to check, not as proof. If a sensation worries you, a doctor can check what it means.

*For example:* At mid-afternoon you notice a tight jaw and a short temper. You ask yourself whether you have eaten, slept badly or been sitting for hours, and you try the cheapest fix first. This is an imagined case, and the cause may well be something else.

## Psychology parallels

- **Interoceptive signals and self-care**, noticing body signals is studied as interoception, and its disruption is linked to several mental health conditions (Khalsa et al., 2018); Craig, 2009; Barrett et al., 2019
- **Accuracy is not the same as confidence**, objective, self-rated and metacognitive interoception are dissociable (Garfinkel et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Notice body signals: hunger, fatigue, tension, emotion. Ask: "what is my body telling me?" Respond (eat, rest, stretch, regulate). Do not ignore or override body signals.

> **Principle 28** — Ground everything in non-harm

First of the restraints: non-harm (ahimsa) in action, speech, and thought. The tradition treats it as the foundation the other restraints interpret; truthfulness, for example, is bounded by non-harm. Practically, when two values collide, let the one that reduces harm win, and include your own long-term welfare in the calculus.

(YS 2.30-2.35; BG 13.7-11 list, where non-injury heads the virtues.)

**Plain words** Non-harm, in action, speech, and thought — first of the restraints, and the foundation the others interpret: truthfulness is bounded by non-harm. When two values collide, let the one that reduces harm win.

*For example:* The moment of the "brutally honest" feedback session — satya and ahimsa collide — and the practitioner finds the version of the truth that does its work without the shrapnel. That search is the restraint in action.

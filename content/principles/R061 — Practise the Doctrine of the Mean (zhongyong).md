> **Principle 323** — Practise the Doctrine of the Mean (zhongyong)

The Zhongyong opens by defining its terms, and the definitions are not about moderation at all. They are about what happens before and after the feelings move:

> "While there are no stirrings of pleasure, anger, sorrow, or joy, the mind may be said to be in the state of EQUILIBRIUM. When those feelings have been stirred, and they act in their due degree, there ensues what may be called the state of HARMONY. This EQUILIBRIUM is the great root from which grow all the human actings in the world, and this HARMONY is the universal path which they all should pursue." (Zhongyong, Legge trans., I.4)

The text's own diagnosis of why the path fails is not laziness but overshoot and undershoot: "The knowing go beyond it, and the stupid do not come up to it... The men of talents and virtue go beyond it, and the worthless do not come up to it" (Zhongyong, Legge trans.). The Analects states the same rule as a verdict on two of Confucius's own students: "Shi goes beyond the due mean, and Shang does not come up to it... To go beyond is as wrong as to fall short" (Analects 11.16, Legge trans.).

So zhongyong is not splitting the difference. It is hitting the measure the situation actually has, which is why the classic insists "The superior man embodies the course of the Mean; the mean man acts contrary to the course of the Mean" (Zhongyong, Legge trans.) and laments "Alas! How is the path of the Mean untrodden!"

(The Doctrine of the Mean; Confucius, The Analects 11.16.)

**Plain words** The mean here is not lukewarm. The Analects verdict is that going too far is as wrong as not going far enough, which means the target is some actual right measure, not the midpoint of whatever you were already doing. Courage is the classic case: the same act is rash in one situation and cowardly in another, and the mean is whatever this one requires.

*For example:* Imagine a parent not banning the teenager's phone and not surrendering to it either, but looking for the actual right measure for this kid, this year, this problem.

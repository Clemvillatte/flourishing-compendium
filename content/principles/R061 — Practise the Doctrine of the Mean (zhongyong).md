> **Principle 323** — Practise the Doctrine of the Mean (zhongyong)

The Zhongyong opens by defining its terms:

> "While there are no stirrings of pleasure, anger, sorrow, or joy, the mind may be said to be in the state of EQUILIBRIUM. When those feelings have been stirred, and they act in their due degree, there ensues what may be called the state of HARMONY. This EQUILIBRIUM is the great root from which grow all the human actings in the world, and this HARMONY is the universal path which they all should pursue." (Zhongyong, Legge trans., [I.4](http://wengu.tartarie.com/wg/wengu.php?l=Zhongyong&no=4&s=1))

The text's own diagnosis of why the path fails:

> "The knowing go beyond it, and the stupid do not come up to it... The men of talents and virtue go beyond it, and the worthless do not come up to it." (Zhongyong, Legge trans., [I.11](http://wengu.tartarie.com/wg/wengu.php?l=Zhongyong&no=11&s=1))

The Analects states the same rule as a verdict on two of Confucius's own students: "Shi goes beyond the due mean, and Shang does not come up to it... To go beyond is as wrong as to fall short" (Analects 11.16, Legge trans., [Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/analects/xian-jin)).

The classic also insists "The superior man embodies the course of the Mean; the mean man acts contrary to the course of the Mean" (Zhongyong, Legge trans., [I.8](http://wengu.tartarie.com/wg/wengu.php?l=Zhongyong&no=8&s=1)) and laments "Alas! How is the path of the Mean untrodden!" (Zhongyong, Legge trans., [I.12](http://wengu.tartarie.com/wg/wengu.php?l=Zhongyong&no=12&s=1))

(The Doctrine of the Mean; Confucius, The Analects 11.16.)

**Plain words** The Zhongyong's own terms are equilibrium and harmony: the mind before the feelings move, and feelings that act in their due degree once moved. The failure it names twice is missing the measure in both directions, going beyond and not coming up to it. The Analects verdict on two students makes the two miss alike: to go beyond is as wrong as to fall short.

*For example:* Imagine working out how much freedom to give a teenager this year: the two wrong answers are to go beyond the due measure and to not come up to it.

> **Principle 47** — Learn concrete rumination-stopping techniques: do not just "stop ruminating."

Ordering yourself to stop does not work well, and Wegner's review of the research on thought suppression opens with the reason: "A person who is asked to think aloud while trying not to think about a white bear will typically mention the bear once a minute" (Wegner, 2011, abstract, [American Psychologist 66(8)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22082381/)). The same review lists approaches that "have some promise", among them "focused distraction", "thought postponement" and "mindfulness".

In Watkins's review of lab studies, rumination, compared with distraction, is "reliably found to have negative consequences on mood and cognition", but only when "participants are already in a dysphoric mood" (Watkins, 2008, [Psychological Bulletin 134(2), 163-206](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2672052/)). Nolen-Hoeksema and colleagues add that "dozens of experimental studies show positive distractions relieve depressed mood" (Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco and Lyubomirsky, 2008, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26158958/)).

The cognitive route changes how you think about it, not whether. In a study Watkins reports, "concrete rumination resulted in fewer negative intrusions than did abstract rumination and distraction" (Watkins, 2008). In a trial of 42 people with medication-refractory residual depression, therapy aimed at rumination "significantly improved residual symptoms and remission rates" when added to usual care. It lacked an attention-matched control group (Watkins et al., 2011, [British Journal of Psychiatry 199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21778171/)).

**Plain words**
"Stop thinking about it" tends to bring the thought back. Do better with something to do instead: a change of activity, a shift to what you can see and hear, or a switch from "why does this always happen to me" to "what is one specific thing I could do next". Choose the method before the spiral starts.

**For example**
It is 2 a.m. and you are replaying a meeting. You get up, make tea, and write one concrete step for the morning, such as "email Sam before 10". The loop has less to do.

## Psychology parallels

- **Rumination can be interrupted by [[Behavioral activation|behavioural activation]] and attentional shift**, engaging in absorbing activity or shifting attention reduces rumination (Nolen-Hoeksema et al., 2008)
- **Rumination-focused CBT teaches concrete over abstract processing**, shifting from abstract ("why does this happen to me?") to concrete ("what specifically is the problem?") reduces rumination (Watkins, 2008; Watkins et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right action|Right action over rumination]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as engagement]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 63)
- **Activity as cure for melancholy** (Burton, The Anatomy of Melancholy)

## Practice

- When ruminating, do not try to think your way out. Do something absorbing (exercise, creative work, social connection) or shift to concrete problem-solving ("what is one specific thing I can do?").

> **Principle 24** — Learn to break goals into subgoals: small wins sustain motivation

Amabile and Kramer report what they learned from diaries kept by knowledge workers:

> Through exhaustive analysis of diaries kept by knowledge workers, we discovered the progress principle: Of all the things that can boost emotions, motivation, and perceptions during a workday, the single most important is making progress in meaningful work. (Amabile & Kramer, 2011)

On small wins specifically:

> When we think about progress, we often imagine how good it feels to achieve a long-term goal or experience a major breakthrough. These big wins are great—but they are relatively rare. The good news is that even small wins can boost inner work life tremendously. (Amabile & Kramer, 2011)

And on ordinary progress:

> Even ordinary, incremental progress can increase people's engagement in the work and their happiness during the workday. (Amabile & Kramer, 2011)

The compendium's exercise connects the finding to how goals are divided: if progress is what sustains motivation, divide the work so progress is visible often rather than rarely.

(Amabile & Kramer, 2011, "The Power of Small Wins," Harvard Business Review, May 2011.)

**Plain words**
The diary study found that people's best days were days they moved something forward, and that the steps were usually small. The compendium's exercise: take a long pursuit, divide it into steps small enough that one can be finished today, and note each finished step as you go.

*For example:* A thesis looks like one enormous object until it is a list of sections. Finishing one subsection on a Tuesday is a minor step for the project; the diary research suggests it can still be a major event for how the day feels.

## Practice

- Break large goals into small subgoals with short time horizons. Celebrate small wins. Make progress visible (checklist, tracker). Small wins are the fuel.

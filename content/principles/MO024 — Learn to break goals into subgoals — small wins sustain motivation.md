> **Principle 24** — Learn to break goals into subgoals: small wins sustain motivation

Large goals are motivating but distant. Breaking them into smaller subgoals provides frequent experiences of progress, which sustain motivation. Small wins are the fuel of long-term pursuit.

**Plain words** Big goals inspire and paralyse — cut them into subgoals so small you can't fail, and let the small wins feed the engine. Progress isn't a nice extra; it's the fuel itself. Manufacture it on purpose.

*For example:* The book written one terrible page a day — nobody wrote a book; a person wrote 300 pages one at a time. The subgoal was the whole trick.
## Psychology parallels

- **Subgoal setting and progress**, breaking goals into subgoals increases motivation and attainment (Bandura & Schunk, 1981; Latham & Seijts, 1999)
- **[[The progress principle|Progress principle (Amabile)]]**, small, regular progress is the strongest predictor of motivation and positive affect (Amabile & Kramer, 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step** (Laozi, Daodejing 64)
- **[[Gradual training|The gradual training]]** (Buddhism, Gradual Training Sutta)
- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean: large vision, small steps]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)

## Practice

- Break large goals into small subgoals with short time horizons. Celebrate small wins. Make progress visible (checklist, tracker). Small wins are the fuel.

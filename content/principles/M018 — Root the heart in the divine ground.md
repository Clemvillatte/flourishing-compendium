> **Principle 18** — Root the heart in the divine ground

Yolteotl, "deified heart", is the term León-Portilla's account of the painter uses: the tlacuilo is described as needing to become a yoltéotl, one with "a heart rooted in God". The passage presents this as the painter's interior condition. This page's application, stated as such: settling yourself before important work is an optional exercise, one way of trying to work from a steadier place than anxiety or display.

> "THE GOOD PAINTER IS WISE, GOD IS IN HIS HEART. HE PUTS DIVINITY INTO THINGS; HE CONVERSES WITH HIS OWN HEART" — the Toltec artist, in León-Portilla's translation ([Flower and Song](https://islandsofhistory.org/volume-3/flower-and-song-pre-columbian-literatures-of-mesoamerica/))

(Yolteotl as presented in Leon-Portilla's account of the painter.)

**Plain words** The painter described here understands the work as sacred. For an ordinary task, you might ask what you want the work to serve, beyond finishing it or impressing someone. Pausing to ask that question is an optional modern exercise, not a translation of yolteotl.

*For example:* A painter takes a commission for a parish hall and treats the work as service to something sacred; the settling beforehand is part of the work, not a warm-up to it.

> **Principle 47** — Take mastery as a spiritual path

The term toltec, in the tradition's own usage, means the master of a craft and its lineage: the stone-worker, the scribe, the feather-worker, the healer, the singer whose work is exact, beautiful, and transmitted. The tradition does not split mastery from wisdom: the tlamatinime are described in the texts as craftspersons of the heart, and the craftsperson's discipline is understood as a path of formation. Practically, choose one craft as your path (not your hobby, not your job title), give it years, and let it form you.

(The toltec tradition as presented in Leon-Portilla; the huehuetlatolli's craft counsel.)

**Plain words** Toltec, in the tradition's own usage, means the master of a craft and its lineage — the stone-worker, the scribe, the healer, the singer whose work is exact, beautiful, and transmitted. The tradition doesn't split mastery from wisdom: the sage is a craftsperson of the heart. Take mastery as a spiritual path.

*For example:* The carpenter on year thirty of the same joint — the discipline, the humility, the exactness — who is, by the tradition's lights, further along the path than a hundred retreat-goers.

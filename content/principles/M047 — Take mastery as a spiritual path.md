> **Principle 47** — Take mastery as a spiritual path

In Florentine Codex Book X, the craftsman (toltecatl) is described, in the translation quoted by a [philosophy survey](https://philarchive.org/archive/PUROWT), as "well instructed... The good craftsman is able, discreet, prudent, resourceful, retentive... patient, calm. He works with care." The same survey separates the toltecatl from the philosopher (tlamatini): the philosopher has wisdom (tlamatiliztli) but not artisanal knowledge (toltecayotl). So this source does not support the seed's claim that the tradition "does not split mastery from wisdom".

A looser link comes from León-Portilla's reading. A [study of his work on the toltecatl](https://repository.uaeh.edu.mx/revistas/index.php/icshu/article/download/12408/11855/) says he translates the word as "artist", and that the artist "dialoga con su propio corazón" (converses with his own heart). The same study notes the term's field is wider than "artist": it also means wise and refined, and the toltecayotl covers the best human achievements in society. The "master of a craft and its lineage" in the seed is an interpretation.

The research on mastery is mixed. A meta-analysis found that deliberate practice explained 26% of performance variance in games, 21% in music, 18% in sports, 4% in education and under 1% in professions, and its authors concluded it is "important, but not as important as has been argued" ([Macnamara et al. 2014](https://www.psychologie.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:658d39fe-2f4b-467f-9ae2-ffeb25caf2b6/McNamara.PsychSci.25.2014.pdf)). [Ericsson and Harwell](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824411/) reanalysed it and estimated 61% after correcting for unreliability.

(The toltec tradition as presented in Leon-Portilla; the huehuetlatolli's craft counsel.)

### Scope

Flow research (Csikszentmihalyi) was not opened. Nothing found shows that craft practice makes a person wiser.

**Plain words**
Nahua sources praise the craftsman as patient, careful and prudent. Whether craft also makes someone wise is the compendium's hope, not a finding.

**For example**
Choose one craft and keep a log of dated practice sessions for a year. At each month's end, write one sentence on what the craft asked of your patience.

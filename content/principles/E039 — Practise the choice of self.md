> **Principle 431** — Practise the choice of self

Sartre's lecture Existentialism Is a Humanism gives the position in one sentence: "Man is nothing else but that which he makes of himself. That is the first principle of existentialism." He reaches it from the claim that for human beings existence precedes essence: man "first of all exists, encounters himself, surges up in the world — and defines himself afterwards." There is no ready-made self to consult before acting; the acting is what there is.

Being and Nothingness gives the same point a sterner wording. There the choice of self is not one decision made early in life but a structure that must be kept up:

> Since freedom is a being-without-support and without-a-springboard, the project in order to be must be constantly renewed. I choose myself perpetually and can never be merely by virtue of having-been-chosen; otherwise I should fall into the pure and simple existence of the in-itself. The necessity of perpetually choosing myself is one with the pursued-pursuit which I am. (Sartre, Being and Nothingness, Part IV, ch. 1, p. 480, Barnes trans.)

The lecture and the book agree on the practical consequence: waiting to discover who you are is a misuse of the time, because the self is not the kind of thing that arrives on its own. What you repeat, you are choosing again; what you interrupt, you are choosing the other way.

(Sartre, Existentialism Is a Humanism, 1946, Mairet trans.; Sartre, Being and Nothingness, 1943/1956, Part IV, ch. 1, p. 480.)

**Plain words**
You are not a fixed thing waiting to be found. What you count as your character was built by choices and is kept in place by further choices, including the choice to repeat what was already there. The practice is to catch one ordinary decision and see it as one of those choices, not as a mere fact happening to you.

*For example:* Take a claim you make about yourself, such as "I'm bad with names." List the choices this week that kept it true — the introductions you let pass without repeating the name back. Then repeat one name tomorrow and see whether the claim still describes you.

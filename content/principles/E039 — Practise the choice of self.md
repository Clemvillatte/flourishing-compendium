> **Principle 431** — Practise the choice of self

Sartre writes in Existentialism Is a Humanism:

"Man is nothing else but that which he makes of himself. That is the first principle of existentialism." He reaches it from the claim that for human beings existence precedes essence: man "first of all exists, encounters himself, surges up in the world — and defines himself afterwards."

In Being and Nothingness, Sartre describes the project as requiring continual renewal:

> Since freedom is a being-without-support and without-a-springboard, the project in order to be must be constantly renewed. I choose myself perpetually and can never be merely by virtue of having-been-chosen; otherwise I should fall into the pure and simple existence of the in-itself. The necessity of perpetually choosing myself is one with the pursued-pursuit which I am. (Sartre, Being and Nothingness, Part IV, ch. 1, p. 480, Barnes trans.)

(Sartre, Existentialism Is a Humanism, 1946, Warwick-hosted text; Sartre, Being and Nothingness, 1943/1956, Part IV, ch. 1, p. 480.)

**Plain words**
The compendium uses this passage to revisit a commitment you made earlier. Ask what keeping it would involve today. Consider an action you could take, given your circumstances, and whether you still want to carry that commitment forward.

*For example:* Suppose you volunteered to help edit a friend's essay. When the draft arrives, consider whether you can still give it the time you promised. You might agree on a time to read it or explain that you need to change the arrangement.

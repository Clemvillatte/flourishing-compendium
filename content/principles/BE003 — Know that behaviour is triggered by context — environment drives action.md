> **Principle 3** — Know that behaviour is triggered by context: environment drives action

Behaviour is not primarily driven by willpower or motivation; it is triggered by context. Environmental cues (time, place, preceding action, people, objects) automatically activate behaviours. Changing the context changes the behaviour.

**Plain words** Context beats character. The same person is punctual in one office and flaky in another — because the environment is doing most of the steering. If you want reliable change, edit the context; willpower is the backup generator, not the grid.

*For example:* The runner whose shoes live by the door runs; the one whose shoes live under the bed doesn't. Same character, different hallway.

- **[[Context-dependent behaviour (Wood & Neal)|Context-dependent behaviour (Wood & Neal)]]**, environmental cues trigger behaviour automatically (Wood & Neal, 2007; Gardner, 2015)
- **[[Choice architecture|Choice architecture (Thaler & Sunstein)]]**, designing the environment shapes behaviour more effectively than willpower (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008; Marteau et al., 2012)
- **Habit disruption through context change**, changing environment disrupts habits (Wood & Neal, 2007; Gardner, 2015)

## Practice

- Design your environment: make desired behaviours easy (reduce friction) and undesired behaviours hard (increase friction). Do not rely on willpower; rely on context.

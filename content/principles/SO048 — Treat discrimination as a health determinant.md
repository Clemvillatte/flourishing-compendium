> **Principle 48** — Treat discrimination as a health determinant

Paradies and colleagues' meta-analysis of 293 studies reported in 333 articles found reported racism associated with poorer health across outcomes: "Racism was associated with poorer mental health... including depression, anxiety, psychological stress and various other outcomes", and "also associated with poorer general health... and poorer physical health" (2015). The authors' summary is measured: the relationship is "stronger for poor mental health and weaker for poor physical health", and the mental-health effect sizes "were stronger in cross-sectional compared with longitudinal data", so the associations are associations. The compendium's point sits upstream of the statistics: if reported racism reliably travels with worse health, then the social level is a place health work has to reach.

> "Racism was associated with poorer mental health (negative mental health: r = -.23, 95% CI [-.24,-.21], k = 227; positive mental health: r = -.13, 95% CI [-.16,-.10], k = 113), including depression, anxiety, psychological stress and various other outcomes. Racism was also associated with poorer general health (r = -.13 (95% CI [-.18,-.09], k = 30), and poorer physical health (r = -.09, 95% CI [-.12,-.06], k = 50)." (Paradies et al., 2015, abstract)

**Plain words** The reviewed studies linked reported racism with poorer health, especially mental health. Experiences of unfair treatment belong in a discussion of health alongside other relevant circumstances.

*For example:* Imagine a clinic intake form that asks about experiences of discrimination as well as individual habits.

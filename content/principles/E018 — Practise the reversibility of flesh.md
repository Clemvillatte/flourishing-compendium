> **Principle 410** — Practise the reversibility of flesh

> To begin with, we spoke summarily of a reversibility of the seeing and the visible, of the touching and the touched. It is time to emphasize that it is a reversibility always imminent and never realized in fact. My left hand is always on the verge of touching my right hand touching the things, but I never reach coincidence ...

(Merleau-Ponty, The Visible and the Invisible, "The Intertwining—The Chiasm", trans. Lingis, Northwestern University Press, 1968, pp. 147-148, [chapter scan](https://doubleoperative.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/12/merleau-ponty-the-intertwining.pdf); page 147 is also given by [Wiskus](https://avant.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/J_Wiskus-From_the_Body.pdf).)

Earlier in the chapter he describes "a veritable touching of the touch, when my right hand touches my left hand while it is palpating the things" (pp. 133-134). In that moment the "touching subject" passes "over to the rank of the touched."

The next page limits the claim: "either my right hand really passes over to the rank of touched, but then its hold on the world is interrupted; or it retains its hold on the world, but then I do not really touch it." Flesh, in his words, "is not matter. It is the coiling over of the visible upon the seeing body, of the tangible upon the touching body." So you are both toucher and touched, and never exactly at once.

**Plain words** You can feel the touch and be the thing touched, but the two do not fully merge. Merleau-Ponty says the switch is always about to happen and is never completed. That gap matters: you are inside the world you perceive, and you never become a pure observer or a pure object.

*For example:* Press your right hand against your left. Notice whether you can attend to the pressing and to being pressed at the same moment. Merleau-Ponty's answer is that you cannot fully, and that the near-miss is what shows your body belongs to both sides.

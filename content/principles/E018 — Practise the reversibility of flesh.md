> **Principle 410** — Practise the reversibility of flesh

Recognise that you are both perceiver and perceived, both touching and touched. The concept of flesh (chair), developed in Merleau-Ponty's later work, names the fundamental reversibility of the perceiving body: the hand that touches is also the hand that can be touched. You are not a pure subject confronting objects; you are part of the same flesh of the world.

(Merleau-Ponty, 1964/1968, The Visible and the Invisible, ch. 4; Merleau-Ponty, 1960/1968, "Eye and Mind," in The Primacy of Perception.)

**Plain words** You are both perceiver and perceived, touching and touched — the hand that touches is also the hand that can be touched. Flesh (chair): the fundamental reversibility of the perceiving body. You're on both sides of the window.

*For example:* Someone scratches their own back and feels both the scratching and the itch from two sides at once — the everyday proof that you're not a camera pointed at the world; you're made of the same stuff the world is made of.

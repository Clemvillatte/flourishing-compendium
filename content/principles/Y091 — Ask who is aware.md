> **Principle 91** — Ask who is aware

The self-inquiry (atma-vichara) of the Advaita line: turning attention from the object toward the one aware of it, not to find a thing but to exhaust the search's assumptions. The book states the standing dispute plainly: Advaita's atman and Buddhism's anatman disagree about what the inquiry finally shows, and the practice teaches the inquiry, not the verdict. Practically, ask "who is aware of this?" and treat the question, not the answer, as the practice.

(Ramana Maharshi, modern articulation of the Advaita line; the Upanishadic "who" questions, Kena 1.1-2.4.)

**Plain words** Atma-vichara, self-inquiry: turn attention from the object toward the one aware of it — not to find a thing, but to exhaust the search's assumptions. The standing dispute (Advaita's atman, Buddhism's anatman) is honestly flagged: the procedure works either way; the metaphysics is disputed.

*For example:* Someone asks "who is aware of this worry?" and looks back for the looker — finds no object there, but the worry has lost its grip in the meantime. The inquiry's value, most days, is in the asking.

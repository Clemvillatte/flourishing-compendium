> **Principle 477** — Practise the promise

Recognise that your capacity to make and keep promises is the foundation of your identity as a self. Ricoeur links the promise to ipse identity — the self as agent who can say "I will" and be held to it. Your capacity to commit, to promise, to be faithful to your word, is the ground of your selfhood.

(Ricoeur, 1990/1992, Oneself as Another, "The Self and the Ethical Aim"; Marcel, 1951a, Being and Having.)

**Plain words** Your capacity to make and keep promises is the foundation of your identity as a self — Ricoeur ties the self to the one who can say "I will" and be held to it. The self is built out of kept words. Guard the capacity; it's the load-bearing part.

*For example:* The friend who has quietly kept twenty years of small promises — rides, calls, showings-up — and whose identity, in everyone's eyes, is built out of exactly that ledger. The self is the archive of the kept words.

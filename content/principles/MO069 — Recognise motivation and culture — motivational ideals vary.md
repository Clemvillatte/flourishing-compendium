> **Principle 69** — Recognise motivation and culture: motivational ideals vary

What counts as "good" motivation varies across cultures. Individualist cultures idealise autonomous, self-directed motivation; collectivist cultures may value relational and duty-based motivation. Neither is wrong.

**Plain words** What counts as "good motivation" is cultural — some cultures idealise the self-chosen path, others the path that serves family and duty. Neither is wrong; they're different operating systems. Judge your own motivation against your own culture's best, not someone else's.

*For example:* The doctor studying medicine for the family's honour — individualist metrics call this "controlled motivation," and it outperforms them all anyway. The fuel was never the problem; the ruler was.

- **Cultural variation in motivation**, motivational ideals differ across cultures (Markus & Kitayama, 1991; Iyengar & Lepper, 1999)
- **Autonomy support varies in effect across cultures**, autonomy support is not universally ideal (Iyengar & Lepper, 1999; Bao & Lam, 2008)

## Practice

- Recognise that your motivational ideals are partly cultural. Do not assume your way of motivating is universal. What works varies by context.

> **Principle 13** — Learn to set attentional intentions: decide what to attend to in advance

Implementation intentions link an anticipated situation to a planned goal-directed response. Gollwitzer and Sheeran reviewed whether forming these if-then plans helps people carry out their goals. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006.)

> "Findings from 94 independent tests showed that implementation intentions had a positive effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d= .65) on goal attainment." (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, [abstract](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f))

The review reports process findings concerning the accessibility of specified opportunities and the automation of goal-directed responses. The compendium applies this planning format to attention in the exercise below. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006.)

**Plain words**
Choose a situation in which you want to listen closely. Decide what you will do if something interrupts you, and make the response specific enough to try. Afterwards, describe whether you followed your plan.

*For example:* Imagine making a plan before a family dinner: if the phone lights up while your sister is talking, you will leave it on the table and return your attention to her.

## Practice

- Before beginning work, write down: "for the next X minutes, I will attend to [specific task]." When distracted, return to this intention. Implementation intentions ("if I notice distraction, then I will return to the task") work well.

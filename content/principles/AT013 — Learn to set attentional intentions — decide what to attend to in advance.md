> **Principle 13** — Learn to set attentional intentions: decide what to attend to in advance

An attentional intention is a decision made in advance about what to attend to. The strongest evidence for the move comes from the if-then plan literature: Gollwitzer and Sheeran's meta-analysis of implementation intentions, plans that spell out the when, where and how of goal striving in advance, reports a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment across 94 independent tests. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006.)

> "Findings from 94 independent tests showed that implementation intentions had a positive effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d= .65) on goal attainment." (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006, [abstract](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f))

The review also reports the process findings behind the effect: forming the plan enhanced the accessibility of the specified opportunities and automated the goal-directed responses. (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006.) The compendium's attentional intention is the same move pointed at attention rather than at a whole behavior. Decide what you will listen for or work on before the situation starts, and the decision is already there when the situation arrives.

**Plain words**
Deciding what to attend to works better before the situation starts, because the decision then competes with nothing. An attentional intention is one sentence, written or said to yourself, that names the object: what you are listening for, or which task the next hour is for. When your attention gets pulled, you do not have to decide again from scratch; the sentence is already there.

*For example:* In the car, before a family dinner, you say to yourself: at this dinner, I am listening to my sister. When the phone glows on the table, the decision was made an hour ago.

## Practice

- Before beginning work, write down: "for the next X minutes, I will attend to [specific task]." When distracted, return to this intention. Implementation intentions ("if I notice distraction, then I will return to the task") work well.

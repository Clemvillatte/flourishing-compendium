> **Principle 13** — Learn to set attentional intentions: decide what to attend to in advance

Before starting a task or entering a situation, deliberately decide what you will attend to. This top-down intention acts as a filter that reduces capture by irrelevant stimuli and improves focus.

**Plain words** Decide what you're attending to before you enter the situation, and the deciding is already done when the noise starts. An attentional intention is a pre-made decision, waiting to be used. It's the cheapest focus tool there is.

*For example:* "At this dinner, I'm listening to my sister" — one sentence decided in the car, and when the phone glows on the table, the decision's already made.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Goal-directed attentional control|Goal-directed attentional control]]**, setting intentions improves attentional selection and reduces distraction (Gollwitzer, 1999; Shelton & Alain, 2014)
- **[[Implementation intentions|Implementation intentions ("if-then plans")]]**, specifying when and where to act dramatically improves goal attainment (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right intention|Right intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Prohairesis (deliberate choice)|Prohairesis (deliberate choice)]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires intention]]** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Before beginning work, write down: "for the next X minutes, I will attend to [specific task]." When distracted, return to this intention. Implementation intentions ("if I notice distraction, then I will return to the task") work well.

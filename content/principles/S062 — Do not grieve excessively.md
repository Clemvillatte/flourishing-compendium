> **Principle 184** — Do not grieve excessively

Seneca's Letter 63 (Gummere translation) is gentler and stranger than the summary suggests. He writes "We may weep, but we must not wail" and "Let not the eyes be dry when we have lost a friend, nor let them overflow". He also says "There is an element of self-seeking even in our sorrow" and "To me, the thought of my dead friends is sweet and appealing". He then adds "It is better to replace your friend than to weep for him", and "Nothing becomes offensive so quickly as grief" once it has become chronic ([Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/seneca/moral-letters-to-lucilius/richard-m-gummere/text/letter-63)). The seed's definition, "excessive grief is a judgement that loss is unbearable", does not appear in those quoted lines. I did not open Letter 99, Marcus Aurelius or Epictetus.

The psychology parallels cut both ways. The dual process model has the bereaved at times confront and at times avoid loss and restoration tasks, and argues for "dosage of grieving", taking respite from either ([Stroebe and Schut 1999, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10848151/)). That fits "feel it, then return to what can be done". Klass, Silverman and Nickman (1996) challenge the model that grief should cut bonds with the deceased, and report that healthy resolution can keep a continuing bond ([Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Continuing-Bonds-New-Understandings-of-Grief/Klass-Silverman-Nickman/p/book/9781560323396)). Seneca's "sweet" memory fits that better than "replace your friend" does.

On "excessive": prolonged grief disorder requires grief lasting beyond 6 months (ICD-11) or 12 months (DSM-5-TR, adults), exceeding cultural norms, with impaired functioning ([Psychiatria Polska](https://www.psychiatriapolska.pl/pdf-159024-112359?filename=Zespol-zaloby-przedluzone.pdf)). Seneca’s test is a letter-writer’s judgement of proportion.

**Plain words** Seneca allows tears, asks you to avoid wailing, and says the memory of the dead can be sweet. Grief research supports moving between sorrow and daily tasks. It also treats lasting bonds with the dead as normal. Excessive means different things in a letter and in a clinic.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. After a parent's death, you mourn fully and also call the funeral home the next morning.

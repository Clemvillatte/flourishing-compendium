> **Principle 42** — Guard the center

Don't swing with every impulse, news cycle, or mood. The sage keeps to the middle: few words, steady state, excess held at arm's length.

(DDJ 5, 29.)

### Few words, guarded center

The line that gives the principle its wording is Legge's rendering of chapter 5:

> "Much speech to swift exhaustion lead we see;
> Your inner being guard, and keep it free." (DDJ 5, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The same chapter compares the space between heaven and earth to a bellows — emptied, yet it "loses not its power". Chapter 29 then names what the center is guarded against: "Hence the sage puts away excessive effort, extravagance, and easy indulgence" (DDJ 29, Legge) — excess on every side, including the effortful one.

**Plain words** A swing is any position that exists only while something is pushing it: a news cycle, a mood, an impulse. The center is the one position still there when the pushing stops. Practically: you do not need an opinion on every headline, and most moods can pass through without a decision attached. Few words too, because speech spends the very thing being guarded.

*For example:* A modern illustration, not the text's: draft the angry reply and do not send it; decide in the morning whether anything still needs saying.

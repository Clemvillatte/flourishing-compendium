> **Principle 4** — Stop reading purposes into events

Nature has no goal set before it, Spinoza argues in the Appendix to Part I of the Ethics. Because people act on purposes, they assume Nature does too, and then read the weather, the illness and the loss as messages addressed to them. ([Ethics I, Appendix, Elwes tr.](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)) His diagnosis of the habit: people are born ignorant of causes, desire what is useful to them, and are conscious of that desire, so they look for the final causes of events and judge other natures by their own. The compendium's application is that when something painful happens, you separate the event from the stories about what it means, and notice how much of the pain lives in the story.

> "There is no need to show at length, that nature has no particular goal in view, and that final causes are mere human figments." (Ethics I, Appendix, Elwes tr., [Gutenberg #3800](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

**Plain words** Purposes come from people, not from the sky. When you catch yourself asking what an event is trying to tell you, treat that as a habit of mind rather than a discovery about the world. The storm has causes, not messages.

**For example (imagined):** The illness arrives and the search begins: what is this teaching me, what did I do to attract it? The Spinozist answer is that the virus has causes and no message. Any meaning in the situation is made rather than found, and the making is yours.

> **Principle 4** — Stop reading purposes into events

Nature has no goal set before it, Spinoza argues in the appendix to Part I. All final causes are human fictions: because people act on purposes, they assume Nature does too, and then read the weather, the illness, and the loss as messages addressed to them. The appendix is one of the most practical pages in early modern philosophy, because it removes an entire layer of interpretive suffering: the event itself is bad enough; the conviction that it was aimed at you is the addition. Practically, when something painful happens, separate the event from every story about what it means, and notice how much of the pain lives in the story.

(Ethics I, Appendix.)

**Plain words** Nature has no goal set before it — all final causes are human fictions. People act on purposes, so they assume Nature does too, and then read the weather, the illness, and the loss as messages addressed to them. Stop reading purposes into events. The storm isn't about you.

*For example:* The illness arrives and the search begins: what is this teaching me? what did I do to attract this? — and the Spinozist answer is quieting: nothing; it's a virus. Meaning you make; the event just happened. The meaning-making is yours, which is better news, not worse.

> **Principle 79** — Recognise the impact of cognitive ageing: some capacities decline, others improve

Research on cognitive ageing is specific about the shape of the curve. Adult age differences are "documented on a wide variety of cognitive variables", but the reasons "are still poorly understood". What the evidence does show is that "age-related declines in measures of cognitive functioning (a) are relatively large, (b) begin in early adulthood, (c) are evident in several different types of cognitive abilities, and (d) are not always accompanied by increases in between-person variability" (Salthouse, 2004).

The rise side of the curve is just as specific. Across 48,537 online participants plus normative test data, "results reveal considerable heterogeneity in when cognitive abilities peak: Some abilities peak and begin to decline around high school graduation; some abilities plateau in early adulthood, beginning to decline in subjects' 30s; and still others do not peak until subjects reach their 40s or later" (Hartshorne & Germine, 2015). Different abilities move on different schedules.

The application here: take stock of which of your abilities sit on the early-declining side of the record and which sit on the still-rising side, and design around that stock. Offloading search-heavy work to external systems that do not age with you is one such design; what counts as search-heavy for you is something to observe rather than assume.

**Plain words** Nobody is simply "losing it" with age; every mind is losing some things and still gaining others, on different clocks. Speed of search goes early; pattern-knowing arrives late and stays. Treating that as bad news is a category error. The useful response is bookkeeping, not mourning: know which of your abilities are on the falling side and which on the rising side, give the falling ones help from tools and younger colleagues, and keep counting, because the mix keeps moving.

*For example:* The older engineer who can't out-debug the twenty-five-year-old on speed — and wins every time on pattern recognition and system knowledge — has stopped apologising for the split. The young ones search; the old ones recognise. Both are needed.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Cognitive ageing|Cognitive ageing]]**, processing speed and some fluid abilities decline from the 20s-30s, while other abilities do not peak until the 40s or later (Salthouse, 2004; Hartshorne & Germine, 2015)
- **Cognitive reserve protects against decline**, education, engagement, and intellectual activity build reserve (Stern, 2002; Lövdén et al., 2010)
- **[[Positivity effect|Positivity effect in older adults]]**, older adults attend more to positive and less to negative information (Carstensen et al., 2003; Reed & Carstensen, 2012)

## Practice

- As you age, do not fight the decline in speed; lean into the growth in wisdom, knowledge, and vocabulary. Build cognitive reserve through sustained intellectual engagement.

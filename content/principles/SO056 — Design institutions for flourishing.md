> **Principle 56** — Design institutions for flourishing

Design institutions — workplaces, schools, hospitals, governments, and the built environment — for adaptive flourishing, not only for efficiency or profit. The nurturing-environments framework (Biglan et al., 2012) and the population-prevention literature (Wahlbeck et al., 2015) show that environments can be designed to reduce suffering and promote flourishing.

The framework's own summary of what such environments do:

> "Environments that foster successful development and prevent the development of psychological and behavioral problems are usefully characterized as nurturing environments. First, these environments minimize biologically and psychologically toxic events. Second, they teach, promote, and richly reinforce prosocial behavior, including self-regulatory behaviors and all of the skills needed to become productive adult members of society. Third, they monitor and limit opportunities for problem behavior. Fourth, they foster psychological flexibility—the ability to be mindful of one's thoughts and feelings and act in the service of one's values, even when one's thoughts and feelings discourage taking valued action." (Biglan, Flay, Embry & Sandler, 2012, [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3621015/))

The article's convergence claim is the design brief this principle endorses: "Biological, behavioral, etiological, and intervention evidence converges on a fairly simple and straightforward principle: If we want to prevent multiple problems and increase the prevalence of young people who develop successfully, we must increase the prevalence of nurturing environments." (Biglan et al., 2012)

**Plain words** The framework gives you a checklist instead of a vibe: does the place reduce toxic conditions, reinforce the behaviour it wants, limit opportunities for trouble, and leave room for people to act on their values? That is a design brief you can hold an institution to.

*For example:* The hospital designed around patient recovery — light, quiet, gardens — versus the one designed around staff logistics. Same medicine, different outcomes. The building was practising healthcare.

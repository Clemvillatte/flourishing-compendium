> **Principle 28** — Use the outside view: base rates beat inside views

When predicting an outcome, do not reason from the specific details of your case (inside view). Instead, look at the actual distribution of outcomes for similar cases (outside view / reference class). This is one of the most effective debiasing techniques.

**Plain words** The outside view: when predicting an outcome, don't reason from the specific details of your case (inside view) — look at the actual distribution of outcomes for similar cases. Base rates beat inside views. Your project is probably an instance of the class.

*For example:* The couple planning the wedding, the founder planning the launch — both reasoning from their unique details, both ignoring the reference class. "How do weddings like this usually go?" isn't pessimism; it's the only honest actuarial question in the room.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Outside view / reference class forecasting|Outside view / reference class forecasting]]**, using base rates of similar cases improves prediction accuracy (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979; Flyvbjerg, 2006)
- **Reference class forecasting improves project estimates**, applied widely in infrastructure planning (Flyvbjerg, 2006; Lovallo & Kahneman, 2003)

## Practice

- When predicting how long something will take or how likely success is, first ask: "what has happened in similar cases?" Use the base rate as your starting estimate, then adjust for specifics.

> **Principle 49** — Learn to work with panic: panic is fear of fear

Panic attacks are intensely frightening, and a core maintaining factor is fear of the panic itself (fear of the body's sensations, fear of losing control, fear of dying). The treatment is not to avoid panic but to reduce the fear of panic's sensations.

**Plain words** Panic is fear of fear — the fear of the body's own sensations (the racing heart, the breathlessness, the dying feeling). The treatment is not avoiding panic but reducing the fear of it: interoceptive exposure, riding the wave, learning the body's alarm is loud but not lethal.

*For example:* The person who runs at the first heart-race — every staircase of the last year avoided — and the protocol: deliberately induce the racing heart (stairs, spinning), and learn the alarm can ring without the building burning. The fear of the fear was the fire.
## Psychology parallels

- **Panic disorder is maintained by catastrophic misinterpretation of bodily sensations**, the fear of the sensations is the maintaining factor (Clark, 1986)
- **Interoceptive exposure is effective**, deliberately inducing feared sensations (spinning, hyperventilation) reduces panic (Barlow et al., 2000)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Facing the monster|Facing the monster]]** (Kierkegaard, The Concept of Anxiety)
- **[[The fearless mind|The fearless mind]]** (Buddhism, Abhayaraja-kumara Sutta)

## Practice

- If panic is a pattern, learn that the sensations, while extremely unpleasant, are not dangerous. Deliberately inducing and tolerating the sensations (with professional guidance if severe) reduces the fear over time.

> **Principle 47** — Recognise attachment patterns: early relationships shape self-relation

Attachment patterns (secure, anxious, avoidant) formed in early relationships shape how you relate to yourself and others. Secure attachment is associated with better self-soothing, better relationships, and better outcomes. Attachment patterns can be revised ("earned security").

**Plain words** The way you learned to be close to people as a small child is still running in the background — secure, anxious, avoidant. It's not destiny, but it's the default setting. Knowing your pattern is half the escape from it; the other half is choosing differently when it activates.

*For example:* The person who reads a four-hour gap between texts as abandonment isn't responding to the text. She's responding to a childhood where warmth could vanish without warning. Naming that — "that's my pattern, not the situation" — is what keeps her from sending the third follow-up.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Attachment theory|Attachment theory (Bowlby)]]**, early attachment shapes internal working models of self and others (Bowlby, 1969; Mikulincer & Shaver, 2007)
- **Secure attachment is associated with better outcomes**, secure attachment predicts better self-soothing, relationships, and well-being (Mikulincer & Shaver, 2007; Gillath et al., 2005)
- **[[Earned security|Earned security]]**, people with difficult early experiences can develop secure attachment through later relationships and therapy (Roisman et al., 2002; Siegel, 1999)

## Practice

- Notice your attachment pattern: do you tend toward anxiety (cling, worry) or avoidance (distance, withdraw)? Recognise this as a learned pattern, not a fixed trait. Earned security is possible through relationships and work.

> **Principle 55** — Train generosity as the first perfection

Gampopa's Jewel Ornament of Liberation puts generosity first among the perfections and defines it inwardly: "The definition of generosity is the practice of giving fully without attachment" (ch. 12, [opened PDF](https://karunabuddhistcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Gampopa_-Konchog-Gyaltsen-Rinpoche-Tr-The-Jewel-Ornament-of-Liberation_-The-Wish-Fulfilling-Gem-of-the-Noble-Teachings-1998.pdf)). The three kinds are practical, not financial: "The practice of giving wealth will stabilize others' bodies, giving fearlessness will stabilize others' lives, and giving Dharma stabilizes others' minds."

The measure is the mind, not the ledger. Shantideva's verse on the same perfection:

> "The perfection of giving is said to be through the mind that would give away to everyone all that is mine, together with its results; thus, it's the mind itself." (Bodhicharyavatara 5.10)

His proof that it cannot be the outer act: "if the perfection of giving were that the poverty of wandering beings was all gone; then how could the Guardians of old have perfected it, since wandering beings have hunger still now?" (5.9). And the grading is built in: Shantideva holds back the body itself until compassion ripens, "I shall not give this body away while my thought of compassion is still not pure" (5.87) (translation carried on the opened [redzambala page](https://buddhism.redzambala.com/shantideva/bodhisattva-behaviour-by-shantideva-part-5.html)).

Practically, give something small daily for a month, and watch the grip-meter rather than the ledger.

**Plain words** Generosity comes first among the perfections because it works on the tightest grip first. The definition is inward: giving fully without attachment. Three channels: wealth stabilizes bodies, fearlessness stabilizes lives, teaching stabilizes minds. The measure is the mind that would give, not the amount given, and Shantideva's proof is that if the perfection were ending others' poverty, no one could have perfected it yet. The body itself waits until compassion is ripe. So the practice is small, real gifts, repeated; the repetition is the point.

For example: Giving up an evening to help a friend move, when you'd rather rest, and noticing a small release.

> **Principle 469** — Practise the liberation of others

De Beauvoir's claim is a conclusion she argues for, not a slogan. In The Ethics of Ambiguity she writes that "man can find a justification of his own existence only in the existence of other men", and then draws the line: "To will oneself free is also to will others free. This will is not an abstract formula. It points out to each person concrete action to be achieved" (Part II, "Personal Freedom and Others", trans. Frechtman).

### Why your freedom depends on theirs

Her reasons are practical. "Only the freedom of others keeps each one of us from hardening in the absurdity of facticity" (Part II). In Part III, "Freedom and Liberation", she adds that "a freedom wills itself genuinely only by willing itself as an indefinite movement through the freedom of others", and that "the existence of others as a freedom defines my situation and is even the condition of my own freedom". Freedom is not the power "to do anything you like"; it is "to be able to surpass the given toward an open future" (Part III, "Freedom and Liberation"). She also sets a limit: "We have to respect freedom only when it is intended for freedom" (Part III, "Freedom and Liberation").

### In The Second Sex

In the Conclusion of The Second Sex the same idea becomes social. She does not wait for the oppressors to change: "oppressors cannot be expected to make a move of gratuitous generosity", and "liberty can break the circle" of inferiority and exclusion (Conclusion, trans. Parshley).

**Plain words** If your freedom costs other people theirs, it is not freedom you can rely on, because it depends on someone staying down. De Beauvoir's view is that your choices only mean something when other free people can take them up, answer them or reject them. Acting for others' freedom is therefore part of using your own, and she is clear that the work is concrete and difficult.

*For example:* Imagined example: a manager who has been the only woman on her team argues for a transparent promotion process, even though the old system favoured her. The test is whether the people behind her get more room to choose.

(de Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947), Parts II-III, trans. Bernard Frechtman, full text at Marxists Internet Archive; The Second Sex (1949), Conclusion, trans. H. M. Parshley, Marxists Internet Archive; both opened 2026-10-21. The old cite "Part III" for the first quote is corrected to Part II.)

> **Principle 41** — Learn to work with identity transitions: grieving and rebuilding

McAdams and McLean summarise research on how people narrate difficult experiences. Their review associates detailed exploration with personal growth and positive resolution with happiness; it does not establish a required grieving sequence for every identity transition. ([McAdams & McLean 2013, open course mirror](https://www.studeersnel.nl/nl/document/hogeschool-van-amsterdam/psychologie-de-basis/mc-adams-dan-p-and-kate-c-mc-lean-2013-narrative-identity/44583338))

> "In the first step, the person explores the negative experience in depth, thinking long and hard about what the experience felt like, how it came to be, what it may lead to, and what role the negative event may play in the person's overall life story. In the second step, the person articulates and commits the self to a positive resolution of the event. Research suggests that the first step is associated with personal growth—the second, with happiness."

(McAdams & McLean, 2013, "Narrative Identity", Current Directions in Psychological Science, [open course mirror](https://www.studeersnel.nl/nl/document/hogeschool-van-amsterdam/psychologie-de-basis/mc-adams-dan-p-and-kate-c-mc-lean-2013-narrative-identity/44583338).)

The compendium takes this as a writing prompt for an identity transition, rather than a prescribed grief process.

**Plain words** Describe one part of a former role that you miss. In a separate paragraph, sketch something you might try in your present circumstances. Neither paragraph has to turn the loss into a benefit or bring the story to a tidy ending.

*For example:* Imagined: after retirement, a person writes that they miss daily contact with colleagues, then considers arranging a weekly lunch.

## Practice

- Optional writing exercise: describe what changed and what you miss, then identify one activity you would like to explore. Leave questions open if no resolution feels honest.

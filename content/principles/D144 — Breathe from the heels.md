> **Principle 144** — Breathe from the heels

The True Person breathes from the depths: a breath that reaches the heels, not a shallow chest-sip. The picture comes from the Zhuangzi, where breathing marks the difference between the true man and everyone else:

> "Their breathing came deep and silently. The breathing of the true man comes (even) from his heels, while men generally breathe (only) from their throats."
> (Zhuangzi 6, [Legge's translation](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The lines that follow apply the contrast to argument: when men are defeated in argument, "their words come from their gullets as if they were vomiting. Where lusts and desires are deep, the springs of the Heavenly are shallow." The chapter gives breath as part of its portrait of the true man: deep and silent, from the heels, where other men breathe from their throats. The page's modern application is a reflective pause rather than a physiological protocol: when you notice your own breath is hurried and high, pause for one slower exhale and notice what changes. (Zhuangzi 6.)

**Plain words** Legge's true man breathes deep and silent, from the heels; everyone else, from the throat. The image belongs to a portrait, and the chapter makes no physiology claims you could test. The borrowed use is a checkpoint: notice how high your breathing sits right now, and let the noticing be the reason to slow one exhale.

*For example:* Suppose you are about to give a difficult answer in a meeting. Take one slower exhale first. The exercise is a pause, and the pause is the point; the chapter does not promise a particular effect from it.

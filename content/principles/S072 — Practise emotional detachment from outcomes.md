> **Principle 194** — Practise emotional detachment from outcomes

Before visiting the baths, Epictetus tells his reader to anticipate splashing, jostling, abuse and theft. The purpose of the outing includes bathing and keeping one's conduct consistent with one's principles when something interferes ([Enchiridion 4, Carter translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)).

> I will now go bathe, and keep my own mind in a state conformable to nature.

Epictetus, Enchiridion 4, [Elizabeth Carter translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html).

The passage applies the preparation to every action. Epictetus asks what remains of your intention when the activity is blocked; he does not offer a promise that preparation will secure the result ([Enchiridion 4](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)).

(Ench. 4.)

### Plain words

Before an important task, decide how you want to behave if it goes badly. When a setback comes, that decision still gives you something to work on, as the interrupted bath does in Epictetus' example ([Enchiridion 4](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)).

### For example

As a modern application, prepare carefully for an interview and decide beforehand how you will respond to rejection. You can be disappointed and still choose a courteous reply, applying the passage's concern for conduct under disruption ([Enchiridion 4](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)).

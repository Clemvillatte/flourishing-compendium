> **Principle 52** — Treat collective efficacy as a community resource

Treat collective efficacy — a group's shared belief in its capacity to achieve goals — as a community resource. Collective efficacy predicts neighborhood safety, health, and well-being (Sampson et al., 1997). The adaptive stance is to build collective efficacy through successful collective action.

**Plain words** Collective efficacy — a group's shared belief that it can get things done — predicts safer, healthier neighbourhoods. Not optimism; track record plus trust. Communities build it the way individuals build confidence: small wins, stacked.

*For example:* The street that organised one clean-up day, then a crossing campaign, then a park revival — three years on, the whole street believes things can change, because the street keeps changing them. That's collective efficacy compounding.

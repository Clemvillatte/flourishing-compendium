> **Principle 19** — Recognise the paradox of hedonic pursuit: chasing positive affect can reduce it

Mauss and colleagues investigated whether valuing happiness can undermine happiness in particular circumstances. ([Mauss, Tamir, Anderson and Savino, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3160511/)) The compendium uses these findings as a prompt to examine the standards we impose on our feelings.

(Mauss, Tamir, Anderson and Savino, "Can seeking happiness make people unhappy? Paradoxical effects of valuing happiness," Emotion 11(4), 2011; Ford et al., 2015.)

> "We argue that this may not always be the case. Instead, valuing happiness could be self-defeating because the more people value happiness, the more likely they will feel disappointed. This should apply particularly in positive situations, in which people have every reason to be happy." (Mauss, Tamir, Anderson and Savino, [Can seeking happiness make people unhappy?](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3160511/), Emotion 11(4), 2011)

"In Study 1, female participants who valued happiness more (vs. less) reported lower happiness when under conditions of low, but not high, life stress." ([Mauss et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3160511/))

"In Study 2, compared to a control group, female participants who were experimentally induced to value happiness reacted less positively to a happy, but not a sad, emotion induction. This effect was mediated by participants' disappointment at their own feelings." ([Mauss et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3160511/))

The paper closes: "Paradoxically, therefore, valuing happiness may lead people to be less happy just when happiness is within reach." ([Mauss et al., 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3160511/))

**Plain words**
Watching yourself for happiness can crowd out the happiness. In the studies behind this page, the people most determined to feel happy were most disappointed in their feelings, and the disappointment did the damage. The everyday version: set up the conditions, the visit, the meal, the walk, and let the feeling arrive or not. You are hosting an event, not auditing it.

**Reflection exercise:** Compare "I must feel happy during this visit" with "I want to spend time with my friend." What does each make your standard of success?

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Valuing happiness paradox|Valuing happiness paradox]]**, people who place a high value on happiness report lower happiness and greater depressive symptoms (Mauss et al., 2011)
- **Indirect strategies outperform direct strategies**, pursuing meaning and connection increases positive affect more than pursuing positive affect directly (Ford et al., 2015)

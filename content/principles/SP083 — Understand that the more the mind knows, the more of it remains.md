> **Principle 83** — Understand that the more the mind knows, the more of it remains

The proposition in Part V reads: "The more things a mind understands by the second and third kinds of knowledge, the less it is acted on by bad affects and the less it fears death" ([Ethics V P38, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The proof gives the doctrine's core: "the more things a mind knows by the second and third kinds of knowledge, the greater the part of it that remains" when the body is destroyed ([Ethics V P38 proof, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The corollary adds that this remaining part, "however large or small it is, is more perfect than the rest" ([Ethics V P38 corollary, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)), and the note states the identity plainly: "our mind, insofar as it understands, is an eternal mode of thinking" ([Ethics V, note on 20-40, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The next proposition ties the doctrine to life in time: "Someone whose body is capable of a great many things has a mind whose greatest part is eternal" ([Ethics V P39, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)).

**Plain words**
Spinoza's eternity doctrine is quiet, not consoling: the mind, insofar as it understands, is an eternal mode of thinking, and the imagination is not. Everything else you have is a state of the body in time, and goes the way of bodies. Understanding is different in kind, not just in durability: it does not rise with good fortune or shrink with loss. The practical reading is modest: of the things you can build, one kind is not hostage to circumstance.

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: two people lose the same portfolio in the same crash. For one, the loss is total because the self was made of holdings; for the other it is severe but external, because the capacities that earned the holdings remain.

(Ethics V, P38-39, with the corollary and the note on 20-40.)

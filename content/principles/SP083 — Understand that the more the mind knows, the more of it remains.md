> **Principle 83** — Understand that the more the mind knows, the more of it remains

Spinoza's doctrine of eternity is quietly practical: the more the mind understands, the greater the part of it that is not destroyed with the body's fortunes; understanding is the one asset that cannot be confiscated by circumstance. This is the deep answer to loss: everything except understanding is on loan. Practically, when you must choose between building a possession and building an understanding, know which one the tradition calls permanent, and invest accordingly.

(Ethics V, P38-39, with scholia.)

**Plain words** The more the mind understands, the greater the part of it that is not destroyed with the body's fortunes — understanding is the one asset that cannot be confiscated. Quietly practical: build the part of you that the market, the illness, and the reorg can't touch.

*For example:* Two people lose everything in the same crash: one's self went with the portfolio (it was made of it), the other's self is made of understanding, relationships, and capacities — which is why one rebuilds in a year. The asset allocation was the difference.

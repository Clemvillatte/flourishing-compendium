> **Principle 78** — Recognise when affect requires professional help: clinical boundaries

The American Psychiatric Association describes depression as follows:

> "Depression (major depressive disorder) is a common and serious mental disorder that negatively affects how you feel, think, act, and perceive the world." ([APA, What Is Depression?](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/depression/what-is-depression))

On how the diagnosis is made:

> "To diagnose depression, a healthcare professional will conduct a thorough diagnostic evaluation that includes a comprehensive interview to discuss your symptoms in addition to your personal, medical and family histories." ([APA, same page](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/depression/what-is-depression))

The same page describes depression as among the most treatable of mental disorders and names seeing your family physician or psychiatrist as a first step if you are experiencing symptoms. The page concerns depression specifically; the compendium extends the boundary-reading to other persistent, impairing patterns as a matter of prudence, not as a diagnostic claim.

**Plain words** You do not have to decide whether you have depression before asking for help. The APA recommends seeing a family physician or psychiatrist if you are experiencing symptoms; diagnosis involves a professional evaluation.

*For example:* In an imagined case, your mood has been low and work has become difficult. Describe those changes when arranging an evaluation, rather than deciding the diagnosis yourself.

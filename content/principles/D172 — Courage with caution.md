> **Principle 172** — Courage with caution

In Legge's translation of chapter 73, refusing to dare to do wrong is itself called boldness. The sage's difficulty follows a question about knowing why Heaven strikes someone. Applying this passage to everyday choices is a modern reading, not a decision procedure supplied by the chapter.

(DDJ 73.)

> "He whose boldness appears in his daring (to do wrong, in defiance of the laws) is put to death; he whose boldness appears in his not daring (to do so) lives on." (DDJ 73, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

### Plain words

Being able to carry out a request does not make it right to do so. When the pressure to go along is strong, consider what refusing would protect. Then decide how to say no without pretending the choice is effortless.

### For example

If a colleague asks you to sign a report you know is false, draft a direct refusal and one that offers to correct it together. Compare which states your boundary clearly. Keep that wording for the next request: the practice concerns overt behaviour, not proving how fearless you are.

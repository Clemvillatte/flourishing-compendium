> **Principle 96** — Keep urgency without hurry

The Katha's summons, "Arise, awake; having reached the great, learn" (Katha 1.3.14, with the razor's-edge path image that follows), is the tradition's answer to procrastination at the level of life itself: the stakes are real, the time is finite, and practice delayed is the one reliable loss. The famous rendering "arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached" is Vivekananda's modern paraphrase of this verse, and is labeled as such here. Practically, convert urgency into scheduled practice rather than into pressure on yourself.

(Katha Upanishad 1.3.14.)

**Plain words** "Arise, awake; having reached the great, learn" — with the razor's-edge image — is the tradition's answer to procrastination at the level of life itself: the stakes are real, the time is finite, practice delayed is the one regret that compounds. Urgency without hurry.

*For example:* The person who's been "meaning to start" for a decade — reads the Katha's summons as fuel, not as panic: no sprinting, but no more pretending there's no clock either.

> **Principle 44** — Treat every person as another you

The Maya-identified formulation "in lak'ech a la kin" ("I am another you" / "you are another me") circulates widely in living Maya and diaspora communities as a greeting and an ethic. Its pre-Columbian pedigree cannot be established in the textual record, and this compendium labels it as a modern, living formulation, used on its merits: as a daily stance, it is a compact version of the exchange-of-self-and-other trainings. Practically, use the phrase internally at your hardest interpersonal moments: "this person is another me," and let the stance choose the next sentence.

(The modern Maya-identified formulation, labeled; see also the Popol Vuh's common-origin teaching below.)

**Plain words** In lak'ech — "I am another you" — a living Maya greeting and ethic: treat every person as another you. The book is honest that its ancient pedigree can't be established; it's used on its merits, and the merits are real: the whole golden rule in a compact daily stance.

*For example:* The driver who lets the merging car in — because that car is a person running late to something that matters to them, the way you are — and the whole commute runs on that arithmetic.

> **Principle 7** — Understand that behaviour change follows stages: precontemplation to maintenance

Behaviour change is a process, not an event. It moves through stages: precontemplation (not thinking about change), contemplation (thinking about it), preparation (preparing), action (doing), and maintenance (sustaining). Interventions must match the stage.

**Plain words** Change is a staircase, not a switch: not thinking about it, thinking about it, preparing, doing, keeping it going. Knowing which stair you're on changes what's useful — a precontemplator needs information, not a gym membership.

*For example:* Nagging a smoker to quit doesn't work because they're two stairs below the argument. The talk that helps at that stair sounds like "what would make you even consider it?" — not "here's your patch."
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Transtheoretical model (Prochaska & DiClemente)|Transtheoretical model (Prochaska & DiClemente)]]**, behaviour change moves through stages (Prochaska & DiClemente, 1983; Prochaska et al., 1992)
- **Stage-matched interventions are more effective**, interventions matched to stage produce better outcomes (Prochaska et al., 1992; Spencer et al., 2006)

- **[[Gradual training|The gradual training]]** (Buddhism, Gradual Training Sutta)
- **[[The journey of a thousand miles|The journey of a thousand miles]]** (Laozi, Daodejing 64)

## Practice

- Identify your stage for a behaviour change. If precontemplative, raise awareness. If contemplative, resolve ambivalence. If in preparation, make a plan. If in action, build skills and environment. If in maintenance, prevent relapse.

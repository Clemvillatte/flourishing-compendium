> **Principle 7** — Understand that behaviour change follows stages: precontemplation to maintenance

Prochaska and Velicer's transtheoretical model is the opened source. Its abstract:

> "The transtheoretical model posits that health behavior change involves progress through six stages of change: precontemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, maintenance, and termination." ([Prochaska & Velicer, 1997, American Journal of Health Promotion 12, full text](https://sdlab.fas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5291/files/sdlab/files/prochaska_1997_transtheoretical_model_of_health_behaviour_change.pdf))

Two of the stage definitions, in their words:

> "Precontemplation is the stage in which people are not intending to take action in the foreseeable future, usually measured as the next 6 months." ([Prochaska & Velicer, 1997, same text](https://sdlab.fas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5291/files/sdlab/files/prochaska_1997_transtheoretical_model_of_health_behaviour_change.pdf))

> "Maintenance is the stage in which people are working to prevent relapse but they do not apply change processes as frequently as do people in action." ([Prochaska & Velicer, 1997, same text](https://sdlab.fas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5291/files/sdlab/files/prochaska_1997_transtheoretical_model_of_health_behaviour_change.pdf))

And the distribution finding:

> "Basic research has generated a rule of thumb for at-risk populations: 40 % in precontemplation, 40 % in contemplation, and 20% in preparation." ([Prochaska & Velicer, 1997, same text](https://sdlab.fas.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5291/files/sdlab/files/prochaska_1997_transtheoretical_model_of_health_behaviour_change.pdf))

The model was developed for health behaviours in at-risk populations. The compendium's use of the staircase for any behaviour change is its own application.

**Plain words** Change is a staircase, not a switch: not intending, intending, preparing, doing, keeping it going. The model's own rule of thumb for at-risk groups: 40% not intending to act, 40% thinking about it, 20% preparing — most people are nowhere near action. That is why the talk that helps depends on the stair.

*For example:* (Imagined case.) Nagging a smoker to quit fails because they are two stairs below the argument. The talk that helps at that stair sounds like "what would make you even consider it?" — not "here's your patch."

## Practice

- Identify your stage for a behaviour change. If precontemplative, raise awareness. If contemplative, resolve ambivalence. If in preparation, make a plan. If in action, build skills and environment. If in maintenance, prevent relapse.

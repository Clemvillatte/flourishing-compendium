> **Principle 492** — Practise the ontological difference

In the second section of Being and Time, Heidegger states what the question asks about: "Being—that which determines entities as entities, that on the basis of which entities are already understood, however we may discuss them in detail. The Being of entities 'is' not itself an entity." He adds that the first philosophical step is "not 'telling a story'—that is to say, in not defining entities as entities by tracing them back in their origin to some other entities, as if Being had the character of some possible entity" (Heidegger, 1927/1962, Being and Time, §2, H. 6, trans. Macquarrie and Robinson, [full text](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf)).

The two sides stay tied together: "Being is always the Being of an entity" (§3, H. 9), so the distinction does not set Being in a realm apart from things.

The phrase "ontological difference" is not Being and Time's label for this. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes that Heidegger "later dubs this 'the ontological difference'", and uses it to separate philosophical inquiry from the ontic sciences, which study particular entities and their interactions ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Martin Heidegger," 2025, main text and note 5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/notes.html)).

**Plain words**
Knowing a lot about particular things (what a house is made of, what a person has done) is different from asking what it is for anything to be there, to matter and to show up for us. The second question is not one more fact about things. It asks how the facts become available to you at all.

**For example**
You can list everything about your career: salary, tasks, colleagues. That is a set of facts. Ask separately what it means for work to be a place where your life happens, and you have crossed the distinction.

> **Principle 492** — Practise the ontological difference

Recognise the difference between beings (entities, things that are) and Being (the fact that they are). The ontological difference, as Heidegger calls it, is the distinction between the ontic (what entities are) and the ontological (what it means to be at all). Do not confuse your understanding of particular things with an understanding of Being itself.

(Heidegger, 1927/2008, Being and Time, Introduction I; Heidegger, 1953, Introduction to Metaphysics.)

**Plain words** The ontological difference: beings (the things that are) versus Being (the fact that they are). The distinction between what entities are and what it means to be at all. You can inventory your life without ever touching the second question. Make the distinction; visit the second floor.

*For example:* Someone optimises everything that is — career, body, schedule, portfolio — and one quiet evening notices that the entire project never once asked what all this being-of-things is. The inventory was complete; the question was unopened.

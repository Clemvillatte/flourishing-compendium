> **Principle 75** — Let the object fill the field

Sutra 3.3 defines the last of the three inner stages, concentration (samadhi), by what happens to the object:

> This same [contemplation], shining forth [in consciousness] as the intended object and nothing more, and, as it were, emptied of itself, is concentration.

(Yoga Sutras 3.3, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, pp. 204-205, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary reads it this way: the contemplation "shines forth [in consciousness] in the form of the object-to-be-contemplated and not in the form of the contemplation." It is "empty of itself" in that it becomes "identical with the presented-idea as such". The Tika explains the phrase "as it were" and quotes a Purana: the knowing of the object "when free from two-termed-relations" is concentration, where a two-termed relation is "a distinction between the contemplation and the object-to-be-contemplated. Concentration is free from this."

So what empties out is the contemplation, the act of attending, and the distinction between it and its object. The text says nothing here about the observer's self-sense thinning or the state feeling like disappearance. That description is this page's own gloss. The advice to treat each absorption as a passing state, not an arrival, is also this page's own. The sutras do not say that sentence, although the next sutras go on to a combined practice (3.4) and the text elsewhere treats certain attainments as obstacles.

**Plain words**

In the deepest stage of attention, the sutra says only the object is left in view, and the act of attending seems to vanish into it. The text does not say what that is like for the person. If you recognise a taste of it, treat it as a state that comes and goes.

**For example**

A cellist plays a slow passage and afterwards cannot say what she was thinking. She did not feel absent. She remembers only the sound, and takes it as a good rehearsal.

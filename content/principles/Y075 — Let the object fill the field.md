> **Principle 75** — Let the object fill the field

Absorption (samadhi) is defined by what happens to the object: it shines alone, as if emptied of the observer's own form. The tradition's point is that the observer's self-sense thins as attention fills, which is why the state feels like disappearance and is sought for the wrong reasons. Practically, treat each absorption as a state that passes, not as an arrival; the tradition measures progress by stability, not by states.

(YS 3.3.)

**Plain words** Absorption is defined by what happens to the object: it shines alone, as if emptied of the observer's own form. The observer's self-sense thins as attention fills — which is why the state feels like disappearance, and why it's so often sought for the wrong reasons.

*For example:* The musician who plays the passage and later can't recount "themselves" during it — only the music. The self-sense didn't die; it thinned while attention filled. That's the shape of it.

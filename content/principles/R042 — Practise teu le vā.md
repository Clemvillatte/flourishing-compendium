> **Principle 304** — Practise teu le vā

Wendt's formulation is the standard citation, and it is worth quoting at length because the practice depends on what the space is:

> "Important to the Samoan view of reality is the concept of Va or Wa in Maori and Japanese. Va is the space between, the betweenness, not empty space, not space that separates but space that relates, that holds separate entities and things together in the Unity-that-is-All, the space that is context, giving meaning to things. The meanings change as the relationships/the contexts change … A well-known Samoan expression is 'Ia teu le va'. Cherish/nurse/care for the va, the relationships. This is crucial in communal cultures that value group, unity, more than individualism: who perceive the individual person/creature/thing in terms of group, in terms of va, relationships." (Wendt, 1996, pp. 18-19, as quoted in [ANU Press, 2026](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n14864/html/ch07.xhtml))

So teu le vā is not a courtesy campaign; it is upkeep on the thing that makes things meaningful. Tui Atua's account of Samoan peace, a related religious context rather than a definition of teu le vā itself, gives the same structure: "There are four key harmonies that hold the balance of peace for Samoans. These are: harmony with the cosmos; harmony with the environment; harmony with one's fellow men; and harmony with one's self. When all four harmonies come together there is peace." ([Tui Atua, 2005](https://www.library.auckland.ac.nz/external/ebooks/TupuolaEfiTuiatuaColloquiumpaperDec05.pdf)) The harmonies are kept by recognised boundaries and sacred covenants, and the paper is explicit about what breaks them: "Disharmonies arise in the collective when conflict assumes. Conflict assumes when the tuaoi or boundaries within are transgressed or misunderstood" (Tui Atua, 2005).

(Wendt, 1996; Tui Atua, 2005.)

**Plain words** The vā is not an empty gap between you and someone else; it is the relation itself, and Wendt's whole point is that it is not empty. Tending it is closer to keeping a garden than to being nice: specific, regular, unglamorous work. And the garden is not private; family, village, environment and cosmos are all planted in it.

*For example:* The colleague who always leaves the shared kitchen better than they found it, remembers birthdays, sends the thank-you text — not sainthood, just someone who gardens the between.

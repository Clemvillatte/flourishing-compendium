> **Principle 69** — Recognise behavioural symptoms of depression: withdrawal and inactivity

Depression produces specific behavioural symptoms: withdrawal, inactivity, avoidance, anhedonia. These are symptoms, not choices. They are treatable with [[Behavioral activation|behavioural activation]].

**Plain words** Depression has behavioural fingerprints: withdrawal, inactivity, the cancelled plans, the curtains. These aren't causes of shame — they're symptoms, as physical as a fever. And symptoms respond to behavioural treatment: gentle re-entry, scheduled activity.

*For example:* The friend who cancels everything for two months isn't lazy — that's the disease's signature. The invitation that keeps arriving anyway, no pressure attached, is sometimes the lifeline.
## Psychology parallels

- **Depression and behavioural symptoms**, withdrawal, inactivity, and avoidance are core symptoms (APA, 2022; Martell et al., 2001)
- **BA treats behavioural symptoms**, activity scheduling is effective for depression (Jacobson et al., 1996; Dimidjian et al., 2011)

## Practice

- When depressed, recognise behavioural symptoms (withdrawal, inactivity) as symptoms, not choices. Treat with behavioural activation: schedule small activities, do not obey the withdrawal urge. Seek treatment if severe.

> **Principle 11** — Prevent suffering before it arrives

One of the most actionable lines in the corpus: "the suffering that has not yet come is to be avoided." The tradition's point is temporal: most people treat suffering only when it is upon them, while the entire training is designed to intercept the chain earlier each time. Practically, after any storm, trace it back to its earliest link, and put your practice there.

(YS 2.16.)

**Plain words** One of the most actionable lines in the corpus: "the suffering that has not yet come is to be avoided." Most people treat suffering only when it's upon them; the entire training is designed to intercept the chain earlier each time. Move your intervention point upstream.

*For example:* Someone who waits for the burnout to arrive before resting — versus the one who reads the first two warning signs (sleep, snark) and takes the rest week before the collapse. Same life, different interception point.
## Psychology parallels

- **Prevention science and early intervention**, the finding that acting on upstream causes outperforms late treatment (relapse prevention: Marlatt & Gordon, 1985)

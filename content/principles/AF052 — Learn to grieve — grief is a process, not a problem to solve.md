> **Principle 52** — Learn to grieve: grief is a process, not a problem to solve

Grief, on the dual process model of coping with bereavement, moves between two orientations rather than descending through fixed stages. Loss-orientation is the concentration on, and dealing with, processing of some aspect of the loss experience itself, most particularly with respect to the deceased person. Restoration-orientation deals with the substantial changes that are secondary consequences of the loss. The coping process that joins the two is oscillation, and the model treats dosage of grieving, meaning respite from either stressor, as an integral part of adaptive coping rather than a lapse in it ([Stroebe & Schut, 1999](https://scispace.com/papers/the-dual-process-model-of-coping-with-bereavement-2k5n0x0z5f)).

> "This refers to the alternation between loss- and restoration-oriented coping, the process of juxtaposition of confrontation and avoidance of different stressors associated with bereavement." (Stroebe, M., & Schut, H. (1999). The dual process model of coping with bereavement: Rationale and description. Death Studies, 23(3), 197-224, p. 215, [full text](https://scispace.com/papers/the-dual-process-model-of-coping-with-bereavement-2k5n0z0z5f))

**Plain words** Grief does not ask you to face the loss every minute. You will swing between dealing with the death itself and dealing with everything the death changed, and some of the time you will need to think about neither. That swing is the process working, not you failing it. A grief that cannot move in either direction is the signal to get help.

*For example (imagined):* A widow spends a morning sorting her husband's papers, an afternoon learning the online billing, and an evening at a friend's comedy night. On this model the third hour is not a break from grieving; the respite is one of its parts.

## Practice

- Allow grief to move through its natural oscillation. If grief is persistent, intensely impairing, and stuck (no movement, no oscillation), this is complicated grief and warrants professional help.

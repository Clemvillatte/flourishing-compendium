> **Principle 52** — Learn to grieve: grief is a process, not a problem to solve

Stroebe and Schut's dual process model distinguishes attending to the loss itself from adapting to changes following it. Loss-oriented coping concerns the deceased person and the experience of loss; restoration-oriented coping concerns its secondary consequences. The model describes movement between these demands and includes periods of respite as part of adaptive coping ([Stroebe & Schut, 1999](https://scispace.com/pdf/the-dual-process-model-of-coping-with-bereavement-rationale-3wmilz408m.pdf)).

> "This refers to the alternation between loss- and restoration-oriented coping, the process of juxtaposition of confrontation and avoidance of different stressors associated with bereavement." (Stroebe, M., & Schut, H. (1999). The dual process model of coping with bereavement: Rationale and description. Death Studies, 23(3), 197-224, p. 215, [full text PDF](https://scispace.com/pdf/the-dual-process-model-of-coping-with-bereavement-rationale-3wmilz408m.pdf))

**Plain words** After a death, you may be remembering the person at one moment and dealing with a changed part of daily life at another. This model includes pauses from those demands. It does not ask you to attend to the loss continuously.

*For example (imagined):* After sorting her husband's papers, a widow accepts a friend's invitation to watch a comedy.

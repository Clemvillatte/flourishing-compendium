> **Principle 75** — Learn emotional validation: acknowledging a feeling reduces its intensity

Validation — acknowledging that someone's feeling makes sense given their perspective — reliably reduces the intensity of the feeling and the escalation of conflict. Invalidation ("you shouldn't feel that way," "it's not a big deal") amplifies it.

**Plain words** Validation — acknowledging that someone's feeling makes sense given their perspective — reliably reduces the feeling's intensity and the conflict's escalation. Invalidation ("you shouldn't feel that way") reliably does the opposite. Validate first; solve second.

*For example:* The child melting down over the wrong colour cup — and the parent's two options: "that's silly, it's just a cup" (invalidation: escalation) or "you really wanted the blue one" (validation: de-escalation, usually within ninety seconds). The technique works on adults, boards, and nations.
## Psychology parallels

- **Validation reduces negative affect and escalation**, in couples and families, validation de-escalates conflict; invalidation escalates it (Linehan, 1993; Fruzzetti & Iverson, 2004)
- **Validation is a core mechanism in DBT and couples therapy** (Linehan, 1993; Gottman & Levenson, 2000)

## Practice

- When someone is emotionally activated, before problem-solving, validate: "that makes sense, given what you're experiencing." Then, and only then, offer perspective or solution.

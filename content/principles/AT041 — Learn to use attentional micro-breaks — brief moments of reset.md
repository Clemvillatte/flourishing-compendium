> **Principle 41** — Learn to use attentional micro-breaks: brief moments of reset

Albulescu and colleagues define micro-breaks as "short discontinuities in one's tasks of no longer than 10 minutes". Their meta-analysis of 22 study samples (N = 2,335) found "statistically significant but small effects of micro-breaks in boosting vigor (d = .36 ...), reducing fatigue (d = .35 ...), and a non-significant effect on increasing overall performance (d = .16 ...)". Performance gains showed up only for tasks with lower cognitive demand, and "recovering from highly depleting tasks may need more than 10-minute breaks" (Albulescu et al. 2022, [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0272460)).

The attention claim is weaker than the title suggests. Ariga and Lleras reported that sporadically switching tasks during a long vigilance task averted the usual performance decline (Ariga & Lleras 2011, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21211793/)). A later study with 498 participants found that "all experimental groups had an equivalent robust vigilance decrement" and that the Bayesian analyses favoured the null result (Helton & Russell 2012, [Experimental Brain Research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00221-012-3065-0)).

**Plain words**
A pause of up to ten minutes tends to leave you slightly more energetic and slightly less tired. Whether it also makes the work itself better is unclear, and the attention-reset result did not hold up in a larger study. Take the break for how you feel, and for a hard, draining task expect to need more than a few seconds.

*For example:* After fifty minutes of drafting, stand up, look out of a window for two minutes and drink some water before you sit back down. This is an imagined routine within the up-to-ten-minute range the meta-analysis covers, and the evidence supports a modest lift in energy rather than a sharper mind.

## Psychology parallels

- **Micro-breaks and well-being**, short breaks raise vigor and lower fatigue, with no clear effect on overall performance (Albulescu et al., 2022)
- **Task switches and vigilance**, one lab study found switches prevented the vigilance decline (Ariga & Lleras, 2011) and a larger study did not (Helton & Russell, 2012)

## Practice

- Build short breaks into work, up to ten minutes each: look away from the screen, stand, stretch, breathe or look out of a window. Judge them by how you feel afterwards.

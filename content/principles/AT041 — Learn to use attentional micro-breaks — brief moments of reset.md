> **Principle 41** — Learn to use attentional micro-breaks: brief moments of reset

Micro-breaks — brief (10 seconds to 5 minutes) pauses during work — can restore attention and reduce fatigue. They work by briefly disengaging from the task, allowing a "reset" of attentional capacity.

**Plain words** Attention doesn't need a week off — it needs ten-second resets sprinkled through the day. Look out a window, stretch, one slow breath. Micro-breaks are the snack-sized maintenance that prevents the 4 p.m. collapse.

*For example:* The 60-second rule: every hour, one minute of anything that isn't a screen. That's it. The afternoon stops being a cliff.
## Psychology parallels

- **Micro-breaks improve sustained attention**, brief pauses between task blocks restore performance (Ariga & Lleras, 2011; Leroy, 2009)
- **Brief disengagement improves subsequent focus**, even 10-15 second breaks can restore attentional capacity (Ariga & Lleras, 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The rhythm of small pauses|The rhythm of small pauses]]** (Zen Buddhism, kinhin walking meditation)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as brief non-doing]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 16)

## Practice

- Build micro-breaks into work: every 25-30 minutes, pause for 10-30 seconds. Look away from screen, stretch, breathe, or look out window. Do not need long breaks; brief resets work.

> **Principle 28** — Practice sending and taking on the breath

Tonglen, the central lojong practice: on the in-breath, take in what is painful (yours or another's), on the out-breath, send out what is helpful. The instruction is mounted on the breath deliberately, because the breath is always present and always rhythmic, which makes the practice portable into real situations. The counter-intuitive direction (inhale the bad, exhale the good) is the training: it reverses the reflex of pushing away pain and hoarding the good. Practically, in any moment of others' suffering you witness, run three breaths of it.

(Seven-Point Mind Training, Point Two: sending and taking should be practiced alternately, mounted on the breath.)

**Plain words** Tonglen means breathing in what hurts, yours or someone else's, and breathing out what helps. It's tied to the breath because the breath is always there and always has a rhythm, so you can do it in real situations. The direction is the training: it reverses the reflex to flee pain and cling to comfort.

*For example:* Sitting with a friend's grief in the hospital corridor and quietly breathing in her fear, breathing out something steady.

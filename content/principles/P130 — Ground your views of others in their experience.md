> **Principle 130** — Ground your views of others in their experience

Addams asks settlement residents to combine careful fact-gathering with sympathy and a willingness to revise their views. She describes relationships with neighbours as something developed through living alongside them, rather than knowledge that residents bring ready-made (Twenty Years at Hull-House, 1910, [Oxford excerpt](https://learninglink.oup.com/access/content/schaller-3e-dashboard-resources/document-excerpt-from-jane-addams-twenty-years-at-hull-house-1910)).

> "They must be content to live quietly side by side with their neighbors, until they grow into a sense of relationship and mutual interests." (Addams, Twenty Years at Hull-House, [Oxford excerpt](https://learninglink.oup.com/access/content/schaller-3e-dashboard-resources/document-excerpt-from-jane-addams-twenty-years-at-hull-house-1910))

Her account also requires residents to understand the neighbourhood's needs and provide information for legislation. Listening belongs to a continuing civic responsibility, rather than ending with an outsider's impression of a visit (Twenty Years at Hull-House, [Oxford excerpt](https://learninglink.oup.com/access/content/schaller-3e-dashboard-resources/document-excerpt-from-jane-addams-twenty-years-at-hull-house-1910)).

### Plain words

Take one belief you hold about a group and ask what you actually know. Which experiences have you heard directly, and whose are missing? The compendium's practical suggestion is to make your judgment answerable to those experiences, while avoiding treating one person as a spokesperson for everyone.

### For example

Imagine a volunteer assuming that missed appointments show a lack of commitment. Before repeating that judgment, ask people what makes attendance difficult and compare their accounts with how appointments are arranged. Their answers may require changing the arrangement, the judgment, or both; decide after investigating.

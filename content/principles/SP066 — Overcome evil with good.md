> **Principle 66** — Overcome evil with good

Spinoza's summary of the free person's social stance, from the appendix to Part IV: the free person overcomes evil with good, and does not repay hatred with hatred. The instruction is mechanical, not ornamental: repaying evil with evil extends the causal chain of weakness; repaying it with good inserts a new cause into a system that was running on the old one. Practically, in your hardest relationship, practice one deliberate non-retaliation per week and record what the system does; you are not being saintly, you are debugging.

(Ethics IV, Appendix; III, P43; the note to IV, P73: hatred overcome with love.)

**Plain words** The free person overcomes evil with good and does not repay hatred with hatred. The instruction is mechanical, not ornamental: repaying evil with evil extends the causal chain; answering with good ends it where you stand. Overcome evil with good — it's the physics of the feud.

*For example:* The competitor who spreads the rumour — and the response that kills it: the competitor's clients keep getting excellent service, publicly. Not saintliness; chain-termination. The feud needs two participants, and one of them just left.

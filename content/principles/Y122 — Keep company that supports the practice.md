> **Principle 122** — Keep company that supports the practice

Chapter 13's list of what counts as knowledge includes this line about company:

> ... service of the Guru ... an inclination for solitary places and an aversion for mundane society ...

(BG 13.8-12, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/13/verse/8/).)

The commentary explains the first: "Spiritual knowledge is received from the Guru", and serving the Guru builds the "humbleness and commitment that enables the Guru to impart knowledge". It points back to the instruction to approach a teacher and inquire with reverence (BG 4.34). On the second, it describes the "sign of a materialistic mind" as finding pleasure "in talks about worldly people and worldly affairs", and says a person cultivating divine consciousness "develops a natural distaste for these activities".

So the verses support seeking the wise and avoiding worldly talk. Neither the translation nor the commentary mentions fault-finding company, and the text does not speak of a closest five. Auditing your five most-contacted people is this page's own tool. The commentary does allow for necessary company: a devotee takes part in society for service while staying unaffected.

**Plain words**

The people you spend the most time with shape what you pay attention to. The Gita points in two directions: spend time with people who know something, and spend less on gossip. Make a list of your five most-contacted people and ask whether each one pulls you toward your practice or away. Then adjust how much time each gets.

**For example**

Someone sees that the group chat has become a two-hour nightly complaint session. They move a third of that time to the friend who is building something, and nothing dramatic happens.

> **Principle 243** — Practise voluntary discomfort

The Stoic occasionally chooses discomfort: sleeping on a hard bed, eating plain food, dressing simply, going without. Not as asceticism but as training: to rehearse the loss of preferred indifferents and to discover that you can survive without them.

(Seneca, Letters 18, 67; Med. III.1, III.5, XI.3; Disc. I.25, III.12, III.25; Ench. 15, 29; Musonius Rufus, Lectures.)

**Plain words** Now and then, choose the hard bed on purpose. Eat plainly, dress simply, go without something you like — not to punish yourself, but to rehearse losing things you'd rather keep, and find out you're still intact. The Stoics called it training; you could call it a fire drill for misfortune.

*For example:* Someone who takes the stairs, skips the takeaway coffee, and sleeps without the phone for a week — and discovers that the "essentials" were mostly habits wearing the costume of needs.

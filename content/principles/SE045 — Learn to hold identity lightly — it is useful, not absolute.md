> **Principle 45** — Learn to hold identity lightly: it is useful, not absolute

The earliest version of this test is the Buddha's, in the Anattalakkhana Sutta: whatever constitutes a person (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) is inconstant, and the question is whether it is fitting to claim what is inconstant as "This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am" (SN 22.59). Hume reached the same verdict by another route: the mind is "nothing but a bundle or collection of different perceptions", and the identity we ascribe to it "is only a fictitious one" (Treatise 1.4.6). The identity still earns its keep: it organises experience, guides action and lets other people relate to you. It is a working summary that changes, not a fact you were born with.

> "Any form whatsoever that is past, future, or present; internal or external; blatant or subtle; common or sublime; far or near: every form is to be seen as it actually is with right discernment as: 'This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am.'" (SN 22.59, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu)

**Plain words** Your identity is a tool, like a map: useful, and not true the way a rock is true. "I'm a lawyer," "I'm a mother," "I'm bad with numbers" — these organise your life until they run it. Hold them the way you hold a job title: firm grip, loose wrist. When the role stops serving the person holding it, the grip is the thing to adjust.

*For example:* The woman whose whole identity was "the responsible one" in her family — the one everyone calls — discovers at fifty that the role was assigned when she was nine. The role served the family. The question is whether it still serves her.
## Psychology parallels

- **Identity flexibility and well-being**, the capacity to hold identity lightly (commit without rigidity) is associated with better outcomes (Hayes et al., 2011; Kabat-Zinn, 2013)
- **[[Self-as-context|Self-as-context (ACT)]]**, holding identity as content rather than as the self enables flexibility (Hayes et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Anatta|Anatta (non-self)]]** (Buddhism, Anatta-lakkhana Sutta)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei: non-attachment to self]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 13)
- **The self as useful fiction** (Hume, Treatise)

## Practice

- Practise holding identity lightly: "I am a [role/belief/identity], and I am also more than that." Use identity; do not be used by it. Do not defend it as if it were your life.

> **Principle 68** — Find the gap between stimulus and response

The dependent-arising sequence distinguishes contact, feeling and craving. SN 12.2 describes each as conditioned, placing them within a larger account of the arising and cessation of suffering (trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [text](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.002.than.html)).

> "From contact as a requisite condition comes feeling. From feeling as a requisite condition comes craving." (SN 12.2, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [text](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.002.than.html))

This is not a measurement of a half-second window for conscious control. The discourse also describes cessation through other conditions in the sequence, so it does not identify this link as the only possible place for practice (SN 12.2, [text](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.002.than.html)).

### Plain words

The compendium suggests examining an urge before treating it as an instruction. What happened, how did it feel, and what do you now want to do? These questions offer a way to investigate a reaction; they do not promise that every impulse comes with a detectable pause or that noticing always prevents action.

### For example

Imagine hearing a phone notification while reading. If you notice an urge to check it, ask whether opening it serves your current purpose. You might wait, respond, or change the notification settings later. The example offers choices to consider without claiming that every reaching movement is craving.

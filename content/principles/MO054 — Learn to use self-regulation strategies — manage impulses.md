> **Principle 54** — Learn to use self-regulation strategies: manage impulses

Self-regulation — the capacity to control impulses and direct behaviour toward goals — is a trainable skill. Strategies include: removing temptation, reappraisal, implementation intentions, and self-monitoring.

**Plain words** Self-regulation is trainable — the capacity to notice an impulse and choose. Not suppression; noticing. The gap between urge and action is where the whole skill lives, and the gap widens with practice. Every resisted cookie is a rep.

*For example:* The urge to snap in the meeting, noticed, held for three seconds, and answered calmly — that three-second gap was the entire skill, and it grew the way muscles do.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-regulation|Self-regulation strategies (Baumeister & Vohs)]]**, strategies include [[Situation selection|situation selection]], attentional deployment, cognitive change, response modulation (Baumeister & Vohs, 2004; Gross, 2015)
- **[[Situation selection|Situation selection]] may be especially effective**, removing temptation can be more efficient than resisting it (Duckworth et al., 2016; Milkman et al., 2011)
- **Note: ego depletion as depletable resource failed replication**, but self-regulation strategies remain effective (Hagger et al., 2016)

## Practice

- Use self-regulation strategies: (1) remove temptation ([[Situation selection|situation selection]]), (2) redirect attention, (3) reframe, (4) use if-then plans. Do not rely on willpower; use strategies.

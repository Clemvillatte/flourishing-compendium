> **Principle 26** — Recognise common humanity: your suffering is not unique or isolating

Neff's scale paper defines self-compassion with this component built in:

> Self-compassion, therefore, entails three basic components: 1) extending kindness and understanding to oneself rather than harsh self-criticism and judgment; 2) seeing one's experiences as part of the larger human experience rather than as separating and isolating; and 3) holding one's painful thoughts and feelings in balanced awareness rather than over-identifying with them.

(Neff, 2003, "The Development and Validation of a Scale to Measure Self-Compassion", Self and Identity 2(3), p. 224; [full text](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/empirical.article.pdf))

The same page frames the component as acknowledging that suffering, failure and inadequacies are part of the human condition (p. 224). The principle statement carries the second component on its own: the suffering is not a private defect but shared material.

**Plain words**
The component works by changing who is in the room. Suffering narrated as a private defect has an audience of one; narrated as part of the human condition, the audience is everyone who has failed at something. The move is not comparing miseries; it is reclassifying the experience from unique to shared.

*For example:* Imagine the new mother convinced she is the only one failing at it, and the mothers' group where every "am I the only one who...?" is answered by twelve hands.

## Psychology parallels

- **Common humanity as component of [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]]**, recognising that suffering is shared reduces isolation and shame (Neff, 2003, 2011)
- **Common humanity reduces shame**, shame thrives on the belief that one is uniquely flawed; recognising shared humanity reduces shame (Neff, 2011; Gilbert, 2009)

## Practice

When you suffer or fail, remind yourself: this is part of being human. Others have felt exactly this. I am not alone. The scale's own items model the phrasing: "I try to see my failings as part of the human condition."

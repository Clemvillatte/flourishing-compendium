> **Principle 26** — Recognise common humanity: your suffering is not unique or isolating

A core component of [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]] is recognising that suffering and imperfection are part of being human, not a sign of personal failure or isolation. Everyone struggles; everyone is imperfect. This recognition reduces isolation and shame.

**Plain words** Common humanity: suffering and imperfection are part of being human, not a sign of personal failure or isolation. Everyone struggles; everyone is imperfect. The suffering feels private; it's actually the most widely shared thing about you.

*For example:* The new mother convinced she's the only one failing at it — and the mothers' group where every "am I the only one who...?" is answered by twelve hands. The isolation was the suffering's costume; the commonality was the fact underneath.
## Psychology parallels

- **Common humanity as component of [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]]**, recognising that suffering is shared reduces isolation and shame (Neff, 2003, 2011)
- **Common humanity reduces shame**, shame thrives on the belief that one is uniquely flawed; recognising shared humanity reduces shame (Neff, 2011; Gilbert, 2009)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Ubuntu|Ubuntu: I am because we are]]** (African Philosophy, Desmond Tutu)
- **[[The human condition|The human condition]]** (Hannah Arendt, The Human Condition)
- **Nothing human is alien to me** (Terence, Heauton Timorumenos)

## Practice

- When you suffer or fail, remind yourself: "this is part of being human. Others have felt exactly this. I am not alone." This is not minimising; it is contextualising.

> **Principle 66** — Practise patience with the timeline

Bolier and colleagues' meta-analysis of 39 randomised trials found standardised effects of 0.34 for subjective well-being, 0.20 for psychological well-being and 0.23 for depression, "indicating small effects"; at three to six months the effects remained small but significant, though the authors urge caution because of few studies and high attrition ([Bolier et al. 2013, BMC Public Health](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2458-13-119)). A later review summarises Bolier's further findings: most studies were of low or medium quality, so effects could be inflated, and longer interventions showed larger effects ([review in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9638203/)). A reanalysis put the true effect near r = .10 ([PLOS ONE reanalysis](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0216588)). Small effects that grow with duration support patience. The seed's claim that gains "compound" is not shown by these sources.

On timelines, Lally and colleagues followed 96 volunteers who repeated a daily behaviour for 12 weeks. Reaching 95% of each person's plateau of automaticity took from 18 to 254 days, and "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process" ([Lally et al. 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsp.674)). The 66-day figure is a median for a subset, not a rule ([Surrey interview with Lally](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally)).

(Sheldon et al., 2013; Lyubomirsky, 2008.)

### Scope

I did not open Sheldon et al. 2013 or Lyubomirsky 2008, which the seed cites. The studies above cover positive psychology interventions and daily habits, not every well-being practice.

**Plain words**
Well-being practices tend to give small gains, and building a daily habit takes anywhere from weeks to many months. A missed day is not a reset.

**For example**
Choose one practice and one daily cue, such as after breakfast. Keep a tally for 12 weeks and do not restart the count after a missed day.

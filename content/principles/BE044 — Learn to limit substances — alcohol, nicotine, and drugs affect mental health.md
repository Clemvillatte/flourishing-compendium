> **Principle 44** — Learn to limit substances: alcohol, nicotine, and drugs affect mental health

Substance use (alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, other drugs) affects mental health. While they may provide short-term relief, they disrupt sleep, worsen anxiety and depression, and impair cognitive function.

**Plain words** Substances are mental health inputs — alcohol worsens sleep and anxiety, nicotine and cannabis have their own costs. Not a moral lecture; a chemistry lecture. The "relaxing" drink is often the anxiety's sponsor, billed the next morning.

*For example:* The "wine helps me unwind" pattern — unwind at 8 p.m., anxious at 3 a.m., exhausted at 7 a.m. The loan shark of relaxants, always collecting with interest.
## Psychology parallels

- **Alcohol and mental health**, alcohol disrupts sleep, worsens anxiety and depression (Koob & Le Moal, 2008; Boden & Fergusson, 2011)
- **Nicotine and anxiety**, smoking is associated with increased anxiety and worse mental health (Ziedonis et al., 2008)
- **Cannabis and mental health**, heavy use is associated with psychosis risk and worse outcomes (Volkow et al., 2016; Moore et al., 2007)

## Practice

- Limit substances: if you use alcohol, nicotine, or other substances, monitor their effect on your mental health. Reduce or eliminate if they worsen mood, sleep, or anxiety. Seek help if unable to reduce.

> **Principle 44** — Learn to limit substances: alcohol, nicotine, and drugs affect mental health

Reviews of alcohol, nicotine and cannabis give different strengths of evidence.

Alcohol: Boden and Fergusson's review found that "the presence of either disorder doubled the risks of the second disorder, with pooled AORs ranging from 2.00 to 2.09" for alcohol use disorders and major depression. They conclude that the literature suggests "a causal linkage between alcohol use disorders and major depression, such that increasing involvement with alcohol increases risk of depression" (Boden & Fergusson 2011, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21382111/)). This concerns disorders, and not social drinking.

Nicotine: a meta-analysis of 26 longitudinal studies found that "smoking cessation is associated with reduced depression, anxiety, and stress and improved positive mood and quality of life compared with continuing to smoke" (Taylor et al. 2014, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24524926/)). A later analysis of trial data found that group membership bias and confounding may partly, but not completely, explain this ([Taylor et al. 2015, BMJ Open](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26490099/)).

Cannabis: a systematic review of 35 longitudinal studies found a pooled adjusted odds ratio of 1.41 for any psychotic outcome in ever-users, rising to 2.09 in the most frequent users. For depression, suicidal thoughts and anxiety the findings "were less consistent" and "a substantial confounding effect was present" (Moore et al. 2007, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17662880/)).

None of the sources cited here addresses sleep, so this page makes no claim about it.

**Plain words**
Alcohol, nicotine and cannabis each have measurable links to mental health, and the strength differs. The firmest signals here are alcohol use disorders going with depression, smoking cessation going with lower anxiety and depression, and heavy cannabis use going with higher psychosis risk. A drink that feels like relief is not evidence that it helps your mood. Watch how you actually feel across the following days.

*For example:* For two weeks you note your mood and how rested you feel each morning, alongside whether you drank the night before. This is an imagined self-experiment. It is a way of testing the claim on yourself and not a diagnostic tool, and if you cannot cut back, speak to a health professional.

## Psychology parallels

- **Alcohol and depression**, alcohol use disorders and major depression doubled each other's risk, with evidence that alcohol increases depression risk (Boden & Fergusson, 2011)
- **Smoking cessation and mental health**, quitters showed lower anxiety, depression and stress than continuing smokers (Taylor et al., 2014), though confounding may explain part of this (Taylor et al., 2015)
- **Cannabis and psychosis**, ever-use went with a pooled adjusted odds ratio of 1.41 for psychotic outcomes, rising with frequency (Moore et al., 2007)

## Practice

- Limit substances: if you use alcohol, nicotine, or other substances, monitor their effect on your mental health. Reduce or eliminate if they worsen mood, sleep, or anxiety. Seek help if unable to reduce.

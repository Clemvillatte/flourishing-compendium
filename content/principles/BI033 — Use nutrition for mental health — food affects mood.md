> **Principle 33** — Use nutrition for mental health: food affects mood

The SMILES trial ([Jacka et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/)) tested the claim directly. Adults with moderate to severe depression were assigned either to seven sessions with a clinical dietitian supporting a modified Mediterranean diet, or to social support on the same visit schedule ([Jacka et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/)). At twelve weeks the diet group had improved more on the Montgomery-Åsberg depression rating scale (Cohen's d = -1.16), and 32.3% met remission criteria against 8.0% of the control group ([Jacka et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/)). The authors emphasise the study's preliminary nature and the need for replication ([Jacka et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/)).

The wider evidence agrees in direction but is smaller: across sixteen randomised trials with 45,826 participants, dietary interventions reduced depressive symptoms by a small amount (Hedges' g = 0.162), with no detectable effect on anxiety ([Firth et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30720698/)). An observational meta-analysis of 21 studies finds the same split by pattern: diets high in vegetables, fish and olive oil go with less depression, western-style diets high in processed meat and sweets with more ([Li et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28431261/)).

> "this is the first RCT to explicitly seek to answer the question: If I improve my diet, will my mental health improve?" (Jacka et al. 2017, [BMC Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/))

**Plain words**
SMILES tested a whole dietary pattern, not a single food: its design cannot identify which ingredients explain the improvement ([Jacka et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/)). The observational studies compare existing eating patterns, and they do not establish cause ([Li et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28431261/)).

*For example:* An imagined case: someone receiving care for depression asks their clinician whether dietitian support could be added to their current care.

## Psychology parallels

- **Diet and mental health (SMILES trial)**, Mediterranean diet improves depressive symptoms (Jacka et al., 2017; Francis et al., 2019)
- **Gut-brain axis**, the microbiome affects mood and cognition (Cryan et al., 2019; Dash et al., 2015)
- **Inflammation and depression**, pro-inflammatory diets are associated with depression (Jacka et al., 2017; Akbaraly et al., 2009)

## Practice

- Use nutrition for mental health: eat a Mediterranean-style diet (vegetables, fruits, fish, olive oil, whole grains), the whole-diet pattern tested in the SMILES trial ([Jacka et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/)).

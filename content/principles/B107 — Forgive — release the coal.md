> **Principle 107** — Forgive: release the coal

The Dhammapada's first chapter gives the mechanism. Verse 3 says that those who keep replaying the injury do not still their hatred, and verse 4 says that those who do not replay it do. Verse 5 follows:

> Hatred is never appeased by hatred in this world. By non-hatred alone is hatred appeased. This is a law eternal. ([Dhammapada 5, trans. Buddharakkhita, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.01.budd.html))

Thanissaro Bhikkhu translates the same lines this way: "Hostilities aren't stilled through hostility, regardless. Hostilities are stilled through non-hostility: this, an unending truth." ([Dhammapada 3-6, Access to Insight PDF](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/dhammapada.pdf))

The verse describes how hatred ends and does not mention a hot coal. The coal in the principle's title is a modern image for the same point, so treat it as the compendium's gloss. The point it carries is that the replaying of the grievance is something you do, and it is the thing the verses name as keeping hatred going. Forgiving does not mean the wrong was fine. It means ending the replay, and a boundary can stay in place.

(Dhammapada 3-5, trans. Buddharakkhita; Thanissaro's wording for verses 3-6.)

### Plain words

Going over a grievance again and again keeps it alive, and you are the one doing it. The Dhammapada says hatred does not end by more hatred. So the practical step is to notice the replay and stop feeding it. You can still keep your distance from the person who hurt you.

### For example

Two people are wronged by the same friend. One goes over it daily and avoids every event the friend might attend. The other keeps a clear boundary but stops rehearsing the story. Five years on, the second person has more of their week back. This one is imagined.

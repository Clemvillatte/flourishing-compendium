> **Principle 61** — Act before things arise

Daodejing 64 opens with the case for early action:

> "Action should be taken before a thing has made its appearance; order should be secured before disorder has begun." (Daodejing 64, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The reasoning comes just before it: "That which is at rest is easily kept hold of; before a thing has given indications of its presence, it is easy to take measures against it; that which is brittle is easily broken; that which is very small is easily dispersed." The chapter illustrates small beginnings with a large tree growing from a tiny sprout and a nine-storey tower rising from a small heap of earth. It also names the failure mode at the other end: "people in their conduct of affairs are constantly ruining them when they are on the eve of success. If they were careful at the end, as (they should be) at the beginning, they would not so ruin them."

(DDJ 64.)

**Plain words** The chapter's reasoning is that things are easiest to handle before they show any sign of themselves, so the early action happens when nothing is visibly wrong. That is the awkward part: it looks like effort spent on nothing. The chapter's own images are a sprout and a heap of earth, and both grow into something large.

*For example:* The ten minutes spent backing up files tonight, against the weekend lost to a dead drive six months from now.

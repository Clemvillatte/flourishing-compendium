> **Principle 42** — Take the body as a vessel, not a treasure

Shantideva's verse on the body's standing:

> "Regard your body as a vessel, / A simple boat for going here and there. / Make of it a wish-fulfilling gem / To bring about the benefit of beings." (Shantideva, Bodhicharyavatara 5.70, [verse rendering as carried in Ringu Tulku's chapter 5 teaching](http://bodhicharya.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Ringu-Tulku-Rinpoche-Bodhicharyavatara-Chapter-5.pdf))

The verses before it set the terms. The body, "though you pamper it, will leave you", so the instruction is to "pay this body due remuneration, / But then be sure to make it work for you" and not "lavish everything / On what will not bring perfect benefit" (5.68-5.69). Ringu Tulku's commentary on the vessel verse draws the line both ways: the aim is "not as the master, not as something that you always pamper and do whatever it says and then completely become the slave of it, but as something that we are master of", and "In the Buddhist way, it is not helpful to make the body suffer or to pamper the body. The middle path is to use the body in the proper way such that it will bring some benefit ourselves and others". ([chapter 5 teaching PDF](http://bodhicharya.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Ringu-Tulku-Rinpoche-Bodhicharyavatara-Chapter-5.pdf))

**Plain words**
The verse asks for the attitude you would take toward a boat you rely on: maintain it, pay its running costs, use it for the crossing. Not a jewel to polish for display, not an enemy to punish. The test of any care you give the body is whether the work it is for gets done.

For example: Sleeping properly before a big week, not for vanity and not as a grudging chore, just because the vessel has work to do.

> **Principle 30** — Practice humility as accuracy, not self-abasement

The huehuetlatolli's humility counsels are famously extreme in form ("you are a poor wretch, a beggar, a nobody") and precise in function: they inoculate against arrogance at the moment of any elevation, and they locate worth in conduct rather than in status. Read carefully, the speeches also affirm the hearer's dignity and potential in the same breath. The practical core: hold your achievements lightly and your responsibilities fully. Practically, when praised, practice the elders' response: acknowledge the contributing others and conditions before accepting any remainder.

(Florentine Codex, Book VI, the threshold speeches.)

**Plain words** The elders' humility speeches sound brutal ("you are a poor wretch, a nobody") but function precisely: they inoculate against arrogance at the moment of any elevation, and they relocate worth into conduct rather than status. Read the whole speech: it affirms your dignity in the same breath. Humility as accuracy, not self-abasement.

*For example:* Someone promoted young who takes the promotion with the elders' formula in mind — the title is weather; the conduct is the self — and neither the arrogance nor the crash lands on them.

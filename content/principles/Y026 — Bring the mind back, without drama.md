> **Principle 26** — Bring the mind back, without drama

Krishna tells Arjuna to bring the wandering mind back under the soul's control each time it strays. This instruction belongs to a chapter that seeks steadiness in meditation. Later, Arjuna compares controlling the mind to holding the wind; Krishna answers that habit and self-command can restrain it. Repeated return belongs to that larger discipline ([Arnold, Chapter VI](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2388/2388-h/2388-h.htm)).

> But, as often as the heart
> Breaks--wild and wavering--from control, so oft
> Let him re-curb it, let him rein it back
> To the soul's governance;
>
> — Bhagavad Gita 6.26 (Arnold, The Song Celestial; [Gutenberg 2388](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2388/2388-h/2388-h.htm))

**Plain words** One practical reading of this principle is to resume your meditation without first passing judgment on the session. Suppose you notice that you have been planning tomorrow instead of meditating. You can leave the plan unfinished and begin attending again. The compendium's phrase without drama describes that way of applying the instruction.

For example (imagined): During a sitting, someone notices that they are rehearsing an argument. They put the imagined reply aside and resume the meditation.

(BG 6.26.)

> **Principle 6** — Know that behaviour has a functional analysis: every behaviour serves a function

Every behaviour, even "maladaptive" ones, serves a function. Avoidance reduces anxiety. Complaining may elicit support. Procrastination repairs mood. Understanding the function of a behaviour is the key to changing it.

**Plain words** Every behaviour, including the "bad" ones, is doing a job — avoidance soothes, procrastination protects, complaining bonds. You can't fire a behaviour without knowing what it's being paid to do. Find the function, then hire a replacement.

*For example:* The doom-scroller at 10 p.m. isn't undisciplined — they're tired and buying comfort. The fix that works isn't shame; it's giving the 10 p.m. self a better job to do.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Functional analysis|Functional analysis (applied behaviour analysis)]]**, behaviour is understood by its function (antecedent-behaviour-consequence) (Baer et al., 1968; Hanley et al., 2003)
- **[[Functional contextualism|Functional contextualism (ACT)]]**, behaviour is understood in its context and function (Hayes et al., 2011; Vilardaga et al., 2009)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires examined behaviour** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Everything serves a purpose|Everything serves a purpose]]** (Nietzsche, Genealogy of Morals)
- **[[Right view|Right view about function]]** (Buddhism, dependent origination)

## Practice

- When you notice a behaviour you want to change, ask: "what function does this serve?" (What does it give me? What does it avoid?) Understanding the function allows you to find a replacement behaviour that serves the same function.

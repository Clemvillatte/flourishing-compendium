> **Principle 499** — Practise the creation of values

Nietzsche's claim starts from a point about origins. In The Gay Science he writes that whatever has value in the present world "has it not in itself, by its nature", but "a value was once given to it, bestowed upon it, and it was we who gave and bestowed!" (Nietzsche, 1882, The Joyful Wisdom, §301, trans. Thomas Common, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52881/pg52881.txt)). Zarathustra says the same of peoples: "Valuing is creating: hear it, ye creating ones! [...] Through valuation only is there value" ("The Thousand and One Goals," Thus Spake Zarathustra, Part I, trans. Common, [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1998/pg1998.txt)).

The aim follows: "We, however, would seek to become what we are,—the new, the unique, the incomparable, making laws for ourselves and creating ourselves!" (Gay Science, §335). It comes with a demand: "We must be physicists in order to be creators in that sense", because earlier ideals rested on "ignorance of physics, or in contradiction to it". Honesty about the world comes first.

Beyond Good and Evil adds that creating takes preparation. The philosopher must first "traverse the whole range of human values and estimations", and then the task "requires him to create values" (§211, trans. Helen Zimmern, [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4363/pg4363.txt)).

**Plain words**
Every value you hold was given to something by somebody, and you can examine where yours came from. Learn what is actually true, learn the values of other people and traditions, then choose and live the ones you can stand behind. Starting from scratch is not the point.

**For example**
You were taught that rest is laziness. Before keeping or dropping the belief, trace where it came from, test it against what you know about sleep and recovery, and then decide what rest means in your life.

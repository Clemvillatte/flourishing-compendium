> **Principle 27** — Choose communities that align with your values

> legislators make the citizens good by forming habits in them, and this is the wish of every legislator

(Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1, 1103b, trans. W. D. Ross, [MIT Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html).)

Aristotle's point is that the shared life of a city trains character through habit. The same passage gives the general rule: "we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts." He is describing a state's laws, so applying it to the clubs and circles a person joins is an extension made by this entry.

A modern study with a related shape is Christakis and Fowler's analysis of 12,067 people in the Framingham Heart Study. It found that a person's chance of becoming obese "increased by 57%" if a friend became obese, and that these clusters "did not appear to be solely attributable to the selective formation of social ties among obese persons" (New England Journal of Medicine 357(4), 2007, abstract; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17652652/)). The topic is weight, not values, and the findings are associations in a network.

**Plain words** The people around you shape what feels normal to you. Because that happens whether you pick them or not, it is worth choosing deliberately and checking now and then what the group is doing to your habits.

*For example:* A person who joins a running club finds, within a year, that early mornings and training plans have become ordinary. Nobody told them to change.

## Practice

- Audit your communities for value alignment (see principle 26). Where misaligned, either work to change the community or leave it. In community selection, treat the community's norms as you would treat your own habits.

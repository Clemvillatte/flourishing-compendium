> **Principle 33** — Do not mock the vulnerable

The Nahua counsel against mocking the vulnerable is hard to pin to a single passage. I found it in a newspaper column that summarises a huehuetlatolli collected by Fray Andrés de Olmos, not in Sahagún's Book VI. The column's paraphrase reads: "No te burles del hombre desdichado, del cieguito, del sordito, del manco" (do not mock the unfortunate man, the blind, the deaf, the one-handed), followed by "Si desprecias a la gente, te aborrecerás a ti mismo" ([Milenio column](https://www.milenio.com/opinion/pedro-miguel-funes-diaz/vision-social/nobleza-y-humildad)). The column says the Nahuatl diminutives are reverential, not pitying. It summarises a translation by Librado Silva, so the wording is approximate.

The seed's warning that the mocker "invites the same" is not in that paraphrase, and I did not find the list "old, sick, deformed, poor, deaf, simple" in Book VI either. The Book VI passages I did open ask the young not to laugh at the elders' counsel ([study of the huehuetlatolli](https://journals.lapub.co.uk/index.php/HB/article/download/2615/1793/7896)), which is a related but different point.

Humor research supports a social cost. Martin and colleagues (2003) defined aggressive humor as ridicule and disparagement of others, as summarised in [Robert and Wall 2019](https://business.missouri.edu/sites/default/files/2022-03/Robert_and_Wall_Humor_2019.pdf). In two studies, [targets described as using aggressive humor](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/147470491301100118) were rated lower in warmth and trustworthiness. This research did not test harm to the people mocked.

(Florentine Codex, Book VI, the counsel on mockery.)

### Scope

The Nahuatl source text was not opened. The locator "Book VI" may be wrong.

**Plain words**
The counsel is to treat the unfortunate, the blind, the deaf and the poor with respect and not to laugh at them. People who use ridicule are also judged as less warm.

**For example**
For one week, note each joke you tell or laugh at. Mark any whose target is a person who cannot easily answer back.

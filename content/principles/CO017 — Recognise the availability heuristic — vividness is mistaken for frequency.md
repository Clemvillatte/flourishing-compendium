> **Principle 17** — Recognise the availability heuristic: vividness is mistaken for frequency

Tversky and Kahneman define the heuristic precisely: "A person is said to employ the availability heuristic whenever he estimates frequency or probability by the ease with which instances or associations could be brought to mind." They add that "in general, availability is correlated with ecological frequency, but it is also affected by other factors", so relying on it "leads to systematic biases" (Tversky & Kahneman 1973, Cognitive Psychology 5, p. 208, [abstract and text via ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0010028573900339)). Their ten studies include the judged frequency of classes of words, of combinatorial outcomes and of repeated events.

The popular extension to media and fear is on weaker ground. Pachur re-analysed Lichtenstein and colleagues' classic study of judged causes of death together with its replications. Media coverage consistently over-represented dramatic risks, but "a reliable overestimation (underestimation) of dramatic (nondramatic) risks in people's frequency judgments emerged only in Lichtenstein et al.'s dataset; it did not replicate in the other datasets". The results suggest that people draw on "samples from their personal social networks rather than from the media" (Pachur 2024, Cognition 246:105736, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38368678/)).

**Plain words**
When you guess how common something is, your mind checks how easily examples come to you. That usually works, because common things are easier to recall. It fails when something is easy to recall for another reason, such as a recent or vivid case. The idea that news coverage drives this is less certain than it sounds, because a re-analysis found that people's guesses seemed to lean on the people around them. Before trusting a feeling that something is common or dangerous, ask whether you are counting examples or remembering one.

*For example:* After a friend tells you about a burglary on their street, you feel burglaries are rising. Look up your local police figures for the last three years before changing any plans. This is an imagined case, and what the figures show for your area is for you to find.

## Practice

- When you feel something is common or dangerous, ask: "is this feeling based on data or on how easily I can recall examples?" Check actual base rates.

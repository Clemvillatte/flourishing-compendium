> **Principle 67** — Notice feeling tone first (vedana)

MN 10 instructs the monk to recognize a feeling as painful, pleasant, or neither while experiencing it ([MN 10, Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN10.html)). For the compendium's exercise, begin by identifying which description fits the feeling that is present.

> "And how does a monk remain focused on feelings in & of themselves? There is the case where a monk, when feeling a painful feeling, discerns, 'I am feeling a painful feeling.' When feeling a pleasant feeling, he discerns, 'I am feeling a pleasant feeling.' When feeling a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, he discerns, 'I am feeling a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling.'"
>
> (Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, MN 10, Thanissaro, contemplation of feelings)

MN 118 includes breathing instructions for sensitivity to rapture and pleasure, followed by sensitivity to and calming of mental fabrication. It relates these instructions to remaining focused on feelings in and of themselves ([MN 118, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.118.than.html)).

> "On whatever occasion a monk trains himself, 'I will breathe in...&...out sensitive to rapture'; trains himself, 'I will breathe in...&...out sensitive to pleasure'; trains himself, 'I will breathe in...&...out sensitive to mental fabrication'; trains himself, 'I will breathe in...&...out calming mental fabrication': On that occasion the monk remains focused on feelings in & of themselves — ardent, alert, & mindful — putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world."
>
> (Anapanasati Sutta, MN 118, Thanissaro, breath steps and their classification)

The feelings section in MN 10 also directs attention to feelings arising and passing away. Its refrain includes mindfulness that "There are feelings" and remaining "independent, unsustained by [not clinging to] anything in the world".

**Plain words**
For this exercise, ask how an experience feels while it is happening. Is the feeling pleasant, unpleasant, or neither? Describe that feeling separately from your judgment about what happened. Then consider what response you want to make.

*For example:* Imagine reading an email you take to be curt. Before writing a reply, note that reading it feels unpleasant. Then reread the message and decide what you want to say.

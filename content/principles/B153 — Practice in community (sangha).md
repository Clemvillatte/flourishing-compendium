> **Principle 153** — Practice in community (sangha)

Practice sustained alone is fragile; a community holds the rhythm, the reminders, and the feedback. Find or build one.

(SN 45.2; Dhammapada 87.)

### Why community is the whole of it

When Ananda suggests that admirable friendship is half the holy life, the Buddha's correction is blunt:

> "Don't say that, Ananda. Don't say that. Admirable friendship, admirable companionship, admirable camaraderie is actually the whole of the holy life. When a monk has admirable people as friends, companions, & comrades, he can be expected to develop & pursue the noble eightfold path." (SN 45.2, [Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.002.than.html))

The emphasis falls on admirable companions — people whose friendship helps someone develop the path — not on attendance for its own sake. The Buddha then names himself as an admirable friend through whom beings subject to birth, aging, death, sorrow and despair have gained release (SN 45.2): the path is learned from a person, not from a curriculum.

**Plain words** Suppose you keep putting off sitting down to practise. Practising with a friend gives you an occasion to return, and a good companion can also question you when your conduct falls short of what you practise. Belonging to a group is not, by itself, the point — look for people who help you practise well, not merely people who expect you to show up.

*For example:* The gym-goer who quits in winter versus the running club that shows up in rain — same muscles, different infrastructure.

> **Principle 22** — Recognise the spotlight effect: you attend to what you think others notice about you

People overestimate how much others notice and evaluate them. This "spotlight effect" — feeling that everyone is watching and judging — is a bias. In reality, others are mostly attending to themselves.

**Plain words** Everyone is the star of their own movie, which means almost nobody is watching yours as closely as you fear. The coffee you spilled at the meeting is a blooper reel that only you kept.

*For example:* Replaying the joke that bombed for a week — ask anyone from that dinner what they remember. Your sweater, the wine, their own joke that also bombed. Nobody's keeping your footage.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Spotlight effect (Gilovich)|Spotlight effect (Gilovich)]]**, people overestimate how much others notice them (Gilovich et al., 2000; Savitsky et al., 2001)
- **[[The illusion of transparency|The illusion of transparency]]**, people overestimate how visible their internal states are to others (Gilovich et al., 1998; Savitsky & Gilovich, 2003)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The fear of judgment as suffering** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **[[Care|Care about the right audience]]** (Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations)
- **The crowd as projection** (Kierkegaard, The Crowd is Untruth)

## Practice

- When you feel scrutinised, remember the spotlight effect: others are mostly attending to themselves, not to you. This reduces social anxiety and self-consciousness.

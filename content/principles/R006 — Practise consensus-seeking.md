> **Principle 268** — Practise consensus-seeking

When making decisions that affect a community, seek consensus rather than imposing majority rule. The African palaver (community meeting) tradition values the process of dialogue until all voices are heard and agreement is reached.

(Ubuntu philosophy; Wiredu, "Social Philosophy in Postcolonial Africa," in A Companion to African Philosophy.)

**Plain words** When a decision belongs to the group, don't race to a vote and let half the room lose. The palaver tradition sits with the question until every voice has been heard and something like real agreement appears. Slower, and it holds.

*For example:* The team lead who stops calling votes in the first ten minutes and starts asking the quiet ones directly — the decision lands later, and nobody spends the next month quietly undermining it.

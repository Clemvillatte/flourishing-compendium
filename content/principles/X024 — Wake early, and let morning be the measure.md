> **Principle 24** — Wake early, and let morning be the measure

Thoreau's morning doctrine: to be awake is to be alive; and the morning, when the mind is unencumbered and the day's noise has not yet filled it, is the hour of the gods. The distinct practice is the morning-first rule: the day's first hour belongs to the highest thing, not to the inbox. Practically, for one month, guard the first sixty minutes for the deliberate life (reading, walking, writing, sitting) before any input arrives, and note the month's character.

(Thoreau, Walden, the morning passages; "Morning.")

**Plain words** To be awake is to be alive, and the morning is the hour of the gods: the day's first sixty minutes belong to the highest thing, not the inbox. Guard them for reading, walking, writing, sitting — anything but input. The first hour scripts the day.

*For example:* The person who spent years waking straight into notifications — then takes the first hour back for a walk and a book for a month, and finds the whole day follows a different director.

> **Principle 57** — Recognise the importance of self-concordant goals: your goals, not others'

Sheldon and Elliot define self-concordance as the "consistency with the person's developing interests and core values". In their model, "those pursuing self-concordant goals put more sustained effort into achieving those goals and thus are more likely to attain them", and "those who attain self-concordant goals reap greater well-being benefits from their attainment". The effect runs through "daily activity-based experiences of autonomy, competence, and relatedness". The model fitted three longitudinal data sets ([Sheldon & Elliot, 1999, JPSP 76, 482-497](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101878/)).

Kasser and Ryan add a related finding from three studies. When money-related values and expectancies were more central than other values, they were "negatively related to college students' well-being and mental health", and in a non-college sample high centrality of financial aspirations went with "lower global adjustment and social productivity and more behavioral disorders" ([Kasser & Ryan, 1993, JPSP 65, 410-422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8366427/)). That study concerns the centrality of financial goals, not goal ownership as such.

(Sheldon & Elliot, 1999; Kasser & Ryan, 1993.)

**Plain words** Goals that fit your own interests and values led to more sustained effort and larger well-being gains in the self-concordance studies. The page's contrast with goals pursued for approval or pressure goes a step beyond this abstract. Asking whose goal it is can still be a useful check.

For example (imagined): someone completes a qualification because their family expects it and feels relief, not satisfaction. A project they chose themselves leaves them wanting more.

- **Money-centred goals and well-being**, high centrality of financial aspirations was linked with lower adjustment and well-being (Kasser & Ryan, 1993); Kasser (2002) not verified here

## Practice

- Examine your goals: are they yours (aligned with your values) or absorbed (from others)? Pursue self-concordant goals. Let go of goals that are not truly yours.

> **Principle 57** — Recognise the importance of self-concordant goals: your goals, not others'

Goals that are self-concordant (aligned with your own values and interests) are pursued more effectively, achieved more often, and produce more well-being. Goals pursued for external reasons (approval, pressure) are less effective and less satisfying.

**Plain words** Your goals or borrowed ones — self-concordant goals (yours, aligned with your values) run on clean fuel; inherited goals (your parents', your industry's) run on fumes and guilt. Audit the goal's ownership. Whose is it, really?

*For example:* The MBA pursued because serious people do MBAs — two years of dragging a boulder uphill. The same person, two years later, learning furniture-making, cannot be stopped. Same effort; different ownership.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-concordance theory|Self-concordance theory (Sheldon & Elliot)]]**, self-concordant goals predict attainment and well-being (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999; Sheldon et al., 2004)
- **Intrinsic goals predict well-being**, intrinsic goals (growth, connection) are associated with higher well-being (Kasser & Ryan, 1993; Kasser, 2002)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right intention|Right intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Autonomy|Autonomy and authenticity]]** (Rogers, On Becoming a Person)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires examined goals** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Examine your goals: are they yours (aligned with your values) or absorbed (from others)? Pursue self-concordant goals. Let go of goals that are not truly yours.

> **Principle 77** — Learn to assess and monitor behaviour: track what you do

Behaviour can be tracked. Simple self-monitoring (activity log, habit tracker, behaviour diary) provides feedback for adjustment and maintenance.

**Plain words** Track what you do — a simple log, one line a day. The self-report is unreliable; the log isn't. Two weeks of data beats a year of impressions about your own behaviour. You'll be surprised; that's the point.

*For example:* "I barely drink" — says the log with nine glasses this week. Not a moral audit; a mirror. The log has no ego, which is exactly why it's useful.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-monitoring of behaviour**, tracking behaviour is a highly effective behaviour-change technique (Michie et al., 2011; Gardner, 2015)
- **Feedback improves performance**, measurement and feedback improve behaviour (Kluger & DeNisi, 1996; Hattie & Timperley, 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires data]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **Know thyself through tracking** (Delphic maxim)

## Practice

- Track behaviour: keep an activity log, use a habit tracker, monitor what you actually do (not what you think you do). Use this data for adjustment.

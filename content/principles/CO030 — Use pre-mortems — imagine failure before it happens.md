> **Principle 30** — Use pre-mortems: imagine failure before it happens

Klein's premortem asks a team to assume its project has failed and independently write plausible reasons before sharing them and reviewing the plan ([“Performing a Project Premortem,” 2007, method description](https://lmscontent.embanet.com/USC/PPD554/Week10/PPD554_W10_HBR_Klein_Performaing_a_Project_Premortem.pdf)). The exercise is intended to make reservations discussable before commitment hardens ([opening and method description](https://lmscontent.embanet.com/USC/PPD554/Week10/PPD554_W10_HBR_Klein_Performaing_a_Project_Premortem.pdf)).

> The team members’ task is to generate plausible reasons for the project’s failure.

(Klein, “Performing a Project Premortem,” method description; [opened text](https://lmscontent.embanet.com/USC/PPD554/Week10/PPD554_W10_HBR_Klein_Performaing_a_Project_Premortem.pdf).)

### Plain words

Give yourself permission to describe how the plan could fail before defending it. Then turn each plausible concern into a question you can investigate or a change you can consider.

Plausibility is not proof: Mitchell and colleagues' experiments examined explanations under different certainty and time perspectives, not whether every imagined cause predicts an actual project failure ([1989 study, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/bdm.3960020103)). Treat the list as material for checking, not a forecast.

### For example

Suppose your writing group plans a public reading. Someone imagines that the venue falls through; the group could check the booking and discuss a backup rather than assuming the imagined failure is likely.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Pre-mortem technique|Pre-mortem technique]]**: Klein's method generates plausible failure explanations for review, rather than verified predictions ([Klein, 2007](https://lmscontent.embanet.com/USC/PPD554/Week10/PPD554_W10_HBR_Klein_Performaing_a_Project_Premortem.pdf)).
- **Prospective hindsight and explanation**: experimentally varying outcome certainty changed the nature of explanations; temporal perspective showed little influence in the first experiment ([Mitchell, Russo and Pennington, 1989, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/bdm.3960020103)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Premeditatio malorum|Premeditatio malorum]]** (Stoics, Seneca, Letters)
- **[[Negative visualisation|Negative visualisation]]** (Stoics, Epictetus, Discourses)
- **[[Memento mori|Memento mori]]** (Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations)

## Practice

- Before committing to a plan, spend 5 minutes writing: "it is six months from now, and this has failed. What went wrong?" Use the answers to strengthen the plan.

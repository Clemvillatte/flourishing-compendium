> **Principle 60** — Do not recall; do not imagine; do not think; do not examine; do not control; rest

Tilopa's Six Words of Advice is a Mahamudra practice text in six words: five negations, then one word. Ken McLeod, who translated the text many times over the years of three-year retreats, gives two renderings on one page. The expanded one spells out what each instruction addresses: "Let go of what has passed. Let go of what may come. Let go of what is happening now. Don't try to figure anything out. Don't try to make anything happen. Relax, right now, and rest." ([Tilopa's Six Words of Advice](https://unfetteredmind.org/practice/tilopas-advice/))

The page's laconic translation reads:

> Don't recall.
> Don't imagine.
> Don't think.
> Don't examine.
> Don't control.
> Rest.
>
> (Tilopa, Six Words of Advice, laconic translation)

**Plain words** A quiet moment can easily become another job: work out the problem, predict the outcome, make yourself relax. The six words ask you to leave the problem unfinished for a moment, without trying to produce any particular feeling. The pause is short and you return to what needs doing afterwards.

*For example:* while waiting for a reply to a difficult message, you notice you are rehearsing the argument again. You leave the rehearsal alone for a brief pause, without pushing it away, and then return to the task at hand.

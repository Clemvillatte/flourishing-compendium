> **Principle 296** — Practise tika

The seed text calls tika "the Māori equivalent of moral integrity". The scholars I opened warn against exactly that equation. Stewart, Smith, Diamond, Paul and Hogg write that "to equate pono with respect, tika with integrity, and aroha with compassion falls far short of the full Māori meanings", and that "tika is a central principle of ethical behaviour towards other people and the world" ([Stewart et al. 2021, AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/5920bd9b-32c3-4389-a2ad-fc0e90a25190/content)).

What tika means. The same paper lists meanings of tika as "straight, accurate, fair, apt, correct, decent". It quotes Tate: tika "also means justice, and authority, in which case it is synonymous with mana". It quotes Mead: "The concept of tika, or being correct, is a base principle that applies to all tikanga". The idea is that a practice has to be correct and right in its own setting, and tika is the test applied to it. The [New Zealand Law Commission study paper](https://www.lawcom.govt.nz/assets/Publications/StudyPapers/NZLC-SP9.pdf) quotes Bishop Manuhuia Bennett: tikanga "indicates the obligation to do things in the 'right' way: doing the right thing for no other reason than because it is the right thing to do."

How it connects to other values. The 2021 paper reports that "tika presupposes pono, and is the basis for aroha", and that "pono can be exercised with tika, and tika must be exercised with pono, but both can be superseded by aroha". So asking "is this tika?" is not a free-standing test. It sits beside truth and care.

For the psychology parallel, Aquino and Reed (2002) define moral identity as "a self-conception organized around a set of moral traits" (quoted in [a 2016 paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5014871/)). That is a different thing from tika. Blasi (1984) was not opened.

**Plain words** Tika is rightness, in the sense of doing things correctly, fairly and justly within a set of relationships. It is not simply a personal sense of integrity, and it works alongside truth and care.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A builder tells a client that the cheaper option will not last, and loses the extra sale. The builder's question was what the right way to do the job is, and not what the margin is.

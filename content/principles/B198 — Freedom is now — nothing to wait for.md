> **Principle 198** — Freedom is now: nothing to wait for

The Dhammapada puts the work in the present tense: "One by one, little by little, moment by moment, a wise man should remove his own impurities, as a smith removes his dross from silver." The moment-by-moment phrasing leaves no season in which the work is not current.

> One by one, little by little, moment by moment, a wise man should remove
> his own impurities, as a smith removes his dross from silver.
>
> (Dhammapada 239, Buddharakkhita)

The Platform Sutra makes the availability explicit: "In the self-nature of each of you there is limitless wisdom. Maha Prajna Paramita is originally fully present within your self-nature. You need not seek it outside." And: "You should enlighten yourself and not seek outside. If you hear the Sudden Teaching you may become enlightened right away." Its verse closes the case: "Search out Bodhi only in the mind. / Why toil outside in search of the profound? / Just as you hear these words, so practice: / Heaven then appears, right before your eyes."

(Dhammapada 239-240; Platform Sutra.)

**Plain words**
The postponing mind has a specific shape: freedom is real, but it is scheduled for after the degree, the move, the children, the retirement. The two texts answer differently. The Dhammapada's answer is gradual: the removal of impurities happens "moment by moment", so the work is always current. The Platform Sutra's answer is sudden: the wisdom is "originally fully present within your self-nature", so there is nothing to wait for. The compendium's exercise: pick the thing you are postponing until conditions are right, and do the part of it that is possible in the current hour.

*For example:* The person who decides, mid-Tuesday, mid-dishwasher, that this is the life they were waiting for: nothing external has changed in that moment.

> **Principle 344** — Practise the primacy of relation

The book that fixed this idea does not open with a relation, but it does not stay that way long:

> "I become through my relation to the Thou; as I become I, I say Thou. All real living is meeting." (Buber, I and Thou, [Smith translation, opened full text](https://ia800608.us.archive.org/16/items/IAndThou_572/BuberMartin-i-and-thou_text.pdf))

Buber's formula reverses the order the principle describes: not an I that then relates, but an I that appears in the relating. The fuller statement:

> "In the beginning is relation—as category of being, readiness, grasping form, mould for the soul; it is the a priori of relation, the inborn Thou." (Buber, I and Thou, [Smith translation, opened full text](https://ia800608.us.archive.org/16/items/IAndThou_572/BuberMartin-i-and-thou_text.pdf))

The "between" of the later work is where this lives: "the realm that 'hides in our midst, in the between'" (Buber, I and Thou, p. 99, as cited in [Welz, 2025](https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/429218485/Buber_s_Relational_Ontology_and_Dialogical_Listening.2025.pdf)). A recent study of Buber's ontology states the philosophical consequence plainly: "the relations between entities are regarded as ontologically more fundamental than these entities themselves. Relations are primary as they form and transform the relata, whereas in a substance ontology, the category of 'relation' is regarded as derivative" (Welz, 2025).

(Buber, 1923; Buber, 1965.)

**Plain words** You don't exist first and then connect — you exist in relation from the very start. The "between" isn't the gap between two finished people; it's the ground the people themselves grow out of. Which means: relation is maintenance, not accessory.

*For example:* The twins' shared language, the old couple's shorthand, the team's unspoken rhythm — none of it lives in either person; it lives in the between, and it needs both of them showing up to survive.

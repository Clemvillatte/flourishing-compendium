> **Principle 80** — Prefer the common law to solitude's freedom

The person guided by reason is more free in a commonwealth, living under common law, than in solitude where they obey only themselves: the state's protections buy back more power than they cost in freedom. Spinoza's counterintuitive point is that structured obligation, well designed, is a net increase in effective freedom rather than a subtraction from it. Practically, before opting out of a structure (a team, a discipline, a law), price what its protections are actually buying you.

(Ethics IV, P73.)

**Plain words** The person guided by reason is more free in a commonwealth, under common law, than in solitude where they obey only themselves — the state's protections buy back more power than they cost in freedom. The counterintuitive Spinozist position: the law is a net gain.

*For example:* The homesteader's fantasy of total freedom — no rules, no obligations — and the reality: no enforcement, no infrastructure, no leverage. The person under common law has highways, courts, and hospitals; the "free" one has a well and a shotgun.

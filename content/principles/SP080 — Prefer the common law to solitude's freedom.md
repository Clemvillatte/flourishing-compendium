> **Principle 80** — Prefer the common law to solitude's freedom

The proposition is Ethics IV, 73: "The man, who is guided by reason, is more free in a State, where he lives under a general system of law, than in solitude, where he is independent." The proof does not price protections against freedom. It says the reasoning person "does not obey through fear", and "in so far as he endeavours to live in freedom, he desires to order his life according to the general good", so he "desires to possess the general rights of citizenship" ([Ethics, Part IV, Elwes](https://sacred-texts.com/phi/spinoza/ethics/eth04.htm)). The cost-benefit framing in the seed comes from the compendium.

Hasana Sharp reads the passage as a direct answer to the idea that solitude suits the rational person better, and points to the Political Treatise 6.1, where "all men fear being alone". She argues that the free person "lives better and more freely when joined to the coordinated mental and physical powers of others" ([Sharp, Spinoza on the Fear of Solitude](https://philarchive.org/archive/SHASOT-16)).

(Ethics IV, P73.)

### Scope

The claim concerns a person guided by reason in a state of law, which Spinoza treats as an ideal, not a description of all legal systems. I did not open the text of IV P37's scholia, where he discusses law and the common good, so I do not go further. The page does not defend any particular law.

**Plain words**
For Spinoza, a person who lives by reason is freer inside a lawful community than alone. They want the shared rules because they want the common good, and not because they fear punishment.

**For example**
Before leaving a group or rule you dislike, list what it gives you that you could not easily get alone, and what it asks in return.

> **Principle 1** — Flourish socially

Aristotle said it first: "man is by nature a political animal." He meant it strictly — man is "more of a political animal than bees or any other gregarious animals," and "he who by nature and not by mere accident is without a state, is either a bad man or above humanity." The EEMM's sociocultural level makes the same claim in modern dress: adaptive flourishing is not an individual achievement with social trimmings. Flourishing — [[Eudaimonia|eudaimonia]] to the Greeks, subjective and psychological well-being to modern researchers — is achieved inside a life-world of relationships, institutions, and cultural meanings. The compendium's application: treat your social fabric as material, not decoration. Everything else in the sociocultural domain builds on this recognition, that adaptation happens in context and the context is partly yours to build.

**Plain words** Nobody flourishes alone — the hermit's hut is built by a village. Your relationships, institutions, and culture aren't decoration on your flourishing; they're the material it's made of. Feed the social fabric and it feeds you back.

*For example:* The person who "doesn't need anyone" and has their worst year exactly when the friendships were let lapse — the body kept score of the isolation even when the philosophy said otherwise.
## Psychology parallels

- Social relationships are among the strongest correlates of well-being, health, and mortality (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010, 2017; House et al., 1988). The effect is not a mere association: loneliness predicts cardiovascular, metabolic, and immune outcomes through identifiable mechanisms (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2017; Hawkley and Cacioppo, 2010; Cacioppo et al., 2011). Sociality is a primary evolutionary adaptation of the human species (Caporael, 1997; Brewer and Caporael, 2006; Tomasello, 2014).

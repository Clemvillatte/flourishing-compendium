> **Principle 383** — Practise intergenerational ecological responsibility

Hans Jonas proposed a new imperative for an age of powerful technology: "Act so that the effects of your action are compatible with the permanence of genuine human life" (quoted from Jonas 1984, p. 11, in [Coyne and Hauskeller](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3069413/1/COYNE%20&%20HAUSKELLER%20Jonas%20Transhumanism%20and%20What%20It%20Means%20to%20Live%20a%20Genuine%20Human%20Life.pdf)). In the same passage, as reproduced in [a course handout](https://up.lublin.pl/files/biochemia/Etyka/ethics,_may,_28.pdf), the imperative "addresses itself to public policy rather than private conduct".

Gardiner's book explains why acting on this is hard. He argues that "the current generation, and especially the most affluent, are in a position to pass on most of the costs of their behavior (and especially the most serious harms) to the global poor, future generations and nonhuman nature", and he calls this "tyranny of the contemporary" ([Gardiner, A Perfect Moral Storm, OUP abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195379440.001.0001)). The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-intergenerational/) describes the same dynamic as one in which "each generation's pursuit of modest present benefits predictably imposes severe cumulative costs on all successors".

(Jonas, 1979, The Imperative of Responsibility; Gardiner, 2011, A Perfect Moral Storm.)

### What the sources do and do not say

The word "trustee" and the image of debt come from the compendium, not from Jonas or Gardiner in the passages I opened. The Stanford Encyclopedia mentions trusteeship only as an institutional proposal. I did not open the book chapters themselves.

**Plain words**
Before a big decision, ask what it hands to people who will live after you and cannot object. Some of the weight belongs to laws and institutions, not only to you.

**For example**
A couple choosing between a house extension and a solar retrofit can write down what each option leaves behind in thirty years, then also check what their local council or utility does about the same question.

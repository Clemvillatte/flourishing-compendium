> **Principle 1** — Return to experience before the labels

Nishida's opening concept in An Inquiry into the Good is pure experience. In his words, quoted by a later commentator: "To experience means to know facts just as they are, to know in accordance with facts by completely relinquishing one's own fabrications. What we usually refer to as experience is adulterated with some sort of thought, so by pure I am referring to experience just as it is without the least addition of deliberative discrimination … When one experiences one's own state of consciousness, there is not yet a subject or an object, and knowing and its object are completely unified." ([Shields, Critical Buddhism, quoting Nishida, An Inquiry into the Good, New Haven, 1987, pp. 3-4](https://ia902905.us.archive.org/30/items/criticalbuddhismengagingwithmodernjapanesebuddhistthoughtjamesmarkshields_202003_771_Y/Critical%20Buddhism%20Engaging%20with%20Modern%20Japanese%20Buddhist%20Thought%20James%20Mark%20Shields.pdf))

A commentary on the book (Mitsuhara 2021, pp. 26-27) adds a caution that matters here: it is a mistake to reduce the concept to experience before judgment, because Nishida himself says there is neither absolutely pure nor absolutely non-pure experience; every experience is pure, depending on how one looks at it. Nishida also treats thinking and judging as activities that, while going on, can have the same immediate character as seeing a colour.

(Nishida, An Inquiry into the Good, Part I, ch. 1.)

**Plain words** An experience need not begin as a person observing a separate thing. Nishida also includes thinking and judging while they happen, so his account reaches beyond moments when you stop naming what you notice. The principle's point is that the report's division into "someone seeing" and "a thing seen" is not the whole of the experience it reports.

*For example:* Imagine listening to a piece of music without naming the instrument or forming a view about the performance, and noticing how the experience feels when nothing is being said about it. The compendium's exercise is to try attending to a sound this way for ten seconds.

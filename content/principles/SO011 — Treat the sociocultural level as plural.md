> **Principle 11** — Treat the sociocultural level as plural

Treat the sociocultural level as plural. There is no single cultural form of flourishing; there are many. The pluralism of flourishing (emotional, psychological, social) extends to the cultural level: Latin American relational forms (Vergara, 2023), East Asian interdependent forms (Kitayama et al., 2007), African communal forms (Ubuntu; Tutu, 1999), Indigenous holistic forms (Durie, 1985, 1998; Fernando, 2014), and European-American individualistic forms (Markus and Kitayama, 1991) are all legitimate cultural achievements. The adaptive stance is to respect the plurality without collapsing into relativism.

**Plain words** There is no single correct human flourishing — Latin American relational versions, East Asian versions, East African versions, each complete and valid. Plural, not ranked. Your version is a dialect, not the language.

*For example:* The Australian definition of a good life and the Japanese one disagreeing about individuality — and both producing centenarians who smile. The outcomes converge; the paths differ.

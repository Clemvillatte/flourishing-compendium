> **Principle 145** — Live as a line, not an assembly

Tim Ingold contrasts the trace of a freehand gesture with a pattern made by joining predetermined points. He uses that distinction to examine travel, mapping and storytelling. To understand how people inhabit their environments, he suggests attending to the paths they make as they go.

> "What we see is no longer the trace of a gesture but an assembly of point-to-point connectors. The composition stands as a finished object, an artefact. Its constituent lines join things up, but they do not grow or develop." (Ingold, Lines: A Brief History, 2007, ch. 3, "Up, across and along".)

The compendium proposes a practical application: describe how one activity developed through your participation in particular places and relationships. Use that history to consider possible next steps.

### Plain words

Choose something you know how to do. Think back to how you learned it, including the people who helped and the occasions when you changed your approach. Describe what happened between the milestones that would appear on a résumé. Consider what you would like to explore next.

### For example

Imagine someone who learned to bake with a parent and later worked in a bakery. They describe how a failed batch led them to ask a colleague about fermentation. For the exercise, they choose one further question to investigate.

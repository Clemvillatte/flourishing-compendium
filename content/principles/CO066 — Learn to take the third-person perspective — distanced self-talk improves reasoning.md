> **Principle 66** — Learn to take the third-person perspective: distanced self-talk improves reasoning

The Kross et al. (2014) paper opens with the question the research was built to answer.

> Does the language people use to refer to the self during introspection influence how they think, feel, and behave under social stress? (Kross et al., "Self-Talk as a Regulatory Mechanism: How You Do It Matters," Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 106(2), 2014, [author-site PDF](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/emotion-selfcontrol-psych/wp-content/uploads/sites/1322/2024/07/KrossJ_Pers_Soc_Psychol2014Self-talk_as_a_regulatory_mechanism_How_you_do_it_matters.pdf))

Seven studies addressed that question (total N = 585). The two proof-of-principle studies showed that using one's own name or non-first-person pronouns during introspection increased self-distancing; the two performance studies showed the non-first-person group did better on rated speech and first-impression tasks and reported less distress; the appraisal studies showed they read upcoming stressors in more challenging and less threatening terms; the meta-analysis found no moderation by trait social anxiety.

> Together, these findings demonstrate that small shifts in the language people use to refer to the self during introspection consequentially influence their ability to regulate their thoughts, feelings, and behavior under social stress, even for vulnerable individuals. (Kross et al., 2014, same PDF)

**Plain words** The technique is one word of grammar. Instead of asking "why am I anxious about this," ask "why is [your name] anxious about this." The paper found this shift is associated with better rated performance and less reported distress under stress.

*For example:* Imagine someone preparing for an interview the night before, reflecting in the third person: "Why is Maria anxious about this interview? Maria has prepared for it before." The studies found that people instructed to reflect this way, rather than with "I," performed better on rated tasks and reported less distress.

- **[[Distanced self-talk (Kross)|Distanced self-talk (Kross)]]**, using one's own name or third-person pronouns reduces emotional reactivity and improves self-control (Kross et al., 2014; Moser et al., 2017)

## Practice

- When emotionally activated, describe the situation in the third person ("[Name] is feeling anxious about this meeting") or from a temporal distance ("how will this look in a year?"). This creates psychological distance and improves reasoning.

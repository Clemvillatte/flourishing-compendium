> **Principle 66** — Learn to take the third-person perspective: distanced self-talk improves reasoning

Viewing your own situation from a third-person perspective (using your name or "he/she" rather than "I") reduces emotional reactivity, increases wise reasoning, and improves self-control. This is a simple but powerful cognitive technique.

**Plain words** Distanced self-talk: view your own situation in the third person — your name, "he/she" — and emotional reactivity drops, wise reasoning improves, self-control strengthens. One word of grammar; measurable effects. Talk about yourself like you'd talk about a friend.

*For example:* The person in the interview spiral who switches internally: "Okay — Maria has an interview tomorrow. Maria's prepared. Maria's done this before." — and the nervous system, hearing the coach instead of the panicked first person, settles. It's the cheapest known regulation tool.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Distanced self-talk (Kross)|Distanced self-talk (Kross)]]**, using one's own name or third-person pronouns reduces emotional reactivity and improves self-control (Kross et al., 2014; Moser et al., 2017)
- **Third-person perspective increases wise reasoning**, distanced self-talk improves wise reasoning about personal challenges (Grossmann & Kross, 2014)

## Practice

- When emotionally activated, describe the situation in the third person ("[Name] is feeling anxious about this meeting") or from a temporal distance ("how will this look in a year?"). This creates psychological distance and improves reasoning.

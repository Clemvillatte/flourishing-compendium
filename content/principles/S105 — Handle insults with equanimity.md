> **Principle 227** — Handle insults with equanimity

An insult is an impression: someone's words about you. It cannot harm your character. The person who insults you is revealing their own character, not yours. Respond with silence or with calm.

(Disc. I.18, I.28, III.7, III.22; Ench. 1, 42; Med. II.1, II.13, IV.3, VI.6, VI.16, VII.22, IX.4, IX.11, XI.18; Seneca, De Ira III.36, III.38, III.40.)

**Plain words** An insult is an impression: someone's words about you. It can't harm your character. The person insulting you is revealing theirs, not yours. Respond with silence or calm.

*For example:* A stranger yells at you in traffic. You let it go by and notice it only as weather.

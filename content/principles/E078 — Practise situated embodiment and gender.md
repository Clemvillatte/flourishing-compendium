> **Principle 470** — Practise situated embodiment and gender

Recognise that embodiment is always situated — in gender, in race, in class, in ability. De Beauvoir's insight that "one is not born, but rather becomes, a woman" applies not only to gender but to all embodied social positions. Do not treat your embodied situation as natural or inevitable; recognise it as constituted through social and historical forces.

(de Beauvoir, 1949/2010, The Second Sex, Introduction and Book II; Merleau-Ponty, 1945/2012, Phenomenology of Perception, Part II, ch. 4; Fanon, 1952/2008, Black Skin, White Masks.)

**Plain words** Embodiment is always situated — in gender, race, class, ability. "One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman" applies to every embodied social position: the becoming is real, social, and yours to work with. Don't treat your situated body as either destiny or illusion.

*For example:* Two runners in the same park at night — the same route, entirely different embodied situations, both real. The analysis that treats their experience as identical is describing neither; the situated body is where the truth lives.

> **Principle 68** — Recognise addiction and motivation: wanting without liking

Addiction involves a specific motivational pattern: strong wanting (craving, pursuit) without corresponding liking (enjoyment). The dopamine system is dysregulated. This is a clinical condition requiring treatment.

**Plain words** Addiction is wanting without liking — the craving fires full strength, the enjoyment barely registers. The pull says "this will be great"; the experience says "meh"; the memory says "let's do it again." Knowing the con is the first defence.

*For example:* The scroll that "relaxes" — an hour of it, and the shoulders are tighter than before. The wanting was promised a reward it never delivers, and books the same appointment tomorrow.
## Psychology parallels

- **Addiction as wanting-liking dissociation**, dopamine dysregulation produces strong wanting without enjoyment (Berridge & Robinson, 2016; Koob & Le Moal, 2008)
- **Addiction is a clinical condition**, evidence-based treatments exist (Koob & Le Moal, 2008; APA, 2022)

## Practice

- If you notice wanting something you do not enjoy (substances, behaviours, compulsive checking), recognise this as a dysregulated wanting system. Seek professional help. Willpower alone is not sufficient.

> **Principle 406** — Practise the habit body

Merleau-Ponty's sentence for it:

> "The body is our general medium for having a world." (Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Colin Smith's translation, p. 146, [quoted here](https://philarchive.org/archive/THAQTB))

The passage continues through motor habits such as dancing to instruments the body builds itself, projecting "around itself a cultural world" ([p. 146, ibid.](https://philarchive.org/archive/THAQTB)). That is the habit body: the body as it has learned. The dancer's phrase, the instrument absorbed into the hand, the driver's route home, all of it running below deliberation. If every movement needed deciding, nothing else would ever get decided. And the automatism runs the snacking, the phone-checking and the tone with the kids too. A habit, on this account, is structure more than a choice you keep making; changing one is retraining more than re-deciding. In a 12-week study of new daily behaviours in consistent contexts, the model-estimated time to plateau varied enormously: "The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days; indicating considerable variation in how long it takes people to reach their limit of automaticity and highlighting that it can take a very long time" ([Lally et al. 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsp.674)). The same study carries a small mercy: "Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." The compendium's related account is [[Body memory]].

**Plain words**
Your body has learned your life. It drives the route, plays the scales, reaches for the phone; none of it needs you. That is the deal: the automatism that frees your attention is the same machinery that runs the snacking and the snapping. To work on a routine, repeat the new behaviour after a stable cue, and give it weeks or months rather than days; Lally's study found wide variation in how automaticity developed, and missing one opportunity did not materially disrupt the process, so a missed day is not a broken streak.

*For example:* The driver who arrives home with no memory of the route — the habit body did the driving — and realizes the same autopilot also drives the after-work snacking, the phone-checking, the tone with the kids. The habit body has opinions.

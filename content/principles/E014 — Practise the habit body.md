> **Principle 406** — Practise the habit body

Merleau-Ponty's sentence for it:

> "The body is our general medium for having a world. Sometimes it is restricted to the actions necessary for the conservation of life, and accordingly it posits around us a biological world; at other times, elaborating upon these primary actions and moving from their literal to a figurative meaning, it manifests through them a core of new significance: this is true of motor habits such as dancing. Sometimes, finally, the meaning aimed at cannot be achieved by the body's natural means; it must then build itself an instrument, and it projects thereby around itself a cultural world." (Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, Colin Smith's translation, p. 146, [quoted here](https://philarchive.org/archive/THAQTB))

That is the habit body: the body as it has learned. The blind man's cane, the typist's fingers, the driver's route home, all of it running below deliberation. This is not a deficit but the condition for having a world at all; if every movement needed deciding, nothing else would ever get decided. And the mechanism is not confined to muscles: the same automatism runs the snacking, the phone-checking, the tone you take with the kids when tired. Because habit is structure rather than choice, changing one means retraining rather than re-deciding, and the modern evidence describes the training honestly. In a 12-week study of people adopting a new daily behaviour in a consistent context, automaticity rose along a curve, and the time to plateau varied enormously: "The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days; indicating considerable variation in how long it takes people to reach their limit of automaticity and highlighting that it can take a very long time" ([Lally et al. 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsp.674)). The same study carries a small mercy: "Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." The compendium's related account is [[Body memory]].

**Plain words**
Your body has learned your life. It drives the route, plays the scales, reaches for the phone; none of it needs you. That is the deal: the automatism that frees your attention is the same machinery that runs the snacking and the snapping. You cannot argue with a habit, you can only retrain it, and retraining means repetition in the same place at the same time until the body does the new thing without being asked. Give it weeks, not days, and if you miss one, the study found the process is not set back.

*For example:* The driver who arrives home with no memory of the route — the habit body did the driving — and realizes the same autopilot also drives the after-work snacking, the phone-checking, the tone with the kids. The habit body has opinions.

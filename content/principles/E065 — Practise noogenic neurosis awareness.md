> **Principle 457** — Practise noogenic neurosis awareness

Recognise that some forms of distress are not neurotic but existential — what Frankl called noogenic neurosis. When the will to meaning is frustrated, the result is not merely unhappiness but a specific form of suffering that requires not therapy but meaning. Distinguish between psychological symptoms that require treatment and existential questions that require engagement.

(Frankl, 1969, The Will to Meaning; Frankl, 1978, The Unheard Cry for Meaning.)

**Plain words** Some distress is not neurotic but existential — Frankl's noogenic neurosis: the suffering of a frustrated will to meaning. It requires not therapy but meaning. When the distress is the absence of purpose, the antidepressant treats the smoke while the fire burns.

*For example:* The successful, healthy, well-rested person who is inexplicably miserable — every clinical box unchecked — and the year the "what's it all for?" question is finally treated as the actual diagnosis. The treatment was a purpose, not a pill.

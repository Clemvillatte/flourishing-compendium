> **Principle 397** — Practise the horizon

In Ideas I §44, Husserl describes the perception of a physical thing as one-sided. Other sides are co-given with what is directly presented; further perceptions can bring them into view while previously presented sides cease to be presented.

> Necessarily there always remains a horizon of determinable indeterminateness, no matter how far we go in our experience, no matter how extensive the continua of actual perceptions of the same thing may be through which we have passed. (Husserl, Ideas I §44, Kersten trans., [full text PDF](https://www.finophd.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Husserl-Ideas-First-Book.pdf))

The compendium uses this description as an exercise: distinguish what you currently see from what you anticipate seeing next.

(Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §44.)

**Plain words**
You recognise a whole object while seeing only part of it. As you move, some things you expected become visible and others pass out of view. Try describing separately what you can see now and what you expect to see from another position.

*For example:* Imagine looking at a cup from a position where you cannot see whether it has a handle. Describe what you see, then what you expect to find when you turn it.

> **Principle 397** — Practise the horizon

Every perception gives you one side of a thing. The front of the cup is presented; the back is only co-given, meant along with it without being seen. Husserl's term for that surrounding fringe is the horizon: in the perception of a physical thing, what is actually presented is "surrounded by a horizon of 'co-givenness' which is not givenness proper, and of more or less vague indeterminateness" (Ideas I §44).

The indeterminateness is determinable, in a prescribed style. Each further look converts part of the fringe into givenness and leaves a new fringe.

> Necessarily there always remains a horizon of determinable indeterminateness, no matter how far we go in our experience, no matter how extensive the continua of actual perceptions of the same thing may be through which we have passed. (Husserl, Ideas I §44, Kersten trans., [full text PDF](https://www.finophd.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Husserl-Ideas-First-Book.pdf))

The same structure covers the whole world. What is perceived and co-present is "penetrated and surrounded by an obscurely intended to horizon of indeterminate actuality" (§27), and the present moment carries "its two-sidedly infinite temporal horizon" (§27). Any actual experience "points beyond itself to possible experiences which, in turn, point to new possible experiences and so ad infinitum" (§47). To practise the horizon is to treat what you see as a view from somewhere: to ask what is implied, what is anticipated, and what would have to arrive over the edge to change your judgment.

(Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §§27, 44, 83; Husserl, 1929/1960, Cartesian Meditations, §§19, 32, 48.)

**Plain words**
You see the front of things, and the front is never the whole. Around every presented bit there is a half-meant edge: the back of the object, the room behind you, the years of a person you have not met. The edge never goes away, however long you look, so certainty about the whole is always borrowed. When you feel you have someone or something figured out, ask what could still be over the edge, and what would have to show up to change your judgment.

*For example:* Three chats with a new colleague can feel like a complete picture. The horizon holds the rest: the marriage, the grief, the humour, the history you have not met. Before acting on the picture, name one thing that could still be over the edge.

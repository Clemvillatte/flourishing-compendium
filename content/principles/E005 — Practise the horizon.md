> **Principle 397** — Practise the horizon

Recognise that every experience has a horizon — a fringe of implicit meanings, anticipations, and possibilities that surround what is explicitly given. You never experience an object in its totality; you always experience it from a perspective, with a horizon of further possibilities. Attend to the horizons of your experience: what is implied but not said? What is anticipated but not yet present?

(Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §§27, 44, 83; Husserl, 1929/1960, Cartesian Meditations, §§19, 32, 48.)

**Plain words** Every experience has a horizon — a fringe of implicit meanings, anticipations, and possibilities around what's explicitly given. You never see the whole of anything; you see from a perspective, with more implied. Remember that your view is a view.

*For example:* Someone's "complete picture" of their new colleague turns out to be three interactions wide — and the horizon they forgot about holds everything not yet shown. The marriage, the grief, the humour: still over the edge.

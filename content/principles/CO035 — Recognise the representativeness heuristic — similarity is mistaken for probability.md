> **Principle 35** — Recognise the representativeness heuristic: similarity is mistaken for probability

You judge how likely something is by how similar it is to a prototype or stereotype, rather than by actual statistical probability. This leads to ignoring base rates and overestimating the likelihood of vivid, stereotypical cases.

**Plain words** Representativeness heuristic: you judge likelihood by similarity to a prototype — "she seems like a librarian" — rather than by statistical probability. Similarity is seductive and statistically illiterate. The prototype is a story; the probability is a number.

*For example:* The "obviously technical" candidate versus the quietly excellent one — the similarity to the stereotype is doing the hiring. The portfolio managers who ignore the story and check the track record outperform; the prototype was never data.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Representativeness heuristic|Representativeness heuristic]]**, similarity judgments substitute for probability judgments (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974; Kahneman & Tversky, 1973)
- **[[Conjunction fallacy|Conjunction fallacy]]**, judging a conjunction (Linda is a feminist and a bank teller) as more probable than a component (Linda is a bank teller) (Tversky & Kahneman, 1983)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The typical vs the actual** (Plato, Republic)
- **Appearance vs reality** (Kant, Critique of Pure Reason)

## Practice

- When judging probability, ask: "am I judging by how typical this seems or by actual frequencies?" Deliberately check the base rate.

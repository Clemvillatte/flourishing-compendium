> **Principle 51** — Contemplate a settled model

Sutra 1.37 is one of several optional supports for steadying the mind:

> Or the mind-stuff [reaches the stable state] by having as its object [a mind-stuff] freed from passion.

(Yoga Sutras 1.37, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary names who counts: "Those freed from passion are Krsnadvaipayana and certain others." The mind "is affected by having as the supporting-object the mind-stuff of these." So the object is a particular mind that is free of craving, taken as something to rest the attention on, and the effect described is steadiness of the practitioner's own mind. The next sutra, 1.38, offers a different support: a perception in dream or sleep.

The text introduces this as one of a list of "or" options, and says nothing about the support being only a step toward something else. Calling it a support rather than a destination is this page's own framing. Choosing one living or historical exemplar and asking "what would stillness look like here?" is also the page's own practice, and the text names only the great sages.

**Plain words**

One way the sutras give for steadying the mind is to hold in attention a person whose mind is free of craving. You borrow their steadiness while you grow your own. It is one option among several, to be picked if it suits.

**For example**

A trainee nurse recalls her ward sister's slow, level voice during a bad night shift and finds her own pace dropping to match it. She doesn't try to copy the sister, only to hold the picture while she works.

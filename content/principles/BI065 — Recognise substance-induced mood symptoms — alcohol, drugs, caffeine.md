> **Principle 65** — Recognise substance-induced mood symptoms: alcohol, drugs, caffeine

Three substances, three literatures. Alcohol: a pooled analysis found diagnosable alcohol problems and diagnosable depression each roughly doubling the odds of the other, with the more plausible causal direction running from the drinking ([Boden & Fergusson, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21382111/)). Cannabis: a meta-analysis of 14 longitudinal studies found cannabis users had somewhat higher odds of developing depression (OR 1.17), and heavy users higher still (OR 1.62) ([Lev-Ran et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23795762/)). Caffeine: a review of caffeine and mental health notes that in rare cases high doses can induce psychotic and manic symptoms, and more commonly anxiety, with panic-disorder patients particularly sensitive to the anxiogenic effects ([Lara, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20164571/)).

> "The OR for cannabis users developing depression compared with controls was 1.17 [95% confidence interval (CI) 1.05-1.30]. The OR for heavy cannabis users developing depression was 1.62 (95% CI 1.21-2.16), compared with non-users or light users." (Abstract, Results; Lev-Ran et al., 2014, Psychol Med 44(4): 797-810, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23795762/))

> "Conversely, in rare cases high doses of caffeine can induce psychotic and manic symptoms, and more commonly, anxiety. Patients with panic disorder and performance social anxiety disorder seem to be particularly sensitive to the anxiogenic effects of caffeine." (Abstract; Lara, 2010, J Alzheimers Dis 20 Suppl 1: S239-48, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20164571/))

**Plain words**
Substances move mood in both directions. Caffeine at moderate intake is associated with less depressive symptomatology and fewer cognitive failures, while high doses more commonly produce anxiety, and people with panic disorder are especially sensitive ([Lara, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20164571/)). Cannabis and depression travel together more often than chance allows, most strongly for heavy users ([Lev-Ran et al., 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23795762/)). Alcohol's two-way odds with depression are stated on its own page ([Boden & Fergusson, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21382111/)). The practical move is an input audit before a self-diagnosis: what is in the glass, the mug, the joint.

**For example**
(Imagined) The "generalised anxiety" that improves during a month off alcohol, or off the fourth daily espresso. The month off is a diagnostic test in the compendium's sense: an observation about inputs, not a treatment claim.

## Practice

- If you have mood symptoms, examine substance use: alcohol, cannabis, stimulants, caffeine. Reduce or eliminate substances that worsen mood. Do not treat substance-induced symptoms as purely psychological.

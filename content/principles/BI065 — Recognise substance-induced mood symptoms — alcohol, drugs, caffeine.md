> **Principle 65** — Recognise substance-induced mood symptoms: alcohol, drugs, caffeine

Substances (alcohol, cannabis, stimulants, caffeine) can produce, worsen, or mimic mood symptoms. Recognising substance effects is important for accurate diagnosis and treatment.

**Plain words** Substances produce, worsen, and mimic mood symptoms — alcohol, cannabis, stimulants, caffeine. The anxiety may be the espresso; the low mood may be the comedown. Before diagnosing your soul, audit your inputs.

*For example:* The "generalised anxiety" that improved dramatically during Dry July — the diagnosis was accurate; the cause was in the glass. The month off is a legitimate diagnostic test.
## Psychology parallels

- **Substance-induced mood symptoms**, alcohol, cannabis, and stimulants can produce depression and anxiety symptoms (Koob & Le Moal, 2008; Volkow et al., 2016)
- **Caffeine-induced anxiety**, caffeine can worsen anxiety and panic (Lara, 2010; Broderick & Benjamin, 2004)

## Practice

- If you have mood symptoms, examine substance use: alcohol, cannabis, stimulants, caffeine. Reduce or eliminate substances that worsen mood. Do not treat substance-induced symptoms as purely psychological.

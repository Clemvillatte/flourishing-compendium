> **Principle 358** — Practise the third

The citation on this page dates Otherwise than Being to 1969. It was published in French in 1974, with Alphonso Lingis's English translation in 1981 ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/levinas/)). The third also appears in Totality and Infinity (1961), and one article says it is treated "more extensively" in Otherwise than Being ([BMC Medical Ethics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12910-026-01453-z)).

Levinas's own wording is more precise than the seed's. "The third party introduces a contradiction in the saying whose signification before the other until then went in one direction. It is of itself the limit of responsibility and the birth of the question: What do I have to do with justice?" (Otherwise than Being, p. 157, quoted in the [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2019/entries/levinas/)). He also writes that the third "is other than the neighbour, but also another neighbour, and also a neighbour of the other, and not simply his fellow" (same book, quoted in a [PhilArchive paper](https://philarchive.org/archive/HORWTF)). Justice then "calls for judgment and comparison". A commentator writes that for Levinas the third's entry is not a contingent "empirical" fact: already in proximity, "all the others than the Other obsess me, and already this obsession cries out for justice" ([Philosophy Today, quoting OB 158](https://www.pdcnet.org/scholarpdf/show?id=philtoday_1998_0042Supplement_0059_0070&pdfname=philtoday_1998_0042Supplement_0059_0070.pdf&file_type=pdf)). I read these through quotations, not the book.

The psychology bullets (Kohlberg 1981, Haidt 2012) were not opened. A secondary document describes Kohlberg's theory as centred on a justice perspective of fairness, rights and equality, which Gilligan criticised for leaving little room for care ([Scribd document on the Gilligan-Kohlberg controversy](https://www.scribd.com/document/598509194/The-Gilligan-Kohlberg-Controversy-and-It)). Levinas's route differs: justice grows out of responsibility to a face and is not derived from impartial stages of reasoning.

**Plain words** Beyond you and one other person there is always a third, and then others. Levinas says that this limits your responsibility to any one person and raises the question of justice, which means comparing and judging among those who claim you.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A manager would do anything for one struggling colleague but has eleven others, so she has to turn personal care into fair rules.

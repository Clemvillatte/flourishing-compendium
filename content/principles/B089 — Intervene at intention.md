> **Principle 89** — Intervene at intention

SN 12.38 locates the leverage point directly: what one intends, arranges and obsesses about is the support for the stationing of consciousness, and from that landing comes the production of renewed becoming, birth, aging and death, and the mass of suffering. The discourse does not tell a person to manage each action; it identifies intention as the place the chain is fed. (SN 12.38.)

> "What one intends, what one arranges, and what one obsesses about: This is a support for the stationing of consciousness." (SN 12.38, [Thanissaro, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.038.than.html))

The Dhammapada's opening pair gives the same priority from the other side: mind precedes all mental states, and they are all mind-wrought. In the compendium's reading, the intervention point is upstream of the act: the act follows the mind that issued it. (Dhammapada 1-2.)

> "If with an impure mind a person speaks or acts suffering follows him like the wheel that follows the foot of the ox." (Dhammapada 1, [Buddharakkhita, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.01.budd.html))

**Plain words**
Take one action you keep correcting at the surface and look for the intention underneath. Ask what you are trying to get, prove or avoid. State that intention to yourself in plain words; it often sounds different said aloud than it does running silently. The act is downstream of it.

*For example:* Imagine a diet reframed: the same foods chosen, but as care for a body that has to carry you, rather than as punishment for looking wrong. Nothing on the plate changed.

> **Principle 69** — Recognise the hedonic treadmill: adaptation is inevitable; plan for it

Brickman and Campbell (1971) named the hedonic treadmill: good and bad events shift happiness only briefly before people adapt back toward their baseline. The planning advice follows from the fading. If the intensity of a new circumstance will wear off, decisions and expectations should be made with that wearing-off in view, for gains and losses alike.

> "According to the hedonic treadmill model, good and bad events temporarily affect happiness, but people quickly adapt back to hedonic neutrality." (Diener, Lucas & Scollon, 2006, American Psychologist 61(4): 305-314, abstract; [full text reprint, Caltech](http://www.its.caltech.edu/~squartz/Dienerbook.pdf))

The model has limits, and the limits matter for planning. In their 2006 review Diener, Lucas and Scollon concluded that set points are not hedonically neutral, differ across people, and can change under some conditions. Adaptation varies by event: widows and widowers, people laid off from work, people who divorced, and individuals with disabilities all showed long-lasting changes in life satisfaction. Their summary sentence: "Adaptation may proceed slowly over a period of years, and in some cases the process is never complete." Plan for the fade, but do not treat it as a law that finishes on schedule.

**Plain words**
The thrill of a change wears off. The new job, the move, the gadget, after a while they are just your life again. Expect that when you plan: do not build a budget or a life around a permanent lift in happiness, and when something bad lands, do not read the first months as how it will always feel. The fade is the default expectation; for a few losses it takes years and may not finish.

**For example**
Someone budgets a raise on the assumption that the good feeling is permanent. Six months later the salary is the new normal. The budget that assumed permanence is the one that breaks; the one that expected the fade still works.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Hedonic adaptation|Hedonic adaptation]]**, both positive and negative changes fade toward baseline (Lyubomirsky, 2011)
- **Adaptation is faster for stable states than for varied experiences**, variety and novelty slow adaptation (Sheldon & Lyubomirsky, 2012)

## Philosophy parallels

- **This too shall pass** (Persian Sufi tradition)
- **[[The insatiability of desire|The insatiability of desire]]** (Schopenhauer, World as Will and Representation)
- **The cycle of becoming** (Nietzsche, Eternal Recurrence)

## Practice

- When making a decision based on how you think you will feel, discount the predicted duration. When you notice a pleasure fading, this is adaptation, not a sign that something is wrong.

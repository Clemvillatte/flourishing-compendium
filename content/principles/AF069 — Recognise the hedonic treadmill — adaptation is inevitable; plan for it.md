> **Principle 69** — Recognise the hedonic treadmill: adaptation is inevitable; plan for it

Positive and negative experiences both fade toward baseline. The new car, the promotion, the relationship — each will feel less intense over time. This is not a failure; it is the architecture of the affect system. Planning for it prevents both over-investment in future highs and catastrophising future lows.

**Plain words** The hedonic treadmill: positive and negative experiences both fade toward baseline. The new car, the promotion, the relationship — each will feel less intense over time. Not a failure; the architecture of the system. Plan for adaptation instead of being ambushed by it.

*For example:* The person who thought the raise would change their life — and the six-month audit: same happiness, bigger apartment. Not cynicism; forecasting. The plan that accounts for the treadmill spends the raise on experiences, security, and others instead.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Hedonic adaptation|Hedonic adaptation]]**, both positive and negative changes fade toward baseline (Lyubomirsky, 2011)
- **Adaptation is faster for stable states than for varied experiences**, variety and novelty slow adaptation (Sheldon & Lyubomirsky, 2012)

## Philosophy parallels

- **This too shall pass** (Persian Sufi tradition)
- **[[The insatiability of desire|The insatiability of desire]]** (Schopenhauer, World as Will and Representation)
- **The cycle of becoming** (Nietzsche, Eternal Recurrence)

## Practice

- When making a decision based on how you think you will feel, discount the predicted duration. When you notice a pleasure fading, this is adaptation, not a sign that something is wrong.

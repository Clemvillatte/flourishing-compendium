> **Principle 456** — Practise meaning through the attitude toward suffering

When suffering is unavoidable, find meaning in the attitude you take toward it. This is Frankl's third path to meaning: not the avoidance of suffering but the choice of how to bear it. The last of the human freedoms is the freedom to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances.

(Frankl, 1946/2006, Man's Search for Meaning; Frankl, 1969, The Will to Meaning; Frankl, 1969/1978, The Unheard Cry for Meaning.)

**Plain words** When suffering is unavoidable, meaning lives in the attitude you take toward it — the third path. Not avoidance, not masochism: the choice of how to bear it. The last of the human freedoms is choosing your stance in the given circumstance.

*For example:* Two patients with the same ward, the same diagnosis, the same prognosis — one endures it as pure delay, one as something to meet with as much dignity as the situation allows. The illness is identical; the freedom was never the illness's to take.

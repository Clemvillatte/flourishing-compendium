> **Principle 21** — Understand affective presence: your feelings influence others' feelings

Your affect does not stay inside you. People you interact with catch your emotional state (emotional contagion), and over time your average affective presence — whether you make people feel better or worse — becomes a stable trait with real social consequences.

**Plain words** Your affect doesn't stay inside you — people catch your emotional state (contagion), and over time you develop an affective presence: whether you reliably make people feel better or worse. You are weather for others. Know your climate.

*For example:* The team member whose entrance changes the room's temperature — everyone knows it, nobody says it — and the honest question they finally ask: what's it like to be around me? The answers reorganised their whole management style.
## Psychology parallels

- **Affective presence as a stable trait**, some people reliably make others feel better or worse across interactions (Anderson et al., 2003)
- **Emotional contagion is measurable and automatic**, facial mimicry and affective transfer occur without conscious awareness (Hatfield et al., 2014)

## Practice

- Notice the affective trace you leave in a room. Ask a trusted person: "after spending time with me, do you generally feel better or worse?"

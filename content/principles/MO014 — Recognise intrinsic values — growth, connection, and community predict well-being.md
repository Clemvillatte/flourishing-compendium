> **Principle 14** — Recognise intrinsic values: growth, connection, and community predict well-being

Not all values are equal in their effects on well-being. Intrinsic values (personal growth, close relationships, community contribution) are associated with higher well-being. Extrinsic values (money, fame, image) are associated with lower well-being.

**Plain words** Not all values feed you equally — growth, connection, and community predict well-being; money, image, and status predict the treadmill. You can hold both; just know which ones actually pay. Audit the portfolio.

*For example:* The promotion that was supposed to change everything and changed nothing for more than a fortnight — the status value is real, but it's a hunger that eats its own meal.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Intrinsic versus extrinsic goals|Intrinsic vs extrinsic values (Kasser]] & Ryan)**, intrinsic values (growth, connection, community) predict well-being; extrinsic values (money, fame, image) do not (Kasser & Ryan, 1993; Kasser, 2002)
- **Materialism is associated with lower well-being**, prioritising money and image is associated with lower life satisfaction (Kasser, 2002; Dittmar et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia vs external goods]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **The insufficiency of wealth** (Ecclesiastes, 5:10)
- **[[Right livelihood|Right livelihood]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Examine your values: are they intrinsic (growth, connection, contribution) or extrinsic (money, status, image)? Prioritise intrinsic values. When pursuing extrinsic goals, connect them to intrinsic ones.

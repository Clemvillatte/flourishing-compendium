> **Principle 27** — Learn to make progress visible: feedback sustains motivation

Feedback — seeing progress — is essential for motivation. Without feedback, motivation declines. Making progress visible (tracking, measuring, milestones) is a motivational practice.

**Plain words** Motivation starves without visible progress — feedback is food. Unseen progress feels like standing still, and standing still kills engines. Make it visible: charts, tick lists, streaks, logs. Crude, and it works.

*For example:* The savings app that shows the growing bar versus the one that just holds money — the same dollars, one of them motivating. The chart is a motivational prosthesis.
## Psychology parallels

- **Feedback and goal pursuit**, feedback improves performance and motivation (Kluger & DeNisi, 1996; Hattie & Timperley, 2007)
- **Progress principle**, visible progress is the strongest predictor of motivation and affect (Amabile & Kramer, 2011)
- **Goal gradient effect**, motivation increases as progress toward goal increases (Hull, 1932; Kivetz et al., 2006)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires data]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **Know thyself through observation** (Delphic maxim)

## Practice

- Make progress visible: track, measure, use checklists, celebrate milestones. Do not rely on feeling; use data. Visible progress is the fuel of motivation.

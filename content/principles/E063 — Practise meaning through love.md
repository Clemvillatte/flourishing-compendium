> **Principle 455** — Practise meaning through love

Frankl lays out three routes to meaning in Man's Search for Meaning, as reproduced on [Ed Batista's page](https://edbatista.com/2010/04/life.html) (pp. 111-115, edition not stated there):

> We can discover this meaning of life in three different ways: (1) by creating a work or doing a deed; (2) by experiencing something or encountering someone; and (3) by the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering. The first, by way of achievement or accomplishment, is quite obvious.

On the second route he writes:

> Love is the only way to grasp another human being in the innermost core of his personality. No one can become fully aware of the essence of another human being unless he loves him.

On the psychology side, [Diener and Seligman (2002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11894851/) found, in 222 undergraduates, that the very happiest 10% "had stronger romantic and other social relationships than less happy groups", and that "good social relations were necessary" for happiness though "no variable was sufficient".

(Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning, pp. 111-115.)

**Plain words** Frankl claims that loving someone lets you see what they could become. Diener and Seligman show a link between close relationships and happiness, which is not the same as meaning. The study is correlational and used a small student sample.

For example (imagined): a friend says, "I think you'd be good at this", and the idea sticks because they have watched you for years. That is the kind of seeing Frankl describes.

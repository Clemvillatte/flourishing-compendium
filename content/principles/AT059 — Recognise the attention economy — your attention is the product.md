> **Principle 59** — Recognise the attention economy: your attention is the product

Modern digital media is designed to capture and hold attention. Notifications, infinite scroll, autoplay, and variable rewards are engineered to maximise engagement. Understanding this changes how you relate to technology.

**Plain words** Your attention is the product in a trillion-dollar industry — engineered, harvested, auctioned. You're not the customer of the feed; you're the crop. Knowing this changes nothing by itself; acting on it changes everything.

*For example:* The free app that's beautiful, addictive, and costs nothing — because you're not paying with money. Look at what it's farming.
## Psychology parallels

- **Persuasive design and attention capture**, digital products are engineered to capture and hold attention (Fogg, 2003; Eyal, 2014)
- **Variable reward schedules maximise engagement**, intermittent, unpredictable rewards (likes, notifications) create compulsive checking (Skinner, 1953; Rosen et al., 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] includes examined technology** (Socrates, Apology)
- **Right livelihood includes right media use** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Simplicity|Simplicity and frugality]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 19)

## Practice

- Recognise that your attention is the target of engineered design. Do not treat technology as neutral. Set deliberate boundaries: turn off non-essential notifications, use apps with intention, design your usage.

> **Principle 32** — Recognise the endowment effect: you value what you own more

You place a higher value on things simply because you own them. This affects decisions about selling, giving up, or changing — you overvalue the status quo because it is yours.

**Plain words** The endowment effect: you value things more simply because you own them. This distorts decisions about selling, changing, letting go — you overvalue the status quo because it's yours. Run the "would I buy this at this price today?" test.

*For example:* The garage full of "valuable" things nobody would pay anything for — and the house the owner prices $100k above every comparable because it's THEIR house. Ownership re-prices everything it touches. The market's price is the price; yours is a feeling.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Endowment effect|Endowment effect]]**, ownership increases valuation (Kahneman et al., 1990; Thaler, 1980)
- **The effect is robust but moderated by culture and context**, it is weaker in cultures with less individual ownership norms (Maddux et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Non-attachment|Non-attachment to possessions]]** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **[[Simplicity|Simplicity and frugality]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 19)
- **[[Preferred indifferents|Stoic indifference to externals]]** (Epictetus, Enchiridion)

## Practice

- When deciding whether to keep or release something, ask: "if I did not already own this, would I choose to acquire it today?" If no, ownership is driving the valuation.

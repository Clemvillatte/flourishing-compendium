> **Principle 23** — Learn to distinguish self-worth from performance

Your worth as a person is not identical to your performance — your achievements, productivity, or success. Conflating the two creates fragile self-esteem (dependent on outcomes) and unnecessary suffering (when performance dips).

**Plain words** Your worth as a person is not identical to your performance — achievements, productivity, success. Conflating them creates fragile esteem and unnecessary suffering. The dignity is issued at birth; the performance is a separate scoreboard.

*For example:* The person whose value rises and falls with the quarterly numbers — a stock price with a pulse — versus the one who keeps two ledgers: what I produced (variable) and who I am (not for sale). Only one of them can have a bad quarter and a good life.
## Psychology parallels

- **Contingent self-worth on competence predicts stress**, self-worth contingent on achievement is associated with worse outcomes (Crocker & Wolfe, 2001; Crocker, 2002)
- **Performance and self-worth are distinct**, separating them improves resilience and well-being (Neff, 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The inner citadel|The inner citadel]]** (Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations)
- **Dignity is not achievement** (Kant, Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals)
- **[[Non-attachment|Non-attachment to fruits of action]]** (Bhagavad Gita)

## Practice

- Notice when you equate your worth with your performance. Remind yourself: "my worth is not my output." You can care about performance without making it the measure of your value.

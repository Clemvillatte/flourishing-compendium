> **Principle 64** — Recognise intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation: intrinsic predicts well-being

Intrinsic motivation (doing something for its own sake, for interest or value) and extrinsic motivation (doing something for external reward or avoidance) have different outcomes. Intrinsic motivation is associated with better performance, persistence, and well-being.

**Plain words** Doing things for their own sake — interest, meaning, care — predicts well-being. Doing things for reward, image, or approval predicts burnout with a trophy on top. The external stuff isn't evil; it just doesn't nourish. Check what's actually driving your calendar.

*For example:* Two lawyers bill the same hours. One loves the puzzle of the work; one loves the partner track. In year ten, one of them still finds the work interesting and the other has a nice car and a vague dread of Mondays. The car was real; it just wasn't food.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-determination|Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)]]**, intrinsic motivation (autonomous) outperforms extrinsic (controlled) for quality and well-being (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Extrinsic goals do not predict well-being**, pursuing money, fame, and image is associated with lower well-being (Kasser & Ryan, 1993; Kasser, 2002)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia vs external rewards]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **[[Right intention|Right intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Autonomy|Autonomy as need]]** (Rogers)

## Practice

- Notice whether your goals are intrinsic (aligned with values, interesting) or extrinsic (for reward, approval). When possible, pursue intrinsic goals. When extrinsic goals are necessary, connect them to intrinsic values.

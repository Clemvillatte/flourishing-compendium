> **Principle 76** — When habits conflict, let deliberation do its work

Conflicting habits create the hesitation we call deliberation: don't force a snap resolution: let the competing tendencies play out in imagination until one integrates the situation.

Dewey's account of deliberation in Human Nature and Conduct starts from blocked action. When conflicting habits hold overt activity in suspense, each habit and impulse "takes its turn in being tried out" in imagination. His summary assertion is that "deliberation is a dramatic rehearsal (in imagination) of various competing possible lines of action", an experiment in finding "what the various lines of possible action are really like". The trial runs in imagination, not overt fact: "Thought runs ahead and foresees outcomes, and thereby avoids having to await the instruction of actual failure and disaster." An act tried in the world is irrevocable; an act tried in imagination "is not final or fatal. It is retrievable." So deliberation is not the opposite of action but action redirected: "activity is turned from execution into intra-organic channels, resulting in dramatic rehearsal." And the rehearsal ends in the doing: when imagination pictures an outcome that "supplies an adequate stimulus and releases definitive action", choice is made, and "All deliberation is a search for a _way_ to act, not for a final terminus".

(Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, 1922.)

> "Deliberation is a dramatic rehearsal (in imagination) of various competing possible lines of action... Deliberation is an experiment in finding out what the various lines of possible action are really like. It is an experiment in making various combinations of selected elements of habits and impulses, to see what the resultant action would be like if it were entered upon. But the trial is in imagination, not in overt fact." (Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, Part II, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41386))

The compendium's extension: the statement's "don't force a snap resolution" is a rule the compendium draws from Dewey's description; his text describes how deliberation works, and warns against choice by an impulse that "overrides all competitors", without stating a pacing rule.

**Plain words**
The stall is not dysfunction; it is the machine doing its work. Next time a decision hangs, run the competing movies to the end instead of forcing the coin-flip. One of the endings usually starts to feel like yours; that is the decision forming.

*For example:* The compendium's hypothetical: the job offer tug-of-war, ambition pulling one way, family the other. Live each choice vividly for a week and watch which one gathers the rest of your life into it.

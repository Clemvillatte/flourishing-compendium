> **Principle 42** — Follow your own path over borrowed scripts

Svadharma, one's own duty or nature, is the Gita's repeated counsel: better one's own path imperfectly walked than another's perfectly imitated, and Arjuna's tragedy begins with an attempt to abandon his. This is not relativism; the tradition pairs it with the virtue lists that bound it. A historical note: svadharma originally named the duties of one's station in a given social order; the formulation offered here is chosen responsibility, and the classical context is named so the principle is not read as unconditional obedience to given roles. Practically, when a practice works for everyone but not for you, believe your data over the consensus.

(BG 3.35; BG 18.47; BG 18.48, "one should not abandon one's natural work because of its faults.")

**Plain words** Svadharma, your own path: better to walk yours imperfectly than imitate another's perfectly. Arjuna's tragedy begins with trying to abandon his — not because his was better, but because it was his. Not relativism: the tradition pairs it with the virtues.

*For example:* The natural teacher who spent a decade being a mediocre executive because executive was the admired script — and the year they returned to the classroom, everyone recognized what had been misplaced for ten years.

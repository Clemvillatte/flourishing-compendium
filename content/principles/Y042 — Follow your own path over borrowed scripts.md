> **Principle 42** — Follow your own path over borrowed scripts

Svadharma, one's own duty or nature, is the Gita's repeated counsel. In K. T. Telang's 1882 translation, as quoted at [DharmaCode](https://dharmacode.net/guides/bhagavad-gita-verses-on-work):

> One's own duty, though defective, is better than another's duty well performed. Death in (performing) one's own duty is preferable; the (performance of the) duty of others is dangerous. (BG 3.35)

The verse comes back near the end of the poem: "Performing the duty prescribed by nature, one does not incur sin." (BG 18.47). Arjuna's crisis begins with an attempt to abandon his duty as a warrior.

A historical note: svadharma named the duties of one's station in a given social order. My reading is chosen responsibility, and it names that classical context so the principle is not taken as unconditional obedience to given roles. Practically, when a practice works for everyone but not for you, believe your data over the consensus.

(Bhagavad Gita 3.35 and 18.47, trans. Telang, Sacred Books of the East vol. 8.)

### Plain words

Better to walk your own path badly than someone else's path well. This does not mean anything goes, because the tradition sets virtues around it. It means that a script borrowed from someone else's life will cost you more than your own flawed one.

### For example

A natural teacher spends ten years as a mediocre executive because executive was the admired script. In the year they return to the classroom, they are good at their work in a way they had not been.

> **Principle 20** — Know the base rate neglect: you ignore background frequencies

When judging the probability that something is true (a diagnosis, a person's profession), you often focus on how well the case matches a stereotype or description while neglecting the base rate (how common the category is in the population). This leads to systematic errors.

**Plain words** Base rate neglect: when judging probability, you focus on how well the case matches a description and neglect how common the thing actually is. The vividness of the match drowns the background frequency. Always ask: how often does this happen, to people like this?

*For example:* The profile fits the "visionary founder" perfectly — every trait matches — but the base rate of startups failing is unchanged. The description seduces; the denominator decides. The investors who check the denominator keep their money.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Base rate neglect / representativeness heuristic|Base rate neglect / representativeness heuristic]]**, people judge by similarity to a prototype rather than by prior probability (Kahneman & Tversky, 1973; Tversky & Kahneman, 1974)
- **Bayesian reasoning is unnatural but trainable**, explicit training in base rates improves judgment (Gigerenzer & Hoffrage, 1995; Sedlmeier & Gigerenzer, 2001)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The prior probability of the world|The prior probability of the world]]** (Hume, Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding)

## Practice

- When judging how likely something is, first ask: "how common is this category in the relevant population?" Then adjust for the specific case. Do not skip the base rate step.

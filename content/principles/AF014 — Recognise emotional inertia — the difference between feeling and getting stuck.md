> **Principle 14** — Recognise emotional inertia: the difference between feeling and getting stuck

A feeling that does not fluctuate — flat, persistent dysphoria or agitation — is a stronger predictor of depression than a feeling that fluctuates but is intense. Emotional inertia means the affect has stopped responding to the environment; this is a signal, not just a discomfort.

**Plain words** A feeling that does not fluctuate — flat, persistent dysphoria or agitation — predicts depression better than a feeling that's intense but moving. Emotional inertia: the difference between feeling and getting stuck. Watch the flat lines.

*For example:* The friend who cries at the funeral and laughs at the dinner after — healthy fluctuation — versus the one who's been "fine, just fine" for three months. The second one is the one to check on. The flat line is the louder alarm.
## Psychology parallels

- **Autocorrelation of affect predicts depression**, higher inertia in daily negative affect is prospectively associated with depressive symptoms (Kuppens et al., 2010)
- **Inertia is distinct from intensity**, a high-intensity but fluctuating pattern is less predictive than a low-intensity but rigid pattern (Houben et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Track mood daily for 2 weeks. If your negative affect is flat and persistent rather than fluctuating, this is a signal worth acting on.

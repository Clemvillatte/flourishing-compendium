> **Principle 92** — Read the great statements as prompts, not doctrines

The four classical mahavakyas ("consciousness is the absolute" from the Aitareya; "that thou art" from the Chandogya; "I am the absolute" from the Brihadaranyaka; "this self is the absolute" from the Mandukya) are given in the tradition as liberating truths: statements whose full assimilation is held to release the hearer from misidentification, and they are not offered as debate propositions. This book's bounded adaptation: use each as a meditation prompt for a week, and notice what loosens.

(Aitareya Upanishad 3.3; Chandogya 6.8.7; Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10; Mandukya 2.)

**Plain words** The four great statements — "consciousness is the absolute," "that thou art," "I am the absolute," "this self is the absolute" — are given as prompts for inquiry, not doctrines to recite. Use them as questions to stand in, not answers to hold.

*For example:* The practitioner who repeats "that thou art" as a slogan gets nothing but smugness — versus the one who uses it as a probe: "that? this ordinary irritation is what?" — and the statement starts doing its actual work.

> **Principle 72** — Grow by incorporating the other

Whitehead built the idea into his cosmology at the level of the actual occasion, and what gets included becomes constitution: "A positive prehension is the definite inclusion of that item into positive contribution to the subject's own real internal constitution" (Process and Reality, Part II, ch. 1, [text](https://data.openml.dev/books/process-and-reality.pdf)). Mead gives the social version: "The very universality and impersonality of thought and reason is from the behavioristic standpoint the result of the given individual taking the attitudes of others toward himself, and of his finally crystallizing all these particular attitudes into a single attitude or standpoint which may be called that of the 'generalized other'" (Mind, Self and Society, p. 90, [text](https://archive.org/download/mead-1934/mead%201934.pdf)).

> "Accordingly I have adopted the term 'prehension,' to express the activity whereby an actual entity effects its own concretion of other things." (Whitehead, Process and Reality, Part II, ch. 1, [text](https://data.openml.dev/books/process-and-reality.pdf))

Whitehead is describing actual occasions, not people, and Mead is describing the genesis of thought. Neither prescribes a personal practice. The compendium's exercise is a proposed application: when something unfamiliar is resisted, treat the resistance as information about the current structure, and ask what would have to be reorganized for the new material to be taken in.

### Plain words

An occasion of experience takes in what is around it and becomes what it took in. You are built from what you have absorbed: people, places, ideas, food, all of it taken in and reorganized. Growth is digestion of what is not yet you, and the unfamiliar is the only thing on the menu that changes the structure.

### For example

Take the year abroad that reorganized a person's taste in food, friends and furniture. Nothing was added from nowhere; the unfamiliar was absorbed and became structure. The exercise is to name one thing currently outside your range, a cuisine, a discipline, a person's view, and ask not whether you approve of it but what your repertoire would look like once it was digested. The exercise does not promise the digestion is comfortable.

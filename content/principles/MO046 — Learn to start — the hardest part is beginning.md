> **Principle 46** — Learn to start: the hardest part is beginning

The resistance to starting a task is usually greater than the task itself. Once started, motivation often follows. The skill is starting despite not feeling motivated.

**Plain words** Starting is the hard part — the resistance before the task is almost always bigger than the task. Once in motion, motivation tends to show up late and take the credit. So don't wait for it; just begin badly.

*For example:* The essay that looms for a week and takes forty minutes once started — the week was the real cost. The dread billed you 90% and delivered 10%.
## Psychology parallels

- **The Zeigarnik effect**, started tasks create a tension that motivates completion (Zeigarnik, 1927; Masicampo & Baumeister, 2011)
- **Action precedes motivation**, starting a behaviour often generates motivation rather than requiring it (Jacobson et al., 1996; Martell et al., 2001)
- **[[Behavioral activation|Behavioural activation]]**, doing the activity generates motivation (Dimidjian et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The journey begins with a single step|The journey begins with a single step]]** (Laozi, Daodejing 64)
- **[[Virtue|Virtue as action]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort: begin]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Start despite not feeling like it. Use the "5-minute rule": commit to just 5 minutes. Once started, you will often continue. Motivation follows action.

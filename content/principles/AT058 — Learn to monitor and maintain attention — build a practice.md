> **Principle 58** — Learn to monitor and maintain attention: build a practice

Attentional capacity is not a one-time achievement; it requires ongoing maintenance. Regular practice, environmental design, and periodic assessment maintain attentional quality over time.

**Plain words** Attention is a garden, not a purchase — it needs tending or it weeds over. A short daily practice, a protected hour, a weekly audit of your environment. Stop tending and the drift starts within weeks, quietly.

*For example:* The meditator who stops for a month and finds, by week three, the phone's back at breakfast. No drama — just untended ground returning to its default crop.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional skills degrade without practice**, like physical fitness, attention requires ongoing maintenance (Ericsson et al., 1993; Newport, 2016)
- **Regular meditation maintains attentional capacity**, sustained practice is more effective than intensive but brief training (Tang et al., 2015; Goleman & Davidson, 2017)

- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] as ongoing practice** (Socrates, Apology 38a)

## Practice

- Build a maintenance routine: daily attention practice (meditation, deep work, single-tasking), weekly assessment (how is my attention?), periodic environmental cleanup (remove accumulated distractions).

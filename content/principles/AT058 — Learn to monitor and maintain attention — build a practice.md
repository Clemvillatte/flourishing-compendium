> **Principle 58** — Learn to monitor and maintain attention: build a practice

The closest evidence I found links attention-related capacity to how much people keep practising. Jha and colleagues followed two military cohorts through a high-stress period before deployment. One group took an 8-week mindfulness course and logged its practice time, and the other did not. Working memory capacity "degraded in the MC group", the military control group. In the trained group, it fell for those with little practice and rose for those with a lot:

> In the MT group, WMC decreased over time in those with low MT practice time, but increased in those with high practice time.
>
> Jha et al., Emotion (2010)

The groups were small (31 and 17) and were not randomly assigned. Working memory capacity is close to attention without being the same thing. I found no study among those I opened showing that attention fades at a set rate once practice stops.

(Jha et al., 2010.)

**Plain words** One course or one burst of effort does not set your attention for good. In this study, people who practised a lot improved their working memory, and people under heavy load who practised little or not at all saw it slip. Treat attention as something to look after at a steady pace. Checking in regularly on how well you are focusing, and on what is getting in the way, shows you when to adjust.

For example (imagined): you keep a short note each Friday on how many hours of focused work you managed and what interrupted them. After three weeks the pattern shows you which change to try next.

## Sources

- Jha, Stanley, Kiyonaga, Wong & Gelfand (2010), "Examining the protective effects of mindfulness training on working memory capacity and affective experience", Emotion 10:54-64: [PubMed 20141302](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20141302/)

## Psychology parallels

- **Working memory capacity fell in a high-stress control group and in low-practice trainees, and rose with high practice time**, in a non-randomised military study (Jha et al., 2010)
- **More practice time went with lower negative affect and higher positive affect**, in the same study (Jha et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Build a maintenance routine: daily attention practice (meditation, deep work, single-tasking), weekly assessment (how is my attention?), periodic environmental cleanup (remove accumulated distractions).

> **Principle 22** — Learn to use opposite action: do the opposite of what the emotion urges

Opposite action is a core DBT skill, and Linehan's manual defines it plainly, verbatim:

> "Opposite action is changing your emotions by acting opposite to your emotional urge to do something." (Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual, 2nd edition, [Guilford reproducible teaching notes](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-3teaching.pdf?t=1))

The skill is conditional, not universal. The teaching notes state the condition and the promise, verbatim: "When emotions do not fit the facts, and knowing the facts does not change your emotion, then acting opposite to your emotions—all the way, repeatedly—will change your emotional reactions." When the fear fits the facts, the manual's example is a child running into traffic: experience the fear, do not suppress it. The skill is for emotions that do not fit.

The demand is completeness, verbatim: "When you do opposite action, do it all the way. This means opposite posture, facial expression, thinking, what you say, and how you say it. 'Halfway' opposite action does not work."

**Plain words** First check whether the emotion fits the facts. If it does, acting on the urge may be the right move. If it does not, do the opposite of the urge with your whole body: posture, face, voice, words. Keep at it until the feeling moves. Halfway does not count.

*For example:* Suppose the urge after a conflict is to withdraw and wait to be pursued. Whether opposite action fits depends on the facts: if the fear of abandonment does not fit them, the opposite action might be a message that says you are still in this. The skill is conditional, not automatic, and it does not imply renewed contact is appropriate after every conflict.

## Practice

- When an emotion urges a behaviour, first check whether the emotion fits the facts. If it does not, do the opposite of the urge: fear says avoid → approach; sadness says withdraw → engage; anger says attack → gently avoid and be kind; shame says hide → show up. Do it all the way, and repeat until the emotion eases.

> **Principle 22** — Learn to use opposite action: do the opposite of what the emotion urges

Opposite action involves deliberately doing the opposite of what an emotion urges (approach when fear says avoid, engage when sadness says withdraw, step toward when anger says attack). This is a core DBT skill.

**Plain words** Emotions come with action urges — and sometimes the healthiest move is to do the exact opposite. Fear says flee: approach. Anger says attack: step closer, speak low. The emotion's advice is about the past; the opposite action is about the life you want.

*For example:* The urge to withdraw after a fight, and the opposite action — sending the "I'm still in this" text. The fear predicted abandonment; the action rewrote the scene.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Opposite action (Linehan)|Opposite action (Linehan)]]**, acting opposite to the emotion's urge reduces the emotion (Linehan, 1993; Rizvi & Linehan, 2005)
- **Opposite action for fear**, anger, sadness, shame, guilt, each emotion has a specific opposite action (Linehan, 1993)

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as the mean between extremes]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Right action|Right action over instinct]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[The courage to act against impulse|The courage to act against impulse]]** (Kant, Groundwork)

## Practice

- When an emotion urges a behaviour, do the opposite. Fear says avoid → approach. Sadness says withdraw → engage. Anger says attack → step toward. Shame says hide → show up. Do this deliberately.

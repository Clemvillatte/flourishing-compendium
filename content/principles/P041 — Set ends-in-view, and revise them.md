> **Principle 41** — Set ends-in-view, and revise them

Dewey's argument in Human Nature and Conduct is that ends are not places you arrive: "Ends are foreseen consequences which arise in the course of activity and which are employed to give activity added meaning and to direct its further course. They are in no sense ends of action. In being ends of deliberation they are redirecting pivots in action." The last move is the pragmatist one: "In a strict sense an end-in-view is a means in present action; present action is not a means to a remote end."

> "Men do not shoot because targets exist, but they set up targets in order that throwing and shooting may be more effective and significant." (Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/41386))

The compendium applies the target line to the five-year plan: the plan is equipment for the present acting, and its revision is the system working, not failing.

**Plain words**
A goal on Dewey's account is more like a target than a destination: something set up so that today's shooting has direction, and moved when the shooting changes. The test of a goal is what it does to this week's action. When the action produces information the goal did not expect, the goal moves; that is the design, not a defect.

*For example:* Suppose a five-year plan revised in year two. As a modern application, the revision is the outcome Dewey predicts: the acting produced information, and the end-in-view was redirected.

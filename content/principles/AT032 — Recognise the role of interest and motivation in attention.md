> **Principle 32** — Recognise the role of interest and motivation in attention

The opened sources are Hidi and Renninger's four-phase model and a later chapter by Renninger and Hidi expanding on it. The model's phases:

> "The model describes 4 phases in the development and deepening of learner interest: triggered situational interest, maintained situational interest, emerging (less-developed) individual interest, and well-developed individual interest." ([Hidi & Renninger, 2006, Educational Psychologist 41, abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ736298))

The chapter states the interest-attention link directly:

> "The presence of even some interest beneficially affects individuals' attention and memory, as well as their motivation and meaningful engagement." ([Renninger & Hidi, 2019, chapter abstract](https://works.swarthmore.edu/fac-education/150/))

And the scaffolding finding:

> "We review findings which provide evidence that the structure of tasks and activities, as well as interactions with other people, may be helpful to interest development..." ([Renninger & Hidi, 2019, same abstract](https://works.swarthmore.edu/fac-education/150/))

The model and chapter concern interest development in learners; the compendium's reading for attention is its own application.

**Plain words** The chapter's evidence: even some interest helps attention, memory, and engagement, and interest can be triggered and then maintained by task structure and by other people. The upshot for attention: interest is not a fixed trait of the task. A task with no interest yet needs external scaffolding — structure, stakes, company — and the chapter's evidence is that such supports help interest develop.

*For example:* (Imagined case.) Someone dreading their tax return picks one concrete question to answer first — what the work-from-home allowance actually covered — and works from there. The question supplies the interest; the return gets finished in the process.

## Practice

- When a task is boring, either (a) find something interesting or meaningful in it, (b) make it more engaging (gamify, challenge, novelty), or (c) accept that it will require more effort and plan accordingly.

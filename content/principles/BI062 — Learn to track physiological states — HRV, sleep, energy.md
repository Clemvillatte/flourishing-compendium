> **Principle 62** — Learn to track physiological states: HRV, sleep, energy

Each of the three tracks carries a different level of trust. For HRV, a meta-analysis linking heart rate variability to brain function proposes that HRV may provide an index of how strongly top-down appraisals shape brainstem activity and autonomic responses ([Thayer et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22178086/)), and a methods review notes HRV indexes cardiac vagal tone, which is linked to self-regulation at the cognitive, emotional, social and health levels ([Laborde et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28265249/)). For sleep, a laboratory comparison of seven consumer trackers with polysomnography found high sensitivity for detecting sleep but low-to-medium specificity for wake, inconsistent sleep-stage estimates, and worse performance on disrupted nights ([Chinoy et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33378539/)).

> "Heart rate variability may provide an index of how strongly 'top-down' appraisals, mediated by cortical-subcortical pathways, shape brainstem activity and autonomic responses in the body." (Abstract; Thayer et al., 2012, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 36(2): 747-56, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22178086/))

> "Cardiac vagal tone, which represents the contribution of the parasympathetic nervous system to cardiac regulation, is acknowledged to be linked with many phenomena relevant for psychophysiological research, including self-regulation at the cognitive, emotional, social, and health levels." (Abstract; Laborde et al., 2017, Front Psychol 8: 213, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28265249/))

> "Overall, epoch-by-epoch sensitivity was high (all ≥0.93), specificity was low-to-medium (0.18-0.54), sleep stage comparisons were mixed, and devices tended to perform worse on nights with poorer/disrupted sleep." (Abstract, Results; Chinoy et al., 2021, Sleep 44(5): zsaa291, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33378539/))

**Plain words**
A tracker is a mirror with known distortions, and the distortions differ by what is being tracked. The HRV number is a real physiological marker whose interpretation is easy to get wrong ([Laborde et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28265249/)). The sleep tracker finds sleep well but calls much of your lying-awake "sleep", and its stage estimates are not worth trusting ([Chinoy et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33378539/)). The honest use is pattern-watching over weeks, not single-number readings. Energy is a subjective log, and the log's value is that it has no theory to defend.

**For example**
(Imagined) Someone who believes they "sleep fine" wears a tracker for a month: the tracker agrees about the sleep, disagrees about the wake-ups, and the month of notes shows the pattern the impressions missed. The tracker did not settle the question; it made the pattern visible.

## Practice

- Track physiology if you find it useful: an HRV app or wearable for HRV, sleep duration from a tracker with its known biases, and a simple energy log. Use the patterns over weeks rather than single readings to adjust sleep, exercise and stress management.

> **Principle 56** — Learn to use attentional cues and rituals: structure supports focus

Wood and Neal's model of habit gives the mechanism behind cues: "Habits emerge from the gradual learning of associations between responses and the features of performance contexts that have historically covaried with them (e.g., physical settings, preceding actions). Once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal." (Wood & Neal 2007, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17907866/)). A fixed desk, hour and opening move are the "physical settings" and "preceding actions" in that sentence.

Building that association takes time. In a 12-week study of 96 volunteers who repeated a chosen behaviour in the same context, "the time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days", and "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process" (Lally et al. 2010, [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsp.674)).

On rituals specifically, the evidence is thinner than it looks. A 2016 study (Brooks et al.) claiming that rituals improve performance by reducing anxiety has been retracted: its authors "no longer have confidence in the integrity of the data" ([retraction notice](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074959781630437X)). A small pre-registered study found that one week of an arbitrary ritual reduced a brain marker of error monitoring, with "no evidence that the reduction in neural monitoring led to performance deficit (nor a performance improvement)" (Hobson et al. 2017, [PeerJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5452956/)).

**Plain words**
A cue is anything that regularly comes right before you start, such as the same desk, the same hour or the same first action. Repeat it in the same setting and the start gets easier without needing a decision each time. Give it weeks, not days, and one missed day will not undo it. Treat the ritual part as a comfort that might help rather than a proven lever, since a 2016 study claiming it works was retracted.

*For example:* Every morning you clear the desk, open the same notebook and write one sentence before checking messages. This is an imagined routine. In Wood and Neal's terms, the desk and the notebook are the context cues that, after enough repetitions, trigger the starting response without a fresh decision.

## Psychology parallels

- **Context cues and habit**, repeated responses become linked to the settings where they occur and are triggered by them (Wood & Neal, 2007)
- **Time to form a habit**, automaticity took 18 to 254 days to plateau in daily behaviours repeated in a stable context (Lally et al., 2010)
- **Ritual and error monitoring**, one week of an arbitrary ritual lowered a neural marker of error monitoring without changing task performance (Hobson et al., 2017)

## Practice

- Create attentional rituals: a specific workspace, a specific time, an opening ritual (clear desk, specific music, candle). Use the same cues consistently to condition focused states.

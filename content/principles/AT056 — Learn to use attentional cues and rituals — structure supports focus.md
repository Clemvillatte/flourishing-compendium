> **Principle 56** — Learn to use attentional cues and rituals: structure supports focus

Attentional cues (specific location, time, objects, rituals) signal the brain to enter a specific attentional mode. A dedicated workspace, a specific time, a ritual (lighting a candle, opening a specific notebook) all condition attentional states.

**Plain words** Cues and rituals are starting pistols for attention: same desk, same hour, same coffee, and the brain learns the pattern. Structure isn't a cage; it's a runway. Focus takes off from ritual.

*For example:* The writer who lights the same candle and opens the same document every morning. Silly? Maybe. But the brain reads the ritual and starts the engines before the first sentence.
## Psychology parallels

- **Context-dependent attention and performance**, specific environments and rituals cue specific attentional states (Smith & Vela, 2001; Wood & Neal, 2007)
- **Rituals improve performance and reduce anxiety**, pre-performance rituals improve focus and reduce anxiety (Brooks et al., 2016; Hobson et al., 2017)

## Practice

- Create attentional rituals: a specific workspace, a specific time, an opening ritual (clear desk, specific music, candle). Use the same cues consistently to condition focused states.

> **Principle 71** — Recognise the peak-end rule: memory of experiences is shaped by peaks and endings

You do not remember the average of an experience; you remember its peak (most intense moment) and its end. This has direct implications for how you design experiences, both for yourself and for others.

**Plain words** The peak-end rule: you don't remember the average of an experience — you remember its peak and its end. Design experiences accordingly: engineer a high moment and a good ending into the things you'll remember.

*For example:* The family holiday that was 80% fine and 20% chaos — and the parents' deliberate fix: the last night's good dinner, the ritual goodbye to the beach. What the kids will remember is the ending. Design it.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Peak-end rule|Peak-end rule]]**, retrospective evaluation of experiences is disproportionately shaped by the peak and the final moments (Kahneman et al., 1993; Redelmeier & Kahneman, 1996)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The whole is in the part** (Whitehead, Process and Reality)
- **[[Ending as completion|Ending as completion]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I)

## Practice

- When designing an experience (a trip, a project, a gathering), invest in the peak and the ending. A strong ending can redeem a mediocre middle.

> **Principle 71** — Recognise the peak-end rule: memory of experiences is shaped by peaks and endings

In the original cold-water experiment, participants held a hand in 14 degree water for 60 seconds, and in a second trial for 60 seconds plus 30 more seconds as the water warmed slightly. A significant majority chose to repeat the longer trial, "apparently preferring more pain over less". The authors concluded that evaluations of aversive experiences "are often dominated by the discomfort at the worst and at the final moments of episodes" ([Kahneman et al. 1993](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x)). In a randomised colonoscopy trial of 682 outpatients, those whose procedure was extended with a milder ending rated the final moments as less painful (1.7 vs 2.5) and the whole experience as less unpleasant (4.4 vs 4.9) ([Redelmeier, Katz and Kahneman 2003](https://sparq.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj19021/files/media/file/redelmeier_et_al._2003_-_memories_of_colonoscopy.pdf)).

The claim that you do not remember the average needs one correction. A meta-analysis of 174 effect sizes found a large peak-end effect (r = 0.581) that was "comparable to the effect of the overall average (mean) score", and stronger than that of trend, variability, the beginning or duration, which was "essentially nil" ([Alaybek et al. 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v170y2022ics0749597822000334.html)). So peaks and ends matter a lot, but so does the average.

### Scope

I did not open Kahneman's 1993 full text or the Redelmeier and Kahneman 1996 paper, and the evidence concerns ratings of specific episodes, such as pain, films and workdays. I found no study of family holidays, so the seed's holiday example is dropped.

**Plain words**
When you look back on an experience, its worst or best moment and its last moments weigh heavily, along with the overall average. Duration counts for little.

**For example**
When you plan a long meeting or a hard task, decide in advance how it will end, and let the last few minutes be the gentlest.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Peak-end rule|Peak-end rule]]**, retrospective evaluation of experiences is disproportionately shaped by the peak and the final moments (Kahneman et al., 1993; Redelmeier & Kahneman, 1996)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The whole is in the part** (Whitehead, Process and Reality)
- **[[Ending as completion|Ending as completion]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I)

## Practice

- When designing an experience (a trip, a project, a gathering), invest in the peak and the ending. A strong ending can redeem a mediocre middle.

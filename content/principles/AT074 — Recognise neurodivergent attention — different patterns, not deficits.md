> **Principle 74** — Recognise neurodivergent attention: different patterns, not deficits

Two opened sources carry the principle's evidence. On autism, Happé and Frith's review of the "weak coherence" account explicitly reframes the deficit reading:

> "'Weak central coherence' refers to the detail-focused processing style proposed to characterise autism spectrum disorders (ASD)." ([Happé & Frith, 2006, Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders 36, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16450045/))

> "The original suggestion of a core deficit in central processing resulting in failure to extract global form/meaning, has been challenged in three ways. First, it may represent an outcome of superiority in local processing. Second, it may be a processing bias, rather than deficit." ([Happé & Frith, 2006, same abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16450045/))

> "A review of over 50 empirical studies of coherence suggests robust findings of local bias in ASD, with mixed findings regarding weak global processing." ([Happé & Frith, 2006, same abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16450045/))

On ADHD, Brown's summary, opened on the interest-based-attention page:

> "Every child or adult with ADHD whom I have ever evaluated has a few favored activities in which they have no problem focusing, sustaining effort, and utilizing working memory." ([Brown, 2013](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/kids-and-adhd/201304/the-truth-about-adhd))

The studies establish variation in pattern — a local bias, an interest-gated focus — not the value claim that these are gifts. The compendium's strengths framing is its own application.

**Plain words** The autism research itself made the principle's move: what looked like a deficit in extracting the global picture may instead be superiority in local processing — a bias, not a broken part. The ADHD account says focus is interest-gated, not absent. In both cases the pattern differs; whether it disables depends on the environment's fit.

*For example:* (Imagined case.) An autistic engineer who cannot work in the open-plan office but out-focuses the department from the quiet room: the pattern is not broken; the office was.

## Practice

- If you are neurodivergent, do not judge your attentional patterns by neurotypical standards. Learn your own pattern and design around it: detail-focus, interest-driven, non-linear. Build on strengths rather than fighting differences.

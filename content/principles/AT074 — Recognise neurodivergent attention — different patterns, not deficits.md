> **Principle 74** — Recognise neurodivergent attention: different patterns, not deficits

Autistic, ADHD, dyslexic, and other neurodivergent attentional patterns differ from neurotypical patterns. These are variations, not deficits. Treating them as disordered creates unnecessary suffering and missed strengths.

**Plain words** Neurodivergent attention isn't a deficit version of standard — it's a different architecture with different defaults, different drains, and different gifts. Managing it means learning your own machine's manual, not borrowing everyone else's.

*For example:* The autistic engineer who can't do the open-plan office but out-focuses the whole department from the quiet room — the pattern isn't broken; it's specialised.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Neurodiversity paradigm|Neurodiversity paradigm]]**, cognitive and attentional variation is normal and valuable (Singer, 1998; Kapp et al., 2013)
- **Autistic attention: detail-focused**, autistic individuals often show strength in detail-oriented processing and pattern recognition (Happé & Frith, 2006)
- **ADHD: interest-based attention**, attention driven by interest and novelty (Brown, 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Neurodiversity|Neurodiversity as variation]]** (Singer, 1998)
- **Different, not less** (Grandin, Thinking in Pictures)
- **The tree that is not judged by the forest** (Zhuangzi)

## Practice

- If you are neurodivergent, do not judge your attentional patterns by neurotypical standards. Learn your own pattern and design around it: detail-focus, interest-driven, non-linear. Build on strengths rather than fighting differences.

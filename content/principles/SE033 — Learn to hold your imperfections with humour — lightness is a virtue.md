> **Principle 33** — Learn to hold your imperfections with humour: lightness is a virtue

Taking yourself too seriously — being unable to laugh at your own flaws, contradictions, and absurdities — is a form of self-rigidity. Lightness (the capacity to see yourself with humour) is a virtue and is associated with better well-being.

**Plain words** Taking yourself too seriously — being unable to laugh at your own flaws, contradictions, and absurdities — is self-rigidity. Lightness is a virtue: the capacity to see yourself with humour loosens the death-grip on the self-image. Laugh at yourself; it's the cheapest liberation.

*For example:* The person who can't mention their own mistakes without the full defence brief — versus the one who opens the meeting with "I have three slides of evidence that I misread the spreadsheet." The lightness isn't unserious; it's the confidence that the self survives its own comedy.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-deprecating humour and well-being**, the ability to laugh at oneself is associated with better mental health (Martin, 2007; Kuiper et al., 1992)
- **Humour as coping**, adaptive humour styles are associated with better outcomes (Martin, 2007; Dozois et al., 2009)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The lightness of being|The lightness of being]]** (Kundera, The Unbearable Lightness of Being)
- **[[Zhuangzi's laughter|Zhuangzi's laughter]]** (Daoism, Zhuangzi)
- **The fool as wise** (Shakespeare, King Lear's fool)

## Practice

- Practise lightness: notice your own absurdities, contradictions, and flaws with humour rather than contempt. Self-deprecating (but not self-denigrating) humour is a healthy self-relational skill.

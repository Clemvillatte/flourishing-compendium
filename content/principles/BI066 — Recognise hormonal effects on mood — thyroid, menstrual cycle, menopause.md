> **Principle 66** — Recognise hormonal effects on mood: thyroid, menstrual cycle, menopause

Hormones (thyroid, oestrogen, progesterone, testosterone) affect mood. Thyroid disorders, premenstrual changes, perimenopause, and menopause can all produce mood symptoms.

**Plain words** Hormones move mood — thyroid, menstrual cycle, perimenopause, menopause, testosterone. The "anxiety" that arrives with the cycle is chemistry with a calendar. Track the mood against the biology; the correlation is information.

*For example:* The mood log with a monthly rhythm that no amount of journaling explained — but the calendar did. Perimenopause can produce depression, rage, and despair that are treatable. The biology isn't a mood; it's a mechanism.
## Psychology parallels

- **Hormones and mood**, thyroid, menstrual cycle, and menopausal changes affect mood (Schmidt et al., 2015; Sundström-Poromaa, 2018)
- **Perinatal mood disorders**, pregnancy and postpartum involve specific hormonal mood effects (Stuart-Parrigon & Stuart, 2014; Yim et al., 2015)

## Practice

- If you have mood changes related to hormonal cycles (menstrual, perimenopausal, postpartum, thyroid), recognise these as physiological. Seek medical evaluation. Do not treat hormonal mood changes as purely psychological.

> **Principle 55** — Learn to express gratitude behaviourally: thank others

Expressing gratitude to others (not just feeling it) is a specific social behaviour that improves both the giver's and receiver's well-being and strengthens relationships.

**Plain words** Gratitude works when it's behavioural — felt gratitude is a mood; expressed gratitude is an intervention. Thank the person, specifically, out loud. Both parties get the dose.

*For example:* The thank-you text that names the exact thing — "you didn't have to drive me, and you did" — reread by the receiver for days. The feeling was private; the sentence did work.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Gratitude|Gratitude expression (Emmons, Seligman)]]**, expressing gratitude to others increases well-being (Emmons & McCullough, 2003; Seligman et al., 2005)
- **Gratitude letter intervention**, writing and delivering a gratitude letter increases well-being (Seligman et al., 2005; Wood et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Gratitude|Gratitude as virtue]]** (Stoics, Seneca, On Benefits)
- **[[Gratitude|Counting blessings]]** (Cicero)
- **[[Right speech|Right speech: gratitude]]** (Buddhism)

## Practice

- Express gratitude behaviourally: thank people specifically ("thank you for doing X, it made a difference"), write and deliver gratitude letters, acknowledge contributions. Do not just feel grateful; express it.

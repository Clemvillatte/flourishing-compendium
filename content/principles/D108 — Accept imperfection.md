> **Principle 108** — Accept imperfection

Chapter 45 of the Daodejing runs a list of reversals: what is fullest seems empty, what is straightest seems bent, the greatest art seems stupid, eloquence a stammering scream. In Legge's rendering the opening line reads as a counsel: "Who thinks his great achievements poor / Shall find his vigour long endure."

> Who thinks his great achievements poor
>   Shall find his vigour long endure.
> Of greatest fulness, deemed a void,
>   Exhaustion ne'er shall stem the tide.
> Do thou what's straight still crooked deem;
>   Thy greatest art still stupid seem,
> And eloquence a stammering scream.
>
> (Daodejing 45, Legge)

Legge links regarding great achievements as poor with vigour that endures. The compendium reads this as permission to acknowledge shortcomings without treating an achievement as worthless.

(DDJ 45.)

**Plain words**
A finished piece can still have faults. Decide which shortcomings prevent it from doing its job. You can acknowledge the others without hiding them or postponing completion indefinitely.

*For example:* Imagine an essay whose argument is clear but whose transitions you would still like to improve. You decide it is ready to share and note the transitions for a later revision.

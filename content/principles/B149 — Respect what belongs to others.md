> **Principle 149** — Respect what belongs to others

In AN 10.176, Cunda describes purification rituals and the Buddha answers with an account of conduct through body, speech and mind. Refraining from taking what is not given appears under skilful bodily action ([Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an10/an10.176.than.html)).

> Abandoning the taking of what is not given, he abstains from taking what is not given.

(AN 10.176, [Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an10/an10.176.than.html).)

The next sentence specifies things belonging to others in a village or wilderness, taken in the manner of a thief ([Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an10/an10.176.than.html)). Applying the principle to credit for work or demands on someone's time is a further ethical comparison. It should not be mistaken for a literal list of possessions in this passage.

**Plain words** Being able to reach something does not settle whether you may take it. Ask what was offered and for what purpose. If the answer is unclear, ask the person before using it.

*For example:* Imagine finding a colleague's notebook in a shared meeting room. You keep it aside so they can retrieve it, rather than assuming it is available for your own notes. The shared room does not make the notebook shared property.

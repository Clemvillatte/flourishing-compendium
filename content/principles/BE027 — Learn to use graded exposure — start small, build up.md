> **Principle 27** — Learn to use graded exposure: start small, build up

Graded exposure is a clinical workhorse. In Prolonged Exposure, a manualized treatment for PTSD, the client and provider first make a list of situations the client has been avoiding, then work through it step by step. The National Center for PTSD describes both moves directly: the list-making, and the grading:

> "You will work with your provider to make a list of people, places, or activities that you have stayed away from since your trauma. Over the course of therapy, you will work through your list step-by-step, gradually confronting these situations. […] You will start with activities that are manageable for you, and you will work up to activities that are more challenging." (National Center for PTSD, [Prolonged Exposure (PE) for PTSD](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/understand_tx/prolonged_exposure.asp))

The modern learning-theory account adds a caution that matches this principle's Plain words. In the inhibitory-learning model, extinction does not erase the original fear association: "the original CS-US association learned during fear conditioning is not erased during extinction, but rather is left intact as new, secondary inhibitory learning about the CS-US develops." What exposure builds is a competing expectation — which is why the aim is a different relationship with the fear rather than its disappearance.

**Plain words**
Build a ladder of feared situations and climb it from the bottom — least scary first, one rung at a time. The aim is a different relationship with the fear, not its disappearance. For significant anxiety, build the ladder with a clinician.

**For example**
As an everyday illustration: the socially anxious person's hierarchy: coffee with one friend → small gathering → the work party. Each rung is a data point ("I did it"), and the data, not the fear level, is what counts.

## Practice

- Create a fear hierarchy: list feared situations from least to most frightening. Start with the easiest, approach it repeatedly until anxiety decreases, then move up. Do not start with the hardest.

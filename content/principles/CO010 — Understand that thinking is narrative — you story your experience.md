> **Principle 10** — Understand that thinking is narrative: you story your experience

Humans do not just process information; they construct narratives. Your memories, identity, and understanding of events take narrative form. The story you tell about your life shapes how you experience it.

**Plain words** Humans don't just process information — they construct narratives. Your memories, identity, and understanding of events take story form, and the story you tell shapes how you experience what happens next. You are a storytelling animal; edit accordingly.

*For example:* The same failure told two ways — "I'm someone whose plans collapse" versus "I'm someone who attempts hard things and learns" — and the next attempt is genuinely different: the second narrator takes the risk, the first doesn't. The facts never changed; the story ran the show.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Narrative identity|Narrative identity]]**, people construct life stories that shape identity and well-being (McAdams, 2001; McAdams & McLean, 2013)
- **Redemption sequences in life narratives predict well-being**, life stories with redemption (bad leads to good) are associated with higher well-being (McAdams et al., 2001)
- **[[Narrative therapy|Narrative therapy]]**, changing the story changes the experience (White & Epston, 1990)

## Practice

- Notice the story you are telling about a difficult event. Is it a story of loss, growth, redemption, or stuckness? The facts may not change, but the narrative arc can.

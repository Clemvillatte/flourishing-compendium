> **Principle 10** — Understand that thinking is narrative: you story your experience

The research backbone is Dan McAdams's life story model of identity: people provide their lives with unity and purpose by constructing internalized and evolving narratives of the self. The memories, the identity and the account of events come in story form.

> people living in modern societies provide their lives with unity and purpose by constructing internalized and evolving narratives of the self. (McAdams, "The Psychology of Life Stories", Review of General Psychology 5(2), 2001, [abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/1089-2680.5.2.100))

The same review ties the story to the rest of psychology: self-understanding, autobiographical memory, personality structure and change, and the relation between individual lives and cultural modernity. The story is the format in which experience is held, from what a memory means to what a life adds up to.

**Plain words**
Everybody explains their life to themselves as a running story: what happened, who did it, what it means, where it is heading. The story is doing real work. It decides which events count and what they were. Two people can hold the same event with different stories attached, and the stories, not the events, set what each does next. When the story is bad, the thing to examine is the story.

**For example**
A failed course told as "my plans always collapse" leads to not signing up again. The same failure told as "I attempted something hard and learned what broke" leads to a second attempt with the fix. The facts did not change; the story picked the next move.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Narrative identity|Narrative identity]]**, people construct life stories that shape identity and well-being (McAdams, 2001; McAdams & McLean, 2013)
- **Redemption sequences in life narratives predict well-being**, life stories with redemption (bad leads to good) are associated with higher well-being (McAdams et al., 2001)
- **[[Narrative therapy|Narrative therapy]]**, changing the story changes the experience (White & Epston, 1990)

## Practice

- Notice the story you are telling about a difficult event. Is it a story of loss, growth, redemption, or stuckness? The facts may not change, but the narrative arc can.

> **Principle 62** — Treat social capital as a community resource

Treat social capital — the networks, norms, and trust that enable collective action — as a community resource. Robert Putnam (2000) documented the decline of social capital in America (Bowling Alone); the health literature shows that social capital predicts health outcomes (Kawachi et al., 1999).

**Plain words** Social capital — networks, norms, trust — is a community resource that can be built and spent. It's been declining for decades. You build it with boring things: showing up, joining, reciprocating. The compound interest is real.

*For example:* The bowling league, the church committee, the parents' group — the "pointless" meetings that turn out to be the infrastructure of a functioning neighbourhood. Decline them all and find out what they were holding up.

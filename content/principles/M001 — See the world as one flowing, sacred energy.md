> **Principle 1** — See the world as one flowing, sacred energy

The tlamatinime's metaphysics, as reconstructed by James Maffie, centers on teotl. He presents the founding thesis this way:

> "At the heart of Nahua philosophy stands the thesis that there exists a single, dynamic, vivifying, eternally self-generating and self-regenerating sacred power, energy or force: what the Nahuas called teotl." (Maffie, "Aztec Philosophy", [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/aztec-philosophy/))

What humans call nature (heavens, earth, rain, people, trees, rocks) is generated by teotl as moments of its endless self-generation. Maffie's gloss on the consequence: "Nahua metaphysics is processive. Process, movement, becoming and transmutation are essential attributes of teotl." There are no static things, strictly speaking; there are movements of the one process. For reflection, the compendium adapts this account into a question: what is changing in your circumstances, and what needs your attention now?

**Plain words**
One old Nahua way of seeing it: there is no stuff sitting still anywhere — one sacred energy folding itself into everything, and you are one of the folds.

*For example (imagined):* Someone in a bad year keeps waiting for their "real life" to resume. The teotl view reframes the wait: everything, including them, is already movement.

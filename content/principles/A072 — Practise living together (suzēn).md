> **Principle 72** — Practise living together (suzēn)

Friendship is shared life rather than affection alone. Friends live together, share activities, eat together, talk together, work together. The deepest friendship is the one that becomes a shared form of life. Invest in shared activity, more than in shared feeling.

(NE VIII.5, IX.10.)

### What friendship's activity is

> "...to live together is the chief mark of friendship, but it is quite clear that it is not possible to live with and to share oneself among a large number of people." — Aristotle, NE IX.10, 1171a (H. Rackham trans., [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0054%3Abekker+page%3D1171a))

And Aristotle notes what time together runs on: "it is impossible for men to spend their time together unless they give each other pleasure, or have common tastes" ([NE VIII.5, 1157b](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0054%3Abekker+page%3D1157b)).

### Why the circle stays small

Aristotle caps the quantity without sentimentality: "Perhaps therefore it is a good rule not to seek to have as many friends as possible, but only as many as are enough to form a circle of associates", since "warm friendship is only possible with a few" (IX.10, 1171a). The Sunday cooking, done badly, is the point: living together is the chief mark, and warm friendship is possible with only a few.

**Plain words** Affection is the cheap part; anyone can feel warmly from a distance. The suzēn test is calendars: who do you actually eat, talk and work with, week to week? That small circle is the friendship; the feelings ride on it.

*For example:* Friends who cook together every Sunday, even badly, build more than friends who text "we should catch up" for years.

> **Principle 15** — Learn to filter: not all information deserves attention

Attention is selective, but the selection is often not deliberate. Much of what enters awareness is noise — irrelevant, unimportant, or actively harmful. The skill is deliberate filtering.

**Plain words** Being selective isn't rudeness; it's the whole job of a mind. Most of what arrives doesn't deserve a seat — treat inputs like applicants, not guests.

*For example:* The person who unsubscribes from 40 newsletters in an afternoon isn't being antisocial. They're firing the noise that was freelancing in their head.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Selective attention (Broadbent's filter model)|Selective attention and filtering]]**, the ability to select relevant information and ignore irrelevant information is a core attentional capacity (Broadbent, 1958; Lavie, 1995)
- **[[Load theory of attention|Load theory of attention]]**, higher cognitive load reduces the ability to filter distractors (Lavie, 1995; Forster & Lavie, 2008)

## Practice

- Before engaging with information (news, social media, conversation), ask: "does this deserve my attention?" Filter aggressively. Attention is a scarce resource.

> **Principle 70** — Recognise when body-based practices are insufficient: clinical conditions

Body-based practices (breathing, movement, relaxation) are helpful supports but are not sufficient for clinical conditions (severe depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis, severe trauma). These require professional treatment.

**Plain words** Body-based practices are supports, not substitutes — breathing and movement help, but severe depression, bipolar, psychosis, severe trauma need clinical care. The breathwork complements the treatment; it doesn't replace it. Know which one the situation is.

*For example:* The cold-plunge enthusiast urging it on someone with untreated bipolar — the plunge is great for Tuesday nerves, useless and harmful as a treatment plan. Tools have scopes. Respect the label.
## Psychology parallels

- **Clinical conditions require professional treatment**, severe depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis, and severe trauma require evidence-based treatments (APA, 2022)
- **Body-based practices as adjuncts**, breathing, exercise, and relaxation are complements, not substitutes, for treatment (Schuch et al., 2018; Hofmann et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Know thy limits** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[Humility|Humility as strength]]** (Aquinas)

## Practice

- If you have a clinical condition, seek professional treatment. Use body-based practices as complements (supports), not substitutes. Do not self-treat severe conditions with lifestyle alone.

> **Principle 70** — Recognise when body-based practices are insufficient: clinical conditions

The opened clinical pages draw the same line for each condition this principle names. For PTSD, the main treatments are talk therapy, medicines, or both, and the page adds that you need to work with a mental health professional to find the best treatment for your symptoms ([MedlinePlus, PTSD](https://medlineplus.gov/posttraumaticstressdisorder.html)). For bipolar disorder, the main treatments are medicines, psychotherapy, or both, in a lifelong illness that ongoing treatment helps manage ([MedlinePlus, bipolar disorder](https://medlineplus.gov/bipolardisorder.html)). The federal schizophrenia page describes a serious mental illness whose treatment helps people engage in school or work ([NIMH, schizophrenia](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/schizophrenia)). Severe depression escalates further: ECT is most often used for severe depression that is not getting better with other treatments ([MedlinePlus, depression](https://medlineplus.gov/depression.html)).

Body-based practices sit one notch down on the same pages. The bipolar page lists them under lifestyle changes: "Healthy lifestyle changes, such as getting regular exercise, having a consistent sleep schedule, and keeping a mood journal, can also help with your symptoms" ([MedlinePlus, bipolar disorder](https://medlineplus.gov/bipolardisorder.html)). The word also does the scoping: the practices help alongside treatment.

> "If you, or someone you love is depressed, see your provider or a mental health provider. It's important to get treatment at the earliest sign of a problem." (MedlinePlus, Depression, [medlineplus.gov](https://medlineplus.gov/depression.html))

**Plain words**
Breathing exercises, walks and relaxation have their place, and the opened pages give it to them in one word: also. When the trouble is severe depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis or severe trauma, the same pages put talk therapy, medicines and specialists first. The mistake is doing the first thing and skipping the second: managing a clinical condition with lifestyle alone, or putting off the doctor's visit because the practices feel like they are helping.

*For example:* An imagined case: someone with untreated bipolar disorder rides out high-energy weeks with long runs and breathing exercises, feels steadier, and keeps postponing the psychiatrist. The steadier feeling is real support; the postponement is the cost. On the opened page, exercise and sleep schedule sit in the also-list beside the main treatments ([MedlinePlus, bipolar disorder](https://medlineplus.gov/bipolardisorder.html)).

## Psychology parallels

- **Clinical conditions require professional treatment**, severe depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis, and severe trauma require evidence-based treatments (APA, 2022)
- **Body-based practices as adjuncts**, breathing, exercise, and relaxation are complements, not substitutes, for treatment (Schuch et al., 2018; Hofmann et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Know thy limits** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[Humility|Humility as strength]]** (Aquinas)

## Practice

- If you have a clinical condition, seek professional treatment. Use body-based practices as complements (supports), not substitutes. Do not self-treat severe conditions with lifestyle alone.

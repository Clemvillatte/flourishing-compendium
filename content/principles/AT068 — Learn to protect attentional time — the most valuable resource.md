> **Principle 68** — Learn to protect attentional time: the most valuable resource

Time is not the scarcest resource; attention is. You can have time without attention (present but distracted) and attention without much time (fully present for 10 minutes). Protecting attentional time — periods of full, uninterrupted attention — is a foundation of well-being.

**Plain words** Attention, not time, is the scarcest resource — you can have an empty calendar and a shredded mind. Guard attentional blocks like they're money, because everyone's trying to spend them for you.

*For example:* "I have all Saturday to write" — and Saturday produces nothing because the attention was spent on errands, pings, and worry. The hours were free; the mind wasn't.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional time is scarcer than clock time**, quality of attention determines quality of experience (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Killingsworth & Gilbert, 2010)
- **Interruptions destroy attentional time**, fragmentation reduces the value of time spent (Leroy, 2009; Monsell, 2003)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires protected time]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Simplicity|Simplicity and focus]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 19)

## Practice

- Protect attentional time as your most valuable resource. Schedule blocks of uninterrupted, fully attentive time. Guard these blocks from interruption. Do not let others (or technology) fragment your attentional time.

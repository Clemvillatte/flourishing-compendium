> **Principle 55** — Work with envy: it signals a value, not a defect

Envy is the pain at another's good fortune, particularly in domains you value. It is not a character flaw; it is a signal about what you want. The adaptive response is to extract the value information and either pursue it or release it, not to feed the resentment.

**Plain words** Envy is the pain at another's good fortune in domains you value — not a character flaw, a signal about what you want. The adaptive response: extract the value information, then either pursue the thing or release the comparison.

*For example:* The sting every time the colleague publishes — decoded: not hatred, information — and the extraction: "I want to be writing." The envy, followed, becomes a writing schedule. The envy, suppressed, becomes bitterness with a publishing schedule of zero.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Benign versus malicious envy|Benign vs malicious envy]]**, benign envy motivates self-improvement; malicious envy motivates hostility toward the envied person (van de Ven et al., 2009)
- **Envy is amplified by social comparison frequency**, upward social comparison amplifies envy; reducing comparison reduces envy (Festinger, 1954; Smith, 2004)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Schadenfreude|Schadenfreude as the shadow of envy]]** (Nietzsche, On the Genealogy of Morals)
- **[[Mudita (sympathetic joy)|Mudita (sympathetic joy)]]** (Buddhism, Four Brahmaviharas)

## Practice

- When you notice envy, ask: "what does this tell me I want?" Then decide whether to pursue it or release it. Do not feed it with rumination or comparison.

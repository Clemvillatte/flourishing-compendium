> **Principle 47** — Learn focused attention meditation: concentration on a single object

Focused-attention meditation attends to a chosen object in a sustained fashion — the breath, a body sensation, a sound. The training happens in the return: attention drifts, and the practitioner notices the drift, releases the distraction, and comes back. Lutz and colleagues describe the practice as developing exactly those skills.

> "Thus, while cultivating the acuity and stability of sustained attention on a chosen object, this practice also develops three skills regulative of attention: the first is the monitoring faculty that remains vigilant to distractions without destabilizing the intended focus. The next skill is the ability to disengage from a distracting object without further involvement. The last consists in the ability to redirect focus promptly to the chosen object." (Lutz, Slagter, Dunne & Davidson, "Attention regulation and monitoring in meditation", Trends in Cognitive Sciences 12, 2008, Box 1, "Focused Attention meditation", [full text via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2693206/))

The same passage sets the measure of progress:

> "Progress in this form of meditation is measured in part by the degree of effort required to sustain the intended focus." (Lutz et al., 2008, Box 1, [full text via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2693206/))

By that measure, a session with many returns is a session with much practice.

**Plain words**
You pick one thing, such as the breath. Your mind will leave it. The practice is the noticing and the coming back: spotting the drift, letting go of the distraction, getting back. A session full of returns is a session full of practice.

**For example**
Sitting with the breath at the nostrils, the focus slides to the ache in a knee. The practice is right there: notice the shift, release the ache, return to the breath. That is one repetition, and the session is made of them.

## Psychology parallels

- **Focused attention meditation improves concentration**, regular practice improves sustained attention and reduces distractibility (Lutz et al., 2008; Tang et al., 2015)
- **FA meditation reduces mind-wandering**, training the return from distraction reduces spontaneous mind-wandering (Mrazek et al., 2013; Hasenkamp et al., 2012)

## Practice

- Practise focused attention: 10-20 minutes daily, attending to breath. When attention wanders, notice and gently return. The return itself is the training. Build gradually.

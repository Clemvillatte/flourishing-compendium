> **Principle 347** — Practise availability (disponibilité)

Marcel's own illustration of the failure is the man in the room who is not there:

> "We could say that the man sitting beside us was in the same room as ourselves, but that he was not really present there, that his presence did not make itself felt... what we have with this person, who is in the room, but somehow not really present to us, is communication without communion: unreal communication, in a word. He understands what I say to him, but he does not understand me." (Marcel, [The Mystery of Being, as quoted in Cali and Gustafson, 2026](https://www.dialogic-ethics.org/uploads/1/3/8/4/138406736/vol._5_no._1_cali_gustafson.pdf))

The positive term he offers is disponibilité, and his own definition of it is a test you can run on anyone, including yourself:

> "The person who is at my disposal is the one who is capable of being with me with the whole of himself when I am in need; while the one who is not at my disposal seems merely to offer me a temporary loan raised on his resources. For the one I am a presence; for the other I am an object." (Marcel, [as quoted in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/))

The encyclopaedia summary of his usage: disponibilité "refers to the measure in which I am available to someone, the state of having my resources at hand to offer", and the disponible soul "cannot think in terms of cases; in its eyes there are no cases at all" (Marcel, as quoted in SEP). Availability is also what lets presence do its work: "presence does really make itself felt, it can refresh my inner being; it reveals me to myself, it makes me more fully myself than I should be if I were not exposed to its impact" (Marcel, [as quoted in Cali and Gustafson, 2026](https://www.dialogic-ethics.org/uploads/1/3/8/4/138406736/vol._5_no._1_cali_gustafson.pdf)).

(Marcel, Being and Having and related works, as carried in the opened sources below.)

**Plain words** Disponibilité: availability — the disposition to be actually present and open, versus the absorbed, distracted, enclosed-in-oneself default. When someone speaks to you, be there for it. The opposite of availability isn't rudeness; it's absence wearing your face.

*For example:* Imagine the parent who puts the phone in another room during the bedtime story; Marcel's test is whether the child gets the whole of them or a temporary loan.

> **Principle 50** — Learn to question your thoughts: not every thought deserves belief

Thoughts are automatic, generated by the mind without your consent. Many are distorted, exaggerated, or simply false. The capacity to evaluate a thought — is it accurate? helpful? kind? — before believing it is a core cognitive skill.

**Plain words** Thoughts are automatic — the mind generates them without your consent, and many are distorted, exaggerated, or false. Not every thought deserves belief. Evaluate before believing: is it accurate? helpful? kind? The belief is a second act; you have a veto.

*For example:* The thought "I'm going to be exposed as a fraud" arrives unbidden at every meeting — and the person who finally treats it like a popup, not a prophecy: "accurate? no evidence. helpful? no. dismissed." The thought keeps coming; the belief stopped signing.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Cognitive restructuring|Cognitive restructuring in CBT]]**, identifying and challenging distorted thoughts reduces depression and anxiety (Beck, 1976; Burns, 1980)
- **[[Cognitive distortions|Common cognitive distortions]]**, catastrophising, all-or-nothing thinking, mind-reading, personalisation, emotional reasoning (Burns, 1980; Beck, 2011)

## Practice

- When you notice a strong negative thought, ask: "is this thought accurate? What is the evidence for and against? Is there another way to see this?" Do not believe every thought you think.

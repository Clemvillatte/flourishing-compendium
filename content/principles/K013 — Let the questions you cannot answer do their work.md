> **Principle 13** — Let the questions you cannot answer do their work

> Then concentrate your whole self into this Mu, making your whole body with its 360 bones and joints and 84,000 pores into a solid lump of doubt. Day and night, without ceasing, keep digging into it, but don't take it as "nothingness" or as "being" or "non-being".

(Mumonkan, Case 1 "Jōshū's Dog", Mumon's commentary, in the English translation published by [Upaya Zen Center](https://www.upaya.org/uploads/pdfs/Mumonkan.pdf), p. 4. The same passage in [Senzaki and Reps's version on ibiblio](https://www.ibiblio.org/zen/gateless-gate/1.html) reads "filled with this question: What is Mu? and carry it day and night.")

The case itself is one question and one word. A monk asks, "Does a dog have Buddha nature or not?" and Jōshū answers, "Mu!" Mumon adds that the question must be held like "a red-hot iron ball which you have gulped down and which you try to vomit but cannot."

The practice is to keep the question alive and to refuse both quick answers. Mumon names the two quick answers to avoid: reading Mu as "nothingness" and reading it as "being" or "non-being." The Stanford Encyclopedia records that Nishida passed this koan after nearly a decade of Zen practice ([Stanford Encyclopedia, "Kyoto School", section 3.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kyoto-school/)).

The entry's two sample questions, "who am I when no role is active?" and "what is this before I name it?", and its link to [[The unexamined life|the unexamined life]] are the compendium's own, not Mumon's. The Nishitani reference in the original entry was not confirmed in the sources opened.

**Plain words** A question like Mu is not there to be answered quickly. It is there to be carried, day and night, without settling for the first neat reply. Mumon warns off two quick replies in particular. Pick one open question of your own and keep it open on purpose.

*For example:* Instead of settling "what do I want from my work?" with the first flattering answer, write it on a card and return to it each evening for a month, adding what you notice.

> **Principle 13** — Let the questions you cannot answer do their work

The Kyoto School's respect for the unanswerable is not mystification; it is pedagogy. Some questions ("who am I when no role is active?", "what is this before I name it?") are not information gaps but instruments, and their power is in their being kept open. Closing them early with a borrowed answer is the tradition's definition of the [[The unexamined life|unexamined life]]. Practically, carry one open question deliberately, and resist the first answer that flatters you.

(The Zen koan tradition; Nishitani on the self-question that cannot be handed over.)

**Plain words** Some questions aren't gaps in information. They're instruments. "Who am I when no role is active?" does its work by being held open. Closing it early with a borrowed answer is, for this tradition, what an unexamined life is.

*For example:* Carrying "what is this before I name it?" around for a month, glancing at it while washing dishes, without trying to answer.

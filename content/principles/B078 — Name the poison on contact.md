> **Principle 78** — Name the poison on contact

MN 2 makes attention the fork. When a monk attends inappropriately, unarisen fermentations arise and arisen ones increase; when he attends appropriately, unarisen fermentations do not arise and arisen ones are abandoned. The sutta pairs this with sense-door restraint: dwelling restrained with the eye-faculty, so that the vexation that would arise from dwelling unrestrained does not. (MN 2.) Dhammapada 3-4 shows the same fork with a grudge: those who keep the thought "He abused me" do not still their hatred, and those who do not harbor it still it. (Dhammapada 3.)

> "When a monk attends inappropriately, unarisen fermentations arise, and arisen fermentations increase. When a monk attends appropriately, unarisen fermentations do not arise, and arisen fermentations are abandoned." (MN 2, [Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.002.than.html))

The compendium's application is the naming practice: naming greed, hatred or delusion as it arises is a way of attending at the contact point, before it has shaped an action.

**Plain words**
The skill is catching the reaction while it is still just weather. You feel the pull toward the purchase, the flash of resentment, the story you are telling yourself, and you name it in plain words: wanting, anger, story. The name does not argue with the feeling; it moves you one seat back from it, and from that seat you can choose whether to act.

*For example:* Imagine feeling the pull to buy something during an argument you are losing online. Saying "that's greed talking" takes the half-second between the impulse and the click.

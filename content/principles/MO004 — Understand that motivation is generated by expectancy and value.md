> **Principle 4** — Understand that motivation is generated by expectancy and value

Wigfield and Eccles review a model in which "ability beliefs, expectancies for success, and the components of subjective task values" are tied to "performance and choice of activities" ([Wigfield & Eccles, 2000, Contemporary Educational Psychology 25, 68-81](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10620382/)). The abstract names the two factors but does not state a product rule. The multiplication comes from the integrative work of Steel and König, who combined expectancy theory with picoeconomics, prospect theory and need theory to "construct a temporal motivational theory (TMT)" ([Steel & König, 2006, Academy of Management Review 31, 889-913](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/AMR.2006.22527462)). In applied papers, TMT is written as expectancy times value, divided by one plus impulsiveness times delay ([a recent paper on ADHD and procrastination](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13033046/)).

So "motivation equals expectancy times value" is a simplified numerator of that model, not a finding that two numbers fully explain effort.

(Wigfield & Eccles, 2000; Steel & König, 2006.)

**Plain words** Two questions help: do I think I can do this, and do I care about it? If one answer is near zero, motivation is likely low, as the formula suggests. Treat it as a diagnostic aid, not an exact calculation.

For example (imagined): you stall on a job search. If the stall is "nothing I send gets a reply", that is an expectancy problem. If it is "I am not sure I want this career", it is a value problem. The fixes differ.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Expectancy-value theory (Eccles, Wigfield)|Expectancy-value theory (Eccles, Wigfield)]]**, motivation is a function of expectancy of success and subjective value of the task (Eccles & Wigfield, 2002; Wigfield & Eccles, 2000)
- **[[Temporal motivation theory|Temporal motivation theory (Steel)]]**, motivation = (Expectancy × Value) / (Impulsiveness × Delay) (Steel & König, 2006; Steel, 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Belief and desire as the components of action** (Hume, Treatise; not verified here)
- **Practical reason: means and end** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III)
- **[[Hope|Hope as expectation plus value]]** (Gabriel Marcel, Homo Viator)

## Practice

- When unmotivated, diagnose: is expectancy low (I do not believe I can) or value low (I do not care)? If expectancy is low, build self-efficacy. If value is low, clarify values or connect the task to something you care about.

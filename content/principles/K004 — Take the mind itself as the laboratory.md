> **Principle 4** — Take the mind itself as the laboratory

The Tibetan traditions begin from a different starting point with the same result: the mind is the instrument of every experience, and an unexamined instrument distorts everything it measures. Shantideva's chapter on vigilance makes the examination a standing discipline: check the mind repeatedly, "with a hundred cares," as a mother watches her only child. Practically, schedule the looking (a fixed daily minute of asking "what is the mind doing right now?") rather than waiting for crisis to force it.

(Shantideva, Bodhicharyavatara, ch. 5, the vigilance chapter.)

**Plain words** Every experience you have goes through one instrument, your mind, and an instrument that's never been checked distorts everything it measures. The Tibetan teachers turn this into a standing habit: look at the mind regularly, carefully, the way a mother watches her only child.

*For example:* Setting a timer at three in the afternoon and asking yourself, "What am I like right now?" Irritable. Hungry. Rehearsing an argument. Just knowing it changes the next hour.

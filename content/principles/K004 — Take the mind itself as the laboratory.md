> **Principle 4** — Take the mind itself as the laboratory

Shantideva's chapter on vigilance makes watching the mind a standing task. It opens with the claim that "Those who wish to keep a rule of life / Must guard their minds in perfect self-possession" (5.1), because untended, "the elephant of mind" can do more harm than any wild beast (5.2). Fear and suffering have the same root: "Their source and wellspring is the mind itself" (5.6). The remedy is two helpers. Mindfulness is "stationed as a sentinel, / A guard upon the threshold of the mind", and "Mental scrutiny is likewise present, / Returning when forgotten or dispersed" (5.33). The check is practical: "If at the outset, when I check my mind, / I find within some fault or insufficiency, / I'll stay unmoving, like a log" (5.34).

The chapter ends by defining the task as repetition: "To keep a guard again and yet again / Upon the state and actions of our thoughts and deeds— / This and only this defines / The nature and the sense of mental watchfulness" (5.108) (Shantideva, Bodhicharyavatara, ch. 5, trans. Padmakara Translation Group, The Way of the Bodhisattva, 1997). I did not find the mother-and-only-child image or a "hundred cares" phrase in this chapter, so I have left them out.

**Plain words**
Everything you experience passes through your mind, so it pays to look at it often. Shantideva asks for short, repeated checks, not a single deep inspection. Look, name what you find, and return to what you meant to do.

**For example**
Before replying to a message that irritated you, you pause and ask what your mind is doing. You notice you are rehearsing a comeback, and you wait ten minutes.

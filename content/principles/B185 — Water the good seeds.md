> **Principle 185** — Water the good seeds

Thich Nhat Hanh describes the mind in two layers: store consciousness, which holds every mental formation as a seed, and mind consciousness, the living room where seeds manifest. His practice instruction follows directly from the model: a seed grows each time it is watered, so the work is selective watering, choosing which seeds to feed in yourself and in the people around you. (TNH, The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching.)

> "The Buddha said that in the depth of our store consciousness, alayavijñana, there are all kinds of positive and negative seeds — seeds of anger, delusion, and fear, and seeds of understanding, compassion, and forgiveness. Many of these seeds have been transmitted to us by our ancestors. We should learn to recognize every one of these seeds in us in order to practice diligence. If it is a negative seed, the seed of an affliction like anger, fear, jealousy, or discrimination, we should refrain from allowing it to be watered in our daily life. Every time such a seed is watered, it will manifest on the upper level of our consciousness, and we will suffer and make the people we love suffer at the same time. The practice is to refrain from watering the negative seeds in us." (TNH, The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching, ch. 25, "The Six Paramitas")

> "These seeds of happiness, love, loyalty, and reconciliation need watering every day." (TNH, The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching, ch. 14, "Right Diligence")

In Hanh's account, a seed becomes stronger when it manifests in mind consciousness and returns to store consciousness.

**Plain words**
Think of something helpful someone did today. Tell them exactly what you appreciated. You can try the same exercise with yourself by recalling a moment when you responded with care. If anger is already present, acknowledge it and meet it with mindfulness.

*For example:* Imagine opening a team meeting by naming something a colleague did that helped the work, and thanking them for it.

> **Principle 16** — Cultivate competence: motivation requires feeling effective

Ryan and Deci's review in American Psychologist places competence among three needs: "the needs for competence (Harter, 1978; White, 1963), relatedness (Baumeister & Leary, 1995; Reis, 1994), and autonomy (deCharms, 1968; Deci, 1975)". They describe these as essential for "optimal functioning of the natural propensities for growth and integration, as well as for constructive social development and personal well-being" (Ryan and Deci, 2000, introduction, [American Psychologist 55(1)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

For motivation in particular, they report that "optimal challenges, effectance-promoting feedback, and freedom from demeaning evaluations were all found to facilitate intrinsic motivation", while "early studies showed that positive performance feedback enhanced intrinsic motivation, whereas negative performance feedback diminished it" (Ryan and Deci, 2000, section on cognitive evaluation theory). The same review warns of "nonoptimal challenges" as one of the conditions that disrupt growth.

There is a condition. Citing Fisher (1978) and Ryan (1982), the authors write that "feelings of competence will not enhance intrinsic motivation unless accompanied by a sense of autonomy". People "must not only experience competence or efficacy, they must also experience their behavior as self-determined" (Ryan and Deci, 2000). On this account, competence in a task you experience as imposed does less for motivation than competence in one you chose.

**Plain words**
Effort is easier to sustain when you can see yourself getting better. Tasks that are too hard crush that feeling, and tasks that are too easy give you nothing to feel. If motivation has gone, look at the difficulty first, then at whether the task is really yours.

**For example**
A language learner stuck on a textbook that is far too advanced loses heart. Switching to a graded reader she chose herself, one where she understands most of each page, gives her small wins daily.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Competence|Competence as basic need (SDT)]]**, feeling effective is fundamental to motivation (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Optimal challenge**, tasks that match skill level (not too easy, not too hard) produce competence and motivation (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Elliot et al., 2005)
- **[[Self-efficacy|Self-efficacy (Bandura)]]**, belief in one's capacity predicts effort and persistence (Bandura, 1997)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The unity of the virtues|Virtue as built capacity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **Mastery and craft** (Dreyfus & Dreyfus, Mind Over Machine)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Cultivate competence: set achievable challenges, build mastery experiences, and seek optimal challenge (not too easy, not too hard). Feeling effective is a foundation of motivation.

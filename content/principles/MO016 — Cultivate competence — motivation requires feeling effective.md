> **Principle 16** — Cultivate competence: motivation requires feeling effective

Competence — feeling effective and capable — is a basic psychological need and a foundation of motivation. Feeling competent (through mastery experiences and optimal challenge) sustains motivation.

**Plain words** Competence is a nutrient — feeling effective. Motivation dies in two places: too hard (hopeless) and too easy (pointless). The zone is just-hard-enough. If you're bored or drowning, the difficulty dial is the problem, not your character.

*For example:* The video game you played for six hours — perfectly tuned difficulty. Real life is badly tuned sometimes; re-tune it. Add a challenge or get help, but adjust the dial.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Competence|Competence as basic need (SDT)]]**, feeling effective is fundamental to motivation (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Optimal challenge**, tasks that match skill level (not too easy, not too hard) produce competence and motivation (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990; Elliot et al., 2005)
- **[[Self-efficacy|Self-efficacy (Bandura)]]**, belief in one's capacity predicts effort and persistence (Bandura, 1997)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The unity of the virtues|Virtue as built capacity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **Mastery and craft** (Dreyfus & Dreyfus, Mind Over Machine)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Cultivate competence: set achievable challenges, build mastery experiences, and seek optimal challenge (not too easy, not too hard). Feeling effective is a foundation of motivation.

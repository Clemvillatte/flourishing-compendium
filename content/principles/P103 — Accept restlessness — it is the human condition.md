> **Principle 103** — Accept restlessness: it is the human condition

James reports Starbuck's finding that the stormy passage young people go through is "a normal phase of adolescence in every class of human beings", with the same symptoms as religious conversion: "sense of incompleteness and imperfection; brooding, depression, morbid introspection, and sense of sin; anxiety about the hereafter; distress over doubts, and the like" ([The Varieties, 1902](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt), Lecture IX, "Conversion"). The result, he notes, is the same: "a happy relief and objectivity, as the confidence in self gets greater through the adjustment of the faculties to the wider outlook."

> But to find religion is only one out of many ways of reaching unity; and the process of remedying inner incompleteness and reducing inner discord is a general psychological process, which may take place with any sort of mental material, and need not necessarily assume the religious form.
>
> (James, The Varieties of Religious Experience, 1902, Lecture VIII)

The compendium uses these accounts as a prompt for examining restlessness. In the conversion cases James studies, the subject's "present incompleteness or wrongness" sits next to "the positive ideal which he longs to compass" (Lecture IX).

**Plain words**
James describes efforts to resolve inner conflict in religious and nonreligious settings. For the compendium's exercise, choose one feeling of being unfinished. Name what you want to change and write one small step toward that change.

*For example:*
For a proposed exercise, take a restlessness about what to do after a big commitment ends. Write down what the restlessness reaches for and what it pulls away from, and compare the two lists: which activities named on the reaching side could be started this week?

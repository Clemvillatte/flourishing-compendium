> **Principle 103** — Accept restlessness: it is the human condition

James's treatment of the restless states in The Varieties of Religious Experience reads them as development rather than disease. He reports Starbuck's finding that the stormy passage young people go through is "a normal phase of adolescence in every class of human beings", with the same symptoms as religious conversion: "sense of incompleteness and imperfection; brooding, depression, morbid introspection, and sense of sin; anxiety about the hereafter; distress over doubts, and the like" ([The Varieties, 1902](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt), Lecture VIII). The result, he notes, is the same: "a happy relief and objectivity, as the confidence in self gets greater through the adjustment of the faculties to the wider outlook."

> The process of remedying inner incompleteness and reducing inner discord is a general psychological process, which may take place with any sort of mental material, and need not necessarily assume the religious form.
>
> (James, The Varieties of Religious Experience, 1902, Lecture VIII)

The compendium's rule follows from the framing: restlessness is the material development works on, not a diagnosis. In the conversion cases James studies, the subject's "present incompleteness or wrongness" sits next to "the positive ideal which he longs to compass"; the striving is aimed by the very unease that precedes it.

**Plain words**
Everyone goes through seasons of feeling unfinished, and the feeling usually gets labeled a problem. James's data treat it as the normal weather of growing: the symptoms show up in every class of people, and they typically end in "a happy relief and objectivity" rather than in collapse. The compendium's exercise: write down one current restlessness, what it is reaching for, and what it is pulling away from.

*For example:*
Consider retirement that lasts eleven days before the volunteer project starts. James's framing reads the eleven days as the between-state of a person whose faculties are adjusting to a wider outlook, not as a failure to relax.

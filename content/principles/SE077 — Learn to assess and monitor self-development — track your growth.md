> **Principle 77** — Learn to assess and monitor self-development: track your growth

Hattie and Timperley's synthesis of the feedback evidence opens with a finding and a warning: "Feedback is one of the most powerful influences on learning and achievement, but this impact can be either positive or negative" (2007, p. 81). Their definition makes room for the self as one of the agents: feedback is "information provided by an agent (e.g., teacher, peer, book, parent, self, experience) regarding aspects of one's performance or understanding" (p. 81). Peirce supplies the philosophy of the practice: doubt is "an uneasy and dissatisfied state from which we struggle to free ourselves and pass into the state of belief", and the struggle itself is inquiry (1877). Tracking your own development is inquiry of exactly this kind, with your own growth as the opinion under settlement.

> "Effective feedback must answer three major questions asked by a teacher and/or by a student: Where am I going? (What are the goals?), How am I going? (What progress is being made toward the goal?), and Where to next? (What activities need to be undertaken to make better progress?)" (Hattie & Timperley, 2007, p. 86)

**Plain words** You can track your own development the way you'd track anything you care about. A quarterly check-in — identity clarity, self-esteem stability, [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]], values alignment — turns "how am I doing as a self?" from a mood into a measurement. What you notice, you can steer.

*For example:* Once a quarter, the same three questions in a notebook: Who am I becoming? What did I say no to? What do I keep doing that I don't respect? Ten minutes. The notebook becomes the most honest mirror in the house, because it has no incentive to flatter you.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-monitoring improves self-development**, tracking self-related patterns provides feedback (Zimmerman, 2002; Hattie & Timperley, 2007)
- **Identity clarity and well-being**, identity clarity (a clear, stable sense of self) predicts well-being (Vleioras & Bosma, 2005; Luyckx et al., 2008)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires assessment]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **Know thyself as ongoing** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[Fallibilism|Fallibilism and feedback]]** (Peirce)

## Practice

- Periodically assess your self-development: identity clarity (do I know who I am?), self-compassion (how do I relate to myself?), values alignment (am I living my values?). Use this for adjustment.

> **Principle 77** — Learn to assess and monitor self-development: track your growth

Self-development can be tracked. Simple self-assessment (identity clarity, self-esteem stability, [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]] level, values alignment) provides feedback for adjustment.

**Plain words** You can track your own development the way you'd track anything you care about. A quarterly check-in — identity clarity, self-esteem stability, self-compassion, values alignment — turns "how am I doing as a self?" from a mood into a measurement. What you notice, you can steer.

*For example:* Once a quarter, the same three questions in a notebook: Who am I becoming? What did I say no to? What do I keep doing that I don't respect? Ten minutes. The notebook becomes the most honest mirror in the house, because it has no incentive to flatter you.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-monitoring improves self-development**, tracking self-related patterns provides feedback (Zimmerman, 2002; Hattie & Timperley, 2007)
- **Identity clarity and well-being**, identity clarity (a clear, stable sense of self) predicts well-being (Vleioras & Bosma, 2005; Luyckx et al., 2008)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires assessment]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **Know thyself as ongoing** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[Fallibilism|Fallibilism and feedback]]** (Peirce)

## Practice

- Periodically assess your self-development: identity clarity (do I know who I am?), self-compassion (how do I relate to myself?), values alignment (am I living my values?). Use this for adjustment.

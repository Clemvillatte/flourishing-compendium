> **Principle 76** — Obey from understanding, not from fear

The best obedience, Spinoza argues, flows from knowing why the law is good; obedience from fear alone trains neither citizen nor soul, it trains a prisoner. The distinction is the psychological engine of the whole Treatise: fear-based compliance decays at the first drop of surveillance. Practically, for each rule you live under (and each rule you impose), be able to state the reason or change the rule; a rule you cannot justify is a rule you are teaching people to break quietly.

(Theological-Political Treatise, ch. 16 and 19; the obedience analysis.)

**Plain words** The best obedience flows from knowing why the law is good; obedience from fear alone trains neither citizen nor soul — it trains a prisoner. Fear-based compliance ends when the fear does. Obey from understanding, and the order survives the supervisor.

*For example:* Two teenagers under the same curfew: one complies from fear of the phone confiscation, one from understanding the street issue. Same behaviour for a year — and the first one's behaviour ends the week the enforcement slips. The second one has a law; the first has a hostage situation.

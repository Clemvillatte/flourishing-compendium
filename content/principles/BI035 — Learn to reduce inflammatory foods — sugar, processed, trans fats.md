> **Principle 35** — Learn to reduce inflammatory foods: sugar, processed, trans fats

The evidence is observational, and the researchers themselves are careful with it. In the Whitehall II cohort, 3486 middle-aged civil servants were split by dietary pattern: those eating the most processed food (sweetened desserts, fried food, processed meat, refined grains, high-fat dairy) had higher odds of depression five years later than those eating the least, while a whole-food pattern (vegetables, fruits, fish) went the other way ([Akbaraly et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19880930/)). A meta-analysis of the dietary inflammatory index, which scores a diet by its inflammatory potential, pooled four prospective cohorts and two cross-sectional studies and found the most pro-inflammatory diets carried a 23% higher risk of depression than the least ([Wang et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30319085/)).

> "In middle-aged participants, a processed food dietary pattern is a risk factor for CES-D depression 5 years later, whereas a whole food pattern is protective." (Abstract, Conclusions; Akbaraly et al., 2009, Br J Psychiatry 195(5): 408-13, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19880930/))

> "Overall, individuals in the highest DII v. the lowest DII category had a 23 % higher risk of depression (risk ratio (RR)=1·23; 95 % CI 1·12, 1·35)." (Abstract, Results; Wang et al., 2019, Public Health Nutr 22(4): 654-60, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30319085/))

**Plain words**
These studies describe differences between groups with different dietary patterns ([Akbaraly et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19880930/); [Wang et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30319085/)). They do not establish that replacing a particular food will change one person's mood. The practical application is to review the overall pattern of meals, rather than interpret an afternoon's feelings as the effect of one snack.

**For example**
(Imagined) Two shoppers fill similar trolleys, one with ready meals, sweetened desserts and processed meat, one with vegetables, fish and fruit. The studies cannot promise the second shopper a better mood; they can say that over years, that pattern belonged to the group reporting fewer depressive symptoms.

## Psychology parallels

- **Inflammatory diet and depression**, more pro-inflammatory dietary patterns are associated with higher depression risk (Akbaraly et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2019)
- **Dietary Inflammatory Index**, higher DII scores are associated with depression, particularly in women (Wang et al., 2019)

## Practice

- Compendium exercise: review your usual meals against the two observed patterns: vegetables, fruit and fish, versus frequent sweetened desserts, fried food, processed meat, refined grains and high-fat dairy. This is an application of observational findings, not a tested food-substitution treatment ([Akbaraly et al., 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19880930/)).

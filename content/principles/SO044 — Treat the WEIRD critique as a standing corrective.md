> **Principle 44** — Treat the WEIRD critique as a standing corrective

Treat the WEIRD critique — the observation that much psychological research over-relies on Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic samples (Henrich et al., 2010) — as a standing corrective. The critique does not invalidate psychology; it requires the field to test its claims across populations and to treat cultural generality as an empirical question, not an assumption.

**Plain words** Most psychology research studies WEIRD samples — Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, Democratic — and treats the findings as human nature. Sometimes they are; sometimes they're local. The critique is a standing corrective, not a wrecking ball: check the sample before you apply the finding to your life.

*For example:* The famous fairness-findings that turn out to shift with the culture they're tested in — the effect was real in Boston and different in other places. The science wasn't wrong; the generalisation was.

## Practice

- In applying research findings, ask: was this tested in populations like mine? In research, prioritize diverse sampling. In applying these principles, treat cultural generality as a question (see principle 11).

> **Principle 343** — Practise I-Thou

Relate to the other person not as an object (an It) but as a subject (a Thou). The I-Thou relation is the primary mode of authentic encounter, in which the other is encountered in their full, irreducible personhood. Do not reduce the other to a category, a role, or a function. Encounter them as a whole person.

(Buber, 1923/1970, I and Thou; Buber, 1965, The Knowledge of Man.)

**Plain words** Buber's distinction: you can meet a person as an It — a category, a role, a function — or as a Thou, a whole irreducible person. Most of daily life runs on It, which is fine for the bus timetable. But when it's a friend, a child, a client — the encounter itself is the thing. Don't reduce them.

*For example:* The barista is "coffee transaction" forty times a day — and once a week someone greets them as a Thou, asks about the violin they mentioned, and it changes both days.

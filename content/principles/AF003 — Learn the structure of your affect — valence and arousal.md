> **Principle 3** — Learn the structure of your affect: valence and arousal

Russell proposes representing affective concepts in a circular space defined by pleasure–displeasure and degree of arousal ([Russell, 1980](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/others/1980/russell1980a.pdf)). In this model, excitement and distress both occupy the activated half of the circle while differing in pleasantness ([Russell, 1980](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/others/1980/russell1980a.pdf)). The exercise below uses those dimensions to describe a feeling separately.

> The evidence suggests that these inter-relationships can be represented by a spatial model in which affective concepts fall in a circle in the following order: pleasure (0°), excitement (45°), arousal (90°), distress (135°), displeasure (180°), depression (225°), sleepiness (270°), and relaxation (315°).
>
> James A. Russell, "A circumplex model of affect," Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 39(6), 1161–1178, [paper PDF](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/others/1980/russell1980a.pdf).

> It was also hypothesized that this space could be defined in terms of two orthogonal dimensions, pleasure-displeasure and degree of arousal.
>
> James A. Russell (1980), "A circumplex model of affect," [paper PDF](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/others/1980/russell1980a.pdf).

(Russell, 1980, JPSP 39(6): 1161–1178.)

### Plain words

Try describing a current feeling in two ways: how pleasant or unpleasant it feels, and how activated or sleepy you feel. Keep both descriptions even if a single emotion word does not fit.

For example: Before a presentation, you might write "very activated; somewhat unpleasant." Afterwards, answer both questions again, recording whatever you notice rather than requiring a particular change.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Circumplex model of affect|Circumplex model of affect]]**, valence and activation are orthogonal and empirically robust (Russell, 1980; Barrett & Russell, 1999)
- **[[Arousal reinterpretation|Arousal reinterpretation]]**, relabelling arousal as excitement rather than anxiety improves performance under stress (Brooks, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Yin and yang|Yin-yang polarity]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 42)
- **Passions differ in intensity and direction** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)

## Practice

- When you feel activated, ask "is this pleasant-activation or unpleasant-activation?" and if unpleasant, test the "excitement" label before acting on the "anxiety" label.

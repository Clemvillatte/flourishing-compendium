> **Principle 3** — Learn the structure of your affect: valence and arousal

Affect is not a single axis. It has at minimum two dimensions — valence (pleasant/unpleasant) and arousal (activated/deactivated). Anxiety and excitement share high arousal and differ in valence; sadness and calm share low arousal and differ in valence. Misreading arousal (e.g., treating excitement as anxiety) is a common and correctable source of unnecessary suffering.

**Plain words** Affect has two dials, not one: valence (pleasant/unpleasant) and arousal (activated/deactivated). Anxiety and excitement share high arousal and differ only in valence; sadness and calm share low arousal. Learn the dials — most "bad feelings" are misread arousal.

*For example:* Someone notices the pre-date jitters and labels them terror — then remembers the dials: high arousal, valence TBD — and relabels them excitement. Same heart rate, entirely different evening.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Circumplex model of affect|Circumplex model of affect]]**, valence and activation are orthogonal and empirically robust (Russell, 1980; Barrett & Russell, 1999)
- **[[Arousal reinterpretation|Arousal reinterpretation]]**, relabelling arousal as excitement rather than anxiety improves performance under stress (Brooks, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Yin and yang|Yin-yang polarity]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 42)
- **Passions differ in intensity and direction** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)

## Practice

- When you feel activated, ask "is this pleasant-activation or unpleasant-activation?" and if unpleasant, test the "excitement" label before acting on the "anxiety" label.

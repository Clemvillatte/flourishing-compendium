> **Principle 80** — Practise time affluence

The seed's claim that time affluence "predicts well-being more strongly than material wealth" is not what the sources I opened show. Whillans, Weidman and Dunn built a measure of people's stable preference to prioritise time over money. Across six studies (N = 4,690), they report that this preference is "associated with greater well-being", and that the finding "could not be explained by materialism, material striving, current feelings of time or material affluence, or demographic characteristics such as income" ([Whillans et al. 2016, HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=52948)). So the measure captures what people choose, not how much free time they have, and the design is correlational.

Hershfield, Mogilner and Barnea report a related pattern. Although "the majority of people chose more money, choosing more time was associated with greater happiness", even after controlling for available time and money ([Hershfield et al. 2016](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/marketing/Hershfield_Mogilner_Barnea_SPPS_2016_People_Who_Choose_Time_over_Money_Are_Happier.pdf)).

Causal evidence comes from a field experiment: 60 working adults spent $40 on a time-saving purchase one weekend and on a material purchase another, and felt happier after the time-saving purchase ([ScienceDaily summary of Whillans et al. 2017](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/07/170724161258.htm)). A Harvard Magazine profile adds that repeated time-saving purchases can lead to complacency ([Harvard Magazine](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/2017/10/buying-things-and-happiness)).

(Whillans et al., 2016; Mogilner and Norton, 2016.)

### Scope

I did not open Mogilner and Norton 2016. I found no source comparing time affluence directly with wealth as predictors.

**Plain words**
People who prefer time to money report being happier, but that is a correlation. In one small experiment, paying to save time lifted people's mood more than buying things.

**For example**
Spend a small, fixed sum on a task you dislike, such as delivered groceries or a cleaner, and note your mood that evening.

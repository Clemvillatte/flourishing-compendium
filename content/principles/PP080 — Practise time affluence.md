> **Principle 80** — Practise time affluence

Time affluence, the subjective sense of having enough time for what matters, predicts well-being more strongly than material wealth. The practice is not about having more time (which may be impossible) but about spending time on what matters and refusing to trade it for things that do not. Research on time versus money shows that people who prioritise time over money report greater happiness.

(Whillans et al., 2016; Mogilner and Norton, 2016.)

**Plain words** Feeling you have enough time predicts well-being more strongly than wealth. The practice isn't having more time, which may be impossible, but spending what you have on what matters and refusing good trades for bad ones.

*For example:* Turning down a well-paid weekend job to keep Saturday morning free, and feeling rich.

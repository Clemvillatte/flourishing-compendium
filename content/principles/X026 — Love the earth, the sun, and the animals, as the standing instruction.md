> **Principle 26** — Love the earth, the sun, and the animals, as the standing instruction

Whitman's 1855 preface, stated as a list of direct commands: love the earth and sun and the animals, despise riches, give alms to everyone that asks, take off your hat to nothing known or unknown, re-examine all you have been told at school or church or in any book, and your very flesh shall be a great poem. The passage is the Transcendentalists' summary of the whole stance, and this epilogue's closing principle for the same reason: it is a curriculum (love, despise, give, salute, re-examine) compact enough to memorize and long enough to live. Practically, take the five commands as a month's five weekly themes, one each.

(Whitman, Leaves of Grass, the 1855 preface.)

**Plain words** Whitman's curriculum, as direct commands: love the earth, sun, and animals; despise riches; give alms to everyone that asks; take off your hat to nothing known or unknown; re-examine all you have been told — and your very flesh shall be a great poem. It's the whole stance in one list: love, give, bow to nothing, and re-verify everything.

*For example:* Someone who runs the list literally for a month — alms when asked, hat-tip to no authority as such, every inherited belief re-checked — and reports the strange result: they like who they're becoming.

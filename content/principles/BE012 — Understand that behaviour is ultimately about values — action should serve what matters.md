> **Principle 12** — Understand that behaviour is ultimately about values: action should serve what matters

Behaviour is not an end in itself. It serves values and goals. Behavioural change without [[Values clarification|values clarification]] is mechanical; values without behavioural change is wishful. The two together create meaningful change.

**Plain words** Behaviour serves values or it's just motion. The techniques here are powerful — they can build any habit, including ones that hollow you out. The question under every technique: is this in service of what actually matters to me?

*For example:* The person who optimises their mornings perfectly and spends the reclaimed hours on things they don't care about — the machine works beautifully, and it's pointed at nothing. Aim before you accelerate.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Values|Values-based behaviour (ACT)]]**, committed action toward values is the core behavioural process (Hayes et al., 2011; Wilson et al., 2010)
- **Self-concordant goals and behaviour**, behaviours aligned with values are more sustainable (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia as virtuous activity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **[[Right action serves right view|Right action serves right view]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires examined values** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Connect behaviours to values: why does this matter? What does this serve? Behavioural change without values is empty; values without behavioural change is impotent. Do both.

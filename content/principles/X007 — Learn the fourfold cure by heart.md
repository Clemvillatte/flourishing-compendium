> **Principle 7** — Learn the fourfold cure by heart

The tradition's own summary of its whole teaching (the tetrapharmakos, as preserved through its ancient epitomes): do not fear the gods; do not fear death; what is good is easy to get; what is terrible is easy to endure. The four lines are a compact cognitive therapy, each targeting a specific chronic fear, and the tradition treats memorizing them as the treatment's method. Practically, learn the four lines by heart, and apply the relevant line at the relevant fear.

(The tetrapharmakos as preserved in the Herculaneum inscription and Philodemus's tradition; the Principal Doctrines.)

**Plain words** Four lines, learned by heart, each aimed at a chronic fear: don't fear the gods; don't fear death; the good things are easy to get; the terrible things are easy to endure. When a specific fear shows up, apply the matching line. Memorization is the treatment.

*For example:* Someone lying awake at 3 a.m. dreading mortality runs the second line like a mantra — and the dread, having nothing to argue with, thins out.

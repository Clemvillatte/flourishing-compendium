> **Principle 7** — Learn the fourfold cure by heart

The four lines come from a damaged papyrus, Herculaneum Papyrus 1005, column IV, lines 10-14. The work is by the Epicurean Philodemus, not by Epicurus ([Gilleland, with the Greek and the papyrus location](https://laudatortemporisacti.blogspot.com/2013/03/the-epicurean-tetrapharmakos.html)). In Hutchinson's translation they read:

> Don't fear god, / Don't worry about death; / What is good is easy to get, / What is terrible is easy to endure.
>
> (Philodemus, PHerc. 1005, in Hutchinson's translation, as [quoted by Epicurus Today](https://www.epicurustoday.com/introduction/tetrapharmakon/).)

### What Epicurus himself wrote

Each line has a root in Epicurus. Principal Doctrine 1 says a blessed being "has no trouble himself and brings no trouble upon any other being", and PD 2 says "Death is nothing to us" ([Hicks, Diogenes Laertius X](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0258:book=10:chapter=1)). The last two lines echo Menoeceus 133, where the wise person "understands how easily the limit of good things can be reached and attained, and how either the duration or the intensity of evils is but slight" ([Hicks](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0258:book=10:chapter=1)).

Epicurus also asks for memorising. In the Letter to Herodotus he says the main heads must be continually returned to and memorized, so that the details can be rebuilt from them ([Hicks, X.35-36](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0258:book=10:chapter=1)).

### A caution about the slogan

The papyrus's title is mostly lost, and the context of the four lines is disputed. Epicurus Today reports that Sbordone read them as an example of the shortcuts Philodemus was warning against, while Angeli attributes them to Philodemus himself ([Epicurus Today](https://www.epicurustoday.com/introduction/tetrapharmakon/)). So the card is a memory aid for the arguments, not a replacement for them.

**Plain words**
Write the four lines on a card and keep them where the fear shows up. Next to each line, write the reason Epicurus gives for it. A slogan you cannot back up will not hold at 3 a.m.; a slogan with its reason attached gives the fear something to argue with.

**For example**
Beside "Don't worry about death" write PD 2: "the body, when it has been resolved into its elements, has no feeling, and that which has no feeling is nothing to us" ([Hicks](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0258:book=10:chapter=1)). When the dread comes, read the line and the reason together.

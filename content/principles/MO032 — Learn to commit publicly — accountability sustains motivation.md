> **Principle 32** — Learn to commit publicly: accountability sustains motivation

Telling others your goal helps, but the evidence is narrower than "public commitment is powerful". A meta-analysis of 141 randomised studies (384 effect sizes, N = 16,523) found a small positive effect of goal setting (d = .34), larger when the goal was difficult, set publicly, or a group goal ([Epton, Currie and Armitage, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29189034/)). The same review found weaker evidence for outside monitoring without feedback.

A second meta-analysis found that progress monitoring worked better when outcomes were "reported or made public" ([Harkin et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/)).

Both are about behaviour change, mostly health goals. They do not show that announcing a goal on its own produces follow-through.

**Plain words**
Other people knowing what you are aiming for can keep you honest. What counts is that someone will ask how it went.

**For example**
Tell one person your goal, the date, and the check-in: "By Friday I'll have the first draft of section two. Ask me on Saturday." Report back, whichever way it went.

## Psychology parallels

- **Public commitment and goal attainment**, telling others increases follow-through (Cialdini, 2001; Bayer et al., 2010)
- **Accountability partners**, having someone to report to increases goal attainment (Wing & Jeffery, 1999; Rao et al., 2017)

## Practice

- Commit publicly: tell someone your goal, use an accountability partner, join a group. The social commitment increases follow-through. Do not rely solely on private willpower.

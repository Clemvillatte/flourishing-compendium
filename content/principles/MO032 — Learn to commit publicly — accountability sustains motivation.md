> **Principle 32** — Learn to commit publicly: accountability sustains motivation

Public commitment — telling others about your goals — increases the likelihood of following through. Social accountability (others knowing your goal) is a powerful motivational force.

**Plain words** Tell people your goal — public commitment adds social weight to the follow-through. Not for applause; for the mild discomfort of going back on your word. Use the species' pride as scaffolding.

*For example:* "I'm running the 10k in May" said to five friends — and suddenly May has witnesses. The training runs get done because the alternative is a conversation you'd rather avoid.
## Psychology parallels

- **Public commitment and goal attainment**, telling others increases follow-through (Cialdini, 2001; Bayer et al., 2010)
- **Accountability partners**, having someone to report to increases goal attainment (Wing & Jeffery, 1999; Rao et al., 2017)

## Practice

- Commit publicly: tell someone your goal, use an accountability partner, join a group. The social commitment increases follow-through. Do not rely solely on private willpower.

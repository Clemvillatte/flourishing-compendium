> **Principle 350** — Practise the distinction between problem and mystery

Recognise that some questions are problems (solvable by technique) and others are mysteries (which cannot be solved but only entered). The meaning of life, the nature of love, the experience of suffering — these are mysteries, not problems. Do not approach them with the attitude of a technician. Enter them with the attitude of a participant.

(Marcel, 1951b, The Mystery of Being; Marcel, 1967, The Philosophy of Existentialism, "On the Ontological Mystery.")

**Plain words** Marcel's distinction: problems get solved by technique; mysteries get entered. The meaning of life, love, suffering — you can't fix these, only live inside them. Approaching a mystery with a technician's attitude is the error; participation is the mode.

*For example:* Someone who tries to optimise their grief into "closure" with worksheets and timelines — and finally sits shiva with the family, entering it, and the mystery does what no worksheet could.

> **Principle 350** — Practise the distinction between problem and mystery

Marcel's canonical statement of the distinction, from Being and Having:

> "A problem is something which I meet, which I find completely before me, but which I can therefore lay siege to and reduce. But a mystery is something in which I am myself involved, and it can therefore only be thought of as a sphere where the distinction between what is in me and what is before me loses its meaning and initial validity." (Marcel, Being and Having, trans. Katharine Farrer, p. 117, as quoted in [Treanor and Sweetman, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/))

The test is the questioner. Treanor and Sweetman explain the difference: with a problem, "the identity of the person asking the question is not an issue", because all the relevant information is before the questioner; with a mystery, "the identity of the questioner is tied to the question and, therefore, the questioner is not interchangeable" ([Treanor and Sweetman, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/)). Marcel also describes a mystery as a "problem that encroaches on its own data" (Marcel, The Philosophy of Existentialism, p. 19, as quoted in [Treanor and Sweetman, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/)). His own example of the difference, on evil:

> "Evil which is only stated or observed is no longer evil which is suffered: in fact, it ceases to be evil. In reality, I can only grasp it as evil in the measure in which it touches me—that is to say, in the measure in which I am involved… being 'involved' is the fundamental fact." (Marcel, The Philosophy of Existentialism, p. 19, as quoted in [Treanor and Sweetman, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/))

He also distinguishes mystery from the unknowable: "The mysterious is not the unknowable, the unknowable is only the limiting case of the problematic" (Marcel, Being and Having, p. 118, as quoted in [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/)).

(Marcel, Being and Having; The Philosophy of Existentialism, as carried in the opened SEP entry.)

**Plain words** A technical question can be handed to another person without changing what is being asked. A mystery includes your own involvement; removing yourself would change the question. Marcel is distinguishing kinds of inquiry, not simply naming questions we have not solved yet.

*For example:* Imagine asking what commitment to someone you love means for you. Replacing you with another questioner would change the question.

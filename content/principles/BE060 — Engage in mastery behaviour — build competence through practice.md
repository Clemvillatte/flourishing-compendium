> **Principle 60** — Engage in mastery behaviour: build competence through practice

Mastery behaviour — practising skills, learning, improving — is associated with self-efficacy, competence, and well-being. It is a specific behavioural practice.

**Plain words** Mastery behaviour — practising, learning, improving at anything — builds self-efficacy, and self-efficacy is the base layer of confidence everywhere. Get good at anything, and the competence leaks into everything.

*For example:* The person who learns to bake bread and finds themselves slightly braver at work — the skill was bread; the transfer was "I can learn hard things." That's the mechanism.
## Psychology parallels

- **Mastery activities and depression**, mastery-oriented behaviour is antidepressant (Martell et al., 2001; Dimidjian et al., 2011)
- **[[Deliberate practice|Deliberate practice (Ericsson)]]**, structured practice builds expertise (Ericsson et al., 1993)
- **Self-efficacy through mastery**, mastery experiences build self-efficacy (Bandura, 1997)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The unity of the virtues|Virtue as built capacity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **Craft and excellence** (Dreyfus & Dreyfus)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort: mastery]]** (Buddhism)

## Practice

- Engage in mastery behaviour: practise a skill deliberately (music, language, craft, sport). Structure the practice (specific goals, feedback, gradual increase). Mastery builds well-being.

> **Principle 3** — Know that heart rate variability (HRV) is a marker of autonomic flexibility and health

HRV is the beat-to-beat variation in the heart, and the overview paper's line has become the field's summary:

> "A healthy heart is not a metronome." (Shaffer & Ginsberg, 2017)

The technical definition: "Heart rate variability (HRV) consists of changes in the time intervals between consecutive heartbeats called interbeat intervals (IBIs)." The oscillations "allow the cardiovascular system to rapidly adjust to sudden physical and psychological challenges to homeostasis", and the overview links lower HRV to "common mental (e.g., stress, depression, anxiety, PTSD) and physical disorders" that "create a self-perpetuating cycle that produces autonomic imbalance and greater allostatic load." The training side: biofeedback has participants breathe slowly at the rate that maximises the oscillations, "approximately 0.1 Hz (six breaths per minute)" (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014).

**Plain words** Heart rate variability, the tiny beat-to-beat variation in your heart, is a fitness score for your nervous system's flexibility. Higher is better: more resilience, better regulation. The good news: it is trainable.

*For example* The wearable showing HRV in the basement every Monday: the body keeping score of the weekend's chaos. Not a medical device; a mirror. A useful one.

## Psychology parallels

- **HRV as autonomic flexibility index**, the oscillations of a healthy heart allow the cardiovascular system to rapidly adjust to sudden physical and psychological challenges to homeostasis (Shaffer & Ginsberg, 2017; Thayer et al., 2012)
- **HRV biofeedback**, the procedure feeds back beat-by-beat heart rate during slow breathing to maximize RSA; maximum effects at approximately 0.1 Hz (six breaths per minute) (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014; Lehrer et al., 2020)

## Practice

- Practise HRV biofeedback: breathe slowly at about six breaths per minute, watching your heart-rate curve and aiming for the smooth sine-wave-like peaks and valleys. In the paper's protocol, people practise twice daily at home over about a 3-month period.

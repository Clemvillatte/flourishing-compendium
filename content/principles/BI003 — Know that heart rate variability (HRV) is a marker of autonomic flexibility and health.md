> **Principle 3** — Know that heart rate variability (HRV) is a marker of autonomic flexibility and health

Heart rate variability — the variation in time between heartbeats — reflects autonomic flexibility. Higher HRV is associated with better emotion regulation, stress resilience, and health. HRV can be improved through breathing practices.

**Plain words** Heart rate variability — the tiny beat-to-beat variation in your heart — is a fitness score for your nervous system's flexibility. Higher is better: more resilience, better regulation. The good news: it's trainable.

*For example:* The wearable showing HRV in the basement every Monday — the body keeping score of the weekend's chaos. Not a medical device; a mirror. Useful one, though.
## Psychology parallels

- **HRV as autonomic flexibility index**, higher HRV is associated with better emotion regulation and health (Thayer et al., 2012; Laborde et al., 2017)
- **HRV biofeedback improves mental health**, breathing at ~6 breaths per minute increases HRV and reduces stress, anxiety, and depression (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014; Lehrer et al., 2020)
- **HRV-BF produces small-to-moderate reductions in anxiety and depressive symptoms in non-clinical and at-risk populations** (Cambridge, 2020-2025 synthesis)

## Practice

- Practise HRV biofeedback: breathe at ~6 breaths per minute (5 seconds in, 5 seconds out) for 10-20 minutes daily. This increases HRV and improves autonomic flexibility. Use an HRV app or device for feedback.

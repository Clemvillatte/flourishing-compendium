> **Principle 38** — Learn to manage blood sugar: stability supports mood

The mechanism story is easy to overstate, and the findings are narrower than the folklore. David Benton's review of carbohydrate and mood reported two associations: people with lower blood glucose reported poorer mood while doing cognitively demanding tasks, and a tendency for blood glucose to fall rapidly was associated with irritability across a range of situations ([Benton, 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12034132/)). A randomised trial in 74 school children compared breakfasts by glycaemic index and glycaemic load: 95 to 140 minutes after eating, the children who got the low-GI meals reported feeling more alert and happy, and less nervous, than after the high-GI meals ([Micha et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736777/)).

> "There is a tendency for those with lower blood glucose, when performing cognitively demanding tasks, to report poorer mood. In a range of situations an association between a tendency for blood glucose levels to fall rapidly, and irritability, has been found." (Abstract; Benton, 2002, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 26(3): 293-308, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12034132/))

> "Low-GI meals predicted feeling more alert and happy, and less nervous and thirsty (P < 0·05 for each); high-GL meals predicted feeling more confident, and less sluggish, hungry and thirsty (P < 0·05 for each)." (Abstract, Results; Micha et al., 2011, Br J Nutr 106(10): 1552-61, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736777/))

**Plain words**
An irritable afternoon is not a measurement of blood glucose. Benton's review reports associations, while the breakfast trial concerns schoolchildren; neither lets an adult identify a glucose problem from mood alone ([Benton, 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12034132/); [Micha et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736777/)). Record the meal and the later feeling as separate observations.

**For example**
(Imagined) After a lunch that is mostly refined carbohydrate, someone notices their late-afternoon mood and energy without assuming the meal caused them. A note kept for a week or two is their own evidence, not the trial's.

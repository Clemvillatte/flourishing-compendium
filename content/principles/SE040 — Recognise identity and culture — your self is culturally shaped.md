> **Principle 40** — Recognise identity and culture: your self is culturally shaped

Identity is not purely personal; it is shaped by culture. What counts as a "good" self, what roles are valued, what identities are available — these are culturally specific. Understanding this prevents both arrogance (assuming your culture's self is universal) and confusion (not knowing which self is yours).

**Plain words** Identity is not purely personal — it's shaped by culture: what counts as a "good" self, which roles are valued, which identities are available. Understanding this prevents two errors: universalising your self-rules, and mistaking cultural limits for personal limits.

*For example:* The person whose culture says ambition is vulgar and whose self-judgment has been quietly running that rule — and the discovery that the "limit" was a setting, not a ceiling. The self was culturally furnished; the furniture can be rearranged.

## Practice

- Recognise that your identity is partly cultural. If you are bicultural or multicultural, work on integration rather than choosing one. If monocultural, remember that your way of being a self is not the only way.

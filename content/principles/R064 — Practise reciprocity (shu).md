> **Principle 326** — Practise reciprocity (shu)

Do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire. Tsze-kung asked whether one word could serve as a rule of practice for all of life, and the Master named it: "Is not RECIPROCITY such a word? What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others." (Confucius, The Analects 15.24, tr. Legge, https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf1.htm.) The same rule appears inside his instructions on virtue: "not to do to others as you would not wish done to yourself" (12.2).

The brake has an engine behind it. Asked what perfect virtue looks like, Confucius answered: "Now the man of perfect virtue, wishing to be established himself, seeks also to establish others; wishing to be enlarged himself, he seeks also to enlarge others." (6.30.) Reciprocity runs in both directions off one question: how would this land if it landed on me?

(Confucius, The Analects 6.30, 12.2, 15.24; [[The doctrine of the mean|The Doctrine of the Mean]], ch. 13.)

**Plain words** The rule cuts both ways at once. Whatever you would hate arriving in your own inbox, stop sending it; whatever you would love arriving, start passing it on. Restraint and help are the same skill, run in opposite directions.

*For example:* The person who stops forwarding the cruel joke because they know exactly how it feels to be its subject — and starts forwarding the opportunity because they know how that lands too.

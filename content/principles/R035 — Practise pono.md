> **Principle 297** — Practise pono

Te Aka Māori Dictionary gives pono as an adjective meaning "be true, valid, honest, genuine, sincere" and as a noun meaning "truth, non-fiction, validity" ([Te Aka, pono](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/search?keywords=pono)).

(Māori tikanga; Mead, 2003, Tikanga Māori; Durie, 1994, Whaiora.)

### What I could confirm

The dictionary supports the plain sense of the principle: truth, honesty, genuineness. It does not describe how pono operates in tikanga, for instance its link with tika (what is correct) or its role in leadership. The sources listed in the locator (Mead 2003; Durie 1994) were not opened, so this page makes no claims about them.

**Plain words**
Say what is so, and do what you said you would. Honesty here is about being reliable, not about being blunt.

**For example**
For one week, write down each commitment you make, such as "I'll send it Friday". On Friday, mark each as kept or not kept, and tell the person if one slipped.

> **Principle 46** — Practice fasting of the mind (xinzhai)

The source is the Yan Hui dialogue in Zhuangzi 4. Yan Hui plans to reform a violent ruler; asked how, he offers his preparations, and is told "All this only shows (in you) the mind of a teacher!" Asked for the method instead, Confucius calls it fasting. When Hui protests that his household has fasted for months, "For months together we have no spirituous drink, nor do we taste the proscribed food or any strong-smelling vegetables", the reply is: "It is the fasting appropriate to sacrificing, but it is not the fasting of the mind."

> "You will not wait for the hearing of your ears about it, but for the hearing of your mind. You will not wait even for the hearing of your mind, but for the hearing of the spirit. Let the hearing (of the ears) rest with the ears. Let the mind rest in the verification (of the rightness of what is in the will). But the spirit is free from all pre-occupation and so waits for (the appearance of) things. Where the (proper) course is, there is freedom from all pre-occupation; such freedom is the fasting of the mind." (Zhuangzi 4, trans. James Legge, [ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The passage's order runs ears, then mind, then spirit, and each stage is described as doing less: the ears rest, the mind rests in verification, the spirit is free from pre-occupation and waits for things. The principle's "qi" is Legge's "spirit" here. The chapter adds an image for the emptied mind: "Look at that aperture (left in the wall); the empty apartment is filled with light through it."

(Zhuangzi 4.)

**Plain words** The dialogue says the spirit is free from pre-occupation, and that this freedom is what lets it wait for things. In conversation terms, the response you are rehearsing is the pre-occupation. Dropping it is the fast, and it is harder than skipping a meal because the rehearsal feels like preparation. The practice: arrive at a familiar conversation with nothing prepared, and see what comes in.

*For example:* In your next hard conversation, notice when your attention is on your rebuttal rather than on the words arriving. That noticing is the fast beginning.

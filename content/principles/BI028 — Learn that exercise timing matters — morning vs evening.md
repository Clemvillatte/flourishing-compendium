> **Principle 28** — Learn that exercise timing matters: morning vs evening

Exercise shifts the body clock, and the direction depends on the hour. In a phase-response-curve study of 99 aerobically fit adults doing 60 minutes of moderate treadmill exercise at one of eight times of day, peak phase advances of melatonin timing came with exercise at 7:00 am and from 1:00 to 4:00 pm, and delays with exercise from 7:00 to 10:00 pm, with amplitudes comparable to bright light of equal duration. Morning exercise, on this evidence, nudges the clock earlier, which is the direction an evening person usually wants.

The sleep side of the old advice does not hold up as a blanket rule. A meta-analysis of 23 studies found evening exercise did not negatively affect sleep: it increased REM latency and slow-wave sleep and decreased stage 1 sleep. The qualifier is narrow: sleep-onset latency, total sleep time and sleep efficiency might be impaired after vigorous exercise ending within an hour of bedtime.

> "Overall, the studies reviewed here do not support the hypothesis that evening exercise negatively affects sleep, in fact rather the opposite. However, sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, and SE might be impaired after vigorous exercise ending ≤ 1 h before bedtime." (Stutz, Eiholzer & Spengler 2019, [Sports Medicine](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/e97ceae9-be5e-4ed8-ad86-dd282b3fa7ed/content), Conclusion)

**Plain words**
Two practical readings replace the old rule. If your clock runs late and you want it earlier, morning or mid-afternoon exercise is the phase-advancing window the study found. If you train in the evening, the meta-analysis says disruption is not the default: what might cost you is vigorous work ending inside the last hour before bed. So the rule is not "never train at night"; it is "give the hard session an hour of margin".

*For example:* An imagined case: a night owl who trains at 9 p.m. and sleeps at 10:30 moves the vigorous part of the session earlier and keeps the wind-down gentle. The change is a margin calculation from the meta-analysis's qualifier, not a new religion about mornings.

## Psychology parallels

- **Exercise timing and sleep**, morning exercise supports circadian rhythm; evening vigorous exercise can disrupt sleep (Buman et al., 2014; Dolezal et al., 2017)
- **Morning outdoor exercise is doubly beneficial**, combines light exposure and movement (Wright et al., 2013; Youngstedt et al., 2019)

## Practice

- Time exercise: morning or early-afternoon exercise is the phase-advancing window in the study; if you train in the evening, the qualifier concerns vigorous exercise ending within an hour of bedtime. Outdoor morning exercise also adds daylight, the other clock lever (see BE043).

> **Principle 28** — Learn that exercise timing matters: morning vs evening

Exercise timing affects sleep and circadian rhythm. Morning exercise (especially outdoors) supports circadian entrainment. Evening vigorous exercise can disrupt sleep in some people.

**Plain words** Exercise timing interacts with your clock — morning outdoor exercise anchors the rhythm, late-evening vigour can disrupt sleep in some people. Experiment: the body keeps records. Find your window and defend it.

*For example:* The 6 a.m. runner whose sleep improves within a fortnight — the run wasn't just exercise, it was a clock-setter. The body loves a schedule more than it loves the workout.
## Psychology parallels

- **Exercise timing and sleep**, morning exercise supports circadian rhythm; evening vigorous exercise can disrupt sleep (Buman et al., 2014; Dolezal et al., 2017)
- **Morning outdoor exercise is doubly beneficial**, combines light exposure and movement (Wright et al., 2013; Youngstedt et al., 2019)

## Practice

- Time exercise: morning outdoor exercise (walking, running) is ideal (combines light and movement). If you exercise in the evening, finish at least 3 hours before bed or keep it gentle.

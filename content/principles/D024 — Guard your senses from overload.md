> **Principle 24** — Guard your senses from overload

In Legge's Daodejing 12, colors, music and flavors are described as impairing the senses, while racing and hunting madden the mind. The warning extends to rare objects: pursuing them can lead people into wrongdoing.

> Colour's five hues from th' eyes their sight will take;
> Music's five notes the ears as deaf can make;
> The flavours five deprive the mouth of taste;
> The chariot course, and the wild hunting waste
> Make mad the mind; and objects rare and strange,
> Sought for, men's conduct will to evil change.
>
> (Daodejing 12, Legge)

Legge ends the chapter with the sage seeking to satisfy the belly and rejecting the eyes' insatiable longing. The compendium reads this contrast as a prompt to consider what a pursuit is serving and when it has received enough attention.

(DDJ 12.)

**Plain words**
If you enjoy an activity, you can still decide how much attention to give it. Ask what you wanted from it and whether that purpose has been served. More being available is not, by itself, a reason to continue.

*For example:* Imagine opening your phone to check a train time. Once you have the answer, put the phone away rather than continuing through unrelated posts.

> **Principle 68** — Engage in legacy behaviour: contribute beyond yourself

Legacy behaviour — creating things that outlast you, mentoring, contributing to future generations, building institutions — is associated with meaning, generativity, and purpose. It is a specific meaningful behaviour.

**Plain words** Legacy behaviour — building things that outlast you, mentoring, planting for the next people — connects you to time bigger than your own. It's the antidote to the shrinking horizon. Contribute past your own expiry date.

*For example:* The gardener planting trees whose shade she'll never sit in — and knowing that's the point. The self gets small and the life gets large at exactly the same moment.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Generativity theory|Generativity (Erikson)]]**, contributing to future generations is a developmental task of midlife and beyond (Erikson, 1968; McAdams, 2001)
- **Legacy and meaning**, creating a legacy is a source of meaning (McAdams, 2001; Steger, 2012)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Generativity vs stagnation** (Erikson, The Life Cycle Completed)
- **[[The eternal contribution|The eternal contribution]]** (Nietzsche, eternal recurrence)
- **Planting trees under whose shade you will never sit** (proverb)

## Practice

- Engage in legacy behaviour: mentor, create things that outlast you, contribute to institutions and future generations. Legacy is a source of meaning.

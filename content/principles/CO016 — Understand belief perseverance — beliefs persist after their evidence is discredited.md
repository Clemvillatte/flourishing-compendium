> **Principle 16** — Understand belief perseverance: beliefs persist after their evidence is discredited

Even when the original evidence for a belief is debunked, the belief often persists. This is why fact-checking alone rarely changes minds. Beliefs are embedded in networks of other beliefs, identity, and emotion.

**Plain words** Belief perseverance: even when the original evidence is debunked, the belief often persists — beliefs live in networks of other beliefs, values, and identities. This is why fact-checking alone rarely changes minds. Address the network, not just the fact.

*For example:* The rumour that gets debunked and believed anyway — because the believer's trust in the source, distrust of the establishment, and sense of the world are all load-bearing. Correcting the fact does nothing; the fact was never carrying the weight.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Belief perseverance effect|Belief perseverance effect]]**, beliefs persist after their evidential basis is removed (Ross et al., 1975; Anderson et al., 1980)
- **The backfire effect is contested**, early claims that corrections strengthen false beliefs are not consistently supported; corrections often help but effects are partial (Nyhan & Reifler, 2010; Wood & Porter, 2019)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Paradigm shifts are slow** (Kuhn, The Structure of Scientific Revolutions)
- **Conversion as transformation, not argument** (James, The Varieties of Religious Experience)

## Practice

- When correcting a false belief (yours or someone else's), do not just remove the false evidence. Replace it with an alternative explanation. Beliefs abhor a vacuum.

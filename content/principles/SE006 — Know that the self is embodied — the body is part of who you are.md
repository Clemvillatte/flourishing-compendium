> **Principle 6** — Know that the self is embodied: the body is part of who you are

The self is not a disembodied mind; it is an embodied being. Your body — posture, energy, health, appearance, interoceptive signals — is part of your self-concept and affects how you relate to yourself. Self and body are not separate.

**Plain words** The self is embodied: posture, energy, health, interoceptive signals — part of your self-concept, affecting how you relate to yourself. The body isn't the self's vehicle; it's part of the self. Treat it accordingly.

*For example:* The person whose entire self-sense shifts with the chronic pain flare — not "I feel bad" but "I am worse" — and the reframe that helps: the self is running on different hardware this week. The body was always part of the identity; the identity needs the body's maintenance schedule.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Embodied self|Embodied self]]**, bodily states and interoception shape self-concept and self-awareness (Barrett et al., 2019; Craig, 2009)
- **Body image and self-esteem**, body image is a central domain of self-evaluation, particularly in Western cultures (Cash & Pruzinsky, 2002; Tylka, 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The lived body|The lived body (Leib)]]** (Husserl, Ideas II; Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception)
- **[[Yangsheng (cultivating vitality)|Yangsheng (body cultivation)]]** (Daoism, Neiye)
- **A sound mind in a sound body** (Juvenal, Satires)

## Practice

- Include your body in your sense of self. Care for it (sleep, nutrition, movement) not as maintenance of a machine but as care of the self. Notice how body state affects self-perception.

> **Principle 386** — Practise ecological grief work

Cunsolo and Ellis define ecological grief as "the grief felt in relation to experienced or anticipated ecological losses, including the loss of species, ecosystems and meaningful landscapes due to acute or chronic environmental change". They argue it is "a natural response to ecological losses, particularly for people who retain close living, working and cultural relationships to the natural environment" ([Cunsolo and Ellis 2018, Nature Climate Change](https://www.uwosh.edu/sirt/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Cunsolo-and-Ellis-2018.pdf)). Their evidence comes from their own research in Northern Canada and the Australian Wheatbelt, plus a literature synthesis, and they call the topic "an underdeveloped area of inquiry" ([abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41558-018-0092-2)).

Glenn Albrecht's related term, solastalgia, is "the distress that is produced by environmental change impacting on people while they are directly connected to their home environment". In his drought and open-cut mining studies in rural New South Wales, people "experienced negative affect that is exacerbated by a sense of powerlessness or lack of control" ([Albrecht et al. 2007, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18027145/)). A later review says Albrecht clarified that solastalgia "was never intended to constitute a medically diagnosable psychiatric illness" ([Cambridge, Transnational Environmental Law](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/transnational-environmental-law/article/solastalgia-a-new-type-of-harm-for-a-more-sustainable-environmental-liability-framework/D2C31EB61850CCFCCDC5737D278F76DB)).

(Cunsolo and Landman, 2017, "Mourning Nature," in Mourning Nature; Albrecht, 2019, Earth Emotions: New Words for a New World.)

### Limits

The link from grief to action is a hope in these sources, not a measured result. One secondary article reports that Albrecht sees solastalgia as something that "invites grieving and then a call to action" ([Taylor and Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17524032.2024.2427289)). I did not open Albrecht's book or the Cunsolo and Landman volume. Persistent distress that disrupts sleep, work or relationships is a reason to talk to a health professional.

**Plain words**
Grief over a lost place makes sense when the place mattered to you. Name it, talk about it with someone, and see whether it points toward something you want to protect.

**For example**
Write down one place that has changed and what you valued in it. Then look for a local group working on that kind of place, such as a landcare or bush regeneration group, and go once.

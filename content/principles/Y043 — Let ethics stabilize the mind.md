> **Principle 43** — Let ethics stabilize the mind

Patanjali places ethics first in his sequence. Sutra 2.29 lists eight aids: "Abstentions and observances and postures and regulations-of-the-breath and withdrawal-of-the-senses and fixed attention and contemplation and concentration are the eight aids", and the commentary adds "The following up of these must be performed in succession" (trans. Woods, p. 177). The abstentions come before any technique of sitting or concentration.

### What the abstentions are

Sutra 2.30 names them: "Abstinence from injury and from falsehood and from theft and from incontinence and from acceptance of gifts" (Woods, p. 178). Vyasa treats non-injury as the root: "the other abstentions and observances are rooted in it", taught "in order to teach it" (p. 178). He also says truthful speech must be considered "[first] what is good for all creatures" (p. 178). Sutra 2.31 says that when these are "unqualified by species or place or time or exigency", they form "the Great Course-of-conduct" (p. 180).

### A related Gita point

In Arnold's translation, Krishna also puts moderation before yoga: "Religion is not his who too much fasts or too much feasts, nor his who sleeps away an idle mind" (ch. 6).

### What the sources do not say

Neither text says that an unsettled conscience is what stalls meditation, or recommends auditing conduct when practice stalls. That is the principle's interpretation of the sequence.

**Plain words** The texts place the ethical limits before the sitting practices, as steps in order. The conduct rules are treated as part of the path, not as extras. It is reasonable to look at your own conduct first when meditation goes badly, though that rule of thumb is modern, not quoted.

*For example:* Imagined example: you sit every morning but keep stalling on a lie you told a colleague. Putting it right does not guarantee calm, but it removes one thing that your mind keeps returning to.

(Yoga Sutras 2.29-2.31 with Vyasa's Bhasya, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 177-180, Internet Archive; Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, Project Gutenberg #2388; both opened 2026-10-21. Sutra 2.28 not read.)

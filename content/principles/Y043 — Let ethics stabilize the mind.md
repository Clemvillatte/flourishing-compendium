> **Principle 43** — Let ethics stabilize the mind

The tradition's structural claim, worth stating as its own principle: the restraints and observances are not moral decoration around the real work of meditation; they are the conditions of a mind steady enough to meditate. A mind at war with its conduct cannot settle, because it has ongoing business. Practically, when meditation stalls, audit conduct first; a working rule of thumb: a stuck sit is often an unsettled account.

(YS 2.28-2.32, the limbs defined as a sequence; BG 6.16-6.17, moderation before meditation.)

**Plain words** The structural claim: the restraints and observances aren't moral decoration around the real work of meditation — they're the conditions of a mind steady enough to meditate. A mind at war with its conduct cannot settle. Ethics is the equipment, not the garnish.

*For example:* The meditator who sits beautifully for years while running a covertly dishonest business — and wonders why the practice never deepens. The tradition's answer was written first: the war comes to the cushion.

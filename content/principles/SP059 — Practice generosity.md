> **Principle 59** — Practice generosity

The second mark of strength is generosity (generositas): the desire to aid others and to join them in friendship, again by reason's dictate rather than by pity or mood. Spinoza's generosity is not charity as sacrifice; it is the rational recognition that nothing is more useful to a human being than other human beings, and acting accordingly. Practically, treat your helpfulness as a capacity to train, not a mood to await: one deliberate act of aid per day, however small, with the reason attached.

(Ethics III, P59; definition of Generosity; IV, P35 corollary and P37 scholium.)

**Plain words** Generosity (generositas): the desire to aid others and join them in friendship, by reason's dictate rather than by pity or mood. Not charity as sacrifice — the rational recognition that others' flourishing is yours. The second mark of strength, and it's structural, not sweet.

*For example:* The person who helps the junior colleague for no warm glow — no pity, no mood — just the accurate reading: the team compounds or it doesn't. Spinoza's generosity is colder on the surface and far more reliable underneath.

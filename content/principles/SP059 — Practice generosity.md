> **Principle 59** — Practice generosity

Strength of character (fortitudo), Spinoza says in the note to III, P59, divides into two: courage, which aims at the agent's own good, and "highmindedness (generositas)", which aims at others'. The definition, in Elwes's rendering of the term:

> "By highmindedness I mean the desire whereby every man endeavours, solely under the dictates of reason, to aid other men and to unite them to himself in friendship." (Ethics III, P59 note, [Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

The grounding is IV, P35's corollary: "to man there is nothing more useful than man--nothing, I repeat, more excellent for preserving their being can be wished for by men, than that all should so in all points agree, that the minds and bodies of all should form, as it were, one single mind and one single body". Aided others are allies in your own perseverance, which is why the strong man "should desire for others the good which he seeks for himself" (IV, P73 note). The page's exercise drawn from the account: treat helpfulness as a capacity to train, one deliberate act of aid per day, however small, with the reason attached.

**Plain words**
Generosity for Spinoza is not warm sacrifice. It is the accurate reading of your situation: other people are the most useful thing in your environment, so helping them is not charity from your surplus but investment in the only thing that compounds. The definition is a desire "solely under the dictates of reason": not pity, not mood.

*For example:* Imagined: a mentor who helps a junior colleague for the one reason that holds: the team compounds or it does not. Same help as the warm-hearted one gives, available on days the warm feeling is not.

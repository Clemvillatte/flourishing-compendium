> **Principle 60** — Design digital environments for flourishing

Design digital environments — social media, platforms, and the online public sphere — for adaptive flourishing. The evidence is mixed but concerning: passive social-media use predicts lower well-being (Verduyn et al., 2017; Kross et al., 2013), while active use and connection predict higher well-being (Valkenburg et al., 2022). The design of platforms — algorithmic feeds, engagement metrics, notification — shapes outcomes (see principle 76).

**Plain words** Digital environments are designed environments — feeds, autoplay, engagement metrics. Passive use harms; active connection helps. The environment is adjustable: curate it like a garden, not a weather system you merely endure.

*For example:* The same app, used to message three friends versus scrolled for two hours — two different mental-health outcomes. The tool isn't neutral; the usage pattern is the design.
## Psychology parallels

- [[Verduyn et al|Verduyn et al.]] (2017) on passive vs. active use; Kross et al. (2013) on Facebook and well-being; Valkenburg et al. (2022) on social media and well-being; Twenge et al. (2018) on smartphones and adolescent mental health; the digital-wellbeing literature (Vanden Abeele, 2021).

## Practice

- In personal use, prefer active (messaging, creating) to passive (scrolling) use. In platform design, build for connection and well-being rather than engagement alone. In parenting, treat digital environments as designable (see principle 76).

> **Principle 1** — Understand that the body is the foundation of the mind: physiology shapes psychology

The mind is not separate from the body; it is the body in action. Sleep is the strand with the strongest experimental record:

> "Sleep loss reliably triggers changes in negative (aversive) emotional processing, including irritability, emotional volatility, anxiety and aggression, as well as suicidal ideation, suicide attempts and suicide completion."
> (Krause et al., The sleep-deprived human brain, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6143346/))

A meta-analysis of the mood findings quantifies the direction: sleep loss "moderately increases negative mood states... and has a large, diminishing effect on positive mood states" (Tomaso, Johnson and Nelson, 2021). The compendium's application: when mood or thinking changes, check the physiological basics before interpreting the change as psychological news. (Tomaso, Johnson and Nelson, 2021.)

**Plain words** One strand of the evidence, stated plainly: sleep loss reliably changes emotional processing, and the meta-analytic summary is that it moderately increases negative mood and largely diminishes positive mood. An everyday application: when mood or thinking shifts, check the physiological basics first (sleep, food, movement, illness) before treating the shift as psychological news.

*For example:* Suppose three weeks of short nights and skipped meals coincide with a bleak mood. The practice is to check the physiological basics first, not to diagnose the mood's meaning first; the sleep evidence is the strand the record is clearest on.

- **[[Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI)|Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI)]]**, the immune, nervous, and endocrine systems interact to shape mood and cognition (Ader et al., 1995; Dantzer et al., 2008)
- **Embodied cognition**, the body is not peripheral to mind; it is constitutive (Varela et al., 1991; Barsalou, 2008)

## Practice

- When you notice a psychological change (mood, energy, cognition), check the body first: sleep, food, movement, illness, medication. Often the fastest path to psychological change is through physiology.

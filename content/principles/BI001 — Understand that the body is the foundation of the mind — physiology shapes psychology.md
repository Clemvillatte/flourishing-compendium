> **Principle 1** — Understand that the body is the foundation of the mind: physiology shapes psychology

The mind is not separate from the body; it is the body in action. Sleep, nutrition, exercise, hormones, inflammation, and autonomic state all directly shape mood, cognition, attention, and behaviour. Treating psychological problems without addressing physiology is incomplete.

**Plain words** The mind is the body in action — sleep, food, movement, hormones, inflammation: all of it directly steers mood and thinking. The "psychological" problem is often the body sending a memo. Check the meat before you interrogate the soul.

*For example:* The "existential crisis" that was three weeks of 5-hour sleep and skipped meals — the philosophy improved dramatically after the sandwich and the nap.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI)|Psychoneuroimmunology (PNI)]]**, the immune, nervous, and endocrine systems interact to shape mood and cognition (Ader et al., 1995; Dantzer et al., 2008)
- **Embodied cognition**, the body is not peripheral to mind; it is constitutive (Varela et al., 1991; Barsalou, 2008)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The lived body|The lived body (Leib)]]** (Husserl, Ideas II; Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception)
- **[[Yangsheng (cultivating vitality)|Yangsheng (life-nourishment)]]** (Daoism, Neiye)
- **[[Care|Care of the body as care of the soul]]** (Montaigne, Essays)

## Practice

- When you notice a psychological change (mood, energy, cognition), check the body first: sleep, food, movement, illness, medication. Often the fastest path to psychological change is through physiology.

> **Principle 19** — Let contradiction stand as structure, not error

> Nishida was fond of calling this type of totality, which holds together differences without sublating them into a higher unity, a "self-identity of contradictories."

([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Nishida Kitarō", section 4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nishida-kitaro/). The words in quotation marks are Nishida's term; the sentence is the Encyclopedia's description. Quoted at second hand.)

On this reading, a contradiction is not always a mistake to be resolved. Nishida seeks "the topos or common space that underlies a distinction", such as the one between seeing and acting, and calls the result another case of his "self-identity of contradictories." In the same entry he allows for "a continuity of absolute discontinuities" between self and other.

The Buddhist background is the "logic of soku-hi", "a logic of 'is and is not' or affirmation by way of negation" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, "Kyoto School"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kyoto-school/)). Nishida also says the self is "at bottom, a self-contradiction", and can be itself only through negating and opening itself.

**Plain words** Some pairs of opposites do not cancel out. They belong together and make the whole. Nishida's example is seeing and acting, which an artist does as one thing. Before you try to settle a tension in your life, ask whether it is an error or a structure.

*For example:* Wanting both closeness and independence in a relationship can be treated as a problem to fix. It can also be treated as the shape that relationship has, to be lived with attention.

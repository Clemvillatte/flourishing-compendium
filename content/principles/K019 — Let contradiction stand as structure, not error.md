> **Principle 19** — Let contradiction stand as structure, not error

Nishida's mature logic, self-contradictory identity, holds that the real is not uniform: the one and the many, self and world, individual and community are related as contradictory identity, each requiring the other. This is not tolerance of sloppy thinking; it is the observation that the deepest things (a person, a marriage, a self) are precisely what hold opposites together. Practically, when you find yourself forced to choose between two truths that both seem real, ask whether the thing you are thinking about is a unity-of-opposites before you amputate one side.

(Nishida, the late essays on contradictory self-identity.)

**Plain words** Nishida's later logic says the real isn't uniform. One and many, self and world, individual and community are related as contradictory identity: each needs the other. That's not permission for sloppy thinking. It's noticing that the deepest things, a person, a marriage, a self, hold opposites together.

*For example:* In a good marriage, closeness and independence pull in opposite directions, and the couple that thrives learns to live inside the pull rather than pick a side.

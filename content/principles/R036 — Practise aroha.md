> **Principle 298** — Practise aroha

[Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/414) lists, for the verb, "to love, feel pity, feel concern for, feel compassion, empathise", and for the noun "affection, sympathy, charity, compassion, love, empathy". The word covers more than compassion, though. Stewart, Smith, Diamond, Paul and Hogg write that "aroha is a supreme power and the essence of humanity" and that "to equate pono with respect, tika with integrity, and aroha with compassion falls far short of the full Māori meanings" ([Stewart et al. 2021](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/5920bd9b-32c3-4389-a2ad-fc0e90a25190/content)). The same paper reports that "aroha is based on pono and tika", and that pono and tika "can be superseded by aroha". A column on a similar theme says "aroha is the closest Māori equivalent for love, but the two concepts do not completely match in meaning" ([PESA Agora](https://pesaagora.com/columns/aroha-as-critique/)).

The seed line that aroha is "the affective dimension of tikanga" is a tidy summary that I could not match to any opened source, so I have dropped it as a claim.

For the psychology parallel, Underwood's working definition of compassionate love covers "attitudes and actions related to giving of self for the good of the other". She also says the word "compassion" alone is not a synonym, because "it might imply a focus limited to those who are suffering" ([Underwood, chapter](https://www.lynnunderwood.com/wp-content/uploads/cleancompassionatelovebookchapterblackwell.pdf)). Her construct is therefore close to the "love with action" reading of aroha, and it is a Western research concept, not a translation of aroha.

**Plain words** Aroha is love, sympathy and compassion together, and Māori scholars say English words only cover part of it. In the sources I read, it is tied to truth and to doing the right thing, and it can override both.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. When a flood reaches the marae, people begin cooking before anyone has asked them to. What moves them is care for the people arriving, and it is acted on straight away.

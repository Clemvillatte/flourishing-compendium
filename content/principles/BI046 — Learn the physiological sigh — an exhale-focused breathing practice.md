> **Principle 46** — Learn the physiological sigh: an exhale-focused breathing practice

The physiological sigh (double inhale, extended exhale) is a breathing pattern that emphasises the exhale. In a randomised trial, five minutes of daily practice over one month improved mood and reduced resting respiratory rate (Balban et al., 2023).

**Plain words** The physiological sigh: double inhale through the nose, long exhale through the mouth. A breathing pattern that emphasises the exhale — free, invisible, and in the trial that studied it, five minutes a day for a month lifted mood.

*For example:* Five minutes each morning for a month: in the trial, the people who practised daily ended the month in a better mood than the mindfulness group, with a slower resting breathing rate — the practice, not the knowing about it, did the work.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Physiological sigh (Balban et al., Stanford)|Physiological sigh (Balban et al., Stanford)]]**, a randomised trial of five minutes of daily practice over one month found exhale-focused cyclic sighing improved mood and reduced resting respiratory rate (Balban et al., 2023; [trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/))

## Practice

- Practise the physiological sigh: two quick inhales through the nose, followed by a slow, extended exhale through the mouth, for about five minutes daily. In the trial, daily practice over a month improved mood; it is not a promise.

> **Principle 46** — Learn the physiological sigh: an exhale-focused breathing practice

> Using a mixed-effects model, we show that breathwork, especially the exhale-focused cyclic sighing, produces greater improvement in mood (p < 0.05) and reduction in respiratory rate (p < 0.05) compared with mindfulness meditation. Daily 5-min cyclic sighing has promise as an effective stress management exercise.

(Balban et al., "Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal," Cell Reports Medicine 4(1), 2023, abstract; [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/); full text on [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/).)

The trial's instructions for cyclic sighing were to "inhale slowly" and, "once their lungs were expanded, to inhale again once more to maximally fill their lungs -- even if the second inhale was shorter in duration and smaller in volume than the first, and then to slowly and fully exhale all their breath," repeating for five minutes, ideally with both inhales through the nose and the exhale through the mouth. Of 108 enrolled participants, 30 were randomised to cyclic sighing and 24 to the mindfulness control. The authors describe it as a study originally meant to be exploratory, registered retrospectively, run remotely during the pandemic, limited to four weeks with no follow-up, and too small in each group to compare the breathwork conditions with each other.

The effect was therefore shown for the breathwork conditions combined against mindfulness meditation, with cyclic sighing "especially" strong. The paper's competing-interest statement notes that one author, Andrew Huberman, became an advisor to WHOOP in June 2022. Whether the benefit lasts beyond a month is not known from this paper.

**Plain words** Breathe in through the nose, take a second short sip of air on top, then let the air out slowly. Done for five minutes a day for a month, this lifted mood and slowed resting breathing more than five minutes of mindfulness did. The study was small and early, so it counts as a promising idea rather than a settled result.

*For example (imagined):* Someone sits on the edge of the bed with a timer after waking, breathes this way for five minutes, and keeps a short note of mood each evening to see whether anything shifts.

## Practice

- Practise the physiological sigh: two quick inhales through the nose, followed by a slow, extended exhale through the mouth, for about five minutes daily. In the trial, daily practice over a month improved mood; it is not a promise.

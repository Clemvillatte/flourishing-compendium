> **Principle 102** — Trust the creative thrust of life

Bergson begins Creative Evolution with the changing course of conscious life. Experience accumulates, so even apparently similar situations meet a person whose history has changed. He compares our actions with a painter's work: making the work also modifies the maker ([Creative Evolution, chapter I, pp. 5-7, Mitchell translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26163/26163-h/26163-h.htm)).

> "It is then right to say that what we do depends on what we are; but it is necessary to add also that we are, to a certain extent, what we do, and that we are creating ourselves continually." (Bergson, Creative Evolution, chapter I, Arthur Mitchell translation.)

### Creation without a promised outcome

The book's wider account proposes a vital impulse to explain life's creativity and divergence ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Henri Bergson,” section 5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)). That philosophical proposal does not establish a treatment for low mood. Here, trusting creation means leaving room to attempt something whose result you cannot fully specify beforehand.

(Bergson, Creative Evolution, 1907 — secularized as the tendency of living systems to create.)

### Plain words

You can begin a piece of work without knowing exactly what it will become. Choose an attempt small enough to undertake with the resources you actually have. This is a proposed way to approach making, not a claim that difficulty proves you have unused energy.

### For example

Imagine wanting to write but having no finished argument. Try a paragraph that names the question and one reason it matters, then decide whether to continue, revise, or stop. This hypothetical exercise makes no prediction about improved mood or restored motivation.

> **Principle 3** — Practise measurement humility

Well-being is difficult to measure. Self-report scales are subject to mood at the time of completion, cultural response biases, and the tendency to report what is socially desirable. Physiological measures (heart rate variability, cortisol, inflammatory markers) provide convergent evidence but do not capture the subjective dimension. The solution is not to abandon measurement but to triangulate: use multiple measures, attend to change over time rather than absolute scores, and treat single data points with caution.

(Diener et al., 2018; the MULTIS method, Luhmann, 2017.)

**Plain words** Well-being is hard to measure. Self-report depends on your mood that day, on cultural habits of answering, and on wanting to look good. Body measures like heart-rate variability add something but miss the felt side. Treat any single number with suspicion.

*For example:* Asked "how satisfied are you with your life?" on a sunny Friday, you'd give a higher number than on a rainy Monday.

> **Principle 33** — Repair ruptures promptly

In Gottman's account, a repair attempt is any effort a couple makes "to deescalate the tension during a touchy discussion", such as "Let's take a break" or "Wait, I need to calm down" (Gottman and Silver, The Seven Principles for Making Marriages Work, ch. 2, [chapter text](https://content.csbs.utah.edu/~herrin/sevenprinMMW.ch2.rtf)).

(Gottman, 1999; Driver and Gottman, 2004.)

### What the research shows

Gottman reports that "The failure of repair attempts is an accurate marker for an unhappy future", and that "84 percent of the newlyweds who were high on the four horsemen but repaired effectively were in stable, happy marriages six years later" (same chapter). Adding failed repairs to the four horsemen, he says, lifts divorce prediction from 82 percent "into the 90s". This is Gottman's own summary in a trade book, and the figures are from his team's newlywed samples.

Driver and Gottman's observational study of 49 newlywed couples found that everyday playfulness and enthusiasm went with more humor and affection during conflict, but it was "strictly observational and therefore correlational" ([Family Process 43, 2004](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1545-5300.2004.00024.x)).

The 48-hour window in the principle is a rule of thumb. It does not come from the texts above, which measure repairs inside a conflict conversation, not the days after it.

**Plain words**
After a bad moment, the question is not who was right. It is whether either of you can signal "this matters more than the argument." A small signal, offered early and received, matters more than a long speech given late.

**For example**
After a fight, send a short message that names the distance and asks for a conversation, such as "I don't like this distance. Can we talk tonight?" Do not use the message to restate the argument.

> **Principle 295** — Practise utu

Utu is often translated as revenge, and the sources I opened agree that this is too narrow. Te Ara notes: "A common translation of utu is revenge; however, culturally it refers to returning balance" ([Te Ara, Traditional Māori warfare](https://teara.govt.nz/en/traditional-maori-warfare-riri/print)). A legal article on utu describes it, "at the most general level", as "the process of restoring these physical and spiritual relationships to an equal or harmonious state", and says it "can also involve a positive response to a friendly or gracious act". It quotes Raymond Firth (1929): "for every gift another of at least equal value should be returned" ([Ahu and Hoare, NZ Association for Comparative Law Yearbook 16, 2010](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/law/research/publications/about-nzacl/publications/nzacl-yearbooks/yearbook-16,-2010/03-Ahu-Hoare.pdf)).

The same article warns against reading utu as strict proportion. It cites Patterson: "it is common when giving or taking utu to do or take rather more than was done to or taken from you", and concludes "we should think of a dynamic balance rather than a static balance". It also says that restoring balance can, in certain situations, require "an act of vengeance or punishment". So "a proportional response" is a simplification.

One paper argues that non-violent redress was preferred in pre-1840 Māori society ([Global Policy, abstract and excerpt](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1758-5899.70010)). I read only the excerpt, so treat it as one view.

For the psychology parallel, Gouldner (1960) distinguished reciprocity "as a pattern of mutually contingent exchange of gratifications", a folk belief, and a generalised moral norm, and hypothesised that the moral norm is "one of the universal 'principal components' of moral codes" ([Gouldner 1960, abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/rfo/sfi_rfo/search/publication?pid=10.2307/2092623)). That is a hypothesis, and utu is not the same thing as the norm of reciprocity.

**Plain words** Utu is about keeping relationships in balance. It includes returning a gift as well as answering a wrong, and the scholars I read say the balance is dynamic, not a strict one-for-one score. Revenge is only one of its senses.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A neighbour returns your borrowed ladder with firewood, a little more than the loan was worth. Nothing is said, and the exchange keeps the relationship warm.

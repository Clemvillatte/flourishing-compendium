> **Principle 292** — Practise kotahitanga

Kotahitanga is the Māori word for unity. [Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/3094) glosses it as "unity, togetherness, solidarity, collective action". A teaching resource from Ako Aotearoa, written by Williams, Dayman and Williams (2025), describes it this way: "Kotahitanga refers to the development and maintenance of unity of purpose, shared vision, and collective direction." It adds that it "embodies a commitment by all to work together with one mind and in unison, ensuring that individual strengths and contributions are valued and directed towards a common goal", and "encourages inclusive participation, where everyone has a voice and a role in shaping outcomes" ([Ako Aotearoa, primary-school resource](https://ako.ac.nz/assets/Knowledge-centre/Nga-Taonga-Whakaako-Tikanga-Maori-2025/Nga-Taonga-Whakaako-Tikanga-Maori-2-PRIMARY-Kotahitanga.pdf)).

That wording matters for the principle. The resource speaks of "one mind and in unison" and of "consensus" and also of every voice being heard. The phrase "unity without uniformity of thought" is a fair gloss of the second half, but it is this site's phrasing, not a quotation.

Kotahitanga also names a political history. [Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/kotahitanga-unity-movements/print) describes Kotahitanga movements as "Māori political movements that aim to unify Māori on a pan-tribal basis". In 1892 the Kotahitanga Parliament (Paremata Māori) was set up at Waitangi, with a lower house and an upper house, and it held its final meeting in 1902. Its focus was "legal validation from the New Zealand Parliament and retention of Māori land". Unity there was a strategy for protecting land and standing, not only a mood of harmony.

Bandura's psychology gives a parallel. He writes that perceived collective efficacy is "an emergent group-level property" and that beliefs of collective efficacy "predict level of group performance" ([Bandura 2001, as excerpted](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5f515c06be95ce214b7e209d/t/6160e885b97706442cbc37ed/1633740943508/Bandura,+A.+(2001).+Social+cognitive+theory:+An+agentic+perspective.+Annual+review+of+psychology,+52(1),+1-26.)). That is a different concept from kotahitanga, so treat it as a comparison and not as an equivalent.

**Plain words** Kotahitanga means unity of purpose. In the opened sources, it asks for a shared direction and for everyone to have a voice. It also has a history as a political strategy.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A village committee of elders, young people and sceptics all want different things. They keep meeting until they find one project each can support, and each person's reasons are spoken aloud.

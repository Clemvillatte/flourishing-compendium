> **Principle 1** — See experience as constructed before reacting to it

Patañjali's opening sutras distinguish the seer from the [[Chitta-vritti|mind's changing activities]]: when these cease, the seer abides in itself; otherwise, it takes their form ([Woods, YS 1.2–1.4](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/)). This distinction belongs to classical Yoga's account of mind and self ([Bryant, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yoga/)).

> Yoga is the restriction of the fluctuations of mind-stuff.
>
> Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra 1.2, translated by James Haughton Woods (1914), [reprinted text](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/).

(YS 1.2-1.4.)

### Plain words

As an everyday exercise, describe what happened separately from what you think it means. Before acting, examine the interpretation you are about to treat as fact.

For example: An unanswered message leads you to assume a friend is angry. Before sending an accusation, write down what you know and what you have inferred.
## Psychology parallels

- **Constructed emotion and appraisal**, the finding that affective responses are built from prediction and interpretation rather than received whole (Barrett, 2017; Gross's extended process model of reappraisal, 2015)

> **Principle 310** — Practise ofo

The source behind this page's central claim could not be confirmed. Pratt's 19th-century Samoan dictionary gives ofo as "astonishment, wonder", "to salute on meeting", "to make an offer of food, services", and "the first speech made on presenting food to visitors". It does not give compassion ([Pratt, A Grammar and Dictionary of the Samoan Language](https://dokumen.pub/a-grammar-and-dictionary-of-the-samoan-language.html)). The same dictionary defines alofa as "love, compassion" and, as a verb, "to love, to compassionate". A modern dictionary might add further senses, so "ofo as compassion" stays unverified. The Tuhiwai Smith (1999) citation is a book on Māori research methods, and nothing in the opened material ties it to ofo.

What the sources do support is that compassion sits at the centre of Samoan values under the name alofa. The Oranga Tamariki practice centre describes alofa as the "Samoan heart", covering "love/compassion, reciprocity, respect, humility and dignity" ([Oranga Tamariki](https://practice.orangatamariki.govt.nz/practice-framework/whai-oranga/vaaifetu/tautua-samoan-cultural-approach)). Research by Samoan authors calls it "the cornerstone of fa'asamoa" ([Journal of Indigenous Wellbeing](https://journalindigenouswellbeing.co.nz/media/2024/05/We-are-not-brown-palagi-Navigating-cultural-boundaries-in-Samoan-research.pdf)). A New Zealand government speech for Samoan Language Week adds that alofa "must be manifested and reciprocated", through gifting or a tangible act ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/speech/launch-samoan-language-week-fale-o-samoa-mangere)). That supports the seed's idea that compassion should move to action.

Batson's empathy-altruism hypothesis is the psychology parallel. He defines empathic concern as "other-oriented emotion elicited by and congruent with the perceived welfare of someone in need", including "sympathy, compassion, tenderness". After more than 30 experiments he concluded, "tentatively", that it produces altruistic motivation ([Batson, chapter PDF](https://people.uncw.edu/hakanr/documents/batsononempathy.pdf)). A review notes that high personal cost can override empathy, and that it is unclear how much this matters outside the laboratory ([book review in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426832/)).

**Plain words** Compassion in Samoan sources is usually called alofa. Whether ofo is a recognised word for it remains unconfirmed. The shared idea is that the feeling should lead to a helping act.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. Someone sees a neighbour's flooded house and, instead of scrolling on, takes round food and a mop that evening.

> **Principle 6** — Let the skeptics' slogans guard the practice's humility

The tradition's summary phrases ("we determine nothing," "everything is inapprehensible," "no more this than that") are worn as reminders that the practice is a method, not a new doctrine. The distinct warning: the skeptic who starts asserting skepticism has merely changed teams. Practically, hold your own suspension-practices' results lightly; the moment you are certain that certainty is bad, the method has become a doctrine.

(Sextus Empiricus, Outlines, the slogans and their cautionary notes.)

**Plain words** The moment you're certain that certainty is bad, you've just founded a new church. Wear the skeptic's slogans — "we determine nothing," "no more this than that" — as reminders that this is a method, not a doctrine you've now won. Hold your own results lightly, including these.

*For example:* The ex-believer who becomes the most aggressive atheist in the room — and the one who finally relaxes is the person who notices they're doing it.

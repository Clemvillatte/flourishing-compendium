> **Principle 191** — Joy is the felt signature of creation; distinct from pleasure

Bergson distinguishes pleasure from joy. Pleasure is what nature uses to make an organism conserve itself: it tells you that a biological function has been performed. Joy is different. Joy, for Bergson, is the felt announcement that life has *created* something — that it has, as he puts it, gained ground, won a victory, called something new into being. The mother watching her child, the artist who has realized a thought, the person who has built a working thing — their joy is not the same as a pleasure. It is the felt knowledge that something new exists because of you.

This is not a guarantee. Bergson is offering a phenomenological reading of the *experience* of joy, not a clinical test of whether someone is flourishing.

**Plain words** Pleasure and joy are different things. Pleasure is what nature uses to make an organism conserve itself — it tells you a biological function has been performed. Joy is the felt announcement that life has created something: gained ground, won a victory, called something new into being. The mother watching her child, the artist who has realized a thought — their joy is the felt knowledge that something new exists because of you.

*For example:* The satisfaction after the good meal (pleasure: function performed) versus the thing that happens in the chest watching the kid ride the bike alone for the first time (joy: something new now exists). Same body, different currencies.

## Practice

- When you feel a flash of joy, ask: what was created here? What did I help bring into being? Not as a test — as a way of learning to distinguish pleasure (biological signal) from joy (felt creation).

## Limits

- Not a guarantee that joy is always caused by a new creation. Not a test of whether someone is flourishing. Do not pathologize sadness or romanticize struggle.

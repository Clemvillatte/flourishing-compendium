> **Principle 33** — Let repentance go

Spinoza's most modern page: repentance is not a virtue but a doubling of the problem. The repentant person is sorry, and then sorry for having been sorry, and the pain is consumed twice while nothing changes in the machinery. The free person, he says, avoids repentance not by never erring but by integrating the error once, as information, and moving on. Practically, when you catch the loop of self-recrimination, ask what single lesson the event contains, extract it in one sentence, and stop paying interest on the same mistake.

(Ethics IV, P54; the analysis of repentance as doubled sorrow.)

**Plain words** Repentance, Spinoza's most modern page: not a virtue but a doubling of the problem — sorry, and then sorry for being sorry, the pain consumed twice while nothing changes in the machinery. The free person avoids repentance — not by being shameless, but by correcting and moving.

*For example:* The person who spent the year after the failed business apologising to everyone — and the Spinozist correction: the machinery needs one thing (the post-mortem, the repayment plan) and got another (twice-consumed pain). The apology tour was the second consumption.

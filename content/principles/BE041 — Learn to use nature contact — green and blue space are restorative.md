> **Principle 41** — Learn to use nature contact: green and blue space are restorative

In Bratman and colleagues' experiment, healthy participants took 90-minute walks in either a natural or an urban setting. The researchers measured self-reported rumination and activity in the subgenual prefrontal cortex. These outcomes concern repetitive self-focused thought and a particular brain measure; they do not establish a general prescription for mood, stress, or every green and blue setting.

> "We show in healthy participants that a brief nature experience, a 90-min walk in a natural setting, decreases both self-reported rumination and neural activity in the subgenual prefrontal cortex (sgPFC), whereas a 90-min walk in an urban setting has no such effects on self-reported rumination or neural activity." (Bratman et al., 2015, Abstract)

Rumination, in the study's words, is "a maladaptive pattern of self-referential thought that is associated with heightened risk for depression and other mental illnesses"; the nature walk reduced it. The authors' conclusion is scoped to mechanism and population: "This study reveals a pathway by which nature experience may improve mental well-being and suggests that accessible natural areas within urban contexts may be a critical resource for mental health in our rapidly urbanizing world."

**Plain words** If you keep replaying an upsetting thought about yourself, choosing a natural route for a walk is one option to try. Afterwards, notice whether you are still caught in that thought.

*For example* Imagined: someone replaying a mistake at work chooses a park route for their planned walk. Afterwards, they notice whether they are still revisiting the mistake.

## Practice

- For a walk you already plan to take, try an accessible natural route and notice your repetitive thoughts before and afterwards. This is an exercise to try, not a promised result.

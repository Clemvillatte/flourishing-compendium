> **Principle 41** — Learn to use nature contact: green and blue space are restorative

Contact with nature (green space, blue space, natural environments) is a robust mental health behaviour. It reduces stress, improves mood, restores attention, and is associated with better well-being.

**Plain words** Green and blue space are a health behaviour — trees, parks, water — with real effects on mood and stress. This isn't sentiment; it's dose. The prescription is embarrassingly cheap and rarely filled.

*For example:* The 20-minute park walk that beats the 20-minute mall walk for restoring a frayed mind — the same legs, the same hour, different scenery. Nature is doing pharmacology for free.
## Psychology parallels

- **Nature contact and mental health**, nature exposure reduces stress, improves mood, and restores attention (Hartig et al., 2014; Bratman et al., 2019)
- **[[Attention restoration|Attention restoration theory (Kaplan)]]**, nature restores directed attention (Kaplan, 1995; Ohly et al., 2016)
- **Green space and well-being**, living near green space is associated with better mental health (White et al., 2019; Engemann et al., 2019)
- **Note: effects are real but effect sizes vary; the mechanism is debated** (Hartig et al., 2014; McMahan & Estes, 2015)

## Practice

- Use nature contact: spend time in green or blue space (park, forest, beach, garden) regularly. Even 15-20 minutes helps. Combine with walking for double benefit.

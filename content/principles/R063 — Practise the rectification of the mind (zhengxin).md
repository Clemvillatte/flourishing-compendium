> **Principle 325** — Practise the rectification of the mind (zhengxin)

When you are angry, fearful, anxious, or desiring, your judgment is distorted. Rectify your mind by attending to your emotions, calming them, and restoring clarity. A mind that is not rectified cannot see things as they are.

(The Great Learning; [[The doctrine of the mean|The Doctrine of the Mean]], ch. 1.)

**Plain words** Zhengxin, rectification of the mind: anger, fear, anxiety, and craving distort judgment — you literally cannot see straight through them. So the practice is to attend to the emotion first, calm it, restore clarity, and only then decide. A distorted mind sees a distorted world.

*For example:* Someone who waits 24 hours to send the reply written at midnight in full fury — and in the morning sends a different, better one that actually gets a result.

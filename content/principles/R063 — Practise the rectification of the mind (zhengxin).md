> **Principle 325** — Practise the rectification of the mind (zhengxin)

The Great Learning connects cultivating oneself with rectifying the mind, then makes inattention painfully ordinary ([commentary on rectifying the mind](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf2.htm)).

> "When the mind is not present, we look and do not see; we hear and do not understand; we eat and do not know the taste of what we eat." (The Great Learning, [James Legge translation](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf2.htm)).

Its next discussion warns that affection can hide a loved person's faults and hatred can hide someone's merits ([commentary on cultivating the person](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf2.htm)). This need not make every feeling an error: [[The doctrine of the mean|The Doctrine of the Mean]] calls feelings expressed in due degree harmony ([chapter 1](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf3.htm)).

**Plain words** Check whether you can describe the other person's conduct without quietly changing the subject to how much you like or dislike them. You can take a feeling seriously without letting it supply every piece of evidence.

*For example:* Suppose you draft a reply after a colleague criticises your work. Before sending it, you separate the sentence answering the criticism from the sentence meant to hurt them; you keep the argument without the insult.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Emotion regulation|Emotion regulation and cognitive clarity]]**: Gross distinguishes identifying a need to regulate, choosing a strategy and implementing it; different strategies have different consequences, rather than calmness being a universal recipe ([2015 review abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1047840X.2014.940781)).
- **[[Mindfulness|Mindfulness]]**: Kabat-Zinn's operational definition concerns purposeful, present-focused, nonjudgmental awareness, a comparison rather than an identical account of Confucian moral cultivation ([2003 commentary, definition section](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1093/clipsy.bpg016)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Rectification of the mind|Rectification of the mind]]**: the Great Learning connects attention and partiality with the conduct of family life ([commentary](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf2.htm)).
- **[[Apatheia|Apatheia]]**: the Stoic ideal excludes passions understood as mistaken, excessive impulses, but allows good feelings; it is not Aristotle's doctrine or simple emotional numbness ([Stoicism, section 4.7](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/)).

> **Principle 54** — Learn body scan meditation: systematic body awareness

The Satipatthana Sutta is the classical Buddhist text behind mindfulness of body. The sentence quoted below establishes one thing: attention is placed on the body.

> "There is the case where a monk remains focused on the body in & of itself — ardent, alert, & mindful — putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world." (Thanissaro Bhikkhu trans., [Satipatthana Sutta, MN 10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html))

A first-person account of an MBSR course published by Mindful.org reports that the body scan consists of "a toes-to-head 'body scan,' focusing in sequence on individual regions," and notes that "Repeating this 20-minute drill, and applying its whole-body awareness throughout the day, is at the heart of our first week's assignments" ([Mindful.org, 2017](https://www.mindful.org/the-8-week-journey-to-now/)). That account establishes the procedure as reported by a participant: sequential attention through the body's regions. The principle here is about the mechanics of the procedure; it does not assert that efficacy follows.

**Plain words** Most of what your body is doing right now you are not aware of. Tension in the jaw, a held breath, a clenched stomach; these run for hours without being noticed. A body scan is simply walking your attention through each part and asking what is actually there, without fixing anything. What turns up is information you would otherwise have missed.

*For example:* Imagine sitting down at the end of a workday and walking attention slowly from the top of the head to the feet, pausing at each region to notice what is there. Suppose the scan turns up tension in the shoulders that has been there since morning; the finding itself, made visible by the attention, is the benefit the practice is aiming at.

## Practice

- Practise body scan: 15-30 minutes, systematically moving attention from head to toe (or toe to head). Notice sensations (warmth, tension, tingling, numbness) without judging or changing. Return to body when mind wanders.

> **Principle 54** — Learn body scan meditation: systematic body awareness

The Satipatthana Sutta is the classical Buddhist text behind mindfulness of body. The sentence quoted below establishes one thing: attention is placed on the body.

> "There is the case where a monk remains focused on the body in & of itself — ardent, alert, & mindful — putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world." (Thanissaro Bhikkhu trans., [Satipatthana Sutta, MN 10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html))

The Greater Good Science Center at UC Berkeley publishes a body-scan practice whose instructions walk attention through the body's regions in sequence: "You can notice your feet on the floor, notice the sensations of your feet touching the floor. The weight and pressure, vibration, heat." The guide continues through the legs, back, stomach, hands, arms, neck and throat, jaw, face, and finally the whole body ([Greater Good in Action, UC Berkeley](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/body_scan_meditation)). A participant's account of an MBSR course describes the same procedure as "a toes-to-head 'body scan,' focusing in sequence on individual regions" ([Mindful.org, 2017](https://www.mindful.org/the-8-week-journey-to-now/)). The principle here is about the mechanics of the procedure; it does not assert that efficacy follows.

**Plain words** A body scan is walking your attention through each part of the body in turn and noticing what sensations are actually there. The Berkeley instructions name what to look for at each stop: weight, pressure, vibration, heat, pulsing, heaviness, lightness. Some regions will have obvious sensation and some will have little or none; the instructions note that this is normal: "We are more connected to certain areas of the body than others, at different times of the day."

*For example:* Imagine sitting down at the end of a workday and following the Berkeley sequence from the feet to the face. Suppose the scan turns up tension in the jaw that has been there since morning; the finding itself, made visible by the attention, is the benefit the practice is aiming at.

## Practice

- Practise body scan following the Berkeley guide: bring attention into the body, then move it through the regions in sequence (feet, legs, back, stomach, hands, arms, neck, jaw, face, whole body), noticing the sensations named at each stop. When sensations are absent in an area, notice that too.

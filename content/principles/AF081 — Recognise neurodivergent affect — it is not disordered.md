> **Principle 81** — Recognise neurodivergent affect: it is not disordered

Nick Walker's glossary of the field's terms defines the word this way:

> "Neurodivergent, sometimes abbreviated as ND, means having a mind that functions in ways which diverge significantly from the dominant societal standards of “normal.”" (Walker, [Neurodiversity: Some Basic Terms & Definitions](https://neuroqueer.com/neurodiversity-terms-and-definitions/))

The standard in that definition is social. Walker's neurodiversity paradigm adds that the idea of "one “normal” or “healthy” type of brain or mind" is "a culturally constructed fiction". Calling autistic or ADHD affect a disorder is therefore a disputed framing, and Walker acknowledges that some advocates for autistic people still regard autism as a medical pathology.

One study shows where part of the cost can sit. Sasson and colleagues found that observers formed far less favourable first impressions of autistic people within seconds, and those impressions went with less willingness to interact. The bias disappeared when impressions came from conversational content without audio-visual cues, "suggesting that style, not substance, drives negative impressions of ASD" (Sasson et al. 2017, [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep40700)). The study covers autism only.

**Plain words**
The way someone shows feeling can be a flat voice, a burst of excitement or a delayed reaction. It is easy to read as a fault if you assume everyone shows feeling the same way. In that study, observers judged autistic people harshly after a few seconds of watching, and the harshness went away when they only read what had been said. Before deciding someone is cold, rude or too much, check what they actually said and meant.

*For example:* A colleague speaks in a flat voice and looks away while you explain a plan, and you read it as disinterest. This is an imagined case. Ask them to write back their summary of the plan; if it is accurate and specific, the first reading was about their style rather than their attention, which is the pattern the transcript condition in Sasson's study points to.

## Practice

- If you are neurodivergent, do not judge your affective patterns by neurotypical standards. Learn your own pattern and design regulation that works for you.

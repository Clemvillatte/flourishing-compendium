> **Principle 81** — Recognise neurodivergent affect: it is not disordered

Autistic, ADHD, and other neurodivergent affective patterns differ from neurotypical patterns in intensity, expression, and regulation. These are not necessarily pathological; they are variations. Treating them as disordered creates unnecessary suffering.

**Plain words** Autistic, ADHD, and other neurodivergent affective patterns differ in intensity, expression, and regulation — variations, not necessarily pathologies. Treating them as disordered does harm. Recognise the pattern, work with the actual architecture.

*For example:* The employee whose flat affect in meetings reads as disengagement — and is actually a processing style — versus the one whose exuberance reads as unprofessional. Both misread by the neurotypical rubric. Adjusting the rubric, not the person, was the fix.
## Psychology parallels

- **Neurodivergent affect differs in pattern, not necessarily in health**, autistic alexithymia, ADHD emotional dysregulation, and sensory-affective differences are variations, not deficits (Kerns et al., 2015; Bellis, 2018)
- **Misdiagnosis and pathologisation of neurodivergent affect is common and harmful** (Kerns et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Neurodiversity|Neurodiversity as variation]]** (Singer, 1998)
- **The tree that is not judged by the forest** (Zhuangzi)

## Practice

- If you are neurodivergent, do not judge your affective patterns by neurotypical standards. Learn your own pattern and design regulation that works for you.

> **Principle 19** — Learn to set approach goals, not avoidance goals

The approach-avoidance distinction in achievement goals is set out in Elliot and McGregor's 2 x 2 framework. One dimension of their framework is valence:

> Competence is valenced in that it is either construed in terms of a positive, desirable possibility (i.e., success) or a negative, undesirable possibility (i.e., failure). (Elliot & McGregor, 2001)

The framework distinguishes task-based or intrapersonal standards from standards based on performance relative to others, and crosses these with positive or negative valence:

> The trichotomous achievement goal framework comprises three of the four "cells" of this 2 X 2 framework—mastery-approach goals (in which competence is defined in absolute/intrapersonal terms and is positively valenced), performance-approach goals (in which competence is defined in normative terms and is positively valenced), and performance-avoidance goals (in which competence is defined in normative terms and is negatively valenced). (Elliot & McGregor, 2001)

And the paper names the remaining cell:

> Thus, the remaining cell of the 2 X 2 framework is that representing mastery-avoidance goals (in which competence is defined in absolute/intrapersonal terms and is negatively valenced). (Elliot & McGregor, 2001)

The distinction between the two valences is the one the principle works with: an approach goal aims at the positive possibility, an avoidance goal at keeping the negative one from happening.

(Elliot & McGregor, 2001, "A 2 x 2 achievement goal framework," Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 80(3).)

**Plain words**
The compendium uses this distinction to examine an achievement goal. Write down the competence you want to develop and the failure you want to avoid. Compare the outcomes each version puts in focus, keeping the task and level of detail unchanged.

*For example:* Suppose you are studying the argument in chapter three. Compare "understand the argument" with "avoid misunderstanding the argument." Which outcome have you been concentrating on as you study?

## Practice

- Frame goals as approach rather than avoidance. Instead of "avoid failure," aim for "achieve success." Instead of "avoid being lazy," aim for "be active." Reframe avoidance into approach.

> **Principle 37** — Learn to stack habits: link new behaviours to existing ones

[[Habit stacking|Habit stacking]] involves attaching a new habit to an existing one ("after I [existing habit], I will [new habit]"). The existing habit serves as the trigger for the new one.

**Plain words** Habit stacking: bolt the new behaviour onto an existing one — "after coffee, I write." The old habit becomes the ignition key for the new one. No new willpower required, just adjacency.

*For example:* "After I drop the kids, I walk ten minutes" — the school run was already automatic; the walk just catches its ride. Existing habits are infrastructure; build on them.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Habit stacking|Habit stacking]]**, linking new behaviours to existing habits increases adoption (Clear, 2018; Fogg, 2019)
- **Anchor habits**, existing strong habits serve as reliable triggers for new ones (Fogg, 2019; Clear, 2018)

## Practice

- Stack habits: "after I [existing habit], I will [new habit]." For example: "after I pour my morning coffee, I will write for 10 minutes." The existing habit anchors the new one.

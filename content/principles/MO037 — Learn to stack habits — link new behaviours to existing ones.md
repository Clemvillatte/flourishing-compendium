> **Principle 37** — Learn to stack habits: link new behaviours to existing ones

Lally and colleagues asked 96 volunteers to choose an eating, drinking or activity behaviour and carry it out "daily in the same context (for example 'after breakfast') for 12 weeks". Automaticity rose along an asymptotic curve, and "the time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days". They also found that "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process" ([Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40, 998-1009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674)). The design used an existing cue, but it did not compare cues against no cue.

Judah and colleagues tested the placement of a new behaviour in a routine. In 50 participants, "those who flossed after brushing (rather than before) tended to form stronger flossing habits and, at 8-month follow-up, had stronger habits and flossed more frequently" ([Judah, Gardner & Aunger, 2013, British Journal of Health Psychology 18, 338-353](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22989272/)). The authors call the study exploratory.

(Lally et al., 2010; Judah et al., 2013.)

**Plain words** [[Habit stacking|Habit stacking]], attaching a new action to a steady daily cue, is supported by these studies, though they do not prove stacking works better than other methods. Expect weeks to months, not days. One missed day did not undo the process in Lally's data.

For example (imagined): "after I pour my morning coffee, I write for ten minutes". Keep the cue exactly the same each day.

- **[[Habit stacking|Habit stacking]]**, behaviours repeated daily after a stable cue grew more automatic over 18 to 254 days (Lally et al., 2010); Clear (2018) and Fogg (2019) not verified here
- **Anchor habits**, flossing after brushing formed stronger habits than before brushing in an exploratory study (Judah et al., 2013)

## Practice

- Stack habits: "after I [existing habit], I will [new habit]." For example: "after I pour my morning coffee, I will write for 10 minutes." The existing habit anchors the new one.

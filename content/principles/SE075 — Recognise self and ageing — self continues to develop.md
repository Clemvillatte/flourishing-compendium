> **Principle 75** — Recognise self and ageing: self continues to develop

In Carstensen and colleagues' study of over 10 years of experience sampling, "aging is associated with more positive overall emotional well-being, with greater emotional stability and with more complexity (as evidenced by greater co-occurrence of positive and negative emotions)" (2011). The authors interpret the finding through socioemotional selectivity: "constraints on time horizons result in the chronic activation of goals related to emotional meaning". The finding is about average emotional experience in the studied samples; it does not show that every older self is steadier than every younger one. Ecclesiastes supplies the oldest framing: "To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven".

> "Cross-sectional and growth curve analyses indicate that aging is associated with more positive overall emotional well-being, with greater emotional stability and with more complexity (as evidenced by greater co-occurrence of positive and negative emotions)." (Carstensen et al., 2011, abstract)

**Plain words** Your later chapters can read differently from the early ones. On average, people's emotional experience tends to steady and even improve with age, so if your older years surprise you by being calmer, you have company.

*For example:* The woman at seventy-five who says "I don't do parties I don't want to attend" isn't becoming difficult. She's becoming specific. Fifty years of data on her own preferences finally outranks forty years of being polite about them.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-development across the lifespan**, self-concept and identity continue to develop in older age (Carstensen et al., 2003; McAdams, 2001)
- **Positivity effect in older adults**, older adults attend more to positive and less to negative self-relevant information (Carstensen et al., 2003; Reed & Carstensen, 2012)
- **[[Ego integrity (Erikson)|Ego integrity (Erikson)]]**, successful ageing involves ego integrity — acceptance of one's life as it has been (Erikson, 1968; Torges et al., 2005)

## Practice

- As you age, recognise that the self is still developing. Lean into the strengths of older self (integration, wisdom, positivity). Work toward ego integrity — acceptance of your life as it has been.

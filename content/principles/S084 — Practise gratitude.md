> **Principle 206** — Practise gratitude

The Stoic practises gratitude for what they have. Marcus lists the things he is grateful for: his grandfather, his father, his mother, his teachers, his friends. The practice of gratitude shifts attention from what is lacking to what is present.

(Med. I.1, I.3, I.5, I.11, I.15, I.16, I.17; Seneca, Letters 9, 31; Disc. I.6, I.9, I.14, III.24.)

**Plain words** Marcus opens the Meditations by thanking his grandfather, father, mother, teachers and friends, one by one. Gratitude moves attention from what's missing to what's here. It works because it's specific, not because it's a mood.

*For example:* Writing three names on a card each Sunday, with one concrete thing each person did for you that week.

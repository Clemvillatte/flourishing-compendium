> **Principle 55** — End where the tradition ends: rooted, singing, serving, renewed

This final principle is an editorial synthesis, and the summary should be read that way. Its materials come from Nahua thought as scholars describe it. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Aztec philosophy says education aimed at perfecting children by developing in them "a wise face and a strong, humanized heart", a character formed within "well-ordered social living" ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Aztec Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/aztec-philosophy/)). The same entry describes the teacher as one who "makes wise the countenances of others; to them he gives a face; he leads them to develop it." "Flower and song" (poetry, painting, music) was the way the tlamatinime, the sages, presented what they understood of the world.

I join these with the other Mesoamerican principles into one shape: rooted in place and people, making beauty against the fading, carrying a share of the commons, practising a craft as service, and renewing on the calendar's rhythm. That list is the book's. The sources above support only the face-and-heart and flower-and-song parts. Practically, choose one principle per layer, one practice per week, and begin.

(Aztec Philosophy, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, sections 3, 4, 6 and 7; the synthesis itself is editorial.)

### Plain words

The Nahua ideal was to grow a wise face and a strong heart, with a teacher's help and inside a community. Beauty, in the form of song and flowers, was how people made sense of a fleeting world. The rest of the list is the book's attempt to turn that into a week.

### For example

Think of someone you know who seems to have it together. Check whether there is a place they belong to, something they make, people they show up for, and a rhythm that renews them. That is a test to try on your own life, and not a finding from the sources.

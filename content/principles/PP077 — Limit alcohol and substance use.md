> **Principle 77** — Limit alcohol and substance use

Koob and Volkow describe addiction as a cycle with a binge/intoxication stage, a withdrawal/negative affect stage and a preoccupation/anticipation stage. In the second stage, "increases in negative emotional states and dysphoric and stress-like responses" accompany "decreases in the function of the dopamine component of the reward system" ([Koob and Volkow 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475769/)). That is the short-term-reward, longer-term-cost picture.

On sleep, a review of studies in healthy volunteers found that at all doses alcohol shortens sleep onset, then causes "an increase in sleep disruption in the second half of sleep", and delays the first REM period ([Alcohol and sleep I, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347102/)).

The original entry's stricter rule, that abstinence "may be the only workable option" after dependence, is not what a review of controlled-drinking treatment found. In randomised trials, abstinence-oriented and controlled-drinking treatment did not differ significantly: "available evidence does not support abstinence as the only approach" ([controlled drinking review, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/add.15329)). A separate review concluded abstinence is not required for many, though it is "likely to lead to better functioning and greater well-being" ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8858850/)).

(Grant et al., 2015; Koob and Volkow, 2016.)

### Scope

Grant et al. 2015 is cited at second hand. The sleep review covers alcohol only; no source was found for relationship and cognition costs. Anyone with dependence should decide their goal with a clinician.

**Plain words**
A drink is a loan against tomorrow's mood, and the interest gets collected while you sleep, in the back half of the night. The habit version is worse: the loop of using, feeling worse, using again. One piece of folk wisdom deserves correcting — for people already dependent, total abstinence is not the only road the trials support, though for many it is the easier one. The choice of road belongs with a clinician.

**For example**
For two weeks, skip alcohol on weeknights and note your sleep each morning.

> **Principle 77** — Limit alcohol and substance use

Alcohol and recreational substances produce short-term pleasure and long-term costs to mood, sleep, cognition, and relationships. The principle is not prohibition. It is the recognition that the hedonic gains are temporary and the eudaimonic costs accumulate. For people with a history of dependence, the principle is stricter: abstinence may be the only workable option.

(Grant et al., 2015; Koob and Volkow, 2016.)

**Plain words** Alcohol and other substances trade short-term pleasure for long-term costs to mood, sleep, thinking and relationships. This isn't a moral claim but an accounting one. For anyone with a history of dependence, the honest rule is stricter: abstinence.

*For example:* The relaxed evening drink that quietly becomes the reason sleep is broken all week.

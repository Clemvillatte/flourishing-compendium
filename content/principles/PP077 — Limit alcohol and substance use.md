> **Principle 77** — Limit alcohol and substance use

Koob and Volkow describe addiction as a cycle with a binge/intoxication stage, a withdrawal/negative affect stage and a preoccupation/anticipation stage. In the second stage, "increases in negative emotional states and dysphoric and stress-like responses" accompany "decreases in the function of the dopamine component of the reward system" ([Koob and Volkow 2016, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475769/)). This supports the seed's picture of short-term reward followed by longer-term cost.

On sleep, a review of studies in healthy volunteers found that at all doses alcohol shortens sleep onset, then causes "an increase in sleep disruption in the second half of sleep", and delays the first REM period ([Alcohol and sleep I, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23347102/)).

The seed's stricter rule, that abstinence "may be the only workable option" after dependence, is not what a review of controlled-drinking treatment found. In randomised trials, abstinence-oriented and controlled-drinking treatment did not differ significantly, and the authors wrote that "available evidence does not support abstinence as the only approach" ([controlled drinking review, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/add.15329)). A separate review concluded that abstinence is not required to overcome a substance problem for many, though it is "likely to lead to better functioning and greater well-being" ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8858850/)).

(Grant et al., 2015; Koob and Volkow, 2016.)

### Scope

I did not open Grant et al. 2015. The sleep review covers alcohol only. For relationship and cognition costs I found no source. Anyone with dependence should decide their goal with a clinician.

**Plain words**
Alcohol and other drugs trade short-term reward for later costs, and alcohol disrupts the second half of the night's sleep. For people with dependence, abstinence is not the only option that research supports, but it may suit many.

**For example**
For two weeks, skip alcohol on weeknights and note your sleep each morning.

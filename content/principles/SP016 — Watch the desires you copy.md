> **Principle 16** — Watch the desires you copy

Emulation, in the affect definitions, is desire for a thing that arises because we imagine other people desire the same thing:

> "Emulation is the desire of something, engendered in us by our conception that others have the same desire." (Ethics III, Definitions of the Affects, XXXIII, [Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

The explanation attached to that definition distinguishes emulation from imitation: someone who runs away because he sees others running away imitates another's emotion. Emulation is the word for imitating what we judge honourable, useful, or pleasant. The definition gives a mechanism for desire that arrives through other people: you notice that they want the thing, and the wanting starts in you. Spinoza adds that the emotion is generally coupled with envy (III. xxxii. note). As an examination prompt rather than a verdict, the definition suggests asking, when a wish appears alongside other people's enthusiasm, what appeals about the thing itself.

**Plain words**
When someone else's enthusiasm starts a wish of your own, look at what appeals about the thing. Would you enjoy using it? Does it fit something you want to do? The questions are for examining a shared desire, not for rejecting it automatically; a want that others share can still be yours.

*For example:* Imagine wanting a camera after a colleague describes theirs. Before buying the same model, name the photographs you would take and whether the camera you already own would serve them.

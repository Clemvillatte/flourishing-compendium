> **Principle 16** — Watch the desires you copy

Emulation, in the affect definitions, is desire for a thing that arises because we imagine other people desire the same thing. Spinoza's insight is that much of wanting is borrowed: the crowd's appetite for an object is part of the object's perceived value, which is why fashions, bubbles, and envy share a single root. Practically, before pursuing something, strip out the others-want-it component and check what remains; if nothing remains, you have located a copied desire.

(Ethics III, definition of Emulation.)

**Plain words** Emulation: desire for a thing that arises because we imagine others desire it. Much of wanting is borrowed — the crowd's appetite for an object is part of the object's perceived value. Watch the desires you copy; audit them for authorship.

*For example:* The wishlist assembled entirely from what others are seen wanting — the bag, the car, the coast — and the audit question that empties half of it: did I want this before I saw them with it? No author, no purchase.

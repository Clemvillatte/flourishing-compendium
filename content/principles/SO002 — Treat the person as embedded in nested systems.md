> **Principle 2** — Treat the person as embedded in nested systems

Bronfenbrenner's ecological approach examines development across settings and the relationships between them. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) A microsystem concerns activities, roles and interpersonal relations within a setting. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) A mesosystem concerns relationships between settings. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) An exosystem includes connections involving a setting that does not contain the developing person but can indirectly influence their immediate setting. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf))

(Bronfenbrenner & Morris, "The Bioecological Model of Human Development," ch. 14 in the Handbook of Child Psychology, 6th ed., 2006.)

> "the ecological environment is conceived as a set of nested structures, each inside the other like a set of Russian dolls" (Bronfenbrenner, 1979, p. 3, quoted in [Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf))

Bronfenbrenner's own later restatement gives the levels. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) The innermost structure is the microsystem:

> "A microsystem is a pattern of activities, roles, and interpersonal relationships experienced by the developing person in a given setting with particular physical and material characteristics." (Bronfenbrenner, 1979, quoted in [Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf))

Beyond it, the mesosystem is "defined as comprising the relationships existing between two or more settings; in short, it is a system of two or more microsystems," and the exosystem "comprises the linkages and processes taking place between two or more settings, at least one of which does not contain the developing person, but in which events occur that indirectly influence processes within the immediate setting in which the developing person lives." ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) The model's premise is that development is a function of forces emanating from multiple settings and from the relations among these settings. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf))

**Plain words**
Look at both the person's immediate settings and the connections between them. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) A difficulty may involve relationships between home and school, or influences from a setting the person does not enter. ([Bronfenbrenner & Morris 2006](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/courses/3615/Readings/BronfenbrennerModelofDevelopment.pdf)) The compendium recommends examining these contexts alongside individual factors.

*For example:* For a pupil struggling to concentrate, investigate conditions at school and at home rather than assuming the explanation lies only within the pupil.

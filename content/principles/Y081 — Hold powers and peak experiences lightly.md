> **Principle 81** — Hold powers and peak experiences lightly

Deep practice is said to bring extraordinary abilities, and the Yoga Sutras do not treat them as a prize. Sutra 3.37 says:

> In concentration these [supernal activities] are obstacles ; in the emergent state they are perfections (siddhi).

(Yoga Sutras 3.37, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, p. 266, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary explains why. A man whose mind is not yet concentrated "thinks highly of these perfections, just as a man born in misery considers even a small bit of wealth a pile of wealth." It warns of someone who, "because of the power (prabhava) of these [perfections]", thinks "he has effected his purpose, and so might cease the constraint." And it says that "a yogin whose mind-stuff is concentrated must avoid these [perfections] even when brought near to him", since they "go counter to [the attainment]" of the final goal.

So the text says the obstacle is to concentration, not to "integration", which is the older wording of this page. Sutra 4.1 does not call powers an obstacle. It lists five sources of them: birth, drugs, spells, self-castigation and concentration. The wider rule, that any flattering peak experience re-inflates the self, is this page's own extension. So is the advice to tell one grounded person.

A related line appears in the Analects: "The subjects on which the Master did not talk, were: extraordinary things, feats of strength, disorder, and spiritual beings" (7.21, [ctext.org](https://ctext.org/analects/shu-er/ens)).

**Plain words**

The text warns against stopping at a striking result and calling the work finished. A striking experience can feel like arrival. Note it, and carry on with the practice that produced it.

**For example**

A woman on a long retreat has a night of vivid, luminous dreams. She tells her teacher the next morning, hears a brief word of encouragement, and goes back to the cushion.

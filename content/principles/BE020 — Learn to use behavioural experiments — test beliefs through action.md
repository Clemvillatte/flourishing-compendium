> **Principle 20** — Learn to use behavioural experiments: test beliefs through action

Behavioural experiments involve designing actions specifically to test beliefs or predictions. This is a core CBT technique that combines behaviour and cognition, and the clinical literature states its shape plainly:

> "Behavioural experiments aim to test a belief or prediction and to discover something new."
> (Murray and El-Leithy, Verhaltenstherapie, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611432/))

The same article lists their functions: to test aspects of a case formulation, to demonstrate the effects of an unhelpful cognitive or behavioural strategy, to test the validity of negative beliefs, and to construct and strengthen adaptive beliefs. They "lie at the heart of therapeutic change in CT and encompass a wide range of experiential tasks that have the primary goal of learning something new". (Bennett-Levy et al., 2004; Beck, 1976.)

**Plain words** A belief can be treated as a prediction and put to the test. Design a small action that the belief says will go badly, run it, and record what actually happens. The article's term for the point of this is discovering something new; the discovery, either way, is data the inner monologue cannot supply.

*For example:* Suppose the prediction is that speaking up in the meeting will be met with mockery. Design the smallest version of the action, run it, and write down the outcome as it happened, not as predicted. The record, not the rehearsal, is the result.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Behavioral experiments in CBT|Behavioural experiments (CBT)]]**, designing actions to test beliefs is a core CBT technique (Bennett-Levy et al., 2004; Beck, 1976)
- **Experiments are more effective than verbal disputation**, testing beliefs through action is more effective than arguing (Bennett-Levy et al., 2004; Fennell, 1989)

## Practice

- Design behavioural experiments: when you have a belief or prediction, for instance that you will fail or that others will judge you, design an action to test it. Run the experiment. Observe the actual outcome. Use the data.

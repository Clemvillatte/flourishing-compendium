> **Principle 20** — Learn to use behavioural experiments: test beliefs through action

Behavioural experiments involve designing actions specifically to test beliefs or predictions. "If I go to the party, I will be rejected" — go and test. This is a core CBT technique that combines behaviour and cognition.

**Plain words** Beliefs can be tested like hypotheses: design the action, run it, read the result. "If I speak up they'll mock me" — go speak up and see. Experience is a better debater than your inner monologue.

*For example:* The person convinced they'd be rejected who asks anyway and gets a "sure, why not" — the prediction wasn't evidence, it was weather. Now there's data.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Behavioral experiments in CBT|Behavioural experiments (CBT)]]**, designing actions to test beliefs is a core CBT technique (Bennett-Levy et al., 2004; Beck, 1976)
- **Experiments are more effective than verbal disputation**, testing beliefs through action is more effective than arguing (Bennett-Levy et al., 2004; Fennell, 1989)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Socratic method: test through experience|Socratic method: test through experience]]** (Plato, Meno)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires experiments** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Empiricism: experience over argument|Empiricism: experience over argument]]** (Hume, Enquiry)

## Practice

- Design behavioural experiments: when you have a belief or prediction ("I will fail," "they will judge me"), design an action to test it. Run the experiment. Observe the actual outcome. Use the data.

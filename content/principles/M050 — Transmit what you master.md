> **Principle 50** — Transmit what you master

The toltec lineage is a transmission: the master teaches the apprentices, and the craft's continuity is a moral obligation, not a market choice. The tradition treats a mastery that dies with its holder as a failure of the mastery itself. Practically, attach to your principal skill one apprentice (a junior colleague, a student, a child, a friend) and transmit deliberately: not only techniques but standards and the stance behind them.

(The toltec transmission tradition; the huehuetlatolli's teaching counsels.)

**Plain words** The toltec lineage is a transmission: the master teaches the apprentices, and the craft's continuity is a moral obligation, not a market choice. A mastery that dies with its holder is a failure of the mastery. Attach one apprentice to your principal skill and transmit deliberately.

*For example:* The veteran nurse who takes the new graduate under wing for a year — the IV techniques, yes, but the real curriculum: how to sit with a dying patient — and the ward's standard survives her retirement.

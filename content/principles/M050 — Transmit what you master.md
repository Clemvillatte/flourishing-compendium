> **Principle 50** — Transmit what you master

The huehuetlatolli present their own counsel as an inheritance to be kept and handed on. In a father's counsel in chapter 22 of the Florentine Codex, the tradition's words are counsel attributed to earlier generations, stored and passed down:

> "Right here are a word or two which merit being taken, being guarded, being grasped, which our forefathers went putting in their coffers, in their reed chests; for all courtesy, all prudence come from [and] are taken from this." (Florentine Codex, Book 6, ch. 22, Anderson and Dibble translation, p. 125)

The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy frames Aztec wisdom in the same direction: wisdom is "the practical ability to conduct one's life in such a way as to creatively participate in, reinforce, adapt, and extend into the future the way of life inherited from one's predecessors". That is the extent of what the opened passages establish. The compendium's suggested application: attach to your principal skill one apprentice (a junior colleague, a student, a child, a friend) and transmit deliberately: the techniques, and also the standards and the stance behind them.

(Florentine Codex, Book 6, ch. 22; IEP, "Aztec Philosophy".)

**Plain words** Pick a skill you can demonstrate. Show a learner how you perform it, explain the standards you use, and give them an opportunity to ask questions.

*For example (imagined):* A veteran nurse takes a new graduate under wing for a year: the IV techniques, and also how to sit with a dying patient.

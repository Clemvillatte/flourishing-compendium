> **Principle 31** — Learn to use behavioural experiments for social anxiety: test social predictions

Social anxiety involves predictions about being judged, rejected, or humiliated. Behavioural experiments involve deliberately testing these predictions by entering social situations and observing outcomes.

**Plain words** Social anxiety runs on predictions — "they'll judge me, I'll go red, it'll be humiliating." Test them like claims, not verdicts: go to the thing, gather the data. The prediction is almost always wrong; only the running of it proves that.

*For example:* "If I speak up in the meeting they'll think I'm stupid" — spoken, no one laughs, someone says "good point." The prediction had a perfect track record only because it was never cross-examined.
## Psychology parallels

- **Social anxiety and behavioural experiments**, testing social predictions reduces social anxiety (Clark & Wells, 1995; Hedman et al., 2013)
- **Video feedback and surveys**, using video and surveys to test predictions is effective for social anxiety (Harvey et al., 2000; Hedman et al., 2013)

## Practice

- Test social predictions: when you predict "I will say something stupid and they will laugh," deliberately enter the situation and observe. Use video feedback (record and watch yourself) to calibrate your self-perception. Test the prediction.

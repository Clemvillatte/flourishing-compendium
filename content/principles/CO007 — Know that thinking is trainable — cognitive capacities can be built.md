> **Principle 7** — Know that thinking is trainable: cognitive capacities can be built

Reasoning, problem-solving, and cognitive control are not fixed traits. They can be improved through deliberate practice, though the effects are domain-specific and do not transfer as broadly as early "brain training" claims suggested.

**Plain words** Reasoning, problem-solving, and cognitive control are trainable — through deliberate practice, with domain-specific effects (transfer is narrower than the early promises). Build the capacities in the domains you actually use.

*For example:* The person who "does brain training apps" for general IQ — and the one who practises the actual skill: doing taxes faster, reading contracts better. The second one's gains are real because the practice is specific. Train the muscle you use.
## Psychology parallels

- **Cognitive training effects are domain-specific**, working-memory training improves trained tasks but shows limited far transfer to untrained domains (Melby-Lervåg & Hulme, 2013; Simons et al., 2016)
- **Deliberate practice improves domain-specific performance**, expertise is built through structured practice (Ericsson et al., 1993)
- **Education and cognitive engagement show broader effects on cognition**, long-term educational attainment is associated with cognitive reserve (Lövdén et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Do not expect "brain training" games to make you smarter generally. Instead, train the specific cognitive skill you want (writing, reasoning, chess, mathematics) through deliberate practice with feedback.

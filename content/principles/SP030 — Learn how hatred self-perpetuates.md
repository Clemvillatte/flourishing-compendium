> **Principle 30** — Learn how hatred self-perpetuates

Spinoza argues that reciprocated hatred strengthens hatred and that love can destroy it. In Ethics IV, P46, he recommends returning love or kindness for hatred, anger and contempt, "as far as possible". These passages offer a philosophical reason to examine retaliation. Someone may still need to challenge an accusation or make a firm objection; the question is how to do so without returning the hostility. Try looking at one running disagreement in these terms, distinguishing the response you can choose from the reconciliation you hope for.

(Ethics III, P43; IV, P46.)

> "Hatred is increased by being reciprocated, and can on the other hand be destroyed by love." (Ethics III, P43, [Elwes, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/948/pg948.html))

> "He, who lives under the guidance of reason, endeavours, as far as possible, to render back love, or kindness, for other men's hatred, anger, contempt, &c., towards him." (Ethics IV, P46, [Elwes, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/971/pg971.html))

**Plain words** An angry reply can carry a useful complaint and a wish to hurt. Separate them before sending it. What happened? What do you want changed? Keep those points; leave out the extra wound.

*For example (imagined):* A relative revives an old accusation at a family gathering. Their cousin answers the practical question about the arrangements and offers help, without returning the insult. The disagreement remains unresolved.

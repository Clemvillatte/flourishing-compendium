> **Principle 72** — Design the environment to support the practice

The strongest self-control strategy is choosing environments, not resisting temptations. The habit mechanism, from Wood and Neal's review:

> "Habits emerge from the gradual learning of associations between responses and the features of performance contexts that have historically covaried with them (e.g., physical settings, preceding actions). Once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal." (Wood & Neal, "A New Look at Habits and the Habit-Goal Interface", Psychological Review, 2007)

The design move follows from the mechanism: if contexts trigger responses directly, controlling exposure to the context controls the response. The review names that route upstream control — it "occurs upstream when people's exposure to relevant context cues is altered or disrupted in some way" — and notes its use in addiction treatment and in eating contexts, where smaller plates and utensils reduce the amounts of food and drink people serve and consume. This page's application: put the phone in another room, leave the guitar visible, lay out the running shoes — design the physical and digital environment so that the desired practice is the easy choice and the undesired habit the hard one.

**Plain words**
Once a habit exists, the environment pulls it and the goal is not consulted. That is why resisting in place is the weak move and moving the cue is the strong one: the fight you skip is the fight you win. Make the wanted behaviour easy and the unwanted one hard.

*For example:* Not buying the biscuits beats not eating the biscuits in the cupboard.

> **Principle 72** — Design the environment to support the practice

Choosing environments is one self-control strategy this page suggests. The habit mechanism, from Wood and Neal's review:

> "Habits emerge from the gradual learning of associations between responses and the features of performance contexts that have historically covaried with them (e.g., physical settings, preceding actions). Once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal." (Wood & Neal, "A New Look at Habits and the Habit-Goal Interface", Psychological Review, 2007)

The design move follows from the mechanism: if contexts trigger responses directly, changing exposure to the context cues is a route the review names. It calls that route upstream control — it "occurs upstream when people's exposure to relevant context cues is altered or disrupted in some way" — and notes its use in addiction treatment and in eating contexts, where, per research the review cites, smaller plates and utensils reduce the amounts of food and drink people serve and consume. This page's suggested practice: put the phone in another room, leave the guitar visible, lay out the running shoes — arrange the environment so that the desired practice is the easy choice, and see what happens.

**Plain words**
Once a habit exists, the environment pulls it and the goal is not consulted. Rearranging the cues is therefore a strategy worth trying: make the wanted behaviour easy and the unwanted one hard, and watch what happens. Whether it works for any given habit is for the person to observe.

*For example:* Choosing not to buy the biscuits, rather than buying them and relying on not eating them.

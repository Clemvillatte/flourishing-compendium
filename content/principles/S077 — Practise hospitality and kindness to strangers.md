> **Principle 199** — Practise hospitality and kindness to strangers

The Stoic is a citizen of the world. Marcus says it plainly: "My city and country as I am Antoninus, is Rome; as a man, the whole world." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Project Gutenberg ebook 2680 — translator not stated in that edition; printed as Bk VI §39.) If the whole world is the city, every human is a fellow citizen. Epictetus draws the duty out of the word: "you are a citizen of the world, and a part of it, not one of the subservient (serving), but one of the principal (ruling) parts... What then does the character of a citizen promise (profess)? To hold nothing as profitable to himself; to deliberate about nothing as if he were detached from the community, but to act as the hand or foot would do, if they had reason and understood the constitution of nature, for they would never put themselves in motion nor desire anything otherwise than with reference to the whole." (Epictetus, Discourses, "How we may discover the duties of life from names.")

So treat strangers as you would treat neighbours — fellow citizens by definition, even though Enchiridion 30 is careful that duties are measured by relations, and a stranger's claim is the general one of shared humanity. The kindness itself is done without keeping score. The doer of a good turn, Marcus writes, should be "like unto the vine, which beareth her grapes, and when once she hath borne her own proper fruit, is contented and seeks for no further recompense." (Printed as Bk V §6.) "Unto him that is a man, thou hast done a good turn; doth not that suffice thee?" (Printed as Bk IX §43.)

(Med., Gutenberg 2680; Disc., "How we may discover the duties of life from names"; Ench. 30.)

**Plain words** Hold the door, give the directions, make the introduction — the small unrecorded helps. The Stoic ground: this person belongs to your city, the human one, whatever their papers say, so basic goodwill is citizenship rather than charity. The help is done without a receipt — doing the good turn was the point, not being seen doing it.

*For example:* Giving directions to a lost tourist, walking her to the corner.

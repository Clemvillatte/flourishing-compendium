> **Principle 4** — Cultivate de: your embodied character-power

Chapter 51 of the Daodejing gives the canonical chain, and de is the nurturing half of it:

> "All things are produced by the Tao, and nourished by its outflowing operation." (Daodejing 51, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

What makes this power de, the chapter says, is how it operates:

> "It produces them and makes no claim to the possession of them; it carries them through their processes and does not vaunt its ability in doing so; it brings them to maturity and exercises no control over them;--this is called its mysterious operation." (Daodejing 51, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The Neiye treats the same power as something built rather than policed:

> "When your body is not aligned,
> The inner power will not come.
> When you are not tranquil within,
> Your mind will not be well ordered.
> Align your body, assist the inner power,
> Then it will gradually come on its own." (Neiye XI, [Roth, Inward Training](https://archive.org/stream/original-tao-inward-training-nei-yeh-and-the-foundations-of-taoist-mysticism-tra/Original%20Tao%20Inward%20Training%20(Nei-yeh)%20and%20the%20Foundations%20of%20Taoist%20Mysticism%20(Translations%20from%20the%20Asian%20Classics)%20by%20Harold%20David%20Roth%20(z-lib.org)_djvu.txt))

That gradual arrival is the argument against rule-policing: a rule supervises behaviour from outside, de is capacity grown inside, and it keeps working when the supervision stops. The Neiye claims the effect radiates without words, saying that once the mind is made stable, all under the heavens will listen (Neiye XVIII).

(DDJ 51; Neiye.)

**Plain words**
A rule is a fence, and it needs you standing next to it. De is more like fitness: it comes from practice, it stays when nobody is watching, and it covers situations the rule never anticipated. The investment question is whether your honesty, patience or courage would survive the removal of the audience. If it would not, the thing being polished is your appearance, not your character.

**For example**
Someone who has genuinely practised honesty for years does not agonize over whether to fudge a report; integrity has become the reflex rather than the rule.

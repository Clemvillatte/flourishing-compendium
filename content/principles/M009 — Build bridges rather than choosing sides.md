> **Principle 9** — Build bridges rather than choosing sides

The nepantla position tempts toward two exits: assimilation (abandoning one side) and counter-assimilation (fortifying against it). The tradition's third option is the bridge: to become the person who can walk between and carry understanding both ways. The bridge is a role with a cost (you are never fully at home) and a yield (you are needed everywhere). Practically, in your between-places, adopt the bridge role deliberately rather than drifting into it resentfully.

(The nepantla bridge analysis; the mestiza consciousness, labeled modern.)

**Plain words** The nepantla position tempts you toward two exits: assimilation (abandon one side) or counter-assimilation (fortify against it). The third option is the bridge: become the person who can walk between and carry understanding both ways. Cost: you're never fully at home. Yield: you're needed everywhere.

*For example:* The engineer who crossed into management — could go native and forget the code, or sneer at the suits — instead becomes the translator both sides trust, and makes themselves close to irreplaceable.

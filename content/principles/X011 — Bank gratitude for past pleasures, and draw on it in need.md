> **Principle 11** — Bank gratitude for past pleasures, and draw on it in need

The Epicurean memory-practice: past pleasures are not lost when the source is gone; the wise person recalls them deliberately, and the recollection is itself a pleasure. The instrument is distinct in this compendium: a savings account of remembered good, drawn on in hardship. Practically, keep a written bank of specific past pleasures (moments, meals, companions, places), one entry per week, and draw on it deliberately in the next hard stretch.

(Vatican Sayings, the memory doctrines; the tradition's counsel to the aged and ill.)

**Plain words** Past pleasures aren't spent — they're banked. The wise deliberately re-taste the good meal, the old friendship, the summer place, and the recollection is itself a pleasure, available when new ones are scarce. Keep the account written down; draw on it in hard stretches.

*For example:* Someone housebound for a month who reads their one-line-per-week "bank" of past moments — a rooftop dinner, a child's laugh — and finds the days shorten.

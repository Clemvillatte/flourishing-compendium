> **Principle 64** — Monitor for adverse effects

Well-being interventions can produce harm. Mindfulness can increase anxiety in trauma survivors. Gratitude can produce guilt in people who feel they have nothing to be grateful for. Positive reframing can feel invalidating to people in genuine distress. The principle is not to avoid these practices but to monitor their effects and adjust or abandon them when they produce harm.

(Van Gordon et al., 2017; Dobkin, 2021.)

**Plain words** These practices can hurt. Mindfulness can worsen anxiety in trauma survivors. Gratitude can create guilt in someone who feels they have nothing. Reframing can invalidate real distress. Monitor the effects and adjust rather than pushing through.

*For example:* Someone in acute grief tries forced gratitude and feels worse; a gentler practice fits better.

## Psychology parallels

- Adverse effects of meditation (Van Gordon et al., 2017)
- Negative effects of positive psychology interventions (Dobkin, 2021)

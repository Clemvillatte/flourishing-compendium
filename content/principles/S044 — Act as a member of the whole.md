> **Principle 166** — Act as a member of the whole

Marcus Aurelius gives the frame as a standing exercise of perception:

> "Constantly regard the universe as one living being, having one substance and one soul; and observe how all things have reference to one perception, the perception of this one living being; and how all things act with one movement; and how all things are the cooperating causes of all things which exist." (Marcus Aurelius, [Meditations IV.40, Long translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.4.four.html))

The membership claim is the bee: that which is not good for the swarm, neither is it good for the bee ([Med. VI.54](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html)). The same book gives the dual citizenship: my nature is rational and social; my city and country, so far as I am Antoninus, is Rome, but so far as I am a man, it is the world ([Meditations VI, Long translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html)). What is useful to these cities is the only thing useful to me ([Med. VI](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html)).

(Med. II.3, IV.10, V.8, V.30, VI.42, VII.9, VII.19, VII.47, VIII.7, VIII.34, VIII.50, VIII.52, X.1, X.6, X.33, XI.1, XI.18, XII.36; Disc. I.12, I.14, I.25, III.10, III.24; Seneca, Letters 71, 95, 107; Ench. 31, 53.)

**Plain words**
The whole-part idea is not a demand for self-sacrifice; it is a claim about where your interests actually live. The bee is not a donor to the swarm; the swarm is the condition of the bee. A decision that looks purely private lands somewhere in the web the passage describes, and that somewhere is not outside your life.

*For example:*
Throwing away a plastic bottle becomes a slightly different act when you remember where it ends up.

> **Principle 253** — Make Stoicism a lifelong practice, not a phase

> How long, then, will you put off thinking yourself worthy of the highest improvements and follow the distinctions of reason? ... This instant, then, think yourself worthy of living as a man grown up, and a proficient.

(Epictetus, Enchiridion 50, trans. Elizabeth Carter, [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html), page opened; the ellipsis omits several sentences.)

Seneca stresses repetition. In Letter 16 he says the idea that wisdom is needed for a happy life "must be strengthened and implanted more deeply by daily reflection; it is more important for you to keep the resolutions you have already made than to go on and make noble ones" ([Gummere trans., Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_16), opened). He also writes that happiness comes "when our wisdom is brought to completion, but that life is at least endurable even when our wisdom is only begun." The reading on this page is the compendium's: if even begun wisdom makes life endurable, the daily keeping of resolutions is not a stage you graduate from — Seneca can allow completion of wisdom and still make the work of each day the point.

For the psychology parallel, a meta-analysis of 92 longitudinal samples found that people increase in social dominance, conscientiousness and emotional stability, "especially in young adulthood (age 20 to 40)," and that change continues in middle and old age for four of six trait categories ([Roberts, Walton & Viechtbauer, Psychological Bulletin, 2006; PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16435954/)). That shows personality change across the lifespan, and says nothing about Stoic practice.

**Plain words** Epictetus tells his reader to stop postponing and start living as a proficient person now. Seneca treats philosophy as daily repetition and keeping resolutions. Even on his own terms, where wisdom can be completed, the road there is repetition — so the practice cannot turn into a phase you did once.

*For example (imagined):* A twenty-year meditator still sets the timer for ten minutes each morning.

> **Principle 135** — Samsara and nirvana are one mind

In the Mulamadhyamakakarika, Nagarjuna challenges the separation of samsara and nirvana. Garfield and Priest quote verse 25.20 as one of his positive assertions, describing it as his assertion that nirvana and samsara are identical ([Garfield and Priest](https://scholarworks.smith.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1021&context=phi_facpubs)):

> Whatever is the limit of nirvana,
> That is the limit of cyclic existence.
> There is not even the slightest difference between them,
> Or even the subtlest thing.

(MMK 25.20, as quoted by [Garfield and Priest](https://scholarworks.smith.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1021&context=phi_facpubs).)

Their reading treats emptiness as lacking an independent essence, not simply not existing ([Garfield and Priest](https://scholarworks.smith.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1021&context=phi_facpubs)). How to understand the claimed sameness remains an interpretive question: Garth Mason compares identity, equivalence and nihilist models, preferring equivalence ([Mason](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1011-76012014000200006)).

The title is the compendium's shorthand. The quoted verse does not say "one mind"; that wording belongs to the compendium's title and is not established by this passage. Nor does the verse settle what kind of sameness it asserts; that remains disputed. The shorthand should not turn the argument into a promise that changing one's attitude removes pain or makes harmful circumstances acceptable.

**Plain words** The verse rests on an idea from the same chapter of the MMK: nothing holds its identity on its own. For something to have an essence, Garfield and Priest explain, is for it to be what it is, in and of itself, independently of all other things; to be empty is precisely to have no essence in that sense. Ordinary version: a thing is what it is through its relations, the way a borrowed tool is a loan only while someone expects it back. So when the verse says there is not the slightest difference between nirvana and cyclic existence, it is refusing to treat liberation as a separate, self-contained realm.

*For example:* Take a thought experiment: try to describe your own job, marriage or neighbourhood as if it existed entirely on its own, with nothing outside it contributing to what it is. The description runs out quickly; each turns out to be made of relations. That illustrates the explanation of emptiness only. It is not a demonstration that samsara and nirvana are one, or that your difficulties are unreal.

> **Principle 37** — Choose deliberate difficulty in the right dose

Tapas appears twice in the corpus. Sutra 2.1 names it first among the three parts of the yoga of action: "Self-castigation and study and devotion to the Isvara are the Yoga of action" (trans. Woods, [p. 103](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft)). Vyasa's comment gives its use: "Yoga is not perfected in him who is not self-castigated", because impurity "is not reduced except by 'self-castigation'" (p. 103). Sutra 2.43 names the fruit: "Perfection in the body and in the organs after impurity has dwindled as a result of self-castigation" (p. 189).

### The dose limit

The commentary sets the limit itself. Vyasa notes the qualifying kind, self-castigation "not being inhibitory to the undisturbed calm of the mind-stuff" (p. 103). Vacaspati's gloss answers an objector who says austerity causes disorders of the humours:

> Self-castigation should be performed only so long as it does not bring on a disorder of the humours. This is the meaning.

(Tattva-vaicaradi on Yoga Sutra 2.1, trans. Woods, [p. 104](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

### What the principle adds

"Voluntarily accepted, dose-controlled discomfort, undertaken for growth" is the principle's modern formulation; the commentary's own terms are a use (reducing impurity), a kind that leaves the mind's calm intact, and a stop-line at disorder. The one-discomfort-per-week practice is the principle's own prescription.

**Plain words** The rule has two halves: without some accepted difficulty, the impurity does not thin; past some point, the difficulty itself harms the practice. The commentary's stop-line is a disorder of the humours, and its marker is the undisturbed calm of the mind-stuff. The skill is watching for the point where the difficulty starts costing the calm instead of serving it.

*For example:* Imagined: a fasting day leaves you clear and steady by evening, and you note the effect. A fast that leaves you irritable, sleepless and unable to read has crossed the commentary's line. The dose, not the will, is what to adjust.

(Yoga Sutra 2.1 and 2.43 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's Tattva-vaicaradi, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 103-104, 189.)

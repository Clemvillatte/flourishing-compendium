> **Principle 37** — Choose deliberate difficulty in the right dose

Austerity (tapas) appears twice in the corpus: as one of the three practices of Kriya Yoga (with self-study and surrender) and as an observance whose fruit is strength. The bounded formulation: voluntarily accepted, dose-controlled discomfort, undertaken for growth. The dose matters; the tradition itself warns against extremes, and the body of evidence on hormesis supports small stressors, not large ones. Practically, choose one voluntary discomfort per week and log its effects.

(YS 2.1; YS 2.43.)

**Plain words** Austerity, in the bounded formulation: voluntarily accepted, dose-controlled discomfort, undertaken for growth — not punishment, not endurance theater. The fruit the tradition names is strength. Choose your deliberate difficulty in the right dose.

*For example:* Someone takes the cold showers, the fasting day, the hard training week — sized so they're difficult but not derailing — and the strength shows up elsewhere, in rooms that have nothing to do with cold water.

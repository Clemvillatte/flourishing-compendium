> **Principle 62** — Moderate the pleasures of the table and of comfort

Spinoza treats pleasure as a question of what it does to the body. In Ethics Part IV he says mirth "consists in all parts of the body being affected equally", so it "cannot be excessive". Localized pleasure, which he calls titillatio, can be:

> Localized pleasure or stimulation (titillatio) is pleasure, which, in so far as it is referred to the body, consists in one or some of its parts being affected more than the rest [...] and may remain obstinately fixed therein, thus rendering it incapable of being affected in a variety of other ways ([Ethics IV, P43, trans. Elwes, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt))

In the note to P45 he asks, "why is it more lawful to satiate one's hunger and thirst than to drive away one's melancholy?" and answers that it is "the part of a wise man to refresh and recreate himself with moderate and pleasant food and drink, and also with perfumes, with the soft beauty of growing plants, with dress, with music, with many sports, with theatres, and the like." Enjoyment is to be had "as much as possible (not to the point of satiety, for that would not be enjoyment)."

The note to P44 shows the danger. When one emotion is attached to one part of the body, it "fix[es] the mind in the contemplation of one object", and Spinoza says "Avarice, Ambition, Lust, &c., are species of madness". My test for any comfort follows from this: does it spread the body's capacity around, or fix attention on one thing?

(Ethics IV, P42-P45, trans. Elwes.)

### Plain words

Spinoza says nothing forbids enjoying yourself. The trouble starts when one pleasure grabs a single part of you and keeps it, so that the rest of you goes quiet. Ask of each comfort whether it leaves you more able to do things afterwards, or less.

### For example

Compare the fourth hour of the same video game, with the eyes busy and everything else dull, with a walk with friends where the whole body and mind are working at a lower pitch. Only the second leaves more capacity behind.

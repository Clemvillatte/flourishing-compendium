> **Principle 56** — Learn to practise kindness and helping: acts of service

Performing acts of kindness and helping others is a specific social behaviour that increases well-being, connects people, and contributes to meaning. It is both a personal and social practice.

**Plain words** Kindness is a behaviour, not just a value — and it's mood-medicine for the giver as much as the receiver. Small acts, deliberately done. The effect is so reliable it's almost a cheat code.

*For example:* The awful, grey week broken up by buying the stranger behind you a coffee — nothing changed in your life except the chemistry. Five minutes of doing something for someone else outperformed an hour of self-focus.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Acts of kindness (Lyubomirsky)|Acts of kindness (Lyubomirsky)]]**, performing kind acts increases well-being (Lyubomirsky et al., 2005; Curry et al., 2018)
- **Helping and volunteering**, volunteering is associated with better health and well-being (Post, 2005; Jenkinson et al., 2013)
- **Kindness interventions reduce loneliness**, acts of kindness reduce loneliness (Holt-Lunstad, 2021; Cregg & Cheavens, 2021)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Metta (loving-kindness)|Metta: loving-kindness in action]]** (Buddhism, Metta Sutta)
- **[[Agape|Agape: selfless love]]** (Christian tradition)
- **[[Ren|Ren: benevolence]]** (Confucius, Analects)

## Practice

- Practise acts of kindness: do something kind for someone daily (small or large), volunteer regularly, help without being asked. Kindness is both a moral and a well-being behaviour.

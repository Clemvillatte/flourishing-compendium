> **Principle 56** — Learn to practise kindness and helping: acts of service

The experimental question was asked directly in a meta-analysis of kindness interventions, 27 studies with 4,045 participants:

> "Multi-level modeling revealed that the overall effect of kindness on the well-being of the actor is small-to-medium (δ = 0.28)." (Curry et al., 2018)

The authors' discussion explains why kindness might pay this way: "it is equally straightforward to explain why performing acts of kindness might make people happy: it is because caring for family, maintaining coalitions, trading favours and increasing status are also important adaptive goals." The effect is a small-to-medium nudge to the doer's well-being, not a transformation.

**Plain words** Kindness is a behaviour, not just a value, and the experimental literature finds it pays the giver too: doing kind things for others nudges the doer's well-being up. Small acts, deliberately done, add up to that small-to-medium effect.

*For example* Imagined: on a grey Tuesday you buy the stranger behind you a coffee. One deliberate kind act; in the studies, the doer's well-being is what gets measured.

## Practice

- Practise acts of kindness: do something kind for someone daily (small or large), volunteer regularly, help without being asked. Kindness is both a moral and a well-being behaviour.

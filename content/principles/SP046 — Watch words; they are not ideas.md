> **Principle 46** — Watch words; they are not ideas

Spinoza puts words on the imagination's side of the ledger. In the Emendation he warns that "words may, equally with the imagination, be the cause of many and great errors," because we form many conceptions "in accordance with confused arrangements of words in the memory" ([TIE §88, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding)); words are "signs of things as existing in the imagination, not as existing in the understanding" ([TIE §89, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding)). The Ethics gives the mechanism: the universal term "man" means an animal that stands upright to those "impressed by men's stature" and something else to others, for "each will form universal images of things according to the disposition of his body" ([Ethics II P40 scholium 1, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The compendium's reading: two people can exchange a term, both nod, and hold different ideas behind it.

> "distinguish accurately between an idea or concept of the mind and the images of things that we imagine, and between ideas and the words by which we signify things. Many people either completely confuse these three—ideas, images, and words—or don't distinguish them accurately enough or carefully enough." (Spinoza, Ethics II P49 scholium, [Bennett clear-language translation](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf))

**Plain words**
A word points at your private picture of a thing, not at the thing. You learned the word from other people using it, and the picture it raises in you comes from your own encounters. Two people can say "freedom" for an hour, agree with every sentence, and leave with opposite plans. The repair is to ask what image a term is carrying: describe your picture, have the other person describe theirs, and argue about the pictures rather than the word.

**For example**
Two colleagues agree a plan is "agile". One pictures weekly feedback and small experiments; the other pictures no documentation and no deadlines. Nothing in the word corrects either picture, so the project inherits both.

(Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect §§88-89; Ethics II P40 scholium 1; Ethics II P49 scholium.)

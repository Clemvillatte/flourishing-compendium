> **Principle 46** — Watch words; they are not ideas

Words are tokens, and the mind that reasons from words alone reasons from counters without coins. Spinoza blames a large share of philosophical confusion on language: the same word carries different ideas in different minds, and arguments built on word-shapes look like progress while the underlying ideas never touch. Practically, when a dispute heats up, drop the contested word entirely and have each side state the experience behind it; most disputes end there.

(Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect, sections 88-95; Ethics II, P40 scholium 1.)

**Plain words** Words are tokens — the mind that reasons from words alone reasons from counters without coins. The same word carries different ideas in different minds, and arguments built on the mismatch collapse in translation. Watch words; they are not the ideas.

*For example:* The couple's endless fight about "respect" — one means punctuality, one means praise, neither has noticed — and the hour they finally trade definitions, half the war ends in an armistice nobody expected.

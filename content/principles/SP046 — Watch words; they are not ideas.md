> **Principle 46** — Watch words; they are not ideas

Spinoza puts words on the imagination's side of the ledger ([TIE §§88-89, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding/Part_1)). In the Emendation he warns that "words may, equally with the imagination, be the cause of many and great errors," because we form many conceptions "in accordance with confused arrangements of words in the memory" ([TIE §88, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding/Part_1)); words are "signs of things as existing in the imagination, not as existing in the understanding" ([TIE §89, Elwes](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding/Part_1)). The Ethics gives the mechanism: the universal term "man" means an animal that stands upright to those "impressed by men's stature" and something else to others, for "each will form universal images of things according to the disposition of his body" ([Ethics II, First note on 37-40, Bennett](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf)). The compendium's reading: two people can exchange a term, both nod, and hold different ideas behind it.

> "distinguish accurately between an idea or concept of the mind and the images of things that we imagine, and between ideas and the words by which we signify things. Many people either completely confuse these three—ideas, images, and words—or don't distinguish them accurately enough or carefully enough." (Spinoza, Ethics II, Concluding Note of Part II, [Bennett clear-language translation](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf))

**Plain words**
As a practical application, ask each person what they mean by a disputed word and which particular cases they include; sharing the word need not mean that they understand the same thing ([Elwes, §§88-89](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_Improvement_of_the_Understanding/Part_1)).

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: two colleagues agree a plan is "agile"; one means weekly feedback and small experiments, the other no documentation and no deadlines.

(Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect §§88-89; Ethics II, First note on 37-40; Ethics II, Concluding Note of Part II.)

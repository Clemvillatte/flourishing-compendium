> **Principle 88** — Recognise that pleasure varies with the activity

Book X argues the point from the nature of activities: things different in kind are completed by different things, and "activities differing in kind are completed by things differing in kind". The activities of thought differ from those of the senses, "so, therefore, do the pleasures that complete them". The fit matters: activities are made more precise and better by their proper pleasure, and injured by alien ones, which do pretty much what proper pains do. The goodness follows the activity, in the quotation below. The practical question: which activity does this pleasure belong to, and what does that say about both?

(NE X.5, Ross.)

> The pleasure proper to a worthy activity is good and that proper to an unworthy activity bad; just as the appetites for noble objects are laudable, those for base objects culpable.
>
> Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics X.5, translated by W. D. Ross, [full text](https://people.bu.edu/wwildman/courses/wphil/readings/wphil_rdg09_nichomacheanethics_entire.htm).

**Plain words**
The pleasure is glued to the activity it finishes. Eating has its pleasure, thinking has another, and the Ethics carries the ranking with the activity: sight's pleasures are purer than touch's, because sight is the more precise sense. A pleasure tells you what activity it belongs to, and the activity grades the pleasure.

**For example**
The satisfaction of solving a puzzle and the satisfaction of a good meal are different pleasures; the passage's question is what each completes, not which wins.

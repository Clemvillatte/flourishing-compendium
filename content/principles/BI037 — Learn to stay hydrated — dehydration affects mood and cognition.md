> **Principle 37** — Learn to stay hydrated: dehydration affects mood and cognition

Two laboratory studies examined mild dehydration in healthy young adults. In 26 young men, a mean loss of 1.59% of body mass increased errors on a visual vigilance task, slowed responses on a visual working-memory task, and raised fatigue and tension/anxiety at rest ([Ganio et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/)). In 25 young women at 1.36% dehydration, mood degraded (vigor down, fatigue up, headaches, lower concentration), while most aspects of cognitive performance were not affected ([Armstrong et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/)).

> "Dehydration degraded specific aspects of cognitive performance: errors increased on visual vigilance (P = 0·048) and visual working memory response latency slowed (P = 0·021)." (Abstract, Results; Ganio et al., 2011, Br J Nutr 106(10): 1535-43, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/))

> "In conclusion, degraded mood, increased perception of task difficulty, lower concentration, and headache symptoms resulted from 1.36% dehydration in females." (Abstract; Armstrong et al., 2012, J Nutr 142(2): 382-8, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/))

**Plain words**
If concentrating feels harder after exercise, recent fluid loss is one possible contributor to consider. These experiments compared dehydrated conditions with hydrated controls; they do not establish that extra water improves thinking in someone already hydrated ([Ganio et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/); [Armstrong et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/)).

**For example**
(Imagined) Imagine a student planning a walk before an afternoon of proofreading. They bring water and, if concentration is poor afterward, consider fluid loss alongside other possible explanations.

## Psychology parallels

- **Hydration and mood**, mild dehydration was associated with degraded mood, fatigue and headaches in controlled trials (Armstrong et al., 2012; Ganio et al., 2011)
- **Dehydration and cognition**, mild dehydration slowed vigilance and working-memory responses in young men (Ganio et al., 2011)

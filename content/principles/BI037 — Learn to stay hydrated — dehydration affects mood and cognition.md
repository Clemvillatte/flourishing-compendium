> **Principle 37** — Learn to stay hydrated: dehydration affects mood and cognition

Two companion laboratory studies supply the numbers, and they disagree in an instructive way. In 26 young men, dehydration of about 1.6% of body mass increased errors on a visual vigilance task, slowed visual working memory, and raised fatigue and tension/anxiety at rest ([Ganio et al., 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/)). In 25 young women at about 1.36% dehydration, mood degraded (vigor down, fatigue up, headaches, lower concentration), but most aspects of cognitive performance were not affected ([Armstrong et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/)). Both trials produced the deficit with treadmill walking in a warm room, three 40-minute walks per trial, sometimes with a diuretic.

> "Dehydration degraded specific aspects of cognitive performance: errors increased on visual vigilance (P = 0·048) and visual working memory response latency slowed (P = 0·021)." (Abstract, Results; Ganio et al., 2011, Br J Nutr 106(10): 1535-43, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21736786/))

> "In conclusion, degraded mood, increased perception of task difficulty, lower concentration, and headache symptoms resulted from 1.36% dehydration in females." (Abstract; Armstrong et al., 2012, J Nutr 142(2): 382-8, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22190027/))

**Plain words**
Somewhere around one and a half percent down in body water, measurable things start to happen: in the men's study, attention and working memory slipped; in the women's study, mood, headaches and concentration bore the brunt while most thinking tests held steady. That is not a huge water deficit; three 40-minute walks in a warm room produced it. The practical point is that a free intervention has a detectable effect in controlled trials, and the studies' own authors close by calling for more emphasis on optimal hydration.

**For example**
*(imagined)* A hiker sets out on a warm afternoon with a half-litre bottle, drinks nothing for three hours, and comes back irritable and headachy over the map. Drinking is the first, cheapest thing to try before reading the irritability as anything deeper.

## Psychology parallels

- **Hydration and mood**, mild dehydration impairs mood, energy, and cognitive performance (Armstrong et al., 2012; Ganio et al., 2011)
- **Dehydration and cognition**, even 1-2% dehydration impairs attention and memory (Ganio et al., 2011; Adan, 2012)

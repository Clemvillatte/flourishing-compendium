> **Principle 40** — Meet death with a made face

The tradition's ideal of the good death is prepared for: the elder's speeches, the warrior's tradition, and the songs all counsel facing death with composure rather than with panic. The preparation is practical (affairs in order, farewells made) and inner (the heart settled about the fact of death). This book keeps the composure and excludes the destination cosmologies. Practically, keep your affairs in order (will, letters, instructions) as a standing practice, and notice how much everyday anxiety the order quietly removes.

(The huehuetlatolli's death counsels; the tradition's death-preparedness practices; the cosmologies excluded on stated scope.)

**Plain words** The good death is prepared for: the elder's speeches, the affairs in order, the farewells made, the heart settled about the fact. Composure rather than panic — preparation practical (documents, goodbyes) and inner. The tradition's counsel is to meet it with a made face.

*For example:* The grandfather who spends his last good year recording the passwords, the stories, the map of where things are — so that when the time comes, the family's grief isn't doubled by chaos.

> **Principle 40** — Meet death with a made face

The Nahua sources give two kinds of material on death. One is plain acknowledgement. In the poems León-Portilla attributes to Nezahualcóyotl, the poet says: "Thus we are, We are mortal, Men through and through, We all will have to go away, We all will have to die on earth. Like a painting, We will be erased" (León-Portilla 1995, [Voices of Mexico](http://www.revistascisan.unam.mx/Voices/pdfs/3010.pdf)). The other is funeral speech in Book VI of the Florentine Codex, which addresses the dead and their destination; that cosmology is outside this book's scope. The opened Codex text contains no counsel on putting affairs in order or making farewells, so that part of the principle is a modern application.

Modern evidence for that application is about planning, and its benefit shows up mainly in families. In a randomised trial of 309 hospital inpatients aged 80 or older in Melbourne, facilitated advance care planning meant that, among the 56 patients who died by six months, end-of-life wishes were known and followed for "25/29, 86%" in the intervention group versus "8/27, 30%" in the control group. Surviving relatives in the intervention group had "significantly less stress… anxiety… and depression" (Detering et al. 2010, BMJ 340:c1345, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20332506/)). The trial did not test whether planning lowers the patient's own everyday anxiety.

**Plain words**
Nahua poets faced death plainly: we are mortal, and we will be erased like a painting. The opened sources give no instructions for practical preparation, so that part is a modern addition. A large Australian trial found that when older patients made advance care plans, their wishes were far more likely to be followed and their relatives were less distressed afterwards. Writing down your wishes, and the details your family will need, is a practical step that carries evidence of benefit.

*For example:* A grandfather uses his last good year to record passwords, stories and a map of where things are kept, so that the family's grief is not doubled by confusion. This is an imagined case.

> **Principle 52** — Learn assertiveness: express needs and boundaries directly

Assertiveness involves expressing your needs, feelings, and boundaries directly and respectfully. It is distinct from aggression (violating others) and passivity (violating self). It is a trainable skill.

**Plain words** Assertiveness is the third way — not passive (swallow it) and not aggressive (spit it), but direct and respectful: here's what I need, here's my line. It's a learnable skill with words and posture. Most people have never seen it modelled; it can be taught.

*For example:* "I can't take this on this week" — no essay, no apology tour, no aggression. Just a clean line, delivered standing up. The other person can work with that.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Assertiveness training|Assertiveness training (Speed, Linehan)]]**, expressing needs and boundaries directly (Speed et al., 2018; Linehan, 1993)
- **Assertiveness improves outcomes and reduces resentment**, assertive communication is associated with better relationships and self-esteem (Speed et al., 2018; Kelly, 2004)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean between aggression and passivity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Right speech|Right speech: honest and direct]]** (Buddhism)

## Practice

- Practise assertiveness: express needs directly ("I need..."), set boundaries ("I cannot..."), say no without over-explaining. Be respectful of both self and other. Do not be passive or aggressive.

> **Principle 68** — Expect relapse and plan the return

Sutra 4.27 sits near the end of the Yoga Sutras and says the work is not finished even for an advanced practitioner:

> In the intervals of this [mind-stuff] there are other presented-ideas [coming] from subliminal-impressions.

(Yoga Sutras 4.27, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, p. 339, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary says the mind headed toward discrimination "has in its intervals other presented-ideas, either 'It is I' or 'It is mine' or 'I think' or 'I do not think'", and that they come "from the dwindling seeds, from previous subliminal-impressions". An objector points out that even a person who goes the rounds for alms is seen to lapse, and the sutra is the reply.

Sutra 4.28 gives the way back: "The escape from these [subliminal-impressions] is described as being like [the escape from] the hindrances." The commentary says the old impressions "must be restricted by the subliminal-impressions of discriminative thinking".

All this concerns lapses of attention in a practitioner who is close to the goal, and the text does not speak of everyday relapse into old habits. Carrying it over, so that relapse becomes expected terrain, is this page's reading, as are the written relapse plan and its minimum practice.

**Plain words**

Your mind will slip even after good practice, because the old patterns are still dwindling. The sutra treats that as expected and gives a way back. When you are steady, write down the smallest version of your practice and the first three steps of returning, so that a bad month does not have you designing it from scratch.

**For example**

Someone writes a card while calm. It says four minutes in the morning, no exceptions. Then a hard month hits and the practice shrinks to four minutes a day instead of stopping, and the next month it grows again.

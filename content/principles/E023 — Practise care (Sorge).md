> **Principle 415** — Practise care (Sorge)

> The Being of Dasein means ahead-of-itself-Being-already-in-(the-world) as Being-alongside (entities encountered within-the-world). This Being fills in the signification of the term "care" [Sorge].

(Heidegger, Being and Time, §41, trans. Macquarrie & Robinson, [complete text](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf).)

Heidegger uses "care" in "a purely ontologico-existential manner." He says "every tendency of Being which one might have in mind ontically, such as worry [Besorgnis] or carefreeness [Sorglosigkeit], is ruled out." So care here is not an emotion, and not worry. It is the structure of being ahead of yourself, already in a world, and alongside the things you deal with.

The structure has three parts: being ahead of oneself, being already in a world, and being alongside entities within it. In his vocabulary, dealing with things is concern and dealing with other people is solicitude, and he says that "care is always concern and solicitude, even if only privatively." The formula's "alongside" names things, so the entry's "alongside others" blurs the terms.

Heidegger then gives an old fable in which Care first shaped a creature of clay, and Saturn judges that "since 'Care' first shaped this creature, she shall possess it as long as it lives" (§42).

**Plain words** A human life has a shape before it has a mood: it is already underway in a situation it did not pick, busy with what is at hand, and leaning toward what is next. Heidegger calls that shape care. His fable makes the possession lifelong, since Care shaped the creature and Saturn ruled she keeps it. The attitude version — worry, or carefreeness — sits on top of the shape and does not replace it.

*For example:* Choosing what to eat for dinner already looks ahead (to hunger tomorrow), starts from where you are (this kitchen, this budget) and deals with things (the pan, the food).

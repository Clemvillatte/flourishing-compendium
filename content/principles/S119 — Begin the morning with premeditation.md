> **Principle 241** — Begin the morning with premeditation

Marcus records the exercise as a morning rehearsal: telling himself what kind of day he will have, that he will meet difficult people, be tempted to anger, face challenges, and meet them with virtue. Adopt this morning practice: anticipate the day's challenges and prepare your response.

> "Betimes in the morning say to thyself, This day I shalt have to do with an idle curious man, with an unthankful man, a railer, a crafty, false, or an envious man; an unsociable uncharitable man. All these ill qualities have happened unto them, through ignorance of that which is truly good and truly bad." (Meditations I.15 in this edition, [Casaubon translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2680/2680-h/2680-h.htm))

(Med. II.1; Seneca, Letters 16, 101; Disc. I.1, I.4, III.12, III.23, IV.4.)

**Plain words** Before the day starts, tell yourself the truth about it: you will meet someone rude, something will break, something will tempt you to snap. Marcus sets the exercise down as a morning rehearsal, not to be gloomy, but so the difficult parts arrive as expected guests instead of ambushes. A rehearsed response is already half-chosen.

*For example (imagined):* A nurse spends two minutes in the car park thinking "today someone will be rude to me, and I will not be rude back." At 10 a.m., when someone is, she has already decided how she will answer.

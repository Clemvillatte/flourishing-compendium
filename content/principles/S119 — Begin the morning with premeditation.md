> **Principle 241** — Begin the morning with premeditation

Marcus begins each day by telling himself what kind of day he will have: he will meet difficult people, he will be tempted to anger, he will face challenges, and he will meet them with virtue. Adopt this morning practice: anticipate the day's challenges and prepare your response.

> "Betimes in the morning say to thyself, This day I shalt have to do with an idle curious man, with an unthankful man, a railer, a crafty, false, or an envious man; an unsociable uncharitable man. All these ill qualities have happened unto them, through ignorance of that which is truly good and truly bad." (Meditations I.15 in this edition, [Casaubon translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2680/2680-h/2680-h.htm))

(Med. II.1; Seneca, Letters 16, 101; Disc. I.1, I.4, III.12, III.23, IV.4.)

**Plain words** Before the day starts, tell yourself the truth about it: you will meet someone rude, something will break, something will tempt you to snap. Marcus did this every morning, not to be gloomy, but so the difficult parts arrive as expected guests instead of ambushes. A rehearsed response is already half-chosen.

*For example:* The nurse who spends two minutes in the car park thinking "today someone will be rude to me, and I will not be rude back" — and isn't knocked sideways at 10 a.m. when it happens.

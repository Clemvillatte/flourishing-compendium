> **Principle 46** — Recognise identity and meaning: identity provides meaning

> people living in modern societies provide their lives with unity and purpose by constructing internalized and evolving narratives of the self.

(McAdams, "The Psychology of Life Stories," Review of General Psychology 5(2), 2001, 100-122, abstract, describing his life story model of identity; [SAGE page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/1089-2680.5.2.100). Only the abstract was opened, since the article is behind a paywall.)

In a later summary McAdams and McLean define narrative identity as "a person's internalized and evolving life story, integrating the reconstructed past and imagined future to provide life with some degree of unity and purpose" ([Current Directions in Psychological Science, 2013, abstract](https://is.muni.cz/el/1421/podzim2013/PSB_443/um/5__Oct_29__Narrative_Identity.pdf)).

So the research tradition ties identity, as a story, to a sense of unity and purpose. The entry goes further, in two ways that these abstracts do not state. They do not say that an identity crisis is a meaning crisis, and they do not say that a person with the same facts but no story has less meaning. Those are the entry's own claims. The wording "in modern societies" is also McAdams's own limit.

**Plain words** Who you are includes a story about why your life matters. When the story holds together, the facts of a life feel like they add up. When it breaks, a person may have plenty of events and little sense of what they mean.

*For example:* Two people retire with the same pension and the same health. One says "forty years in the same plant." The other says "I built things with my hands and raised two kids." The facts match, and the stories differ.

## Psychology parallels

- **Identity and meaning**, coherent identity is associated with meaning and purpose in life (McAdams, 2001; Steger, 2012)
- **Meaning in life and well-being**, presence of meaning predicts well-being (Steger, 2012; Steger et al., 2006)

## Practice

- Attend to the meaning dimension of identity: what makes my life meaningful? What is my story about? If identity feels empty, the issue may be meaning, not identity per se.

> **Principle 94** — Use intuition to enter the flow of things

Bergson opens the essay by distinguishing two ways of knowing a thing: "The first implies that we move round the object; the second that we enter into it." The first is analysis; the second he calls intuition.

> "By intuition is meant the kind of intellectual sympathy by which one places oneself within an object in order to coincide with what is unique in it and consequently inexpressible. Analysis, on the contrary, is the operation which reduces the object to elements already known, that is, to elements common both to it and other objects." (Bergson, An Introduction to Metaphysics, trans. T. E. Hulme, essay opening, [Hulme translation](https://reasoned.org/dir/lit/int-meta.pdf))

Bergson also insists that intuition requires extensive preparatory knowledge:

> "For we do not obtain an intuition from reality - that is, an intellectual sympathy with the most intimate part of it - unless we have won its confidence by a long fellowship with its superficial manifestations." (Bergson, An Introduction to Metaphysics, trans. T. E. Hulme, conclusion, [Hulme translation](https://reasoned.org/dir/lit/int-meta.pdf))

His concluding example is literary composition: anyone who has attempted it knows that when the subject has been studied at length, the materials all collected, and the notes all made, "something more is needed in order to set about the work of composition itself, and that is an often very painful effort to place ourselves directly at the heart of the subject, and to seek as deeply as possible an impulse, after which we need only let ourselves go." (concluding paragraphs, [Hulme translation](https://reasoned.org/dir/lit/int-meta.pdf)) The compendium's proposed application: when analysis has gathered the material and the work has stalled, the entry into the thing itself is a separate act.

**Plain words**
Analysis fixes a thing long enough to label its parts. Intuition follows the thing while it changes. The move is hard, as Bergson warns: the mind has to reverse its habitual direction and recast its categories. That is why the attempt comes after the preparation, not instead of it.

*For example:* Imagine a writer with the research done and the notes made, the composition still not started. The attempt Bergson describes is the effort to place themselves directly at the heart of the subject and seek, as deeply as possible, the impulse that will guide the composition.

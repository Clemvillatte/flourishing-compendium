> **Principle 53** — Learn to balance autonomy and connection: both are needs

Autonomy (being one's own person, self-directed) and connection (belonging, being related) are both fundamental needs. Neither should be sacrificed to the other. The capacity to hold both — to be connected without losing oneself, and autonomous without isolating — is a foundation of well-being.

**Plain words** You need two things that sound like opposites: to be your own person, and to belong to people. Sacrifice either one long enough and you'll get sick of the trade. The art of a good life is holding both — close enough to be held, separate enough to be someone.

*For example:* The partner who gave up all her friendships for the relationship and the one who kept every friendship but never came home for dinner are making the same mistake in opposite directions. Both are starving one need to feed the other.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-determination|Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)]]**, autonomy, competence, and relatedness are three basic needs (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Autonomy and connection are both necessary**, sacrificing either predicts lower well-being (Deci & Ryan, 2000; La Guardia et al., 2000)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean between independence and dependence]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **[[I and Thou|I and Thou: both autonomy and relation]]** (Buber, I and Thou)
- **[[Ubuntu|Ubuntu and individuality together]]** (African Philosophy)

## Practice

- Check both: am I maintaining my autonomy (my own values, choices, direction) and my connection (belonging, relationship)? If you sacrifice one for the other, both suffer.

> **Principle 53** — Learn to balance autonomy and connection: both are needs

Ryan and Deci's account proposes three basic psychological needs: competence, autonomy and relatedness, whose satisfaction predicts well-being and whose thwarting predicts the opposite (2000). Autonomy, in this literature, does not mean detachment: "within SDT, autonomy refers not to being independent, detached, or selfish but rather to the feeling of volition that can accompany any act, whether dependent or independent, collectivist or individualist" (p. 74). So the principle's pair is not two lifestyles to choose between; both are needs, and a life that trades one away runs a standing deficit.

> "The findings have led to the postulate of three innate psychological needs—competence, autonomy, and relatedness—which when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being." (Ryan & Deci, 2000, p. 68)

**Plain words** You need two things that sound like opposites: to be your own person, and to belong to people. You can care about someone and still say honestly what you want; agreeing freely is not the same as agreeing because disagreement feels forbidden. Frustrate either need long enough and motivation and well-being suffer.

*For example:* A partner says, "I want to spend Sunday with you, and I also want to keep my afternoon class." They talk it through until each can accept the plan freely, rather than treating disagreement as disloyalty.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-determination|Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)]]**, autonomy, competence, and relatedness are three basic needs (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Autonomy and connection are both necessary**, sacrificing either predicts lower well-being (Deci & Ryan, 2000; La Guardia et al., 2000)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean between independence and dependence]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **[[I and Thou|I and Thou: both autonomy and relation]]** (Buber, I and Thou)
- **[[Ubuntu|Ubuntu and individuality together]]** (African Philosophy)

## Practice

- Check both: am I maintaining my autonomy (my own values, choices, direction) and my connection (belonging, relationship)? Chronically frustrating either one tends to cost motivation and well-being.

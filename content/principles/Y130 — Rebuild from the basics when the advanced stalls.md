> **Principle 130** — Rebuild from the basics when the advanced stalls

The sutras present the lower aids as the ground of the higher ones. Sutra 2.28 says that "after the aids to yoga have been followed up, when the impurity has dwindled, there is an enlightenment of perception reaching up to the discriminative discernment" (trans. Woods, p. 172). Sutra 2.29 lists them from abstentions and observances upward and the commentary adds, "The following up of these must be performed in succession" (p. 177).

### Basics named in the texts

The abstentions in 2.30 are "injury and from falsehood and from theft and from incontinence and from acceptance of gifts" (p. 178), and the observances in 2.32 are "cleanliness and contentment and self-castigation and study and devotion to the Isvara" (p. 181). On moderation, Arnold's Gita says "Religion is not his who too much fasts or too much feasts, nor his who sleeps away an idle mind" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Arnold).

### What the texts do not say

Nothing I opened tells a practitioner to go back to the foundations when advanced practice stalls, or describes a maintenance cycle. The idea that returning is not regression, and the annual "foundations month", are the principle's own proposals. They follow from reading the sequence as cumulative, as the commentary does with "in succession", but that is an inference, not a quote. The principle's list also adds posture, breath and a moderation audit. Posture and breath are aids in 2.29, while moderation comes from the Gita and is not one of the five observances in Woods.

**Plain words** The texts place conduct and daily habits first and say practice goes in order. Going back to them when things stall is a reasonable reading of that order. A scheduled yearly review is a modern device, not found in the sources.

*For example:* Imagined example: after a stretch where your sitting has felt flat, you spend a month on sleep, honest dealings and a settled posture before returning to concentration practice.

(Yoga Sutras 2.28-2.30 and 2.32, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 172, 177-178, 181, Internet Archive; Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388; both opened 2026-10-21. Arnold's verses are unnumbered.)

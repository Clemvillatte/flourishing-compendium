> **Principle 67** — Recognise the role of physical environment in attention: light, space, sound

The opened evidence for this principle is about noise, one of the three things in its title. Szalma and Hancock's meta-analysis pulled together 242 studies of noise and performance, and the effects varied with the noise and with the task. (Szalma & Hancock, 2011.)

> "Collective findings identified continuous versus intermittent noise, noise type, and type of task as the major distinguishing characteristics that moderated response." (Szalma & Hancock, 2011, [Psychological Bulletin 137(4):682-707](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ930167))

The compendium's working rule follows that moderation finding rather than a blanket law: treat the environment as something to test, not to obey. The light, space and sound entries in this principle's title have research literatures of their own, cited below as parallels; the noise finding alone was opened for this page, and it says the effect depends on the kind of noise and the kind of task. (Szalma & Hancock, 2011.)

**Plain words**
Your attention runs on equipment you did not choose: the light in the room, the sound in it, the space on the desk. The research on sound says the effect is real but particular: some noise hurts some tasks and not others. The practical move is to experiment: try the work in the quiet and try it in the noise, and keep the version where the work goes better.

*For example:* Moving a desk to face the window, or away from the kitchen, is a one-afternoon experiment. The check is the work: what got finished, and how the sitting felt.

## Psychology parallels

- **Environmental factors and attention**, lighting, noise, temperature, and air quality affect attentional performance (Hygge & Knez, 2001; Wargocki et al., 2005)
- **Natural light improves attention and mood**, exposure to natural light improves cognitive performance and well-being (Figueiro et al., 2017; Boubekri et al., 2014)
- **Noise impairs attention**, background noise (especially speech) degrades concentration (Szalma & Hancock, 2011; Hongisto, 2005)

## Practice

- Optimise physical environment: natural light where possible, quiet or white noise (not speech), comfortable temperature, clean air, organised space. Small environmental changes produce measurable attentional improvements.

> **Principle 67** — Recognise the role of physical environment in attention: light, space, sound

Physical environment — lighting, space, sound, temperature, air quality — directly affects attentional capacity. Poor environments degrade attention; well-designed environments support it.

**Plain words** Light, sound, and space are attention chemistry — a windowless grey room and a bright messy one produce different minds. You don't need a designer; you need light you can see by, quiet you can think in, and a surface with nothing on it.

*For example:* Moving the desk to face the window and the mind follows — the eyes get a horizon, the focus gets a gear. Cheap renovation, real effect.
## Psychology parallels

- **Environmental factors and attention**, lighting, noise, temperature, and air quality affect attentional performance (Hygge & Knez, 2001; Wargocki et al., 2005)
- **Natural light improves attention and mood**, exposure to natural light improves cognitive performance and well-being (Figueiro et al., 2017; Boubekri et al., 2014)
- **Noise impairs attention**, background noise (especially speech) degrades concentration (Szalma & Hancock, 2011; Hongisto, 2005)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Designing for virtue|Designing for virtue]]** (Aristotle, Politics)
- **[[Feng shui (harmonious environment)|Feng shui (harmonious environment)]]** (Chinese tradition)

## Practice

- Optimise physical environment: natural light where possible, quiet or white noise (not speech), comfortable temperature, clean air, organised space. Small environmental changes produce measurable attentional improvements.

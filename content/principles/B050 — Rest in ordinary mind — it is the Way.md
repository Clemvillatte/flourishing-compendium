> **Principle 50** — Rest in ordinary mind: it is the Way

In case 19 of The Gateless Gate, Jōshū asks Nansen about the Way. Nansen answers through a paradox: deliberately seeking it can move the seeker away from it, while ordinary mind is already its expression (trans. and commentary Kōun Yamada, [text](https://terebess.hu/zen/gat.pdf)).

> "The ordinary mind is the Way." (Nansen's answer to Jōshū, The Gateless Gate, case 19, trans. Kōun Yamada, [text](https://terebess.hu/zen/gat.pdf))

Wumen's comment still speaks of years of further inquiry after Jōshū's realisation, and Yamada illustrates ordinary mind through everyday activities while continuing to instruct students in practice. The case therefore gives no simple licence to abandon cultivation or treat every habitual reaction as wisdom (case 19, [text](https://terebess.hu/zen/gat.pdf)).

### Plain words

The compendium draws an invitation to examine the activity you are already in before searching for an exceptional experience. You can attend to making tea, speaking to someone, or walking to work without requiring it to feel profound. This suggestion does not decide whether a retreat or formal practice would be useful.

### For example

Imagine pausing over morning tea. Notice its warmth and taste, then return to what the day requires. If the moment feels unremarkable, you need not label it a failed practice or invent a story about having found ultimate peace.

> **Principle 40** — Distinguish accepting a feeling from accepting a circumstance

You can accept that you feel grief without accepting that the loss was acceptable. You can accept that you feel anger without accepting that the harm was acceptable. Conflating these is a common [[Acceptance|barrier to acceptance]]: people fear that accepting the feeling means approving of the situation.

**Plain words** You can accept that you feel grief without accepting that the loss was acceptable; accept the anger without accepting the harm. Conflating the two is the common barrier. Two acceptances, two different departments — feelings get accepted, circumstances get judged.

*For example:* The person who refuses to "accept" the anger because they think acceptance means approving the betrayal — and the clarification that unlocks it: the feeling gets a full welcome; the betrayal still gets a response. Both things, in their separate lanes.
## Psychology parallels

- **Acceptance of internal experience is distinct from behavioural passivity**, ACT explicitly distinguishes acceptance of feelings from acceptance of harmful circumstances (Hayes et al., 2011)
- **Values-based action alongside acceptance prevents passivity**, acceptance paired with committed action is the core ACT stance (Hayes et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Distinguishing what is up to us** (Stoics, Epictetus, Enchiridion)
- **[[The dichotomy of control|The dichotomy of control]]** (Stoics, Epictetus, Discourses)

## Practice

- When you notice resistance to accepting a feeling, check: "am I afraid that accepting this feeling means accepting the situation?" Remind yourself these are separate.

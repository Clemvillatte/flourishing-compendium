> **Principle 3** — Know that thinking has limited capacity: cognitive load is real

Working memory and attentional resources are finite. When cognitive load is high (stress, distraction, fatigue, information overload), thinking quality degrades. This is not a character flaw; it is an architectural constraint.

**Plain words** Working memory and attention are finite. When cognitive load is high — stress, distraction, fatigue, overload — thinking quality degrades. Not a character flaw; an architecture fact. Manage the load, not the blame.

*For example:* The person who tries to make a life decision at 9 p.m. after a full workday — and the discovery that deferring the same decision to a rested morning changes the decision's quality. The mind was never broken; it was loaded.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Working memory capacity limits|Working memory capacity limits]]**, roughly 4±1 chunks of information can be actively maintained (Cowan, 2001; Baddeley, 2003)
- **[[Cognitive load theory|Cognitive load theory]]**, learning and problem-solving degrade under high cognitive load (Sweller, 1988; Paas & Van Merriënboer, 1994)
- **The ego depletion model (willpower as a depletable resource) failed to replicate**, the specific claim that self-control is a depletable muscle is not supported by large multi-lab replications (Hagger et al., 2016); however, the experience of mental fatigue is real even if the mechanism is not a "resource" (Inzlicht et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Do not make important decisions when cognitively loaded (tired, stressed, distracted, multitasking). Reduce load first: write things down, remove distractions, rest.

> **Principle 90** — Participate: do with, not to

Dewey judges social life by how widely its interests are shared and how freely groups exchange experience. Working beside someone is insufficient if one person supplies all the purposes and the other merely follows instructions (Democracy and Education, VII.1, [text](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/democracy-and-education/text/chapter-7)).

> "A democracy is more than a form of government; it is primarily a mode of associated living, of conjoint communicated experience." (Dewey, Democracy and Education, 1916, VII.2, [text](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/democracy-and-education/text/chapter-7))

Addams asks settlement residents to live beside their neighbours until relationships and mutual interests develop; the settlement itself must remain ready to change its methods as circumstances require (Twenty Years at Hull-House, 1910, [Oxford excerpt](https://learninglink.oup.com/access/content/schaller-3e-dashboard-resources/document-excerpt-from-jane-addams-twenty-years-at-hull-house-1910)). The compendium reads these accounts as a practical invitation to share decisions about a task with the people involved.

### Plain words

Before offering help, ask what the other person wants to accomplish and what part they want to take. Leave room for their answer to change the plan. On this reading, a useful test is whether someone can influence the work or is only allowed to carry out instructions.

### For example

Consider a parent preparing dinner with a child: offer a manageable choice of dishes, agree who will do each step, and discuss what to change next time. The point is to let the child help decide what happens, rather than only carry out the parent's plan.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-determination|Self-determination]]**: autonomy and relatedness are compatible needs, although unsupportive conditions can put them in conflict ([Deci and Ryan, 2000, pp. 242, 253](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf)).
- **[[Cooperative learning|Cooperative learning]]**: a review reports achievement and relationship benefits from cooperative learning, but stresses that putting students into groups does not ensure cooperation ([Johnson and Johnson, 2014, pp. 843-846](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/167/16731690008.pdf)).

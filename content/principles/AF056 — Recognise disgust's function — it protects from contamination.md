> **Principle 56** — Recognise disgust's function: it protects from contamination

The core claim has a clear source. Curtis, de Barra and Aunger write: "Disgust is an evolved psychological system for protecting organisms from infection through disease avoidant behaviour" ([Philosophical Transactions B 366, 2011, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21199843/?dopt=Abstract)). Their abstract adds that "group hygiene is extended symbolically to cultural rules about purity and pollution, which create social separations and are enforced as manners". So the same system that keeps you from spoiled food also helps draw lines between people.

### Where the extension goes wrong

Hodson and Costello found that interpersonal-disgust sensitivity "predicted negative attitudes toward immigrants, foreigners, and socially deviant groups, even after controlling for concerns with contracting disease". The effect ran through ideological orientations and "dehumanizing perceptions of the out-group" ([Psychological Science 18(8), 2007, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17680940/)). Their sample was 103 English Canadian students, and the data are correlational ([British Psychological Society Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/people-sensitive-disgust-are-more-likely-hold-right-wing-views)). So the evidence shows a link, not a proven cause.

**Plain words**
Disgust evolved to keep you away from things that can make you ill. Over time people also attached it to rules about who and what counts as clean. When it lands on a person, it feels like information, but it can be a reflex about belonging. The principle asks you to ask which one you are feeling.

**For example**
Notice a flash of disgust at a person or group, and ask two questions. Is there an actual physical risk? Or did the feeling arrive with a thought about who they are? If it is the second, treat it as a signal about your own reaction and not as evidence about them. This is a practice for reflection, not a tested method for reducing prejudice.

## Psychology parallels

- **Disgust as pathogen avoidance**, core disgust is an evolved defence against contamination (Rozin et al., 2000)
- **Moral disgust can be misapplied**, extending disgust to people or groups is associated with dehumanisation and prejudice (Haidt et al., 1997; Hodson & Costello, 2007)

## Practice

- Notice when you feel disgust toward a person or group. Ask: "is this protecting me from actual harm, or is it a moral judgement dressed as contamination?" Disgust toward people is worth questioning.

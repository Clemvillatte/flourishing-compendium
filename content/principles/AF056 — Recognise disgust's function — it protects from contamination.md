> **Principle 56** — Recognise disgust's function: it protects from contamination

Disgust is a rejection response to potential contamination (physical or moral). It is adaptive in protecting from pathogens and toxic social influence. But moral disgust can be misapplied — treating people as "contaminated" because of their views or identity — and is a mechanism of prejudice and dehumanisation.

**Plain words** Disgust is a rejection response to contamination, physical or moral — adaptive for pathogens and toxic influence. But moral disgust misapplied treats people as contaminated, and dehumanisation follows. Clean your disgust of its moral overreach.

*For example:* The visceral recoil at the opposing side's rally footage — the contamination system doing its job on ideas — and the moment it starts reading people as unclean, the conversation is over and the damage has begun. The reaction is natural; the target is chosen.
## Psychology parallels

- **Disgust as pathogen avoidance**, core disgust is an evolved defence against contamination (Rozin et al., 2000)
- **Moral disgust can be misapplied**, extending disgust to people or groups is associated with dehumanisation and prejudice (Haidt et al., 1997; Hodson & Costello, 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Purity vs pollution|Purity vs pollution]]** (Douglas, Purity and Danger)
- **The face of the other** (Levinas, Totality and Infinity)

## Practice

- Notice when you feel disgust toward a person or group. Ask: "is this protecting me from actual harm, or is it a moral judgement dressed as contamination?" Disgust toward people is worth questioning.

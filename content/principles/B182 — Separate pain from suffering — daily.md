> **Principle 182** — Separate pain from suffering: daily

The Sallatha Sutta pictures pain arriving as two arrows: the first is the bodily feeling, the second is the sorrow, grief, lamentation and distress that follow it. The instructed disciple still feels the first arrow; the teaching is that the second is added by reaction, and can be left unshot. (SN 36.6; McCracken.)

> "Just as if they were to shoot a man with an arrow and, right afterward, were to shoot him with another one, so that he would feel the pains of two arrows; in the same way, when touched with a feeling of pain, the uninstructed run-of-the-mill person sorrows, grieves, & laments, beats his breast, becomes distraught. So he feels two pains, physical & mental." (SN 36.6, Thanissaro)

> "Now, the well-instructed disciple of the noble ones, when touched with a feeling of pain, does not sorrow, grieve, or lament, does not beat his breast or become distraught. So he feels one pain: physical, but not mental." (SN 36.6, Thanissaro)

The sutta leaves the first arrow alone; it works on the second.

**Plain words**
The headache is one event; your commentary on the headache is a second one. In daily life you can check: what is the actual sensation right now, and what am I adding to it? Rain is wet; the outrage about rain is a separate event. The sutta's trained disciple still feels the first arrow; the second arrow is the one you can decline to shoot.

*For example:* The cold is real: put on a coat. The twenty-minute internal monologue about the cold is a second, colder weather system.

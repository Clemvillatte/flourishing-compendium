> **Principle 12** — Make friendship the load-bearing wall

Epicurus gives friendship a central place in a happy life: Principal Doctrine 27 ranks acquiring friends above the other means wisdom provides for happiness, and 28 connects friendship with security ([Principal Doctrines 27-28, trans. R. D. Hicks](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/princdoc.html)).

> Every friendship is an excellence in itself, even though it begins in mutual advantage.

(Vatican Saying 23, [trans. Peter Saint-Andre](https://monadnock.net/epicurus/vatican-sayings.html).)

Friendship may begin with help, yet the relationship has worth beyond the next favour. Saying 34 emphasises confidence in friends' help, while 39 rejects both constant benefit-seeking and a friendship from which all hope of help is excluded ([Vatican Sayings 23, 34, 39](https://monadnock.net/epicurus/vatican-sayings.html)). Saint-Andre's note to 23 records a textual variant: some editors read “choiceworthy” where his translation has “excellence” ([translator's note](https://monadnock.net/epicurus/vatican-sayings.html)). The ethical reading remains disputed: O'Keefe argues that choosing friendship for itself need not rule out an egoistic explanation ([O'Keefe, argument and conclusion](https://philarchive.org/archive/OKEIEF)).

(Principal Doctrines 27-28; Vatican Sayings on friendship.)

### Plain words

You needn't be in trouble today to value knowing someone will answer tomorrow. The friendship also asks something of you: make room for the other person's needs instead of counting what each encounter pays back ([Vatican Sayings 34, 39](https://monadnock.net/epicurus/vatican-sayings.html)).

### For example

Optional application (Motivation; Overt behaviour): invite a friend to an unhurried meeting before the week fills up. If the timing fails, offer a different time or kind of contact. Afterwards ask whether it suited both of you; repeat the arrangement that did. This scheduling exercise is a modern application, not an ancient prescription.

## Psychology parallels

- **Social relationships and health**: Holt-Lunstad and colleagues' meta-analysis links stronger relationships with higher survival odds across 148 studies. This is a broader association about social relationships, not a trial of Epicurean friendship or proof that adding meetings extends life ([2010 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20668659/)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **Aristotle's philia**: Nicomachean Ethics IX.9 argues that a happy person needs virtuous friends and shared activity. Compare its reasons with Epicurus's emphasis on confidence and security, rather than treating the arguments as identical ([Aristotle IX.9](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.9.ix.html)).

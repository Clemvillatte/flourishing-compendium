> **Principle 164** — Discard scheming

Chapter 19 of the Daodejing lists three renunciations it says would benefit the people a hundredfold: renouncing sageness and discarding wisdom; renouncing benevolence and discarding righteousness, after which the people would again become filial and kindly; and, the clause this principle takes as its core, renouncing artful contrivances and discarding scheming for gain, which the chapter says would leave no thieves nor robbers. Chapter 57 gives the same rule from the other direction: the more acts of crafty dexterity that men possess, the more do strange contrivances appear; and the more display there is of legislation, the more thieves and robbers there are.

(DDJ 19, 57.)

> "If we could renounce our artful contrivances and discard our (scheming for) gain, there would be no thieves nor robbers." (DDJ 19, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

The chapters address rulers and the naming of virtues. The compendium's personal reading is the third clause's shape: scheming is listed as a thing a person can renounce, and its fruits are named as what follow when they do not.

**Plain words**
The chapters' claim is that contrivance multiplies: more crafty dexterity, more strange contrivances; more scheming for gain, more thieves and robbers. The compendium's exercise: name one scheme you are running, and list what it has required so far — each item on the list is the chapter's "more".

*For example:* The compendium's picture: the office politician who wins the quarter and loses the decade — everyone eventually updates their model of them.

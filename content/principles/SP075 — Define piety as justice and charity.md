> **Principle 75** — Define piety as justice and charity

Religion's whole teaching, reduced by Spinoza to its practical content: help the poor, do not kill, do not covet, do not steal; and ceremonies, festivals, and creeds are decorations that become dangerous when mistaken for the content. This is Spinoza's famous reduction of faith to practice, and it generalizes beyond religion: in every tradition you touch, sort the load-bearing ethics from the costume, and keep your energy for the first. Practically, judge your communities by how members treat the vulnerable, not by the beauty of their observances.

(Theological-Political Treatise, ch. 12-14.)

**Plain words** Religion's practical content, Spinoza's reduction: help the poor, do not kill, do not covet, do not steal — and ceremonies, festivals, and creeds are decorations that become dangerous when mistaken for the content. Piety is justice and charity. The rest is interior design.

*For example:* The community that spends the year on the building fund, the robes, the schedule — and the audit question: what did the poor of the district actually receive? Whatever the answer, it's now the real sermon.

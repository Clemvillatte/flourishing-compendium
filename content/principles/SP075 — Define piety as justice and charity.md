> **Principle 75** — Define piety as justice and charity

The Treatise reduces religion's teaching to its moral core. Chapter XIV's definition of faith:

> "Faith consists in a knowledge of God, without which obedience to Him would be impossible, and which the mere fact of obedience to Him implies." (Treatise, ch. XIV, [Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/991))

Faith is not salutary in itself, but only in respect to the obedience it implies, "or as James puts it in his Epistle, ii:17, 'Faith without works is dead'". The test follows: "we can only judge a man faithful or unfaithful by his works. If his works be good, he is faithful, however much his doctrines may differ from those of the rest of the faithful: if his works be evil, though he may verbally conform, he is unfaithful." The fifth dogma of the universal faith states the worship: "That the worship of God consists only in justice and charity, or love towards one's neighbour." The moral core is small, chapter XII: the precepts "to uphold justice, to aid the weak, to do no murder, to covet no man's goods", resting on the doctrine of charity, which the chapter says is extolled above all others. The political version, chapter XIX: "To avoid such evils in a state, there is no safer way, than to make piety and religion to consist in acts only - that is, in the practice of justice and charity, leaving everyone's judgment in other respects free." The last clause reads: creeds and ceremonies are left to free judgment, not treated as the thing itself. The page's exercise drawn from the account: judge your communities by how members treat the vulnerable, not by the beauty of their observances.

**Plain words**
Spinoza asks what a religion's teaching amounts to in practice, and finds the core is small: uphold justice, aid the weak, do no murder, covet no one's goods. Faith shows in works; doctrine is not the test. Ceremonies and creeds are left free, because they are not what the law rests on.

*For example:* Imagined: a congregation's year runs on the building fund, the robes, the schedule. The audit question the test asks: what did the weak and needy of the district actually receive? On that answer, and not on the observances, the judgment rests.

> **Principle 57** — Work with boredom: it is a signal, not a void to fill

Boredom is the discomfort of being unengaged or unstimulated. It is not inherently bad; it can signal that an activity is not meaningful, that you need rest, or that you need a new challenge. The maladaptive response is to immediately fill it with distraction (scrolling, substances, compulsive behaviour).

**Plain words** Boredom is the discomfort of being unengaged — a signal, not a void. It can mean the activity is meaningless, you need rest, or you need a new challenge. The maladaptive response is the reflexive scroll; the adaptive one is reading the signal.

*For example:* The Sunday afternoon restlessness that gets fed reels for four hours — versus the person who sits with it a minute and finds the actual message: "the job is under-stimulating." One treats the itch; the other treats the skin.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Boredom|Boredom as a signal of meaninglessness or insufficient challenge]]**, boredom is associated with low engagement and can motivate either exploration or avoidance (Eastwood et al., 2012)
- **Boredom proneness is associated with impulsivity and substance use**, difficulty tolerating boredom predicts impulsive and compulsive behaviour (Mercer-Lynn et al., 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Acedia|Acedia (spiritual apathy)]]** (Evagrius Ponticus, Praktikos)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei and stillness]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 16)

## Practice

- When bored, do not immediately fill the void. Ask: "what is this boredom telling me?" Sometimes it means rest, sometimes it means change, sometimes it means sit with it and let it pass.

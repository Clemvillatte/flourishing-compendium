> **Principle 2** — Use "no more this than that" as a working formula

The formula is one of Sextus's "Sceptic formulae concerning things non-evident": "No more (one thing than another)". Its setting, from Book I's account of what scepticism is: it "opposes appearances to judgments in any way whatsoever, with the result that, owing to the equipollence of the objects and reasons thus opposed, we are brought firstly to a state of mental suspense and next to a state of 'unperturbedness' or quietude". Equipollence means "that no one of the conflicting judgments takes precedence of any other as being more probable"; the main basic principle is "opposing to every proposition an equal proposition". The formula's special property, chapter 7:

> "the formula 'No more' asserts that itself like all the rest, is 'No more this than that,' and thus cancels itself along with the rest." (Sextus Empiricus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism I.14, trans. Bury)

Because the formula cancels itself, saying it is not a new doctrine; the skeptic "states what appears to himself and announces his own impression in an undogmatic way". He still acts, "in accordance with appearance", and the End is "quietude in respect of matters of opinion and moderate feeling in respect of things unavoidable". The page's applications drawn from the account: no more pleasure than pain in the thing, no more natural than conventional in the claim. The page's exercise: when two options have argued you to exhaustion, say the formula, act if action is needed, and let the formula carry the residual weight.

**Plain words**
The sceptic's move is to set every appearance against every judgment about it and find the reasons evenly matched, which first suspends the argument and then, oddly, calms the arguer. The slogan "no more this than that" just reports the tie. It is not a new position — it applies to itself too — so using it costs nothing and commits you to nothing. When a decision has argued itself to a standstill, say it, then pick anyway if life requires picking.

*For example:* torn for a week between two nearly identical job offers, someone says the formula, takes the one with the shorter commute, and the jaw finally unclenches.

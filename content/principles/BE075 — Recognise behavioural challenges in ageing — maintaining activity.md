> **Principle 75** — Recognise behavioural challenges in ageing: maintaining activity

The sleep change is documented: in a meta-analysis of 65 studies (3,577 people aged 5 to 102), adult sleep showed less total time, less slow-wave and REM sleep, and more wake after sleep onset with age, and only sleep efficiency continued to decrease significantly after 60.

The activity side has cohort evidence behind it. Fifteen community cohorts totalling 189,192 people aged 44 to 79 found the most physically active were more likely to age successfully than sedentary adults (OR 1.64, 95% CI 1.40 to 1.94). The association was stronger in the younger group than the older group, and the meta-regression estimated it attenuating by approximately 3% per year of follow-up.

> "In adults, total sleep time, sleep efficiency, percentage of slow-wave sleep, percentage of REM sleep, and REM latency all significantly decreased with age, while sleep latency, percentage of stage 1 sleep, percentage of stage 2 sleep, and wake after sleep onset significantly increased with age." (Ohayon et al. 2004, [Sleep](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article-abstract/27/7/1255/2696819), Abstract, Results)

**Plain words**
The documented change is a sleep change: nights get lighter and more broken with age, and in this review only sleep efficiency showed a further significant decline after 60. The activity evidence is an association: people who stayed physically active were more likely to age successfully in these cohorts, with the measured association stronger in younger groups and attenuating across longer follow-ups. The practical task is negotiation, not denial; adjust the form, keep the behaviour.

*For example:* An imagined case: a lifelong runner whose knees and nights both object at 70 switches to swimming and a shorter morning routine, treating the change of form as part of the training rather than as its end.

## Psychology parallels

- **Ageing and activity**, maintaining activity is associated with healthy ageing (Carstensen et al., 2003; Rowe & Kahn, 1997)
- **Use it or lose it**, cognitive and physical activity maintain function (Stern, 2002; Lövdén et al., 2010)

## Practice

- As you age, maintain behavioural engagement: keep exercising, stay socially active, keep learning, maintain routines. Do not accept decline passively; maintain engagement.

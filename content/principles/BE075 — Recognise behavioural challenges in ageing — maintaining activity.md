> **Principle 75** — Recognise behavioural challenges in ageing: maintaining activity

Ageing produces specific behavioural changes: reduced activity, slower recovery, changes in sleep and energy. Maintaining behavioural engagement (activity, exercise, social, cognitive) is key to healthy ageing.

**Plain words** Ageing changes the behaviour portfolio — activity takes longer to recover, sleep shifts, routines matter more. The task isn't fighting the changes; it's adapting around them. An adjusted routine is a maintained life.

*For example:* The runner who becomes the walker who becomes the swimmer — same identity, adjusted equipment. The behaviour kept; the form negotiated. That's successful ageing.
## Psychology parallels

- **Ageing and activity**, maintaining activity is associated with healthy ageing (Carstensen et al., 2003; Rowe & Kahn, 1997)
- **Use it or lose it**, cognitive and physical activity maintain function (Stern, 2002; Lövdén et al., 2010)

## Practice

- As you age, maintain behavioural engagement: keep exercising, stay socially active, keep learning, maintain routines. Do not accept decline passively; maintain engagement.

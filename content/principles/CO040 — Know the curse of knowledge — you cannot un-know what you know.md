> **Principle 40** — Know the curse of knowledge: you cannot un-know what you know

Once you know something, you cannot accurately simulate what it is like not to know it. This affects teaching, communication, and empathy. You assume others know what you know and cannot understand their confusion.

**Plain words** The curse of knowledge: once you know something, you cannot accurately simulate not knowing it. This breaks teaching, communication, and empathy — you assume others know what you know. The fix: check what they actually have before you build on it.

*For example:* The expert who explains the new system in terms of concepts the newcomer doesn't have yet — "just use the pipeline" — and the fix: the one-line check, "does that make sense so far?" The curse is invisible from inside; the question makes it visible.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Curse of knowledge|Curse of knowledge]]**, experts overestimate what novices know (Camerer et al., 1989; Keysar et al., 2003)
- **The curse of knowledge impairs communication and teaching**, experts cannot accurately predict what learners find difficult (Newton, 1990; Nickerson, 1999)

## Practice

- When explaining something, ask: "what does this person actually know?" Do not assume. Test understanding rather than assuming it.

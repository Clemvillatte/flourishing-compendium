> **Principle 3** — Know that the self is multilayered: actual, ideal, and ought selves

The theory comes from E. Tory Higgins: each person holds self-state representations — the actual self (who you believe you are), the ideal self (who you or significant others hope you will be), and the ought self (who you or significant others believe you have a duty to be). The emotion depends on which gap is open.

> "Discrepancies between the actual/own self-state... and ideal self-states... signify the absence of positive outcomes, which is associated with dejection-related emotions (e.g., disappointment, dissatisfaction, sadness). In contrast, discrepancies between the actual/own self-state and ought self-states... signify the presence of negative outcomes, which is associated with agitation-related emotions (e.g., fear, threat, restlessness)." (Higgins, "Self-Discrepancy: A Theory Relating Self and Affect", Psychological Review 94, 1987, p. 319)

The two gaps read differently: an actual-ideal gap is an absence (hopes unmet), felt as dejection; an actual-ought gap is a presence of negative outcomes (duties violated), felt as agitation. Higgins' later regulatory focus work maps ideal-related regulation onto promotion focus (accomplishments and aspirations) and ought-related regulation onto prevention focus (safety and responsibilities) (Higgins, 1997).

**Plain words**
Write three lists: who I think I actually am; who I would love to be; who I owe it to others — family, culture, myself — to be. Then notice the feelings attached to each gap. The gap between actual and ideal shows up as sadness and flatness: a missing positive. The gap between actual and ought shows up as fear and guilt: a present negative. The distinction matters for repair: one gap calls for hope and a plan, the other for either compliance or an honest renegotiation of the duty.

*For example:* Imagine a person who "should" be a doctor (ought, inherited) while wanting to build things (ideal) while actually doing neither (actual) — the emotional signature: guilt toward the family, longing toward the workshop, flatness in the job. The three-selves map shows the exact fault lines.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-discrepancy|Self-discrepancy theory (Higgins)]]**, discrepancies between actual, ideal, and ought selves produce distinct emotional states (Higgins, 1987; Carver & Scheier, 1990)
- **Actual-ideal discrepancy → dejection (sadness, disappointment)**, actual-ought discrepancy → agitation (anxiety, guilt) (Higgins, 1987)
- **Promotion vs prevention focus**, ideal-related goals (promotion) vs ought-related goals (prevention) have distinct motivational patterns (Higgins, 1997)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The tripartite soul|The divided soul]]** (Plato, Republic)
- **The ideal and the real** (Kant, Critique of Practical Reason)
- **[[Self-overcoming|Self-overcoming]]** (Nietzsche, Thus Spoke Zarathustra)

## Practice

- Map your three selves: actual (who I am now), ideal (who I want to be), ought (who I should be). Notice where the discrepancies are and what emotions they produce. Some discrepancies are motivational; others are sources of unnecessary suffering.

> **Principle 1** — Understand rather than condemn

Spinoza states his method in the preface to Part III: he will consider human actions and appetites exactly as if they were lines, planes, and bodies. The maxim that summarizes the method is given in the Political Treatise: not to mock, not to lament, not to detest, but to understand. This is the master attitude of the whole Ethics. Anger, addiction, envy, and grief are treated as natural events with causes, like weather, and the first move is always the same: understand the mechanism before judging the person, including yourself. Practically, when a strong reaction arises, in yourself or another, postpone the verdict and look for the cause first.

(Ethics III, Preface; Political Treatise I.4, the maxim's formulation.)

**Plain words** Spinoza's maxim: not to mock, not to lament, not to detest, but to understand. Anger, addiction, envy, grief — treat them as natural events with causes, like weather. The first move is always the same: postpone the verdict, look for the mechanism.

*For example:* The colleague's meltdown stops being "what's wrong with him" and becomes "what's the mechanism?" — the sleep debt, the custody fight, the threatened role. Understanding doesn't excuse the behaviour; it replaces gossip with engineering.

> **Principle 68** — Recognise chronic illness and mood: illness affects mental health

Chronic illness (diabetes, heart disease, autoimmune, cancer) is associated with higher rates of depression and anxiety. Addressing illness is a mental health intervention.

**Plain words** Chronic illness raises depression and anxiety rates — diabetes, heart disease, autoimmune, cancer. Not weakness; the biology and the burden both bill the mind. Treating the illness is treating the mood; treating the mood helps the illness.

*For example:* The diabetes diagnosis that brings a mood collapse — and the mood support that improves glucose management. The body and the mind bill each other. Treat both accounts.
## Psychology parallels

- **Chronic illness and depression**, chronic physical illness is associated with depression (Barnett et al., 2012; Naylor et al., 2012)
- **Bidirectional relationship**, illness worsens mood; mood affects illness outcomes (Naylor et al., 2012; Cohen et al., 2019)

## Practice

- If you have a chronic illness, recognise its effect on mood. Address the illness (medical care) alongside the mood (psychological support). Do not treat illness-related mood as purely psychological.

> **Principle 86** — Examine what you hear

The second step, manana, is deliberation: testing the teaching against reason and experience, taking it apart to see whether it holds. The tradition treats listening without examination as the cause of borrowed certainty, and examination without listening as the cause of reinvention. Practically, for each important idea you receive, write one paragraph testing it against your own evidence.

(Shankara, Upadesasahasri; Vivekachudamani, attributed.)

**Plain words** The second step, manana, is deliberation: testing the teaching against reason and experience, taking it apart to see if it holds. Listening without examination breeds borrowed certainty; examination without listening breeds reinvention. Do both, in order.

*For example:* The student who hears the teaching and immediately demands "but how do we know?" — right instinct, wrong order. First receive it whole; then take it apart. The tradition's sequence is shravana, then manana, not both at once.

> **Principle 495** — Practise self-overcoming (Selbstüberwindung)

The idea is Zarathustra's. In Thomas Common's translation, the chapter is titled "Self-Surpassing". Zarathustra says he heard three things from life itself. The second and third are about obedience and command:

> "Whatever cannot obey itself, is commanded. Such is the nature of living things. ... Yea, even when it commandeth itself, then also must it atone for its commanding. Of its own law must it become the judge and avenger and victim."
> — Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, "Self-Surpassing," trans. Thomas Common

### The central claim

Then life speaks:

> "Behold," said she, "I am that WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF."
> (capitals in the translation)
> — same chapter

Life here is not a self-improvement project. Nietzsche ties the drive to will to power: "Wherever I found a living thing, there found I Will to Power." And surpassing includes values: "he who hath to be a creator in good and evil—verily, he hath first to be a destroyer, and break values in pieces."

### What to be careful about

The principle's other two citations do not carry this idea. Beyond Good and Evil §259 is a passage on life as "appropriation, injury, conquest of the strange and weak", and does not discuss self-overcoming. The Gay Science §289 ("Aboard Ship!") calls for "a new justice" and "new philosophers", not for self-overcoming as such.

"Do not be content with who you are" is the compendium's instruction, not a line of Nietzsche's. In the chapter, surpassing is described as something life does, with cost: the self that commands itself must "atone" for it.

(Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, "Self-Surpassing"; The Gay Science §289; Beyond Good and Evil §259, noted above as non-supporting.)

**Plain words** Nietzsche's claim is that anything alive is driven past its current form, and that a self which governs itself pays for it. That is a harder picture than "keep improving". New values mean breaking old ones, and the person doing the breaking is also the one on trial.

*For example:* Imagine a musician who has mastered a style and now has to break it to make anything new. The old work is not worthless, but it is what the new work has to get past.

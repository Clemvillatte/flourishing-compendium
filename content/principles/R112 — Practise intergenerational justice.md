> **Principle 374** — Practise intergenerational justice

Jonas proposes an imperative for what he calls a new type of human action, addressed to the new type of agency that operates it ([Philosophical Essays, 1980, quoted in the opened Lublin text](https://up.lublin.pl/files/biochemia/Etyka/ethics,_may,_28.pdf)):

> An imperative responding to the new type of human action and addressed to the new type of agency that operates it might run thus: "Act so that the effects of your action are compatible with the permanence of genuine human life"; or expressed negatively: "Act so that the effects of your action are not destructive of the future possibility of such life"; or simply: "Do not compromise the conditions for an indefinite continuation of humanity on earth"; or most generally: "In your present choices, include the future wholeness of Man among the objects of your will."

(Hans Jonas, quoted in [University of Life Sciences in Lublin course text, opened](https://up.lublin.pl/files/biochemia/Etyka/ethics,_may,_28.pdf); the document cites H. Jonas, Philosophical Essays. From Ancient Creed to Technological Man, University of Chicago Press, 1980.)

Jonas states an honest limitation himself: no rational contradiction is involved in violating the imperative; you can will the present good at the cost of the future one (Jonas, [Philosophical Essays, quoted in the Lublin text](https://up.lublin.pl/files/biochemia/Etyka/ethics,_may,_28.pdf)). It is a demand, not a proof. Acting as trustee means taking the demand as binding on decisions that outlive you.

### Plain words

You inherit a world you did not make and leave one you will not live in. The trustee question to ask of any decision that outlives you: does it use up something the people coming after will need?

### For example

Suppose a family is weighing whether to sell a forest to a pulp mill. They can ask what the fifth generation would want them to have done. The question does not settle the sale by itself; it changes what counts as a reason.

> **Principle 48** — Learn open monitoring meditation: awareness without narrow focus

Open monitoring (OM) meditation involves observing whatever arises in awareness (thoughts, sensations, sounds, emotions) without narrow focus or judgment. This trains meta-awareness, flexibility, and the capacity to notice contents without being captured.

**Plain words** Open-monitoring meditation is the opposite of gripping: sit back and let whatever comes up come up, watching it pass. Nothing to fix, nothing to follow far. It trains the wide mode — the floodlight, not the laser.

*For example:* Sitting for ten minutes watching thoughts arrive like trains — you don't board them, they leave anyway. That's the whole practice, and it changes the relationship with your own head.
## Psychology parallels

- **Open monitoring develops meta-awareness**, the capacity to notice the contents of awareness without narrow focus (Lutz et al., 2008; Tang et al., 2015)
- **OM meditation improves attentional flexibility**, monitoring without capture improves the ability to notice and let go (Lutz et al., 2008; Mrazek et al., 2013)

## Practice

- Practise open monitoring: 10-20 minutes, sitting with open awareness. Notice whatever arises (thought, sensation, sound, emotion) without following or judging. Let it pass. Notice the noticing.

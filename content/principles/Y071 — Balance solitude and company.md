> **Principle 71** — Balance solitude and company

No single verse sets the balance. Three passages give the two sides, and the principle is the pairing of them.

On solitude, the Gita's list of the marks of knowledge includes "an inclination for solitary places and an aversion for mundane society" (BG 13.8-12, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/13/verse/8/)). The same list includes "service of the Guru". Mukundananda's commentary adds that the devotee "accepts" society where it serves others, "and develops the strength to remain mentally unaffected by it".

On companionship, the person dear to Krishna is "free from malice toward all living beings", "friendly, and compassionate" (BG 12.13-14, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/12/verse/13/)). Sutra 1.33 asks for four attitudes toward other people: "friendliness towards happiness and compassion towards pain and joy towards merit and indifference towards demerit" (Yoga Sutras 1.33, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, p. 71, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft)). These are attitudes you can only practise with other people around.

The warning that unmixed solitude drifts into avoidance and unmixed company into diffusion is not in these texts. It is this page's reading of the pairing, and so is the monthly adjustment.

**Plain words**

The tradition wants time alone and time with people, and each trains something the other cannot. Alone, you can steady your attention. With others, you practise friendliness and compassion, which only exist in relation to someone. A reasonable habit is to look once a month at your ratio and adjust it toward whichever side you have been neglecting.

**For example**

A writer who spends most weeks alone realizes the only people she is friendly to are the ones she emails. She adds a weekly meal with friends and shortens her solitary mornings by half an hour.

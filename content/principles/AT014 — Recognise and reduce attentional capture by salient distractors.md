> **Principle 14** — Recognise and reduce attentional capture by salient distractors

Salient stimuli — loud noises, movement, notifications, bright colours — automatically capture attention. This is a bottom-up, largely involuntary process. The remedy is not willpower but environmental design.

**Plain words** Loud, bright, moving, sudden — your attention gets grabbed by design, not by accident. The modern world is an arms race of salience. You can't out-willpower it; you can change what's in the room.

*For example:* Willpower doesn't beat the notification. The phone in the drawer beats the notification. Design beats discipline.
## Psychology parallels

- **Salience-driven attentional capture**, novel, intense, and moving stimuli automatically capture attention (Theeuwes, 2010; Itti & Koch, 2001)
- **Distraction is not a failure of will but a property of the attention system**, even small, irrelevant stimuli disrupt focus (Forster & Lavie, 2008; SanMiguel et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Reduce salient distractors in your environment: silence notifications, remove visual clutter, work in quiet spaces. Do not rely on willpower to resist salience.

> **Principle 14** — Recognise and reduce attentional capture by salient distractors

Theeuwes opens with the everyday case: reading in the library when someone walks in — does attention shift because you meant it to, or despite the goal of reading? His experiments showed that salient singletons capture attention regardless of the observer's goals and intentions. Capture is stimulus-driven, a property of the display rather than a lapse of will.

> "Stimulus-driven, or bottom-up, selection refers to the capacity of certain stimulus attributes to attract our attention, irrespective of our goals and beliefs." (Theeuwes, "Top-down search strategies cannot override attentional capture", Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 11, 2004, p. 65, [publisher's PDF via VU Research Portal](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/1975176/Theeuwes%20Psychonomic%20Bulletin%20and%20Review%2011(1)%202004b%20u.pdf))

The remedy result is in the same paper: capture was governed by relative salience. When the salient element was made less salient — by shrinking the color difference between it and the surrounding elements — it stopped interfering. The intention to ignore did not do that work; the changed properties of the distractor did. That is the experimental footing for treating the room, rather than the resolve, as the thing to manage.

> "When the color singleton was made less salient (by reducing the color difference between the target and the nontarget elements), the color singleton no longer interfered with the search for the shape singleton." (Theeuwes, 2004, p. 66, [publisher's PDF via VU Research Portal](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/1975176/Theeuwes%20Psychonomic%20Bulletin%20and%20Review%2011(1)%202004b%20u.pdf))

**Plain words**
Some things pull at your eyes and ears whether or not you wanted them to. The pull tracks how loud, bright, or sudden they are, and trying harder to ignore them does not remove it. Making the thing quieter, dimmer, or absent is what removes it.

**For example**
The phone buzzing on the desk wins the first glance even during a sentence you meant to finish. The same phone in a drawer in another room does not compete; nothing there has the salience to pull. The drawer removes the competition; resolve only argues with it.

## Psychology parallels

- **Salience-driven attentional capture**, novel, intense, and moving stimuli automatically capture attention (Theeuwes, 2010; Itti & Koch, 2001)
- **Distraction is not a failure of will but a property of the attention system**, even small, irrelevant stimuli disrupt focus (Forster & Lavie, 2008; SanMiguel et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Reduce salient distractors in your environment: silence notifications, remove visual clutter, work in quiet spaces. Do not rely on willpower to resist salience.

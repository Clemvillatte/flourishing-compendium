> **Principle 64** — Cultivate awe: it expands the self by diminishing it

Awe is the response to vastness that transcends your current frame of reference. It is associated with reduced self-focus, increased generosity, and a sense of connection to something larger. It is one of the most distinct and underused positive emotions.

**Plain words** Awe: the response to vastness that transcends your current frame — associated with reduced self-focus, increased generosity, connection to something larger. One of the most direct shortcuts to a smaller, quieter ego. Cultivate awe on purpose.

*For example:* The person who stands under the actual night sky, away from the city — the inbox-sized self dissolving into something a billion years wide — and returns to the inbox with a strangely lighter grip. The awe did in ten minutes what the productivity apps never did.
## Psychology parallels

- **Awe reduces self-focus and increases prosocial behaviour**, awe is associated with small-self effect and increased generosity (Piff et al., 2015)
- **Awe interventions improve well-being and reduce depressive symptoms**, awe walks and awe experiences reliably improve well-being (Stellar et al., 2018; Anderson et al., 2025)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The sublime|The sublime]]** (Burke, Philosophical Enquiry; Kant, Critique of Judgement)
- **[[Mysterium tremendum|Mysterium tremendum et fascinans]]** (Rudolf Otto, The Idea of the Holy)
- **The Dao that cannot be named** (Daoism, Daodejing 1)

## Practice

- Seek out experiences of vastness — nature, art, music, the night sky, extraordinary human achievement. Let yourself feel small in a way that is liberating rather than diminishing.

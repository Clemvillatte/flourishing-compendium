> **Principle 433** — Practise anguish (angoisse)

Sartre distinguishes anguish (angoisse) from fear: fear concerns beings in the world, while anguish concerns oneself ([Being and Nothingness, Part I, ch. 1, Barnes translation; opened full text](https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.6651/2015.6651.Being-And-Nothingness_djvu.txt)). He connects anguish with becoming conscious of one's freedom. His examples are vertigo at a precipice and the gambler who, having resolved to quit, finds that nothing prevents him from gambling.

> Vertigo is anguish to the extent that I am afraid not of falling over the precipice, but of throwing myself over.

(Sartre, Being and Nothingness, trans. Hazel E. Barnes, 1956, Part I, ch. 1; [opened full text, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.6651/2015.6651.Being-And-Nothingness_djvu.txt).)

### Plain words

When a choice unsettles you, ask what remains yours to decide. You do not have to intensify the feeling or treat it as evidence that you are living more honestly. Feeling calm does not show that you have avoided responsibility. This is philosophical reflection, not a diagnosis.

This page is not a clinical assessment. Persistent anxiety that interferes with daily life is a clinical matter: the [NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/generalized-anxiety-disorder-gad) notes that treatment for generalized anxiety disorder typically involves psychotherapy, medication, or both.

### For example

Someone finishing a degree compares two directions, weighs what matters to them, and makes a provisional decision. The unease that came with the comparison need not be read as a verdict on the choice or on the person.

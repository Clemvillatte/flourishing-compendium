> **Principle 152** — Align your work with your values (right livelihood, revisited)

AN 5.177 gives the practice its most concrete form. The Buddha tells monks that a lay follower should not engage in five types of business: weapons, human beings, meat, intoxicants, and poison ([AN 5.177, Thanissaro translation](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an05/an05.177.than.html)). The list is specific and prohibitive. It does not describe how to choose among the many livelihoods outside the list, and it says nothing about how meditation or any other practice will feel after a job change.

> "Monks, a lay follower should not engage in five types of business. Which five? Business in weapons, business in human beings, business in meat, business in intoxicants, and business in poison. These are the five types of business that a lay follower should not engage in." (Vanijja Sutta, AN 5.177, Thanissaro translation, [Access to Insight](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an05/an05.177.than.html))

The compendium's application is broader than the sutta's list: treat your livelihood as your largest single practice, and check what the work trains you in. This application is proposed; the sutta does not promise a result from acting on it.

### Plain words

The five businesses the sutta names are easy to check; the daily character of everything else is not. Ask what the job makes you practice: persuasion you would not use on your friends, patience you do not have, indifference you have to summon. If the answer is something you would not defend to yourself, that is the information, whatever the salary says.

### For example

Take someone doing manipulative marketing who also meditates daily. The exercise is to write down what the eight hours train, beside what the hour trains, and read the two lists together.

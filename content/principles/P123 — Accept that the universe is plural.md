> **Principle 123** — Accept that the universe is plural

James questions whether reality must form one completely inclusive whole. His pluralistic alternative allows real connections without requiring everything to be gathered into a single experience; he presents this as a possibility to defend against monism (A Pluralistic Universe, 1909, I and VIII, [text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11984/pg11984.html)).

> "Things are 'with' one another in many ways, but nothing includes everything, or dominates over everything." (James, A Pluralistic Universe, VIII, [text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11984/pg11984.html))

This is a philosophical hypothesis, not a finding that all beliefs are equally sound or that people can never resolve a disagreement. James explicitly leaves open the rival possibility of a closed whole (VIII, [text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11984/pg11984.html)).

### Plain words

The compendium draws a practical lesson: you can look for ways to cooperate without first demanding agreement about everything. Ask which differences matter to the decision at hand and which can remain unresolved. This does not require treating every answer as correct.

### For example

Imagine relatives with different religious views arranging care for an older family member. They might agree on visits, transport and meals while disagreeing about life's ultimate meaning. They still need to examine whether the arrangement is fair and meets the person's wishes; sharing a task does not settle their wider disagreements.

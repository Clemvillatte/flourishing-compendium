> **Principle 6** — Expect flourishing to require external goods

Aristotle is clear: eudaimonia is not fully available to the destitute, the friendless, or the isolated. Some external goods are necessary conditions: resources, friends, political stability, good birth, good looks. Do not pretend that virtue alone suffices. Work for justice and material conditions that make flourishing possible for more than the lucky.

(NE I.8, I.9.)

**Plain words** Aristotle doesn't pretend virtue is enough. A person with no money, no friends, and no security isn't going to flourish just by being good. Some outside things are real conditions. So caring about justice and decent material conditions is part of the job, not a distraction from it.

*For example:* A talented kid who skips meals and works nights to help their family isn't failing at character. The conditions are doing the damage, and fixing them counts as ethical work.

> **Principle 6** — Expect flourishing to require external goods

Aristotle is direct about this: eudaimonia is not fully available to the destitute, the friendless, or the isolated. External goods such as resources, friends, political influence, good birth, even appearance function in part as instruments of noble action, and their absence mars happiness. Virtue alone does not suffice. Working for justice and material conditions is part of the work of making flourishing possible.

(NE I.8, I.9.)

### The instruments

The Book I passage, in the Chase translation:

> "Still it is quite plain that it does require the addition of external goods, as we have said: because without appliances it is impossible, or at all events not easy, to do noble actions: for friends, money, and political influence are in a manner instruments whereby many things are done: some things there are again a deficiency in which mars blessedness; good birth, for instance, or fine offspring, or even personal beauty: for he is not at all capable of Happiness who is very ugly, or is ill-born, or solitary and childless; and still less perhaps supposing him to have very bad children or friends, or to have lost good ones by death." — Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.8, D. P. Chase translation ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm))

Hence the same chapter's conclusion: "the addition of prosperity of this kind does seem necessary to complete the idea of Happiness" (NE I.8, Chase trans.).

### Complete virtue, complete life

The famous case is Priam, prosperous and then ruined:

> "For to constitute Happiness, there must be, as we have said, complete virtue and a complete life: for many changes and chances of all kinds arise during a life, and he who is most prosperous may become involved in great misfortunes in his old age, as in the heroic poems the tale is told of Priam: but the man who has experienced such fortune and died in wretchedness, no man calls happy." — Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.9, Chase trans. (same source)

**Plain words** Virtue is the engine; external goods are the road. No road, and the engine goes nowhere. So the question of what people need to flourish has a material answer: money enough, friends enough, a stable enough place. Supporting those things counts as flourishing work.

*For example:* A talented kid who skips meals and works nights to help their family isn't failing at character. The conditions are doing the damage, and fixing them counts as ethical work.

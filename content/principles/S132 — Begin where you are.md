> **Principle 254** — Begin where you are

In Discourses I.2, Epictetus rejects abandoning effort because one does not expect to reach the highest standard. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10661/pg10661.txt))

> "What then, since I am naturally dull, shall I, for this reason, take no pains? I hope not. Epictetus is not superior to Socrates; but if he is not inferior, this is enough for me; for I shall never be a Milo, and yet I do not neglect my body; nor shall I be a Croesus, and yet I do not neglect my property; nor, in a word, do we neglect looking after anything because we despair of reaching the highest degree."

(Disc. I.2, George Long translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10661/pg10661.txt).)

The compendium applies this argument to beginning a practice with the time and ability presently available.

**Plain words** Imagine refusing to learn to cook because you will never be a great chef. You could still learn to make dinner. When you feel too far behind to begin a practice, ask whether you are making the same demand of yourself.

*For example:* Imagined: an exhausted parent spends two minutes reviewing a difficult exchange with their child and writes down one response to try next time.

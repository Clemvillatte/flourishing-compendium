> **Principle 40** — Recognise the restorative power of awe: vastness expands attention

Awe — the response to vastness that transcends your frame of reference — has a specific effect on attention. It reduces self-focus, broadens attentional scope, and produces a sense of connection. Awe experiences are restorative in a distinct way.

**Plain words** Awe — the vast thing that shrinks you pleasantly — resets attention by loosening the grip. The sky, the ocean, the night sky: they make your to-do list quiet down without a fight. Vastness is a solvent for self-focus.

*For example:* Standing under a sky full of stars, and the inbox thoughts just… stop. Nothing was suppressed; they were outmatched. That's the awe effect, and it's free.
## Psychology parallels

- **Awe broadens attention and reduces self-focus**, awe is associated with a "small self" effect and expanded attention (Piff et al., 2015; Stellar et al., 2018)
- **Awe walks improve well-being**, deliberate awe experiences (nature, art, music) improve mood and reduce depressive symptoms (Anderson et al., 2025; Stellar et al., 2018)

## Practice

- Seek out experiences of vastness: mountains, ocean, night sky, great art, great music. Let yourself feel small in a way that is liberating. Awe is a specific attentional and emotional state with restorative properties.

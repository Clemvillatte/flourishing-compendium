> **Principle 40** — Recognise the restorative power of awe: vastness expands attention

Piff and colleagues define awe as "an emotional response to perceptually vast stimuli that transcend current frames of reference" (Piff et al. 2015, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25984788/)). Across five studies (N = 2,078) they tested whether awe can lead to "a diminishment of the individual self and its concerns". In the final study, people who stood in a grove of towering trees helped more and felt less entitled than a control group, and the effect was explained "in part, by feelings of a small self".

The restorative part has a narrower evidence base. In a randomised trial, 60 older adults took weekly 15-minute walks for eight weeks, half of them oriented to look for awe. The awe group reported more awe and joy on the walks and greater drops in daily distress, but "anxiety, depression, and life satisfaction did not change from baseline in either group" (Sturm et al. 2022, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32955293/)). Both papers measure self-focus, generosity and mood, and neither abstract reports a test of attentional breadth, so the title's claim that vastness "expands attention" is the compendium's reading and not a finding from these papers.

**Plain words**
Awe makes you feel smaller, and in these studies that smaller feeling went with more generosity and, over weeks of practice, less everyday distress. It is a short break from being the centre of your own story. It is not a treatment for depression or anxiety, because the walking trial found no change in either.

*For example:* Take a fifteen-minute walk this week and spend it looking for one thing that is bigger or older than you can quite take in, such as a large tree, a cliff face or a wide sky. This is an imagined routine, modelled on the awe-walk instruction in Sturm's trial, and what it does for you is for you to notice, not a promised result.

## Psychology parallels

- **Awe and the small self**, awe diminishes the sense of the individual self and raises prosocial behaviour (Piff et al., 2015)
- **Awe walks**, a trial in older adults found more daily prosocial positive emotions and less daily distress, with no change in depression, anxiety or life satisfaction (Sturm et al., 2022)

## Practice

- Seek out experiences of vastness: mountains, ocean, night sky, great art, great music. Let yourself feel small in a way that is liberating. Awe is a specific attentional and emotional state with restorative properties.

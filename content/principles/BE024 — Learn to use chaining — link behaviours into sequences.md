> **Principle 24** — Learn to use chaining: link behaviours into sequences

Chaining involves linking multiple behaviours into a sequence (habit chain). Each behaviour becomes the cue for the next. This builds complex behavioural routines.

**Plain words** Chain behaviours so each one triggers the next — kettle on, then journal, then shoes. Sequences reduce the decision count to one: start the chain, and the rest runs itself. Habits are stronger in convoys.

*For example:* "After coffee, I write for 20 minutes" — the coffee isn't separate from the writing anymore; it's the first domino. One decision, three habits.

## Practice

- Chain behaviours: link desired behaviours into sequences. "After I wake up, I will drink water. After I drink water, I will stretch. After I stretch, I will meditate." Each behaviour cues the next.

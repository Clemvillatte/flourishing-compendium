> **Principle 448** — Practise revolt

Revolt against the absurd — not by denying it, but by refusing to surrender to it. Camus's Sisyphus, pushing his boulder up the hill forever, is the image of revolt: the refusal to be defeated by meaninglessness. Practise revolt by living fully, passionately, and authentically in the face of the absurd.

(Camus, 1942/1955, The Myth of Sisyphus, "The Myth of Sisyphus" and "Absurd Creation"; Camus, 1951/1956, The Rebel, Part One.)

**Plain words** Revolt against the absurd — not by denying it but by refusing to surrender to it. Sisyphus pushing the boulder forever is the image: the refusal to be defeated by meaninglessness. Practise revolt by living fully, passionately, and authentically in spite of the silence.

*For example:* The nurse in the underfunded ward who knows the system won't be fixed by her shift — and does the shift anyway, fully, with style. That's revolt: not the denial of the boulder, but the quality of the pushing.

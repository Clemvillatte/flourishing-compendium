> **Principle 161** — Be a good family member

The Stoic does not treat family as a distraction from philosophy. Marcus opens the Meditations with a list of debts to the people who shaped him, and one of the debts is love of family itself:

> "From my brother Severus, to love my kin, and to love truth, and to love justice." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book I, George Long translation)

The debt runs in both directions:

> "From my mother, piety and beneficence, and abstinence, not only from evil deeds, but even from evil thoughts." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, Book I, George Long translation)

A century later Musonius Rufus answered the question head on when a student proposed that a household was a burden to study:

> "Again when someone said that marriage and living with a wife seemed to him a handicap to the pursuit of philosophy, Musonius said that it was no handicap to Pythagoras, nor to Socrates, nor to Crates, each of whom lived with a wife, and one could not mention better philosophers than these." (Musonius Rufus, Lecture XIV)

Musonius's ground is that "marriage, if anything, is manifestly in accord with nature". The philosopher teaches what is appropriate to human nature, and the household is where that teaching gets done. The practice starts with gratitude: name what each family member actually gave you, and the duties stop reading as interruptions to philosophy.

(Med. I.1, I.5, I.17; Disc. II.10, III.7; Seneca, Letters 4, 63, 99, 104, 107; Musonius Rufus, Lectures on marriage and family.)

**Plain words** Family is the first classroom of philosophy and the hardest one. It is easy to be wise in private; the dinner table, the sickbed and the difficult relative are where patience and justice get tested on people you cannot swap for easier ones. Start by naming what each person actually taught you.

*For example:* Imagined: someone whose evening practice is Stoic reading, and whose mother calls every night to worry, takes the call itself as the practice: listening without fixing, keeping the tone even, ending warmly.

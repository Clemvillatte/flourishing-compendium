> **Principle 161** — Be a good family member

The Stoic does not neglect family for philosophy. Epictetus discusses the duties of a parent, a child, a spouse. Marcus writes about his gratitude to his family. Seneca writes letters of consolation to his mother. Treat your family life as a primary site of Stoic practice.

(Med. I.1, I.5, I.17; Disc. II.10, III.7; Seneca, Letters 4, 63, 99, 104, 107; Musonius Rufus, Lectures on marriage and family.)

**Plain words** The Stoic doesn't sacrifice family to philosophy. Epictetus discusses the duties of a parent, child and spouse, Marcus thanks his family, Seneca consoles his mother. Treat family life as a main training ground.

*For example:* Staying calm at the dinner table when your teenager is rude, because this is the practice.

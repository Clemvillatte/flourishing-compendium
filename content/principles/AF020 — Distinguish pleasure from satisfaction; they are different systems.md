> **Principle 20** — Distinguish pleasure from satisfaction; they are different systems

Kahneman and Deaton distinguish two aspects of subjective well-being: emotional well-being, the emotional quality of an individual's everyday experience — the frequency and intensity of experiences of joy, stress, sadness, anger, and affection that make one's life pleasant or unpleasant — and life evaluation, the thoughts people have about their life when they think about it. ([Kahneman & Deaton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1011492107)) A related distinction in reward research: the brain circuitry that mediates wanting a reward is dissociable from the circuitry that mediates how much it is liked. ([Berridge & Robinson, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000059)) The compendium's application is to notice, when a pursuit has stopped being enjoyable, that the pull and the pleasure may be different processes — and, as a proposed activity, to consider something whose satisfaction does not depend on the chase.

> "Emotional well-being refers to the emotional quality of an individual's everyday experience—the frequency and intensity of experiences of joy, stress, sadness, anger, and affection that make one's life pleasant or unpleasant. Life evaluation refers to the thoughts that people have about their life when they think about it." (Kahneman, D., & Deaton, A. (2010). High income improves evaluation of life but not emotional well-being. PNAS, 107(38), 16489-16493, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2944762/))

**Plain words** Two open definitions: everyday emotional experience, and the thoughts people have about their life when they think about it — measured separately in this research ([Kahneman & Deaton, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1011492107)). Separately, wanting and liking can come apart in the brain ([Berridge & Robinson, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000059)). The exercise: when a chase stops being fun, ask which of the two the chase is feeding.

**For example (imagined):** A weekend of good food and screens ends flat. As a proposed activity, the person tries something whose satisfaction does not depend on the chase — a hard hike, a finished shelf — and observes what, if anything, changes.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[The wanting/liking dissociation|Wanting vs liking dissociation]]**, dopamine mediates wanting (pursuit) and opioids mediate liking (consumption); the two can come apart (Berridge & Robinson, 2016)
- **Satisfaction is more stable than pleasure**, evaluative well-being (life satisfaction) has different correlates than experienced well-being (momentary affect) (Kahneman & Deaton, 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia vs hedonia]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **[[The insatiability of desire|The insatiability of desire]]** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **The distinction between lower and higher pleasures** (Mill, Utilitarianism)

## Practice

- When you notice chasing a pleasure that has stopped being enjoyable, this is wanting without liking. Shift to something that will be satisfying even if not immediately pleasurable.

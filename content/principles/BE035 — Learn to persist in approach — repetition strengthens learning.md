> **Principle 35** — Learn to persist in approach: repetition strengthens learning

Exposure therapy's account of why repetition matters is the inhibitory learning model: Craske and colleagues describe extinction as new inhibitory learning alongside an existing fear association. They discuss strengthening that learning and helping it be retrieved later.

> "One powerful way to reduce conditional fear reactions is through extinction, in which the CS is repeatedly presented in the absence of the associated aversive event (the US)." (Craske et al., 2014)

The model's premise: "Bouton and colleagues propose that after extinction, the CS possesses two meanings; its original excitatory meaning (CS-US) as well as an additional inhibitory meaning (CS-noUS)." On this model the two meanings coexist after extinction, and the new one matters only if it can be retrieved.

**Plain words** During planned exposure practice, compare what you expected with what actually happened. You are learning a different prediction about the situation while an older fearful expectation may still be present.

*For example* Imagined: a speaker treats the first successful talk as a fluke; the plan is to keep collecting occasions rather than argue with the fluke judgment.

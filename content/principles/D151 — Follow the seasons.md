> **Principle 151** — Follow the seasons

Huainanzi 5.3 sets out court ordinances for the last month of spring and predicts consequences for following a different season's rules. The quoted rainfall claim belongs to that court calendar. A personal scheduling practice can borrow its attention to changing conditions without treating the calendar's predictions as modern weather forecasts.

(Huainanzi, ch. 5.)

> "If the ordinances for this month are observed, sweet rain will fall during the three ten-day periods of the month." (Huainanzi 5.3, excerpt from [Major, Queen, Meyer and Roth trans.](https://archive.org/stream/theessentialhuainanziliuan/The%20Essential%20Huainanzi%20-%20Liu%20An_djvu.txt))

### Plain words

You can keep a commitment while changing how or when you do the work. Look at the daylight, weather and obligations you are working with now. A schedule deserves another look when the conditions it was made for have changed.

### For example

For an outdoor task, compare two possible times against the forecast, daylight and available shade. Choose the workable option and note what made it suitable. Repeat the check when conditions change. This overt-behaviour practice retains a way of reviewing the schedule, rather than a time that must always be right.

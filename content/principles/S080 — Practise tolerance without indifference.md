> **Principle 202** — Practise tolerance without indifference

> Men exist for the sake of one another. Teach them then or bear with them.

(Marcus Aurelius, Meditations VIII.59, trans. George Long, [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.8.eight.html), page opened; the section number is from the standard numbering and is not printed in this edition.)

The line pairs forbearance with an effort to change the other person, which is close to the entry's pairing of tolerance and action. Marcus also gives the reason for tolerating wrongdoers. In VII.22 he writes "It is peculiar to man to love even those who do wrong," because "they do wrong through ignorance and unintentionally," and "the wrong-doer has done thee no harm, for he has not made thy ruling faculty worse than it was before" ([Meditations VII](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.7.seven.html), opened). Tolerance of persons is thus grounded in how Marcus views the harm done to himself. His texts opened here do not speak to injustice done to third parties or to campaigns for reform, so the entry's "work to change what is within it" is the compendium's extension.

William James offers a limit from outside Stoicism. In "On a Certain Blindness" he writes: "The first thing to learn in intercourse with others is non-interference with their own peculiar ways of being happy, provided those ways do not assume to interfere by violence with ours" ([Talks to Teachers, Project Gutenberg #16287](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16287/pg16287.txt), opened). The label "Tolerance without indifference" is the compendium's, not James's.

The Tyler (2006) and Hayes et al. (2011) references and Bernstein were not opened. A paper by Tyler on legitimacy exists in the Annual Review of Psychology, but its abstract does not mention tolerance, so it was not used here.

**Plain words** Marcus says people exist for one another, so teach them or put up with them. He grounds patience with wrongdoers in the idea that they act from ignorance and cannot damage his own character. Acting against injustice to others is a reasonable extension that the passages opened do not state.

*For example (imagined):* A person accepts that their father will not change, and still joins a campaign to improve the care home.

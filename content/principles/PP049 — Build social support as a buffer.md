> **Principle 49** — Build social support as a buffer

Social support, the perception that one has people to turn to, buffers the impact of stress on health. The mechanism is not that support eliminates stress but that it reduces the physiological and psychological impact of stressors. Support that is perceived as available is more important than support that is actually received.

(Cohen and Wills, 1985; Uchino, 2006.)

**Plain words** Support buffers stress not by removing the problem but by reducing its impact. What matters most is believing you have people to turn to. Lone coping is a health risk on the level of known physical risks.

*For example:* Facing a diagnosis feels different when you know exactly who you'd call first.

> **Principle 78** — Build self-maintenance: ongoing practice and adaptation

Aristotle grounds the ongoing work in how character is built at all:

> For the things we have to learn before we can do them, we learn by doing them, e.g. men become builders by building and lyreplayers by playing the lyre; so too we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts.

(Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1, 1103a-b, Ross translation; [MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html))

Aristotle uses learning a craft to explain acquiring moral virtue through action. Applied here, that account suggests examining the actions through which you practise a quality you want to develop. Adjusting a routine when circumstances change is a proposed application, rather than a conclusion established by this passage.

**Plain words**
Choose a quality you want to practise. Identify an action that expresses it and a workable opportunity to repeat that action. If the routine no longer fits your circumstances, consider changing the routine.

*For example:* Imagine a parent considering a shorter version of a former morning routine.

## Sources

## Psychology parallels

- **Self-development requires ongoing practice**, like physical fitness, selfhood requires maintenance (Ericsson et al., 1993; McAdams, 2001)
- **Identity and life transitions**, life transitions require ongoing identity work (Ibarra, 2003; McAdams & McLean, 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] as ongoing practice** (Socrates, Apology 38a)
- **Becoming as ongoing** (Nietzsche, Thus Spoke Zarathustra)
- **[[Bhavana (cultivation)|Bhavana (ongoing cultivation)]]** (Buddhism)

## Practice

- Build a self-maintenance routine: regular self-reflection, values review, feedback seeking, and adjustment as circumstances change. Retain what works; discard what does not.

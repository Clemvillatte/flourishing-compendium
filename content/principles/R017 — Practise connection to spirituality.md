> **Principle 279** — Practise connection to spirituality

The original entry's source is the social and emotional wellbeing (SEWB) framework in Working Together. The chapter itself was written by Gee, Dudgeon, Schultz, Hart and Kelly (2014, pp. 55-68), and the editors of the book are Dudgeon, Milroy and Walker ([Bond University record](https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-social-and-emotional-wellbe/)). It defines SEWB as "a multidimensional concept of health that includes mental health, but which also encompasses domains of health and wellbeing such as connection to land or 'country', culture, spirituality, ancestry, family, and community" (same record). One of the framework's domains is connection to spirit, spirituality and ancestors ([Kilcullen et al. 2017, JCU](https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/49387/16/JCU_49387_Kilcullen%20et%20al_2017_accepted%20version.pdf)).

A Queensland Health guide describing the domain says that "stories, rituals, ceremonies and cultural knowledge connect person, land and place" ([Queensland Health SEWB guide](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/1018136/SEWB-card-guide.pdf)). In that study, the most commonly reported conceptualisation of spirituality was "interconnectedness and reciprocity of all things, including connection to spirit and ancestors, and to cultural knowledge" (Kilcullen et al. 2017).

(Aboriginal SEWB framework; Dudgeon et al., 2014, Working Together.)

### Scope

The Working Together chapter text could not be opened, so the original entry's statements about the Dreaming being "a living reality" cannot be confirmed. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are many, with different traditions, so this entry describes a framework, not one belief. Anyone applying it should follow guidance from the relevant community.

**Plain words**
The framework draws wellbeing as a set of ties: to country, family, community, culture, ancestry and spirit. People in the study most often named spirit as the interconnectedness of everything. Honest limits: the chapter could not be opened, and one framework stands for many nations, so practice comes from your own community.

**For example**
If you belong to an Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander community, ask an Elder or a community health service about the stories, ceremony or knowledge you can take part in.

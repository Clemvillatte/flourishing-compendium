> **Principle 41** — Cultivate transcendence

In the VIA classification, transcendence is the strengths that forge connections to the larger universe and provide meaning: appreciation of beauty and excellence, [[Gratitude|gratitude]], [[Hope|hope]], humor and spirituality ([Peterson and Seligman, 2004, Table 1 as reprinted in Ruch and Proyer, 2015](https://www.viacharacter.org/pdf/MAPPING%20STRENGTHS%20INTO%20VIRTUES.pdf)).

> "Distinguishing between more and less relevant positive emotions in a religious/spiritual context, it was found that the effect was mediated by self-transcendent positive emotions (awe, gratitude, love, and peace) but not by other positive emotions (amusement and pride)." (Van Cappellen et al., 2016, [abstract](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/05d717de-4f1e-4476-ac20-6b5841527af9))

Van Cappellen's review defines the family these studies draw on: self-transcendent positive emotions are "short, positive responses to witnessing instances of beauty or good outside the self" ([Van Cappellen, 2017](https://sites.duke.edu/bablab/files/2018/01/Van-Cappellen-2017-PRS-Special-Issue.pdf)). The two studies behind the parallel were cross-sectional, in church and meditation samples, so they support a link rather than a training effect.

(Peterson and Seligman, 2004; Van Cappellen et al., 2016.)

**Plain words** Each strength in this family points attention off yourself: gratitude at what you were given, hope at what could still happen, humor at the gap between how things are and how they were supposed to go, beauty at something made well. The connected feeling is the point, not the religion. The studies behind the parallel found the well-being link ran through awe, gratitude, love and peace, not through amusement or pride.

*For example:* Standing in front of a painting for ten minutes and losing track of yourself.

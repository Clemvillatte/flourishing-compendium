> **Principle 41** — Cultivate transcendence

The VIA classification groups five strengths under transcendence — appreciation of beauty and excellence, [[Gratitude|gratitude]], [[Hope|hope]], humor and spirituality — as "strengths that forge connections to the larger universe and provide meaning" ([Peterson and Seligman, 2004, Table 1 as reprinted in Ruch and Proyer, 2015](https://www.viacharacter.org/pdf/MAPPING%20STRENGTHS%20INTO%20VIRTUES.pdf)).

Van Cappellen and colleagues asked whether positive emotions explain why religion and spirituality go with well-being. Their two cross-sectional studies, one with churchgoers in a European country and one with US university employees interested in meditation, found the link ran through some emotions and not others:

> "Distinguishing between more and less relevant positive emotions in a religious/spiritual context, it was found that the effect was mediated by self-transcendent positive emotions (awe, gratitude, love, and peace) but not by other positive emotions (amusement and pride)." (Van Cappellen et al., 2016, [author-hosted full text](https://sites.duke.edu/bablab/files/2021/01/8_VanCappellen2016_Article_ReligionAndWell-Being.pdf))

The review that frames these emotions defines them as "short, positive responses to witnessing instances of beauty or good outside the self" ([Van Cappellen, 2017](https://sites.duke.edu/bablab/files/2018/01/Van-Cappellen-2017-PRS-Special-Issue.pdf)). Both studies were correlational, so they support an association rather than a training effect.

(Peterson and Seligman, 2004; Van Cappellen et al., 2016.)

**Plain words** Think of a friend who helped you recently. What did they actually do, and what are you thankful for? That is a practical starting point for gratitude, one strength grouped here. Appreciation of beauty offers another: noticing something well made or skilfully performed.

*For example:* Imagine stopping at a painting long enough to name one detail the painter got right.

> **Principle 425** — Practise avoiding bad faith (mauvaise foi)

Recognise bad faith — the self-deception in which you deny either your freedom (by claiming to be determined) or your facticity (by pretending to be pure freedom). The waiter who plays at being a waiter, the person who says "I had no choice," the one who lives as though they were a thing — these are all in bad faith. Notice when you are in bad faith and return to authentic existence.

(Sartre, 1943/2003, Being and Nothingness, Part I, ch. 2; Sartre, 1946/2007, Existentialism Is a Humanism.)

**Plain words** Bad faith (mauvaise foi): the self-deception in which you deny either your freedom (claiming to be determined) or your facticity (pretending to be pure freedom). The waiter playing at being a waiter, the "I had no choice" — both deny one pole. The practice is refusing both denials.

*For example:* The "I'm just not a math person" that has closed doors for twenty years — bad faith on the freedom side — and the "I can become anyone by Friday" that ignores the facticity side. The honest position is harder: given the real constraints, the real range of moves.

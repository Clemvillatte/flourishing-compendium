> **Principle 3** — Act from unity; reflect from unity

Nishida Kitarō's An Inquiry into the Good starts from a claim about experience, and the self comes later than the claim:

> "When one directly experiences one's own state of consciousness, there is not yet a subject or an object, and knowing and its object are completely unified." (Nishida, An Inquiry into the Good, trans. Abe & Ives, pp. 3-4; quotation reproduced in [Gelūnas 2001, Acta Orientalia Vilnensia](https://www.journals.vu.lt/acta-orientalia-vilnensia/article/view/18347))

The looker and the looked-at are carved out of a working whole, and Nishida says the same of willing. Reason and will "share the same foundation. The unifying activity of the will functions at the base of all reason and laws" (p. 28), and "thinking, imagination, and the will are fundamentally identical unifying activities" (p. 91). He states the norm directly: "Fundamentally, truth is singular. Intellectual truth and practical truth must be one and the same. Those who think deeply or are genuinely serious inevitably seek congruence between knowledge and the practical realm of feeling and willing" (p. 37; quotations from the will chapters reproduced in [Rogers 2019, University of Mississippi thesis](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1751&context=etd)). The will is also where the self shows itself: "The deepest power unifying this whole is our so-called self, and the will is that which most completely expresses this power" (p. 125).

**Plain words**
Nishida's starting point: in immediate experience there is not yet a gap between knower and known. The split into a watcher and a watched is something we perform on that material afterwards. Application: when you want to know who you are, treat the engaged moment, where knowing, imagining and willing are not yet separate functions, as a primary source, and the evaluation that follows as the derived one.

*For example:* A good cook mid-service doesn't feel like she is deciding anything. If she wants to know who she is, that moment is a primary source; the self that evaluates dinner afterwards is the derived one.

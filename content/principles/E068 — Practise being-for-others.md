> **Principle 460** — Practise being-for-others

Sartre's analysis of the Look begins with shame:

> "Shame is by nature recognition. I recognize that I am as the Other sees me." (Sartre, Being and Nothingness, [Barnes's translation](http://ahistoryofthepresentananthology.blogspot.com/2013/04/being-and-nothingness-by-jean-paul.html))

His scene is a man absorbed at a keyhole; footsteps arrive, and he suddenly exists as the object of a judgment he did not author: "I am ashamed of myself as I appear to the Other" ([ibid.](http://ahistoryofthepresentananthology.blogspot.com/2013/04/being-and-nothingness-by-jean-paul.html)). The Look makes you a thing in someone else's world, a real dimension of your being rather than a delusion to be overcome. The practical upshot is in the same passage: "Thus shame is shame of oneself before the Other; these two structures are inseparable. But at the same time I need the Other in order to realize fully all the structures of my being" ([ibid.](http://ahistoryofthepresentananthology.blogspot.com/2013/04/being-and-nothingness-by-jean-paul.html)). Denying the dimension is itself a project, usually built out of caring. Cooley's sociology gives the dimension its mechanics, the [[The looking-glass self|looking-glass self]]:

> "A social self of this sort might be called the reflected or looking-glass self: / 'Each to each a looking-glass / Reflects the other that doth pass.'" (Cooley, Human Nature and the Social Order, ch. 5, [Mead Project edition](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Cooley/Cooley_1902/Cooley_1902f.html))

Its three elements: the imagined appearance to the other person, the imagined judgment of it, and a self-feeling, pride or mortification. Cooley's correction matters for practice: "The thing that moves us to pride or shame is not the mere mechanical reflection of ourselves, but an imputed sentiment, the imagined effect of this reflection upon another's mind" ([ibid.](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Cooley/Cooley_1902/Cooley_1902f.html)). The glass runs on imagined judgments; some of yours are real information, and some are your own voice wearing someone else's face. Audit which is which.

**Plain words**
You have an outside. Other people can see you, and what they see is a real part of you, not the whole of you, not just surface. You cannot delete it and you cannot fully control it; you can only own it. Most of what feels like their judgment is your guess about their mind; check the guess against the person. The one who "doesn't care what anyone thinks" usually cares so much they have organized their life around never finding out.

*For example:* The person who insists "I don't care what anyone thinks" — and orders their whole life around the avoidance of judgment, which is caring what anyone thinks with extra steps. The dimension is structural; the only question is whether it's owned.

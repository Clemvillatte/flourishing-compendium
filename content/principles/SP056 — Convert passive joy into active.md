> **Principle 56** — Convert passive joy into active

The distinction is stated in Part III's definitions and first proposition: we act when something takes place, within us or externally, "whereof we are the adequate cause"; we are passive when "we being only the partial cause"; and the mind, "in so far as it has adequate ideas it is necessarily active, and in so far as it has inadequate ideas, it is necessarily passive" (III, Def. ii; III, P1). So a joy is active or passive by the ideas it springs from, not by its object. V, P4's note gives the conversion:

> "all appetites or desires are only passions, in so far as they spring from inadequate ideas; the same results are accredited to virtue, when they are aroused or generated by adequate ideas." (Ethics V, P4 note, [Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

The note's own example: the desire that others live after one's own fashion is "ambition" in a man not guided by reason, and "piety", an activity or virtue, in a man who lives by the dictates of reason. One line of stability belongs to the reason-based side: "an emotion which springs from reason is necessarily referred to the common properties of things... which we always regard as present... Wherefore an emotion of this kind always remains the same" (V, P7 proof). The page's exercise drawn from the account: for a joy that arrived with inadequate ideas attached, examine those ideas; the desire itself can stay, and become an activity.

**Plain words**
The test of an active joy is not what it is about; it is whether your mind's own ideas are enough to produce it. A joy that runs through ideas you only partially have, borrowed from the moment, is a passion. A joy that runs through ideas that are yours, clear and complete, is an activity. Spinoza's claim is that the same desire can run either way: wanting others to live your way is ambition while your idea of it is confused, and becomes an activity when the idea is adequate.

*For example:* Imagined: two people practise the same piece of music. One plays for the praise afterwards, which she cannot produce alone. The other plays out of what she understands in the music itself. The second can enjoy it alone on a Tuesday, because the ideas producing the joy are her own.

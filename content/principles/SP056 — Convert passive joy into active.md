> **Principle 56** — Convert passive joy into active

Part III's distinction comes first: we act when we are the adequate cause of what happens in us; we are acted on when the cause is outside. Joy can come either way. V, P4 and its note give the conversion program. The corollary: "there is no emotion, whereof we cannot form some clear and distinct conception". The note's method: "everyone has the power of clearly and distinctly understanding himself and his emotions, if not absolutely, at any rate in part, and consequently of bringing it about, that he should become less subject to them". And the key line for conversion, the same appetite running either way:

> "all appetites or desires are only passions, in so far as they spring from inadequate ideas; the same results are accredited to virtue, when they are aroused or generated by adequate ideas." (Ethics V, P4 note, [Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

The note's own example: the desire that others live after one's own fashion is "ambition" in a man not guided by reason, and "piety", an activity or virtue, in a man who lives by the dictates of reason. Joy from understanding is also more stable: an emotion which springs from reason is referred to the common properties of things, which "we always regard as present", so "an emotion of this kind always remains the same" (V, P7). The page's exercise drawn from the program: for a joy that arrived from outside, ask what understanding would produce the same joy in you, and build that.

**Plain words**
Joy has two sources. One is outside: the promotion, the praise, the luck; it lasts as long as its cause does. The other is inside the work itself: understanding something, being competent at something, making something. Spinoza's claim is that the same desire can run either way: ambition if it springs from confused ideas, piety if from clear ones. The conversion is not renouncing the desire; it is re-sourcing it.

*For example:* Imagined: someone who enjoys music only when praised for it, and someone who practises it. Same desire for music; one of them can play on a Tuesday when nobody is watching.

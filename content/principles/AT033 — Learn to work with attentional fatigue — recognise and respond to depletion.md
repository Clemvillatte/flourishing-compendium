> **Principle 33** — Learn to work with attentional fatigue: recognise and respond to depletion

Attentional fatigue is real. When concentration degrades, you become more distractible, more error-prone, and less effective. Recognising the signs and responding with rest is more effective than pushing through.

**Plain words** Attentional fatigue is a real state, not a character verdict: more distractible, more error-prone, more irritable. The treatment isn't another coffee — it's a real break, or the end of the day. Learn your own depletion signals.

*For example:* Re-reading the same line for the third time and blaming the material — the material is fine. The tank is empty. That's not a book problem.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional fatigue and directed attention**, prolonged effortful attention depletes directed attention capacity (Kaplan, 1995; Kaplan & Berman, 2010)
- **Vigilance decrement**, performance declines over sustained attention (Mackworth, 1948; See et al., 1995)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Rest as part of work** (Daoism, Daodejing 16)
- **The rhythm of effort and recovery** (Taoism, Neiye)
- **[[Nothing in excess|Nothing in excess]]** (Delphic maxim)

## Practice

- Learn your attentional fatigue signals: increased distraction, error, boredom, restlessness. When you notice these, stop. Take a genuine break (not a "break" of scrolling). Return when capacity is restored.

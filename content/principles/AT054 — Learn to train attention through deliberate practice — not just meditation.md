> **Principle 54** — Learn to train attention through deliberate practice: not just meditation

Attention can be trained through any activity that requires sustained focus with feedback: reading difficult material, playing an instrument, learning a language, solving problems. Deliberate practice with feedback improves attentional capacity.

**Plain words** Meditation doesn't own attention training. Any activity with sustained focus and clear feedback — an instrument, a climb, a difficult read — trains the same machinery. The honest question is whether your hobbies are training focus or training flinch.

*For example:* An hour of real piano practice — error, correction, error, correction — builds more attention than an hour of "being mindful" while scrolling. Feedback is the active ingredient.
## Psychology parallels

- **Deliberate practice builds attentional capacity**, sustained engagement with demanding tasks improves concentration (Ericsson et al., 1993; Newport, 2016)
- **Note: "brain training" games show limited far transfer**, working-memory training improves trained tasks but does not broadly improve attention (Melby-Lervåg & Hulme, 2013; Simons et al., 2016)

## Practice

- Train attention through meaningful activities: read challenging books, learn an instrument, practise a craft, solve problems. Choose activities that require sustained focus and provide feedback.

> **Principle 84** — Act in the situation, not from the rulebook

Aristotle's account of equity is the classic statement of the rule's built-in blind spot. All law is universal, but some cases are not covered by the universal statement; when the legislator "fails us and has erred by over-simplicity", it is right "to correct the omission—to say what the legislator himself would have said had he been present, and would have put into his law if he had known" ([Nicomachean Ethics V.10, Ross](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicomachean_Ethics_(Ross)/Book_Five)).

> The reason is that all law is universal but about some things it is not possible to make a universal statement which shall be correct.
>
> (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics V.10, 1137b, Ross)

The correction is not lawlessness: the equitable is "a correction of law where it is defective owing to its universality". Dewey offers a related account of principles. "Principles are methods of inquiry and forecast which require verification by the event", and situations that change and surprise are a challenge to intelligence "to create new principles" ([Human Nature and Conduct, 1922](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/41386/41386-h/41386-h.htm), p. 239).

The compendium's rule follows: meet the situation first, consult the rulebook second. The rule is a compressed record of how situations usually go, and the situation in front of you is the only place to check whether this is one of them.

**Plain words**
A rule is a summary of past situations, not a substitute for looking at this one. When a rule and a real case collide, the rule is not automatically wrong; it is silent where the case is unusual, and Aristotle's equity names exactly that gap. The compendium's exercise: for one decision currently being made by rule, write down what the actual situation would say if asked directly.

*For example:*
As a compendium analogy, consider a no-exceptions rule against lying, and the case of hiding someone from people who mean them harm. Aristotle's test asks what the rule's legislator would have put into the rule knowing this case; the correction follows what the rule was for, not what it literally says.

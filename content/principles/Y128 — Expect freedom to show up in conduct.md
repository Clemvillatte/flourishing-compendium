> **Principle 128** — Expect freedom to show up in conduct

The Gita raises the question of how freedom looks from outside. In 2.54 Arjuna asks:

> O Keshav, what is the disposition of one who is situated in divine consciousness? How does an enlightened person talk? How does he sit? How does he walk?

(Bhagavad Gita 2.54, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/54/).)

Krishna's answer is a description of conduct and temperament. Verse 2.56 says: "One whose mind remains undisturbed amidst misery, who does not crave for pleasure, and who is free from attachment, fear, and anger, is called a sage of steady wisdom" ([BG 2.56](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/56/)). Verse 2.57 adds that such a person is "neither delighted by good fortune nor dejected by tribulation" ([BG 2.57](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/57/)). In 12.15 the dear devotee is one "not a source of annoyance to anyone" and "not agitated by anyone" ([BG 12.15](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/12/verse/15/)).

Most of what these verses name is inner steadiness (fear, anger, craving) and levelness under gain and loss, with harmlessness toward others as the outward sign. The Gita does not use the later term jivanmukti, for liberation while living. Vidyaranya and the Yoga Vasistha have not been checked for this page, so claims about them should wait for a source.

Treating the Gita's description as a checklist for auditing your own month is this page's own use of it.

**Plain words**

The Gita answers the question "what does a free person look like?" with ordinary signs: steady under pressure, not craving, not frightening or irritating others. It says nothing about special experiences. So the check on any claim of progress is what you did the last time something went wrong.

**For example**

A man tells himself he has stopped caring about status. Looking at last month, he notices he sulked for two days when a colleague was promoted. He keeps the claim but marks it unfinished.

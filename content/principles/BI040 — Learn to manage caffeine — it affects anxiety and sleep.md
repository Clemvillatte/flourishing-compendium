> **Principle 40** — Learn to manage caffeine: it affects anxiety and sleep

Anxiety: a meta-analysis of caffeine challenge studies in panic disorder found that 51.1% of 237 patients had a panic attack after caffeine but none after placebo. The patient group was far more vulnerable than healthy controls (53.9% against 1.7%), and the doses were 400 to 750 mg, "roughly equivalent to 5 cups of coffee" (Klevebrant & Frick 2022, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34871964/)). The authors note that "little is known regarding smaller doses than 480 mg". A later crossover trial of 150 mg found that "low caffeine doses do not have differential anxiogenic effects in patients with PD and HC at rest", although it did raise arousal and avoidance behaviour in both groups (Hoppe et al. 2025, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40577029/)).

Sleep: caffeine taken 6 hours before bedtime still disrupted sleep in one trial (Drake et al. 2013, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24235903/)), and a meta-analysis estimated that coffee should be consumed at least 8.8 hours before bedtime to avoid lost sleep time (Gardiner et al. 2023, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36870101/)).

**Plain words**
Caffeine affects both anxiety and sleep, though the strength of the evidence differs. Large doses, around five cups of coffee, reliably set off panic attacks in people with panic disorder. At 150 mg the one trial found no extra anxiety. Timing is the more reliable lever for sleep: stop several hours before bed. If you are prone to anxiety, a lower dose and an earlier cut-off are low-cost experiments.

*For example:* For one week you keep to two small coffees before noon and note your sleep and any jittery moments each morning. This is an imagined experiment, and a clinician should be consulted if you suspect panic disorder.

## Psychology parallels

- **Caffeine and anxiety**, caffeine at about 400 to 750 mg induced panic attacks in about half of patients with panic disorder (Klevebrant & Frick, 2022), while 150 mg did not raise subjective anxiety (Hoppe et al., 2025)
- **Caffeine and sleep**, caffeine 6 hours before bed disrupted sleep (Drake et al., 2013); pooled across 24 studies it cut total sleep time by 45 minutes (Gardiner et al., 2023)

## Practice

- Manage caffeine: limit to 1-2 cups per day, none after noon. If prone to anxiety or insomnia, reduce or eliminate. Notice the effect on your body and mood.

> **Principle 11** — See the ego's groundlessness as its nature, not its failure

The ego, on this analysis, is a nullity at its base: a construction that borrows its being from what it reflects, as a shadow borrows its shape. This is stated not as an insult but as a fact with a consequence: what is constructed cannot be defended as a fortress, and the energy spent defending it is spent defending nothing. Practically, when you feel the reflex of self-defense rise, ask what exactly is being defended, and notice that the question itself loosens the reflex.

(Nishitani, Religion and Nothingness; Nishida on the place of the self.)

**Plain words** The ego has no solid base. It borrows its shape from what it reflects, like a shadow. That isn't an insult, it's a fact with a consequence: you can't defend a fortress that isn't there, and all the energy spent defending it is spent on nothing.

*For example:* Someone corrects you in public and your whole body tightens. Asking "what exactly am I protecting?" makes the tightness a bit silly and a lot easier to drop.

> **Principle 3** — Use the self-culture link, not a fixed self

Treat the self not as a fixed essence but as a cultural form. Hazel Markus and Shinobu Kitayama (1991) distinguished the independent self, dominant in European-American contexts, from the interdependent self, dominant in East Asian, Latin American, African, and many Indigenous contexts. Neither is the self per se; each is a cultural achievement, and each carries its own forms of flourishing and suffering. Treat your own self-construal as a variable, not a constant.

**Plain words** The self is a cultural shape, not a fixed crystal — some cultures build independent selves, others interdependent ones. Yours isn't the default; it's one of the builds. Knowing this makes your own reactions negotiable instead of obvious.

*For example:* The meeting where one person says "I just think..." and another says "We've been thinking..." — both are just selves, built by different cultures. Neither is lying about what a self is.

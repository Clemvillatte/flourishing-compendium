> **Principle 64** — Trust nothing more useful than other people

Nothing is more useful to a human being than human beings, Spinoza concludes, and the reason is not sentiment but structure: only among others can the rational person find the advantages, protection, and shared understanding that individual power alone cannot supply. The misanthrope and the solitary are not free; they are poor. Practically, invest in your web of people as your primary infrastructure, and treat conflict as damage to infrastructure rather than as entertainment.

(Ethics IV, P35 corollary; P35 scholium.)

**Plain words** Nothing is more useful to a human being than human beings — not sentiment but structure: only among others can the rational person find the advantages, protection, and shared understanding that individual power can't supply. Trust nothing more useful than other people; invest accordingly.

*For example:* The self-sufficient homesteader and the networked townsperson face the same crisis — one has stockpiles, the other has a community. Spinoza's bet is on the community, and every disaster study ever conducted has agreed with him.

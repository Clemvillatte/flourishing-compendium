> **Principle 125** — Use the limbs as a diagnostic grid

Sutra 2.28 states why the sequence of eight aids matters: "After the aids to yoga have been followed up, when the impurity has dwindled, there is an enlightenment of perception reaching up to the discriminative discernment" (trans. Woods, p. 172). The commentary adds that "in proportion as the means [of attaining discriminative discernment are followed up], so the impurity is reduced to a state of attenuation" (p. 172). The aids are then listed in order in 2.29, from abstentions and observances through postures, breath, withdrawal of the senses, fixed attention and contemplation to concentration (p. 177).

### The grid and the list

The principle's downward audit is a way of reading that order backward. The text itself gives the order as a sequence of means, and says "The following up of these must be performed in succession" (p. 177). It does not tell the practitioner to check a lower step when a higher one fails. The lists inside the grid differ from Woods: his abstentions in 2.30 are "injury and from falsehood and from theft and from incontinence and from acceptance of gifts", and his observances in 2.32 are "cleanliness and contentment and self-castigation and study and devotion to the Isvara" (pp. 178, 181). Moderation is not one of the five observances in Woods, and "energy" and "grasping" are other translators' renderings, not Woods'.

### Status of the claim

The principle already calls its claim an editorial rule of thumb, not an empirical finding. That label stands. Nothing I opened supports the claim that many stalls resolve at the level of ethics or the body.

**Plain words** Patanjali lists the aids in a fixed order and says the practice should follow it in turn. Reading that order as a troubleshooting grid, starting at the bottom when something fails, is a modern idea. It may be useful, but it is the principle's, and its sample lists do not match Woods.

*For example:* Imagined example: when your sitting goes badly, you ask first whether you have been sleeping and eating well, then whether something in your conduct is unresolved. This is a way of using the order, not something the text says.

(Yoga Sutras 2.28-2.30 and 2.32 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's gloss, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 172, 177-178, 181, Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21.)

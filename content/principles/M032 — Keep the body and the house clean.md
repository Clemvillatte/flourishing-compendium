> **Principle 32** — Keep the body and the house clean

The huehuetlatolli's constant counsel: bathe, wash, keep the garments and the house clean, sweep, order the dwelling. The counsel is hygienic, ceremonial, and psychological at once; the tradition makes no sharp distinction among these. The modern finding on the psychological half is real: environmental order steadies attention and conduct. Practically, keep the elders' rule for one month: the body washed, the garments cared for, the dwelling swept, as a daily practice rather than a weekly crisis.

(Florentine Codex, Book VI, the cleanliness counsels; the midday bath tradition.)

**Plain words** Bathe, wash, keep the garments and house clean, sweep, order the dwelling. The counsel is hygienic, ceremonial, and psychological at once — the tradition makes no sharp distinction. Modern research agrees on the psychological half: environmental order steadies attention and conduct. Keep the elders' rule.

*For example:* Someone in a bad month who can't fix the job, the relationship, or the news — and cleans the kitchen. It's not a metaphor. The counter in the room steadies the head in the skull.
## Psychology parallels

- **Environmental order and self-regulation**, the findings that clutter and disorder impair executive function and that tidying interventions improve mood (the environmental-psychology literatures, as framework)

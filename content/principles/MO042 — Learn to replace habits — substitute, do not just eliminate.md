> **Principle 42** — Learn to replace habits: substitute, do not just eliminate

Research on breaking unwanted habits treats substitution as the mechanism, not a convenience. A habit lives in the association between a cue and a response, so refusing the response leaves the cue and its pull in place. As Lally and Gardner put it in their review of habit breaking, "targeting habit associations requires the displacing of an old habit association with a new one (i.e., habit substitution)".

> "Habit substitution requires the concurrent degrading of an old habit and formation of a new habit; the unwanted existing response to a habit cue must be inhibited so that the new, desired response may be enacted." (Lally & Gardner, "Breaking habits or breaking habitual behaviours?", accepted manuscript)

The substitute response is repeated at the old cue, and the two associations compete for control of it: "As the new association strengthens, the new action (eating an apple) acquires dominance over the old (eating chocolate), such that encountering the cue habitually triggers the newer, wanted behaviour." One requirement is strict. A review of habit-based interventions notes that if several different alternatives are performed instead of one fixed substitute, "no new habit will form, so the snacking habit will not decay" (Gardner et al., 2023).

**Plain words** A habit is a cue with a job attached. Take the job away and leave it at that, and the cue keeps knocking while you spend your day saying no. Substitution gives the cue a new job: same kitchen, same three in the afternoon, an apple instead of the biscuit tin. The substitute has to be the same every time; rotate three different replacements and the new association never gets strong enough to take over.

*For example (imagined):* A smoker who lights up on work breaks takes a short walk at break times instead. The break still happens, the walk is repeated in the same slot, and the break gradually comes to cue the walk.

## Practice

- Replace habits: instead of "stop snacking," replace with "eat an apple." Instead of "stop scrolling," replace with "read a page." Substitute, do not just eliminate. Name the cue that triggers the unwanted habit, choose one fixed substitute that can happen in that same context, and repeat the substitute at the cue every time it fires.

> **Principle 25** — Build concentration capacity through practice: attention is like a muscle

A randomized trial of intensive training states the problem:

> The ability to focus one's attention underlies success in many everyday tasks, but voluntary attention cannot be sustained for extended periods of time.
>
> (MacLean et al., "Intensive meditation training improves perceptual discrimination and sustained attention", Psychological Science, 2010, abstract — [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/))

The paper describes the measured decline: sustained-attention failure appears as "a decline in perceptual sensitivity with increasing time on task, known as the vigilance decrement" ([MacLean 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/)). Its training was roughly five hours a day for three months of "meditation practice that involved sustained selective attention on a chosen stimulus" ([MacLean 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/)). The abstract reports: "Training produced improvements in visual discrimination that were linked to increases in perceptual sensitivity and improved vigilance during sustained visual attention" ([MacLean 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/)).

Mrazek and colleagues tested a shorter programme with different outcome measures, randomly assigning undergraduates to a two-week mindfulness class or a nutrition class ([Mrazek 2013](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/psychology/Research/ResearchArticles/Mrazek%20et%20al_%20(2013).pdf)). The class met "45 min four times a week for 2 weeks" and required "10 min of daily meditation outside of class", with exercises "requiring focused attention to some aspect of sensory experience", ([Mrazek 2013](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/psychology/Research/ResearchArticles/Mrazek%20et%20al_%20(2013).pdf)). The result: "Mindfulness training improved both GRE reading-comprehension scores and working memory capacity while simultaneously reducing the occurrence of distracting thoughts during completion of the GRE and the measure of working memory" ([Mrazek 2013](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/psychology/Research/ResearchArticles/Mrazek%20et%20al_%20(2013).pdf)).

The studies reported improvements on their particular measures after meditation training ([MacLean 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/); [Mrazek 2013](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/psychology/Research/ResearchArticles/Mrazek%20et%20al_%20(2013).pdf)). Mrazek's results concern the full course, including classes and daily practice, rather than ten minutes of meditation alone ([Mrazek 2013](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/psychology/Research/ResearchArticles/Mrazek%20et%20al_%20(2013).pdf)).

(Psychological Science, 2010 and 2013.)

**Plain words**
These studies tested specific training programmes, not whether any short daily routine will improve concentration ([MacLean 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3132583/); [Mrazek 2013](https://apps.weber.edu/wsuimages/psychology/Research/ResearchArticles/Mrazek%20et%20al_%20(2013).pdf)). For your own practice, choose a manageable exercise, notice when attention wanders, and return to what you were doing.

*For example:*
Imagined: Before reading, someone chooses a short section to work through. When attention wanders, they return to the paragraph. Afterwards, they note the interruptions and decide how to arrange the next session.

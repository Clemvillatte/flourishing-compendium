> **Principle 51** — Live by a rhythm larger than your moods

In the highlands of Guatemala the 260-day count is still kept by daykeepers. The University Press of Colorado's introduction to K'iche' divinatory calendars says they "continue to calculate the days and interpret their qualities in order to reveal answers about mental and physical dispositions, the causes of evil or success and failure of events, and consequently the best day for undertaking such essential activities as planting and harvesting or marriage" ([Maya Daykeeping excerpt](https://upcolorado.com/excerpts/9780870819969.pdf)). The same excerpt notes that Barbara Tedlock's Time and the Highland Maya (1982, 1992) was based on her training as a K'iche' calendar diviner in Momostenango. It is a divinatory system, so the days are read for meaning and not only used as a schedule.

The modern research on calendars is about landmarks, not sacred counts. Across three archival studies, Google searches for "diet", gym visits and goal commitments "all increase following temporal landmarks (e.g., the outset of a new week, month, year, or semester; a birthday; a holiday)" (Dai et al. 2014, Management Science 60(10):2563-2582, [abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)). Five laboratory experiments found "causal evidence that emphasizing a temporal landmark denoting the beginning of a new time period increases people's intentions to initiate goal pursuit" (Dai et al. 2015, Psychol Sci 26(12):1927-1936, [PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Dai-Milkman-Riis_2015_TemporalLandmarks_APS_0956797615605818.pdf)). A later paper found that an upcoming landmark can lower effort on a current goal ([Dai et al. 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v161y2020icp142-157.html)).

**Plain words**
Highland Maya daykeepers keep a 260-day count and read each day's character to choose when to plant, harvest or marry. The research on calendars is different: people start new goals more often after landmarks such as a new week or month. A fixed rhythm can help with starting, but waiting for the next landmark can also make people ease off now. Pick a regular review day and keep to it.

*For example:* A writer who only writes when inspired sets a fixed writing morning each week. This is an imagined case.

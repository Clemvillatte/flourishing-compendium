> **Principle 51** — Live by a rhythm larger than your moods

The 260-day sacred count (the tzolk'in in Maya terms) structures the daykeepers' world: each day has a character, and the calendar's rhythm, not the mood's weather, sets the frame for what is done when. The principle to keep is the structure itself: a rhythmic, pre-committed calendar of practices (days for planting and pruning, days for rest, days for counsel) protects the life from both mood-driven drift and mood-driven burnout. Practically, build one calendar rhythm beyond the week (a monthly review day, a seasonal renewal, an annual retreat) and keep it as the tradition keeps its count.

(The calendar tradition; Barbara Tedlock, Time and the Highland Maya.)

**Plain words** The 260-day count structures the daykeeper's world: each day has a character, and the calendar's rhythm — not the mood's weather — sets the frame for what's done when. Keep the structure, whatever your calendar: a rhythmic, pre-committed schedule of practices that outranks your feelings.

*For example:* The writer who only writes when inspired (i.e., rarely) — and moves to the calendar's rhythm: writing days are writing days, whatever the weather inside. The calendar carries what the mood drops.

> **Principle 23** — Set specific and difficult goals: vagueness kills motivation

Specific, difficult goals (challenging but achievable) produce better performance than vague ("do your best") or easy goals. Specificity provides direction; difficulty mobilises effort.

**Plain words** Vague goals are dead goals — "do your best" produces less than a number, and "get fit" is a mood. Specific and challenging beats vague and easy, every time. Sharpen the target until it has edges.

*For example:* "Read more" dies in February; "one chapter every night before the light goes off" survives. The difference isn't will — it's specificity.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Goal setting theory (Locke & Latham)|Goal setting theory (Locke & Latham)]]**, specific, difficult goals produce better performance than vague or easy goals (Locke & Latham, 2002, 2006; Latham & Locke, 2007)
- **Specificity and difficulty**, specific goals provide direction; difficult goals mobilise effort (Locke & Latham, 2002; Miner, 2005)
- **Note: the effect is robust but moderated by goal commitment and ability** (Locke & Latham, 2002; Miner, 2005)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right effort|Right effort requires clear aim]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- Set specific, difficult goals: not "exercise more" but "walk 30 minutes, 4 times per week." Not "write more" but "write 500 words daily." Specific and challenging beats vague and easy.

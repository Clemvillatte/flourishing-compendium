> **Principle 73** — Bind attention to one place

Sutra 3.1 defines concentration of the first kind, fixed attention (dharana), in one line: "Binding the mind-stuff to a place is fixed-attention" (trans. Woods, p. 203). The commentary names the places. One is within the body, "to the navel", and "the palate and so forth". The other is an external object, though "with an external object the mind-stuff as such cannot have a relation" except "in so far as it is a fluctuation", in other words as a perception (pp. 203-204).

### What the commentary adds

"The binding is a relation" (p. 203). A Purana verse quoted there recommends that the yogin, having restrained breath and senses, "should make a localization of the mind-stuff upon some auspicious support" (pp. 203-204). The supports named are deities such as Hiranyagarbha. A second quotation describes a visualized form in detail and says: "Fixed-attention is not possible without something on which to fix it" (p. 204).

### Where the principle differs

The principle says the definition excludes "visualizing" the object and thinking about it, and puts the practice in the return of attention. The commentary I opened does the opposite on the first point: its supports include a vividly pictured divine form. The idea that the practice is the return is a modern teaching about how attention behaves, not a reading of the sutra. I have not found it in Woods.

**Plain words** Fixed attention is holding the mind to one chosen place or object. The text says it needs something to fix on, and the object can be a point in the body or an outside support. The advice to return again and again is useful, but it comes from the principle, and the commentary does not rule out picturing the object.

*For example:* Imagined example: you hold attention on the sensation at the tip of your nose. When it drifts to a plan, you notice and come back. That is a modern use of the idea, not a quotation.

(Yoga Sutra 3.1 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's gloss, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 203-204, Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21.)

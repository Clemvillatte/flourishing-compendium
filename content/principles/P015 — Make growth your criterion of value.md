> **Principle 15** — Make growth your criterion of value

Dewey's criterion comes from "The Good of Activity" (Human Nature and Conduct, Part IV.I): morality is a continuing process rather than a fixed achievement, and the good of growth in present action is the only good within our control ([Dewey 1922, HNC IV.I](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/human-nature-and-conduct/text/chapter-4-1)).

> The good, satisfaction, "end," of growth of present action in shades and scope of meaning is the only good within our control, and the only one, accordingly, for which responsibility exists. (Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, "The Good of Activity," [Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/human-nature-and-conduct/text/chapter-4-1))

The surrounding paragraph closes the loop: morals is education, learning the meaning of what we are about and employing that meaning in action; growing and growth are the same fact ([Dewey 1922, HNC IV.I](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/human-nature-and-conduct/text/chapter-4-1)). Growth as criterion therefore asks a specific question of any course of action: does it expand the meaning and scope of what you are doing, or merely its comfort?

(Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, 1922; Experience and Nature, 1925.)

**Plain words**
Before deciding between two options, ask what each would let you learn or understand that the other would not. Dewey's criterion makes growth the thing to check: which choice expands the present action in meaning and scope.

**For example**
When a job offer lands, run the two-column test: what you would actually be doing in month six under each option, and which column would let you learn more. The pay gap is a fact; the learning question is the criterion.

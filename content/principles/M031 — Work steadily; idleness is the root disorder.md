> **Principle 31** — Work steadily; idleness is the root disorder

The huehuetlatolli's counsel on work: rise early, work always, let the hands be occupied, for the idle person is the tradition's picture of the degenerating person. The counsel pairs with the counter-principle (also in the tradition): work without becoming a slave to work, and rest as a duty. The modern translation keeps both halves: structure and occupation protect the mind, and overwork is a failure of the same virtue. Practically, keep the elders' frame for one week: each day has its work, done early, done steadily, and the day's rest is earned and taken.

(Florentine Codex, Book VI, the counsel on labor.)

**Plain words** Rise early, work steadily, keep the hands occupied — the idle person is the tradition's picture of the degenerating person. But the tradition keeps both halves: work without becoming its slave, and rest as a duty. Structure and occupation, with the counter-principle loaded.

*For example:* The retiree who thought rest was the reward and found it was the illness — and takes the modest part-time craft work, not for the money, for the structure.

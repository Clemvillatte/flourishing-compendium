> **Principle 32** — Cultivate an inner ally: develop a supportive internal voice

The internal voice — how you talk to yourself — can be an inner critic (harsh, judgmental, undermining) or an inner ally (supportive, encouraging, honest). Developing an inner ally is a trainable skill and is associated with better outcomes.

**Plain words** The internal voice can be an inner critic (harsh, undermining) or an inner ally (supportive, encouraging, honest). Developing the ally is a trainable skill — and the ally is honest; it just doesn't lie about the good news the way the critic lies about the bad.

*For example:* The person whose inner monologue is a bored, contemptuous sports commentator — and the deliberate training of the alternative voice: "okay, that section was weak; the structure is strong; fix the section." Same information, different coach. Only one of them gets players to stay.
## Psychology parallels

- **Internal dialogue and self-regulation**, the quality of internal self-talk affects emotion and behaviour (Morin, 2005; Brinthaupt et al., 2009)
- **Supportive self-talk improves performance and well-being**, encouraging internal dialogue improves outcomes compared to critical self-talk (Kross et al., 2014; Dobson, 2012)

## Practice

- Develop your inner ally: when facing difficulty, speak to yourself as a good coach or friend would. "This is hard, and you can do it. Let's take it one step at a time." Be honest but kind.

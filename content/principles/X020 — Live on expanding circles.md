> **Principle 20** — Live on expanding circles

Emerson's essay Circles: the life of man is a self-evolving circle, which from a ring imperiously streams onward into a new and larger circumference; each generation, each conversation, each book that moves you must break the last circle. The principle is the tradition's distinct model of growth: not ascent but expansion, with the current circle's edge always provisional. Practically, name your current circle's edge (the commitments and convictions that bound this year), and pick one deliberate stretch beyond it per season.

(Emerson, Circles.)

**Plain words** Life is a self-evolving circle that keeps breaking into a larger ring — each conversation, each book, each generation bursts the last circumference. Growth isn't climbing; it's expanding, and the current edge is always provisional. Name your edge, and deliberately stretch past it.

*For example:* The person whose convictions about work were formed at 25 by one bad boss — and at 40 finally reads, talks, and works past that circle, discovering most of the map was drawn by a single bad summer.

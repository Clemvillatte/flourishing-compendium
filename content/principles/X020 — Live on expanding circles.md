> **Principle 20** — Live on expanding circles

Emerson's essay Circles gives the model in one sentence: the life of man is a self-evolving circle, which, from a ring imperceptibly small, rushes on all sides outwards to new and larger circles, and that without end. Each thought forms itself into a circular wave of circumstance that heaps and solidifies to hem in the life; but if the soul is quick and strong it bursts over that boundary and expands another orbit. The essay's rule is that around every circle another can be drawn, that every end is a beginning, and there are no fixtures in nature; permanence is but a word of degrees. Growth here is not ascent but expansion, and the current circle's edge is always provisional: conversation is a game of circles, and the heart, in its first and narrowest pulses, already tends outward.

As a compendium exercise, name your current circle's edge — the commitments and convictions that bound this year — and pick one deliberate stretch beyond it per season.

> "Our life is an apprenticeship to the truth that around every circle another can be drawn; that there is no end in nature, but every end is a beginning; that there is always another dawn risen on mid-noon, and under every deep a lower deep opens." (Emerson, Circles, 1841)

**Plain words**
Think of your life as a circle that keeps outgrowing itself. The commitments and convictions that hem you in were once new thoughts; they hardened into a boundary, and a strong soul bursts over it. Every end is a beginning, so the edge you're living at is provisional. Name that edge, and stretch past it on purpose.

**For example**
In an imagined application, someone whose convictions about work were formed at 25 by one bad boss spends a season reading, talking, and working past that circle, and writes down how much of the map came from that single summer.

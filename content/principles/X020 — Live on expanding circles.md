> **Principle 20** — Live on expanding circles

In Circles, Emerson describes settled thoughts and practices as boundaries that can themselves be exceeded. His image also appears in his account of conversation. Each new boundary remains open to further change.

For a compendium exercise, choose one long-held conviction. Record the experience that formed it and seek a conversation or reading that could challenge it.

> "Our life is an apprenticeship to the truth that around every circle another can be drawn; that there is no end in nature, but every end is a beginning; that there is always another dawn risen on mid-noon, and under every deep a lower deep opens." (Emerson, Circles, 1841)

**Plain words**
Pick a belief you have not reconsidered for a while. Ask what experience it rests on and what would change your mind. Use those questions to choose something to read or someone to talk with.

**For example**
In an imagined exercise, someone who expects managers to resemble a former boss asks two colleagues about their experiences and records similarities and differences.

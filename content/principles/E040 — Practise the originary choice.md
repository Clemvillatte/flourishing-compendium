> **Principle 432** — Practise the originary choice

Sartre's "original choice" is the choice of yourself that sits under every reason you could give. It is not a decision taken after weighing options. "The question here is not of a deliberate choice", he writes, because the choice "is the foundation of all deliberation" (Being and Nothingness, Part IV, ch. 1, pp. 461-462, trans. Barnes). It is also not hidden from you: "as our being is precisely our original choice, the consciousness (of) the choice is identical with the self-consciousness which we have" (p. 462). "Choice and consciousness are one and the same thing" (p. 462).

### What it shapes

This choice "originally creates all causes and all motives which can guide us to partial actions" and "arranges the world with its meaning, its instrumental-complexes, and its coefficient of adversity" (p. 465). So it is the frame in which reasons appear, not one of the reasons.

### Why it can change

Sartre insists on revisability. The initial project can change by "a radical conversion of my being-in-the-world" (p. 464), and "this modification is always possible" (p. 464). We are "perpetually engaged in our choice and perpetually conscious of the fact that we ourselves can abruptly invert this choice" (p. 465). But the same fact makes it fragile: "By the sole fact that our choice is absolute, it is fragile" (p. 465).

**Plain words** Under your daily choices lies a basic stance toward the world and yourself, a project you are already living. Sartre says you do not choose it by weighing pros and cons; weighing only makes sense inside it. You can notice it, because it is not hidden, and you can change it, though not by a simple resolution. Change would be a turn in the whole project, and every old reason would then look different.

*For example:* Imagined example: someone always treats work as a test they might fail. They notice that every reason they give for overworking assumes that stance. Changing it means changing what counts as a reason at all.

(Sartre, Being and Nothingness, Part IV, ch. 1, §I, pp. 461-465, trans. Hazel Barnes, Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21. The old cite "Part IV, ch. 2" was corrected.)

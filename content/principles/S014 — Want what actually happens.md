> **Principle 136** — Want what actually happens

Do not wish for things to be other than they are. The person who wants reality to conform to their desires is perpetually frustrated. The person who wants reality as it is has no frustration. Practise the reframing: "this is what I wanted," meaning, "this is what nature has produced, and I accept it."

(Disc. I.12, I.17; Med. IV.10, VIII.50; Seneca, On Providence.)

### The instruction and the appointment

The Enchiridion's one-line version:

> "Demand not that events should happen as you wish; but wish them to happen as they do happen, and you will go on well." (Epictetus, Enchiridion 8, tr. Higginson, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45109/45109-h/45109-h.htm))

The Discourses' fuller argument:

> "True instruction is this,—learning to desire that things should happen as they do. And how do they happen? As the appointer of them hath appointed. He hath appointed that there should be summer and winter, plenty and dearth, virtue and vice, and all such contrarieties, for the harmony of the whole." (Epictetus, Discourses I.12, tr. Higginson, [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_Epictetus,_His_Discourses,_in_Four_Books,_the_Enchiridion,_and_Fragments/Book_1/Chapter_12))

The same chapter defines the freedom this buys: "For he is free to whom all happens agreeably to his desire, and whom no one can unduly restrain" (Epictetus, Discourses I.12).

Marcus gives the cosmology behind it:

> "Whatsoever it be that happens unto thee, it is that which from all time was appointed unto thee. For by the same coherence of causes, by which thy substance from all eternity was appointed to be, was also whatsoever should happen to it, destinated and appointed." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations X.5, tr. George Long, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2680/2680-h/2680-h.htm))

**Plain words** The move works by narrowing what you require. Your wishes about weather, traffic, and other people's choices sit outside your power, so each one is a standing bet against the world. Epictetus's proposal keeps the wanting and changes the target: align the wish with what is already occurring, and the gap between demand and reality closes without the world changing at all. Run it on one small case first, like the rain example, since the practice scales badly if you start with your worst year.

*For example:* Rain on your wedding day. Wanting dry weather ruins it. Wanting this day, rained on, saves it.

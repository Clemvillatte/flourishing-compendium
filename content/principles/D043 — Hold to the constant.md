> **Principle 43** — Hold to the constant

Beneath changing phenomena find the invariant: cycles, human nature, your own values — and anchor decisions there. Serving the constant is true generosity to your future self.

(DDJ 16, 59.)

### The unchanging rule

Chapter 16 grounds the principle in observation, not taste:

> "All things alike go through their processes of activity, and (then) we see them return (to their original state)... This returning to their root is what we call the state of stillness... The report of that fulfilment is the regular, unchanging rule. To know that unchanging rule is to be intelligent; not to know it leads to wild movements and evil issues." (DDJ 16, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

Chapter 59 adds the practice: "For regulating the human (in our constitution) and rendering the (proper) service to the heavenly, there is nothing like moderation. It is only by this moderation that there is effected an early return (to man's normal state)" (DDJ 59, Legge) — the return is "repeated accumulation", and its image is a plant whose "roots are deep and its flower stalks firm".

**Plain words** The weather changes; the climate does not. Deadlines, arguments and news are weather — they feel like everything because they are close. The instruction is to locate the few things that stay — cycles that always repeat, the parts of human nature that show up in every office, the values you would keep under a different name — and anchor decisions there. One payoff: you stop re-deriving your life from scratch every time the weather changes.

*For example:* A couple arguing about dishes is really arguing about a constant — respect or fairness — name the constant and the dishes shrink to dishes.

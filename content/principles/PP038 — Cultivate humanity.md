> **Principle 38** — Cultivate humanity

In the VIA classification, humanity is one of six core virtues: the interpersonal strengths that involve "tending and befriending" others. Its three strengths are love, kindness and social intelligence ([Peterson and Seligman, 2004, Table 1 as reprinted in Ruch and Proyer, 2015](https://www.viacharacter.org/pdf/MAPPING%20STRENGTHS%20INTO%20VIRTUES.pdf)).

> "The entries in this virtue class [humanity] resemble those we identify as justice strengths, with the difference being that strengths of humanity and love are brought to bear in one-to-one relationships, whereas those of justice are most relevant in one-to-many relationships. The former strengths are interpersonal, the latter broadly social." (Peterson and Seligman, 2004, p. 293, [as quoted in Ruch and Proyer, 2015](https://www.viacharacter.org/pdf/MAPPING%20STRENGTHS%20INTO%20VIRTUES.pdf))

The kindness experiment bears on the cultivability claim. In [Layous and colleagues' 2012 classroom study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0051380), 415 nine-to-eleven-year-olds in 19 Vancouver classrooms were instructed to perform three acts of kindness a week for four weeks, versus visiting three places. Students in both conditions improved in well-being; the difference showed up in the social ledger, where the kindness group gained significantly more peer acceptance than the control group (p = .05), an average of 1.5 friends.

(Peterson and Seligman, 2004; Layous et al., 2012.)

**Plain words** Humanity in the VIA sense is a one-person-at-a-time virtue. Its strengths are noticing what someone needs, doing something about it, and reading the situation well enough to know which is which. A week gives you the evidence: who did you actually help?

*For example:* Noticing a coworker is struggling and quietly taking a task off her plate.

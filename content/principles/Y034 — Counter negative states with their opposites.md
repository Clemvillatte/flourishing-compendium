> **Principle 34** — Counter negative states with their opposites

The tradition's explicit technique for negative thoughts that obstruct practice: cultivate the opposite mental content. Anger is met with patience, fear with safety, self-contempt with [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]]. This is not suppression; it is structurally kin to counterconditioning (a comparison of mechanism, not a scientific translation), and the tradition treats it as the practical bridge between ethics and meditation. Practically, name the negative state, then deliberately install its opposite for a full minute.

(YS 2.33-2.34.)

**Plain words** The explicit technique for obstructive negative thoughts: cultivate the opposite mental content — anger met with patience, fear with safety, self-contempt with self-compassion. Not suppression; structurally kin to counterconditioning. Swap the content, deliberately.

*For example:* Someone mid-spiral about a mistake — instead of arguing with the thought, places beside it the opposite content: the memory of the same skill working fine last month. The spiral needs fuel; the opposite content starves it.

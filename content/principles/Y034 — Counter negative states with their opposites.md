> **Principle 34** — Counter negative states with their opposites

Yoga Sūtras II.33–II.34 ask the practitioner to cultivate opposites when harmful thoughts obstruct practice ([Woods translation](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/)). The next verse places this in an ethical setting: injury committed, instigated or approved, arising from greed, anger or infatuation ([II.34](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/)).

> If there be inhibition by perverse-considerations (vitarka), there should be cultivation of the opposites.
>
> Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra II.33, translated by James Haughton Woods (1914), [reprinted text](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/).

(YS 2.33-2.34.)

### Plain words

For an everyday exercise, choose a response that refuses the harm you are tempted to do. When the target is yourself, try [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]] rather than another round of punishment.

For example: You want to embarrass a colleague who made a mistake. Give the necessary correction privately and accurately, without adding a humiliation.

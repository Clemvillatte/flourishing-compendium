> **Principle 22** — Use cultural humility as a practice

Use cultural humility — the practice of approaching other cultures with openness, curiosity, and a willingness to examine one's own assumptions — as a deliberate practice. Cultural humility differs from cultural competence: competence implies mastery of the other; humility implies lifelong learning and self-examination (Tervalon and Murray-García, 1998).

**Plain words** Cultural humility beats cultural competence — not "I've mastered your culture" but "I'm ready to learn, and to check my own assumptions." Competence claims arrival; humility assumes travel. The stance is beginner's mind with a passport.

*For example:* The clinician who asks "what does your family do when someone's sick?" instead of assuming — that's humility. The one who read a chapter and "knows how Chinese families work" — that's competence overreaching.

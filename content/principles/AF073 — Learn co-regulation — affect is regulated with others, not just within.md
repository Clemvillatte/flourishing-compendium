> **Principle 73** — Learn co-regulation: affect is regulated with others, not just within

Butler and Randall give the term a precise definition rather than a loose one. Coregulation is the bidirectional linkage of oscillating emotional channels between partners, and it contributes to emotional stability for both. They note the term has been used loosely in the literature, and propose fixing it with this operational definition ([Butler & Randall, 2013, Emotion Review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1754073912451630)).

> Coregulation refers to the process by which relationship partners form a dyadic emotional system involving an oscillating pattern of affective arousal and dampening that dynamically maintains an optimal emotional state.
>
> — Butler and Randall, 2013, Emotion Review 5(2) ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1754073912451630))

**Plain words** The compendium's application of this principle asks the reader to treat emotional regulation as something that happens between people, not only inside one person. The definition names the mechanism: an oscillating pattern of arousal and dampening that keeps both partners in a workable range. What a person does in interaction changes what the other person's affect does.

For example (imagined): a panicked caller and a friend who says, slowly, "okay, tell me exactly what's happening." The panic downshifts in the friend's steadier tone. Nothing was solved on that call; the emotional state was regulated.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Co-regulation of affect in relationships|Co-regulation of affect in relationships]]**, partners and close others regulate each other's affect through attunement, responsiveness, and synchronisation (Butler & Gross, 2009; English & Eldreda, 2020)
- **Social support as affect regulation**, perceived support reduces affective reactivity to stressors (Cohen & Wills, 1985)

## Practice

- When you are emotionally activated, sometimes the most effective regulation is a calm, attuned person. Do not treat needing co-regulation as a failure of self-regulation.

> **Principle 73** — Learn co-regulation: affect is regulated with others, not just within

Emotions are not purely intrapersonal; they are regulated in interaction. A calm, attuned other can help regulate your affect, and you can regulate theirs. This is co-regulation, and it is a foundation of close relationships and therapy.

**Plain words** Emotions are regulated in interaction, not just within: a calm, attuned other can regulate your affect, and you theirs. Co-regulation — the foundation of attachment and of good friendships. Borrow a nervous system when yours is jammed.

*For example:* The panicked caller and the friend who just says, slowly, "okay — tell me exactly what's happening" — and the panic downshifts in the friend's steadier tone. Nothing was solved on that call; everything was regulated. That's what co-regulation is for.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Co-regulation of affect in relationships|Co-regulation of affect in relationships]]**, partners and close others regulate each other's affect through attunement, responsiveness, and synchronisation (Butler & Gross, 2009; English & Eldreda, 2020)
- **Social support as affect regulation**, perceived support reduces affective reactivity to stressors (Cohen & Wills, 1985)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Ubuntu: I am because we are** (African Philosophy, Desmond Tutu)
- **[[Aristotelian friendship|Aristotelian friendship as mutual flourishing]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IX)

## Practice

- When you are emotionally activated, sometimes the most effective regulation is a calm, attuned person. Do not treat needing co-regulation as a failure of self-regulation.

> **Principle 8** — Classify every desire before feeding it

The Epicurean taxonomy: desires natural and necessary (food, shelter, safety, the company of friends); desires natural but not necessary (luxury foods, elaborate shelter, status comfort); desires neither natural nor necessary (fame, power, immortality of reputation). The classification is a decision procedure, run before each pursuit: the first class is satisfied easily and durably; the second, expensively and briefly; the third, never. Practically, sort your current pursuits into the three classes, one by one, in writing.

(Epicurus, the letter's classification; Principal Doctrines 26-30.)

**Plain words** Before feeding a desire, file it. Natural and necessary (food, shelter, safety, friends): cheap to satisfy, stays satisfied. Natural but unnecessary (fancy food, plush comfort): expensive, briefly. Neither natural nor necessary (fame, power, legacy): unsatisfiable, full stop. Sort your pursuits in writing and watch the third column shrink on its own.

*For example:* Someone lists their current wants and finds half the list is column three — wanting to be "respected by everyone at the reunion" — and cancels the whole column guilt-free.

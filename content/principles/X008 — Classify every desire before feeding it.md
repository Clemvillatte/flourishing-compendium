> **Principle 8** — Classify every desire before feeding it

The classification is stated in the surviving texts. The Letter to Menoeceus sorts desires by origin: some natural, others groundless; of the natural, some necessary as well as natural, some natural only; and of the necessary, some necessary for happiness, some for the body's ease, some for life itself. Principal Doctrine 29 gives the three-way form: desires natural and necessary, natural but not necessary, and neither, the last due to illusory opinion. Principal Doctrine 26 adds the test for the second class: desires that lead to no pain when ungratified are unnecessary, and their longing is easily got rid of when the thing is difficult to procure or likely to produce harm. The letter states the point of the sorting: someone who understands these things "will direct every preference and aversion toward securing health of body and tranquillity of mind".

As a compendium exercise, sort your current pursuits into the three classes, one by one, in writing.

> "We must also reflect that of desires some are natural, others are groundless; and that of the natural some are necessary as well as natural, and some natural only. And of the necessary desires some are necessary if we are to be happy, some if the body is to be rid of uneasiness, some if we are even to live." (Letter to Menoeceus, Hicks)

**Plain words**
Before you feed a want, ask where it comes from. Does something hurt or go missing without it? That is the necessary class, and the letter says it is small. Is it pleasant, with nothing hurting when it is absent? Natural only. Or does it come from an idea about yourself, status or reputation, that no object can settle? Groundless. The sorting is the practice: name the class, and the pursuit gets clearer.

**For example**
In an imagined application, someone lists their current wants and finds half the list is wanting to be respected by everyone at the reunion. Nothing hurts without it, so it files under the groundless class, and the column is crossed out.

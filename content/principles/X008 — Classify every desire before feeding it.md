> **Principle 8** — Classify every desire before feeding it

The Letter to Menoeceus (127) sorts desires by origin: some natural, others groundless; of the natural, some necessary as well as natural, some natural only; and of the necessary, some necessary for happiness, some for the body's ease, some for life itself. Principal Doctrine 29 gives the three-way form: desires natural and necessary, natural but not necessary, and neither, the last due to illusory opinion. Principal Doctrine 26 calls desires unnecessary when leaving them unfulfilled causes no pain, and adds that their longing is easily relinquished when obtaining the object is difficult or likely to cause harm. The letter connects these distinctions with choices directed toward bodily health and peace of mind.

As a compendium exercise, sort your current pursuits into the three classes, one by one, in writing.

> "We must also reflect that of desires some are natural, others are groundless; and that of the natural some are necessary as well as natural, and some natural only. And of the necessary desires some are necessary if we are to be happy, some if the body is to be rid of uneasiness, some if we are even to live." (Letter to Menoeceus 127, Hicks)

**Plain words**
Ask two questions about a want: where does it come from, and is it necessary? A wish can be unnecessary and still natural. Give a reason for the category you choose before deciding whether to pursue it.

**For example**
In an imagined exercise, someone lists water, a costly lunch and praise at a reunion. Beside each, they write a proposed category and a reason for choosing it.

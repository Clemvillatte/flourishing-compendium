> **Principle 64** — Learn to create a distraction-free environment: physical and digital

A distraction-free environment is the foundation of deep work and sustained attention. This involves both physical (clean workspace, quiet, closed door) and digital (no notifications, closed tabs, blocking apps) design.

**Plain words** A distraction-free environment is built, not wished for: one visible task, everything else in a drawer or a different app, pings off, headphones on. Every removed distraction is a decision you never have to make again. Design once, benefit daily.

*For example:* The desk with just the laptop and water bottle — nothing else in the visual field. Not aesthetic minimalism; decision minimalism.
## Psychology parallels

- **Environmental design improves attention**, removing distractors is more effective than resisting them (Ward et al., 2017; Forster & Lavie, 2008)
- **Attention residue and task switching**, interruptions and switching degrade performance (Leroy, 2009; Monsell, 2003)

## Practice

- Create a dedicated, distraction-free workspace: clear desk, quiet environment, phone in another room or out of sight, notifications off, only necessary tabs/apps open. Protect this space.

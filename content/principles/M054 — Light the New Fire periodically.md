> **Principle 54** — Light the New Fire periodically

At the cycle's turn, the tradition relit the fire: the old hearth fires were extinguished, the instruments renewed, and the new fire kindled and carried back to every hearth. The principle is the scheduled discontinuity: every so often, the old fire is deliberately put out (the habit, the role, the arrangement, the identity) and the new one kindled from ceremony rather than from drift. Practically, once per year, extinguish one thing on purpose (a commitment, a routine, a self-description), mark the turning with ceremony of some kind, and light the replacement deliberately.

(The New Fire ceremony tradition; the xiuhmolpilli cycle.)

**Plain words** At the cycle's turn, the old fires were put out, the instruments renewed, and the new fire kindled and carried back to every hearth. Scheduled discontinuity: every so often, deliberately extinguish the old fire — the habit, the role, the arrangement, the identity — and kindle the new one from ceremony rather than from crisis.

*For example:* The manager who ends the year by actually ending things — closing the projects, deleting the dead committees, letting the outdated version of the team die — so the new year starts with kindling, not ash.

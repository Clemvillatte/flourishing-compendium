> **Principle 54** — Light the New Fire periodically


The New Fire ceremony closed a 52-year cycle. Sahagún's Nahua informants say the years were counted in four groups of thirteen: "the thirteen-year [cycles] had four times made a circle". They add: "Hence was it said that then were tied and bound our years, and that once again the years were newly laid hold of" (Florentine Codex, Book 7, Anderson and Dibble translation, chapter on the binding of the years).

Before the fire came the clearing: "First they put out fires everywhere". Household god-images went into the water. Then "there was much sweeping—there was sweeping very clean. Rubbish was thrown out; none lay in any of the houses." The Book 7 contents list names the act: "Boring new fire on the breast of a sacrificial victim". The rite included human sacrifice, and the principle borrows the shape of the rite, not that part of it.

Afterwards "everyone renewed his clothing and all the household goods". "Thus it was said that truly the year newly started. There was much happiness and rejoicing."

The Codex does not say this improved anyone's wellbeing, and the cycle was 52 years, not one. A yearly rhythm is an adaptation.

Across three archival studies, Google searches for "diet", gym visits and goal commitments "all increase following temporal landmarks (e.g., the outset of a new week, month, year, or semester; a birthday; a holiday)" (Dai, Milkman and Riis 2014, Management Science 60(10):2563-2582, [abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2014.1901)). A later paper found that a landmark still ahead can lower effort on a current goal ([Dai et al. 2020](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v161y2020icp142-157.html)). No opened study tests a ceremony of ending things.

**Plain words** Every so often, end something on purpose. Close the old commitment, clear out what has piled up, then start the replacement as a fresh decision. Do not wait for it to fall apart.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A team lead picks the first week of January. She closes two projects nobody uses, deletes the stale meeting series, and writes one line about what the team will do differently. Then she starts the one new thing, rather than letting last year's habits carry on by default.

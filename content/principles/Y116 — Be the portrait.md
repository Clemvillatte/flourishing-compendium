> **Principle 116** — Be the portrait

Mukundananda's commentary says that in verses 13 to 19 of chapter 12 Krishna describes the qualities of the devotees who are dear to him. The list opens:

> Those devotees are very dear to Me who are free from malice toward all living beings, who are friendly, and compassionate. They are free from attachment to possessions and egotism, equipoised in happiness and distress, and ever-forgiving.

(BG 12.13-14, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/12/verse/13/).)

The next verse supplies the other half of this principle, the person who neither disturbs nor is disturbed:

> Those who are not a source of annoyance to anyone and who in turn are not agitated by anyone, who are equal in pleasure and pain, and free from fear and anxiety, such devotees of Mine are very dear to Me.

(BG 12.15, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/12/verse/15/).)

The agitation clause sits in 12.15, so this page cites 12.13-15. The portrait is also a devotee's portrait: in 12.14 these people are also "steadily united with Me in devotion". A reader who does not share that faith can still use the ethical clauses, but should know they are taking them out of that frame.

Using the portrait as a daily checklist, one clause at a time, is this page's suggestion. Mukundananda's commentary reads the traits as what devotion produces in a person, and not as a to-do list.

**Plain words**

Most of us know what a good person is supposed to be like in general and have no idea how we compare. This passage is concrete enough to check. It asks whether you wish people harm, whether you are warm to them, whether you cling to things, whether you stay level, and whether people feel on edge around you. Pick one question a day and see how you did.

**For example**

Someone tapes the list inside a journal and once a month rates themselves on each line. This month malice is down, possessiveness is holding and forgiving has slipped. Seeing the change on paper is the point of the exercise.

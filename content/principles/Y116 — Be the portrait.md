> **Principle 116** — Be the portrait

The Gita's description of the person dear to the tradition: free from malice toward all beings, friendly, compassionate, without possessiveness and ego, patient, content, self-controlled, resolute, unmoved by the world's agitations and not a cause of the world's agitation. The portrait is given as a practice object: read it, and each day take one clause as the day's practice. Practically, post the portrait where you work, and let one clause per day be your theme.

(BG 12.13-14.)

**Plain words** Be the portrait: free from malice, friendly, compassionate, without possessiveness and ego, patient, content, self-controlled, resolute, unmoved by agitations and not a cause of them. The portrait is given as a mirror — hold it up monthly and check the likeness, feature by feature.

*For example:* Someone tapes the portrait to the inside of the journal and does the monthly check: malice — down one; possessiveness — holding; patience — regressed. The mirror does what mirrors do: makes the changes visible.

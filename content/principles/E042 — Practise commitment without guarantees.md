> **Principle 434** — Practise commitment without guarantees

Sartre's lecture gives no guarantee to the person deciding. Of a student torn between two duties he says ([Existentialism Is a Humanism](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm)):

> No rule of general morality can show you what you ought to do: no signs are vouchsafed in this world.

Even then, he rejects waiting. He quotes the formula that "one need not hope in order to undertake one's work", and says of quietism: "First I ought to commit myself and then act my commitment." He ties commitment to anguish: when a person commits to anything, they cannot escape "the sense of complete and profound responsibility."

(Sartre, 1946.)

**Plain words** Sartre says there is no outside sign that tells you the right choice, and that this is not a reason to hold off. You decide, then act on the decision, and you own it. The page's wider claim that this works better than waiting is an application, not something Sartre or the sources below test.

For example (imagined): someone weighing a job offer in another city lists every risk, then notices the list never ends. Accepting or declining is still a choice, made without proof either way.

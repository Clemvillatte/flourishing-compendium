> **Principle 43** — Recognise the limits of analysis: overthinking can be worse than deciding

Deliberate analysis has costs: time, opportunity cost, decision fatigue, and paralysis. For many decisions, more analysis does not improve the outcome but does delay it. Knowing when to stop deliberating is a cognitive skill.

**Plain words** Deliberation has costs: time, opportunity cost, decision fatigue, paralysis. For many decisions, more analysis doesn't improve the outcome, it just delays it. Know when to stop analysing and decide. Good enough, decided now, beats optimal, decided never.

*For example:* The person who researches laptops for forty hours to save $50 — the analysis cost more than the savings — versus the one with a time-box: one hour, pick a well-reviewed option, done. The time-box isn't laziness; it's accounting.
## Psychology parallels

- **Analysis paralysis / maximising**, people who seek to maximise (find the absolute best option) are less satisfied than those who satisfice (find a good-enough option) (Schwartz et al., 2002; Schwartz, 2004)
- **[[Paradox of choice|Paradox of choice]]**, more options can lead to worse decisions and lower satisfaction, though the effect's magnitude is debated (Iyengar & Lepper, 2000; Scheibehenne et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The sufficient good|The sufficient good]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as non-overthinking]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 48)
- **Perfect is the enemy of good** (Voltaire, Dictionnaire Philosophique)

## Practice

- For most decisions, satisfice rather than maximise. Set a time limit for deliberation. Ask: "is more information going to change my decision?" If not, decide.

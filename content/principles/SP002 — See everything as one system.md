> **Principle 2** — See everything as one system

> Whatsoever is, is in God, and without God nothing can be, or be conceived.

(Spinoza, Ethics, Part I, Proposition 15, trans. R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800). Proposition 14 states that "Besides God no substance can be granted or conceived.")

The formula "God or Nature" is not in these propositions. It appears in the Preface to Part IV: "the eternal and infinite Being, which we call God or Nature, acts by the same necessity as that whereby it exists." The Latin form deus sive natura is quoted in the entry, but only the English phrase was checked here.

The practical reading comes from the Preface to Part III, where Spinoza refuses to treat human troubles as breaks in nature: "Nothing comes to pass in nature, which can be set down to a flaw therein; for nature is always the same, and everywhere one and the same in her efficacy and power of action." So "the passions of hatred, anger, envy, and so on" are to be understood like any other natural events. That Spinoza's frame removes the split between how things should be and how they are, and that this prevents "most preventable suffering," are this entry's readings and are not stated in these passages.

**Plain words** For Spinoza, nothing exists outside nature, so nothing in your life, whether an illness, a mood or an enemy, is an intruder from somewhere else. It all follows from the same causes as everything else, and it can be understood in the same way.

*For example:* Someone treats poor sleep, a short temper and overspending as three separate problems with three culprits. Seen as one system, they find that one pressure, stress, runs through all three.

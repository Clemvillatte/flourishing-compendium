> **Principle 71** — Recognise behavioural symptoms of OCD: compulsions and rituals

Compulsions (often called rituals) are excessive, repetitive, purposeful behaviours that people feel they must do to prevent or reduce the anxiety caused by their obsessive thoughts or to neutralise their obsessions: hand washing, ordering, checking, or mental acts like counting and silent repetition. From outside a compulsion can read like fussiness; from inside it does not feel optional. The behaviours or mental acts are either not realistically connected to what they are designed to prevent, or are clearly excessive.

Behavioural therapy is a treatment option for OCD; its essential element is exposure and ritual prevention: gradually exposing patients to situations that trigger the anxiety-provoking obsessions and rituals while asking them not to perform their rituals. In a network meta-analysis of 54 trials (6,652 participants), behavioural therapy, cognitive therapy, cognitive behavioural therapy, clomipramine and SSRIs all showed greater effects than drug placebo on symptom severity (behavioural therapy mean difference -14.48 Y-BOCS points; cognitive therapy -13.36; CBT -5.37; clomipramine -4.72; SSRIs -3.49). The authors report considerable uncertainty about relative efficacy: most psychotherapy trials allowed patients taking stable doses of antidepressants, and taking all the evidence into account, the combination of psychotherapeutic and pharmacological interventions is likely more effective than psychotherapy alone, at least in severe obsessive-compulsive disorder.

> "Compulsions (often called rituals) are excessive, repetitive, purposeful behaviors that people feel they must do to prevent or reduce the anxiety caused by their obsessive thoughts or to neutralize their obsessions." (MSD Manual Professional Version, [Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/psychiatric-disorders/obsessive-compulsive-and-related-disorders/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-ocd))

**Plain words**
A compulsion is a behaviour the person feels driven to perform, done to prevent or reduce the anxiety caused by obsessive thoughts. The manual's test is excess or disconnection: the behaviour is either not connected in a realistic way with what it is designed to prevent, or is clearly excessive. Distinguishing these symptoms from ordinary caution is assessment work; the manual's criteria, not a count of stove checks, are what a clinician applies. Treatment is gradual therapeutic work with a professional, not a self-directed instruction.

*For example:* An imagined case: a person checks the stove nine times before leaving, because an intrusive thought of the house burning makes the tenth check feel required. The compulsion is the checking the person feels driven to repeat; describing the fear and the checking to a clinician is how the assessment gets made.

## Practice

- If you have compulsive behaviours, recognise them as symptoms, not choices. Treatment is ERP: approach the obsession without doing the compulsion. Seek professional help; OCD is treatable.

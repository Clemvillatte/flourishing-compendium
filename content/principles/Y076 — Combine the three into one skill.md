> **Principle 76** — Combine the three into one skill

The three are given a single name when practiced together: samyama, the integrated application of binding, resting, and absorption to any chosen object. The principle is transfer: attention trained as a unit can be pointed at anything, which is what makes the curriculum practical rather than ritual. Practically, treat the three as one skill practiced on different objects, not three separate achievements.

(YS 3.4.)

**Plain words** The three — binding, resting, absorption — get a single name when practiced together: samyama, integrated and pointed at any chosen object. The principle is transfer: attention trained as a unit can be aimed at anything. That's what makes the curriculum practical.

*For example:* Someone trains the attention unit on the breath for two years — then points the same trained unit at their business problem, their child's behaviour, their own anger, and finds the tool works on every material.

> **Principle 345** — Practise the face of the Other

Encounter the Other's face as the primary moral summons. The face is not a physical feature but the manifestation of the Other's humanity that commands: "Thou shalt not kill." Before you reason about justice, before you calculate consequences, the face of the Other demands your response.

(Levinas, 1961/1969, Totality and Infinity; Levinas, 1969, "Substitution," in Otherwise Than Being.)

**Plain words** Before any reasoning about justice, the other person's face arrives as a command: this one matters, do not harm. That's Levinas — the moral summons is prior to the argument. You can reason after; the face gets first refusal.

*For example:* Someone walks past a hundred statistics about the crisis, unmoved — then one face on one screen, and suddenly they're organising the collection drive. The face is where ethics actually starts.

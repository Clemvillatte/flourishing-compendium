> **Principle 80** — Test what arises for truth

Patañjali's chapter on samādhi names a specific kind of insight. When deliberation has settled into an even calm, "the insight is truth-bearing" (YS 1.48). What arises in that state presents itself as bearing truth, and the sūtra leaves it there. The Gita describes the neighboring condition, an intellect that has crossed "the quagmire of delusion" (BG 2.52). Neither text says the felt certainty of such an insight settles whether it is true, and the compendium's application is the point of this principle: insights that arrive in practice are candidates for testing. Write the insight down, check it against ordinary life and against other people, and act on what survives.

(YS 1.47-1.48; BG 2.52.)

> In this [calm] the insight is truth-bearing.
>
> Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra 1.48, translated by James Haughton Woods (1914), [reprinted text](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/).

**Plain words**
A settled mind produces insights that feel unusually true. The feeling is part of what the practice produces, and by itself it settles nothing. When an insight arrives with that calm certainty, put it in writing and give it two weeks of ordinary life before acting on it.

**For example**
The meditator who returns from retreat with the settled conviction that they should quit their job and move to the coast, and gives the insight two weeks of ordinary-world testing before signing anything.

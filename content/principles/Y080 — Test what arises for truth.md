> **Principle 80** — Test what arises for truth

Yoga Sūtra 1.47 describes an internal undisturbed calm that comes with clarity in the super-reflective balanced state, and sūtra 1.48 says of the insight present there that it is truth-bearing. The Gita, in a neighboring passage, describes an intellect that has crossed "the quagmire of delusion" (BG 2.52). The verses name the insight and its calm; they do not themselves prescribe a test. The testing exercise is this compendium's proposal: when an insight from practice would change an important decision, write down what could show it mistaken, look for that evidence, and check the consequences before acting.

(YS 1.47-1.48; BG 2.52.)

> In this [calm] the insight is truth-bearing.
>
> Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra 1.48, translated by James Haughton Woods (1914), [reprinted text](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/).

**Plain words**
An insight that shows up during practice can still be wrong about your life. Before you let one change a big decision, write down what would prove it mistaken, then go look for that evidence and talk the plan through with someone who will push back.

**For example**
In an imagined case, someone returns from retreat wanting to resign. They review their finances, discuss the plan with a person they trust to push back, and only then give notice.

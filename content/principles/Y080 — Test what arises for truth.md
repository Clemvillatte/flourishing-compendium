> **Principle 80** — Test what arises for truth

The insight that matures from absorption is called truth-bearing (ritambhara), and the tradition adds a caution: the insight feels certain, and certainty is not accuracy. The Gita's parallel is the intellect that crosses delusion. Practically, treat every insight from practice as a hypothesis: write it, test it against life and other people, and let evidence settle it.

(YS 1.47-1.48; BG 2.52-2.53.)

**Plain words** The insight that matures from absorption is called truth-bearing — and the tradition adds a caution: it feels certain, and certainty is not accuracy. Treat every insight from practice as a hypothesis with excellent references, and test it against the world before acting on it.

*For example:* The meditator who returns from retreat with the absolute conviction they should quit their job and move to the coast — and gives the insight two weeks of ordinary-world testing before signing anything. Half of the revelations don't survive the testing. The good half does.

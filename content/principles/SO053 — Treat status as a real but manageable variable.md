> **Principle 53** — Treat status as a real but manageable variable

Treat status — one's position in a social hierarchy — as a real but manageable variable. Status predicts health through the social gradient (Marmot, 2004); the Whitehall studies showed that civil servants' health tracked their rank, even among those not in poverty. The mechanisms include control, stress, and dignity. The adaptive stance is to manage status concerns rather than deny them.

**Plain words** Status is a real biological variable — health tracks rank, even among people who aren't poor. The gradient runs through autonomy, respect, and control over life. The lesson isn't to chase status; it's to build environments with less rank-related stress.

*For example:* The civil servants one grade down with worse heart health than the grade above — same income bracket, different status. The body reads the org chart. Respect and control are nutrients.

## Practice

- In status pursuit, distinguish earned status (competence, contribution) from dominance (force, fear). In health, recognize that status loss (retirement, unemployment) is a health risk and plan for it.

> **Principle 53** — Treat status as a real but manageable variable

Marmot and Bobak's summary of the Whitehall programme states the finding in one line: "A gradient in mortality among civil servants who are not poor argues for the importance of psychosocial factors linked to position in the hierarchy" (2000). The Whitehall II follow-up traced the mechanism toward control: "low control in the workplace was related to increased risk of cardiovascular disease and had a role in mediating the social gradient in coronary heart disease". The authors' proposed factor is autonomy, "the degree of control people have over their lives". The compendium's framing: status is a real variable with bodily effects, and the manageability lies in what the gradient runs through: control, respect and the design of work.

> "A gradient in mortality among civil servants who are not poor argues for the importance of psychosocial factors linked to position in the hierarchy." (Marmot & Bobak, 2000, BMJ 321:1124-1128)

**Plain words** Status is a real health variable — health tracks rank, even among people who aren't poor. The gradient runs through autonomy, respect, and control over life. The lesson isn't to chase status; it's to build environments with less rank-related stress.

*For example:* Whitehall's civil servants were, by any reasonable standard, not poor; the heart disease still tracked the org chart. The body reads rank. Respect and control are nutrients.

## Practice

- In status pursuit, distinguish earned status (competence, contribution) from dominance (force, fear). In health, recognize that status loss (retirement, unemployment) is a health risk and plan for it.

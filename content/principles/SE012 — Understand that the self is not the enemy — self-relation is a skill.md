> **Principle 12** — Understand that the self is not the enemy: self-relation is a skill

The construct behind this principle is self-compassion: Kristin Neff defines it as three components, each naming an alternative to a harsh habit. The stance toward oneself is not fixed furniture; each component marks a swappable relation.

> "Self-compassion entails three main components: (a) self-kindness—being kind and understanding toward oneself in instances of pain or failure rather than being harshly self-critical, (b) common humanity—perceiving one's experiences as part of the larger human experience rather than seeing them as separating and isolating, and (c) mindfulness—holding painful thoughts and feelings in balanced awareness rather than over-identifying with them." (Neff, "Self-Compassion: An Alternative Conceptualization of a Healthy Attitude Toward Oneself", Self and Identity 2, 2003, p. 85)

The page's application: the inner critic is one voice among possible voices, and the relation to the self is the most adjustable relationship you have. Each of Neff's components is a move you can rehearse: kindness instead of the beating, shared humanity instead of isolation, balanced awareness instead of drowning.

**Plain words**
The harsh inner voice feels like a fact of your mental furniture, but it is one of several available tones. Neff's components each name a swap: kindness instead of the beating; common humanity (this happens to people, not only to me) instead of isolation; balanced awareness instead of drowning in the feeling. Practise the swaps on small failures first, because the tone you use there is the tone available in the big ones.

*For example:* Imagine the person who assumes the harsh inner voice is just "how I am", and the discovery, mid-therapy, that the voice has a volume knob and even a different station. The relationship to the self turned out to be the most adjustable relationship they had.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-compassion|Self-compassion as trainable skill]]**, the capacity to treat oneself with kindness is learnable and improves well-being (Neff, 2003, 2011; Zessin et al., 2015)
- **Self-criticism is associated with worse mental health**, harsh self-criticism predicts depression, anxiety, and poor outcomes (Gilbert et al., 2004; Shahar et al., 2012)

## Practice

- Notice how you talk to yourself. Is it how you would talk to a friend? If not, practise changing the tone. Self-relation is a skill that can be trained.

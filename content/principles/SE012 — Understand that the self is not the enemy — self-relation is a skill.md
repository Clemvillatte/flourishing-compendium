> **Principle 12** — Understand that the self is not the enemy: self-relation is a skill

How you relate to yourself — with kindness, criticism, indifference, or curiosity — is not fixed. It is a trainable skill. The internal relationship you have with yourself (the "inner critic" vs the "inner ally") is one of the most important determinants of well-being.

**Plain words** How you relate to yourself — kindness, criticism, indifference, curiosity — is not fixed; it's a trainable skill. The inner critic isn't a feature of the landscape; it's a habit with alternatives. Self-relation is a practice.

*For example:* The person who assumes the harsh inner voice is just "how I am" — and the discovery, mid-therapy, that the voice has a volume knob and even a different station. The relationship to the self turned out to be the most adjustable relationship they had.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-compassion|Self-compassion as trainable skill]]**, the capacity to treat oneself with kindness is learnable and improves well-being (Neff, 2003, 2011; Zessin et al., 2015)
- **Self-criticism is associated with worse mental health**, harsh self-criticism predicts depression, anxiety, and poor outcomes (Gilbert et al., 2004; Shahar et al., 2012)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Amor sui (right self-love)|Amor sui (right self-love)]]** (Spinoza, Ethics IV)
- **[[Metta (loving-kindness)|Metta (loving-kindness toward self)]]** (Buddhism, Metta Sutta)
- **[[The golden rule|The golden rule inverted]]** (Hillel the Elder, Talmud)

## Practice

- Notice how you talk to yourself. Is it how you would talk to a friend? If not, practise changing the tone. Self-relation is a skill that can be trained.

> **Principle 35** — Practice the five strengths

In Chekawa's Seven Points of Mind Training, the fourth point is "Applying the Practice throughout the Whole of Life". Its root text is brief: "The essence of the instruction, briefly stated, / is to apply yourself to the five strengths" (Chekawa Yeshe Dorje, Seven Points of Mind Training, trans. Adam Pearcey, [Lotsawa House](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/geshe-chekhawa-yeshe-dorje/seven-points-mind-training)). In the numbering I read, this is Point Four, not Point Five.

Gyalse Tokme Zangpo's commentary names the strengths. Impetus is to resolve "again and again" to keep training. Familiarization is "to train repeatedly" in the two kinds of bodhicitta. Wholesome seeds means to "accumulate merit as much as possible". Revulsion is "to reflect, whenever thoughts of self-cherishing occur, on how this has been the cause of various sufferings", and "to cast away thoughts of self-cherishing". Aspiration is to "make an aspiration after every virtuous deed". Together they "constitute a practice that brings everything together into a single hūṃ" (Gyalse Tokme Zangpo, Commentary on the Seven Points of Mind Training, trans. Adam Pearcey, [Lotsawa House](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/gyalse-thogme-zangpo/commentary-on-seven-points-mind-training)). I found no "there you are again" in either text, so that wording is a modern gloss.

**Plain words**
Five habits carry a practice through ordinary days: decide to keep going, return to it often, build up small good actions, notice self-centred thinking and its costs, and dedicate what you do to something beyond yourself.

**For example**
On a flat Tuesday you do your short practice anyway. Afterwards you note one good thing you did, and you dedicate the day to someone else's wellbeing.

> **Principle 168** — Do not add to the impression

> "Say nothing more to thyself than what the first appearances report." (Med. VIII.49, [George Long translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.8.eight.html)).

Marcus's example distinguishes a report that someone has spoken ill of him from the further conclusion that he has been injured ([Meditations VIII.49](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.8.eight.html)).

Epictetus offers a smaller test: if someone bathes quickly, describe the speed rather than immediately deciding that they bathe badly, before understanding their reasons ([Enchiridion 45, Elizabeth Carter translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)). This is a discipline of examining what we accept, not a guarantee that every first impression is a bare, neutral fact; Epictetus treats impressions as candidates for acceptance or rejection ([Margaret Graver, Epictetus, section 4.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)).

Nor need restraint in judgment mean inaction: Marcus's next section advises turning aside from briars in the road rather than adding a complaint about their existence ([Meditations VIII.50](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.8.eight.html)). A present-day application can likewise distinguish questioning a sweeping conclusion from denying a problem that needs attention.

**Plain words** You do not have to wait until you feel calm to check what you know. A measured reply, a question or a protective action can leave room for both the evidence and the feeling.

*For example:* Suppose a flight delay means you miss a connection. The need to rebook is real; the thought that every future journey will fail goes beyond what this delay tells you.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Cognitive distortions research|Cognitive distortions research]]**: the Beck Institute's account of CBT describes evaluating distressing thoughts for realism while also solving problems; it does not reduce therapy to denying difficult circumstances ([Understanding CBT](https://beckinstitute.org/about/understanding-cbt/)).
- **[[Affect labeling|Affect labelling]]**: a laboratory fMRI study found lower amygdala responses to negative images during affect labelling than during other encoding tasks; that result is narrower than a promise that naming any emotion will relieve distress ([Lieberman and colleagues, 2007 abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01916.x)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The discipline of assent|The discipline of assent]]**: Epictetus makes reflection on impressions part of rational agency, with practice directed against hasty acceptance ([Epictetus, sections 4.1 and 5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)).
- **[[Two truths|Two truths (conventional and ultimate)]]**: Nāgārjuna's questions concern emptiness, inherent existence and conceptual conventions, not simply a division between neutral facts and emotional additions; the contrast should not be mistaken for an equivalence ([Jan Westerhoff, Nāgārjuna, sections 2 and 3.5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nagarjuna/)).

> **Principle 9** — Understand that affect is constructed, not discovered

The feeling you experience as "anger" or "fear" is not a hard-wired category triggered by the world. Your brain constructs emotional experiences from raw affect (valence + arousal), context, past learning, and available concepts. This is not a denial that feelings are real; it is a recognition that the categories you use shape the experience itself.

**Plain words** The feeling you experience as "anger" or "fear" isn't a hard-wired category triggered by the world — your brain constructs emotions from raw affect (valence + arousal), context, past learning, and available concepts. Affect is constructed, not discovered. Which means the construction is trainable.

*For example:* Two people get the same stomach-drop before the interview — one has the concept "panic attack," one has "adrenaline for performance" — and they have genuinely different experiences. Same raw material, different buildings.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Theory of constructed emotion|Theory of constructed emotion]]**, emotions are built from affect, concepts, and social reality; the brain does not contain pre-wired emotion circuits for each "basic" emotion (Barrett, 2017)
- **Concepts regulate perception**, emotion concepts act as predictions that shape the felt experience (Barrett et al., 2019)
- **This is a contested theory; basic emotion and appraisal accounts disagree**, and the debate is unresolved (Ekman, 2016; Siegel et al., 2018)

## Practice

- When you notice a feeling, remember you have latitude in what concept you apply. The same arousal can be "panic" or "readiness." Choose deliberately.

> **Principle 9** — Understand that affect is constructed, not discovered

Barrett's theory of constructed emotion can be put in one of her sentences: "An emotion is your brain's creation of what your bodily sensations mean, in relation to what is going on around you in the world." The theory distinguishes affect from emotion: "Affect is the general sense of feeling that you experience throughout each day. It is not emotion but a much simpler feeling" whose two features are pleasantness and arousal. And the construction reaches that simple layer itself: "Affect is your brain's best guess about the state of your body budget." On the raw material the brain categorizes: "When you experience affect with unpleasant valence and high arousal, you make meaning from it depending on how you categorize: Is it an emotional instance of fear? A physical instance of too much caffeine? A perception that the guy talking to you is a jerk?" Barrett's point is not that you can relabel feelings at will, but that "Categorization bestows new functions on biological signals," through your knowledge and the context rather than the signals' physical nature alone. And she is careful about what this does not deny: "Emotions are real, but not in the objective sense that molecules or neurons are real. They are real in the same sense that money is real — that is, hardly an illusion, but a product of human agreement." The compendium's application: the concepts you carry are part of how the experience itself is built. (The theory is contested; the parallels below track the debate.)

(Barrett, How Emotions Are Made, Introduction and chs. 4, 6.)

**Plain words** There is a layer of feeling underneath everything: how pleasant or unpleasant, how worked-up or calm. That layer is affect, and it stays simple. Barrett's claim is about what happens on top of it: the same jittery, unpleasant feeling can be categorized as fear, as too much caffeine, or as irritation at someone, depending on the concepts you have. The feeling you end up with is shaped by those concepts.

*For example:* A user slows down when a familiar wave of tension arrives and, before deciding what it is, asks what happened earlier in the day, what was eaten, and what is being avoided.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Theory of constructed emotion|Theory of constructed emotion]]**, emotions are built from affect, concepts, and social reality; the brain does not contain pre-wired emotion circuits for each "basic" emotion (Barrett, 2017)
- **Concepts regulate perception**, emotion concepts act as predictions that shape the felt experience (Barrett et al., 2019)
- **This is a contested theory; basic emotion and appraisal accounts disagree**, and the debate is unresolved (Ekman, 2016; Siegel et al., 2018)

## Practice

- When you notice a feeling, remember you have latitude in what concept you apply. The same arousal can be "panic" or "readiness." Choose deliberately.

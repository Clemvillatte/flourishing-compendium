> **Principle 9** — Understand that affect is constructed, not discovered

Lisa Feldman Barrett separates affect from emotion. Of affect she writes: "Affect is the general sense of feeling that you experience throughout each day. It is not emotion but a much simpler feeling" with two features, how pleasant or unpleasant you feel and how calm or agitated you feel. She adds: "Affect is your brain's best guess about the state of your body budget." Emotion is built on top of that: "An emotion is your brain's creation of what your bodily sensations mean, in relation to what is going on around you in the world" (Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made*, 2017; [text copy](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs379c/resources/inverted/content/Books_and_Journal_Articles_on_Consciousness/How_Emotions_are_Made_by_Lisa_Feldman_Barrett/How_Emotions_are_Made_by_Lisa_Feldman_Barrett.txt)).

> When you experience affect with unpleasant valence and high arousal, you make meaning from it depending on how you categorize: Is it an emotional instance of fear? A physical instance of too much caffeine? A perception that the guy talking to you is a jerk? (Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made*, 2017, [text copy](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs379c/resources/inverted/content/Books_and_Journal_Articles_on_Consciousness/How_Emotions_are_Made_by_Lisa_Feldman_Barrett/How_Emotions_are_Made_by_Lisa_Feldman_Barrett.txt))

She is not saying emotions are fake. Her words: "Emotions are real, but not in the objective sense that molecules or neurons are real. They are real in the same sense that money is real—that is, hardly an illusion, but a product of human agreement" (same book, [text copy](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs379c/resources/inverted/content/Books_and_Journal_Articles_on_Consciousness/How_Emotions_are_Made_by_Lisa_Feldman_Barrett/How_Emotions_are_Made_by_Lisa_Feldman_Barrett.txt)). The theory is contested, and the parallels below track the debate.

**Plain words** Underneath everything there is a plain layer of feeling: how pleasant or unpleasant, how worked-up or calm. Barrett's claim is about what comes next. The same jittery, unpleasant feeling can be taken as fear, as too much caffeine, or as irritation at someone, depending on the concepts you have. My application, which is mine and not Barrett's: the concepts you carry are part of how the experience gets built, so it is worth asking what else this feeling might be.

*For example (my illustration, in the spirit of Barrett's caffeine case):* A wave of tension arrives mid-afternoon. Before deciding what it is, you ask what you ate, how you slept, and what you have been putting off.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Theory of constructed emotion|Theory of constructed emotion]]**, emotions are built from affect, concepts, and social reality; the brain does not contain pre-wired emotion circuits for each "basic" emotion (Barrett, 2017)
- **Concepts regulate perception**, emotion concepts act as predictions that shape the felt experience (Barrett et al., 2019)
- **This is a contested theory; basic emotion and appraisal accounts disagree**, and the debate is unresolved (Ekman, 2016; Siegel et al., 2018)

## Practice

- When you notice a feeling, attend to the bodily sensations and the context around them before describing the experience.

> **Principle 30** — Use weak ties as a bridge

Granovetter's 1973 argument is about structure: "the degree of overlap of two individuals' friendship networks varies directly with the strength of their tie to one another", and he lays "stress ... on the cohesive power of weak ties" ([Granovetter 1973, American Journal of Sociology](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~golbeck/INST633o/granovetterTies.pdf)). Close friends tend to share friends and so hear the same news, while "those to whom we are weakly tied are more likely to move in circles different from our own and will thus have access to information different from that which we receive" (quoted from p. 1371 in [a Springer chapter on migrants' networks](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-94972-3_2)).

A 2022 experiment refines this. Across randomised tests on LinkedIn covering over 20 million people, "weaker ties increased job transmission but only to a point, after which there were diminishing marginal returns to tie weakness", and "weak ties increased job mobility in more digital industries, strong ties increased job mobility in less digital industries" ([Rajkumar et al. 2022, Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abl4476)). Moderately weak ties, roughly ten mutual connections, did best ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2022/weak-ties-linkedin-employment-0915)).

Weak ties also relate to feeling good: on days when students "interacted with more classmates than usual", they "experienced greater happiness and greater feelings of belonging" ([Sandstrom and Dunn 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167214529799)). This is a within-person association, and the ten-day intervention in Sandstrom's thesis found no broad change in happiness or belonging ([UBC thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/soa/cIRcle/collections/ubctheses/24/items/1.0074024)).



### Scope

The advice holds best for job and information searches in digital fields. I did not open Burt or Dunbar.

**Plain words**
Acquaintances can tell you things your close circle does not know. Keep some of them warm, especially people a step or two outside your inner circle.

**For example**
List five acquaintances in different fields. Send one a short note this week asking what they are working on, and ask for nothing.

## Practice

- Maintain casual contacts through regular, low-cost interactions (greetings, brief conversations). Use weak ties for information and opportunity; use strong ties for support and intimacy.

> **Principle 67** — Cultivate hope: it is not wishful thinking; it is agency plus pathway

Hope is not the passive wish that things will improve; it is the active combination of agency (I can do this) and pathways (there is a way). Hope is a distinct predictor of well-being and goal attainment.

**Plain words** Hope is not the passive wish that things improve — it's agency ("I can do this") plus pathways ("there is a way"). Both components, trainable. A distinct predictor of wellbeing and goal attainment. Cultivate hope like a muscle: find one pathway, take one action.

*For example:* The job seeker who "hopes something turns up" for a year — versus the one with three concrete pathways and a weekly quota of applications. Same word; one is weather, the other is architecture. The second one gets hired.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Hope|Hope theory (Snyder)]]**, hope is the combination of agency thinking and pathways thinking (Snyder, 2002)
- **Hope predicts goal attainment and well-being**, higher hope is associated with better academic, athletic, and health outcomes (Snyder et al., 2002)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Elpis (hope)|Elpis (hope)]]** (Hesiod, Works and Days; Plato, Gorgias)
- **The will to hope** (Gabriel Marcel, Homo Viator)
- **[[Praxis|Praxis as hope]]** (Ernest Bloch, The Principle of Hope)

## Practice

- When facing a difficulty, generate multiple pathways ("what are three different ways I could approach this?") and identify your agency ("what is one thing I can do today?").

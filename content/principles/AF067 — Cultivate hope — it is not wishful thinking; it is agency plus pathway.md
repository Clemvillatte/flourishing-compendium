> **Principle 67** — Cultivate hope: it is not wishful thinking; it is agency plus pathway

C. R. Snyder gives the definition the principle rests on: hope is "the perceived capability to derive pathways to desired goals, and motivate oneself via agency thinking to use those pathways" (Snyder 2002, p. 249, [PDF](https://www.tcpea.org/uploads/1/5/6/2/15622000/copy_of_hope_theory_rainbows_snyder_2002.pdf)). Pathways are the routes. Agency is the other half:

> "Agency thought—the perceived capacity to use one's pathways to reach desired goals—is the motivational component in hope theory." (Snyder 2002, p. 251)

Snyder treats the two as partners that "feed each other", and he describes what happens when one is missing. The mixed pattern of low pathways and high agency, he writes, "would entail active motivation that lies fallow without the necessary pathways thoughts" (p. 252). That is the nearest thing in his account to the title's wishful thinking, though he does not use that phrase.

**Plain words**
Hoping comes down to two questions. Can I see at least one way to get there? Do I believe I can follow it, and switch to another if it fails? Wanting something badly answers neither. Someone with plenty of drive and no route stays stuck, and someone with a route and no belief never starts, so check both before calling yourself hopeful.

*For example:* Say you want out of a job you dislike. Write down three routes (apply in a neighbouring field, ask a contact for an introduction, retrain in the evenings) and one step this week, such as sending two messages. This is an imagined case. Snyder notes that when a route is blocked, agency "helps people to channel the requisite motivation to the best alternate pathway" (p. 251).

- **[[Hope|Hope theory (Snyder)]]**, hope is the combination of agency thinking and pathways thinking (Snyder, 2002)
- **Hope and outcomes**, Snyder's abstract reports that higher hope is consistently related to better outcomes in academics, athletics, physical health, psychological adjustment and psychotherapy (Snyder, 2002)

## Practice

- When facing a difficulty, generate multiple pathways ("what are three different ways I could approach this?") and identify your agency ("what is one thing I can do today?").

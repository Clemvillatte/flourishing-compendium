> **Principle 15** — Nothing exists alone (interbeing)

Thich Nhat Hanh explains interbeing with a sheet of paper, in a short reading titled "Clouds in Each Paper" ([Nhat Hanh, "Clouds In Each Paper", Awakin.org](https://www.awakin.org/v2/read/view.php?tid=222)). He starts with the cloud: "Without a cloud, there will be no rain; without rain, the trees cannot grow: and without trees, we cannot make paper. The cloud is essential for the paper to exist." Then he coins the word.

> "'Interbeing' is a word that is not in the dictionary yet, but if we combine the prefix 'inter' with the verb 'to be', we have a new verb, inter-be. Without a cloud, we cannot have paper, so we can say that the cloud and the sheet of paper inter-are." (Thich Nhat Hanh, "Clouds In Each Paper", [Awakin.org](https://www.awakin.org/v2/read/view.php?tid=222))

He keeps looking and finds more in the page: the sunshine, the logger who cut the tree, the wheat that became the logger's bread, and "the logger's father and mother." His conclusion is plain: "When we look in this way we see that without all of these things, this sheet of paper cannot exist."

The paper is made of its conditions, and the cloud and the logger are among them. Reading the sheet as a self-contained object leaves most of what it is out of view.

**Plain words** Pick up any object near you and ask what had to be true for it to be here. Name three things, such as the materials, the people who made it and the place it came from. Nhat Hanh's reading asks you to see the same about yourself.

*For example:* Imagine holding a cup of tea and listing what is in it: water that fell as rain, leaves someone picked, a kettle someone made. The list can be done in a minute, and it changes how the cup looks. This is my illustration of his practice, not his own example.

> **Principle 15** — Nothing exists alone (interbeing)

Thich Nhat Hanh's teaching on interbeing does not begin with a doctrine; it begins with a sheet of paper. "If you are a poet, you will see clearly that there is a cloud floating in this sheet of paper" ([Nhat Hanh, The Sun My Heart, Awakin.org](https://www.awakin.org/v2/read/view.php?tid=222)). The argument is ordinary: no cloud, no rain; no rain, no trees; no trees, no paper. The cloud is essential to the paper's existence. He then adds the logger, the wheat that fed him, and the logger's parents — and the paper stops being a simple object. "'Interbeing' is a word that is not in the dictionary yet, but if we combine the prefix 'inter' with the verb 'to be', we have a new verb, inter-be."

This is not a mystical claim; it is a claim about what a thing is. The paper is not a self-contained item that happens to have a supply chain; the cloud, the rain and the logger are inside what the paper is. The practice is not to feel vague unity — it is to look at an object long enough to see what is actually in it, and to see yourself in the same way.

> "'Interbeing' is a word that is not in the dictionary yet, but if we combine the prefix 'inter' with the verb 'to be', we have a new verb, inter-be. Without a cloud, we cannot have paper, so we can say that the cloud and the sheet of paper inter-are." (Thich Nhat Hanh, The Sun My Heart, [Awakin.org](https://www.awakin.org/v2/read/view.php?tid=222))

**Plain words** Every object you touch is a compressed record of everything that had to exist for it to arrive. The coffee contains rain, a farmer, a road and someone's 4 a.m.; the phone contains a mine, a factory and a person who did not go home for three weeks. This is not sentimentality; it is accurate inventory. The delusion is not caring too much about things — the delusion is treating them as simple. Look at one thing you own and name what is actually inside it.

*For example:* Two people spill the same cup of coffee. One treats it as an inconvenience and mops it up. The other, having actually seen the web inside the cup — the rain, the farmer, the trucker, the barista — pauses for a half-second. Same spill; one of them is holding a much heavier object.

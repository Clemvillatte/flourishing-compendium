> **Principle 53** — Learn to notice and name attentional states: labelling improves control

The review that consolidated the labelling research:

> Putting feelings into words, or "affect labeling," can attenuate our emotional experiences.
>
> (Torre and Lieberman, "Putting Feelings Into Words: Affect Labeling as Implicit Emotion Regulation", Emotion Review, 2018, abstract)

The review's central claim is that labelling works as regulation even when it does not feel like it: "research investigating affect labeling has found it produces a pattern of effects like those seen during explicit emotion regulation, suggesting affect labeling is a form of implicit emotion regulation", and "intent to regulate one's emotions is not required for affect labeling to be effective and can operate even without a regulatory goal present" ([Torre and Lieberman 2018](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/651b09f505bc433349d85ab7/t/651d2f2843e6d165beeccb23/1696411432954/Torre(2018)ER.pdf)). The neural finding: "Affect labeling has been shown to elicit increased vlPFC and decreased amygdala activity compared to other conditions which also require processing an evocative stimulus but importantly do not require labeling affect", and a meta-analysis "reported labeling emotions observed within evocative stimuli yields significantly decreased odds of amygdala activity relative to passively viewing those stimuli" ([Torre and Lieberman 2018](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/651b09f505bc433349d85ab7/t/651d2f2843e6d165beeccb23/1696411432954/Torre(2018)ER.pdf)).

The noticing side comes from the meta-awareness account already opened in this compendium: "meta-awareness directly aids in the identification of mind-wandering lapses, and the subsequent re-engagement of the primary task" ([Schooler et al. 2011](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2025-12/perceptual-decoupling.pdf)).

Scope: the labelling studies measured affective states and evocative stimuli. The compendium's application extends the same practice to attentional states, labelling a caught state ("focused", "distracted", "wandering") as the note-taking step that Schooler's account treats as the operative event.

(Torre and Lieberman, Emotion Review, 2018; Schooler et al., Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2011.)

**Plain words**
The finding: naming an emotional state in words attenuates the experience, and the naming works without any intent to regulate. The studies measured affect; the extension of the practice to attentional states is the compendium's application. Name the state you catch, then decide what to do.

*For example:*
Imagined: While scrolling, a reader says to herself, "I'm doomscrolling." She then decides whether to continue or close the app.

## Practice

- Practise labelling attentional states: "focused," "distracted," "wandering," "restless." The label creates a gap between you and the state, enabling choice rather than automatic reaction.

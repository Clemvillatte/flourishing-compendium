> **Principle 53** — Learn to notice and name attentional states: labelling improves control

The capacity to notice and label your attentional state ("I am distracted," "I am focused," "I am mind-wandering") improves attentional control. Labelling creates distance and enables choice.

**Plain words** Name the state, change the state: "I'm distracted," "I'm gripped," "I'm foggy" — labelling an attentional mode gives you a handle on it. Unnamed states run you; named ones report to you.

*For example:* "I'm doomscrolling" said out loud — and the naming alone loosens much of the spell. That's why it works. Labels are levers.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Affect labeling|Affect labelling]] reduces amygdala response**, naming a state reduces its neural impact (Lieberman et al., 2007; Torre & Lieberman, 2018)
- **Meta-awareness and labelling improve attentional control**, noticing and naming states improves the ability to regulate them (Smallwood & Schooler, 2015; Mrazek et al., 2013)

## Practice

- Practise labelling attentional states: "focused," "distracted," "wandering," "restless." The label creates a gap between you and the state, enabling choice rather than automatic reaction.

> **Principle 10** — Understand that motivation has a physiological substrate: energy and health matter

The clearest evidence that motivation rides on the body comes from illness. Dantzer and colleagues review how infection changes behaviour. Sick people "lose interest in their physical and social environments", "tire easily" and have fragmented sleep. These changes are driven by immune signals that "act on the brain to cause the aforementioned behavioural symptoms of sickness". The review adds that sickness behaviour "is an expression of a previously unrecognized motivational state", one that reorganises "perceptions and actions to enable ill individuals to cope better with an infection". The similarity between sickness behaviour and depression is "striking": "a withdrawal from the physical and social environment that is accompanied by pain, malaise and decreased reactivity to reward (anhedonia)" (Dantzer et al., 2008, [Nature Reviews Neuroscience 9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2919277/)).

Two further lines of evidence come from reviews I opened only as abstracts. Alhola and Polo-Kantola review sleep deprivation and cognitive performance ([abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19300585/)). Ratey and Loehr review how physical activity influences "frontal lobe-mediated cognitive processes, such as planning, scheduling, inhibition, and working memory" ([abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21417955/)). Both concern cognition, not motivation itself, so they support the principle only indirectly.

**Plain words**
Willpower is partly a bodily matter. When you are ill, sleep-starved or run down, your drive falls, and that is part of how the body copes. Before concluding you have lost your motivation, check sleep and illness first.

**For example**
After a week of bad sleep you stop wanting to do things you normally enjoy. The sensible first step is a few early nights, not a pep talk.

## Psychology parallels

- **Sleep deprivation reduces motivation**, sleep loss impairs goal-directed behaviour and increases procrastination (Alhola & Polo-Kantola, 2007; Engle-Friedman, 2017)
- **Exercise increases motivation**, physical activity increases dopamine and motivation (Ratey & Loehr, 2011; Knutson et al., 2012)
- **Illness and inflammation reduce motivation**, sickness behaviour includes reduced motivation (Dantzer et al., 2008; Eisenberger et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Before blaming your motivation, check the body: sleep, food, movement, illness. Often the fastest way to restore motivation is to sleep, eat, or move.

> **Principle 18** — Recognise the difference between needs and wants: not all desires are equal

Self-determination theory names three needs. Ryan and Deci report "the postulate of three innate psychological needs--competence, autonomy, and relatedness--which when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being" ([Ryan & Deci, 2000, American Psychologist 55, 68-78](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/)). Health and safety, which the page also lists as needs, are not part of that list.

Epicurus drew an older line between desires ([Principal Doctrines 29](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/princdoc.html), MIT Classics):

> Of our desires some are natural and necessary others are natural, but not necessary; others, again, are neither natural nor necessary, but are due to illusory opinion.

A large meta-analysis supports one link between wants and needs. Across 259 samples, materialism "was associated with significantly lower well-being", and "mediation analyses suggested that the negative link may be explained by poor psychological need satisfaction" ([Dittmar et al., 2014, JPSP 107, 879-924](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347131/)). The effect sizes were modest (r around -.2 for the main measures), and the data are correlational.

(Ryan & Deci, 2000; Epicurus, Principal Doctrines 29; Dittmar et al., 2014.)

**Plain words** A short list of needs sits under many wants. When a want feels urgent, ask which need it might serve. The research suggests, with modest effect sizes, that a pile of materialist wants goes with lower well-being partly because needs go unmet.

For example (imagined): "I need this promotion" may mean "I need security, and I want the promotion", which opens other routes to security.

## Practice

- When you notice a strong want, ask: "what need is underneath this?" Often a want for achievement, possessions, or approval is a substitute for unmet needs for autonomy, competence, or relatedness. Address the need.

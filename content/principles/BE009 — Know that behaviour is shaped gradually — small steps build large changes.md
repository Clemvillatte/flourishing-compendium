> **Principle 9** — Know that behaviour is shaped gradually: small steps build large changes

Behavioural psychology gives gradual change both a mechanism and a test. The mechanism, shaping, reinforces successive approximations toward a complex behaviour. The test is Bandura and Schunk's experiment on proximal subgoals: children with serious deficits and disinterest in mathematics pursued a self-directed learning program under either proximal subgoals, distal goals, or no goals. The abstract reports, verbatim:

> "Under proximal subgoals, children progressed rapidly in self-directed learning, achieved substantial mastery of mathematical operations, and developed a sense of personal efficacy and intrinsic interest in arithmetic activities that initially held little attraction for them. Distal goals had no demonstrable effects." (Bandura and Schunk, 1981, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 41(3), 586-598, [Digital Greensboro record](https://www.digitalgreensboro.org/record/8817?v=pdf))

Read carefully, this is a comparison of goal structures, not of step sizes: near subgoals worked where distant aims produced nothing demonstrable. The compendium's application is the graded plan: set the next step close enough to reach and to gauge. The study does not show that tiny steps are always optimal; it shows near goals beat far ones in this setting.

**Plain words** Nobody leaps to a complex behaviour; they are shaped: small versions reinforced, then slightly bigger, then bigger. Start embarrassingly small and raise the bar only as fast as the last step holds. The progression is the technique; the smallness at step one is a feature.

*For example:* Push-ups starting from the knees, a run starting at one minute. The plan is not to stay there, and the plan is what matters. Gradual is how anything big actually gets built.

## Practice

- Shape behaviour: start with small, achievable steps and gradually increase. "Walk 5 minutes" → "walk 10 minutes" → "walk 30 minutes." Do not attempt large changes immediately.

> **Principle 9** — Know that behaviour is shaped gradually: small steps build large changes

Behaviour change is incremental. Shaping (reinforcing successive approximations) builds complex behaviours from small components. Starting small and building gradually is more effective than attempting large changes.

**Plain words** Nobody leaps to a complex behaviour; they're shaped — small versions reinforced, then slightly bigger, then bigger. Start embarrassingly small and build. The progression is the technique; the smallness at step one is a feature.

*For example:* Push-ups starting from knees, a "run" starting at one minute — the plan is not to stay there, and the plan is what matters. Gradual is how anything big actually gets built.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Shaping (Skinner)|Shaping (Skinner)]]**, complex behaviours are built by reinforcing successive approximations (Skinner, 1953; Ferster & Skinner, 1957)
- **Graded task assignment**, breaking tasks into graded steps increases completion (Bandura & Schunk, 1981; Beck, 1976)

## Philosophy parallels

- **A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step** (Laozi, Daodejing 64)
- **[[Gradual training|The gradual training]]** (Buddhism, Gradual Training Sutta)
- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean: small steps, large vision]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)

## Practice

- Shape behaviour: start with small, achievable steps and gradually increase. "Walk 5 minutes" → "walk 10 minutes" → "walk 30 minutes." Do not attempt large changes immediately.

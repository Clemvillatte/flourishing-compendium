> **Principle 414** — Practise thrownness (Geworfenheit)

Heidegger introduces the term in Being and Time, section 29, as a feature of how a person finds themselves already placed in a world (Macquarrie and Robinson translation, [Being and Time, H. 135](https://dhspriory.org/kenny/PhilTexts/Heidegger/BeingTime04.doc)):

> we call it the "thrownness" of this entity into its "there"; indeed, it is thrown in such a way that, as Being-in-the-world, it is the "there". The expression "thrownness" is meant to suggest the facticity of its being delivered over.

For Heidegger this is a structure of existing, not a list of unlucky circumstances. The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/) summarises the ethical edge: "I become myself by first owning the fact that I can escape neither from my thrownness, nor from the lack of justification for my existence that comes with that thrownness." The claim that you can "choose how to respond" matches his pairing of thrownness with projection onto possibilities, which the encyclopedia describes as a balance of determination and freedom ([fall 2017 edition](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2017/entries/heidegger/)). Heidegger does not offer it as a coping technique.

(Heidegger, Being and Time, section 29.)

**Plain words** You start from a language, a family and a period you did not pick. Heidegger's point is that no one can step outside that starting place, and owning it is where self-directed action begins.

For example (imagined): someone raised in a household where money was never discussed does not get to start from a blank slate with money. Seeing that clearly comes before deciding what to do about it.

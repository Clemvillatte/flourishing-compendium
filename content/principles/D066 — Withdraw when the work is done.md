> **Principle 66** — Withdraw when the work is done

This is heaven's way: complete the task, step back, don't linger for applause. Ending well is half the work.

(DDJ 9.)

### What the chapter says

Chapter 9 treats the moment of success as a hazard rather than a reward:

> "When the work is done, and one's name is becoming distinguished, to withdraw into obscurity is the way of Heaven." (DDJ 9, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The earlier lines of the same chapter make the case with images of overreach:

> "When gold and jade fill the hall, their possessor cannot keep them safe. When wealth and honours lead to arrogancy, this brings its evil on itself." (DDJ 9, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The overfilled vessel and the over-sharpened point carry the same warning: keeping at a thing past its proper point ruins it.

**Plain words** The chapter treats the moment of success as a hazard rather than a reward. Completing work makes you visible, and visibility invites both arrogance in you and appetite in others. Withdrawing while the work still stands well protects the work, because what follows a peak is usually decline unless you choose the exit yourself. Ending is part of the task, and the guest who leaves at the right hour leaves the party intact.

*For example:* The guest who leaves at the perfect moment is remembered fondly; the one who stays until the host yawns is remembered too.

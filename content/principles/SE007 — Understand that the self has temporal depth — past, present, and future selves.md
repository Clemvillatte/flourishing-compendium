> **Principle 7** — Understand that the self has temporal depth: past, present, and future selves

You have a past self (who you were), a present self (who you are), and future selves (who you could become). These are related but distinct. Future selves (possible selves) are motivational; past selves are narrative; the present self is experiential.

**Plain words** You have a past self, a present self, and future selves — related but distinct, and the future ones are motivational: possible selves drive effort and persistence. Get to know your future self; they're the one your current choices are writing.

*For example:* The person who can't save a dollar for the stranger called "me at 70" — and the exercise that closes the distance: writing a specific day-in-the-life of the 70-year-old. The pension contributions follow the acquaintance. Vagueness was the thief.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Possible selves|Possible selves (Markus & Nurius)]]**, future self-representations (hopes, fears, expectations) motivate behaviour (Markus & Nurius, 1986; Oyserman et al., 2004)
- **[[Temporal self-appraisal theory (Wilson & Ross)|Temporal self-appraisal theory (Wilson & Ross)]]**, people evaluate past and future selves in ways that favour the present self (Wilson & Ross, 2003)
- **[[The end-of-history illusion|The end-of-history illusion]]**, people believe they have changed a lot in the past but will change little in the future (Quoidbach et al., 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eternal recurrence|Eternal recurrence]]** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science, section 341)
- **Becoming** (Nietzsche, Thus Spoke Zarathustra)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life across time]]** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Attend to your possible selves: who could you become? What future self would you be proud to be? Use this as motivation. Also, remember that you will change more than you expect.

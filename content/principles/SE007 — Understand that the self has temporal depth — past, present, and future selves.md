> **Principle 7** — Understand that the self has temporal depth: past, present, and future selves

Markus and Nurius describe possible selves as the part of self-knowledge about one's own potential:

> "Possible selves are the ideal selves that we would very much like to become. They are also the selves we could become, and the selves we are afraid of becoming." (Markus & Nurius, 1986, p. 954)

In their account these imagined selves give hopes and fears a personal form: they "function as incentives for future behavior" and "provide an evaluative and interpretive context for the current view of self" (Markus & Nurius, 1986, p. 954).

Expectations of change run unevenly. Quoidbach and colleagues asked more than 19,000 adults aged 18 to 68 about past and expected change in personality, values and preferences; across age groups, participants believed they had changed substantially but would change relatively little in the future, an error the authors named the end-of-history illusion (Quoidbach et al., 2013, abstract). People "regard the present as a watershed moment at which they have finally become the person they will be for the rest of their lives."

Hershfield and colleagues tested whether contact with age-progressed renderings of oneself could influence financial choices. Across four studies, participants who interacted with future-self renderings "exhibited an increased tendency to accept later monetary rewards over immediate ones" (Hershfield et al., 2011, abstract).

**Plain words**
Someone can remember wanting a very different life ten years ago and still assume today's wishes will last. Planning needs room for that same possibility of change. A hoped-for future can give present efforts a direction without becoming a fixed prediction.

*For example:* Before spending a windfall, someone compares a purchase they want now with keeping money available for later life. They write a possible ordinary day at seventy and consider what they might want then, while recognizing that the picture could change.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Possible selves|Possible selves (Markus & Nurius)]]**, future self-representations (hopes, fears, expectations) motivate behaviour (Markus & Nurius, 1986; Oyserman et al., 2004)
- **[[Temporal self-appraisal theory (Wilson & Ross)|Temporal self-appraisal theory (Wilson & Ross)]]**, people evaluate past and future selves in ways that favour the present self (Wilson & Ross, 2003)
- **[[The end-of-history illusion|The end-of-history illusion]]**, people believe they have changed a lot in the past but will change little in the future (Quoidbach et al., 2013)

## Practice

- Attend to your possible selves: who could you become? What future self would you be proud to be? Use this as motivation. Also, remember that you will change more than you expect.

> **Principle 36** — Train contentment as a skill

The second observance (santosha) is defined as the decision to want what you have; the tradition calls the joy that follows "supreme happiness," not as reward but as definition: contentment is the happiness that does not depend on the next event. Practically, practice contentment at set times (before meals, at day's end) rather than only when circumstances oblige it.

(YS 2.42; the Daoist zhizu principles run parallel.)

**Plain words** Contentment is defined as the decision to want what you have — and the joy that follows is called "supreme happiness," not as reward but as definition: the happiness that doesn't depend on the next event. Train it as a skill, not a mood.

*For example:* Two people with the same apartment, same job, same year: one is waiting for the upgrade that makes life good; the other decided to want this. Only one of them is home.

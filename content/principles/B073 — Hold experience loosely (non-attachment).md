> **Principle 73** — Hold experience loosely (non-attachment)

The Dhammapada runs one argument through four adjacent verses, from the dear, through affection, to attachment: from each springs grief and fear in the one who holds it. Shantideva draws the conclusion in the Bodhicharyavatara: the wise do not become attached, because fear arises from attachment, and the objects of attachment pass away by themselves. (Dhammapada 212; Shantideva.)

> "From endearment springs grief, from endearment springs fear. For one who is wholly free from endearment there is no grief, whence then fear?" (Dhammapada 212, [Buddharakkhita, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.16.budd.html))

> "Thus, the wise do not become attached, for fear arises from attachment. These [objects of attachment] pass away by themselves, so be firm and know this." (Shantideva, Bodhicharyavatara 8.19, [Wallace translation](https://thuvienhoasen.org/images/file/EUaZhZ1G0QgQANVp/shantideva-bodhicaryavatara-wallace.pdf))

The verses are addressed to people who love; the instruction concerns the clutch. In the compendium's reading, the bird image is about pressure: neither crush nor release, hold with a grip the thing can survive.

**Plain words**
Pick one thing you are holding tightly and run the verse's test on it: what grief and fear are already priced into the grip? Try loosening by one notch, keeping the holding, and notice what happens to the fear. The practice is calibration, and calibration only ever moves one notch at a time.

*For example:* Imagine a parent deciding, after an argument, to stop checking the teenager's messages. The care is unchanged; what changes is what the care is allowed to do.

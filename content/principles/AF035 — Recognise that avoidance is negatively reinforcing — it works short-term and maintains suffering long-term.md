> **Principle 35** — Recognise that avoidance is negatively reinforcing: it works short-term and maintains suffering long-term

Avoiding a feared situation reduces anxiety immediately, which reinforces the avoidance. But avoidance prevents the extinction of fear and maintains the anxiety disorder. This is one of the most robust findings in affect science and clinical psychology.

**Plain words** Avoidance is negatively reinforcing: it reduces anxiety immediately (reward!) and therefore strengthens itself — while preventing the extinction of the fear. Works short-term, maintains the suffering long-term. Every avoidance is a donation to the anxiety.

*For example:* The skipped party that brings instant relief — and the slightly larger dread next time, and the slightly smaller world after a year. The avoidance was never neutral; it was compounding.
## Psychology parallels

- **Avoidance maintains anxiety**, preventing avoidance is the core mechanism of exposure therapy (Craske et al., 2014)
- **Experiential avoidance is associated with worse mental health**, the unwillingness to experience internal states is a transdiagnostic risk factor (Kashdan et al., 2010; Levin et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The path of least resistance is the path of suffering** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **[[Courage|Courage as the mastery of fear]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III)
- **[[Amor fati|Amor fati]]** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)

## Practice

- When you notice yourself avoiding something because of how it makes you feel, this is a signal. The avoidance is making the fear stronger, not weaker.

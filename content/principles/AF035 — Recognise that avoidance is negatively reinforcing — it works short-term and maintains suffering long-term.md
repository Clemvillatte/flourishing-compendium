> **Principle 35** — Recognise that avoidance is negatively reinforcing: it works short-term and maintains suffering long-term

Repeatedly avoiding a feared situation to escape the anxiety it provokes can bring immediate relief, and that relief can reinforce the avoidance. Avoidance of reasonably safe situations may also remove opportunities to learn whether what was feared actually happens. Recognise the pattern when it appears.

### The mechanism

The cycle, stated plainly in the exposure-therapy literature:

> "Avoidance behaviors are then negatively reinforced by the temporary relief of anxiety or perception of control." — Petri, Cullum & Hunnicutt-Ferguson 2022 ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10172524/))

Temporary relief can reinforce avoidance; repeated avoidance may also remove opportunities to learn whether feared predictions occur. Relief is the payment; the avoidance is what gets bought.

### What it blocks

Exposure therapy is "repeated approach toward fear provoking stimuli" ([Craske et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4114726/)), and avoidance, on that account, "can interfere with new learning by preventing any violation in expectancy" (Craske et al. 2014). The article makes a similar point about safety signals, which differ from avoidance: they "alleviate distress in the short term, but when they are no longer present, the fear returns" (Craske et al. 2014).

**Plain words** Avoidance often pays on the spot, in relief. The trade is the relief now for the unanswered question later: what would have happened if you had gone? The question stays closed as long as the avoidance keeps it closed. Noticing that trade is the skill.

*For example:* Someone who dreads large gatherings cancels each invitation as it comes. Each cancellation brings relief, and each one keeps untested the prediction that the evening would be unbearable.

## Psychology parallels

- **Avoidance maintains anxiety**, preventing avoidance is the core mechanism of exposure therapy (Craske et al., 2014)
- **Experiential avoidance is associated with worse mental health**, the unwillingness to experience internal states is a transdiagnostic risk factor (Kashdan et al., 2010; Levin et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The path of least resistance is the path of suffering** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **[[Courage|Courage as the mastery of fear]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III)
- **[[Amor fati|Amor fati]]** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)

## Practice

- When you notice yourself avoiding something because of how it makes you feel, this is a signal. The avoidance is making the fear stronger, not weaker.

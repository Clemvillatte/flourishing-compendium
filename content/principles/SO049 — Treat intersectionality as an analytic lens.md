> **Principle 49** — Treat intersectionality as an analytic lens

Treat intersectionality — the analytic framework that examines how multiple group memberships (race, gender, class, sexuality, disability) interact to shape experience and outcomes — as a lens. Kimberlé Crenshaw (1989) introduced the term to capture how Black women's experience was not captured by either race or gender alone. The adaptive stance is to see the person at the intersection, not as a sum of categories.

**Plain words** People sit at intersections — race, gender, class, disability — and the combinations have their own effects, not just the sum of parts. A lens, not a hierarchy of hardships: the point is precision about how experience actually works.

*For example:* "Women face X and Black people face Y" — and the Black woman in the meeting faces neither exactly, but a third thing with its own address. The intersection is real real estate; it's where specific experiences live.

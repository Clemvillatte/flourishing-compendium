> **Principle 11** — Read joy and sadness as power signals

Joy is the passage from a lesser to a greater perfection, sadness the passage from a greater to a lesser. This is Spinoza's geometry of mood: affects are not colors painted on experience but velocity vectors, readings of whether your power of acting is rising or falling. The diagnostic use is immediate. Joy that leaves you weaker in the aftermath was mislabeled by someone; sadness that quietly strengthens you may be growth. Practically, judge each affect by its residue over days, not by its first taste.

(Ethics III, P11 scholium; P6; definitions of Joy and Sadness, Part III end.)

**Plain words** Joy is the passage from lesser to greater perfection; sadness the passage from greater to lesser. Affects are velocity vectors, not colours — readings of whether your power of acting is rising or falling. Track the vector, not just the mood.

*For example:* Someone rates their days by how they felt — and starts rating them by direction: is my capacity rising or falling this week? The flat-but-improving week turns out to be the good one; the pleasant-but-collapsing week was the trap.

> **Principle 11** — Read joy and sadness as power signals

Spinoza defines pleasure as the transition of a man from a less to a greater perfection, and pain as the transition from a greater to a less. ([Ethics III, Definitions of the Emotions, II and III, Elwes tr.](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)) In the note to Part III, Proposition 11, he explains that these passive states of transition are what the emotions of pleasure and pain are, and that the mind can pass sometimes to a state of greater perfection, sometimes to a state of lesser perfection. ([Ethics III, Prop. XI, note, Elwes tr.](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800)) The compendium's application is to read each affect as a reading of whether your power of acting is rising or falling, and to judge each affect by its residue over days rather than by its first taste.

> "II. Pleasure is the transition of a man from a less to a greater perfection. III. Pain is the transition of a man from a greater to a less perfection." (Ethics III, Definitions of the Emotions, Elwes tr., [Gutenberg #3800](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

**Plain words** Mood is a speedometer here, not a painting. The question is not whether today felt good, but whether your capacity is rising or falling. A flat week that ends more capable than it started beats a pleasant week that ends drained.

**For example (imagined):** Someone who rates their days by how they felt starts rating them by direction instead: is my capacity rising or falling this week? The flat-but-improving week turns out to be the one to keep.

> **Principle 288** — Practise whanaungatanga

Te Aka Māori Dictionary defines whanaungatanga as "a relationship through shared experiences and working together which provides people with a sense of belonging". It "develops as a result of kinship rights and obligations, which also serve to strengthen each member of the kin group", and "also extends to others to whom one develops a close familial, friendship or reciprocal relationship" ([Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/10068)). A National Ethics Advisory Committee overview of Māori research ethics adds that it "includes relationships to non-kin who have become kin-like through shared experience and connection", and requires "the culturally appropriate identification of relationships, engagement, connectedness and commitment to others" ([NEAC overview](https://neac.health.govt.nz/assets/Uploads/NEAC/publications/neac-maori-research-ethics-an-overview-2012.doc)).

It is also something one does. A kaupapa Māori mental-health report describes it as "the direct and deliberate action of forming relationships" and says that as a practice it "acknowledges that relationships are at the heart of improving Māori health" ([Awa Association report, 2020](https://www.health.govt.nz/system/files/2020-02/awa-association-report-14feb2020.pdf)). Te Ara notes that kin bonds "extend well beyond the nuclear family", and that whanaungatanga "may decide a particular course of action" ([Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/maori-manners-and-social-behaviour-nga-mahi-tika/page-2)).

I did not open Durie (1994) or Mead (2003), so I rely on the sources above.

**Plain words**
Belonging is built through shared work, time and obligations, and it can reach beyond blood relatives. Make relationships on purpose, wherever you spend your days. People usually belong more fully to a team or street when they have done things together there.

**For example**
A new employee offers to help a colleague with a deadline and later joins the team lunch. Over months, shared effort turns a workplace into a place she belongs.

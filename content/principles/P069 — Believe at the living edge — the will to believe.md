> **Principle 69** — Believe at the living edge: the will to believe

Where evidence is genuinely insufficient and the choice is forced and momentous, you have the right to believe what activates your capacities. Faith in a possibility can help create it.

(James, The Will to Believe, 1896.)

**Plain words** Believing you can finish the marathon is partly how marathons get finished. Where evidence is genuinely insufficient and the choice is forced and momentous, you have the right to believe what activates your capacities.

*For example:* Nobody could prove the startup would work — and founders who believed it did the thousand unprovable tasks that made it true. The belief was a tool, not a conclusion.

## Primary source (verbatim)

> "Our passional nature not only lawfully may, but must, decide an option between propositions, whenever it is a genuine option that cannot by its nature be decided on intellectual grounds; for to say, under such circumstances, "Do not decide, but leave the question open," is itself a passional decision,—just like deciding yes or no,—and is attended with the same risk of losing the truth."
> — William James, "The Will to Believe," in The Will to Believe and Other Essays in Popular Philosophy (Longmans, Green, 1896).

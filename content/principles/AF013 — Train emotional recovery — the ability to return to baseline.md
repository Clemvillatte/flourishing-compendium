> **Principle 13** — Train emotional recovery: the ability to return to baseline

Resilience is not the absence of negative affect; it is the ability to return to baseline after a setback. This is a distinct, trainable capacity. People who recover quickly from negative affect are less likely to develop depression.

**Plain words** Resilience is not the absence of negative affect — it's the ability to return to baseline after a setback. A distinct, trainable capacity. Train emotional recovery: the return, not the never-falling, is the skill.

*For example:* Two colleagues get the same rejection: one spirals for a month, one is functional by Thursday — not because they felt less, but because the return was trained. The rebound is the muscle; it responds to reps.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Resilience|Resilience as trajectory, not trait]]**, most people exposed to potentially traumatic events show a resilient trajectory, not a pathological one (Bonanno, 2004)
- **Emotional inertia as a depression risk factor**, slow recovery from negative affect predicts depressive symptoms prospectively (Kuppens et al., 2012)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Premeditatio malorum|Premeditatio malorum]]** (Stoics, Seneca, Letters)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as non-resistance]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 64)

## Practice

- After a negative event, deliberately do something that has historically helped you recover (walk, connect with a friend, sleep, exercise) rather than waiting for the feeling to pass on its own.

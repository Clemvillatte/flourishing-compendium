> **Principle 59** — Engage in narrative arts

Reading fiction, watching films, attending theatre, and engaging with narrative arts build empathy, perspective-taking, and social cognition. The mechanism is not instruction but simulation: the reader or viewer enters the perspective of characters whose experiences differ from their own. Research on narrative empathy (Mar and Oatley) shows that fiction reading improves social cognition, though the effect sizes are modest and the direction of causation is debated.

(Mar and Oatley, 2008; Kidd and Castano, 2013, with the caveat that the "reading fiction improves empathy" finding has had replication failures.)

**Plain words** Fiction, film and theatre build empathy by simulation: you inhabit lives unlike your own. The effect is modest but real, and it works through being drawn in, not through being told what to think.

*For example:* Reading a novel set in a war you only knew from headlines, and finding you can imagine the people in it.

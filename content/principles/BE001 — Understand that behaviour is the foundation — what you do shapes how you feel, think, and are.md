> **Principle 1** — Understand that behaviour is the foundation: what you do shapes how you feel, think, and are

Behaviour is usually read as the output of inner states. It also works as a causal input. What you do changes how you feel, what you think, and who you become, and the treatment evidence for this is unusually direct.

In Jacobson and colleagues' dismantling study of cognitive therapy for depression, 150 outpatients with major depression were randomly assigned to a treatment focused exclusively on the behavioural activation component, a treatment adding automatic-thought work, or the full cognitive therapy package. The abstract reports the result, verbatim:

> "Despite excellent adherence to treatment protocols by the therapists, a clear bias favoring CT, and the competent performance of CT, there was no evidence that the complete treatment produced better outcomes, at either the termination of acute treatment or the 6-month follow-up, than either component treatment." (Jacobson, Dobson, Truax, Addis, Koerner, Gollan, Gortner and Prince, 1996, Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology 64(2), 295-304, [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8871414/))

The same study found that the behavioural component alone altered negative thinking and dysfunctional attributional styles as well as the full package did. What patients did changed what they thought. Depression lifted without any prior repair of inner states; activity scheduling carried the work.

**Plain words** You can wait to feel like a runner and then run, or run and let the feeling get dragged along behind. The lever works from the outside in: behaviour is the cheapest input to mood and identity there is. The Jacobson result is the clinical version of that: activity alone moved the thinking.

*For example:* The person who "isn't a morning person" who starts walking at 7 a.m. anyway: three weeks in, the body starts waking at 6:50 without an alarm. The behaviour taught the identity.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Behavioral activation|Behavioural activation (BA)]]**, activity scheduling improves depression even when motivation is absent (Jacobson et al., 1996; Martell et al., 2001)
- **Action precedes motivation**, behaviour generates motivation rather than requiring it (Jacobson et al., 1996; Dimidjian et al., 2011)
- **BA is as effective as full cognitive therapy for depression**, the behavioural component alone accounts for much of CBT's effectiveness (Jacobson et al., 1996; Dimidjian et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as habituated action]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **Existence precedes essence: you are what you do** (Sartre, Existentialism is a Humanism)
- **[[Action over intention|Action over intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path: Right Action)

## Practice

- Do not wait to feel motivated to act. Act first; the feeling follows.

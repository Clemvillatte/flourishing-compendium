> **Principle 1** — Understand that behaviour is the foundation: what you do shapes how you feel, think, and are

Behaviour is not merely the output of inner states; it is a causal input. What you do changes how you feel, what you think, and who you become. This is the foundational insight of behavioural psychology: behaviour is not just the result of motivation and cognition; it is a driver of them.

**Plain words** Action isn't just the output of your inner life — it's an input to it. You don't wait to feel like a runner and then run; you run and the feeling gets dragged along behind. Behaviour is the cheapest lever on mood and identity there is.

*For example:* The person who "isn't a morning person" who starts walking at 7 a.m. anyway — three weeks in, the body starts waking at 6:50 without an alarm. The behaviour taught the identity.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Behavioral activation|Behavioural activation (BA)]]**, activity scheduling improves depression even when motivation is absent (Jacobson et al., 1996; Martell et al., 2001)
- **Action precedes motivation**, behaviour generates motivation rather than requiring it (Jacobson et al., 1996; Dimidjian et al., 2011)
- **BA is as effective as full cognitive therapy for depression**, the behavioural component alone accounts for much of CBT's effectiveness (Jacobson et al., 1996; Dimidjian et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as habituated action]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **Existence precedes essence: you are what you do** (Sartre, Existentialism is a Humanism)
- **[[Action over intention|Action over intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path: Right Action)

## Practice

- Do not wait to feel motivated to act. Act first; the feeling follows. This is the core behavioural insight: behaviour is a driver, not just an output.

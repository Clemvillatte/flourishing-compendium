> **Principle 44** — Recognise secondary emotions: feelings about feelings

A primary emotion is a response to the world (fear of a threat). A secondary emotion is a response to the primary emotion (shame about feeling fear, anxiety about feeling anger, guilt about feeling joy). Secondary emotions are often more distressing and more maintainable than primary ones.

**Plain words** Primary emotion: a response to the world (fear of a threat). Secondary emotion: a response to the primary (shame about feeling fear, guilt about feeling joy). Much chronic distress is secondary — the feeling about the feeling. Name which layer you're in.

*For example:* The man crying at the funeral is grieving (primary); the man furious at himself for crying is doing shame about the grief (secondary) — and the secondary is the one that lasts for months. Treat the layer that's actually sick.
## Psychology parallels

- **Meta-emotion amplifies distress**, judging a feeling as unacceptable adds a layer of suffering on top of the feeling (Levitt et al., 2004)
- **[[The second arrow|The second arrow]]** (Buddhist concept, empirically supported), the reaction to the pain is often worse than the pain (Hofmann et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The second arrow|The second arrow]]** (Buddhism, Sallatha Sutta)
- **[[The discipline of assent|Judging the judgement]]** (Spinoza, Ethics III)

## Practice

- When you notice distress about a feeling, separate the layers: "I feel anxious, and I feel ashamed about feeling anxious." Address the shame about the feeling first; it is often the larger burden.

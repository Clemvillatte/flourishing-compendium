> **Principle 63** — Cultivate metacognition: thinking about your own thinking

Metacognition is the capacity to monitor and control your own cognitive processes — knowing what you know, recognising when you are confused, choosing strategies, and evaluating your own performance. It is a foundation of learning and good judgment.

**Plain words** Metacognition: monitoring and controlling your own thinking — knowing what you know, recognising confusion, choosing strategies, evaluating performance. A foundational capacity, and trainable. Think about your thinking; it's the OS above the OS.

*For example:* The student who reads at the same speed whether understanding or not — versus the one who notices "I've absorbed nothing for three pages" and re-reads with a question in mind. Same material; one of them has a supervisor running.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Metacognition|Metacognition (Flavell)]]**, knowledge and regulation of one's own cognition (Flavell, 1979; Dunlosky & Metcalfe, 2009)
- **[[Metacognitive therapy (Wells)|Metacognitive therapy (Wells)]]**, treating metacognitive beliefs ("worrying is useful") reduces anxiety and depression (Wells, 2009)
- **Metacognitive accuracy predicts learning**, students with better calibration learn more effectively (Dunlosky & Rawson, 2012)

## Practice

- Practise asking: "do I actually understand this, or does it just feel familiar?" "Am I sure, or do I just feel sure?" Test yourself rather than assuming understanding.

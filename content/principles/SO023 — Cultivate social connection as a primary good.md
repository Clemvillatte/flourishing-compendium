> **Principle 23** — Cultivate social connection as a primary good

Cultivate social connection as a primary good, not as an optional extra. The evidence is among the strongest in the sociocultural literature: social relationships predict health, well-being, and mortality as strongly as smoking, obesity, and physical inactivity (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010, 2017). The adaptive stance is to treat connection as a design parameter of life, not a residual.

**Plain words** Connection is a primary good, not a luxury — relationships predict health and mortality as strongly as smoking and obesity. That's not a metaphor; it's an effect size. Treat your social life like a vital sign, because it is one.

*For example:* The GP who asks about friendships like they ask about smoking — because the answer predicts the decade. The patient who laughed at the question is now walking with a neighbour.

## Practice

- Schedule time with people as you schedule sleep and exercise. Prioritize one or two close relationships over many weak ones (see principle 25). In periods of transition, treat connection as the first thing to rebuild.

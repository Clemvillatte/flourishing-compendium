> **Principle 66** — Prepare the practice environment

Krishna's setup instructions in the sixth chapter of the Gita are brief. In Arnold's translation: "In a fair, still spot having his fixed abode, not too much raised, nor yet too low, let him abide, his goods a cloth, a deerskin, and the Kusa-grass. There, setting hard his mind upon The One, restraining heart and senses, silent, calm, let him accomplish Yoga" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Arnold). The same passage asks him to sit "sequestered ... solitary, his thoughts controlled", holding "immovable body and neck and head" (ch. 6, Arnold).

### The posture in the Yoga Sutras

Sutra 2.46 is shorter still: "Stable-and-easy posture" (trans. Woods, p. 191). The commentary explains: "Stable means motionless. That posture which is easy, which brings ease is the one intended by the sutra" (p. 191). It lists postures such as the lotus and hero postures as examples.

### What is not in the text

In Arnold's rendering the Gita passage asks for a "fair, still spot" that is neither too high nor too low, and names a cloth, a deerskin and kusa-grass. It does not say the place works because it is constant, or that the instructions are environmental design for attention. That reading, and the advice to keep one dedicated spot, are the principle's own. They are practical but are not quoted from either text.

**Plain words** The Gita asks for a still place that is neither too high nor too low, and a steady body. Patanjali asks that the posture be steady and easy. The idea of using the same spot each time is a modern suggestion that fits those instructions.

*For example:* Imagined example: you keep one cushion in a corner of a quiet room and sit there each morning. After a few weeks, sitting there starts to feel like the beginning of the practice.

(Bhagavad-Gita ch. 6, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388; Yoga Sutra 2.46 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's gloss, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), p. 191, Internet Archive; both opened 2026-10-21.)

> **Principle 177** — Do not argue with impressions

> But in the first place, be not hurried away by the rapidity of the appearance, but say, Appearances, wait for me a little; let me see who you are, and what you are about; let me put you to the test.

(Epictetus, Discourses II.18, "How we should struggle against appearances," trans. George Long, [Project Gutenberg #10661](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10661/pg10661.txt), opened; the chapter number is from the standard numbering and is not printed in this selection.)

Epictetus continues: "do not allow the appearance to lead you on and draw lively pictures of the things which will follow; for if you do, it will carry you off wherever it pleases." His remedy is to oppose it with "some other beautiful and noble appearance." So the passage tells the reader to pause and examine an impression. It does not tell the reader to let every impression pass unexamined, which is closer to the entry's wording. Enchiridion 1 gives the formula "You are but an appearance, and not absolutely the thing you appear to be," followed by an examination of whether it concerns what is in one's control ([Carter trans., Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html), opened). The "visitor" image and the claim that fighting "gives it energy" are the compendium's, and neither appears in the passages opened.

A modern parallel is the "urge surfing" technique. In a randomised study of 123 undergraduate smokers, those given brief mindfulness instructions did not differ on urges, but smoked fewer cigarettes over seven days than a no-instruction group, so the technique "may not initially reduce urges to smoke but may change the response to urges" ([Bowen & Marlatt, 2009; PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20025372/)). The authors call this preliminary. Hayes et al. (2011), Heidegger and Laozi were not opened.

**Plain words** Epictetus advises pausing when an impression arrives: tell it to wait, look at it, and test it before agreeing. Simply letting it go is a different practice that the research on urge surfing has begun to study.

*For example (imagined):* An anxious thought arrives during a meeting. The person tells it to wait, notes what it claims, and carries on with the agenda.

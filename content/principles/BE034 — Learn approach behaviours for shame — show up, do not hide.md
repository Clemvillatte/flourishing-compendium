> **Principle 34** — Learn approach behaviours for shame: show up, do not hide

Shame urges hiding (conceal, avoid, withdraw). The behavioural treatment is the opposite: show up, disclose appropriately, be seen. Do not obey the shame's behavioural instruction.

**Plain words** Shame's instruction is hide — and hiding is the punishment that keeps the shame alive. The opposite action is showing up: disclose to a safe person, be seen anyway. Shame survives in the dark; it's allergic to witnesses.

*For example:* The secret kept for a decade that loses half its weight in one told sentence — the shame was charging rent for the privacy. Telling was the eviction.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Opposite action (Linehan)|Opposite action for shame (Linehan)]]**, showing up rather than hiding reduces shame (Linehan, 1993; Rizvi & Linehan, 2005)
- **Self-disclosure reduces shame**, appropriate disclosure reduces shame (Rizvi & Linehan, 2005; Brown, 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Confession and light** (Augustine, Confessions)
- **[[The truth sets you free|The truth sets you free]]** (Christian tradition, John 8:32)
- **[[Authenticity|Authenticity over concealment]]** (Buber, I and Thou)

## Practice

- When ashamed, do not obey the urge to hide. Show up: be seen, disclose appropriately to safe others, do not conceal. Shame thrives in secrecy; it diminishes in the light.

> **Principle 158** — Do not postpone living well

The opening of On the Shortness of Life (Basore translation) reads: "It is not that we have a short space of time, but that we waste much of it. Life is long enough, and it has been given in sufficiently generous measure to allow the accomplishment of the very greatest things if the whole of it is well invested" ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_shortness_of_life/Chapter_I)). The Latin is "Non exiguum temporis habemus, sed multum perdidimus" ([Quotle](https://quotle.info/who-said/it-is-not-that-we-have-a-short-time-to-live-but-that-we/)). So Seneca's claim is that time is wasted, which fits the seed. I read only chapter 1. The seed's "do not wait for retirement, the project, or the children" is the page's application. I did not open the later chapters it cites (7, 10) and cannot say what Seneca says there.

Two psychology items are loose fits. Kasser and Sheldon (2009) report across four studies that time affluence was positively related to subjective well-being "even after controlling for material affluence", with mindfulness and need satisfaction partly mediating it ([Journal of Business Ethics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-008-9696-1)). The seed's "more than material wealth" goes beyond that abstract. Killingsworth and Gilbert (2010) sampled 2,250 people's moments by smartphone: minds wandered 46.9% of the time, and people were less happy when mind-wandering ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21071660/); [Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/11/wandering-mind-not-a-happy-mind/)). That study concerns attention within a moment. Postponing a project or a phone call is a different behaviour from daydreaming.

**Plain words** Seneca says that life is long enough if it is not wasted. Whatever you are waiting for may never arrive, so do something you value today, however small. Studies on time and attention suggest that having time and being present go with wellbeing. They do not test the act of postponing.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. You call the friend you have been meaning to call, rather than waiting until things calm down.

> **Principle 16** — Pair patience and gratitude as the two meters of every state

The pairing is written into the Quran. After the verse about Moses being sent with signs, it says: "Surely in this are signs for whoever is steadfast, grateful." The Arabic is ṣabbār shakūr, and the translator is Mustafa Khattab (Quran 14:5, [The Clear Quran on Quran.com](https://quran.com/14/5)).

### One rule for two weathers

The hadith sets out the working rule. Suhaib reports the Prophet saying that everything is good for the believer, "for if he has an occasion to feel delight, he thanks (God), thus there is a good for him in it, and if he gets into trouble and shows resignation (and endures it patiently), there is a good for him in it" (Sahih Muslim 2999, [Sunnah.com](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2999)). Ease calls for thanks, trouble calls for patient endurance, and neither state is left without a response.

Al-Ghazali cites Ibn Masud: "Iman is divided into two - patience and gratefulness." His short form is "So patience is in calamity and gratefulness is in happiness" (Revival of Religious Learnings, Book IV, chapter II, Fazl-ul-Karim translation, [ghazali.org](https://www.ghazali.org/ihya/english/ihya-vol4-C2.htm)).

The wording "uses the ease rather than merely consuming it" is this compendium's gloss, not found in these texts.

**Plain words**
Most people have one default response and use it in every weather. This principle gives each weather its own. When something hard arrives, the job is to endure it. When something good arrives, the job is to notice it and be thankful, instead of treating it as owed.

**For example**
A medical result comes on Monday and a bonus on Friday. On Monday, ask what patient endurance asks of you today. On Friday, name the specific thing you are grateful for and to whom. The hadith's test is simple: for each piece of news, which of the two responses does it call for?

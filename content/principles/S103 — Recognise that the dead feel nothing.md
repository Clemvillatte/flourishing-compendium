> **Principle 225** — Recognise that the dead feel nothing

Death is either the end of consciousness (in which case there is nothing to fear) or a transition (in which case there is nothing to fear). The Stoic does not fear the state of being dead. The fear is of dying, not of death. And dying is brief.

(Med. III.1, IV.19, IV.48, VI.28, VII.32, IX.3, X.8, X.11, XI.1; Seneca, Letters 24, 82, 99, 107, 122.)

**Plain words** Death is either the end of consciousness or a passage, and in neither case is there something to fear. What people really fear is dying, which is brief. The Stoic isn't afraid of being dead.

*For example:* A person with a terminal diagnosis spends her last weeks arranging things for her family and finds the fear shrinks.

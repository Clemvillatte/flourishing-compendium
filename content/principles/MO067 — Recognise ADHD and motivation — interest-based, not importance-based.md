> **Principle 67** — Recognise ADHD and motivation: interest-based, not importance-based

ADHD involves a distinct motivational pattern: motivation is driven by interest, novelty, and urgency rather than by importance or consequence. This is not laziness; it is a different motivational architecture.

**Plain words** ADHD motivation is interest-gated, not importance-gated — the boring-critical thing can't start, and the fascinating-irrelevant thing can't stop. The fix is building interest, novelty, or urgency into the critical things, not moralising the engine.

*For example:* The report that waits untouched for days until a deadline lights it — same brain, now on fire. The engine was never broken. It runs on different fuel.
## Psychology parallels

- **ADHD as interest-based motivation**, motivation is driven by interest and novelty (Brown, 2013; Barkley, 1997)
- **ADHD involves dopamine differences**, reward and motivation systems differ in ADHD (Volkow et al., 2011; Faraone et al., 2021)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Different, not less** (Grandin, Thinking in Pictures)
- **[[Neurodiversity|Neurodiversity]]** (Singer, 1998)
- **The tree that is not judged by the forest** (Zhuangzi)

## Practice

- If you have ADHD, do not judge your motivation by neurotypical standards. Design around interest: make tasks interesting (novelty, challenge, urgency), use external structure, and consider professional evaluation for treatment.

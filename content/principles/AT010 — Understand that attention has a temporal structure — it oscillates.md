> **Principle 10** — Understand that attention has a temporal structure: it oscillates

Attention is not a steady beam; it oscillates. There are natural fluctuations in alertness and focus (ultradian rhythms, attentional blinking, vigilance decrements). Understanding this structure allows you to work with, rather than against, your attention's natural rhythm.

**Plain words** Attention isn't a steady beam; it's a wave — alertness rises and falls in cycles over minutes and hours. Fighting the troughs is amateur; riding them is professional. Deep work in the crests, email in the dips.

*For example:* The writer who drafts in the morning's high tide and answers messages in the afternoon's low — same hours, twenty times the output.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Ultradian rhythms in attention|Ultradian rhythms in attention]]**, alertness and attention fluctuate in roughly 90-minute cycles (Kleitman, 1963; Monk, 2005)
- **[[Attentional blink|Attentional blink]]**, rapid shifts of attention create brief windows where a second target is missed (Raymond et al., 1992; Martens & Wyble, 2010)
- **[[Vigilance decrement|Vigilance decrement]]**, sustained attention declines over time without breaks (Mackworth, 1948; See et al., 1995)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Panta rhei|Panta rhei (everything flows)]]** (Heraclitus, fragments)
- **[[Yin and yang|Yin-yang oscillation]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 42)
- **The rhythm of effort and rest** (Taoism, Neiye)

## Practice

- Work in blocks that match your attentional rhythm (roughly 25-90 minutes) with deliberate breaks. Do not expect steady, unbroken focus for hours.

> **Principle 61** — Recognise the role of narrative in identity: your life story shapes who you are

Identity is not just a set of traits; it is a narrative. The story you tell about your life — its themes, arcs, and turning points — shapes how you experience yourself and your future.

**Plain words** Identity is not just a set of traits — it's a narrative. The story you tell about your life, its themes and turning points, shapes how you experience yourself and your future. You are a story; edit the telling.

*For example:* The person whose life story is "a series of bad breaks" versus "a slow apprenticeship" — same events — and the way each telling predicts the next decision. The story isn't a report on the self; it's an input to it.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Narrative identity|Narrative identity]]**, life stories are a core component of identity and predict well-being (McAdams, 2001; McAdams & McLean, 2013)
- **Redemptive narratives predict well-being**, life stories with themes of growth and redemption are associated with higher well-being (McAdams et al., 2001; Adler, 2012)
- **Contamination sequences (good turns bad) predict lower well-being**, narratives where good things turn bad are associated with depression (McAdams et al., 2001)

- **[[Narrative identity|Narrative identity]]** (Ricoeur, Time and Narrative; MacIntyre, After Virtue)

## Practice

- Notice the narrative arc of your life story. Is it a story of growth, redemption, contamination, or stagnation? The facts may not change, but the arc can. Consider how you tell the story of difficult experiences.

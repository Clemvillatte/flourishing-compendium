> **Principle 67** — Schedule solitude deliberately

Chapter 13 lists the marks of true knowledge, and one of them is about where a person likes to be:

> Humbleness; freedom from hypocrisy; non-violence; forgiveness; ... an inclination for solitary places and an aversion for mundane society; constancy in spiritual knowledge; and philosophical pursuit of the Absolute Truth ... all these I declare to be knowledge, and what is contrary to it, I call ignorance.

(BG 13.8-12, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/13/verse/8/); the page prints the five verses as one passage.)

Mukundananda's commentary says devotees "are not driven by the need for company to overcome feelings of loneliness", so their liking for solitude is not a flight from other people. About the second half he adds that if taking part in society is needed "for the sake of service", the devotee "accepts it and develops the strength to remain mentally unaffected by it".

Chapter 6 puts it as a practice: "Those who seek the state of Yog should reside in seclusion, constantly engaged in meditation with a controlled mind and body" (BG 6.10, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/10/)). The commentary suggests, without leaving ordinary work, setting aside "one to two hours every day" of isolated practice.

Booking solitude like a meeting, and keeping the first fifteen minutes after rejoining people free, are this page's additions.

**Plain words**

Time alone is how some kinds of attention get built, and being alone is different from avoiding people. The Gita lists the liking for it among the signs of understanding, and the commentary says it is not about loneliness. Put it in your diary the way you would a meeting, and let yourself return to company gently.

**For example**

A parent who has not been alone in a room for years books a monthly solo walk, guards it like a dentist appointment, and comes back a little steadier each time.

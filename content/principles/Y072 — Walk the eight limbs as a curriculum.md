> **Principle 72** — Walk the eight limbs as a curriculum

Patanjali's list of the eight aids comes in one sutra, in a fixed order:

> Abstentions and observances and postures and regulations-of-the-breath and withdrawal-of-the-senses and fixed attention and contemplation and concentration are the eight aids.

(Yoga Sutras 2.29, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The commentary says "The following up of these must be performed in succession." Woods's own analysis of the text groups the first five as indirect aids and the last three as direct aids. Some steps are linked explicitly: sutra 2.49 begins the breath step "when there is this [stability of posture]".

The page's earlier list, with absorption and integration as its last two, uses other English words from Woods's, whose last three aids are fixed attention, contemplation and concentration. He renders sutras 3.1 to 3.3 as "Binding the mind-stuff to a place is fixed-attention", then "Focusedness of the presented idea upon that [place] is contemplation", then the same contemplation "shining forth [in consciousness] as the intended object and nothing more, and, as it were, emptied of itself, is concentration". Each of the three builds on the one before.

Using the order as a map for tracing a late failure back to an earlier limb is this page's idea. The texts give the order and a few links, and do not say that faults usually sit upstream.

**Plain words**

The eight steps run from conduct and the body to the inner work, and the earlier ones are the base for the later ones. If your concentration falls apart, look at the earlier steps first: your restraints, your seat, your breath. The list is something to return to, not a ladder you climb once and leave behind.

**For example**

A meditator whose mind will not settle checks the list and finds they have been sitting badly and sleeping late. They fix the seat and the nights, and the sitting improves without any new technique.

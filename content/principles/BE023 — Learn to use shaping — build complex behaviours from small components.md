> **Principle 23** — Learn to use shaping: build complex behaviours from small components

Shaping is Skinner's answer to a problem in operant conditioning: a complex response may never occur on its own, so it cannot be reinforced as a whole. His account rests on the continuity of behavior. A skill that looks like a finished unit is the end of a continuous process, not something that appears full grown. In Science and Human Behavior he demonstrates with a pigeon: food first for turning toward a spot, then only for moving closer, then for moving the head forward, then for beak contact with the spot. A hungry bird can reach the final response in two or three minutes.

> "By reinforcing a series of successive approximations, we bring a rare response to a very high probability in a short time. This is an effective procedure because it recognizes and utilizes the continuous nature of a complex act." (Skinner, Science and Human Behavior, 1953, ch. VI, p. 92, [B. F. Skinner Foundation PDF](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf))

The practical point for a learner: the criterion moves with the performance. Each reinforced version makes the next, slightly better version more likely, so the skill is assembled from behavior that is already happening rather than conjured from nothing.

**Plain words**
A finished skill never shows up first try. What shows up is a rough version, and the rough version is the material. Reward it, then raise the bar: only the slightly better version earns the next reward. A parent praising a toddler's scribbles is not pretending they are calligraphy; the praise is what produces the version that later resembles letters. If you want to run, the walk to the corner is not a lesser activity, it is the first approximation, and it is the thing to reward.

**For example**
Skinner's pigeon again, because the procedure is concrete: reinforce turning toward the spot, then movement toward it, then the head moving forward, then beak contact. Each step is close to the last one, and a sequence that would otherwise take weeks of waiting assembles itself in minutes.

## Practice

- Shape behaviours: start with a small version (walk 5 minutes, write 1 sentence, meditate 1 minute) and gradually increase. Do not attempt the full behaviour immediately.

> **Principle 23** — Learn to use shaping: build complex behaviours from small components

Skinner describes shaping as reinforcing progressively closer approximations to a target response. Reinforcement begins with behaviour already occurring, so training can proceed before the final response appears.

> "By reinforcing a series of successive approximations, we bring a rare response to a very high probability in a short time. This is an effective procedure because it recognizes and utilizes the continuous nature of a complex act." (Skinner, Science and Human Behavior, 1953, ch. VI, p. 92, [B. F. Skinner Foundation PDF](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf))

**Plain words**
Begin with an action the learner can already perform that approaches the goal. Reward that attempt, then require a closer version as performance improves. Earlier versions stop earning the reward once the requirement changes.

**For example**
Skinner's procedure starts with food for a pigeon turning toward a spot. It then requires movement toward the spot, progressively closer positions, forward head movement and finally beak contact (ch. VI, p. 92).

## Practice

- Shape behaviours: start with a small version (walk 5 minutes, write 1 sentence, meditate 1 minute) and gradually increase. Do not attempt the full behaviour immediately.

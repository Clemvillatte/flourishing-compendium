> **Principle 23** — Learn to use shaping: build complex behaviours from small components

Shaping involves reinforcing successive approximations of a target behaviour. Start with a small version of the behaviour and gradually increase complexity or difficulty.

**Plain words** Complex behaviours are built by approximation — reward the rough version, then the better version, then the real thing. Nobody executes the finished skill first try; they execute a sketch of it that gets redrawn. That's shaping.

*For example:* The toddler's crayon scribbles praised like calligraphy — the praise isn't dishonest, it's engineering. Each reward redraws the next version slightly closer to the real skill.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Shaping (Skinner)|Shaping (Skinner)]]**, complex behaviours are built by reinforcing approximations (Skinner, 1953; Ferster & Skinner, 1957)
- **Successive approximations**, building from small to complex increases success (Skinner, 1953; Martin & Pear, 2019)

## Practice

- Shape behaviours: start with a small version (walk 5 minutes, write 1 sentence, meditate 1 minute) and gradually increase. Do not attempt the full behaviour immediately.

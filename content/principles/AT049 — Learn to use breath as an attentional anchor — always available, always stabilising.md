> **Principle 49** — Learn to use breath as an attentional anchor: always available, always stabilising

The breath is the most commonly used attentional anchor because it is always available, always present, and has a natural calming effect. Attending to breath stabilises attention and regulates physiology.

**Plain words** The breath is the perfect anchor because you can't lose it — no app, no props, always on. When the mind storms, one conscious breath is the handrail. It's not magic; it's the most reliable object you own.

*For example:* Before the difficult phone call: three slow breaths, feeling the air at the nostrils. You didn't solve anything — you just gave the storm a handrail.
## Psychology parallels

- **Breath-focused attention stabilises and calms**, attending to breath reduces mind-wandering and activates parasympathetic regulation (Tang et al., 2015; Zaccaro et al., 2018)
- **Slow breathing regulates autonomic nervous system**, deliberate slow breathing activates parasympathetic and reduces arousal (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014; Zaccaro et al., 2018)

## Practice

- Use breath as your primary attentional anchor. In meditation, during work, before difficult tasks: attend to the breath. Notice its natural rhythm. Use slow, deliberate breathing to calm and focus.

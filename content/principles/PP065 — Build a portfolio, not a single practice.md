> **Principle 65** — Build a portfolio, not a single practice

No single practice produces flourishing, because well-being has several sides and each responds to different activities. Seligman's well-being theory names five of them: "One attempt to reconcile hedonic and eudaimonic traditions is The Wellbeing Theory or PERMA model, put forth by Seligman (2011), which proposes five building-blocks for wellbeing: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning and Accomplishment." (Seligman 2011, quoted verbatim in Ascenso, Perkins & Williamon 2018, "Resounding Meaning: A PERMA Wellbeing Profile of Classical Musicians," Frontiers in Psychology 9, PMC6232231.) What lifts feeling will not build relationships; what builds meaning will not by itself produce accomplishment.

So the working approach is a portfolio: a set of practices spread across those dimensions, adapted to the person, the context and the season of life. A portfolio needs maintenance like any portfolio — review it regularly: which practices are earning their place? Which have gone stale? Which should be retired, or varied?

(Schueller, 2014; Parks et al., 2012.)

**Plain words** One practice won't cover everything: feeling good, being absorbed, close relationships, purpose and getting things done each draw on different activities. Keep several, spread across those sides of life, and tend them like a garden — pull what has gone stale, keep what earns its place, and let the mix change with your season of life.

*For example:* One person's week includes running, dinner with a friend, hard work on a craft and an evening of quiet — four different practices, four different sides of well-being.

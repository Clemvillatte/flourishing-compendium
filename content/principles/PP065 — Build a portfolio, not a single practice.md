> **Principle 65** — Build a portfolio, not a single practice

Seligman's well-being theory treats flourishing as multidimensional. The authors of a study using the model summarise it: "One attempt to reconcile hedonic and eudaimonic traditions is The Wellbeing Theory or PERMA model, put forth by Seligman (2011), which proposes five building-blocks for wellbeing: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning and Accomplishment." (Ascenso, Perkins & Williamon 2018, "Resounding Meaning: A PERMA Wellbeing Profile of Classical Musicians," Frontiers in Psychology 9, PMC6232231, Introduction.)

This principle's recommendation follows from that multidimensionality: build a portfolio rather than rely on a single practice — a set of practices spread across those dimensions, adapted to the person, the context and the season of life. A portfolio needs maintenance like any portfolio — review it regularly: which practices are earning their place? Which have gone stale? Which should be retired, or varied?

(Schueller, 2014; Parks et al., 2012.)

**Plain words** Check your week against the five: if everything good in it is solitary — running, reading, quiet work — more of the same will not fix a thin patch in relationships. Add a practice that covers the neglected side, and when the season of your life changes, let the mix change with it.

*For example:* One person's week includes running, dinner with a friend, hard work on a craft and an evening of quiet — four different practices, four different sides of well-being.

> **Principle 48** — Recognise anxiety's core: intolerance of uncertainty

The Dugas et al. model of generalised anxiety disorder names four process variables: intolerance of uncertainty, beliefs about worry, poor problem orientation and cognitive avoidance. In their preliminary test with 24 GAD patients and 20 nonclinical controls, all four were highly related to the discriminant function, and intolerance of uncertainty helped distinguish the groups ([Dugas et al., 1998, PubMed 9613027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/)). The study concerns one model of GAD, not every form of anxiety.

> The results show that all main components of the model were highly related to the discriminant function and that intolerance of uncertainty was pivotal in distinguishing GAD patients from non clinical subjects.
>
> — Dugas, Gagnon, Ladouceur and Freeston, 1998, Behaviour Research and Therapy 36(2) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/))

**Plain words** The study names a tendency to react negatively to uncertainty itself. The application is to distinguish between checking or problem-solving and the underlying not-knowing that the checking tries to remove.

For example (imagined): a person notices they check the tracking, the forecast, the reviews, everything, in order to eliminate not-knowing. That is one instance of the pattern the study describes.

## Psychology parallels

- **Intolerance of uncertainty is a core mechanism of GAD**, difficulty tolerating uncertainty maintains worry (Dugas et al., 1998; Carleton, 2016)
- **Worry as cognitive avoidance**, worry is a verbal-linguistic activity that suppresses imagery and the physiological arousal of fear, negatively reinforcing the worry (Borkovec et al., 1983)

- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as trust in the unfolding]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 17)

## Practice

- When you notice worry, ask whether the checking or thinking is aimed at removing uncertainty itself, rather than at solving a problem that can be solved.

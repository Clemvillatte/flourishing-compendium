> **Principle 48** — Recognise anxiety's core: intolerance of uncertainty

The Dugas et al. model of generalised anxiety disorder names four process variables: intolerance of uncertainty, beliefs about worry, poor problem orientation and cognitive avoidance. In their test with 24 GAD patients and 20 nonclinical controls, all four were highly related to the discriminant function, and intolerance of uncertainty did most to distinguish the groups ([Dugas et al., 1998, PubMed 9613027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/)).

> The results show that all main components of the model were highly related to the discriminant function and that intolerance of uncertainty was pivotal in distinguishing GAD patients from non clinical subjects.
>
> — Dugas, Gagnon, Ladouceur and Freeston, 1998, Behaviour Research and Therapy 36(2) ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/))

**Plain words** The compendium's application of this principle asks the reader to distinguish between checking or problem-solving and the underlying intolerance of not-knowing. What the study names is a tendency to react negatively to uncertainty itself, independent of the actual probability of the feared outcome. The application treats deliberately tolerating small uncertainties as the target, not eliminating uncertainty.

For example (imagined): a person notices they check the tracking, the forecast, the reviews, everything, in order to eliminate not-knowing. The compendium's reading of the study suggests treating that as a signal to practise leaving some things unknown for a while.

## Psychology parallels

- **Intolerance of uncertainty is a core mechanism of GAD**, difficulty tolerating uncertainty maintains worry (Dugas et al., 1998; Carleton, 2016)
- **Worry as cognitive avoidance**, worry is a verbal-linguistic activity that suppresses imagery and the physiological arousal of fear, negatively reinforcing the worry (Borkovec et al., 1983)

## Practice

- When you notice worry, ask: "am I trying to eliminate uncertainty by thinking?" Recognise that worry is an avoidance strategy, not a problem-solving strategy. Practise tolerating small uncertainties deliberately.

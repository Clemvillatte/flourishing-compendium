> **Principle 53** — Choose your own contemplative object

Sutra 1.35 to 1.38 list objects that can steady the mind, and 1.39 ends the list with a free choice:

> Or [the mind-stuff reaches the stable state] by contemplation upon any such an object as is desired.

(Yoga Sutras 1.39, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, p. 77, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

Vyasa's commentary says: "Let [the yogin] contemplate whatever object he desires. Having reached stability there, the mind-stuff reaches the stable state elsewhere also." Vacaspati glosses it as "just that, whichsoever particular deity it be". So the tradition does let the practitioner choose, and Vyasa says steadiness won on one object carries over to others.

The limits of that permission are narrower than a modern reader might want. The sutra sits in a list of objects for fixed attention and contemplation, and Vacaspati's example is a deity. Extending the permission to everyday tasks is this page's step. It is a reasonable one, since Vyasa's wording is "whatever object", but the text does not say it.

**Plain words**

Many people give up on meditation because the object they were handed, a breath or a mantra, never held their attention. The sutra says the object matters less than the steadiness, and you may choose. Pick something that actually engages you. The skill of staying with it is what transfers.

**For example**

A woman finds that the pattern on her grandmother's teacup holds her attention for ten minutes, where a mantra lasts thirty seconds. By Vyasa's account the steadiness she builds on the cup is not wasted, because it is the same steadiness she will use elsewhere.

> **Principle 53** — Choose your own contemplative object

The list of supports ends with the open clause: "or whatever" holds the mind steady. The tradition's final word on objects is that the object serves the steadiness, not the reverse, and any object that steadies is legitimate. Practically, take the tradition's permission seriously: if peeling potatoes steadies your mind more than your mantra, practice the peeling.

(YS 1.39.)

**Plain words** The list of supports ends with the open clause: "or whatever" holds the mind steady. The object serves the steadiness, not the reverse — any object that steadies is legitimate. Take the tradition's permission seriously.

*For example:* The woman whose meditation object is her grandmother's teacup — not in any sutra, and it holds her attention like nothing else. The tradition's final word on the matter is the open clause: "or whatever."

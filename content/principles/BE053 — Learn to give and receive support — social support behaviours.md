> **Principle 53** — Learn to give and receive support: social support behaviours

Social support involves both giving and receiving. Giving support (helping, listening, being present) and receiving support (asking, accepting, allowing) are both social behaviours that build connection.

**Plain words** Support is a two-way street — giving it is a well-being behaviour, and so is receiving it. The person who only gives is hiding; the person who only takes is draining. Both directions need traffic.

*For example:* The friend who helps everyone and refuses all help — loved, but slowly hollowed. Letting someone bring the soup is also a gift: it lets them matter to you.
## Psychology parallels

- **Social support and health**, both giving and receiving support are associated with better health (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2010; Uchino, 2009)
- **Perceived support matters more than received support**, feeling supported is more predictive than objective support (Cohen & Wills, 1985; Uchino, 2009)

## Practice

- Practise giving and receiving support: offer help, listen, be present. And ask for help, accept help, allow yourself to be supported. Both are connection behaviours.

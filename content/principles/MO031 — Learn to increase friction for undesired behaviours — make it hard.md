> **Principle 31** — Learn to increase friction for undesired behaviours: make it hard

Just as reducing friction increases desired behaviours, increasing friction decreases undesired ones. Making the undesired behaviour harder (more steps, more effort, less accessible) reduces its likelihood.

**Plain words** Flip the friction: make the bad thing hard — delete the app, pour out the wine, leave the card at home. Ten seconds of annoyance is often all the defence you need. Willpower is unreliable; inconvenience is forever.

*For example:* The person who wants to stop nightly scrolling and simply charges the phone in the kitchen — no heroics, no discipline. Just friction, quietly doing the work every night.
## Psychology parallels

- **Friction and undesired behaviour**, increasing friction reduces undesired behaviours (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008; Wood & Neal, 2007)
- **Habit disruption**, changing environmental cues disrupts habits (Wood & Neal, 2007; Gardner, 2015)

- **[[Guarding the senses|Guarding the senses]]** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **[[Environmental design|Environmental design over willpower]]** (Aristotle, Politics)

## Practice

- Increase friction for undesired behaviours: remove junk food from the house, log out of social media, delete the app, put the phone in another room. Make the undesired behaviour the path of most resistance.

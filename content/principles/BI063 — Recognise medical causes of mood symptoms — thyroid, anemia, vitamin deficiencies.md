> **Principle 63** — Recognise medical causes of mood symptoms: thyroid, anemia, vitamin deficiencies

Medical conditions (thyroid disorders, anemia, vitamin D or B12 deficiency, chronic illness) can produce mood symptoms that look like depression or anxiety. These require medical evaluation and treatment.

**Plain words** Mood symptoms sometimes have physical causes — thyroid, anaemia, deficiencies. That's a reason to involve a clinician early, not a reason to self-diagnose from a checklist.

*For example:* The year of low mood that improved once the B12 deficiency was found and treated — the bloodwork was the missing clue. Testing decisions belong with a clinician; the lesson is to raise the possibility.
## Psychology parallels

- **Medical causes of mood symptoms**, thyroid, anemia, B12, and vitamin D deficiencies can produce depression-like symptoms (Stover & Stover, 2011; Chandra et al., 2017)
- **Medical evaluation belongs with a clinician; testing decisions are theirs to make** (Stover & Stover, 2011; Chandra et al., 2017)

## Practice

- If you have persistent mood symptoms, raise the possibility with a clinician and let them decide what to test (thyroid, iron/B12, vitamin D, general health). Address medical causes before or alongside psychological treatment.

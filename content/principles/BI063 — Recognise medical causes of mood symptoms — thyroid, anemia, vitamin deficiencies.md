> **Principle 63** — Recognise medical causes of mood symptoms: thyroid, anemia, vitamin deficiencies

Several physical conditions are linked with low mood, and the associations are real but often smaller than folklore suggests. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 25 studies with 348,014 participants found hypothyroidism and clinical depression "were associated (OR, 1.30)", and concluded that the effect size "was considerably lower than previously assumed, and the modest association was possibly restricted to overt hypothyroidism and female individuals" (Bode et al. 2021, JAMA Psychiatry 78(12):1375-1383, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34524390/)). A meta-analysis of 19 studies on autoimmune thyroiditis made the clinical point, concluding that the finding "could lead to the choice of early treatment-and not only psychotherapeutic treatment-of the organic disorder" (Siegmann et al. 2018, JAMA Psychiatry 75(6):577-584, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29800939/)).

For vitamin B12, a clinical review in American Family Physician states that symptoms "may include fatigue, brain fog, depression, peripheral neuropathy, and ataxia", that "universal screening is not recommended", and that "testing should be considered in patients with at least one risk factor for and one clinical feature of vitamin B12 deficiency" (Patel & McGuirk 2025, Am Fam Physician 112(3):294-300, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40961307/)). So testing is a clinician's call, guided by risk factors.

**Plain words**
Low mood, anxiety and tiredness sometimes have a physical contributor, such as a thyroid problem or low B12. Large reviews show that the thyroid link is modest, so it is one possibility among many. B12 testing is recommended when there is a risk factor and a matching symptom, not for everyone. Tell a doctor about persistent symptoms, and let them decide what to check.

*For example:* After months of low mood and fatigue, someone books a GP visit and describes both the mood and the physical symptoms. The doctor orders bloodwork on clinical grounds. This is an imagined case, and nobody can predict from the outside what the tests will show.

## Psychology parallels

- **Medical causes of mood symptoms**, hypothyroidism and depression are associated, but modestly (Bode et al., 2021); B12 deficiency can include depression among its symptoms (Patel & McGuirk, 2025); anaemia and vitamin D not sourced here; Stover & Stover, 2011; Chandra et al., 2017
- **Medical evaluation belongs with a clinician**, B12 testing is advised when a risk factor and a clinical feature are both present, not as universal screening (Patel & McGuirk, 2025)

## Practice

- If you have persistent mood symptoms, raise the possibility with a clinician and let them decide what to test (thyroid, iron/B12, vitamin D, general health). Address medical causes before or alongside psychological treatment.

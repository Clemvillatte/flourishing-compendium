> **Principle 79** — Recognise trauma's specific effects on affect: it is not the same as ordinary dysregulation

Trauma produces a specific pattern of affect: hyperarousal, avoidance, intrusive re-experiencing, and numbing. This is not ordinary emotional dysregulation and does not respond to ordinary regulation skills alone. It requires trauma-specific treatment.

**Plain words** Trauma produces a specific pattern: hyperarousal, avoidance, intrusive re-experiencing, numbing — not ordinary dysregulation, and it doesn't respond to ordinary regulation alone. It responds to trauma-specific treatment. Name it correctly and get the right treatment.

*For example:* The accident survivor whose "anger problem" is actually hypervigilance at intersections — the couples counselling for the temper was treating the wrong layer. The trauma-focused work was the treatment; the anger was the presentation.
## Psychology parallels

- **PTSD is a distinct clinical condition**, with specific criteria and specific evidence-based treatments (trauma-focused CBT, EMDR, prolonged exposure) (APA, 2022; Foa et al., 2007)
- **Trauma affects affect processing differently from ordinary dysregulation**, the mechanisms involve fear conditioning, memory consolidation, and avoidance (Craske et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The wound and the healing** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)
- **[[Post-traumatic growth|Post-traumatic growth]]** (Tedeschi & Calhoun, 1996)

## Practice

- If your affective pattern involves intrusion, avoidance, hyperarousal, and numbing after a traumatic event, seek trauma-specific treatment. Ordinary regulation skills are not sufficient.

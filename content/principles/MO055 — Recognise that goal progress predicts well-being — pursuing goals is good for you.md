> **Principle 55** — Recognise that goal progress predicts well-being: pursuing goals is good for you

The evidence base is both long and daily. On the long side, the reviewed literature is summarised in one sentence: "Prior research has demonstrated a robust association between goal progress and subjective well-being; when individuals make progress on their goals, they experience positive emotions and life satisfaction, and they experience less psychological distress" ([Jakubiak & Feeney, 2016, introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5000864/), citing Diener 1984 and Klug & Maier 2014). Of Brunstein's semester study the same article reports: "Students who reported greater goal progress during the semester were more likely to have increases in well-being or to maintain their level of well-being over the semester than students who reported low levels of goal progress" ([Jakubiak & Feeney, 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5000864/)). On the daily side, two seven-day diary studies followed the reported associations into ordinary life:

> "For both young-adult newlyweds (Study 1) and married couples in late adulthood (Study 2), daily independent goal progress predicted same-day and next-day improvements in psychological, physical, and relational well-being." (Jakubiak & Feeney, 2016, [JPSP 111(3), abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5000864/))

The concrete shape of the reported effect: "when participants made more progress on their goals than usual on one day, they reported increases in positive affect, sleep quality, and relationship quality, and decreased physical symptoms, the following day (as well as concurrently)" ([Jakubiak & Feeney, 2016, abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5000864/)). The design is within-person: each participant was compared with their own average over the diary period, and the measures concern progress, not attainment.

**Plain words** The diary studies compared each person with their own average: on days with more progress than usual, participants reported better mood, sleep and relationship quality the same day and the next. The finding is about reported associations over the studied weeks, each person against their own baseline.

*For example (compendium exercise):* Keep a one-week record of daily goal progress and mood, and read the week against your own average.

> **Principle 55** — Recognise that goal progress predicts well-being: pursuing goals is good for you

Making progress toward goals is one of the strongest predictors of well-being. The pursuit of meaningful goals (not just their attainment) is associated with positive affect, life satisfaction, and sense of purpose.

**Plain words** Progress toward goals predicts well-being — it's not the achieving, it's the moving. A life with things underway beats a life with things done and nothing moving. Keep something in progress, always.

*For example:* The retired partner who sinks within a year — everything was achieved, nothing was underway. The goal wasn't the nutrient. The pursuit was.
## Psychology parallels

- **Goal progress and well-being**, progress toward goals predicts positive affect and life satisfaction (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999; Sheldon et al., 2010)
- **[[The progress principle|Progress principle (Amabile)]]**, small, regular progress is the strongest predictor of motivation and affect (Amabile & Kramer, 2011)
- **Goal pursuit itself is beneficial**, the pursuit of meaningful goals is associated with well-being, even before attainment (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia as activity]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I)
- **The journey, not just the destination** (Laozi, attributed)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort as its own reward]]** (Buddhism)

## Practice

- Pursue meaningful goals. Track progress. Do not wait for attainment to feel good; progress itself is a source of well-being.

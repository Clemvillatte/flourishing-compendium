> **Principle 46** — Trust cleverness joined to humility

In Michael Bazzett's 2018 English version of the Popol Vuh, the twins win by trickery more than force. Given a torch and cigars to return unburnt, "they didn't light the torch. They covered it with the scarlet gleam of a macaw's tail feather", and "tucked fireflies into their tips", so that "the cigars were not burnt in the least". Later "the twins went into council. They wove their words into a ruse", and Xbalanque tells a rabbit: "When the ball comes your way leap out and sprint away until my work is done". Facing the lords' oven, they say "do you think we cannot recognize death when we see it?" and then "dove headfirst into the oven". The lords are finally "defeated" after the twins "declared their names" (Bazzett 2018, [translation PDF](https://last303.sites.olt.ubc.ca/files/2023/11/the-popol-vuh_trans-bazzett.pdf), Part Three; line breaks removed).

In psychology, systematic problem-solving training has an evidence base. A meta-analysis of 21 samples found problem-solving therapy "equally effective as other psychosocial therapies and medication treatments and significantly more effective than no treatment" (Bell & D'Zurilla 2009, Clin Psychol Rev 29(4):348-353, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19299058/)). A later, stricter review found that for problem-solving therapy "insufficient evidence is available that [it is] effective because too few low-risk studies were available" (Cuijpers et al. 2019, Epidemiol Psychiatr Sci 28(1):21-30, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29486804/)).

**Plain words**
The twins beat stronger opponents by preparing, hiding their plans and using cleverness at each trial. The myth does not say the plan is easy, and they do pay a price. Problem-solving therapy, which teaches people to define a problem, list options and test them, performs about as well as other therapies in a 2009 review, though a later review says the evidence is thin. When you are outmatched, write down the problem and three possible moves before choosing one.

*For example:* A small shop that cannot match a chain's prices finds a service the chain cannot offer and tests it for a month. This is an imagined case, and success is not guaranteed.

> **Principle 163** — Do your duty even when it is unpleasant

Marcus begins the fifth book of his notes by answering his own reluctance to get up ([Meditations V.1](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)).

> "I am rising to the work of a human being." (Med. V.1, [George Long translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)).

He points to birds, ants and bees doing their part, then asks why acts that serve society should seem less worthy of effort than a craft or the pursuit of money ([Meditations V.1](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)).

The warmth of the bed is not his argument's real target; it is letting what feels pleasant decide whether worthwhile work gets done ([Meditations V.1](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)). He also answers the objection that rest is necessary by conceding that it is, though he insists it has limits ([Meditations V.1](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)).

A careful application therefore asks two questions: does this work genuinely deserve doing, and is discomfort my only reason for avoiding it? Difficulty alone cannot answer either question.

**Plain words** The alarm may settle when to get up, but it cannot tell you what is worth getting up for. Keep the person who needs you in view, and make room for the rest that lets you keep helping.

*For example:* Suppose you have agreed to drive your father to dialysis at six. You make the early start; if repeated journeys become too much, you arrange shared lifts rather than treating exhaustion as proof of devotion.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Behavioral activation|Behavioural activation]]**: this is a structured psychotherapy, not an instruction to try harder; a Cochrane review found possible benefits for depression but limited confidence in the evidence ([Uphoff and colleagues, 2020 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32628293/)).
- **[[Implementation intentions|Implementation intentions]]**: Gollwitzer describes plans that link an anticipated situation to a particular response, helping translate an existing goal into action without deciding whether the goal is morally right ([1999 abstract](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/publications/implementation-intentions-strong-effects-simple-plans)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **Social duty**: Marcus ties effort to the work of a social being, rather than to discomfort for its own sake ([Meditations V.1](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)).
- **[[Courage|Courage as virtue]]**: Aristotle distinguishes acting for a noble end from mere fearlessness; his strict account centres on danger and death in battle, so the comparison with everyday duty is an analogy ([Nicomachean Ethics III.6–9, W. D. Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

## Sources

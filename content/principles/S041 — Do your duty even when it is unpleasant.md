> **Principle 163** — Do your duty even when it is unpleasant

The Stoic does not avoid duty because it is uncomfortable. Marcus writes about getting out of bed in the morning to do the work of a human being. The unpleasant duty is often the most important one. Do not let discomfort determine your actions.

(Med. II.1, V.1, VII.5, VII.45; Disc. III.12, III.24; Seneca, Letters 20, 71, 104.)

**Plain words** The Stoic doesn't dodge duty because it's uncomfortable. Marcus talks himself out of bed to do a human's work. The unpleasant duty is often the most important one. Don't let discomfort pick your actions.

*For example:* Getting up at six to take your father to dialysis, again.

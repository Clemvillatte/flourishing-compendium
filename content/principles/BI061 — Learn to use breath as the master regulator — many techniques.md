> **Principle 61** — Learn to use breath as the master regulator: many techniques

Breath is a directly accessible physiological regulator. There are many breath techniques: slow breathing, box breathing, alternate nostril, physiological sigh, coherent breathing. Each has specific effects.

**Plain words** Breath is the master regulator — the only autonomic function with a manual override. Slow, box, alternate nostril, sigh: a whole toolkit on one dial. Learn three techniques; they cover most weather.

*For example:* The person who knows one breath for panic (the sigh), one for meetings (slow), and one for sleep (long exhale) — a Swiss army knife for the nervous system, always in the pocket.
## Psychology parallels

- **Breath as autonomic regulator (Lehrer, Zaccaro)**, breathing directly regulates the autonomic nervous system (Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014; Zaccaro et al., 2018)
- **Specific techniques**, slow breathing (relaxation), physiological sigh (exhale-focused practice), coherent breathing (~6 breaths/min for HRV) (Balban et al., 2023; Lehrer & Gevirtz, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Breath as anchor** (Buddhism, Anapanasati Sutta)
- **[[Qi|Qi (breath-energy)]]** (Daoism, Neiye)
- **[[Pranayama|Pranayama]]** (Yoga tradition)

## Practice

- Learn breath techniques: slow breathing (5 in, 5 out) for relaxation; physiological sigh (double inhale, extended exhale) for acute stress; coherent breathing (~6 breaths/min) for HRV. Use the appropriate one.

> **Principle 83** — Treat well-being as a practice, not a destination

The good life is a process, not a state of being. It is a direction, not a destination. The practices that produce flourishing are not completed. They are maintained, varied, and adapted over the course of a life. The principle is the recognition that well-being is not something you achieve and keep. It is something you practise, daily, for as long as you live.

(Rogers, 1961; Ryff, 1989.)

**Plain words** The good life isn't a state you reach and keep. It's a direction, practised and adapted over a whole life. The practices are never finished; they're maintained, varied and adjusted. That's not a disappointment. It's the shape of anything alive.

*For example:* A couple who've kept their weekly walk for thirty years, through illness, moves and grandchildren.

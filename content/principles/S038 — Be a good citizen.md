> **Principle 160** — Be a good citizen

> my nature is rational and social; and my city and country, so far as I am Antoninus, is Rome, but so far as I am a man, it is the world.

(Marcus Aurelius, Meditations VI.44, trans. George Long, [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.6.six.html), page opened; the section number is from the standard numbering and is not printed in this edition.)

In Book Four Marcus argues that if reason is common, "we are fellow-citizens; if this is so, we are members of some political community; if this is so, the world is in a manner a state" ([Meditations IV](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.4.four.html), opened). Citizenship here is first of all membership of the human community, and the entry's "political" reading is one way of drawing it out.

Seneca does not give one answer on public office. In Letter 73 he describes a man "who has left the senate and the bar and all affairs of state, that he may retire to nobler affairs" and who still honours the rulers who keep him safe ([Letter 73, Gummere trans., Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_73), opened). In Letter 14 he postpones the question: "we shall consider later whether the wise man ought to give his attention to politics," and points to "those Stoics who, shut out from public life, have withdrawn into privacy for the purpose of improving men's existence" ([Letter 14, Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_14), opened). The entry's firm "do not withdraw" is therefore the compendium's reading, and the sources opened show a more divided Stoic position.

The psychology parallel is also mixed. A systematic review of 53 articles on young adults found that "civic engagement demonstrates a heterogeneous relationship to well-being" ([Taylor & Francis, 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13676261.2022.2156779), abstract opened). The entry's Putterman (2018) citation could not be located and was not used. Claims in the original about Seneca advising Nero and Musonius's exile were not checked, and Bandura, Dewey and Kant were not opened.

**Plain words** Marcus counts himself a citizen of the whole human community, and Seneca's letters disagree about whether a wise person must hold public office. Civic involvement and wellbeing are linked in a mixed way in the research found.

*For example (imagined):* Someone attends a local planning meeting about clinic funding, and speaks up because the outcome affects neighbours they will never meet.

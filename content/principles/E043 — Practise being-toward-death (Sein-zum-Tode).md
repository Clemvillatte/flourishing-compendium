> **Principle 435** — Practise being-toward-death (Sein-zum-Tode)

The article on Heideggerian terminology describes Being-toward-death as not an orientation that brings Dasein closer to its end, in terms of clinical death, but rather a way of being. One's death is not an empirical event, and it is characterized as "ownmost potentiality-for-being, non-relational, and not to be out-stripped": ownmost as what illuminates Dasein in its individuality, non-relational because nobody can take one's death away or die in one's place. The article quotes the translation:

> "as soon as man comes to life, he is at once old enough to die." (quoted in [Wikipedia, Heideggerian terminology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heideggerian_terminology); the article cites Heidegger 1962, H. 255)

The everyday way of talking conceals this: "One dies" is interpreted as a fact, and comes to mean "nobody dies". Authentic being-toward-death calls Dasein's individual self out of its "they-self", and frees it to re-evaluate life from the standpoint of finitude.

(Being and Time, as cited by the opened article at H. 255.)

**Plain words** The everyday voice says "One dies", which means nobody in particular. The practice the principle proposes is to put your own name in that sentence. As a hypothetical: if you had ten summers left, which of your deferred decisions would you book on Tuesday?

*For example:* As an experiment, multiply your someday list by a realistic number of summers left, and see which three items you would book on Tuesday.

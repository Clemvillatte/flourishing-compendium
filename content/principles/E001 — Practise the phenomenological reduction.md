> **Principle 393** — Practise the phenomenological reduction

Suspend your naive acceptance of the natural world. The phenomenological reduction ([[Epoché|epoché]]) is the practice of bracketing your assumptions about the reality of what appears, in order to attend to how it appears. Do not take the world for granted. Ask: how does this appear to me? What is the structure of my experience?

(Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §§31-33; Husserl, 1929/1960, Cartesian Meditations, §§8-11; Husserl, 1954/1970, The Crisis of European Sciences, §§35-38.)

**Plain words** The epoché: suspend your naive acceptance that the world is "just there," and attend instead to how it appears. Don't argue with reality — just notice the appearing. Ask: how does this show up for me? What's the structure of the experience itself?

*For example:* Someone stops mid-argument and asks the phenomenologist's question — "what am I actually seeing here, before my story about it?" — and discovers the "hostility" they were reacting to was mostly their own annotation.

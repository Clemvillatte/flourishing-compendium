> **Principle 158** — Don't tax yourself dry

Daodejing 75 diagnoses a starving population:

> "The people suffer from famine because of the multitude of taxes consumed by their superiors. It is through this that they suffer famine." (DDJ 75, Legge trans., [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The same chapter carries the diagnosis one step further, to what overwork does to the value placed on life:

> "The people make light of dying because of the greatness of their labours in seeking for the means of living. It is this which makes them think light of dying." (DDJ 75)

The chapter is written about rulers and the governed. The compendium reads its logic as applying wherever one part of a system sets the demands another part must meet, including the demands you set for yourself.

(DDJ 75.)

**Plain words** The chapter's diagnosis has two steps: famine comes from what those above consume, and heavy labour in search of a living makes people think lightly of dying. Read for the self: the rules you set over your own days are also a government, and they can tax the one doing the living until the living itself is undervalued. A practical reading: when you notice exhaustion or indifference about your own life, ask what the demands above you are consuming.

*For example:* Imagine operating for months under a self-imposed rule that every hour must be productive. You could review the rule as a policy, asking who it serves and what it costs.

> **Principle 51** — Learn to use touch: self-soothing through contact

The parasympathetic claim is best supported for touch received from others. A review of moderate-pressure massage concludes that the stimulation of pressure receptors leads to increased vagal activity, which in turn seems to mediate the diverse benefits noted for massage therapy ([Field, Diego & Hernandez-Reif 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20402578/)). A study of affective touch, the slow pleasant stroking that activates C-tactile nerve fibres, found it effective in decreasing sympathetic activity and increasing vagal dominance, possibly buffering emotional distress ([Della Longa et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/scan/article/20/1/nsaf090/8248583?login=false)).

Self-touch is a subtler case. The brain-and-heart study that compared being stroked on the forearm, stroking one's own forearm, and stroking a pillow found the parasympathetic advantage sat with the social version: for cardiac parasympathetic indices, social touch consistently had higher values compared to self-touch, with no significant differences found between self- and object-touch ([Candia-Rivera et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07448-z)). Self-soothing touch remains a standard practice in self-compassion exercises, but the opened evidence does not certify it as a strong parasympathetic lever on its own ([Candia-Rivera et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07448-z)).

> "The stimulation of pressure receptors leads to increased vagal activity which, in turn, seems to mediate the diverse benefits noted for massage therapy." (Field, Diego & Hernandez-Reif 2010, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20402578/))

**Plain words**
Touch calms the body through pressure and stroking receptors, and the vagal pathway behind it is why the effects show up in heart-rate and stress measures. One honest limit from the opened studies: the parasympathetic advantage is best documented for touch received from another person, and in the one opened comparison, stroking your own arm measured weaker than being stroked. A hand on your own chest is still a portable practice, just not a certified substitute for human contact.

*For example:* An imagined case: someone alone after bad news puts a hand on their chest and breathes slowly, because that is what is available at midnight; the next day they also seek out people who can put an arm around them. The practice scales with availability, and the evidence favours the version with another person in it ([Candia-Rivera et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07448-z)).

## Psychology parallels

- **Touch and parasympathetic activation (Field)**, gentle touch reduces arousal and releases oxytocin (Field, 2010; Uvnäs-Moberg et al., 2015)
- **Self-soothing touch**, hand on heart, self-hug, and gentle self-touch activate the soothing system (Gilbert, 2009; Neff, 2011)

## Practice

- Use self-soothing touch: hand on heart, hand on arm, self-hug, as a [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]] practice. The parasympathetic evidence is strongest for moderate-pressure or affective touch received from another person ([Field et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20402578/); [Della Longa et al. 2025](https://academic.oup.com/scan/article/20/1/nsaf090/8248583?login=false)); self-touch is the portable version, not the strongest one ([Candia-Rivera et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07448-z)).

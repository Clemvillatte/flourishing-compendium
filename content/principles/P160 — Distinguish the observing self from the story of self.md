> **Principle 160** — Distinguish the observing self from the story of self

You are the context in which your story unfolds, not the story: self-as-content (the narrative) versus self-as-context (the arena). Rest identity in the observing, not the observed.

(Hayes, Strosahl and Wilson, 2011; Mead's "I" and "Me.")

**Plain words** The narrator changes, the chapters change; the one noticing all of it is a steadier vantage point than any single chapter. You are the context in which your story unfolds, not the story — a practical shift of view, not a metaphysical claim.

*For example:* The career failure that was going to 'define' you — ten years on, it's a chapter. The you who watched it happen, and watched the next thing, is still the one reading this. That's the arena, not the plot.

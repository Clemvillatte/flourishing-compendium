> **Principle 64** — Begin with faith, energy, and memory

Sutra 1.20 names the sequence: "[Concentration not conscious of objects,] which follows upon belief [and] energy [and] mindfulness [and] concentration [and] insight, is that to which the others [the yogins] attain" (trans. Woods, [p. 46](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft)). Vyasa's commentary reads it as a chain:

> Belief is the mental approval [of concentration]; for, like a good mother, it protects the yogin.

(Vyasa on Yoga Sutra 1.20, trans. Woods, [p. 46](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

"For him [thus] believing... there is the further (upa) attainment of energy. For him who has reached the further attainment of energy mindfulness is at hand", and when the mind-stuff is thereby concentrated, "he gains as his portion the discrimination of insight, by which he perceives things as they really are" (p. 46). The gloss adds that belief's object is "a reality which is quite accessible by verbal-communication or by inference or by the instruction of teachers" (p. 46).

### What the principle adds

The motivation-design reading, faith as working trust rather than belief in results, and the curiosity start are the principle's modern reading of the chain. What the commentary supplies is the order itself and the good-mother claim: approval of the practice comes first and protects the practitioner from going astray.

**Plain words** The order is the teaching: trust first, effort second, memory third. Trust here is approval of the practice, not conviction about metaphysics; the gloss says its object is something you can be told about, infer, or learn from a teacher. With that approval present, energy follows it, and with energy, memory holds. The repair move when energy is missing is therefore upstream: revisit the trust, not the will.

*For example:* Imagined: a beginner who cannot honestly claim faith can claim curiosity, which funds one week of practice, which produces one small result, which produces the approval the chain starts with. The sequence builds itself once it starts somewhere.

(Yoga Sutra 1.20 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's Tattva-vaicaradi, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), p. 46.)

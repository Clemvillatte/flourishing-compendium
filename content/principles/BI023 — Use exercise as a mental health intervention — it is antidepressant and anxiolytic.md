> **Principle 23** — Use exercise as a mental health intervention: it is antidepressant and anxiolytic

The evidence comes in two kinds. For prevention, a pooled analysis of 49 prospective studies following 266,939 people for 1,837,794 person-years found that people with high physical activity had lower odds of developing depression than people with low activity, with the protection holding across age groups and regions ([Schuch et al., 2018, Am J Psychiatry](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29690792/)). For symptoms, a meta-meta-analysis of randomised trials in non-clinical adults quantified how much activity moves the needle ([Rebar et al., 2015, Health Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25739893/)):

> "Physical activity reduced depression by a medium effect [standardised mean difference (SMD) = -0.50; 95% CI: -0.93 to -0.06] and anxiety by a small effect (SMD = -0.38; 95% CI: -0.66 to -0.11)." (Abstract, Results; Rebar et al., 2015, Health Psychol Rev 9(3): 366-78, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25739893/))

> "Available evidence supports the notion that physical activity can confer protection against the emergence of depression regardless of age and geographical region." (Abstract, Conclusions; Schuch et al., 2018, Am J Psychiatry 175(7): 631-48, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29690792/))

**Plain words**
Two findings sit behind this principle. People who move a lot are less likely to develop depression later; that is what following 266,939 people for years found. Among people who are already low or anxious, activity makes symptoms smaller, by a small-to-medium margin. Both findings are averages, and neither says exercise fixes a life on its own. They say regular movement belongs in the plan.

**For example**
*(imagined)* Someone recovering from a depressive episode schedules three brisk walks a week the way they schedule their therapy sessions. The walk alone does not lift the episode; the evidence puts regular activity inside the overall plan.

## Sources

- [Schuch et al., 2018, Am J Psychiatry 175(7): 631-48, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29690792/) — abstract: 49 prospective studies, adjusted odds ratio 0.83 for incident depression
- [Rebar et al., 2015, Health Psychol Rev 9(3): 366-78, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25739893/) — abstract: meta-meta-analysis, medium effect on depression, small effect on anxiety

## Psychology parallels

- **Exercise and depression**, exercise reduces depressive symptoms (Schuch et al., 2018; Cooney et al., 2013)
- **Exercise and anxiety**, exercise reduces anxiety (Rebar et al., 2015; Herring et al., 2014)
- **Exercise as adjunct treatment**, exercise enhances standard treatments for depression (Schuch et al., 2018; Cooney et al., 2013)
- **Note: effects are robust but moderate; specific dose and type matter** (Cooney et al., 2013; Rebar et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Use exercise as a mental health intervention: regular moderate exercise (30 minutes, 3-5 times per week) reduces depression and anxiety. Do not wait for motivation; schedule it.

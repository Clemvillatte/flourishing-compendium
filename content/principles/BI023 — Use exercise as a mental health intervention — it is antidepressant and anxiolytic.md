> **Principle 23** — Use exercise as a mental health intervention: it is antidepressant and anxiolytic

Exercise is a robust mental health intervention. It reduces depression, anxiety, and stress, and improves mood, cognition, and well-being. It is one of the most evidence-supported lifestyle behaviours for mental health.

**Plain words** Exercise is a real psychiatric intervention — it reduces depression and anxiety with effect sizes that rival medications for mild-to-moderate cases. Not "go for a walk, cheer up" — a prescription with a dose. The side effects are suspiciously good.

*For example:* The GP writing "30 minutes brisk, five times a week" like a drug — because the evidence treats it like one. The pharmacy is a pair of shoes.
## Psychology parallels

- **Exercise and depression**, exercise reduces depressive symptoms (Schuch et al., 2018; Cooney et al., 2013)
- **Exercise and anxiety**, exercise reduces anxiety (Rebar et al., 2015; Herring et al., 2014)
- **Exercise as adjunct treatment**, exercise enhances standard treatments for depression (Schuch et al., 2018; Cooney et al., 2013)
- **Note: effects are robust but moderate; specific dose and type matter** (Cooney et al., 2013; Rebar et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Use exercise as a mental health intervention: regular moderate exercise (30 minutes, 3-5 times per week) reduces depression and anxiety. Do not wait for motivation; schedule it.

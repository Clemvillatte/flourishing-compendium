> **Principle 146** — Test speech before it leaves

Is it true? Is it timely? Is it beneficial? Is it gentle? If it fails any of these, refine or hold it.

(Sutta Nipata 3.3; Abhaya Sutta, MN 58.)

### Where the gates come from

The four questions in the statement draw on two passages of the Canon. In the Abhaya Sutta the standard is truth, benefit and timing: the Buddha speaks only what is factual, true and beneficial, and even then, "he has a sense of the proper time for saying them", with the reason given: "Because the Tathagata has sympathy for living beings" ([MN 58, Thanissaro trans.](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.058.than.html)). In the Sutta Nipāta the four are manner, justice, feeling and truth:

> "Monks, speech endowed with four characteristics is well-spoken, not poorly spoken -- faultless & not to be faulted by the wise. Which four? There is the case where a monk says only what it well-spoken, not what is poorly spoken; only what is just, not what is unjust; only what is endearing, not what is unendearing; only what is true, not what is false." — Sutta Nipāta III.3 (Thanissaro trans., [Primary source scan](https://cdn.britannica.com/primary_source/gutenberg/PGCC_classics/canon/khuddaka/suttanipata/snp3-03.html))

### What the test catches

The MN 58 list is mostly a list of things not to say: of the six combinations of true or untrue, beneficial or not, endearing or not, the Buddha does not speak in four, and in the two he does, timing still governs. Speech that is true but unbeneficial is not said; speech that is endearing but untrue is not said. The gate is not "is it nice": truth and benefit are required, endearment is not, and even what passes waits for its time.

**Plain words**
Take the message you have half-written to a friend who let you down. Ask it four things: is every claim in it one you know to be so? does the friendship come out better if it lands? is tonight the night it can land? is the tone one she can receive? Any no means holding it or rewriting it, and holding is the more common fix. That is your situation; the sutta's own rule is the standard behind it: nothing untrue, nothing unbeneficial, and even the true and beneficial said only at the proper time.

*For example:* The perfect comeback that was true but wrong in tone and wrong for the moment: held at the gate, it aged into wisdom instead of a wound.

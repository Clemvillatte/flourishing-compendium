> **Principle 45** — Read the Popol Vuh's warnings: arrogance falls

The K'iche' creation book's recurring moral: the first beings were destroyed for arrogance (they forgot their makers and their limits), and the true humans were made humble, grateful, and sighted. The narrative's psychology is the tradition's own: inflation (forgetting one's dependence) is the characteristic failure of every success story in the book, and humility-before-the-whole is the survival trait. Practically, at each genuine success, run the book's check: what am I forgetting that this depended on?

(Popol Vuh, Part One, the destruction passages; Part Three, the creation of true humans.)

**Plain words** The Popol Vuh's recurring moral: the first beings were destroyed for arrogance — they forgot their makers and their limits — and the true humans were made humble, grateful, and sighted. Inflation (forgetting your dependence) is the characteristic failure of every success story in the book.

*For example:* The founder who starts believing the press releases — forgets the co-founders, the luck, the teachers, the dead grandmother's sacrifice — and the fall, when it comes, follows the old plot exactly.

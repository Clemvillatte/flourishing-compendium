> **Principle 194** — Be your own island (attadipa)

In the Mahaparinibbana Sutta, near the end of his life, the Buddha tells Ananda to make an island of himself. The sutta defines the island immediately, and the definition is concrete: the four foundations of mindfulness, contemplating body, feelings, mind, and mental objects. Standing on your own means standing on the practice. (DN 16.)

> "Therefore, Ananda, be islands unto yourselves, refuges unto yourselves, seeking no external refuge; with the Dhamma as your island, the Dhamma as your refuge, seeking no other refuge." (DN 16, Vajira & Story)

> "When he dwells contemplating the body in the body, earnestly, clearly comprehending, and mindfully, after having overcome desire and sorrow in regard to the world; when he dwells contemplating feelings in feelings, the mind in the mind, and mental objects in mental objects, earnestly, clearly comprehending, and mindfully, after having overcome desire and sorrow in regard to the world, then, truly, he is an island unto himself, a refuge unto himself, seeking no external refuge; having the Dhamma as his island, the Dhamma as his refuge, seeking no other refuge." (DN 16, Vajira & Story)

The refuge is defined by what is practiced, so it does not depend on anyone being reachable. Teachers, books and communities remain supports; the footing is the practice itself.

**Plain words**
When the crisis comes and no one is reachable, the question is what you can still do. The sutta's answer is small and portable: attend to body, feeling, and mind as they are. Everything else in your life can support the practice, and none of it can substitute for doing it.

*For example:* The crisis that hits when the teacher is unreachable, the book is in storage: and you find your practice actually holds weight.

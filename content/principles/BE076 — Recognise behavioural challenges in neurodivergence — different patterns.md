> **Principle 76** — Recognise behavioural challenges in neurodivergence: different patterns

Neurodivergent individuals (autistic, ADHD, dyslexic, etc.) may have different behavioural patterns — different energy, different social needs, different sensory processing. These are variations, not deficits.

**Plain words** Neurodivergent behaviour isn't broken standard-issue behaviour — it's different architecture with different patterns, different triggers, different needs. The environment does most of the disabling. Adjust the environment, watch the behaviour re-contextualise.

*For example:* The autistic employee who "underperforms" in the open office and outperforms everyone from home — the behaviour didn't change; the sensory load did. That's not accommodation; that's physics.
## Psychology parallels

- **Neurodivergent behaviour**, autistic and ADHD individuals have different behavioural patterns (Happé & Frith, 2006; Brown, 2013)
- **Behavioural design for neurodivergence**, designing around patterns (not against them) improves outcomes (Kapp et al., 2013; Grandin, 2006)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Neurodiversity|Neurodiversity as variation]]** (Singer, 1998)
- **Different, not less** (Grandin)
- **The tree that is not judged by the forest** (Zhuangzi)

## Practice

- If you are neurodivergent, do not judge your behavioural patterns by neurotypical standards. Learn your pattern (energy, social needs, sensory processing) and design around it.

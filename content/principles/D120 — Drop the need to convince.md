> **Principle 120** — Drop the need to convince

Daodejing 56 states the principle at the top:

> "He who knows (the Tao) does not (care to) speak (about it); he who is (ever ready to) speak about it does not know it." (DDJ 56, Legge trans., [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The chapter is not a ban on speech. Its next lines describe someone who still engages, differently: "He will blunt his sharp points and unravel the complications of things; he will attemper his brightness, and bring himself into agreement with the obscurity (of others)." The practice is to remove the edge that turns discussion into contest, and the chapter names the result "the Mysterious Agreement" — read here as agreement reached without the usual machinery of winning.

(DDJ 56.)

**Plain words** The need to convince is a need: it lives in you, whatever the topic is doing. DDJ 56's observation is that constant readiness to argue is itself evidence of not knowing — you are still gripping something you have not finished understanding. Practical version: when you feel the urge to win a point, ask whether the listener needs this point or you do. Sometimes the answer is you, and the honest move is to say so.

*For example:* The person who actually changed your mind did it with one question, not a hundred arguments.

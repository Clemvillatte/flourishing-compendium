> **Principle 70** — Recognise narcissistic patterns: self-esteem that requires others

Narcissistic patterns — grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy — involve self-esteem that is contingent on external validation and is therefore fragile. This is a specific self-relational pattern requiring specific treatment.

**Plain words** Narcissism isn't too much self-esteem; it's self-esteem that can't generate its own supply. It runs on admiration from outside, and it needs more every quarter. The tell is what happens when the applause stops: secure people shrug; this pattern collapses or explodes.

*For example:* The colleague who's magnificent when praised and punitive when corrected isn't having a bad day. His self-worth has no internal engine — it's plugged into the room. Every interaction with him is secretly about keeping the lights on.
## Psychology parallels

- **Narcissism as fragile self-esteem**, grandiosity masks fragile, contingent self-worth (Kernberg, 1975; Morf & Rhodewalt, 2001)
- **Narcissism is associated with worse outcomes**, vulnerable narcissism is associated with depression and anxiety (Miller et al., 2011; Pincus et al., 2009)

## Practice

- If you notice narcissistic patterns (need for admiration, difficulty with empathy, fragile self-esteem), seek professional help. This is a specific pattern requiring specific treatment.

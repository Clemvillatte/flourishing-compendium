> **Principle 175** — To receive, first give

Daodejing 36 lists preparatory opposites: strengthen before weakening, raise before overthrowing, give before despoiling. Legge calls the procedure hiding the light. This is a passage about concealed strategy. Giving before asking can be adapted into a different personal practice, provided the change of purpose is made clear.

(DDJ 36.)

> "when he is going to despoil another, he will first have made gifts to him" (DDJ 36, excerpt from [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

The application here concerns honest offers, not a technique for making someone owe you.

### Plain words

If you offer help, say whether a return is expected. Give only what you are willing to give under the arrangement actually agreed. An exchange can be proposed openly; do not present it as a free gift and charge for it later.

### For example

Draft an offer to read a colleague's essay in two forms: unconditional help, and an explicit exchange of feedback. Compare which you actually intend and could honour as stated. Use the honest form, then keep the distinction for your next offer. The overt-behaviour practice is making the terms clear.

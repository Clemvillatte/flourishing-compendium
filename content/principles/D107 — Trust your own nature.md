> **Principle 107** — Trust your own nature

The duck's legs are short: stretching them is grief; the crane's are long — cutting them is misery. Don't lengthen or shorten yourself to fit.

(Zhuangzi 8.)

**Plain words** The duck's legs are short — stretching them is grief; the crane's are long — cutting them is misery. Don't lengthen or shorten yourself to fit. Every self-improvement project that's actually self-erasure is a pair of scissors aimed at your own legs.

*For example:* The introvert forcing themselves into a sales career to 'fix' themselves — the legs were fine; the job was a mismatch.

## Philosophy parallels



- **[[Conatus|Conatus]]** (Spinoza, Ethics III), each being strives to persevere in its own being
- **[[Amour de soi|Amour de soi]]** (Rousseau, Emile)
- **"become what you are"** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)

**Nietzsche's formulation:** "You shall become the person you are" (*Du sollst der werden, der du bist*). This is often read as a call to realize a latent, pre-existing self. The wording does not, by itself, commit Nietzsche to a pre-existing essential self. A productive comparison (not a verified historical disagreement): Deleuze's concept of becoming is not the unfolding of a prior being — it is *hybrid*, *transversal*, a becoming-other that begins from what is neither one thing nor the other. This is a labeled comparison, not a claim that Deleuze read this specific aphorism.

**Source note:** the Deleuze comparison is verified against Claire Colebrook, *Gilles Deleuze* (Routledge Critical Thinkers, Routledge, 2002), pp. 37 and 133 (see "transversal becoming"). The compilation PDF (115 MB) contains this work; the passage on becoming-animal being hybrid and transversal is at PDF page index 145.

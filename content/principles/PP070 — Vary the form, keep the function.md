> **Principle 70** — Vary the form, keep the function

The idea comes from research on hedonic adaptation. Lyubomirsky, Sheldon and Schkade proposed that activities offer the best chance of lasting gains because, unlike circumstances, they can be varied. In their path model, "only positive activity change predicted maintained happiness gains" at a later time point ([Lyubomirsky, Sheldon and Schkade 2005](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lyubomirsky-Sheldon-Schkade-2005.pdf)). The same group's later hedonic adaptation prevention model says variety can be added by "completing multiple activities simultaneously, alternating among activities, and altering their execution" ([Wiley handbook chapter summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118315927.ch21)).

The direct test is a 10-week randomised trial. Participants who "performed a wide variety of kind acts showed increases in happiness", while those who repeated the same acts "showed decreases in happiness across the intervention" ([Oxford chapter by Layous and Lyubomirsky, as read here](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34277/chapter/290646819?guestAccessKey=)). That summary comes from the model's own authors, and The original paper is cited at second hand. It is also one trial, in kindness.

(Lyubomirsky et al., 2005; Sheldon et al., 2013.)

### Scope

The original entry's rule to keep the function while varying the form is this page's own. These studies varied the acts within one practice, such as kindness, and did not test switching from writing to voice journaling. No evidence on that was found.

**Plain words**
Hedonic adaptation eats circumstances and spares activities, roughly because activities can be varied. The one direct trial is narrower than the rule: people doing varied kind acts gained happiness while people repeating the same acts slid. Nothing in it tests changing the form of a journaling or [[Savoring|savoring]] practice. So take the rule as a hedge: when a practice starts tasting flat, vary how you do it before dropping it.

**For example**
If you do one kind act a week, plan a different act each week for ten weeks and note which ones you would repeat.

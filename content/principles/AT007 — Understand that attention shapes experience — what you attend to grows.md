> **Principle 7** — Understand that attention shapes experience: what you attend to grows

Attention is not neutral. What you attend to becomes more salient, more elaborated, and more influential in your experience and behaviour. Attending to threats makes the world feel more threatening; attending to gratitude makes it feel more abundant. This is not positive thinking; it is the mechanics of attention.

**Plain words** What you attend to grows — not mystically, but it gets elaborated, rehearsed, and weighted in your model of the world. Attention is a vote on what your life will be about. Spend it like it matters, because it compounds.

*For example:* Two neighbours live on the same street. One attends to the broken things, the other to the blooming things. Same street, different worlds — and the difference is just where the beam went.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attention shapes perception and memory**, attended information is processed more deeply and remembered better (Craik & Lockhart, 1972; Chun & Turk-Browne, 2007)
- **Attentional bias maintains anxiety and depression**, attending to threat maintains anxiety; attending to negative self-referential information maintains depression (Bar-Haim et al., 2007; Disner et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right mindfulness|Right mindfulness]]** (Buddhism, Satipatthana Sutta)
- **[[Gratitude]]** (Stoics, Seneca, Letters)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] includes examined attention** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Notice what your attention habitually selects. If it habitually selects threats, problems, and deficiencies, this is a trainable bias, not a truth about the world. Deliberately redirect attention toward what matters and what is working.

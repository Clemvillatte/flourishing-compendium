> **Principle 7** — Understand that attention shapes experience: what you attend to grows

Two findings anchor this principle. First, a meta-analysis of 172 studies concluded that anxious individuals show a reliable bias of attention toward threat, and that the bias does not appear in nonanxious individuals. ([Bar-Haim et al., 2007](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/dominiquelamy/files/2014/08/Bar-Haim_et_2007.pdf)) Second, a review of attention and memory concluded that the two systems cannot operate without each other: attending to something raises the likelihood of remembering it, and what is remembered in turn guides what gets attended next. ([Chun and Turk-Browne, 2007](https://docslib.org/doc/2149068/interactions-between-attention-and-memory-marvin-m-chun-and-nicholas-b-turk-browne))

(Bar-Haim, Lamy, Pergamin, Bakermans-Kranenburg and van IJzendoorn, "Threat-Related Attentional Bias in Anxious and Nonanxious Individuals: A Meta-Analytic Study," Psychological Bulletin 133(1), 2007; Chun and Turk-Browne, "Interactions between attention and memory," Current Opinion in Neurobiology 17(2), 2007.)

> "The main conclusion of this set of meta-analytic studies is that the threat-related bias is a robust phenomenon in anxious individuals and does not exist in nonanxious individuals." (Bar-Haim, Lamy, Pergamin, Bakermans-Kranenburg and van IJzendoorn, [Threat-Related Attentional Bias in Anxious and Nonanxious Individuals](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/dominiquelamy/files/2014/08/Bar-Haim_et_2007.pdf), Psychological Bulletin 133(1), 2007)

The review of attention and memory states the interdependence plainly: "Attention and memory cannot operate without each other." ([Chun and Turk-Browne, 2007](https://docslib.org/doc/2149068/interactions-between-attention-and-memory-marvin-m-chun-and-nicholas-b-turk-browne)) Its authors note that "memory from past experience guides what should be attended." ([Chun and Turk-Browne, 2007](https://docslib.org/doc/2149068/interactions-between-attention-and-memory-marvin-m-chun-and-nicholas-b-turk-browne))

**Plain words**
Where attention goes, memory follows, and where memory goes, attention is sent back. ([Chun and Turk-Browne, 2007](https://docslib.org/doc/2149068/interactions-between-attention-and-memory-marvin-m-chun-and-nicholas-b-turk-browne)) The anxious person's world feels threatening partly because attention keeps sampling for threat, and each sample becomes material for the next. ([Bar-Haim et al., 2007](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/dominiquelamy/files/2014/08/Bar-Haim_et_2007.pdf)) This is not positive thinking; it is the mechanics of attention. Attention is a vote on what your life will be about. Spend it like it matters, because it compounds.

*For example:* Two neighbours live on the same street. One attends to the broken things, the other to the blooming things. Same street, different worlds — and the difference is just where the beam went.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attention shapes perception and memory**, attended information is processed more deeply and remembered better (Craik & Lockhart, 1972; Chun & Turk-Browne, 2007)
- **Attentional bias maintains anxiety and depression**, attending to threat maintains anxiety; attending to negative self-referential information maintains depression (Bar-Haim et al., 2007; Disner et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right mindfulness|Right mindfulness]]** (Buddhism, Satipatthana Sutta)
- **[[Gratitude]]** (Stoics, Seneca, Letters)

## Practice

- Notice what your attention habitually selects. If it habitually selects threats, problems, and deficiencies, this is a trainable bias, not a truth about the world. Deliberately redirect attention toward what matters and what is working.

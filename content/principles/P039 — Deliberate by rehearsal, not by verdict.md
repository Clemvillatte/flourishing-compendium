> **Principle 39** — Deliberate by rehearsal, not by verdict

Dewey's description of deliberation in Human Nature and Conduct is one of the most quotable lines in American philosophy: "deliberation is a dramatic rehearsal (in imagination) of various competing lines of action" ([Dewey 1922, MW 14:132](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey1922_17.html)). The picture is not a judge weighing arguments. It is a theatre: each competing habit and impulse takes its turn projecting itself on the screen of imagination, unrolling the future it would have if it were given head.

The mechanism matters more than the metaphor. Dewey is explicit that this is an experiment, not a verdict: the trial is in imagination, not overt fact; thought runs ahead and foresees outcomes so you do not have to wait for the instruction of actual failure and disaster ([Dewey 1922, MW 14:132-3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey1922_17.html)). An act tried out in the world is irrevocable. An act tried out in imagination is retrievable.

> "Deliberation is an experiment in finding out what the various lines of possible action are really like... But the trial is in imagination, not in overt fact... Thought runs ahead and foresees outcomes, and thereby avoids having to await the instruction of actual failure and disaster. An act overtly tried out is irrevocable, its consequences cannot be blotted out. An act tried out in imagination is not final or fatal. It is retrievable." (John Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, 1922, [Mead Project](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey1922_17.html))

**Plain words** Most people treat a decision as a trial they must win. Dewey's move is to notice that you already own a rehearsal space where nothing is on the record. Run the two options through the imagination as actual scenes — the conversation, the morning after, the response — and something happens that no amount of pros-and-cons listing can produce: you find out which future you actually want to live in. The mistake is not choosing badly; the mistake is choosing without having run the scenes at all.

*For example:* Before quitting a job, two people deliberate. One lists pros and cons on paper for a week. The other spends ten minutes walking through the actual Monday meeting — the words, the face of the manager, the drive home. The imagined scene collapses in ten seconds and the decision is made. The list was still going; the rehearsal had already finished.

> **Principle 29** — Stop before the cup overflows

Chapter 9 of the Daodejing gives three images of the same moment: a filled vessel that cannot be carried, a sharpened point that loses its edge if kept being felt, gold and jade filling a hall beyond safe keeping. Each is a thing taken past its proper stop. The chapter's counsel is withdrawal at the right time: "When the work is done, and one's name is becoming distinguished, to withdraw into obscurity is the way of Heaven."

> It is better to leave a vessel unfilled, than to attempt
> to carry it when it is full.  If you keep feeling a point that has been
> sharpened, the point cannot long preserve its sharpness.
>
> (Daodejing 9, Legge)

The third image adds the social form: "When wealth and honours lead to arrogancy, this brings its evil on itself." The overflow is not only material; the person who keeps pushing past fullness turns the gain into the cause of the loss.

(DDJ 9.)

**Plain words**
A vessel filled to the brim cannot be carried without spilling, and neither can a schedule, a budget or a negotiation. The extra you squeeze in past what you can carry tends to end up on the floor. Stop while the thing still carries.

*For example:* Negotiating one more concession after the deal is already good is how deals die; the seasoned closer stops at good.

> **Principle 78** — Distinguish settling with and without seed

The sutra distinguishes a settling trained on an object (seeded) from another state that follows the practiced cessation of thought and still carries the residue of latent impressions (YS 1.18); the fully seedless absorption (nirbija, YS 1.50-1.51) is the later stage in which even the impression of truth-bearing insight is stilled. The distinction matters practically: cessation-based stillness is trained, and the seedless state is described as what the training finally leaves behind. Practically, end each sitting with one minute of no chosen object, as an adaptation for learning the difference the tradition takes years to draw.

(YS 1.17-1.18 with YS 1.50-1.51.)

**Plain words** The sutra distinguishes a settling trained on an object (seeded) from a state that follows practiced cessation and still carries residue; the fully seedless absorption is a later stage. Practically: don't confuse the pleasant stillness that comes early with the trained states that come later — label them and keep going.

*For example:* The meditator who hits a lovely blank quiet in month two and spends three years trying to recreate it — versus the one who labels it "seeded settling, early," and continues the curriculum. One has a souvenir; the other has a path.

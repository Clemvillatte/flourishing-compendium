> **Principle 78** — Distinguish settling with and without seed

Patanjali separates two kinds of settled concentration. The first has something in front of it. The second has no object left to lean on. Sutra 1.18 describes the second:

> The other [concentration which is not conscious of objects] consists of subliminal-impressions only [after objects have merged], and follows upon that practice which effects the cessation [of fluctuations].

(Yoga Sutras 1.18, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, pp. 41-42, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

Of the first kind the commentary says: "All these kinds of concentrations have an object upon which they rest." Sutra 1.46 calls those forms "the seeded concentration". For the other kind, the commentary names a state "in which only subliminal-impressions are left and in which all fluctuations have come to rest", and calls it "seedless".

Sutra 1.51 goes one step further. The yogin restricts even insight's trace and gains "seedless concentration". The commentary there says the restriction covers "even those subliminal-impressions made by insight and not merely the insight [itself]" (p. 99).

So the text draws one line at whether any object remains, and another at whether even insight's own trace is stilled. Splitting 1.18 as the half-way stage from 1.51 as the seedless one is this page's own tidy reading, because the commentary already calls the 1.18 state seedless. The suggestion of one minute at the end of a sitting with no chosen object is also this page's own adaptation.

**Plain words**

Some stillness still has something to hold, and some does not. The commentary pushes further, to a point where even what insight leaves behind is let go. A quiet stretch with no object is not necessarily the end of the path.

**For example**

A meditator finds a blank, easy quiet in her second month and spends a year trying to bring it back. Reading the sutras, she sees the text counts more than one kind of stillness, so she gives the early one its name and keeps practising.

> **Principle 42** — Learn to use nutrition for mental health: food affects mood

The SMILES trial put the principle to a test. Adults with moderate to severe depression received either seven sessions with a clinical dietitian supporting a modified Mediterranean diet, or social support on the same visit schedule. At twelve weeks the diet group had improved more on the Montgomery-Åsberg depression rating scale (Cohen's d = -1.16), and 32.3% met remission criteria against 8.0% of the control group. The authors emphasise the study's preliminary nature and the need for replication.

The wider evidence agrees in direction but is smaller. Across sixteen randomised trials with 45,826 participants, dietary interventions reduced depressive symptoms by a small amount (Hedges' g = 0.162), with no detectable effect on anxiety. An observational meta-analysis of 21 studies finds the same split by pattern: diets high in vegetables, fish and olive oil go with less depression, western-style diets high in processed meat and sweets with more.

> "this is the first RCT to explicitly seek to answer the question: If I improve my diet, will my mental health improve?" (Jacka et al. 2017, [BMC Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5282719/))

**Plain words**
Two kinds of study sit behind the principle. Trials where people improved their diet found somewhat fewer symptoms of depression afterwards; in the SMILES trial, about a third of the dietitian group met remission criteria, against under a tenth of the control group. Surveys of what people eat find the same direction: more vegetables, fish and olive oil with less depression, more processed food and sweets with more.

*For example:* An imagined case: someone with low mood who lives on takeaway and sweets asks their doctor whether diet matters. The evidence-backed referral is a few sessions with a dietitian on a whole dietary pattern, not a supplement shelf; the trials concern whole diets, and the meta-analysts state the effective components are still unknown.

## Psychology parallels

- **Diet and mental health (SMILES trial)**, Mediterranean diet improves depressive symptoms (Jacka et al., 2017; Francis et al., 2019)
- **Gut-brain axis**, the microbiome affects mood and cognition (Cryan et al., 2019; Dash et al., 2015)
- **Inflammation and depression**, pro-inflammatory diets are associated with depression (Jacka et al., 2017; Akbaraly et al., 2009)

## Practice

- Use nutrition for mental health: eat a Mediterranean-style diet (vegetables, fruits, fish, olive oil, whole grains) and reduce ultra-processed food, the pattern tested in the SMILES trial. Food affects mood.

> **Principle 42** — Learn to use nutrition for mental health: food affects mood

Nutrition affects mental health. A Mediterranean-style diet (vegetables, fruits, fish, olive oil, whole grains) is associated with better mental health. Ultra-processed food, high sugar, and inflammatory foods are associated with worse mental health.

**Plain words** Food is mood chemistry — the Mediterranean pattern (vegetables, fish, olive oil, the boring stuff your grandmother knew) has trial evidence for depression. Not "eat salad, be happy" — a real, modest effect. The kitchen is part of the mental health toolkit.

*For example:* The "nothing works" depressive episode that lifts a notch after three months of actual cooking — not a miracle, a shift in the substrate. The brain is made of groceries.
## Psychology parallels

- **Diet and mental health (SMILES trial)**, Mediterranean diet improves depressive symptoms (Jacka et al., 2017; Francis et al., 2019)
- **Gut-brain axis**, the microbiome affects mood and cognition (Cryan et al., 2019; Dash et al., 2015)
- **Inflammation and depression**, pro-inflammatory diets are associated with depression (Jacka et al., 2017; Akbaraly et al., 2009)

## Practice

- Use nutrition for mental health: eat a Mediterranean-style diet (vegetables, fruits, fish, olive oil, whole grains), reduce ultra-processed food, and stay hydrated. Food affects mood.

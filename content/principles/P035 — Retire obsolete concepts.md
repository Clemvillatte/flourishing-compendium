> **Principle 35** — Retire obsolete concepts

Dewey's account of ideas in The Quest for Certainty states the retirement rule directly:

> "ideas and idealisms are in themselves hypotheses not finalities. Being connected with operations to be performed, they are tested by the consequences of these operations, not by what exists prior to them." (Dewey, The Quest for Certainty, p. 166, [text](https://archive.org/details/questforcertaint032529mbp))

The same chapter describes "conception and systems of conceptions, ends in view and plans" as "constantly making and remaking as fast as those already in use reveal their weaknesses, defects and positive values" (p. 166), and the closing discussion of valuation says of the intellectual instrumentalities drawn from past experience that "they are tools, not finalities" (pp. 371-372). Quine gives the rule its sharpest edge: "Any statement can be held true come what may, if we make drastic enough adjustments elsewhere in the system. Conversely, by the same token, no statement is immune to revision" ("Two Dogmas of Empiricism," 1951, [PDF](https://cla.csulb.edu/departments/philosophy/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Quine-1951-two-dogmas-of-empiricism.pdf)). Neither text supplies a procedure for deciding when a personal concept is obsolete; Dewey's account concerns scientific and philosophic inquiry. The compendium's application: name the problem a concept was purchased for, then check whether that problem still exists.

### Plain words

A concept is a purchase made for a problem. When the problem is gone, the purchase is a cost: it still demands attention and blocks alternatives. Retirement is not a verdict on the past. It is filing the tool under problems you no longer have.

### For example

Take the "boss/employee" vocabulary in a three-person collaborative studio. The exercise is to name what the vocabulary was purchased for, clear authority in a large hierarchy, and to check whether the studio still has that problem. The exercise does not prescribe the replacement; it makes the mismatch visible.

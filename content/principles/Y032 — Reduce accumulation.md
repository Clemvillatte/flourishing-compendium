> **Principle 32** — Reduce accumulation

Sutra 2.30 lists abstinence-from-acceptance-of-gifts (aparigraha) fifth among the observances. Vyasa's commentary grounds the rule in upkeep rather than in sin:

> And even authorized objects, when acquired, are evidently disadvantageous, in that they must be kept and so on. Therefore abstinence-from-acceptance-of-gifts is the refusal to appropriate them.

(Vyasa on Yoga Sutra 2.30, trans. Woods, [p. 179](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

The disadvantage of attachment he quotes from his own gloss on 2.15: "Since passions increase because of application to enjoyments, and the skill of the organs also increases" (p. 179). Sutra 2.39 attaches a fruit to the perfected observance, "a thorough illumination upon the conditions of birth", which Vyasa renders as questions: "Who was I? How was I? ... Or what shall we become?" (p. 187). The Gita's virtue list pairs the observance with "Detachment, lightly holding unto home, Children, and wife, and all that bindeth men" (Arnold, ch. 13; verse breaks joined).

### What the principle adds

The "appointment for attention" phrasing and the subtraction test are the principle's modern reading of the upkeep point. What the commentary supplies is narrower and firmer: the rule operates at acquisition, refusing to appropriate things whose keeping is evidently a disadvantage, however honestly they came your way.

**Plain words** Ownership has a running bill: space to put the thing, effort to mind it, legs to move it. The old rule is set at the door, not at the cleanup: when taking something in is plainly going to cost more in upkeep than it gives, you decline it, even if it comes honestly. A quick way to run the check before you buy is to name what will leave the house when the new thing enters; if nothing will, you have your answer.

*For example:* a fourth jacket bought on sale spends three years moving between closets and being rehung each season. Before buying it, the test would have asked which of the four leaves when it arrives. None was going to, and that was the answer.

(Yoga Sutra 2.30 and 2.39 with Vyasa's Bhasya, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 179, 187; Bhagavad Gita ch. 13 in Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg 2388.)

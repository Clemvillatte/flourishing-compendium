> **Principle 23** — Practice the return to the ordinary as the completion

The Kyoto School's endpoint is deliberately anticlimactic: after the collapse, the doubt, the stepping back, the return is to dishes, work, and family, now without the layer of self-referential noise. The tradition's phrase for the endpoint is "the ordinary mind" and its marks are unremarkable: patience, warmth, availability. Practically, treat ordinariness as the criterion of progress in this whole book; the special states are weather, and the ordinary is the climate you are cultivating.

(The Zen tradition's ordinary mind; Nishitani's return.)

**Plain words** The Kyoto School ends on purpose with something plain: after the collapse, the doubt and the stepping back, you return to dishes, work and family, minus the self-referential noise. The tradition's name for it is the ordinary mind, and its marks are patience, warmth and availability. Ordinariness is the measure.

*For example:* A person who has spent years on retreats and reading shows it by being the one who quietly stays to wash up.

- **Functioning and well-being measures**, the evidence-based point that life functioning (work, relationships, routine) is a core outcome, not a distraction from growth (the well-being measurement literature)

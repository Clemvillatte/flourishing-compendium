> **Principle 160** — Be patient with your own maturation

Chapter 41 of the Daodejing is about what the Tao looks like from outside: the brightest way "seems light to lack", progress "seems drawing back", its even way "like a rugged track". Among the sentence-makers' images is the one this principle takes: "A vessel great, it is the slowest made."

> A vessel great, it is the slowest made;
>   Loud is its sound, but never word it said;
>   A semblance great, the shadow of a shade.
>
> (Daodejing 41, Legge)

The chapter's frame is hearing. Scholars of the highest class "earnestly carry it into practice"; the lowest "laugh greatly at it". "If it were not (thus) laughed at, it would not be fit to be the Tao." What looks like slow progress from inside is the vessel being made; the chapter ends by saying the Tao "is skilful at imparting (to all things what they need) and making them complete".

(DDJ 41.)

**Plain words**
The things that take longest to build show the least while they are being built. Comparing your third year of practice to someone else's thirtieth compares years, not people. The chapter's counsel is to judge the vessel by what it is becoming, not by how finished it looks today.

*For example:* Imagine someone who spends ten years on quiet work before anything public comes of it. The chapter's image asks what the ten years were doing: the vessel was being made.

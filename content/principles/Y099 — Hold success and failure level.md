> **Principle 99** — Hold success and failure level

Krishna tells Arjuna how to act and then names what he has just described:

> Be steadfast in the performance of your duty, O Arjun, abandoning attachment to success and failure. Such equanimity is called Yog.

(BG 2.48, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/48/).)

The Sanskrit word for equanimity is samatvam, evenness. Yoga here is a condition of the mind while you work, and the work is not set aside. Mukundananda's commentary gives the reason: "the effort is in our hands, not the results", so a person who has done their part has nothing left to be tossed about by.

The sharper reading is this principle's own: the habit of valuing yourself by outcomes is what does the swinging, so holding it level takes the swing away. The daily sentence at each peak and valley is a modern exercise, not something the verse prescribes.

**Plain words**

When things go well your mood goes up, and when they go badly it drops. That hands your day to whatever happens to you. The Gita says the steady mind is what it means by yoga. You still care and still work hard. What you give up is letting the result decide how you feel about yourself. A simple way to practise: pick one sentence and say the same one after good news and after bad.

**For example**

Imagine someone who writes the same short note to themselves on the day of a successful launch and again on the day a layoff is announced. The note changes nothing outside. It reminds them that the swing has a floor and a ceiling.

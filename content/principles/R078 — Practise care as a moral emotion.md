> **Principle 340** — Practise care as a moral emotion

Allow yourself to feel the moral emotions that care requires: compassion, sympathy, concern, and sometimes grief. These emotions are not weaknesses; they are the affective ground of moral responsiveness. Do not suppress them; let them guide your action.

(Held, 2006, The Ethics of Care; Noddings, 1984, Caring.)

**Plain words** Let yourself feel the moral emotions — compassion, sympathy, concern, sometimes grief. They're not noise or weakness; they're the affective ground of responding well. Suppressed, they leak; honoured, they steer.

*For example:* The manager who lets themselves actually feel the weight of a redundancy round — and because of that, does it humanely instead of by spreadsheet.

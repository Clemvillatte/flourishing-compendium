> **Principle 120** — Meet people where they are

In the third chapter, Krishna tells Arjuna how the enlightened should act toward those still attached to results. In Arnold's translation: "So let the enlightened toil, sense-freed, but set to bring the world deliverance, and its bliss; not sowing in those simple, busy hearts seed of despair. Yea! let each play his part in all he finds to do, with unyoked soul" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 3, trans. Arnold).

### Do not make them stumble

A few lines later: "Th' untaught live mixed with them, knowing not Nature's way, of highest aims unwitting, slow and dull. Those make thou not to stumble, having the light; but all thy dues discharging, for My sake, with meditation centred inwardly, seeking no profit, satisfied, serene, heedless of issue--fight!" (ch. 3, Arnold). The wise person is told to keep acting well himself, without unsettling the people who are not ready.

### What the principle adds

The text gives two instructions: avoid sowing despair or making the untaught stumble, and continue one's own duty with a settled mind. It says nothing about teaching beyond readiness, "hardening", arguments, or a week of modelling. The pedagogical reading, and the practice of pausing the argument with one person while modelling, are the principle's adaptation, not Arnold's wording.

**Plain words** Krishna tells the person who sees clearly not to shake those who are still busy with outcomes, and to keep doing his own work steadily. It is a limit on how the wise should act around others, not a teaching method. Using it to give up arguing with someone for a week is a modern application.

*For example:* Imagined example: you have been arguing with your brother about his spending. For a week you drop the subject and manage your own money in plain view. You see what he asks.

(Bhagavad-Gita ch. 3, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388, opened 2026-10-21. Arnold's verses are unnumbered, so the old cite, 3.26, is not checked against a numbered edition.)

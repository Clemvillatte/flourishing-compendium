> **Principle 29** — Practice truth that serves

Truthfulness (satya) is not indiscriminate accuracy; the Gita defines beneficial speech as true, agreeable, and promoting good, and the tradition places non-harm above truth-telling when they conflict. Practically, run speech through three gates: is it true, is it kind, is it useful; and pass no speech that fails the first gate even if it passes the others.

(YS 2.36; BG 17.15.)

**Plain words** Truthfulness isn't indiscriminate accuracy: the Gita defines beneficial speech as true, agreeable, and promoting good — and places non-harm above truth-telling when they conflict. Three gates: is it true, is it kind, is it useful? Not all truths need all doors open at once.

*For example:* Someone about to deliver the "honest" verdict on a friend's novel — runs it through the gates and finds a true-and-useful sentence that costs the friendship nothing. The truth arrived; the shrapnel didn't.

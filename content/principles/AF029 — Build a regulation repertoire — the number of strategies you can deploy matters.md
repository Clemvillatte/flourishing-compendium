> **Principle 29** — Build a regulation repertoire: the number of strategies you can deploy matters

Bonanno and Burton define the repertoire as "the ability to utilize a wide range of regulatory strategies that might accommodate divergent contextual demands and opportunities" (Regulatory Flexibility, Perspectives on Psychological Science 8(6), 2013, p. 594, [Columbia PDF](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/gab38/faculty-profile/files/2013_Bonanno_Burton_REGULATORY_FLEXIBLITY.pdf)). They measure it three ways: number of strategies, variability over time, and number of types used (pp. 598-600).

### What the evidence shows

On number, they report that students who stayed low in traumatic stress after a campus shooting also reported access to more strategies, and that students with fewer strategies "had considerably higher levels of traumatic stress" (pp. 598-599). They call the evidence "important preliminary evidence" and ask whether the link between flexibility and adjustment "may not be linear but rather curvilinear" (pp. 600-601, 604-605).

Later work is mixed. In four daily-life studies, using different strategies at the same moment went with less negative affect once overall strategy use was held constant, while change in one strategy over time gave inconsistent results ([Blanke et al., 2020, Emotion](https://www.fsv.uni-jena.de/fsvmedia/159170/blanke-et-al-2020-postprint-emotion-regulation-variability.pdf)). A 2025 study of 2,939 people found more regulatory flexibility went with higher well-being, with "diminishing returns" at the top, and its measure did not target repertoire size ([Werner et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12857431/)).

**Plain words**
With one move for a bad feeling, you are stuck the day it is unavailable. Several moves of different kinds give you something else to try.

**For example**
List what you did last month when you felt low or tense, then sort it into kinds: rethinking it, changing the situation, absorbing activity, company, letting the feeling be. If everything sits in one kind, learn one habit from another. The sorting is mine, not the authors'.

## Psychology parallels

- **Strategy repertoire is a component of flexibility**, the number of strategies a person can use, and the ability to shift between them, predicts affective outcomes (Blanke et al., 2020)
- **Repertoire breadth predicts better outcomes across clinical and non-clinical samples** (Aldao et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Cultivated capacity over innate disposition** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[The ten thousand things|The ten thousand things]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 64)

## Practice

- Deliberately learn and practise multiple regulation strategies: reappraisal, acceptance, problem-solving, self-distancing, [[Behavioral activation|behavioural activation]], mindfulness. Do not rely on one.

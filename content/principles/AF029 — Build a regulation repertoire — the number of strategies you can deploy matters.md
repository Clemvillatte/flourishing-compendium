> **Principle 29** — Build a regulation repertoire: the number of strategies you can deploy matters

Having more regulation strategies available, and being able to switch between them, is associated with better outcomes. A person with only one strategy (e.g., "suppress it") is fragile; a person with five or six is robust.

**Plain words** The number of strategies you can deploy matters: a person with one ("suppress it") is fragile; a person with ten is robust. Build the regulation repertoire — variety is the strength, not intensity.

*For example:* The person whose entire regulation toolkit is the gym — injured, they collapse emotionally, because the one tool is in the shop — versus the one with the walk, the call, the journal, the cooking, and the nap. Portfolio theory, for feelings.
## Psychology parallels

- **Strategy repertoire is a component of flexibility**, the number of strategies a person can use, and the ability to shift between them, predicts affective outcomes (Blanke et al., 2020)
- **Repertoire breadth predicts better outcomes across clinical and non-clinical samples** (Aldao et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Cultivated capacity over innate disposition** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)

## Practice

- Deliberately learn and practise multiple regulation strategies: reappraisal, acceptance, problem-solving, self-distancing, [[Behavioral activation|behavioural activation]], mindfulness. Do not rely on one.

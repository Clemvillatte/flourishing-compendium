> **Principle 43** — Learn to observe feelings without identification: you are not your affect

The validation study defines decentering as "the ability to observe one's thoughts and feelings as temporary, objective events in the mind, as opposed to reflections of the self that are necessarily true" ([Fresco et al., 2007](http://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/Fresco_Papers/Fresco_et_al_Decentering_(2007)_BT.pdf)). In the samples, "decentering evidenced a significant positive correlation with cognitive reappraisal and significant negative correlations with experiential avoidance, brooding rumination, emotion suppression, and self-report measures of current depression and anxiety symptoms" ([Fresco et al., 2007](http://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/Fresco_Papers/Fresco_et_al_Decentering_(2007)_BT.pdf)). The authors also caution that their "measure of decentering was never intended to be synonymous with mindfulness" ([Fresco et al., 2007](http://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/Fresco_Papers/Fresco_et_al_Decentering_(2007)_BT.pdf)). The exercise below tries the observing stance.

> Decentering is defined as the ability to observe one's thoughts and feelings as temporary, objective events in the mind, as opposed to reflections of the self that are necessarily true.
>
> Fresco, Moore, van Dulmen, Segal, Ma, Teasdale and Williams (2007), "Initial Psychometric Properties of the Experiences Questionnaire: Validation of a Self-Report Measure of Decentering," Behavior Therapy 38, 234-246, [author-hosted PDF](http://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/Fresco_Papers/Fresco_et_al_Decentering_(2007)_BT.pdf).

> However, our measure of decentering was never intended to be synonymous with mindfulness.
>
> Fresco et al. (2007), same paper, [author-hosted PDF](http://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/Fresco_Papers/Fresco_et_al_Decentering_(2007)_BT.pdf).

(Fresco et al., 2007, Behavior Therapy 38: 234-246.)

### Plain words

The reported correlations describe how questionnaire measures vary together; they do not establish that changing a sentence changes a feeling ([Fresco et al., 2007](http://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/Fresco_Papers/Fresco_et_al_Decentering_(2007)_BT.pdf)). For reflection, if anger is present, compare "I am angry" with "I notice anger" and record any difference, including none.

For example: mid-argument, the sentence "hang on, there is a lot of anger here" beside "I am angry"; the exercise is writing both sentences and noting any difference. (An invented illustration.)
## Psychology parallels

- **Decentering (reperceiving) is a [[Mindfulness|mechanism of mindfulness]]**, the capacity to observe thoughts and feelings as transient mental events reduces their impact (Fresco et al., 2007)
- **Self-as-context (the observing self) is an ACT process**, distinguishing the self that observes from the content observed (Hayes et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The witness|The witness (sakshi)]]** (Vedanta, Upanishads)
- **[[Anatta|Anatta (non-self)]]** (Buddhism, Anatta-lakkhana Sutta)
- **[[The observing self|The observing self]]** (Deikman, The Observing Self)

## Practice

- Practise saying "there is anger" rather than "I am angry." This small linguistic shift creates a gap between you and the feeling.

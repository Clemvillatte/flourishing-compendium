> **Principle 43** — Learn to observe feelings without identification: you are not your affect

A feeling is something you experience, not something you are. The capacity to observe a feeling ("there is anger") rather than be consumed by it ("I am angry") is a core contemplative skill with measurable regulatory benefits. It creates a gap between stimulus and response.

**Plain words** A feeling is something you experience, not something you are. The shift from "I am angry" to "there is anger" is a core contemplative skill — the difference between being the weather and watching it. Practise the observer position.

*For example:* The person mid-tantrum who suddenly says "hang on — there's a lot of anger here" — and the tantrum loses its author. The feeling didn't vanish; it got a witness. That's the whole move.
## Psychology parallels

- **Decentering (reperceiving) is a [[Mindfulness|mechanism of mindfulness]]**, the capacity to observe thoughts and feelings as transient mental events reduces their impact (Fresco et al., 2007)
- **Self-as-context (the observing self) is an ACT process**, distinguishing the self that observes from the content observed (Hayes et al., 2011)

- **[[The witness|The witness (sakshi)]]** (Vedanta, Upanishads)
- **[[Anatta|Anatta (non-self)]]** (Buddhism, Anatta-lakkhana Sutta)

## Practice

- Practise saying "there is anger" rather than "I am angry." This small linguistic shift creates a gap between you and the feeling.

> **Principle 486** — Practise the encompassing (das Umgreifende)

Recognise that you are always more than what you can know of yourself. The encompassing is Jaspers's term for the horizon within which all knowing occurs — the being that exceeds all objectification. You are not merely what you can observe about yourself; you are the being within which all observation occurs. Honour what exceeds your self-knowledge.

(Jaspers, 1932/1971, Philosophy, vol. 1; Jaspers, 1948/1971, Von der Wahrheit / On Truth.)

**Plain words** The encompassing (das Umgreifende): you are always more than what you can know of yourself — the horizon within which all knowing occurs, the being that exceeds all objectification. You are not merely what you can observe about yourself; the observer is the part that doesn't fit in the report.

*For example:* The person who's done every assessment, knows their profile cold — and still can't shake the sense that the map has an author who isn't on it. That's the encompassing, showing up exactly where the objectification runs out.

> **Principle 37** — Recognise the fundamental attribution error: you blame persons, not situations

When explaining others' behaviour, you overattribute it to their character or disposition and underattribute it to their situation. When explaining your own behaviour, you do the reverse (you blame the situation). This asymmetry drives much interpersonal misunderstanding.

**Plain words** Fundamental attribution error: explaining others' behaviour by their character ("he's rude") while explaining your own by your situation ("I'm stressed"). You blame persons; you excuse yourself. Flip it deliberately: run the situational analysis on others first.

*For example:* The colleague's curt email is "who he is" — your own curt email was "a rough morning" — and the correction: give him the rough morning too. The double standard is automatic; the equal treatment is a practice. Most people, most days, are having a situation.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Fundamental attribution error|Fundamental attribution error]]**, others' behaviour is attributed to disposition, own behaviour to situation (Ross, 1977; Jones & Harris, 1967)
- **The error is weaker in East Asian cultures**, holistic cognition increases attention to situational context (Nisbett et al., 2001; Miyamoto & Kitayama, 2002)

## Practice

- When someone behaves badly, before judging their character, ask: "what situation might be producing this behaviour?" Do not assume the behaviour reveals the person.

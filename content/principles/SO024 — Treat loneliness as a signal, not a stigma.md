> **Principle 24** — Treat loneliness as a signal, not a stigma

Treat loneliness as a signal, not a stigma. Loneliness is not a character defect; it is an evolved signal, like hunger or pain, that prompts reconnection (Cacioppo et al., 2011). The signal can misfire — leading to withdrawal, hypervigilance, and self-fulfilling isolation — but the adaptive stance is to treat it as information, not identity.

**Plain words** Loneliness is a signal, like hunger — an evolved alarm that says "reconnect," not a character verdict. The alarm can misfire, driving withdrawal that deepens the isolation. Hear it as information, not as truth about your worth.

*For example:* The lonely person who cancels plans because "nobody really wants me there" — that's the alarm misfiring, cutting the very wire it's meant to ring. The signal says go; the shame says hide. Believe the signal.

## Practice

- When lonely, treat the signal as a prompt to reach out, not to withdraw (see principle 23). Use the [[Cognitive reappraisal|cognitive reappraisal]] of loneliness as a signal, not a judgment. In chronic loneliness, seek professional support.

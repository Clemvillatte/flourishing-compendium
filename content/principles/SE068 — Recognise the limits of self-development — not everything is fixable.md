> **Principle 68** — Recognise the limits of self-development: not everything is fixable

Some aspects of self (temperament, some abilities, some conditions) are not fully changeable. Recognising limits is not giving up; it is wisdom. The skill is distinguishing what can be changed (effort, skills, patterns) from what cannot (temperament, some traits).

**Plain words** Some things about you won't change much — temperament, certain abilities, certain conditions. Pretending otherwise wastes years; despairing over it wastes the rest. The wisdom is sorting what's movable from what isn't, and spending your effort where it counts.

*For example:* The man who spent his twenties trying to become a morning person, an extrovert, and a natural at small talk — all against his grain — finally builds a life around deep work, long friendships, and evenings. Nothing got fixed. Everything got better.
## Psychology parallels

- **Temperament and traits are partly stable**, personality traits show substantial stability across adulthood (Roberts & DelVecchio, 2000; Costa & McCrae, 1994)
- **Traits can change**, but slowly and modestly, personality changes over the lifespan, but gradually (Roberts et al., 2006; Hudson & Fraley, 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The serenity prayer: change what you can, accept what you cannot** (Niebuhr)
- **[[Wisdom|Wisdom as knowing limits]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Wu wei]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 25)

## Practice

- Distinguish what is changeable (skills, patterns, behaviours) from what is less changeable (temperament, some traits). Invest in what is changeable; accept and design around what is not. This is wisdom, not resignation.

> **Principle 334** — Practise competence

Ensure that your care is competent. Competence is the third element of care. It is not enough to mean well; you must also do well. Develop the skills, knowledge, and resources to provide effective care.

(Tronto, 1993, Moral Boundaries, pp. 127-130.)

**Plain words** Competence: meaning well is not doing well — the third element of care demands actual skill, knowledge, and resources. Bad care with a warm heart is still bad care. Get good at what you're doing for people.

*For example:* The devoted son who manages his mother's medications on vibes — and the pharmacist daughter-in-law who reviews the list, finds two dangerous interactions, and says the loving thing: "let me be competent at this."

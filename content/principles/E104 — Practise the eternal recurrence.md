> **Principle 496** — Practise the eternal recurrence

Nietzsche publishes the thought as a question put to you by a demon, and the section is titled "The Heaviest Burden". The Gay Science §341 stages it: a demon creeps after you into your loneliest loneliness and says:

> "This life, as thou livest it at present, and hast lived it, thou must live it once more, and also innumerable times; and there will be nothing new in it, but every pain and every joy and every thought and every sigh, and all the unspeakably small and great in thy life must come to thee again, and all in the same series and sequence—and similarly this spider and this moonlight among the trees, and similarly this moment, and I myself. The eternal sand-glass of existence will ever be turned once more, and thou with it, thou speck of dust!" (Nietzsche, 1882/1910, The Gay Science §341, trans. Thomas Common.)

The section then gives the test its two answers: throwing yourself down, gnashing your teeth and cursing the demon, or answering, in a tremendous moment, "Thou art a God, and never did I hear aught more divine!" The question that would "lie as the heaviest burden upon thy activity" is "Dost thou want this once more, and also for innumerable times?", and the section closes by asking how you would have to become favourably inclined to yourself and to life so as "to long for nothing more ardently" than for this last eternal sanctioning and sealing. The practice the principle draws from it is to live in a way that you would will the eternal recurrence of every moment.

(Nietzsche, 1882/1974, The Gay Science, §341; Nietzsche, 1883/1961, Thus Spoke Zarathustra, "The Convalescent.")

**Plain words**
The demon's question is a mirror for decisions. Take any ordinary stretch of your life, a normal Tuesday: the commute, the tone you use at dinner, the hour you lose to scrolling. Ask whether you would sign for that stretch to return exactly as it is, forever. You do not need to believe the universe actually repeats; the question works because it converts "is this okay for now?" into "is this okay forever?". The parts you would not sign for become visible, and that is the list to work on.

**For example**
Someone runs the question on an ordinary week. The parts they would not sign for become visible for the first time. The recurrence is only the hypothetical; what it produces is a concrete list.

> **Principle 21** — Learn to use behavioural rehearsal: practise before performing

Behavioural rehearsal practises a target response before performing it in real life. The DBT skills-training teaching notes describe role-play, dialogue rehearsal and covert rehearsal. Role-play can be run with the leader or with other participants; dialogue rehearsal presents the situation in story form for those who decline role-play; and for covert rehearsal:

> "Covert rehearsal. If a participant refuses even dialogue rehearsal, ask him or her to role-play the situation mentally and imagine giving a skilled response." ([Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual, 2nd ed., reproducible teaching notes](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-all-teaching.pdf?t=1))

Two details from the same notes shape the practice. Participants take turns playing both parts, because taking the other person's part "gives participants an idea of what it feels like when someone else uses behavioral skills on them". And the notes set the priority in one line: "Do not sacrifice rehearsal in order to present more material."

**Plain words** Choose a response you want to practise before a difficult conversation. You could say it aloud with another person playing the other role, then exchange roles to hear how it sounds. When someone declines dialogue rehearsal, the teaching notes suggest imagining a skilled response.

*For example* Imagined: someone who dreads asking the landlord for repairs first role-plays the request with a friend playing the landlord, then swaps parts to hear what the request sounds like from the other side.

## Practice

- Rehearse behaviours: before a difficult conversation, presentation, or social situation, practise it (alone, with a friend, in front of a mirror). Build the skill in safety before performance.

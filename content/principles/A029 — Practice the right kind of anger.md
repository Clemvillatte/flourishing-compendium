> **Principle 29** — Practice the right kind of anger

Aristotle holds that anger is not always wrong. The person who never feels anger at injustice is deficient, not virtuous. The question is whether the anger is proportionate, directed at the right object, and expressed in the right way. Do not suppress all anger; train it.

(NE IV.5; EE III.7.)

**Plain words** Anger isn't always wrong. Someone who never gets angry at injustice is deficient, not saintly. Aristotle asks whether it's the right size, aimed at the right target, and expressed the right way. So don't suppress it. Train it.

*For example:* Being furious that a landlord ignored mould in a child's room, and writing a firm letter, is anger well used. Shouting at the child about it isn't.

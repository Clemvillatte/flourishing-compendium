> **Principle 74** — Cultivate creative thinking: divergent and convergent modes

Creativity involves two distinct modes: divergent thinking (generating many possibilities) and convergent thinking (selecting and refining). Both are needed, and they require different mental states. Creativity is trainable.

**Plain words** Creativity runs on two modes: divergent (generate many possibilities — playful, loose, uncritical) and convergent (select and refine — critical, sharp). They require different mental states and they sabotage each other. Separate them in time: generate Tuesday, edit Wednesday.

*For example:* The writer who generates and edits in the same sitting — every idea shot on sight by the internal critic — versus the one who writes the ugly full draft before allowing a single judgement. The separation isn't a quirk; it's the mechanism.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Divergent and convergent thinking|Divergent and convergent thinking]]**, creativity involves both generation and selection (Guilford, 1950; Runco, 2004)
- **Creativity is associated with well-being**, creative activity predicts higher positive affect and life satisfaction (Conner et al., 2016; Silvia et al., 2014)
- **Incubation and mind-wandering support creativity**, breaks and unfocused time enhance creative insight (Baird et al., 2012; Sio & Ormerod, 2009)

## Practice

- Separate divergent and convergent phases. When generating ideas, do not evaluate. When evaluating, do not generate. Allow incubation time (walks, sleep, breaks) between generation and selection.

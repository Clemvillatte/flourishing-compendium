> **Principle 35** — Balance calming and insight (samatha-vipassana; zhiguan)

The Mohe Zhiguan opens by naming the pair as one thing: "Cessation-and-contemplation is bright quiescence; to put an end [to obstacles such as deluded thoughts, attachments, and passionate afflictions] is to attain calm serenity, and to contemplate [things as they are] is to attain clear insight."

> Cessation-and-contemplation is bright quiescence; to put an end [to obstacles such as deluded thoughts, attachments, and passionate afflictions] is to attain calm serenity, and to contemplate [things as they are] is to attain clear insight.
>
> (Zhiyi, Mohe Zhiguan, trans. Swanson)

The Pali canon describes the same tandem: "for him these two qualities occur in tandem: tranquillity & insight." The one who develops the path "comprehends through direct knowledge whatever qualities are to be comprehended through direct knowledge".

(Zhiyi, Mohe Zhiguan; MN 149.)

**Plain words**
Two capacities, one practice. Calm is the settling that lets attention stay; insight is the looking that tells you what is actually going on. As a compendium exercise, spend part of a sitting just steadying on one object, and part examining what is present — noticing, without judgment, which half you default to.

*For example:* Meditators who get very calm but see nothing, and analysts who see everything but can't stop shaking — both halves, one practice.

> **Principle 465** — Practise avoiding the adventurer

Beauvoir describes the adventurer in Part II of The Ethics of Ambiguity ([marxists.org text](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch02.htm)):

> He likes action for its own sake. He finds joy in spreading through the world a freedom which remains indifferent to its content.

She does not dismiss him outright. She calls his choice "very close to a genuinely moral attitude", since he "aims expressly at existence". The fault she identifies is that he "remains indifferent to the content, that is, to the human meaning of his action, who thinks he can assert his own existence without taking into account that of others." In the end, "his fault is believing that one can do something for oneself without others and even against them." The way out is to see that freedom "can will itself only by destining itself to an open future, by seeking to extend itself by means of the freedom of others."

(Beauvoir, 1947, Part II.)

**Plain words** For Beauvoir, the adventurer enjoys freedom and action but not their meaning for other people. She credits the energy and rejects the indifference. Moving constantly is not what she faults, she faults ignoring what the movement does to others.

For example (imagined): someone who moves to a new country every year can be adventurous in her sense, if they stay answerable to the people affected. The same life is her adventurer if the people left behind do not matter.

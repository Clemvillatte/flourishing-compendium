> **Principle 54** — Recognise guilt's adaptive function: it motivates repair

Guilt, when proportionate to a real harm, is adaptive: it signals that you violated a value and motivates apology, reparation, and change. The problem is guilt that is disproportionate, chronic, or attached to things outside your control.

**Plain words** Guilt, proportionate to a real harm, is adaptive — it signals a violated value and motivates apology, reparation, change. The problems are disproportionate guilt, chronic guilt, or guilt attached to things you didn't control. Keep the functional kind; treat the rest.

*For example:* The parent who apologises properly for the unfair snap — the guilt did its job in one evening — versus the one who feels guilty for the child's every struggle, forever, regardless of agency. The first is a system working; the second is a system hijacked.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Guilt motivates reparative behaviour|Guilt motivates reparative behaviour]]**, guilt is associated with apology, confession, and reparation (Tangney et al., 2007)
- **Chronic or disproportionate guilt is maladaptive**, guilt that persists despite repair, or that attaches to uncontrollable events, is associated with depression and OCD (Blatt et al., 1996)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Reparation and atonement** (Judaism, Teshuva)
- **Remorse as moral signal** (Butler, Fifteen Sermons)

## Practice

- When you feel guilty, ask: "did I actually harm someone, and can I repair it?" If yes, repair. If no (disproportionate or uncontrollable), this is not adaptive guilt and warrants [[Self-compassion|self-compassion]] rather than more guilt.

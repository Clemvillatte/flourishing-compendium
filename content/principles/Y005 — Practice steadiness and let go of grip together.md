> **Principle 5** — Practice steadiness and let go of grip together

The two movements are named together in Yoga Sūtra 1.12 (Woods): "the restriction of them is by means of practice and passionlessness." Practice (abhyāsa) is defined in 1.13 as exertion to the end that the mind-stuff shall have permanence in the restricted state, and 1.14 gives its conditions: cultivated for a long time, uninterruptedly, and with earnest attention. Passionlessness (vairagya) is defined in 1.15 as the consciousness of being master on the part of one who has rid himself of thirst for either seen or revealed objects. The compendium exercise pairs the two: keep the repetition under the three conditions of 1.14, and set down the thirst for what the practice will get you.

> Passionlessness is the consciousness of being master on the part of one who has rid himself of thirst for either seen or revealed objects.
>
> Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra 1.15, translated by James Haughton Woods (1914), [reprinted text](https://www.theessentialway.org/yoga-sutras/).

**Plain words**
Sutra 1.12 puts two things side by side: practice and passionlessness. Practice is repetition aimed at steadiness, done for a long time, without breaks, with earnest attention. Passionlessness is setting down the thirst for what you see or imagine getting. Do both together: keep the repetition, set down the thirst.

**For example**
In an imagined exercise, a language learner keeps a daily study streak and marks each conversation in the log as attended, not as passed or failed.

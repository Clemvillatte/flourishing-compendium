> **Principle 5** — Practice steadiness and let go of grip together

The method has two movements, named as one principle in the tradition: abhyasa, the steady repetition that builds capacity, and vairagya, the release of the grip that keeps re-agitating the mind. Doing either alone fails: practice without release becomes striving, and release without practice becomes drift. Practically, pair every effortful repetition with an explicit release of its outcome.

(YS 1.12-1.14.)

**Plain words** Two movements, one principle: abhyasa — steady repetition that builds capacity; vairagya — releasing the grip that keeps re-agitating the mind. Practice without release becomes striving; release without practice becomes drift. Do them together: show up steadily, and loosen your grip on the results.

*For example:* The language learner who studies daily (abhyasa) and stops grading every conversation as pass/fail (vairagya) — the combination is the whole method, and either half alone has failed a million learners.

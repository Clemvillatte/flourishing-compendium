> **Principle 43** — Cultivate authentic identity: be who you are, not who others want

Authenticity — living in accord with your own values, feelings, and self rather than conforming to external expectations — is associated with well-being. It is distinct from selfishness; authenticity includes honesty about who you are.

**Plain words** There's a you that exists before anyone's expectations arrive — your actual values, your actual taste, what you actually think is funny. Authenticity is running your life from that place instead of performing a role for an audience. The well-being research is blunt: people who live from the inside out do better than people who live from the outside in.

*For example:* The accountant who secretly loves cooking and spends every dinner party animated, alive, funny — and every workday grey. The question isn't whether to quit; it's how long she can keep the mask on before it costs more than it pays.
## Psychology parallels

- **Authenticity and well-being**, authenticity is associated with higher well-being and life satisfaction (Wood et al., 2008; Kernis & Goldman, 2006)
- **Authentic living vs external influence**, authenticity involves acting from one's own values rather than external pressure (Wood et al., 2008)

## Practice

- Notice where you are living according to others' expectations rather than your own values. Ask: "is this my choice, or my conditioning?" Practise aligning actions with your actual values.

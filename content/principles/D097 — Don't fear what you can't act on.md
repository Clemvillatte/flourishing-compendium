> **Principle 97** — Don't fear what you can't act on

The Liezi tells of a man from the state of Qi who feared that the sky would fall. The story survives as the Chinese idiom 杞人憂天, literally "the man of Qi fear that the sky might fall" ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9D%9E%E4%BA%BA%E6%86%82%E5%A4%A9)). What happens next is worth reading closely, because the second man in the story does not simply tell him to calm down.

> "There was a man of Qi country who was so worried that heaven and earth might fall down, and his body would have nowhere to lodge, that he forgot to eat and sleep. There was another man who was worried that he should be so worried about it, and therefore went to enlighten him, saying, \"Heaven is nothing but the accumulated air; there is no place where there is not air. You walk and stand all day inside heaven, stretching and bending, breathing in and breathing out; why should you worry about it falling down?\"" (Liezi 1, in the English adapted from A. C. Graham's translation, [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%9D%9E%E4%BA%BA%E6%86%82%E5%A4%A9))

The visitor takes the fear apart piece by piece. When the man asks about the sun, moon and stars, the answer is that they are "air which shines inside the accumulated air" and "Even if they did fall down, they couldn't hit or harm anyone." When he asks about the earth giving way, the answer is that it is "accumulated soil, filling the void in all four directions". The account ends: "The man was satisfied and greatly cheered; and so was the man who enlightened him" (same Wiktionary translation).

The cosmology is ancient and I do not rely on it. What I take from the passage is the method: a vague dread gets split into specific questions, and each question gets asked on its own. For some of the pieces the honest answer is that nothing can be done, and then the worry has nothing left to work on.

**Plain words** A fear that cannot be acted on still costs you sleep and meals, as it did the man of Qi. Ask what exactly you are afraid of, and then whether there is anything you can do about each part. If there is, do it. If there is not, you have at least found out which kind of worry you are carrying.

*For example (my illustration, not from the Liezi):* You hear a rumour of layoffs. You can update your CV and write to two contacts, and you can do both in an afternoon. You cannot decide the company's budget. The first two are actions. The budget is the part to put down.

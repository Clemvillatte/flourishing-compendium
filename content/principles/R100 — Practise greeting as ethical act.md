> **Principle 362** — Practise greeting as ethical act

When you greet another person, do not merely perform a social ritual. A greeting is an ethical act: it acknowledges the existence, dignity, and worth of the other. It says, in effect, "I see you. You exist. You matter." Practise greetings as if they mean something, because they do.

(Levinas, 1961/1969, Totality and Infinity; Buber, 1923/1970, I and Thou; Ubuntu philosophy.)

**Plain words** A greeting is an ethical act, not noise before the content: it says "I see you, you exist, you matter." Practise greetings as if they mean something — because they're the smallest unit of recognition humans have.

*For example:* The security guard nobody greets all day — and the one person who says good morning, every morning, and what that thirty-second exchange does to both of their days.

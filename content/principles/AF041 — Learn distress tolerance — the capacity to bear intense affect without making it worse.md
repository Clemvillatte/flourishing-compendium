> **Principle 41** — Learn distress tolerance: the capacity to bear intense affect without making it worse

Distress tolerance names the capacity to withstand aversive internal states rather than escape them. In their review of the construct, Leyro, Zvolensky and Bernstein record a two-part definition: the perceived capacity to withstand negative emotional or other aversive states, and the behavioral act of withstanding the distressing internal states a stressor elicits ([Leyro et al., 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2891552/)). The same review traces the clinical interest to Linehan's account of borderline personality disorder, in which a persistent unwillingness or inability to tolerate emotional distress is treated as a central mechanism, and discusses the possibility that lower distress tolerance encourages escape or avoidance when these opportunities are available.

> "Specifically, "distress tolerance" has been referred to as (a) the perceived capacity to withstand negative emotional and/or other aversive states (e.g. physical discomfort), and (b) the behavioral act of withstanding distressing internal states elicited by some type of stressor." (Leyro, T. M., Zvolensky, M. J., & Bernstein, A. (2010). Distress Tolerance and Psychopathological Symptoms and Disorders: A Review of the Empirical Literature among Adults. Psychological Bulletin, 136(4), 576-600, section "Conceptual perspective on distress and tolerance", [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2891552/), doi:10.1037/a0019712)

**Plain words** Feeling able to bear discomfort and actually bearing it are different things. For an uncomfortable task you have chosen to continue, consider what you can do while the feeling is still present. The question is about your next action; it does not require the feeling to disappear first.

*For example (imagined):* A man feels a wave of panic in a supermarket queue and stays in the line rather than abandoning the trolley. His task is to keep standing there while the feeling is present.

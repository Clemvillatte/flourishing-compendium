> **Principle 332** — Practise attentiveness

Tronto makes noticing the first phase of care, and gives it a moral weight that is easy to miss. In her account, the four elements of care are "caring about, noticing the need to care in the first place; taking care of, assuming responsibility for care; care-giving, the actual work of care that needs to be done; and care-receiving, the response of that which is cared for to the care", and from these arise four ethical elements: "attentiveness, responsibility, competence, and responsiveness" (Tronto, 1993, as carried in [Acta Philosophica supplement article on Weil and Tronto](https://forum-phil.pusc.it/article/download/4948/2736/4519)). Attentiveness comes first because nothing else in the chain starts without it, and she draws the moral line hard: attention is "a moral achievement", and "ignoring others, ignorance, is a form of moral evil" (Tronto, Moral Boundaries, pp. 127, as carried in the same article).

Her model for the noticing itself is Simone Weil, whom she cites directly. Weil's definition of attention, from her essay on school studies:

> "Attention consists of suspending our thought, leaving it detached, empty, and ready to be penetrated by the object; it means holding in our minds, within reach of this thought, but on the lower level and not in contact with it, the diverse knowledge we have acquired which we are forced to make use of. Our thought should be in relation to all particular and already formulated thoughts, as a man on a mountain who, as he looks forward, sees also below him, without actually looking at them, a great many forests and plains. Above all our thought should be empty, waiting, not seeking anything, but ready to receive in its naked truth the object that is to penetrate it." (Weil, [Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf))

Tronto's use of Weil makes the link explicit: "It is necessary, in a sense, to suspend one's goals, ambitions, life plans and concerns in order to recognize and be attentive to others" (Tronto, Moral Boundaries, p. 128, as carried in the same article).

(Tronto, 1993; Weil, 1942.)

**Plain words** The first move of care is not doing anything; it is noticing. Tronto's point is that the noticing is already moral work, because the alternative, not noticing, is how needs stay unmet while everyone nearby feels fine. Weil's version is more radical: attention is your own thoughts stepping aside so the other person can show up as they actually are.

*For example:* The teacher who notices the kid whose uniform stopped fitting and quietly connects the family with the uniform bank — nobody asked, which is the point.

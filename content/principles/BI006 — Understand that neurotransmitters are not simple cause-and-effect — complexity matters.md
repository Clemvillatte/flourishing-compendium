> **Principle 6** — Understand that neurotransmitters are not simple cause-and-effect: complexity matters

The "chemical imbalance" model of depression (low serotonin causes depression) is an oversimplification. Neurotransmitters (serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine, GABA) are part of a complex system. Medications that affect them work, but the mechanism is not simply "correcting a deficit."

**Plain words** The "chemical imbalance" story is too simple — serotonin isn't a bathtub level. The system is complex: networks, receptors, plasticity. What's true: medication can still help. What's also true: it's not a serotonin refill. Hold the complexity.

*For example:* "My serotonin is low" — a nice story, mostly wrong, still leading to pills that sometimes work through other doors. The pill can help without the story being true. Both facts fit in one head.
## Psychology parallels

- **Serotonin hypothesis is oversimplified**, the specific "low serotonin causes depression" model is not supported by evidence (Moncrieff et al., 2022; Belmaker & Agam, 2008)
- **Antidepressants work**, but the mechanism is complex, SSRIs are effective for many but not through simply "correcting a deficit" (Moncrieff et al., 2022; Jakobsen et al., 2017)
- **Neuroplasticity and antidepressants**, antidepressants may work partly through promoting neuroplasticity and neurogenesis (Duman et al., 2016; Santarelli et al., 2003)

## Practice

- Do not accept the "chemical imbalance" simplification. If you take or are considering antidepressants, understand: they work for many people, but the mechanism is complex, and they are one tool among many (therapy, lifestyle, meaning). Do not stop or start without professional guidance.

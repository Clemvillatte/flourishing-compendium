> **Principle 103** — Never abstain from necessary action

Krishna tells Arjuna that stepping back from action is not the neutral move it looks like. Verse 3.5 gives the reason:

> There is no one who can remain without action even for a moment. Indeed, all beings are compelled to act by their qualities born of material nature (the three guṇas).

(Bhagavad Gita 3.5, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/5/).)

The verses on either side build the case. Verse 3.4 says "One cannot achieve freedom from karmic reactions by merely abstaining from work" ([BG 3.4](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/4/)). Verse 3.6 is the sharpest: "Those who restrain the external organs of action, while continuing to dwell on sense objects in the mind, certainly delude themselves and are to be called hypocrites" ([BG 3.6](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/6/)). Verse 3.7 praises those who "engage the working senses in working without attachment", and 3.8 adds that "action is superior to inaction. By ceasing activity, even your bodily maintenance will not be possible" ([BG 3.8](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/8/)).

So the Gita does say that non-action is not real rest and that outward restraint can hide an inner chase. The sharper formulation, that abstaining from necessary action is itself an action and a worse one, is this page's own inference. The test of necessity before the test of desire is also this page's own working rule. It sits close to 3.8, where the action meant is one's prescribed duty.

**Plain words**

Not acting is a choice with effects. The Gita warns against looking calm while the mind runs after what it wants. When you are unsure whether to step in, ask first what the situation needs, then what you would like.

**For example**

A man sits in a team meeting where a colleague is being talked over, and stays quiet because he is working on equanimity. He later sees that his silence helped the meeting go the way it did, and that his calm was partly a way of avoiding the discomfort of speaking.

> **Principle 72** — Use cultural formulation in clinical practice

Use the cultural-formulation interview (CFI) in clinical practice. The CFI, part of the DSM-5, is a structured way to elicit the client's cultural understanding of their distress, its causes, and its appropriate treatment (Lewis-Fernández and Aggarwal, 2015; Aggarwal et al., 2016). It is the clinical instrument of principles 22 and 64.

**Plain words** The cultural formulation interview — a structured way to ask what the client's culture says about their distress and its proper treatment. Ten questions, better care. Your culture's story about your illness is clinically relevant data.

*For example:* "What do your family and friends think caused this?" — the answer that reveals the whole treatment context. The spirits, the curse, the imbalance, the chemistry: all of it is clinically relevant. Ask, don't assume.

## Practice

- In clinical assessment, use the CFI to elicit the client's cultural model. In treatment planning, adapt interventions to the cultural context (see principle 64). In supervision, attend to cultural dynamics.

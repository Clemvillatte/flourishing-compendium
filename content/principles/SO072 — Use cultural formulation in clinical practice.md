> **Principle 72** — Use cultural formulation in clinical practice

The Cultural Formulation Interview (CFI) asks how a person understands their difficulties, what support or stresses surround them, and what help they want. Its 16 questions include experiences of identity, barriers to care and concerns about the clinician-patient relationship ([APA interview, pp. 1-3](https://www.psychiatry.org/getmedia/5cc5329d-3bd4-4c6a-bae1-dfd0d6496f44/APA-DSM5TR-CulturalFormulationInterview.pdf)).

> Focus on the individual’s own way of understanding the problem.

This instruction accompanies question 1; subsequent questions use the person's own description of the problem ([APA interview, p. 1](https://www.psychiatry.org/getmedia/5cc5329d-3bd4-4c6a-bae1-dfd0d6496f44/APA-DSM5TR-CulturalFormulationInterview.pdf)).

### What the evidence establishes

In a six-country field trial, 318 patients and 75 clinicians generally rated the 14-question pilot feasible, acceptable and useful; clinician feasibility ratings were lower than patient ratings. These were assessments of the interview experience, not tests of improved clinical outcomes ([Lewis-Fernández et al., 2017, abstract and study aims](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/the-british-journal-of-psychiatry/article/feasibility-acceptability-and-clinical-utility-of-the-cultural-formulation-interview-mixedmethods-results-from-the-dsm5-international-field-trial/FD28CDEA89A308AA90C4BF7BAF335D4E)). The APA cautions against using the CFI as the sole basis for diagnosis ([APA interview, introductory notice](https://www.psychiatry.org/getmedia/5cc5329d-3bd4-4c6a-bae1-dfd0d6496f44/APA-DSM5TR-CulturalFormulationInterview.pdf)).

### Plain words

Start by asking what the difficulty means to this person. Two people given the same diagnosis may explain their troubles differently or want different kinds of help; the interview makes room to ask about those differences ([APA interview, questions 1-5 and 14-16](https://www.psychiatry.org/getmedia/5cc5329d-3bd4-4c6a-bae1-dfd0d6496f44/APA-DSM5TR-CulturalFormulationInterview.pdf)).

### For example

Suggested documentation exercise (Attention; Overt behaviour): after using the official CFI, try a note format that separates your initial assumption from the patient's account. Compare whether it captures their stated concern and preferred help. Keep or revise that format at the next assessment; leave the instrument's questions unchanged.

## Psychology parallels

- **Cultural humility**: Hook and colleagues describe an other-oriented stance without superiority towards another person's cultural experience. Client perceptions of this stance were associated with alliance and improvement in their four studies; this is not a trial of the CFI ([2013 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23647387/)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **Gadamer**: the fusion of horizons concerns a shared context of understanding formed through dialogue, in which neither perspective remains unaffected. Comparing that with listening in an interview is a philosophical analogy, not validation of the instrument ([Malpas, section 3.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gadamer/)).
- **Buber**: I-Thou offers a comparison for encountering another person without reducing them to an object to manipulate. It is not interchangeable with a clinical interviewing method ([Zank, section 3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/)).
- **Compendium application**: the CFI can inform reflection on sociocultural context within an EEMM-guided formulation. This page does not identify it as an official EEMM measure.

## Practice

- Use the official CFI alongside a clinical assessment, following its interviewer guidance. The documentation exercise above is an optional compendium suggestion, not a revised CFI or a tested treatment.

> **Principle 442** — Practise the stages of existence

The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/) calls the three-part scheme a common reading and an over-simplification:

> A common way to interpret Kierkegaard's thought is in terms of three "existence-spheres": the aesthetic, the ethical and the religious. The idea that there are precisely three such spheres is an over-simplification

It also warns against reading the stages as a ladder: "even when someone does, the later stages do not simply replace the earlier ones", and "the aesthetic is never really left behind". Transitions "do not happen automatically or inevitably" but are "achievements that require the transformation of the 'passions' that shape a human life". The views come from pseudonyms in Either/Or, Fear and Trembling and the Postscript, so they are not simply Kierkegaard's own (SEP, section 1 and 3.3.1).

(Kierkegaard, Either/Or; Stages on Life's Way; as read via SEP section 3.)

**Plain words** Kierkegaard sketches three ways of living, built around pleasure, commitment and faith. They are rival views of what makes a life worth living, and a person can stay in one by choice. The scheme works better as a set of questions about your own life than as a ranking.

For example (imagined): someone who has organised years around novelty may notice it no longer satisfies and begin to ask what a lasting commitment would involve. That asks the ethical sphere's question without claiming to have left the aesthetic behind.

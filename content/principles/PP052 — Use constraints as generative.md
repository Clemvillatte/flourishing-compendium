> **Principle 52** — Use constraints as generative

A constraint works by closing off one route so that you search another. Stokes's model of "paired constraints" describes this: one constraint precludes an existing solution, and the other promotes "a novel one" ([Stokes, 2008, Journal of Creative Behavior 42(4)](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1360855568790922880)). Her book argues that "it is not boundary-less creative freedom that inspires new ideas, but self-imposed, well-considered constraints" ([publisher's description, Stokes 2006](https://library-search.open.ac.uk/discovery/fulldisplay/alma9952484720102316/44OPN_INST:VU1)). That is a case-study argument drawn from artists, not an experiment.

Acar, Tarakci and van Knippenberg's cross-disciplinary review of the research on creativity under constraints is described by later authors as finding an "inverted U-shaped effect of constraints on creativity" ([Theel and Sydow, 2024, citing Acar et al., 2019, p. 97](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/10564926231191087)). Too few constraints and too many can both hurt. I could not open the full review, so check the original before quoting it.

**Plain words**
A blank page gives you too many choices. A rule, such as a word limit or a fixed form, makes the choice smaller. A rule that is too tight, however, leaves no room to move.

**For example**
Write a paragraph in exactly 80 words with no adjectives. If that proves impossible, loosen it to 120 words and allow adjectives. Keep the version that produces your strongest sentence.

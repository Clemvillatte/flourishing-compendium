> **Principle 19** — Diagnose ambition and the hunger for esteem

In Elwes's translation, Spinoza defines ambition as "the immoderate desire of power." ([Ethics III, Definitions of the Emotions, XLIV](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt)) His account also attends to glory and shame: "an ambitious man desires nothing so much as glory, and fears nothing so much as shame." ([Ethics III, P39 note](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt))

On honour dependent on popular approval, he writes:

> "he whose honour is rooted in popular approval must, day by day, anxiously strive, act, and scheme in order to retain his reputation." ([Ethics IV, P58 note, Elwes translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt))

Use these passages to examine a current ambition. Before changing a plan to please an imagined audience, ask whether the change improves the work. Try completing one small task without announcing it.

**Plain words** Notice when fear of disapproval is deciding what you do. Separate the value of the task from the praise you hope it earns.

*For example:* You finish a piece of writing and immediately want to post it. Leave it private until tomorrow. First write down what you value in it, before anyone responds.

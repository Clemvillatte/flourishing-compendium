> **Principle 19** — Diagnose ambition and the hunger for esteem

Ambition is an immoderate desire for the esteem of others, and Spinoza tracks it with clinical precision: it waxes with every sign of favor, wanes with every slight, and makes its owner hostage to the opinions of strangers. The principle is not that esteem is worthless; it is that esteem placed at the center of identity produces a life run by remote control. Practically, notice where your plans change shape when you imagine an audience, and run one important project entirely without one.

(Ethics III, P39 with scholium; definition of Ambition.)

**Plain words** Ambition: an immoderate desire for the esteem of others — it waxes with every favour, wanes with every slight, and makes its owner hostage to the opinions of strangers. Diagnose it clinically, in yourself first. The hunger is real; the food is unstable.

*For example:* The executive who checks the likes at traffic lights, whose week is set by one comment thread — Spinoza's three-century-old description, running in their pocket. The diagnosis isn't shame; it's the first line of the treatment: whose opinions are holding the keys?

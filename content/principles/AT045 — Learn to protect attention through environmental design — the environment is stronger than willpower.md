> **Principle 45** — Learn to protect attention through environmental design: the environment is stronger than willpower

The habit literature gives the principle its mechanism. Wood and Neal's model of the habit-goal interface describes habits as learned associations between responses and the features of the contexts in which they were repeatedly performed: physical settings, preceding actions, particular people. Once a habit is formed, perception of the context triggers the response without a mediating goal. Habit associations accrue slowly and do not shift appreciably with current goal states.

> "Habits emerge from the gradual learning of associations between responses and the features of performance contexts that have historically covaried with them (e.g., physical settings, preceding actions). Once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal." (Wood & Neal, 2007 — [full text](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/wp-content/uploads/sites/183/2023/10/wood.neal_.2007psychrev_a_new_look_at_habits_and_the_interface_between_habits_and_goals.pdf) — Psychol Rev 114(4), p. 843)

Wood and Neal describe how learned context cues can elicit habitual responses, while also explaining how goals interact with habits. This principle applies that model by treating cue management as one possible way to change a routine — the authors' own discussion of change centres on what they call the self-regulation of habit cuing.

**Plain words**
Habits are triggered by places and objects: perceiving the context fires the associated response without a mediating goal. The learned associations do not shift appreciably with current goal states, though habits do interface with goals. So one practical move is cue-level: move the phone, close the tab, leave the remote in another room.

**For example**
Imagine someone who routinely turns on the television when settling down to work. Before the next work session, they leave it off and put the remote in another room.

## Practice

- Design your environment: remove notifications, clear workspace, use physical barriers (close door, headphones), organise tools for focus. Try changing a distraction cue rather than making stronger resolve your only plan.

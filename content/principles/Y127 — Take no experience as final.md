> **Principle 127** — Take no experience as final

Two texts stand behind this principle, and neither speaks of attainments directly. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6 says, in Max Muller's translation: "Next follows the teaching (of Brahman) by No, no! for there is nothing else higher than this (if one says): 'It is not so.'" The "neti neti" passage is about describing Brahman, which is why every positive description is refused (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6, trans. Muller, Sacred Books of the East vol. 15, as given in the Brhat open library).

### The raft

In the Alagaddupama Sutta (MN 22), in Bhikkhu Sujato's translation, the Buddha says: "I will teach you a simile of the teaching as a raft: for crossing over, not for holding on." A traveller builds a raft to cross a deluge, and when he arrives he should not "hoist it on my head or pick it up on my shoulder and go wherever I want". The Buddha concludes: "By understanding the simile of the raft, you will even give up the teachings, let alone what is not the teachings" (MN 22, trans. Sujato, SuttaCentral). The raft stands for the teachings, not for experiences.

### What the principle adds

Neither passage says that a luminous state is only a state, or tells anyone to write down a breakthrough, thank it and return to basics within a week. The extension from teachings and descriptions to attainments is the principle's own reading, and the weekly return is its practical advice. My sources for the Upanishad were a search excerpt and the Brhat page, not a full edition.

**Plain words** The Upanishad says the highest description of ultimate reality is a denial of every description. The Buddha says the teaching is for crossing, so do not carry it afterwards. The principle takes both as a reason not to cling to a good experience. That reading is the principle's, not either text's.

*For example:* Imagined example: after an unusually clear morning of reflection, you write a paragraph about it. You then go back to the plain routine of your practice the next day.

(Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6, trans. F. Max Muller, Sacred Books of the East vol. 15, via the Brhat open library; Majjhima Nikaya 22, trans. Bhikkhu Sujato, SuttaCentral; both opened 2026-10-21.)

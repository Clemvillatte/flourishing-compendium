> **Principle 20** — Learn to manage naps: short or none

Naps can be restorative or disruptive depending on timing and length. Short naps (10-20 minutes) can restore alertness; long naps (over 30 minutes) or late naps disrupt night sleep.

**Plain words** Naps are tools: 10–20 minutes restores; 30+ minutes or late naps steal from the night. Short, early, or none. The nap is a snack, not a meal.

*For example:* The 4 p.m. hour-long "nap" that costs you the night — now you're tired at midnight and exhausted at 5 a.m. The 20-minute 1 p.m. version pays for itself. Same pleasure, different accounting.
## Psychology parallels

- **Napping and sleep**, short naps (10-20 min) improve alertness; long or late naps disrupt night sleep (Brooks & Lack, 2006; Milner & Cote, 2009)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean in rest]]** (Aristotle)
- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei: balance of rest and activity]]** (Daoism)
- **[[Nothing in excess|Nothing in excess]]** (Delphic maxim)

## Practice

- Nap wisely: if you nap, keep it short (10-20 minutes) and early (before 3pm). Do not nap long or late, as it disrupts night sleep. If you have insomnia, avoid napping.

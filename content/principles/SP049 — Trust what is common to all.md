> **Principle 49** — Trust what is common to all

Whatever is common to all human minds, Spinoza demonstrates, is conceived adequately: the common notions are the building blocks of reason, and reason is reliable precisely where it stays with what is shared. Error lives in the private, the idiosyncratic, the time-and-place particular treated as universal. Practically, when you need a judgment you can trust, build it from what any careful person would observe, and hold your private readings more lightly.

(Ethics II, P38-39 and P40, with scholium.)

**Plain words** Whatever is common to all human minds is conceived adequately — the common notions are the building blocks of reason, and reason is reliable precisely where it stays with what is shared. Error lives in the private and idiosyncratic. Trust what is common to all; audit what is only yours.

*For example:* The plan built entirely on "my unique gut feeling" — versus the one built on what everyone can verify: sleep, cash-flow, evidence. Spinoza's rule isn't anti-individuality; it's that the shared layer is where the checks happen.

> **Principle 49** — Trust what is common to all

Ethics II, Proposition 38 says: "Those things, which are common to all, and which are equally in a part and in the whole, cannot be conceived except adequately", and the corollary adds "that there are certain ideas or notions common to all men" ([Ethics, Part II, Elwes](https://monadnock.net/spinoza/ethics-2.html)). The demonstration works with a feature "common to all bodies".

This is narrower than "what any careful person would observe". The Stanford Encyclopedia gives the standard example: extension "is extension is extension, whether it belongs to me or to a mosquito biting me". It says properties that are both universally present and "non scalar" are "necessarily grasped correctly by any mind" ([SEP, Spinoza's Epistemology and Philosophy of Mind](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza-epistemology-mind/)). It contrasts these with ideas formed through sense experience, which only represent "one way that the sun can affect my body in its current state". Proposition 39 adds relative common notions, based on properties shared by the human body and some external bodies.

(Ethics II, P38-39 and P40, with scholium.)

### Scope

Spinoza's common notions are basic features such as extension, motion and rest, not shared opinions or what a group agrees on. The practical advice to cross-check a private judgment against what others can verify is the compendium's extension. I did not find Spinoza saying that in the passages I opened.

**Plain words**
Spinoza says some ideas, such as that bodies are extended and can move or rest, are the same for every mind and cannot be mistaken. His main contrast is with ideas built from how things happen to affect you.

**For example**
Before a big decision, split your reasoning into two lists. One has facts others can check, such as dates, amounts and records. The other has your impressions. Weigh the first list more heavily.

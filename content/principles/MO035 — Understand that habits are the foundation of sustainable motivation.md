> **Principle 35** — Understand that habits are the foundation of sustainable motivation

Motivation science treats a habit as an action triggered automatically by contextual cues, not chosen anew each time. In the research programme that Gardner, Lally and Wardle summarise, repetition of a simple action in a consistent context leads, through associative learning, to the action being activated when those cues return. Wood and Neal's model states the mechanism: once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal.

> Once initiation of the action is 'transferred' to external cues, dependence on conscious attention or motivational processes is reduced.
> (Gardner, Lally and Wardle 2015)

The transfer is what makes habits the durable base. Motivation and attention wane, whereas habits are likely to persist even after conscious motivation or interest dissipates. Lally's real-world study tracked 96 volunteers performing a chosen daily behaviour in the same context for 12 weeks: automaticity rose along a curve that flattens, plateauing on average around 66 days with wide variation between people and behaviours, and a single missed opportunity did not materially affect the process. Aristotle gives the older version of the point: moral virtue comes about as a result of habit. The compendium's application: spend motivation on building the habit, a fixed cue in a fixed place, and let the cue carry the behaviour once the feeling is gone.

**Plain words** Motivation is a poor engine for anything you need to do often; it is strong on some days and absent on others. A habit moves the behaviour onto something steadier, a cue in your day that asks for it the way bedtime asks for toothbrushing. Building one takes motivation, once, to fix the cue and repeat the action until it sticks. After that, the cue does the asking, and the behaviour no longer waits for the mood.

*For example:* A person who wants to walk daily stops deciding each morning. The shoes live by the door, the walk follows breakfast, and after some weeks the cue asks on mornings when the resolve is missing.

## Practice

- Build habits for desired behaviours: repeat in a consistent context until automatic. Do not rely on motivation; rely on habit. Once automatic, the behaviour does not require willpower.

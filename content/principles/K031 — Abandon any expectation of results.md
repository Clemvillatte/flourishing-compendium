> **Principle 31** — Abandon any expectation of results

The slogan belongs to the sixth point of Chekawa Yeshe Dorje's Seven Points of Mind Training, "The Commitments of Mind Training", where Adam Pearcey's translation renders it ([Lotsawa House](https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/geshe-chekhawa-yeshe-dorje/seven-points-mind-training)):

> Abandon any expectations of results.

It is the sixth of sixteen commitments in that section, and commonly numbered slogan 28 of 59 (the earlier citation to "Point Two" was wrong). Judy Lief, a teacher in the Trungpa lineage, comments:

> This slogan undercuts our attachment to either success or failure. It is a kind of positive giving up. Abandoning any hope of fruition does not mean abandoning our projects and ambitions. Instead it points to a way of going about things that is present focused rather than fixated on results.
>
> Judy Lief, [The Slogans of Atisha](https://judylief.com/offerings/slogans-of-atisha/), slogan 28

(Seven Points of Mind Training, Point Six, commitment 6.)

**Plain words** The instruction targets the grip on results, not goals themselves. Lief says the work continues, with attention on the present. The weekly "this is not going anywhere" exercise is the compendium's own suggestion, not found in these sources.

For example (imagined): sit for ten minutes with no aim of becoming calmer, and notice what the sitting is like when nothing is being measured.

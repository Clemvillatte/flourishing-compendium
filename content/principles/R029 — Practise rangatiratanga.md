> **Principle 291** — Practise rangatiratanga

Te Aka Māori Dictionary defines rangatiratanga as "chieftainship, right to exercise authority, chiefly autonomy, chiefly authority ... leadership of a social group" ([Te Aka, rangatiratanga](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/6480)). Its entry for rangatira gives the qualities of a leader as "a concern for the integrity and prosperity of the people, the land, the language and other cultural treasures", and includes a 1926 line translated as "The chief is a weaver of people" ([Te Aka, rangatira](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/word/6479)).

(Māori tikanga; Mead, 2003, Tikanga Māori; Durie, 1994, Whaiora.)

### Limits of this page

Te Aka also records the older sense of rank by descent and the later sense of self-determination from the Treaty of Waitangi translations. Te Puni Kōkiri describes rangatiratanga today as "leadership and authority within Māori communities" ([Te Tautuhi ō Rongo](https://www.tpk.govt.nz/blogs/download/9121-A/Te%20Tautuhi%20%C5%8D%20Rongo%20Guidelines.pdf)). The "lead by serving" reading in this principle is the compendium's interpretation of the weaver image; Mead 2003 and Durie 1994 are listed as further reading, not summarized here.

**Plain words**
A rangatira's work is to hold people and things together and look after what the group values. Under this meaning, leading is care for the group, not commanding it.

**For example**
Before a meeting, list the people involved and one link between them that could help: who should meet whom, and why. Hold a follow-up that asks whether any link was made.

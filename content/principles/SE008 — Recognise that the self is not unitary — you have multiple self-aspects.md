> **Principle 8** — Recognise that the self is not unitary: you have multiple self-aspects

The self is not a single, unified entity. It consists of multiple self-aspects — self in different roles, relationships, contexts, and domains. A person is simultaneously a parent, a professional, a friend, a person with hobbies, etc. Each aspect can be evaluated separately.

**Plain words** The self is not a single unified entity — it's multiple self-aspects: you in different roles, relationships, contexts, domains. Simultaneously parent, professional, friend, and the person who cries at adverts. The unity was always an achievement, not a fact.

*For example:* The person who is decisive at work and avoidant at home — not hypocrisy, aspects — and the integration question: can the work-self teach the home-self the meeting skills? The aspects aren't in conflict; they're just unacquainted.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-complexity|Self-complexity (Linville)]]**, the number of distinct self-aspects; higher complexity buffers stress (Linville, 1985, 1987)
- **[[Multiple self-aspects model (MSA)|Multiple self-aspects model (MSA)]]**, self-concept is organised into context-dependent aspects (Showers & Zeigler-Hill, 2007; McConnell, 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The many selves|The many selves]]** (William James, Principles of Psychology)
- **[[The polyphonic self|Polyphony of the soul]]** (Bakhtin, Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics)
- **[[The ten thousand things|The ten thousand things]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 34)

## Practice

- Map your self-aspects: in what roles and contexts do you have distinct senses of self? Do not reduce yourself to one aspect ("I am just my work"). Complexity is protective.

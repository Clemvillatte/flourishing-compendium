> **Principle 8** — Recognise that the self is not unitary: you have multiple self-aspects

The self is not a single, unified entity. It consists of multiple self-aspects — self in different roles, relationships, contexts, and domains. A person is simultaneously a parent, a professional, a friend, a person with hobbies, etc. Each aspect can be evaluated separately.

(William James, "The Principles of Psychology," Vol. I, 1890, ch. X; Linville, 1985, 1987; Showers & Zeigler-Hill, 2007; McConnell, 2011.)

> "Properly speaking, a man has as many social selves as there are individuals who recognize him and carry an image of him in their mind. To wound any one of these his images is to wound him." (William James, [The Principles of Psychology, Vol. I, ch. X](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57628/57628-h/57628-h.htm))

James founds the plural self on recognition: each person who knows you holds an image of you, and each of those images is one of your social selves. His chapter goes on to treat the rivalry and conflict of one's different selves, and their hierarchy — some selves matter more to a person than others.

The plural self is not a defect to be integrated away. Each aspect can be known, evaluated, and developed on its own terms, and the psychology of self-complexity treats the number of distinct aspects as a resource. The practical work is acquaintance: knowing which aspect is active, and letting the aspects learn from each other.

**Plain words**
The self is not a single unified entity — it's multiple self-aspects: you in different roles, relationships, contexts, domains. James says a man has as many social selves as there are people who recognize him. The unity was always an achievement, not a fact.

*For example:* The person who is decisive at work and avoidant at home — not hypocrisy, aspects — and the integration question: can the work-self teach the home-self the meeting skills? The aspects aren't in conflict; they're just unacquainted.

## Practice

- Map your self-aspects: in what roles and contexts do you have distinct senses of self? Do not reduce yourself to one aspect ("I am just my work"). Complexity is protective.

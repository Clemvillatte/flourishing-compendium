> **Principle 96** — Let the many become one: and be increased by one

Whitehead's formula concerns how a new event comes into being. In William Jay Garland's explanation, many past actual entities supply data for a new occasion; their synthesis produces one new actual entity. Once complete, that occasion joins the past available to subsequent occasions ([The Whitehead Encyclopedia, section 3](https://encyclopedia.whiteheadresearch.org/entries/thematic/metaphysics/the-mystery-of-creativity/)).

> "The many [entities] become one [entity], and are increased by one [entity]" (Whitehead, Process and Reality, p. 21, as quoted by Garland).

### Receiving and contributing

The first part of the formula describes concrescence, the coming together of many into a new occasion. The second concerns its addition to the world from which further occasions arise. This is a metaphysical account of becoming. The compendium borrows it as an invitation to consider what an action receives from others and what it leaves available to them; that ethical invitation is an application, not a conclusion established by the formula.

(Whitehead, Process and Reality, 1929.)

### Plain words

Consider something you have made or decided. Which earlier contributions made it possible, and what could someone else do with the result? These questions turn the abstract account into a practical reflection without assuming that everything produced is good.

### For example

Imagine writing an essay from several authors' arguments. A proposed exercise is to identify the passages you depend on, distinguish your interpretation, and state the question your essay leaves open. This illustrates receiving and contributing; an essay is not literally one of Whitehead's fundamental actual occasions.

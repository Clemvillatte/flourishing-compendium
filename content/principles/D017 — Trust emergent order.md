> **Principle 17** — Trust emergent order

An instruction can become part of the trouble it was meant to fix. Chapter 57 of the Daodejing warns that multiplying prohibitions impoverishes people, while displaying more legislation brings more thieves ([Legge translation](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm)). It answers with a ruler's restraint:

> "I will do nothing (of purpose), and the people will be transformed of themselves; I will be fond of keeping still, and the people will of themselves become correct." ([Daodejing 57, James Legge translation](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm))

The passage addresses a ruler's conduct. My own application is smaller: before adding a rule, I can ask what people already know how to arrange and whether my intervention is getting in their way. I still have to notice who is being harmed and help them when restraint would leave the harm unchecked.

Chapter 64 supplies a check on the temptation to do nothing: "Action should be taken before a thing has made its appearance; order should be secured before disorder has begun." ([Legge translation](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm)) Read together, these passages ask me to judge when help is needed and when my wish to direct has become the problem.

**Plain words**
Give people room to work things out before telling them how. Keep watching what happens, including what happens to the person with least say.

**For example**

Imagine a writing-group organiser who stops assigning the order of every discussion. Members choose the next piece themselves. When one writer keeps being passed over, the organiser raises it.

> **Principle 17** — Trust emergent order

Laozi's opener for the whole theme is one line:

> "The Tao in its regular course does nothing (for the sake of doing it), and so there is nothing which it does not do." (Dao De Jing 37, [Legge's translation](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm))

That is a claim about systems with their own internal logic: healing, trust, learning, a team finding its rhythm. On the Daoist reading, forcing them adds a second dynamic, the supervision itself, that competes with the first. Laozi makes the point with governance in chapter 57: "the more display there is of legislation, the more thieves and robbers there are," and the sage's remedy is subtraction: "I will do nothing (of purpose), and the people will be transformed of themselves" ([DDJ 57, Legge](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm)). A narrower parallel comes from motivation research: a meta-analysis of 128 studies found that "engagement-contingent, completion-contingent, and performance-contingent rewards significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = -0.40, -0.36, and -0.28, respectively)" ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/)). What helped instead looked like attention without a grip: "Positive feedback enhanced both free-choice behavior (d = 0.33)" ([ibid.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/)). The economics of the principle are tracked in [[Spontaneous order]] and [[The invisible hand]].

**Plain words**
Think of something that grew without you managing it: a friendship, a garden, a child's curiosity, your own taste. You did not manage it into being; it grew on its own internal logic. The Daoist bet is that more of your life is that kind of thing than you admit, and that your forcing is an extra ingredient the recipe never called for. Watch closely, feed where feeding helps, and leave the rest to run. The art is telling apart the things that need your push from the things your push is ruining.

*For example:* A scab you keep checking and picking at heals slower than one you leave alone; the body knows what to do when you stop interfering.

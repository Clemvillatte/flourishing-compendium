> **Principle 56** — Interrupt the chain from stimulus to ruin

The Gita gives a short causal chain for how a mind comes apart, in two verses. Verse 2.62 gives the first half:

> While contemplating on the objects of the senses, one develops attachment to them. Attachment leads to desire, and from desire arises anger.

(Bhagavad Gita 2.62, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/62/).)

Verse 2.63 finishes it: "Anger leads to clouding of judgment, which results in bewilderment of memory. When memory is bewildered, the intellect gets destroyed; and when the intellect is destroyed, one is ruined" ([BG 2.63](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/2/verse/63/)).

Counted out, the steps run: contemplating the object, attachment, desire, anger, clouded judgment, bewildered memory, a destroyed intellect, ruin. The Gita states the sequence but does not tell the reader to interrupt it at a given step. Intercepting at the earliest link, finding your own usual entry point, and the claim that most people enter at dwelling are this page's own reading. So is "the earliest is cheapest", which the text doesn't say. The verses do start at the earliest link, so the reading follows the order the text gives.

**Plain words**

Krishna describes how a mind goes wrong, one step leading to the next, starting with turning an object over in the mind. The verses don't tell you what to do about it. A natural use is to look for the earliest step you can catch.

**For example**

A man notices he has spent an hour looking at a rival's page. He recognises the first step, shuts the laptop and walks round the block. He never finds out how the afternoon would have ended.

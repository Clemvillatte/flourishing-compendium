> **Principle 61** — Recognise self-efficacy is domain-specific: not general confidence

Bandura puts it directly: "the efficacy belief system is not a global trait but a differentiated set of self-beliefs linked to distinct realms of functioning". His example is "a business executive" who "may have a high sense of organizational efficacy but low parenting efficacy" ([Bandura, Guide for Constructing Self-Efficacy Scales, 2006, pp. 307-308](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/websites.uta.edu/dist/1/4994/files/2021/07/Guide-for-Constructing-Self-Efficacy-Scales-Bandura2006.pdf)). He adds that "there is no all-purpose measure of perceived self-efficacy" and that scales "must be tailored to the particular domain of functioning".

The same passage keeps a door open that the principle's wording closes: "social cognitive theory identifies several conditions under which they may co-vary even across distinct domains of functioning". A review of academic self-efficacy in medical education gives the practical reading: "high self-efficacy in one domain does not necessarily mean high efficacy in another", and "domain particularity does not necessarily mean behavioral specificity" ([Academic self-efficacy: from educational theory to instructional practice](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40037-012-0012-5)). Pajares makes the same point, warning that domain specificity "should not be misconstrued as an extreme situational specificity" ([Pajares 1997, as hosted online](https://techofcomm.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/pajares-current-directions-in-self-efficacy-research.pdf)).

### Scope

Confidence does not transfer automatically, but it can carry over when two tasks share sub-skills. I did not open Bandura's 1997 book itself, and I did not look for evidence on how much transfer occurs in practice.

**Plain words**
Ask "confident at what?" before judging yourself. A failure in one area says little about another, and a win in one area does not guarantee the next unless the skills overlap.

**For example**
Write three areas of your life and rate from 0 to 100 how sure you are you could do a specific task in each, such as running a meeting, cooking for six, or having a hard conversation. Notice that the numbers differ.

## Psychology parallels

- **Domain-specificity of self-efficacy**, self-efficacy is specific to domains and tasks (Bandura, 1997; Pajares, 1997)
- **Limited transfer**, efficacy in one domain does not automatically transfer to another (Bandura, 1997; Pajares, 1997)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Phronesis|Phronesis as domain-specific judgment]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VI)
- **Know thyself in each domain** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[The craft-specificity of skill|The craft-specificity of skill]]** (Dreyfus & Dreyfus, Mind Over Machine)

## Practice

- Identify your self-efficacy domain by domain: where do you feel capable, where not? Build efficacy in the specific domains where you need it. Do not expect confidence to transfer.

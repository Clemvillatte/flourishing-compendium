> **Principle 26** — Sit at the feet of the elders' counsel

The huehuetlatolli ("words of the old ones") are the tradition's moral speeches: father to son, mother to daughter, elder to youth, delivered at life's thresholds (birth, marriage, taking office). Their register is unguarded tenderness and unflinching directness, often in the same speech. The principle is the tradition's own pedagogy: the counsel of those who have lived longer is to be sought, received, and weighed, not merely tolerated. Practically, identify your elders (by age, by craft, by suffering survived) and ask them one real question per season.

(Florentine Codex, Book VI; the huehuetlatolli tradition.)

**Plain words** The huehuetlatolli, the words of the old ones: moral speeches — father to son, elder to youth — delivered at life's thresholds, with unguarded tenderness and unflinching directness, often in the same breath. Seek the elders' counsel at your thresholds; give it at others'.

*For example:* The young father who asks his own father the awkward question — "what do you wish you'd known at my age?" — and gets the speech no one had asked for in thirty years.

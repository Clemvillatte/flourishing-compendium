> **Principle 10** — See emptiness: nothing has a fixed essence (shunyata)

Nāgārjuna links emptiness with dependent arising and dependent designation: things lack an independent nature that would make them what they are entirely from their own side. In Garfield's reading, this allows ordinary existence and functioning; emptiness does not mean that nothing exists (Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, XXIV.18-19, translation and commentary, pp. 298, 304-308, [text](https://pages.uoregon.edu/munno/OregonCourses/REL4400002/nagarjuna_middleway24.pdf)).

> "Whatever is dependently co-arisen / That is explained to be emptiness. / That, being a dependent designation, / Is itself the middle way." (Nāgārjuna, MMK XXIV.18, trans. Jay L. Garfield, p. 304; slashes mark verse breaks, [text](https://pages.uoregon.edu/munno/OregonCourses/REL4400002/nagarjuna_middleway24.pdf))

The argument concerns how things exist, not a promise that any difficulty can be changed at will. Garfield's commentary treats suffering and its cessation as dependent phenomena within the Buddhist path (XXIV.20-25, pp. 308-310, [text](https://pages.uoregon.edu/munno/OregonCourses/REL4400002/nagarjuna_middleway24.pdf)).

### Plain words

The compendium suggests asking what supports something you have come to regard as fixed. Consider its conditions and how you are describing it. That inquiry need not deny the problem or assume that every relevant condition is under your control.

### For example

Imagine describing a workplace as permanently hostile. Examine particular practices: how feedback is delivered, who assigns work, and what happens when concerns are raised. You may identify something changeable, or discover a constraint you must take seriously. The example illustrates an inquiry, not proof of emptiness or a guaranteed solution.

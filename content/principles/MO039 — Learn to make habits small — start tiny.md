> **Principle 39** — Learn to make habits small: start tiny

Fogg describes the method's first move in his own account:

> "First, you take any new habits you want, and you scale it back so it's super tiny. In the case of reading more, it might be - read one paragraph. In the case of meditating, it might be three calming breaths. You make it so simple, it's almost like you have no excuse not to do it." (Fogg, in Godoy, 2020, Life Kit episode aired 27 February 2020)

His own case was flossing: "I had the insight that I already know how to floss all my teeth. That's not the issue. The issue was I didn't know how to do it automatically, so I thought, well, let's scale it way back. Make it tiny; just one tooth." The method's other two moves: anchor the tiny behaviour after something you already do ("you find where it fits naturally in your existing routine"), and mark it with a positive emotion ("that technique is called celebration"). Fogg also frames the skill itself as learnable: "Habit formation is a skill. Just like any other skill, the more you practice, the better you get." This is Fogg's recommended method as he explains it; the compendium passes it on as such, not as a demonstrated universal mechanism.

**Plain words** Choose a very small version of the behaviour and an existing routine after which to attempt it. Define what counts as completing that minimum; doing more need not increase the next attempt's requirement.

*For example (imagined):* Someone commits to one push-up a day, right after their morning coffee; additional repetitions are optional.

## Practice

- Fogg's method: start tiny ("floss one tooth", "read one paragraph"), attach the tiny version to an existing routine, and mark it with a small positive emotion. Doing more is optional and need not raise the next attempt's requirement.

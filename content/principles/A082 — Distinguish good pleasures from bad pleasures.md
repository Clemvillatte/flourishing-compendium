> **Principle 82** — Distinguish good pleasures from bad pleasures

In Aristotle's account, pleasures differ with the activities to which they belong. His moral assessment of a pleasure follows the activity's worth:

> "The pleasure proper to a worthy activity is good and that proper to an unworthy activity bad; just as the appetites for noble objects are laudable, those for base objects culpable."

(NE X.5, W. D. Ross translation.)

He also says that a pleasure proper to an activity intensifies that activity. ([Book X, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html))

**Plain words** Enjoying something does not settle whether it is worth doing. Ask what action you are enjoying and whether you have reason to approve of it.

*For example:* Imagined example: a competitor enjoys playing well, then notices she also enjoys her opponent's humiliation. She can assess those sources of enjoyment separately.

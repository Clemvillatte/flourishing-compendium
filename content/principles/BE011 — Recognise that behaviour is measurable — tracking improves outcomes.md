> **Principle 11** — Recognise that behaviour is measurable: tracking improves outcomes

Behaviour can be measured and tracked. Self-monitoring (tracking behaviour) is one of the most effective behaviour-change techniques. Measurement creates awareness and feedback.

**Plain words** What gets measured gets managed — tracking a behaviour changes it, sometimes more than anything else you could do. The log is a mirror, and mirrors are underrated interventions. Simple, honest, daily.

*For example:* The food diary that quietly halves the snacking — no diet, no rules, just the fact that writing down the biscuit makes it real. Observation is half the treatment.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-monitoring (Michie et al.)|Self-monitoring (Michie et al.)]]**, tracking behaviour is a highly effective behaviour-change technique (Michie et al., 2011; Gardner, 2015)
- **Feedback improves performance**, measurement and feedback improve behaviour (Kluger & DeNisi, 1996; Hattie & Timperley, 2007)

- **[[The examined life|The examined life requires data]]** (Socrates, Apology)
- **Know thyself through measurement** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[Fallibilism|Fallibilism and feedback]]** (Peirce)

## Practice

- Track behaviours: log, count, measure. Use a habit tracker, journal, or app. Do not rely on memory or feeling; use data. Measurement is a behaviour-change technique.

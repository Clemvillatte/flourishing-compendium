> **Principle 13** — Make something of every season on earth

Miguel León-Portilla's article on Mesoamerican "flower and song" places an elegy on impermanence beside the poem of Cuacuauhtzin, who says he suffers with songs yet creates them on earth. This page draws one practical suggestion from those passages: make something during the time you have.

The article introduces the elegy as one of several poems attributed to the poet-king Nezahualcoyotl, and it cites the *Cantares Mexicanos*, fol. 17r, "in León-Portilla 1963: 72". It does not credit a translator for the passage.

> "IS IT TRUE THAT ONE LIVES ON EARTH? NOT FOREVER ON EARTH, ONLY A LITTLE WHILE HERE. THOUGH JADE IT MAY BE, IT BREAKS; THOUGH GOLD IT MAY BE, IT IS CRUSHED; THOUGH IT BE QUETZAL PLUMES, IT SHALL NOT LAST. NOT FOREVER ON EARTH, ONLY A LITTLE WHILE HERE" (*Cantares Mexicanos*, fol. 17r, as printed in León-Portilla, ["Flower and Song"](https://islandsofhistory.org/volume-3/flower-and-song-pre-columbian-literatures-of-mesoamerica/))

The Cuacuauhtzin poem (*Cantares Mexicanos*, fol. 26r) opens: "EAGERLY DOES MY HEART YEARN FOR FLOWERS; I SUFFER WITH SONGS, YET I CREATE THEM ON EARTH, I CUACUAUHTZIN" (same article).

**Plain words** The elegy says nothing here lasts, not jade, not gold, not feathers. Cuacuauhtzin writes anyway, and he says it hurts. The suggestion I take from putting them together is mine and not the article's: a short life is a reason to make something now.

*For example (my illustration):* A friend group learns that one of them is ill. Alongside the casseroles, they put together a book of photos, letters and one perfect evening, and they hand it over while there is time.

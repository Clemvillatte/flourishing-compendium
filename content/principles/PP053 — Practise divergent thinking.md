> **Principle 53** — Practise divergent thinking

Divergent thinking is the capacity to generate many different ideas in response to a single prompt. It is not the same as creativity, but it is a component. The practice: generate at least five ideas before evaluating any. The first ideas are typically the most conventional; novelty emerges later in the process. The practice trains the cognitive flexibility that creative work requires.

(Guilford, 1967; Torrance, 1974.)

**Plain words** Divergent thinking is generating many different answers to one prompt. It's trainable and it's a component of creativity. The rule is to produce at least five ideas before judging any, because the first ideas are usually the most conventional.

*For example:* Brainstorming thirty uses for a brick, most of them silly, and finding the thirty-first is actually good.

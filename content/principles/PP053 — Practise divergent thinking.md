> **Principle 53** — Practise divergent thinking

Two findings support this practice, and one commonly repeated claim does not.

### Later ideas tend to be more original

Beaty and Silvia (2012) call the serial order effect, "the tendency for later responses to a divergent thinking task to be better than earlier ones", "one of the oldest and most robust findings in modern creativity work". In their data, "creativity increased sharply with time and flattened slightly by the task's end, and fluency was highest in the task's first minute and then dropped sharply." Intelligence moderated the effect: "as intelligence increased, the serial order effect diminished." They read this as evidence for strategic, executive processes and not just remote associations spreading in memory ([Beaty and Silvia 2012, Psychol Aesthet Creat Arts 6(4):309-319](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/uncg/f/P_Silvia_Why_2012.pdf)). So pushing past the first answers is justified. The specific number five is a convenient rule of thumb, not a result from these studies.

### Training can help

Scott, Leritz and Mumford (2004) reviewed 70 earlier studies and found that "well-designed creativity training programs typically induce gains in performance" ([quantitative review](http://www.psychologie.uni-mannheim.de/cip/tut/seminare_wittmann/meta_fribourg/sources/Scott_etal_2004_Effectiveness_of_creativity_training.pdf)). That covers creativity training in general, not only the five-ideas habit.

### What divergent thinking does not show

Runco and Acar write that divergent thinking "does not guarantee actual creative achievement, but tests of DT are reliable and reasonably valid predictors of certain performance criteria" ([Creativity Research Journal](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400419.2012.652929)). The earlier claim that the practice "trains the cognitive flexibility that creative work requires" was not tested in any source I opened.

**Plain words** Early ideas tend to be the obvious ones, and original ones tend to arrive later, so keep generating before you judge. Divergent thinking is one useful piece of creativity, not a guarantee of it.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. You list thirty uses for a brick. The first ten are the usual ones, and the better ideas show up near the end.

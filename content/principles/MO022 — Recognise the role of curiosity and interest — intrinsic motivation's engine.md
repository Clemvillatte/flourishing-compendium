> **Principle 22** — Recognise the role of curiosity and interest: intrinsic motivation's engine

Curiosity, the desire to know and understand, is a powerful intrinsic motivator, and the research has begun to show why. In the Kang et al. trivia studies, curiosity behaved like anticipated reward (correlated with caudate activity) and improved memory:

> "Higher curiosity in an initial session was correlated with better recall of surprising answers 1 to 2 weeks later." (Kang et al., "The Wick in the Candle of Learning", Psychological Science, 2009)

The same paper's working definition: "Curiosity is the complex feeling and cognition accompanying the desire to learn what is unknown." This page's application: cultivating curiosity is a motivational practice — aim effort at what you are actually curious about, and use the question you cannot yet answer as the engine.

**Plain words**
Notice which questions you actually want answered. That pull is not a distraction from serious learning; in the studies it predicted what people still remembered two weeks later. The practical move is to let the question do the work: before studying something, get yourself to a question you cannot yet answer, and study toward it. Discipline can make you sit there; curiosity has opinions about what sticks.

*For example:* The language absorbed in a year of YouTube rabbit holes and the one that died in a semester of coursework may share a brain and a capacity; the difference is what the curiosity was attached to.

## Psychology parallels

- **Curiosity and motivation**, curiosity is a powerful intrinsic motivator (Kang et al., 2009, opened; Gruber et al., 2014, not re-verified this session)
- **Interest and learning**, interest in material improves learning and memory (Hidi & Renninger, 2006; Silvia, 2006)
- **Curiosity as character strength**, curiosity is associated with well-being (Peterson & Seligman, 2004; Kashdan et al., 2004)

## Practice

- Cultivate curiosity: follow your interests, ask questions, be willing to be a beginner. When a task is boring, find something interesting in it or connect it to something you are curious about.

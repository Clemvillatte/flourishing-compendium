> **Principle 22** — Recognise the role of curiosity and interest: intrinsic motivation's engine

Curiosity — the desire to know and understand — is a powerful intrinsic motivator. Interest in a task dramatically increases motivation, engagement, and learning. Cultivating curiosity is a motivational practice.

**Plain words** Curiosity is the purest fuel there is — it needs no rewards, no stakes, no audience. When you can, aim motivation at what you're actually curious about. The engine runs on it for free.

*For example:* The language learned in a year of YouTube rabbit holes versus the one that died in a semester of coursework — same brain, same capacity. The curiosity did what discipline couldn't.
## Psychology parallels

- **Curiosity and motivation**, curiosity is a powerful intrinsic motivator (Kang et al., 2009; Gruber et al., 2014)
- **Interest and learning**, interest in material improves learning and memory (Hidi & Renninger, 2006; Silvia, 2006)
- **Curiosity as character strength**, curiosity is associated with well-being (Peterson & Seligman, 2004; Kashdan et al., 2004)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Wonder as the beginning of philosophy** (Aristotle, Metaphysics)
- **[[Beginner's mind|Beginner's mind (shoshin)]]** (Zen Buddhism, Suzuki)
- **[[Curiosity|Curiosity as virtue]]** (Dewey, Democracy and Education)

## Practice

- Cultivate curiosity: follow your interests, ask questions, be willing to be a beginner. When a task is boring, find something interesting in it or connect it to something you are curious about.

> **Principle 341** — Practise care across distance

Held's book is titled The Ethics of Care: Personal, Political, and Global. Its publisher's abstract says that where other moral theories "demand impartiality above all, the ethics of care understands the moral import of ties to families and groups", while the approach is also "relevant to global and political matters" ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/2881)). A summary of the book says that, for Held, to reach a peaceful world "we need to globalize caring relations" ([PubPub summary](https://assets.pubpub.org/v1l2q9s3/257f601d-2251-4001-874a-fb95787da1ab.pdf)). That fits the seed: special ties stay, and they do not set the limit. The summaries do not mention care for the natural world, so that part stays unverified, as does Tronto (2013).

Psychology adds a practical difficulty. Cameron and Payne (2011) found that "as the number of people in need of help increases, the degree of compassion people feel for them ironically tends to decrease". In their experiments, people who were good at emotion regulation and expected to help showed this, and those told to experience their emotions did not ([Cameron and Payne, PDF](https://bkpayne.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/7990/2015/02/CameronPayne2011.pdf); [The Unfinishable Map summary](https://unfinishablemap.org/voids/numbing-void/)). So distant care may be held back by a motivated, regulated response and not only by lack of concern. A 2022 paper revisits the finding ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.801150/full)); it is cited as a lead with its results not summarized here. The related identifiable-victim effect is small, r = .05 in a 41-study meta-analysis ([Lee and Feeley, 2016](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15534510.2016.1216891)), so the page does not rest on it.

Singer (2015) is cited without being opened, and Batson (2011) is not used for this question.

**Plain words** Care ethics keeps special bonds and still asks you to care beyond them. Experiments suggest that feeling for large groups may be limited by how people regulate their emotions, so extending care takes deliberate effort.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. Someone whose care has stopped at the family gate starts cooking at a shelter once a month, and finds the distance was mostly a habit of attention.

> **Principle 25** — Learn to use implementation intentions: if-then plans

Implementation intentions — specific if-then plans ("if it is 7am, then I will run") — dramatically increase goal attainment by automating the link between situation and action. They reduce the need for willpower.

**Plain words** If-then plans — "if it's 7 a.m., I run" — hand the decision to the situation instead of the mood. They're stupidly effective, and almost nobody uses them. Decide once, execute forever.

*For example:* "If I feel the urge to check my phone at my desk, then I'll write one more sentence first" — the plan intercepts the impulse before the debate starts. The debate was where you always lost.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Implementation intentions|Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer)]]**, if-then plans increase goal attainment (Gollwitzer, 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006)
- **Meta-analysis shows medium-to-large effects**, implementation intentions reliably increase goal attainment across domains (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006; Belanger-Gravel et al., 2013)
- **Mechanism: automating the situation-action link**, if-then plans make action automatic in the specified situation (Gollwitzer, 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Right intention|Right intention as specific]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **[[Prohairesis (deliberate choice)|Prohairesis (deliberate choice)]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires examined plans** (Socrates, Apology)

## Practice

- Form implementation intentions: "if [specific situation], then I will [specific action]." For example: "if it is 7am Monday, then I will walk for 30 minutes." Be specific about both the situation and the action.

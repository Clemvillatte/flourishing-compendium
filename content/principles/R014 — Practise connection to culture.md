> **Principle 276** — Practise connection to culture

Maintain your cultural identity through language, ceremony, story, and practice. Cultural continuity is a protective factor for social and emotional wellbeing. Do not let your culture be eroded by assimilation or dislocation.

(Aboriginal SEWB framework; Dudgeon et al., 2014, Working Together: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Mental Health and Wellbeing Principles and Practice.)

**Plain words** Keep your language, ceremonies, and stories alive — cultural continuity is protective, not sentimental. Erosion happens through dislocation and assimilation by default, so continuity has to be deliberate. A culture is kept the way a fire is kept: tended, passed, used.

*For example:* The household where the grandmother's tongue is spoken at dinner even though the kids answer in English — and one day, in their twenties, they're grateful the door was never shut.

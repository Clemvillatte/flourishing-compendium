> **Principle 113** — Express by shaping, not venting

Dewey distinguishes an immediate outburst from expression that develops through work on a material. The feeling changes as a person selects, arranges and reshapes what will carry it; artistic expression involves more than releasing a feeling already complete (Art as Experience, IV, LW 10:67-68, [text](https://www.uio.no/studier/emner/uv/uv/UV9406/dewey-john-(1987).-art-as-experience.pdf)).

> "To discharge is to get rid of, to dismiss; to express is to stay by, to carry forward in development, to work out to completion." (Dewey, Art as Experience, 1934, IV, LW 10:67, [text](https://www.uio.no/studier/emner/uv/uv/UV9406/dewey-john-(1987).-art-as-experience.pdf))

This is an account of artistic expression, not a timetable for recovering from distress. Dewey describes a process in which emotion and material shape each other, rather than treating finished expression as a copy of an unchanged inner state (IV, LW 10:68, [text](https://www.uio.no/studier/emner/uv/uv/UV9406/dewey-john-(1987).-art-as-experience.pdf)).

### Plain words

Give the feeling something specific to work through: a paragraph, a tune, a drawing. Make choices about what to keep and what to change, and notice whether those choices alter what you are trying to say. The compendium suggests this as an experiment in expression, without requiring a polished result or a promise of relief.

### For example

Imagine writing about someone you miss. Begin with a particular memory, then revise one sentence until it conveys what mattered in that moment. The question is whether the words express your experience more accurately, not whether completing the page ends your grief.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Affect labeling|Affect labeling]]**: choosing emotion words for faces reduced amygdala responses relative to comparison tasks in a 30-person fMRI study; this did not test artistic shaping or clinical recovery ([Lieberman et al., 2007](https://teams.semel.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/publications/May%202007%20-%20Putting%20Feelings%20Into%20Words.pdf)).
- **[[Expressive writing|Expressive writing]]**: a meta-analysis of 146 randomised disclosure studies found a small average benefit, with variable effects and unresolved mechanisms; it does not establish artistic revision as the cause ([Frattaroli, 2006, pp. 823, 846-850](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/faculty.sites.uci.edu/dist/c/602/files/2019/08/Frattaroli-psych-bulletin-2006.pdf)).

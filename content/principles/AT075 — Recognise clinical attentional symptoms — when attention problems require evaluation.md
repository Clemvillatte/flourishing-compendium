> **Principle 75** — Recognise clinical attentional symptoms: when attention problems require evaluation

Attention problems have a clinical boundary, and this principle marks it. The NIMH describes ADHD as a persistent pattern that makes it hard to function in at least two areas of life, and notes that for adults with ADHD the behaviors are more severe, frequent, and persistent, interfering with daily life, occurring across multiple situations, and lasting at least 6 months. (NIMH, n.d.)

> "Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is marked by a persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity and impulsivity that makes it hard to function in at least two areas of life, such as at home, school, or work." (NIMH, [ADHD in adults](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/adhd-what-you-need-to-know))

The consensus statement behind the other half of the principle puts the boundary in professional hands: when made by a licensed clinician, the diagnosis of ADHD is well-defined and valid at all ages, even in the presence of other psychiatric disorders, which is common. The compendium's rule follows: persistent and impairing attention problems are a matter for evaluation by a licensed clinician, and this page's exercises are complements to that, not alternatives. (Faraone et al., 2021.)

**Plain words**
The line this page draws is between a bad week and a pattern. The pattern the clinical sources describe is: more severe than other people's version, showing up in more than one part of life, and lasting months. If your attention problems look like that, the next step is a clinician, not another app or another routine.

*For example:* Misplacing keys happens to everyone. Losing jobs and relationships to the same pattern across years is the kind of persistence and impairment this page points toward an evaluation.

## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional symptoms as clinical indicators**, concentration problems are symptoms of depression, anxiety, ADHD, sleep disorders, thyroid conditions, and other medical conditions (APA, 2022)
- **Evidence-based treatments for attentional disorders**, ADHD medication, CBT for anxiety/depression, sleep treatment all improve attention (Faraone et al., 2021; Hofmann et al., 2010)

## Practice

- If attentional problems are persistent, impairing, or unexplained, seek professional evaluation. Self-directed practices are complements, not substitutes, for treatment of underlying conditions.

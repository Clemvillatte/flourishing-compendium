> **Principle 48** — Guard the One (shouyi)

The Neiye names the practice and says what it buys:

> "Hold fast to the One; do not lose it,
> And you will be able to master the myriad things." (Neiye IX, [Roth, Inward Training](https://archive.org/stream/original-tao-inward-training-nei-yeh-and-the-foundations-of-taoist-mysticism-tra/Original%20Tao%20Inward%20Training%20(Nei-yeh)%20and%20the%20Foundations%20of%20Taoist%20Mysticism%20(Translations%20from%20the%20Asian%20Classics)%20by%20Harold%20David%20Roth%20(z-lib.org)_djvu.txt))

Its recipe is physical before it is mental. First the alignment, then the concentration:

> "When the four limbs are aligned
> And the blood and vital breath are tranquil,
> Unify your awareness, concentrate your mind,
> Then your eyes and ears will not be overstimulated.
> And even the far-off will seem close at hand." (Neiye XIX)

The Daodejing asks the same holding-together as a question about what you can sustain: "When the intelligent and animal souls are held together in one embrace, they can be kept from separating. When one gives undivided attention to the (vital) breath, and brings it to the utmost degree of pliancy, he can become as a (tender) babe." (DDJ 10, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

(Neiye; DDJ 10, 22.)

**Plain words**
Guarding the One is a decision about what the One thing is right now. If you cannot name it, you are not guarding it. The Neiye's picture: when the four limbs are aligned and the breath tranquil, unify your awareness and concentrate the mind. Pick the one thing, put the body behind it, and notice whether the far-off starts seeming closer, which is the verse's own test.

**For example**
Seventeen open tabs and a half-written paragraph: close them, sit with the one task, and notice what happens to the writing and to the urge to switch.

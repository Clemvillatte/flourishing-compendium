> **Principle 32** — Learn approach behaviours for depression: engage, do not withdraw

Depression urges withdrawal (stay in bed, isolate, avoid). The behavioural treatment is the opposite: engage, activate, connect. Do not obey the depression's behavioural instruction.

**Plain words** Depression's behaviour advice is withdrawal — and the treatment is disobedience: get up, go out, engage, precisely while it all feels pointless. The feeling is the last thing to change. Act first; let it catch up.

*For example:* The person who can't imagine enjoying the party who goes anyway and has one good hour — the disease said stay home. The one good hour was the treatment working.
## Psychology parallels

- **Depression and withdrawal**, depression produces avoidance and withdrawal (Martell et al., 2001; Dimidjian et al., 2011)
- **[[Behavioral activation|Behavioural activation]] counters withdrawal**, engaging in activity reduces depression (Jacobson et al., 1996; Dimidjian et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Action over inertia|Action over inertia]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort despite depression]]** (Buddhism)
- **[[The dark night|The dark night requires action]]** (St John of the Cross)

## Practice

- When depressed, do not obey the urge to withdraw. Engage: get out of bed, go outside, contact a friend, do a small activity. The depression says withdraw; the treatment is engage.

> **Principle 114** — Create as growth: creation is the evidence of growth

Maslow distinguished the creativeness he associated with self-actualizing people from the production of influential artworks. Ian Hansen's scholarly account explains that Maslow looked for an open, spontaneous approach to ordinary activities, including housekeeping and teaching ([“Self-Actualizing Creativity,” Key Information](https://www.york.cuny.edu/file-repository/ihansen/self-actualization_needs.docx/@@download/file/Self-Actualizing_Creativity.docx)).

> "It looked like a tendency to do anything creatively: e.g., housekeeping, teaching, etc." (Maslow, Toward a Psychology of Being, p. 129, as quoted by Hansen.)

### A way of approaching the work

Hansen also notes that Maslow did not regard self-actualization as necessary or sufficient for great artistic or scientific achievements. The title's “evidence of growth” therefore needs care: producing something does not prove psychological health. Hansen raises a further limit, asking whether Maslow's preferred traits generalize beyond his cultural setting. The compendium draws a modest invitation from this account: consider how you approach an ordinary task before deciding that creativity belongs only to artists.

(Maslow, Toward a Psychology of Being, 1962.)

### Plain words

Choose a familiar task and ask whether there is room for an idea of your own. You do not have to make a masterpiece or label yourself a creative person. Trying a different approach is enough for this exercise; it is not a test of your worth.

### For example

Imagine preparing to teach a difficult passage. Try devising an example that fits the learners' questions, then ask which parts need revising. This proposed exercise concerns the approach to teaching, without diagnosing the teacher as self-actualized.

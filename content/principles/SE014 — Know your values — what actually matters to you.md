> **Principle 14** — Know your values: what actually matters to you

Values are your fundamental priorities — what you care about, what gives your life meaning and direction. Knowing your values (as distinct from inherited, absorbed, or assumed values) is a foundation of authentic living and good decisions.

**Plain words** Values are your fundamental priorities — what gives life meaning and direction. Knowing your values (as distinct from inherited, absorbed, or assumed ones) is the foundation of authentic choice. Most people have never audited theirs; they're running borrowed values.

*For example:* The person whose values turn out, on inspection, to be their parents' values with no updates — the career, the house, the status — and the audit that asks: if nobody could see it, would I still want it? The answers reorganise a life.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Values clarification|Values clarification in ACT]]**, identifying and clarifying values is a core ACT process (Hayes et al., 2011; Wilson et al., 2010)
- **Values and well-being**, living in accord with values predicts well-being and life satisfaction (Sheldon & Elliott, 1999; Sheldon et al., 2004)
- **[[Self-concordance theory|Self-concordance theory]]**, goals aligned with values are pursued more effectively and predict well-being (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Know what you value** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] requires examined values** (Socrates)
- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia as living in accord with virtue]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)

## Practice

- Clarify your values: what do you care about most? What would you want your life to stand for? Use values clarification exercises (values card sort, writing exercise). Distinguish your values from inherited ones.

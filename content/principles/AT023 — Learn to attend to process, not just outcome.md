> **Principle 23** — Learn to attend to process, not just outcome

Two classical passages put attention on the doing. In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna's instruction to Arjuna separates the action from its fruits:

> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."
>
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47, [translation with classical commentaries](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-2-47/))

In the Zhuangzi, Cook Ding answers Lord Wenhui's praise of his skill by pointing past skill to the Way, and describes his work as attention to what is in front of the blade:

> "I go along with the natural makeup, strike in the big hollows, guide the knife through the big openings, and follow things as they are. So I never touch the smallest ligament or tendon, much less a main joint."
>
> (Zhuangzi, chapter 3, "The Secret of Caring for Life", [Columbia AFE selection](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps//ps_china-zhuangzi-caring-for-life.htm))

The same attention shows up at the hard part of the work:

> "However, whenever I come to a complicated place, I size up the difficulties, tell myself to watch out and be careful, keep my eyes on what I'm doing, work very slowly, and move the knife with the greatest subtlety, until flop! The whole thing comes apart like a clod of earth crumbling to the ground."
>
> (Zhuangzi, chapter 3)

The compendium's exercise from these passages: during an activity, put attention on what the hands are doing, the sensations and the movement, rather than on the result or its evaluation.

**Plain words**
An activity has two layers you can watch: what you are doing, and what will come of it. The second layer is where the noise is: scores, judgments, comparisons. This principle says to put attention on the first layer while acting. The Gita's version: you hold the action, and the fruits are not yours to hold. The cook's version: eyes on what the blade is meeting, and at the complicated places, slow down and watch still more closely.

*For example:* Imagine a golfer lining up a putt. Between addressing the ball and making the stroke, attention can sit on the grip and the line, or on the leaderboard. The exercise is to notice where it sits and return it to the grip and the line.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Process versus outcome focus|Process vs outcome focus in performance]]**, process focus improves performance and reduces anxiety; outcome focus increases pressure and choking (Jackson & Csikszentmihalyi, 1999; Zimmerman, 2002)
- **[[Flow|Flow requires process attention]]**, optimal experience emerges when attention is fully absorbed in the process (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990)

## Practice

As a compendium exercise, choose one activity and, mid-action, name what your attention is on: the doing, or the result. If it is on the result, return it to one concrete detail of the doing.

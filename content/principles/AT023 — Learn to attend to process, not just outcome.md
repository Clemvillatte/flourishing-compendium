> **Principle 23** — Learn to attend to process, not just outcome

When engaged in an activity, you can attend to the outcome (result, evaluation, comparison) or to the process (the doing itself, the sensations, the flow). Attending to process improves performance, enjoyment, and reduces anxiety.

**Plain words** You can attend to the doing or to the scoreboard. The scoreboard is loud, but it's downstream — the doing is the only part you actually control. Focus on process and outcomes tend to tag along.

*For example:* The golfer who thinks about the trophy shanks it into the pond; the one who thinks about the grip, the stance, the swing, watches the ball behave.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Process versus outcome focus|Process vs outcome focus in performance]]**, process focus improves performance and reduces anxiety; outcome focus increases pressure and choking (Jackson & Csikszentmihalyi, 1999; Zimmerman, 2002)
- **[[Flow|Flow requires process attention]]**, optimal experience emerges when attention is fully absorbed in the process (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Wu wei|Wu wei as absorption in process]]** (Daoism, Zhuangzi, Cook Ding)
- **[[Karma yoga|Karma yoga: action without attachment to fruit]]** (Bhagavad Gita)
- **[[Right concentration|Right concentration]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- During performance (sports, presentation, creative work), attend to the process: the movement, the sensation, the doing. Do not attend to the outcome, the evaluation, or the audience's judgment.

> **Principle 9** — Treat the sociocultural level as measurable

Treat the sociocultural level as measurable, in the same way the biophysiological level is. Cultures, communities, and institutions can be characterized — by value surveys (Schwartz, 1994; Hofstede, 1980; Inglehart-Welzel, 2005), by cultural-consonance measures (Dressler and Bindon, 2000), by social-network analysis (Wasserman and Faust, 1994), by well-being measures (OECD, 2013; Diener, 2000; Clark et al., 2018), and by epidemiological surveillance of social determinants (Marmot and Wilkinson, 2006). Measurement makes design possible; design makes improvement possible.

**Plain words** Culture can be measured — values surveys, cultural-consonance studies, community indicators. "You can't quantify culture" is folklore; the tools exist and are used. What gets measured gets designed. Measure your communities before you redesign them.

*For example:* The community programme that assumed what the neighbourhood needed versus the one that surveyed it first — the survey version found out the priority was the bus route, not the workshops. Data beat the guess.

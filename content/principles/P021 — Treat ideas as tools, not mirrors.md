> **Principle 21** — Treat ideas as tools, not mirrors

Dewey's question about an idea is what it does when you act on it. In *The Quest for Certainty* he describes the older view as "a spectator theory of knowledge", in which the mind looks at reality and either copies it or fails to. He proposes instead that ideas are plans for acting, and that action is where they get tested ([Dewey, *The Quest for Certainty*, ch. V "Ideas at Work"](https://archive.org/stream/questforcertaint029410mbp/questforcertaint029410mbp_djvu.txt)).

> "In like fashion, thought, our conceptions and ideas, are designations of operations to be performed or already performed. Consequently their value is determined by the outcome of these operations. They are sound if the operations they direct give us the results which are required." (Dewey, *The Quest for Certainty*, ch. V)

In chapter X he applies this to belief in general:

> "Any belief as such is tentative, hypothetical ... Consequently, it should be the last thing in the world to be picked up casually and then clung to rigidly. When it is apprehended as a tool and only a tool, an instrumentality of direction, the same scrupulous attention will go to its formation as now goes into the making of instruments of precision in technical fields." (Dewey, *The Quest for Certainty*, ch. X "The Construction of Good")

So the question "is this worldview the true one?" becomes "does this worldview do its job here?" Dewey keeps standards in place. A tool is sound when the operations it directs give the results required, and a belief that fails that test is a poor tool.

**Plain words** Treat what you believe the way you treat a tool. Use it, take care over how it is made, check whether it does the job, and replace it when it stops working. Some tools suit a job far better than others, and what happens when you use them settles which.

*For example:* Two managers face the same unsettled year. The first keeps to the original plan because it was the plan, and treats every departure as failure. The second sees that the plan was built for last year's conditions, replaces it, and the team adjusts. This is my illustration, not Dewey's.

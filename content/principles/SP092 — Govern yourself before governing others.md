> **Principle 92** — Govern yourself before governing others

The note to Ethics IV P37 contrasts two ways of leading. One, "guided by emotion only, endeavours to cause others to love what he loves himself, and to make the rest of the world live according to his own fancy" ([Ethics IV, P37, note I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm)). The other, who "endeavours to lead men by reason... does not act by impulse but courteously and kindly, and his intention is always consistent" ([same note](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm)). The contrast is between imposing one's own preferences on others and leading by reason. The exercise below examines a preferred decision before imposing it.

> He who, guided by emotion only, endeavours to cause others to love what he loves himself, and to make the rest of the world live according to his own fancy, acts solely by impulse, and is, therefore, hateful, especially, to those who take delight in something different, and accordingly study and, by similar impulse, endeavour, to make men live in accordance with what pleases themselves.
>
> Spinoza, Ethics IV, Proposition XXXVII, note I, translated by R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm).

> But he, who endeavours to lead men by reason, does not act by impulse but courteously and kindly, and his intention is always consistent.
>
> Spinoza, Ethics IV, Proposition XXXVII, note I, continued, translated by R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm).

(Ethics IV, P37 note I; Elwes translation.)

### Plain words

The note's contrast is simple: one leader makes others live by his own fancy, another leads by reason courteously. No rule in the passage says when self-government happens. For an exercise, take a decision you are about to impose on others and examine it: where did the preference come from, and would it survive being explained to the people affected?

For example: Before setting a new schedule for a team, a person might ask why this particular arrangement appeals — convenience, habit, an unexamined preference — and what reasons for it others could share. (An invented exercise.)

> **Principle 92** — Govern yourself before governing others

The note to Ethics IV P37 contrasts two ways of leading. One, "guided by emotion only, endeavours to cause others to love what he loves himself, and to make the rest of the world live according to his own fancy" ([Ethics IV, P37, note I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm)). The other, who "endeavours to lead men by reason... does not act by impulse but courteously and kindly, and his intention is always consistent" ([same note](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm)). The order of operations follows from the contrast: what you govern others with is what you yourself are governed by. The compendium's extension for anyone who leads: your first instrument is your own ordered state.

> He who, guided by emotion only, endeavours to cause others to love what he loves himself, and to make the rest of the world live according to his own fancy, acts solely by impulse, and is, therefore, hateful, especially, to those who take delight in something different, and accordingly study and, by similar impulse, endeavour, to make men live in accordance with what pleases themselves.
>
> Spinoza, Ethics IV, Proposition XXXVII, note I, translated by R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm).

> But he, who endeavours to lead men by reason, does not act by impulse but courteously and kindly, and his intention is always consistent.
>
> Spinoza, Ethics IV, Proposition XXXVII, note I, continued, same translation.

(Ethics IV, P37 note I; Elwes translation.)

### Plain words

Two leaders, per the note: one makes everyone live by his own fancy, one leads by reason courteously. The difference is not technique; it is what each is governed by. For an exercise, before your next decision that affects other people, ask what state you are in — hurried, afraid, needing agreement — and whether that state should be making the decision.

For example: the manager who cannot understand why everyone is so anxious while visibly running on caffeine and deadline panic. The illustration is invented; the note's contrast, impulse versus consistent intention, is what it stages.

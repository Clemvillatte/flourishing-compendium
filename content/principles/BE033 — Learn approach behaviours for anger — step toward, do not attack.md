> **Principle 33** — Learn approach behaviours for anger: step toward, do not attack

Anger's action urge is attack, and the DBT opposite-action skill names the alternative in its summary table: for anger, the opposite action is "Gently avoid/be a little nice" ([Linehan, DBT Skills Training Manual, 2nd ed., Emotion Regulation Handout 10](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-handouts.pdf?t=1)). The handout spells the suggestions out:

> "Do the opposite of your angry action urges. For example: 1. Gently avoid the person you are angry with (rather than attacking). 2. Take a time out, and breathe in and out deeply and slowly. 3. Be kind (rather than mean or insulting). 4. Imagine understanding and empathy for the other person." ([Linehan, Emotion Regulation Handout 10](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-handouts.pdf?t=1))

The procedure checks the facts first: opposite action is for anger that "does not fit the facts or if acting on your emotion is not effective", and the instruction is to "Act opposite all the way to your action urges" and to "Repeat acting opposite to your action urges until your emotion changes". The principle's "step toward" names the kind-and-empathy items on that list; the gentle avoidance and the time-out belong to the same skill, and the compendium's reading keeps both halves.

**Plain words** Anger hands you a script with one stage direction: attack. The opposite-action move edits the script. Take the time-out first, with slow breathing rather than a door slam. Then come back and do the kind version: a softer voice, a genuine attempt to see the situation from the other side, an imagined good reason for what happened. Halfway measures with an eye-roll do not qualify; the instruction is to act opposite all the way.

*For example* Imagined: after a partner forgets a commitment, someone takes a slow-breathing time-out instead of opening with the list of past failures, then returns, asks what happened, and tries out a genuinely good reason for it before responding.

## Practice

- When angry, do not obey the urge to attack or withdraw punitively. Step toward: approach, communicate, listen, de-escalate.

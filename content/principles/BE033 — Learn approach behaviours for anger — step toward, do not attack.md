> **Principle 33** — Learn approach behaviours for anger: step toward, do not attack

Anger urges attack (yell, criticise, withdraw punitively, retaliate). The behavioural treatment is the opposite: step toward, engage, communicate, de-escalate. Do not obey the anger's behavioural instruction.

**Plain words** Anger's script is attack — yell, punish, withdraw to wound. The opposite action is stepping toward: lower voice, closer posture, curiosity about what's driving the other side. Not submission; refusing to let the anger write the scene.

*For example:* The argument where one person drops their volume instead of raising it — and the whole room's chemistry changes. The anger was writing a fight scene. Someone rewrote it live.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Opposite action (Linehan)|Opposite action for anger (Linehan)]]**, stepping toward rather than attacking reduces anger (Linehan, 1993; Rizvi & Linehan, 2005)
- **Venting anger does not reduce it**, catharsis is not effective; expression can amplify anger (Bushman, 2002)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Gentle answer turns away wrath|Gentle answer turns away wrath]]** (Proverbs 15:1)
- **[[The doctrine of the mean|The mean in anger]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV)
- **[[Right speech|Right speech: de-escalation]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- When angry, do not obey the urge to attack or withdraw punitively. Step toward: approach, communicate, listen, de-escalate. Do not vent; it amplifies.

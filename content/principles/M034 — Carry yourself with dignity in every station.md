> **Principle 34** — Carry yourself with dignity in every station

The Codex does not hide rank. A speech to a young nobleman begins: "Thou art of noble lineage… thou art a ruler's son, thou art a palace nobleman". It then says what nobility consists of: "the humbling, the bowing, the inclining, the weeping, the tears, the sighing, the meekness — these same are nobility, the estimable, the valued; these are honor" (Sahagún, Florentine Codex Book VI, trans. Anderson & Dibble, 1969, [scanned text](https://archive.org/stream/volume-11-book-11-earthly-things-florentine-codex-general-history-of-the-things-of-new-spain/Volume%206,%20Book%206%20-%20Rhetoric%20and%20Moral%20Philosophy%20-%20%5BFlorentine%20Codex%20General%20History%20of%20the%20Things%20of%20New%20Spain%5D_djvu.txt); the chapter number is not confirmed from the scan). Bearing is defined by conduct, not by birth alone. In a prayer to the god, the speaker says: "I am a commoner; I who am a laborer".

The parts of Book VI cited here address each rank without telling people not to envy the others, so that half of the principle is an interpretation. On envy, the psychological literature gives a definition. Smith and Kim reviewed the research and concluded that envy is "an unpleasant, often painful emotion characterized by feelings of inferiority, hostility, and resentment caused by an awareness of a desired attribute enjoyed by another person or group of persons". They also separate "envy proper (which contains hostile feelings)" from "benign envy (which is free of hostile feelings)" (Smith & Kim 2007, Psychol Bull 133(1):46-64, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201570/)).

**Plain words**
The Codex speaks to people in different ranks and defines honor by how a person behaves. Whether it told people not to envy other ranks is not established by the passages cited here. Psychologists define envy as a painful mix of inferiority and resentment, and note that some envy carries no hostility. If you notice envy of another person's position, treat it as information about what you want, then spend your effort on your own duties.

*For example:* An airport cleaner does the lounge floor corner by corner and takes pride in the standard. This is an imagined case, and nothing in it says her situation is easy.

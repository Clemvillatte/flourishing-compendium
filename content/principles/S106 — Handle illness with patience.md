> **Principle 228** — Handle illness with patience

Illness is a dispreferred indifferent. The Stoic does not seek illness but does not let it destroy their peace. Epictetus, who was physically disabled, taught that illness does not touch the character. Face illness with patience and dignity.

(Disc. I.1, I.6, I.9, I.14, I.25, III.7, III.10; Ench. 1; Med. IV.10, VII.33, VII.64, IX.41, X.33; Seneca, Letters 24, 67, 78.)

**Plain words** Illness is a dispreferred indifferent. The Stoic doesn't seek it and doesn't let it destroy their peace. Epictetus, disabled himself, taught that illness doesn't touch character. Meet illness with patience and dignity.

*For example:* Spending a hospital stay talking kindly with the nurses rather than raging at the delay.

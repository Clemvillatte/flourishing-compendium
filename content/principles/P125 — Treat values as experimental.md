> **Principle 125** — Treat values as experimental

Dewey's Theory of Valuation argues that value judgments can be handled the way scientific propositions are handled: tested by what follows from acting on them. His claim in one line:

> "Appraisals of courses of action as better and worse, more and less serviceable, are as experimentally justified as are nonvaluative propositions about impersonal subject matter." (Dewey, Theory of Valuation, p. 21, [archive.org text](https://archive.org/details/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp))

The mechanism he describes is learning from experience. In his account, maturity is a change from unreflective impulses and hard-and-fast habits to desires and interests that incorporate the results of critical inquiry (pp. 30-31), and the test runs on the gap between the end-in-view and the actual consequence:

> "Agreement between what is wanted and anticipated and what is actually obtained confirms the selection of conditions which operate as means to the desired end; discrepancies, which are experienced as frustrations and defeats, lead to an inquiry to discover the causes of failure." (Dewey, Theory of Valuation, pp. 30-31, [archive.org text](https://archive.org/details/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp))

He notes the point is folk wisdom already: proverbial maxims like "Look before you leap" and "When angry count ten" set forth, in his words, the necessity of not treating desires and interests as final in their first appearance but of treating them as means, appraised by the consequences they tend to produce in practice (p. 32).

The compendium's exercise: pick one value you hold and write down what it directs you to do, the end-in-view, and what you would count as that value succeeding and failing. The value is then treated the way Dewey treats all valuations: a proposal whose test is the consequences of acting on it.

**Plain words**
A value tells you what to aim at. What the aiming produces tells you whether to keep it. The work is noticing the gap between what the value promised and what acting on it delivered, and letting that gap revise the value instead of explaining the gap away. Revising is the experiment working.

*For example:* "Family first" can survive a decade in which family contact reliably produces harm. Keeping the value means explaining away each result as it arrives. Revising it means reading the decade as data: the end-in-view and the attained end have diverged, and one of them has to move.

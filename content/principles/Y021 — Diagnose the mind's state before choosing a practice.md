> **Principle 21** — Diagnose the mind's state before choosing a practice

Vyasa's commentary on Yoga Sūtra 1.1 lists five states of mind: restless (kṣipta), stupefied (mūḍha), distraught (vikṣipta), one-pointed (ekāgra), and arrested (niruddha). The commentary adds a fitness rule: in the distraught mind, samadhi is subordinated to distraction and does not belong to yoga's side, while it is in the one-pointed mind that samadhi brings enlightenment about a real entity and leads toward the arrested state. The list is diagnostic before it is prescriptive, and the compendium exercise follows it: take ten seconds before practice to name which of the five states the mind is in, write the name down, and let the naming select the practice.

> "(Those) mental (citta) conditions (bhūmayaḥ) (are the following:) Kṣipta --restless-- (kṣiptam), Mūḍha --stupefied-- (mūḍham), Vikṣipta --distraught-- (vikṣiptam), Ekāgra --one-pointed-- (ekāgram) (and) Niruddha --arrested or suppressed-- (niruddham iti)." (Vyāsa, Yoga-bhāṣya on YS 1.1, Pradīpaka rendering)

**Plain words**
The commentary names five states of mind: restless, stupefied, distraught, one-pointed, arrested. It says the distraught state does not belong to yoga's side, and the one-pointed state is where concentration does its work. Before practicing, name which state you are in and write it down.

**For example**
In an imagined exercise, someone about to sit for concentration practice names their state as restless, writes the name down, and spends the first ten minutes of the session on walking.

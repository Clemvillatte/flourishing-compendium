> **Principle 21** — Diagnose the mind's state before choosing a practice

The classical commentary names five states of mind: scattered, dull, restless-but-occasionally-collected, collected, and restrained. The practical point is matching: a scattered mind needs grounding, a dull mind needs activation, and only a collected mind is ready for concentration proper. Practically, take ten seconds to name the state before you sit down; the naming chooses the practice.

(Vyasa's Yoga-bhashya on YS 1.1, the five chitta-bhumis.)

**Plain words** Five states of mind, says the commentary: scattered, dull, restless-but-occasionally-collected, collected, restrained. The practical point is matching: a scattered mind needs grounding, a dull mind needs activation — only a collected mind benefits from subtle practices. Diagnose before prescribing.

*For example:* Someone keeps trying to do subtle concentration practices on a scattered morning mind and concludes they "can't meditate" — the diagnosis was wrong: the scattered state needed a walk first, and the practice needed to wait an hour.

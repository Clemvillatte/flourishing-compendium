> **Principle 21** — Diagnose the mind's state before choosing a practice

Vyasa's commentary on Yoga Sūtra 1.1 lists five states of mind: restless (kṣipta), stupefied (mūḍha), distraught (vikṣipta), one-pointed (ekāgra), and arrested (niruddha). The commentary adds a fitness rule: in the distraught mind, samadhi is subordinated to distraction and does not belong to yoga's side, while it is in the one-pointed mind that samadhi brings enlightenment about a real entity and leads toward the arrested state. For a compendium exercise, record how you would describe your attention before practice, then revisit that description afterwards.

> "(Those) mental (citta) conditions (bhūmayaḥ) (are the following:) Kṣipta --restless-- (kṣiptam), Mūḍha --stupefied-- (mūḍham), Vikṣipta --distraught-- (vikṣiptam), Ekāgra --one-pointed-- (ekāgram) (and) Niruddha --arrested or suppressed-- (niruddham iti)." (Vyāsa, Yoga-bhāṣya on YS 1.1, Pradīpaka rendering)

**Plain words**
Before a session, jot down what your attention is doing. Is it jumping between objects, sluggish, or staying with one thing? Afterwards, compare this note with what happened during the session.

**For example**
In an imagined exercise, someone writes "restless" before sitting, then notes when attention moved and when it stayed with the chosen object.

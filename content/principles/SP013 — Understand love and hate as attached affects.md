> **Principle 13** — Understand love and hate as attached affects

Love is joy accompanied by the idea of an external cause; hate is sadness with the idea of an external cause. This is deliberately deflationary: love is not a cosmic mystery but a joy that has been attributed to a person or thing, which is why objects of love and objects of resentment have such a grip. The grip is the attribution, and attributions can be examined. Practically, when an attachment is destroying you, do not attack the love; examine the attribution that this person is the sole cause of your power.

(Ethics III, P13 and P14, definitions of Love and Hate.)

**Plain words** Love is joy accompanied by the idea of an external cause; hate is sadness with the idea of an external cause. Deliberately deflationary: love is a joy attributed to a person or thing — which explains both the idealisation and the blame that follow.

*For example:* The new relationship where every good mood gets credited to the partner — love's bookkeeping — and the first bad month gets debited to them too. The Spinozist note isn't cynical; it's just accurate accounting: the joy was yours; the attribution was yours too.

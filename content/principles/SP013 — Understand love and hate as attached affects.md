> **Principle 13** — Understand love and hate as attached affects

> Love is pleasure, accompanied by the idea of an external cause. [...] Hatred is pain, accompanied by the idea of an external cause.

(Spinoza, Ethics, Part III, Definitions of the Emotions VI and VII, trans. R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800). Elwes renders the Latin as "pleasure" and "pain." The entry's "joy" and "sadness" follow other translations, which were not opened. The earlier locator "III, P13 and P14" does not match the translation read here, where the definitions sit at the end of Part III.)

Spinoza's practical use of this comes in Part V, Proposition 2: "If we remove a disturbance of the spirit, or emotion, from the thought of an external cause, and unite it to other thoughts, then will the love or hatred towards that external cause, and also the vacillations of spirit which arise from these emotions, be destroyed." The proof runs: "That, which constitutes the reality of love or hatred, is pleasure or pain, accompanied by the idea of an external cause," so when the cause is removed from the thought, "the reality of love or hatred is removed with it."

That is close to the entry's advice to examine the attribution, though Spinoza's remedy is to link the emotion to other thoughts, not to doubt the cause. The entry's phrase "sole cause of your power" and its "do not attack the love" advice are its own wording. The Bowlby, Plato and Metta parallels were not opened.

**Plain words** For Spinoza, love is a pleasure you attribute to something outside you, and hatred is a pain you attribute to something outside you. The feeling is real, and the attribution is part of what holds it in place. Loosening the attribution loosens the feeling.

*For example:* In a new relationship every good mood gets credited to the partner. In a bad month every bad mood gets blamed on them. In both cases a feeling that belongs to the person is being assigned to someone else.

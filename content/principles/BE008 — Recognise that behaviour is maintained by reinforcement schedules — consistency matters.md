> **Principle 8** — Recognise that behaviour is maintained by reinforcement schedules: consistency matters

The principle comes from the experimental analysis of behavior. Ferster and Skinner's Schedules of Reinforcement mapped how the timing and frequency of reinforcement shape behavior. Its four basic schedules (fixed-interval, variable-interval, fixed-ratio, and variable-ratio) were defined formally, and each generated a distinct, characteristic pattern of responding. The book's own summary of why this matters:

> "Far from being a mere falling-short of the ideal of inevitable reinforcement, intermittent reinforcement constitutes an important condition of action. Many significant features of behavior can be explained only by reference to the properties of schedules." (Ferster & Skinner, 1957 — [full text](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Schedules_of_Reinforcement_PDF.pdf) — Schedules of Reinforcement, pp. 2-3)

The preface states the general principle directly: reinforcement schedules "direct the persistence and patterning of all behavior." In a variable-ratio schedule, reinforcement occurs after a number of responses that varies unpredictably from reinforcement to reinforcement, and responding under it runs at high, steady rates through long stretches between payoffs. The slot machine is this design in hardware, which is the compendium's application of the finding: the machine that pays at random keeps the lever pulled.

**Plain words**
Habits live on reinforcement schedules, and intermittent rewards — random, occasional payouts — are the sticky kind. That's why slots, feeds, and toxic relationships are so hard to leave: the next pull might pay. Consistency in your own habits works the same physics, on purpose.

**For example**
As an everyday illustration: the occasional windfall is what keeps someone at the machine — the unpredictability is the hook. Design your rewards predictable, and the habit boring.

## Practice

- Use continuous reinforcement to build new behaviours (reward every time), then shift to intermittent to maintain. This produces durable behaviour.

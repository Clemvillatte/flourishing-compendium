> **Principle 5** — Understand that thinking is embodied: the body shapes the mind

Merleau-Ponty's formulation of the position is that the body is not a container that consciousness happens to occupy; it is the general medium through which any world shows up for you at all. The passage he uses to argue this is about habit — dancing, specifically — which is where the claim is easiest to see.

> The body is our general medium for having a world. Sometimes it is restricted to the actions necessary for the conservation of life, and accordingly it posits around us a biological world; at other times, elaborating upon these primary actions and moving from their literal to a figurative meaning, it manifests through them a core of new significance: this is true of motor habits such as dancing. (Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, trans. Colin Smith, section "The spatiality of one's own body and motility," printed p. 169, [archive.org PDF](https://ia601603.us.archive.org/13/items/a077413/Phenomenology+of+Perception.pdf))

In the surrounding text of the same section, Merleau-Ponty describes the organist who, "giving himself entirely to the music, reaches for precisely those stops and pedals which are to bring it into being," and argues that "the body is essentially an expressive space" — not one expressive space among others but the origin of the rest, the thing that causes other things to begin to exist as objects "under our hands and eyes" (same PDF, printed pp. 168-169). What he is describing is the structure of bodily habit: the meaning of an activity can come to be located in the movement itself rather than in a separate verbal account of the movement.

**Plain words** A philosophy that treats thinking as pure computation treats the body as a vehicle that merely carries the brain around. Merleau-Ponty's alternative is that the body is not a thing among things but the medium through which anything else becomes available to you at all. A skill you have embodied, like a musical instrument under your hands, is present to you in a way a written instruction about that skill is not.

*For example:* Imagine someone who has played the organ for years sitting at the console. The stops and pedals are not first reasoned out as objects in space; the music reaches for them, and they appear as part of the activity rather than as items to be located. That is the structure Merleau-Ponty is pointing at — the body makes things available by incorporating them.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Embodied cognition|Embodied cognition]]**, abstract concepts are processed via sensorimotor systems; physical experience shapes conceptual thought (Barsalou, 2008; Lakoff & Johnson, 1999)
- **Interoception influences cognition**, bodily signals shape decision-making and confidence (Dunn et al., 2006; Barrett et al., 2019)
- **Note: some specific embodied-cognition effects (e.g., power pose) failed replication**, the general framework is supported but specific manipulations are contested (Ranehill et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Notice, in a skill you already have, how much of it is located in the movement itself rather than in a verbal account of the movement; that noticing is the experience the principle is describing.

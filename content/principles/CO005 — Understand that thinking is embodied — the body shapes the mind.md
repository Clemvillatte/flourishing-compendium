> **Principle 5** — Understand that thinking is embodied: the body shapes the mind

Merleau-Ponty's formulation of the position is that the body is not a container that consciousness happens to occupy; it is the general medium through which any world shows up for you at all. The passage he uses to argue this is about habit — dancing, specifically — which is where the claim is easiest to see.

> The body is our general medium for having a world. Sometimes it is restricted to the actions necessary for the conservation of life, and accordingly it posits around us a biological world; at other times, elaborating upon these primary actions and moving from their literal to a figurative meaning, it manifests through them a core of new significance: this is true of motor habits such as dancing. (Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, trans. Colin Smith, part 1 chapter 5, printed p. 169, [archive.org PDF](https://ia601603.us.archive.org/13/items/a077413/Phenomenology+of+Perception.pdf))

The claim is about the structure of experience, not a metaphor. When you learn to dance, the moves are not first reasoned out and then executed; the body itself absorbs the pattern, and the meaning of the music ends up located in the movement rather than in a verbal description of it. What Merleau-Ponty is arguing in the surrounding section is that the body is not one expressive space among others but the origin of the rest — the thing that causes them to begin to exist as objects under our hands and eyes.

**Plain words** A philosophy that treats thinking as pure computation treats the body as a vehicle that merely carries the brain around. Merleau-Ponty's alternative is that the same body that moves you also makes things available to you to understand: the habits your body has learned are what you have available to think with, in the sense that a skill you have embodied is available in a way a memorised instruction is not.

*For example:* Imagine learning to dance by being handed a written description of each step and then being asked to perform. Compare that with the same person learning by following the instructor, where the movement becomes a pattern in the body itself. The second learner has something the first does not: the meaning lives in the movement, not in a description of the movement.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Embodied cognition|Embodied cognition]]**, abstract concepts are processed via sensorimotor systems; physical experience shapes conceptual thought (Barsalou, 2008; Lakoff & Johnson, 1999)
- **Interoception influences cognition**, bodily signals shape decision-making and confidence (Dunn et al., 2006; Barrett et al., 2019)
- **Note: some specific embodied-cognition effects (e.g., power pose) failed replication**, the general framework is supported but specific manipulations are contested (Ranehill et al., 2015)

## Practice

- When learning a new skill, favour instruction that involves the body directly over instruction that is purely verbal; notice how much of what you understand is located in the movement rather than in the words.

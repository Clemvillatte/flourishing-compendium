> **Principle 58** — Practise curiosity as a disposition

Curiosity here means two things Kashdan and colleagues measured separately: "motivation to seek out knowledge and new experiences" and "a willingness to embrace the novel, uncertain, and unpredictable nature of everyday life". They called these Stretching and Embracing. In student samples, scores on their 10-item inventory correlated positively with personal growth, purpose in life, psychological flexibility, positive affect and positive social relations ([Kashdan et al., 2009, J Res Pers 43(6)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20160913/)). These are correlations from self-reports, mostly in undergraduates.

On longevity, a 5-year follow-up of older adults found that "trait and state curiosity were higher in survivors than in those who subsequently died", and the state-curiosity association held after adjusting for other risk factors ([Swan and Carmelli, 1996, Psychology and Aging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8893314/)). It was an observational study and cannot show that curiosity causes longer life.

**Plain words**
Curiosity is a habit of meeting the unknown with interest. It goes with a fuller life in the studies above, though they do not show that it causes one.

**For example**
Once a day, when something confuses you, write down the question before you look for the answer. At the end of the week, pick one question and spend twenty minutes on it.

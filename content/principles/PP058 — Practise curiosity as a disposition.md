> **Principle 58** — Practise curiosity as a disposition

Curiosity, the desire for information and experience for its own sake, predicts well-being, engagement, and meaning. It is not about being interested in everything. It is about the disposition to approach novelty and uncertainty with interest rather than anxiety. Research on curiosity (Kashdan) shows that it is associated with well-being, relationship quality, and longevity.

(Kashdan et al., 2009; Kashdan et al., 2018.)

**Plain words** Curiosity is approaching novelty and uncertainty with interest rather than anxiety. It predicts well-being, engagement and meaning. It's a disposition you can strengthen, not a fixed personality feature.

*For example:* A curious person meeting a new colleague asks questions for twenty minutes; an incurious one talks about themselves.

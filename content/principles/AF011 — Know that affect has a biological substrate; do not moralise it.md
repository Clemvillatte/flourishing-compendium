> **Principle 11** — Know that affect has a biological substrate; do not moralise it

Sleep, pain, illness, medication, substance use, hunger, and hormonal cycles all directly alter affect. When you feel persistently irritable, anxious, or flat, the first move is often biological, not psychological. This is not to reduce feelings to biology; it is to recognise that the substrate matters.

**Plain words** Sleep, pain, illness, medication, hunger, and hormonal cycles directly alter affect. When you feel persistently irritable, anxious, or flat, the first move is often biological, not psychological. Don't moralise what should be triaged.

*For example:* The week-long "existential crisis" that turns out to have a blood-sugar signature — the person who finally eats breakfast and sleeps finds the crisis dissolves. The feeling was real; the cause was in the fridge, not the soul.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Sleep deprivation|Sleep deprivation amplifies amygdala reactivity]]**, a single night of sleep loss increases amygdala response to negative stimuli by ~60% and reduces prefrontal regulation (Yoo et al., 2007)
- **Pain and affect share neural circuitry**, chronic pain alters affect regulation and is associated with higher depression rates (Craig, 2009)
- **Substance use alters affective baseline**, both acute and chronic use change valence and arousal patterns (Koob & Le Moal, 2008)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Care|Care of the body as care of the soul]]** (Michel de Montaigne, Essays)
- **The body as the first home of affect** (Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception)

## Practice

- When you notice a persistent mood shift, first rule out: sleep, illness, pain, medication change, substance use, or hormonal cycle. Address the biological substrate before treating the mood as purely psychological.

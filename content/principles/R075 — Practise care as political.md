> **Principle 337** — Practise care as political

Recognise that care is not merely a private matter but a political one. The distribution of care labour, the recognition of caregivers, and the support of dependency are political questions that affect the whole society. Advocate for care to be valued, supported, and distributed fairly.

(Held, 2006, The Ethics of Care; Tronto, 2013, Caring Democracy; Noddings, 2002, Starting at Home.)

**Plain words** Care is political: who does it, who's valued for it, who's left holding it alone — those are societal decisions, not private weather. So advocate: care labour should be seen, supported, and shared fairly. The personal is structural.

*For example:* The team where "someone" always books the cake and remembers the birthdays — until that someone makes it a rotating role, and the whole team discovers how much invisible work was running under the floor.

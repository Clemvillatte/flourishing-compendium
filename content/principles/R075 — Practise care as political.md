> **Principle 337** — Practise care as political

A Democratic Theory article says feminist scholarship, citing Held (2006) and Tronto (2013), centres political thinking on "the rationality and ethic of caring" (Held 2006; Tronto 2013), which "transforms the liberal and androcentric divisions between production and reproduction, as well as public and private" ([Democratic Theory](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/democratic-theory/article/times-of-caring-democracy/7503AB1FFF0484E1C2B8B4F3730F28D0)). Held's book is titled The Ethics of Care: Personal, Political, and Global (same source). Tronto's Caring Democracy adds "caring with" as a fifth phase, defined by "plurality, communication, trust and respect" and solidarity. I did not read Held, Tronto or Noddings directly.

The seed's concern about who does care work has strong data behind it. The ILO, using time-use surveys from 64 countries, found women perform 76.2 per cent of unpaid care work, 3.2 times as many hours as men, at about 16.4 billion hours a day ([ILO, Care work and care jobs](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@dgreports/@dcomm/@publ/documents/publication/wcms_633166.pdf)). An ILO resolution says the current organisation of care "places a disproportionate share of unpaid care work on women" ([ILO resolution](https://www.ilo.org/media/568306/download)). Those figures show an unequal distribution, not what a fair one would be, which is a value question.

Marmot (2005) is a loose psychology parallel. His Lancet paper argues that "social factors" lie at the root of much health inequality, so health concerns policy makers in every sector ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15781105/)). It concerns health, not care labour. The common idea is that individual outcomes have social causes.

**Plain words** Care is political: who does it, who is paid for it and who gets it are social choices. Global data show women do about three-quarters of unpaid care. Advocating for fairer sharing follows from that, though what counts as fair is debated.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A team always leaves the birthday cards to one person. They make it a rotating job, and notice how much unpaid work that person had been doing.

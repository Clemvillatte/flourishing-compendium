> **Principle 72** — Recognise that positive affect alone is not flourishing; it is one component

Positive affect is correlated with well-being, but it is not identical to it. Flourishing requires positive affect plus meaning, connection, mastery, and autonomy. Pursuing positive affect alone, without the other components, is a common and specific form of malaise.

**Plain words** Positive affect is correlated with wellbeing but isn't identical to it — flourishing requires positive affect plus meaning, connection, mastery, and autonomy. Pursuing the pleasant alone produces the pleasant alone: a life that feels good and is empty.

*For example:* The person optimised for fun — every weekend a good time, every night a laugh — and the year-end review that reads strangely flat. The affect was funded; the meaning, mastery, and connection accounts were never opened. Four ledgers, not one.
## Psychology parallels

- **Eudaimonic vs hedonic well-being**, positive affect is a component of flourishing, not its totality (Ryan & Deci, 2001; Seligman, 2011)
- **[[PERMA model|PERMA model]]**, flourishing requires positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning, and accomplishment (Seligman, 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia as activity of the soul in accord with virtue]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **The good life as more than pleasure** (Mill, Utilitarianism)
- **[[Meaning|Meaning as the central human need]]** (Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning)

## Practice

- Do not treat feeling good as the criterion for a good life. Ask: "is my life meaningful, connected, engaged, and accomplishing — regardless of how I feel today?"

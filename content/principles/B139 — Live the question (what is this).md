> **Principle 139** — Live the question ("what is this?")

The question has a lineage. In the Platform Sutra, Huineng greets a visiting monk not with a welcome but with a question: "What is it that comes like this?" (Platform Sutra, trans. Tony Chen, [Terebess edition](https://terebess.hu/zen/platf.pdf)). Korean Seon turned exchanges like that into a standing meditation question, "What is this?"; the scholar Eun-hwa Jang traces the practice back to exactly this scene ([Korea Journal 54.4, 2014](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/investigation.pdf)). Zen master Seung Sahn made keeping the question the whole of practice:

> "If you sincerely ask, 'What am I?' sooner or later you will run into a wall where all thinking is cut off. We call this 'don't know.' Zen is keeping this 'don't know' mind always and everywhere." (Seung Sahn, quoted in Eun-hwa Jang, Korea Journal 54.4, 2014, [PDF](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/investigation.pdf))

Rilke gives the same instruction to a young poet from the other direction: "have patience with all unsolved problems in your heart and to try to love the questions themselves like locked rooms, or books that are written in a foreign tongue. Do not search now for the answers, which cannot be given you, because you could not live them" (Letter Four, 16 July 1903, [Rilke Archive](https://rilkepoetry.com/letters-to-a-young-poet/letter-four/)). Neither source promises the question resolves. The exercise the compendium takes is to carry one real question for a set stretch without demanding an interim answer.

### Plain words

A question is a thing you carry, not a thing you solve on deadline. The point of "What is this?" applied to your own mood is to keep the looking going; a settled theory of the mood stops it. An answer you defend closes the room. The question holds the door open.

### For example

Imagine taking the question "What do I actually want from this friendship?" and refusing to answer it for a week. Each time it resurfaces, you note it and let it stand. The exercise does not promise the answer arrives by day seven; it only keeps the question in the room where the friendship happens.

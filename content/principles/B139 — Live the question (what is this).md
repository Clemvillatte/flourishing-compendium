> **Principle 139** — Live the question ("what is this?")

Eun-hwa Jang traces Korean Seon's meditative question "What is this?" to the Platform Sutra exchange between Huineng and Huairang ([Jang, 2014, note 10](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/investigation.pdf)). Jang also quotes Seung Sahn's account of carrying a question into practice:

> "Zen is keeping this 'don't know' mind always and everywhere" (Seung Sahn, 1976, p. 12, as quoted by [Jang, 2014](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/investigation.pdf))

In his fourth letter to a young poet, Rilke asks his correspondent "to try to love the questions themselves" and suggests that an answer might be lived into over time ([Letter Four, 16 July 1903](https://rilkepoetry.com/letters-to-a-young-poet/letter-four/)). This is personal advice in a letter, not an explanation of Seon meditation. The compendium draws a limited practical exercise from this comparison: keep an unresolved question available for further attention without forcing an answer.

### Plain words

Choose a question you cannot yet answer honestly. Write down what you have noticed and what remains uncertain. Leave room for an answer to emerge, and for your question to change. There is no need to reject an answer once you have good reasons for it.

### For example

Imagine wondering what you want from a friendship. After a conversation, write down what you appreciated, what troubled you, and what you still need to ask. Treat these notes as observations rather than a verdict about the whole relationship.

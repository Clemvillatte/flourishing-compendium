> **Principle 34** — Learn to eat for gut health: fibre, fermented foods, diversity

Gut health — the microbiome — affects mood and cognition. Eating for gut health involves: fibre (vegetables, fruits, whole grains), fermented foods (yogurt, kefir, kimchi, sauerkraut), and dietary diversity.

**Plain words** Eat for the gut: fibre, fermented foods, diversity of plants. The microbiome affects mood, and it eats what you eat. You're feeding a committee — the trillions in your gut — and they vote on your mood.

*For example:* The week with yoghurt, kimchi, and thirty different plants versus the week of beige food — the committee's mood follows the menu. Feed your bacteria well; they outvote your willpower.
## Psychology parallels

- **Gut microbiome and mental health**, microbiome composition is associated with depression and anxiety (Cryan et al., 2019; Chevalier et al., 2020)
- **Fibre and fermented foods support gut health**, these foods promote a diverse, healthy microbiome (Cryan et al., 2019; Mörkl et al., 2018)
- **Note: probiotic evidence for mood is mixed**, and the specific causal pathways are still being established (Nikolova et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2019)

## Practice

- Eat for gut health: plenty of fibre (vegetables, fruits, whole grains), fermented foods daily, and dietary diversity. Do not rely on probiotic supplements alone.

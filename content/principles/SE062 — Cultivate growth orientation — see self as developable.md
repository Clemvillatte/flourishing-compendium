> **Principle 62** — Cultivate growth orientation: see self as developable

The belief that your abilities and self can be developed (growth mindset) is associated with better learning, persistence, and outcomes. It is distinct from fixed mindset (abilities are fixed). The effects are real but smaller than early popular accounts suggested.

**Plain words** The deepest question about your abilities isn't "how good am I?" It's "can this improve?" If you believe abilities are developable, failures become information. If you believe they're fixed, every failure is a verdict. One mindset learns; the other defends.

*For example:* Two employees get the same critical feedback. One hears "I'm not good enough" and starts avoiding the boss. The other hears "this is a skill gap" and books a course. The feedback was identical. The future wasn't.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Growth mindset|Growth mindset (Dweck)]]**, believing abilities are developable is associated with better learning and persistence (Dweck, 2006; Yeager & Dweck, 2012)
- **Note: growth mindset effects are real but modest and context-dependent**, meta-analyses show small effects, particularly for academic achievement (Sisk et al., 2018; Macnamara & Burgoyne, 2023)
- **Growth mindset interventions work best for at-risk students**, effects are larger for disadvantaged students (Yeager et al., 2019)

## Practice

- Hold a growth orientation: treat abilities and self as developable. When facing difficulty, ask "what can I learn?" rather than "am I capable?" This is more productive than fixed self-judgment.

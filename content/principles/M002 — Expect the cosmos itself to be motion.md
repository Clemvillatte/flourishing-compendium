> **Principle 2** — Expect the cosmos itself to be motion

The [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/aztec/) summarises the Nahua view of time (section 2.h):

> The history of the universe falls into five successive ages or "suns," each representing the temporary dominance of a different aspect of teotl. The present era, the "Age of the Fifth Sun," is the final one and the one in which the Aztecs believed they lived. Like its four predecessors, the Fifth Sun is destined to cataclysmic destruction, at which time the earth will be destroyed by earthquakes and humankind will vanish forever.

Underneath this, Nahua metaphysics "is processive": "Process, movement, becoming and transmutation are essential attributes of teotl" (section 2.a). The [Wikipedia article on the Five Suns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Suns) gives the name "Nāhui-Olīn", "Age of Four-Movement", for the current age, and names the Codex Chimalpopoca among the classical Nahua texts that record the ages. The encyclopedia entry itself does not use the name Nahui Ollin.

On the psychological side, [Dugas and colleagues (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/) studied 24 patients with generalised anxiety disorder and 20 controls, and found that intolerance of uncertainty did the most to separate the groups. The design is cross-sectional and does not show that accepting instability causes lower distress.

(IEP, Aztec Philosophy, sections 2.a and 2.h.)

**Plain words** In the Nahua account the present age is one of five and is expected to end. The deeper claim is that reality is movement. How far a person should plan around that is the compendium's step, not Maffie's.

For example (imagined): a couple keeps postponing a decision until things "settle down" and then notices that life is the settling down. They start planning for ongoing change.

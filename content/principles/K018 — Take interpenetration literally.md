> **Principle 18** — Take interpenetration literally

The [Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddhism-huayan/) describes the image: "the god Indra has a net with a jewel at the intersection of every two strands. Each jewel is so bright that it reflects every other jewel in the net." It adds that this is "a metaphor for the manner in which each thing that exists is dependent for both its existence and its identity upon every other thing that exists" (section 1.3).

Nishitani's term appears in Religion and Nothingness (trans. Jan Van Bragt, p. 148), as quoted in [Qin's paper on Nishitani](https://philarchive.org/archive/QINIOT-2):

> That beings one and all are gathered into one, while each one remains absolutely unique in its 'being,' points to a relationship in which [...] all things are master and servant to one another. We may call this relationship, which is only possible on the field of Śūnyatā, 'circuminsessional'

Two limits follow. Each thing stays "absolutely unique", so interpenetration does not erase individuals. And the [Stanford Encyclopedia on the Kyoto School](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kyoto-school/) says Nishitani's field of emptiness is where "a genuine interpersonal encounter can take place" (section 3.5). The suggestion to spend a minute seeing a difficult person as conditioned by everything is the compendium's practice, not a quotation from either source.

(Nishitani, Religion and Nothingness; the Huayan tradition.)

**Plain words** In Indra's net every jewel reflects all the rest. Nishitani applies that to things and people, who can be themselves only through their relations. Seeing someone this way adds a second fact to hold alongside their separateness.

For example (imagined): before a tense conversation with your sister, you remind yourself that how she is with you is partly how you are with her, and your first sentence comes out softer.

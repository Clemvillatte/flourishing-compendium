> **Principle 18** — Take interpenetration literally

The Kyoto School inherits the Huayan image of Indra's net: each jewel reflects all the others, so nothing is itself alone. Nishitani calls the relation circuminsessional: things exist only in their mutual reference. The practical use is relational: the person in front of you is not an isolated unit to be managed but a node in the same field you are, which changes both strategy and tone. Practically, in your hardest relationship, spend one minute seeing them as a being conditioned by everything that made them, before deciding anything.

(Nishitani, Religion and Nothingness, the circuminsessional relation; the Huayan tradition.)

**Plain words** Indra's net has a jewel at each knot, each reflecting all the others. Nothing is itself alone. Nishitani says things exist only in their mutual reference. Take that literally with the person you find hardest: they're not a separate unit to manage, they're a node in the same net you are.

*For example:* Before a tense conversation with your sister, you remind yourself that how she is with you is partly how you are with her, and your first sentence comes out softer.

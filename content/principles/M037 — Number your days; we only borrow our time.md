> **Principle 37** — Number your days; we only borrow our time

The songs state it flatly: we rise and fall, we borrow our days, nothing is forever on this earth; the rich, the lords, the great cities go. The tradition's memento mori is not morbid but clarifying, and it is stated in some of the most beautiful poetry any tradition has produced. Practically, keep a light practice of the count: once per month, note how many days you likely have left at current actuarial rates, and let the number choose the month's priorities.

(The Cantares's transience songs; Nezahualcoyotl's "not forever on earth.")

**Plain words** We rise and fall; we borrow our days; nothing is forever on this earth — the rich, the lords, the great cities go. The tradition's memento mori is not morbid but clarifying. Keep a light practice of the count: once a month, note how many days you've used.

*For example:* The spreadsheet someone keeps of their estimated remaining Sundays — not macabre, just arithmetic — and what it does to how they spend the next one.

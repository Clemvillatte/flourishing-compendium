> **Principle 50** — Learn to practise self-compassion when procrastinating: guilt backfires

Self-criticism after procrastination backfires: it increases negative affect, which increases avoidance. Self-compassion (forgiving yourself, then re-engaging) is more effective.

**Plain words** Guilt after procrastinating backfires — the shame spikes the bad mood, and the bad mood is what drove the avoiding. Self-compassion actually gets you back to the task faster. Forgiveness is the more effective strategy, which offends every inner critic.

*For example:* The student who forgives themselves for the wasted morning and returns to the desk at 2 p.m. — versus the one who flogs themselves and wastes the afternoon too. The flogging felt productive. It wasn't.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-compassion and procrastination**, self-forgiveness reduces future procrastination (Wohl et al., 2010)
- **Guilt increases avoidance**, self-criticism increases procrastination; self-compassion reduces it (Sirois, 2014; Pychyl, 2013)

## Practice

- When you notice you have procrastinated, practise self-compassion: forgive yourself ("this is human"), then re-engage. Do not punish yourself; it backfires. Kindness, then action.

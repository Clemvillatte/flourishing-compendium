> **Principle 50** — Learn to practise self-compassion when procrastinating: guilt backfires


Sirois and Pychyl argue that procrastination "has a great deal to do with short-term mood repair and emotion regulation" (Social and Personality Psychology Compass 7(2):115-127, 2013, [abstract](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/1/Compass%20Paper%20revision%20FINAL.pdf%E2%80%A8)). If that is right, harsh self-blame adds to the bad mood the person is trying to escape, which is the logic behind this principle. That chain is a theory, and the studies below test parts of it.

The best-known test is a two-exam study of 119 first-year students. Among students who reported high self-forgiveness for procrastinating before the first exam, procrastination before the second was lower, and "this relationship was mediated by negative affect" (Wohl, Pychyl and Bennett 2010, Personality and Individual Differences 48(7):803-808, [abstract via ISI Articles](https://isiarticles.com/bundles/Article/pre/pdf/60414.pdf)). A summary of the paper adds that the link appeared only at high levels of procrastination on the first exam ([study summary](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/how-to-stop-procrastinating.pdf)). The data are correlational, and the sample was students.

A second line of work concerns self-compassion. Across four samples, trait procrastination was associated with lower self-compassion and higher stress, and "self-compassion mediated the relationship between stress and procrastination" (Sirois 2014, Self and Identity 13(2):128-145, [abstract](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91791/)). The author concludes only that lower self-compassion "may explain some of the stress experienced by procrastinators". The evidence cited here does not show that self-compassion gets people back to work faster.

**Plain words** After putting something off, a kind response to yourself is associated with less stress and with less delay next time. Harshness is not shown to help. Begin again, kindly and small.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A student loses a morning to scrolling. She tells herself this is common, picks the smallest next step and starts it at two o'clock.

## Psychology parallels

- **Self-compassion and procrastination**, among students high in procrastination, self-forgiveness after the first exam went with less procrastination on the second, mediated by negative affect (Wohl et al., 2010; correlational)
- **Guilt, mood and avoidance**, procrastination is linked to short-term mood repair (Sirois & Pychyl, 2013), and in four samples self-compassion mediated the link between procrastination and stress (Sirois, 2014); Pychyl (2013) is unopened

## Practice

- When you notice you have procrastinated, practise self-compassion: forgive yourself ("this is human"), then re-engage. Self-punishment is not shown to help, so start with kindness, then take a small step.

> **Principle 68** — Cultivate pride (authentic, not hubristic): it consolidates achievement

[[Authentic pride|Authentic pride is the satisfaction of a real achievement aligned with values]]. [[Hubristic pride|Hubristic pride is an inflated, defensive self-evaluation]]. Authentic pride motivates continued effort; hubristic pride is associated with narcissism and fragility.

**Plain words** Authentic pride is satisfaction in a real achievement aligned with values — it consolidates the achievement and motivates continued effort. Hubristic pride is inflated and defensive — it motivates protecting the image. Cultivate the first; audit for the second.

*For example:* The graduate who says "that was hard and I did it well" — authentic, consolidating, energising — versus the one who says "obviously, I'm a genius" — hubristic, fragile, allergic to feedback. Same degree; one of them keeps learning.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Authentic versus hubristic pride|Authentic vs hubristic pride]]**, authentic pride is achievement-based and stable; hubristic pride is self-aggrandising and defensive (Tracy & Robins, 2007)
- **Authentic pride predicts perseverance and achievement**, it is associated with genuine accomplishment and continued effort (Tracy et al., 2010)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Proper pride (megalopsychia)|Proper pride (megalopsychia)]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV)
- **[[Humility|Humility and pride]]** (Aquinas, Summa Theologica)

## Practice

- When you achieve something, allow authentic pride: "I worked hard and did this." Do not inflate it into a global self-evaluation ("I am superior") or dismiss it ("it was nothing").

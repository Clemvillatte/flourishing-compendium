> **Principle 12** — Use cultural values as a lens, not a cage

Treat cultural values as a lens through which to understand your own and others' behavior, not a cage that determines it. The cultural-level taxonomies are descriptions of societies, and the opened Schwartz evidence is about personal values; the two levels must not be confused. At the cultural level, Schwartz's framework distinguishes societies on three bipolar dimensions:

> "At the cultural level, Schwartz identified three bipolar dimensions of values that allow for comparing societies. The embeddedness versus autonomy dimension focuses on the regulation of relationships between the individual and the group; the hierarchy versus egalitarianism dimension focuses on the regulation of responsible behavior among societal members; and the mastery versus harmony dimension focuses on the relationships between humanity and nature." (Elster, 2015, [Wiley Encyclopedia of Management](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781118785317.weom060176))

At the personal level, where you actually live, the variation between individuals is substantial: "The findings reveal substantial differences in the value priorities of individuals" ([Schwartz, 2012](https://scholarworks.gvsu.edu/orpc/vol2/iss1/11/)). The Hofstede and Inglehart-Welzel taxonomies (below) work at the societal level.

Schwartz's definition keeps values psychological rather than determinist:

> "The Values Theory defines values as desirable, trans-situational goals, varying in importance, that serves as guiding principles in people's lives." (Schwartz, Basic Human Values: An Overview, [archive.org](https://archive.org/details/BASICHUMANVALUES))

He adds why the lens feels like physics: "Values are beliefs. But they are beliefs tied inextricably to emotion, not objective, cold ideas" (same paper).

**Plain words** A culture's values are its defaults, and defaults feel like facts. The test is whether you can name the value behind a rule you live by. Naming it turns a cage into a lens: you can keep the rule, but now you keep it on purpose.

*For example:* "In our family we don't discuss money" — a value-lens that feels like physics until you look at it. It's a lens. Lenses can be cleaned, upgraded, occasionally replaced.

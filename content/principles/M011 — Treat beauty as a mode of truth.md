> **Principle 11** — Treat beauty as a mode of truth

The Nahua metaphor-pair in xochitl in cuicatl (flower and song) names poetry, art, and music. On Maffie's account of Nahua thought, the tradition uses it as its term for truth-telling:

> "Composing-and-performing song-poems in particular are the highest form of human artistry and the finest way for humans to present teotl since this activity most closely imitates and participates in teotl's own cosmic, creative artistry." (Maffie, "Aztec Philosophy", Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

The same article closes the point: "Hence song-poems rather than discursive arguments are the appropriate medium of sagely expression, and sages are perforce singer-songwriter-poets." This is not aestheticism; it is an epistemology: some truths require making, not only stating. This page's application: when something true in your life exceeds your prose (grief, love, conviction), let it be made: write it, sing it, draw it, build it.

**Plain words**
Some things you know but cannot say straight. Said straight, they come out flat, the way a joke explained stops being funny. The Nahua answer, on Maffie's account, was to make something: a song, a painted poem, a metaphor that carries what the plain statement drops. If a truth in your life keeps defeating your sentences, that is not a failure of the truth; it is a sign it wants a different form.

*For example:* Imagine a family that could not say "we're sorry" in a decade of conversation. A daughter writes a song in three verses, one candidate form for what the prose could not carry.

- **Expressive writing's effects**, the original experiment: students who wrote 15 minutes on 4 consecutive days about traumatic experiences reported better physical health four months later (fewer health-centre visits) than controls writing about superficial topics; the authors' summary was that writing about earlier traumatic experience was associated with short-term increases in physiological arousal and long-term decreases in health problems (Pennebaker & Beall 1986, as described by Baikie & Wilhelm 2005, [Advances in Psychiatric Treatment](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/emotional-and-physical-health-benefits-of-expressivewriting/ED2976A61F5DE56B46F07A1CE9EA9F9F))

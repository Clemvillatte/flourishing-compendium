> **Principle 92** — Investigate with curiosity (dhammavicaya)

SN 46 lists investigation of qualities (dhammavicaya) as the second factor for awakening, and the Food Sutta says what feeds it: mental qualities that are skillful and unskillful, blameworthy and blameless, gross and refined, siding with darkness and with light. Appropriate attention to those qualities is the food for the factor's arising. (SN 46.51.) The framing makes the factor a practice rather than a talent: it grows by attending to what is actually present in the mind.

> "There are mental qualities that are skillful & unskillful, blameworthy & blameless, gross & refined, siding with darkness & with light. To foster appropriate attention to them: This is the food for the arising of unarisen analysis of qualities as a factor for Awakening" (SN 46.51, [Thanissaro](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn46/sn46.051.than.html))

The compendium's application: the question "what is this?" is the appropriate attention the sutta asks for, and the verdict "this is bad" stops the examination the factor feeds on.

**Plain words**
The verdict ends the investigation; the question continues it. When something unpleasant shows up, insomnia, a mood, an argument, the verdict repeats what you already believe and closes the case. The question, even a bored one, keeps you looking at what the thing actually is, and the looking is what the sutta says to feed. Start with the laziest possible version: huh, what is this?

*For example:* Imagine lying awake at 3 a.m. and meeting it with "huh, what's this?" instead of "not again." One of these has a chance of being interesting.

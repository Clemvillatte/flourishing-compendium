> **Principle 76** — Treat digital and technological change as a designable environment

Treat digital and technological change — social media, AI, and the online public sphere — as a designable environment, not an inevitable fate. The evidence on digital well-being is mixed but actionable: passive use harms well-being, active use helps; algorithmic feeds shape attention and comparison; design choices matter (Verduyn et al., 2017; Valkenburg et al., 2022; Vanden Abeele, 2021).

**Plain words** The digital world is a designable environment, not an inevitable fate — the evidence is mixed but actionable: passive use harms, active use can help, design steers both. You're the designer of your own usage, and partly, of your community's. Treat it as architecture, not weather.

*For example:* The household that redesigned its digital defaults — devices out of bedrooms, one screen-free day — not by willpower, but by moving the furniture. The environment changed; the behaviour followed. That's design, working.

## Practice

- In personal use, prefer active to passive, curate feeds, and set time boundaries. In platform design, build for well-being (see principle 60). In parenting, treat digital environments as designable (see principle 58).

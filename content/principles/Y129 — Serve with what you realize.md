> **Principle 129** — Serve with what you realize

In chapter 6 the Gita gives a sign of the realized yogi that shows in how a person treats others:

> I regard them to be perfect yogis who see the true equality of all living beings and respond to the joys and sorrows of others as if they were their own.

(BG 6.32, trans. Swami Mukundananda, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/32/).)

Just before it, 6.29 describes the seeing itself: true yogis "see with equal eye, all living beings in God and God in all living beings". Mukundananda's translation is theistic here. The Vedanta reading behind this principle, that the self-other boundary was a mistake, is a step beyond what this translation says. The verse itself gives the sign of the seeing: the yogi responds to other people's joys and sorrows as to their own.

Chapter 3 gives the other half, that a person who has attained keeps acting:

> By performing their prescribed duties, King Janak and others attained perfection. You should also perform your duties to set an example for the good of the world.

(BG 3.20, [holy-bhagavad-gita.org](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/3/verse/20/).)

The Gita calls the person in 6.32 perfect and does not call anyone else incomplete. The stronger reading, that insight without service is unfinished, is this page's own.

**Plain words**

Seeing that you and other people are not as separate as they feel should change how you treat them. The Gita's sign of a real change is that someone else's good news or grief lands on you almost as if it were yours. If your inner life deepens and nothing shifts in how you act, it is fair to ask what has changed. A practical step is to attach each year's practice to one person or project you serve.

**For example**

A practitioner's insight deepens, and over a few months their calendar fills with other people's needs. They do not feel it as duty. They see the practice and the service as the same thing.

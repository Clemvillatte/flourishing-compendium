> **Principle 73** — Recognise behavioural symptoms of ADHD: impulsivity and inattention

ADHD is a diagnosed condition with a behavioural signature. The international consensus statement defines it:

> "ADHD is a chronic disorder in which developmentally inappropriate symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity/impulsivity lead to impairments in many aspects of living." (Faraone et al., 2021, Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews 128:789-818, Discussion, [PMC author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8328933/))

The same statement reports that the disorder "starts in childhood or early adolescence and is more common in boys than girls" and "affects 5.9% of youth and 2.8% of adults worldwide" (Discussion). Diagnosis belongs to a clinician: "When made by a licensed clinician, the diagnosis of ADHD is well-defined and valid at all ages, even in the presence of other psychiatric disorders, which is common" (Table 1). The statement was written against stigma: "Misconceptions about ADHD stigmatize affected people, reduce credibility of providers, and prevent/delay treatment" (Abstract, Background).

On treatment the statement compares the options: "Non-medication treatments for ADHD are less effective than medication treatments for ADHD symptoms, but are frequently useful to help problems that remain after medication has been optimized" (Table 1).


**Plain words** Missed deadlines and lost belongings describe difficulties; they do not by themselves establish ADHD. Describe what keeps happening and how it affects daily life, then bring those examples to a qualified clinician. An assessment can guide decisions about treatment.

**For example:** Imagined: someone repeatedly misses expense deadlines. They bring examples of these difficulties to an assessment and ask what might explain them, without deciding the cause beforehand.

## Psychology parallels

- **ADHD as a chronic disorder (Faraone et al., 2021)**, developmentally inappropriate symptoms of inattention and/or hyperactivity/impulsivity lead to impairments in many aspects of living; affects 5.9% of youth and 2.8% of adults worldwide
- **Evidence-based treatments (Faraone et al., 2021)**, medication is more effective for symptoms than non-medication treatments, which are frequently useful for what remains after medication is optimised

## Practice

- If difficulties with attention or impulsivity are affecting daily life, seek a qualified assessment and discuss treatment options.

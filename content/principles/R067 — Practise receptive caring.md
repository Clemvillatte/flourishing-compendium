> **Principle 329** — Practise receptive caring

Be receptive to the reality of the other person. Receptivity is the first phase of caring: it is the openness to being moved by the other's reality, to seeing the world from their perspective. Without receptivity, care becomes imposition.

(Noddings, 1984, Caring, ch. 3; Noddings, 2002, Starting at Home.)

**Plain words** Receptive caring: first, be actually moved by the other person's reality — see the world from where they stand. Without that first step, care becomes imposition: help shaped by your needs, not theirs. Openness before action.

*For example:* The friend who doesn't rush in with advice, but first asks what it's actually like — and only then discovers what would genuinely help, which was never what they'd assumed.

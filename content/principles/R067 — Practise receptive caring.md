> **Principle 329** — Practise receptive caring

In Caring (1984) Noddings called this receptive attention "engrossment", and later said she dropped the word because it "was sometimes misconstrued as some sort of infatuation", preferring "attention" ([Noddings, Abstracta 2010](https://abstracta.journals.hhu.de/article/download/262/279/1056)). In that paper she writes that "attention precedes empathy" and that the carer "is truly open to the other, vulnerable to what she or he is feeling". After attention comes motivational displacement: "our motive energy flows toward the purposes or needs of the other". That supports the seed's order, openness before action.

Two details differ from the seed's wording. First, Noddings says receptivity is not perspective-taking in the usual sense. She is quoted as writing "I do not 'put myself in the other's shoes'" and "I do not project; I receive the other into myself" ([Philopractice, quoting Noddings](https://www.philopractice.org/noddings)). Second, her account is relational. The cared-for must receive the caring and show that it has been received ([Philopractice](https://www.philopractice.org/noddings)). Her motive energy flows toward the other "and perhaps, although not necessarily, towards his ends" (same page), so receptivity is not the same as doing what the other asks.

For the psychology parallel, Ickes defined empathic accuracy as the extent to which people understand each other's unspoken thoughts or feelings as they occur in everyday interaction (quoted in [a Ghent University thesis](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/8659808/8659809)). Noddings describes an attitude, so the two are related and not equivalent. I did not check whether more receptive carers are more accurate.

**Plain words** Noddings says caring starts with attention that receives the other person without judging or projecting, and only then moves to action. This is an attitude to practise, and it differs from measured accuracy in reading minds.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A friend asks "what is it actually like?", listens without advising, and only then offers help that fits.

> **Principle 63** — Recognise burnout: motivation can be exhausted

Burnout — emotional exhaustion, cynicism, and reduced sense of accomplishment — is a specific motivational failure. It is caused by chronic stress, lack of autonomy, and unsupportive environments. It is not laziness; it is depletion.

**Plain words** Burnout is motivation's bankruptcy — exhaustion, cynicism, nothing working. It's not fixed by a weekend; it's fixed by removing the drain and restructuring the load. The tank doesn't refill while the hole is open.

*For example:* The teacher who "just needs a holiday" and returns from two weeks already tired by Wednesday — the holiday paid down interest, not principal. The job itself was the debt.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Burnout|Burnout (Maslach)]]**, three dimensions: exhaustion, cynicism, reduced efficacy (Maslach & Jackson, 1981; Maslach et al., 2001)
- **Burnout is caused by chronic stress and lack of autonomy**, not by individual weakness (Maslach et al., 2001; Demerouti et al., 2001)

## Practice

- Recognise burnout: if you feel exhausted, cynical, and ineffective, this is burnout, not laziness. The remedies are: rest, reduce demands, increase autonomy, and seek support. Do not push through burnout.

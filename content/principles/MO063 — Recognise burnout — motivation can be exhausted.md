> **Principle 63** — Recognise burnout: motivation can be exhausted

The World Health Organization's ICD-11 defines burn-out as "a syndrome conceptualized as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed", with three dimensions: "feelings of energy depletion or exhaustion", "increased mental distance from one's job, or feelings of negativism or cynicism related to one's job" and "reduced professional efficacy". It adds that burn-out "refers specifically to phenomena in the occupational context and should not be applied to describe experiences in other areas of life" ([WHO, 2019](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2019-burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon-international-classification-of-diseases)). The WHO describes it as an occupational phenomenon, "not classified as a medical condition".

Maslach, Schaufeli and Leiter place the individual's experience "within a larger organizational context of people's relation to their work" ([Maslach et al., 2001, Annual Review of Psychology 52, 397-422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11148311/)). Demerouti and colleagues found in 374 workers that "job demands are primarily related to the exhaustion component of burnout, whereas (lack of) job resources are primarily related to disengagement" ([Demerouti et al., 2001, Journal of Applied Psychology 86, 499-512](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419809/)).

The page's claim of causation by "lack of autonomy" is narrower than the evidence here, which points to demands and missing job resources.

(WHO, 2019; Maslach et al., 2001; Demerouti et al., 2001.)

**Plain words** Burnout is exhaustion, distance from the job and a drop in felt effectiveness, tied to ongoing workplace stress. The two studies suggest heavy demands and missing resources both matter, so changing the work may help more than rest alone. They do not test any remedy.

For example (imagined): a teacher returns from two weeks off and is tired again by midweek. The workload, not the lack of rest, is a likely source.

## Practice

- Recognise burnout: if you feel exhausted, cynical, and ineffective, this is burnout, not laziness. The remedies are: rest, reduce demands, increase autonomy, and seek support. Do not push through burnout.

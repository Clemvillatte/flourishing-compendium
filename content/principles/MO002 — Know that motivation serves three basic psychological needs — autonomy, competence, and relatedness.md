> **Principle 2** — Know that motivation serves three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness

Ryan and Deci state the position in their encyclopedia entry on [[Self-determination|self-determination theory]]:

> SDT also postulates that there are basic psychological needs that universally must be satisfied for people to experience ongoing growth, integrity, and wellness, namely needs for competence, autonomy, and relatedness. Although there are a vast number of human desires, goals and preferences, autonomy, competence, and relatedness stand out as essential nutriments for these outcomes. (Ryan & Deci, 2022)

The same entry records what the theory expects of environments:

> Social environments that support the satisfaction of these psychological needs promote capacities for self-regulation and social relationships, and well-being whereas those that thwart satisfaction of these needs lead to a broad array of more impoverished individual and interpersonal outcomes. (Ryan & Deci, 2022)

(Ryan & Deci, 2022, "Self-Determination Theory," in Encyclopedia of Quality of Life and Well-Being Research, Springer.)

**Plain words**
Ryan and Deci propose that satisfaction of autonomy, competence and relatedness supports growth and well-being. The compendium uses these needs as prompts when reviewing an activity. Consider whether support for any of them may be relevant to a change in motivation, while leaving other possible explanations open.

*For example:* Suppose your weekly class has become less appealing. Describe what has changed in the class before considering how it relates to the three needs. Write down one question you could ask the teacher and what information would help you decide whether a change is worth trying.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Self-determination|Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan)]]**, three basic needs: autonomy, competence, relatedness (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Ryan & Deci, 2017)
- **Need satisfaction predicts well-being and motivation**, satisfaction of the three needs predicts autonomous motivation and well-being (Deci & Ryan, 2000; Van den Broeck et al., 2008)
- **Need thwarting undermines motivation**, environments that thwart these needs produce amotivation and ill-being (Bartholomew et al., 2011; Vansteenkiste & Ryan, 2013)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Eudaimonia|Eudaimonia as needs satisfaction]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **The three needs as human flourishing** (Maslow, Toward a Psychology of Being)
- **[[Autonomy|Autonomy and connection]]** (Buber, I and Thou)

## Practice

- Diagnose your motivation by checking the three needs: do I feel autonomous (my choice)? Do I feel competent (capable)? Do I feel related (connected)? Address whichever need is unmet.

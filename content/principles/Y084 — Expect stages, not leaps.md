> **Principle 84** — Expect stages, not leaps

Sutra 2.27 gives the stages in one line:

> For him [there is] insight seven-fold and advancing in stages to the highest.

(Yoga Sutras 2.27, trans. James Haughton Woods, *The Yoga-System of Patanjali*, Harvard Oriental Series 17, 1914, pp. 170-172, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/yogasystemofpata00wooduoft).)

"Him" is the yogin in whom discernment has arisen. Four belong to insight and can be effected: "1. The thing to be escaped has been thought out; nor need [the yogin] think it out again. 2. The reasons for the thing to be escaped have dwindled away; nor need they dwindle away again. 3. The escape is directly perceived by the concentration of restriction. 4. The means of escape in the form of discriminative discernment has been cultivated." Three belong to the release of the mind-stuff: "5. The authority of the thinking-substance is ended", then the aspects (guna) come to rest "like rocks fallen from the top of the mountain peak", and finally the Self is "enlightened by himself and nothing more, is stainless and isolated."

The stages run from settled understanding to something final. The text does not tell the reader to locate themselves, does not say transitions arrive as consolidations, and "gradual or sudden" speeds differ only in 1.21-22. Locating your own stage is this page's suggestion; the commentary places the last three beyond effort.

**Plain words**
A staircase is a poor picture; the commentary's is a mountain path in two halves. The lower switchbacks are climbed by legs, deliberate practices wearing away at misreading. The upper stretch appears once the legs have done their work, the seeing continuing without the pushing. Seven-fold insight is a route description for the whole climb, not a quiz for a walker mid-slope.

**For example**

A woman reads the four effort stages and sees she has reached the first: she understands where her suffering comes from. Her next job is to stop having to think it out again.

> **Principle 1** — Practice equipollence deliberately

The core method: for every argument, construct an argument of equal weight for the opposite; when the two balance, assent suspends itself, and with it the agitation that assent was feeding. This is not indecision; it is a practiced skill of argument-construction with a specific therapeutic target (the verdict). Practically, take one belief that is disturbing you, write the strongest case for its opposite in three sentences, and notice the vertigo and then the quiet that follow the balance.

(Sextus Empiricus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism, Book I, the equipollence passages.)

**Plain words** Take the belief that's tormenting you and argue the other side, properly — three strong sentences for the opposite. When both cases feel equal, the verdict loses its grip, and so does the agitation it was feeding. The point isn't indecision; it's starving the verdict.

*For example:* Someone convinced their neighbour hates them writes out the case that the neighbour is simply distracted — both readings now equally plausible — and the evening stops itching.

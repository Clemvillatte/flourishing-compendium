> **Principle 59** — Learn yoga nidra: deep rest practice

Yoga nidra ("yogic sleep") is a guided practice of lying still while an audio voice directs attention through the body. The evidence for it is real but modest.

An online randomised trial gave participants an 11-minute or a 30-minute version. Against a waiting-list control, the effect sizes were d = 0.08 to 0.19, and the 30-minute version produced reductions in stress (19%), anxiety (10%), depression (9%) and sleep disturbances (12%) ([Moszeik et al. 2025, Stress and Health](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/smi.70049)). The same paper notes that a review of 45 yoga nidra studies found only 13 with a randomised comparison, and only 5 of those with an active control. An earlier study of an 11-minute audio over 30 days (meditation group N = 341) found lower stress, higher well-being and better sleep, which the authors describe as "small effects" ([Moszeik, von Oertzen and Renner 2022, Current Psychology](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12144-020-01042-2.pdf)).

For sleep, a 2026 meta-analysis pooled only five unique studies and rated the certainty of evidence "very low", calling the findings "preliminary and hypothesis-generating" ([2026 systematic review and meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11325-026-03685-0)).



### Scope

I did not open any trauma or PTSD study, so the seed's claim about trauma is not supported here, and the iRest and Datta citations were not opened. The seed's "restores like nothing else" is not supported by these trials, which compared yoga nidra mostly with doing nothing.

**Plain words**
Yoga nidra is guided rest while lying still. Trials show small benefits for stress and sleep, with limited evidence, so treat it as a low-cost rest practice and not as a treatment.

**For example**
For four weeks, play an 11-minute guided yoga nidra recording once a day, as in the 2022 study. Rate your stress and sleep from 1 to 10 each week.

## Psychology parallels

- **iRest (integrative restoration)**, a yoga nidra-based intervention for PTSD and stress (Moszeik et al., 2020; Pence et al., 2018)

## Practice

- Practise yoga nidra: 20-45 minutes of guided deep relaxation (lying down, following a guided audio). This is deeply restorative. Use for stress, sleep, and trauma.

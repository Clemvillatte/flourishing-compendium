> **Principle 423** — Practise radical freedom

Sartre's position, stated in the lecture published as Existentialism Is a Humanism, is that freedom is not something you can decline.

> That is what I mean when I say that man is condemned to be free. Condemned, because he did not create himself, yet is nevertheless at liberty, and from the moment that he is thrown into this world he is responsible for everything he does. (Sartre, Existentialism Is a Humanism, [Warwick PDF](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/cross_fac/complexity/people/students/dtc/students2011/maitland/philosophy/sartre-eih.pdf))

The sentence that follows rules out two escapes. Sartre says the existentialist "will never regard a grand passion as a destructive torrent upon which a man is swept into certain actions as by fate, and which, therefore, is an excuse for them"; the same lecture rejects the reading of a "sign" from outside as a guide, because the person interprets the sign as they choose. On this account, what you make of a situation you did not choose to be in is not excused by either passion or instruction.

**Plain words** You did not choose your birth, your body, your century, or most of what has happened to you. Sartre's claim is that responsibility for what you do next inside those limits cannot be transferred to any of those things. The common escape is "I had no choice"; the position here is that declining to choose is itself a choice.

*For example:* Imagine someone deciding whether to spend a year in a failing job complaining or spend it looking for the next one. Sartre's claim is that the decision, including the decision to present it as forced, is the person's own.

> **Principle 49** — Use vivid sensory attention as a support

Sutra 1.35 reads: "Or [he gains stability when] a sense-activity (pravrtti) arises connected with an object [and] bringing the central-organ into a relation of stability" (trans. Woods, pp. 72-73). The principle calls this "higher sense-experience", a phrase from another translation that I have not reopened. Woods' commentary shows what is meant.

### What the commentary describes

The examples are specific. "The consciousness of supernormal (divya) odour in one who attends fixedly to the tip of his nose is sense-activity with odour [as object]; on the tip of the tongue, the consciousness of supernormal taste; on the palate, supernormal colour", and so on for touch and sound (p. 72). "These sense-activities when arisen bring the mind-stuff into a relation of stability [and] dispel doubt and become a way of approach to concentrated insight" (p. 72). The commentary adds that such experience comes "as a result of success" in fixed attention, contemplation and concentration, and that the point is to make "some one particular thing" an object of perception so that the teaching is believed (p. 73).

### A mismatch to state plainly

The sutra describes supernormal perceptions that arise after sustained practice. It is not an instruction to feel the breath at the nostril or the weight of the body on a seat. Choosing the sense channel that holds you most easily is the principle's own practical adaptation, in the spirit of a steadying object, not a reading of the sutra.

**Plain words** Patanjali says that when a clear sense experience arises tied to one object, the mind settles and doubt falls away. The text gives advanced, meditation-specific examples. For everyday use, picking an ordinary sensation to rest attention on is a sensible adaptation, though not something the sutra says.

*For example:* Imagined example: you cannot settle on a thought, so you rest attention on the feel of your feet on the floor for a few minutes. That is a modern use of a steady sensory object, not the supernormal perception of the sutra.

(Yoga Sutra 1.35 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's gloss, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 72-73, Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21.)

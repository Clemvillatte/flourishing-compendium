> **Principle 54** — Learn to use repair behaviours: fix relationship ruptures

Conflict and ruptures are inevitable in relationships. The skill is repair — behaviours that fix the rupture (apology, humour, affection, understanding). Repair behaviours predict relationship satisfaction.

**Plain words** Ruptures are inevitable; unrepaired ruptures are what actually kill relationships. Repair is a behaviour: the apology, the return, the "that came out wrong, let me try again." The skill isn't conflict-free; it's bounce-back.

*For example:* The fight that ends with one person circling back — "I'm still upset, but I love you, and I shouldn't have said that" — and the relationship survives a fight it otherwise wouldn't have. That sentence is the skill.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Repair attempts|Repair attempts (Gottman)]]**, behaviours that de-escalate conflict predict relationship satisfaction (Gottman & Levenson, 2000; Carrère & Gottman, 1999)
- **Rupture and repair in therapy and relationships**, the capacity to repair ruptures predicts outcomes (Safran & Muran, 2000; Gottman & Levenson, 2000)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Forgiveness|Forgiveness and reconciliation]]** (Buddhism; Christian tradition)
- **Teshuva: return and repair** (Jewish tradition, Maimonides)
- **[[The art of apology|The art of apology]]** (Tutu, No Future Without Forgiveness)

## Practice

- Learn repair behaviours: apology, humour, affection, taking responsibility, understanding. When a conflict escalates, use repair. Do not let ruptures fester; repair them.

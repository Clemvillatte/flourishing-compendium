> **Principle 54** — Learn to use repair behaviours: fix relationship ruptures

Ruptures in a relationship are ordinary; what happens next is the skill. Repair behaviours are the moves that pull an exchange back: an apology, a moment of humour, an arm around a shoulder, saying what you actually meant. Two research literatures bear on it from different directions.

In the marriage observation lab, how a conflict discussion begins carries weight far beyond its length. Trained observers coded the conflict discussions of 124 newlywed couples and found it "possible to predict marital outcome over a 6-year period using just the first 3 minutes of data for both husbands and wives" (Carrère & Gottman, 1999, Family Process 38(3):293-301, [Wiley record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1545-5300.1999.00293.x)). The study measures affect at the start of the talk, not repair itself; the compendium's reading is that the opening minutes are exactly where repair earns its keep, before positions harden.

In psychotherapy research, rupture and repair have their own model. Safran and Muran describe ruptures as problems in the tasks and goals of treatment or in the bond between therapist and client, and their review "presents a schematization of direct and indirect interventions that therapists typically implement to address problems related to the tasks and goals of treatment, or the affective bond between therapist and client" (Safran & Muran, 2000, Journal of Clinical Psychology 56(2):233-243, [Wiley record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1097-4679(200002)56:2%3C233::AID-JCLP9%3E3.0.CO;2-3)). Repair there means stopping to deal with the strain itself, by clarifying, apologising, or exploring what just went wrong, rather than pushing through it.

**Plain words** Most people hear "repair" and think of a big apology hours later. The useful repairs are small and early: "that came out wrong", a hand on the arm, half a joke at the right moment. The test of a couple is not whether fights happen; every couple has fights. It is whether someone can interrupt a fight while it is still small, name what just went wrong, and restart. That is a behaviour, which means it can be practised while the fight is happening, not just regretted after.

**For example:** Imagined: mid-argument about chores, one says, "Hold on. That came out wrong. I don't think you're lazy; I think I'm tired and I'm taking it out on you." The argument resumes smaller. The repair does not settle the chore question; it settles the escalation.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Repair attempts|Repair attempts (Gottman)]]**, the first three minutes of a conflict discussion predicted marital outcome over a 6-year period in a study of 124 newlywed couples (Carrère & Gottman, 1999)
- **Rupture and repair in therapy**, a schematization of direct and indirect interventions for alliance ruptures, including clarifying, apologising and exploring what went wrong (Safran & Muran, 2000)

## Practice

- Learn repair behaviours: apology, humour, affection, taking responsibility, understanding. When a conflict escalates, use repair. Do not let ruptures fester; repair them.

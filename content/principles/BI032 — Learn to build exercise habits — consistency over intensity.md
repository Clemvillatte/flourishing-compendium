> **Principle 32** — Learn to build exercise habits: consistency over intensity

The key to exercise as a mental health intervention is consistency, not intensity. Regular moderate exercise over time is more beneficial than sporadic intense exercise.

**Plain words** For exercise as mental health, consistency beats intensity — regular moderate movement over months outperforms heroic sporadic effort. The person walking daily beats the person beast-modelling every third Saturday. Boring wins.

*For example:* The January gym berserker who vanishes in February versus the daily dog-walker who's still going in December — the modest one compounds. The habit is the intervention; the intensity is decoration.
## Psychology parallels

- **Exercise habits and consistency**, consistent moderate exercise is more beneficial than sporadic intense exercise (Gardner, 2015; Wood & Neal, 2007)
- **Habit formation for exercise**, building exercise habits (context, repetition) sustains the behaviour (Lally et al., 2010; Gardner, 2015)

## Practice

- Build exercise habits: schedule it, make it specific (time, place, type), and start small. Consistency beats intensity. Do not wait for motivation; build the habit.

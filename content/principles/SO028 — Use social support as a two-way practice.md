> **Principle 28** — Use social support as a two-way practice

Use social support as a two-way practice — both receiving and giving. Receiving support buffers stress (Cohen and Wills, 1985); giving support predicts well-being and mortality independently (Brown et al., 2003; Poulin et al., 2013). The adaptive stance is reciprocity, not dependence or martyrdom.

**Plain words** Support is a two-way circuit — receiving buffers stress, giving predicts health. Both directions need traffic. The one-way helper is hiding; the one-way receiver is draining.

*For example:* The friend who lets you bring the soup when she's ill — the letting is the gift. Refusing help isn't independence; it's robbing someone of the chance to matter.

## Practice

- When in need, ask for help directly. When able, give help without expectation of return. Balance the two over time, not at every moment.

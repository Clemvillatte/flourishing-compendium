> **Principle 55** — Prefer the affects of reason; they are stronger

When time is taken into account, the emotions that spring from reason are stronger than those attached to particular objects regarded as absent, because they are attached to what is common and permanent rather than to a passing object. The desire built on understanding does not decay when the stimulus changes, which is why conviction outlasts enthusiasm. Practically, when you want a change to survive the first hard month, anchor it in a reason you can state, not only in a feeling you are having.

(Ethics V, P7-9.)

**Plain words** The emotions that spring from reason are stronger than those attached to particular absent objects — because they're attached to what is common and permanent rather than to a passing object. Prefer the affects of reason; they compound while the cravings decay.

*For example:* The crush-fuelled project (attached to one person, one mood — decaying) versus the craft-fuelled one (attached to the work itself — compounding). Time is the test: Spinoza says take time into account and the rational affect wins by a length.

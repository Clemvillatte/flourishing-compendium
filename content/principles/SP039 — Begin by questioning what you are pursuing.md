> **Principle 39** — Begin by questioning what you are pursuing

The Emendation opens autobiographically: after experience taught Spinoza that all the things men ordinarily pursue are empty and futile, he decided to ask whether there was a real good, capable of being found, such that once found it would give continuous joy. The move is the foundation of the whole book: before improving the means of life, examine the ends. Practically, once a year, write down what you are actually optimizing for, and check whether it would survive the achievement.

(Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect, sections 1-3.)

**Plain words** The Emendation opens autobiographically: after experience taught Spinoza that the things men ordinarily pursue are empty and futile, he decided to ask whether there was a real good which, once found, would give continuous, supreme joy. Begin by questioning what you are pursuing — not as a mood, as a project.

*For example:* Someone mid-career runs the audit seriously: the salary, the title, the house — each examined for the "continuous, supreme joy" quotient. The audit doesn't end the pursuit; it ends the autopilot version of it.

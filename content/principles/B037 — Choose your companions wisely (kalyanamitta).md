> **Principle 37** — Choose your companions wisely (kalyanamitta)

When Ānanda calls admirable friendship half of the holy life, the Buddha corrects him and connects it with developing the entire eightfold path. He also identifies his own teaching relationship as an instance of that friendship (SN 45.2, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [text](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.002.than.html)).

> "Admirable friendship, admirable companionship, admirable camaraderie is actually the whole of the holy life." (SN 45.2, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [text](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.002.than.html))

The passage concerns support for a particular ethical and contemplative path. It does not compare friends' influence with that of books or assign a formula for how a person becomes the average of their companions (SN 45.2, [text](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.002.than.html)).

### Plain words

The compendium suggests looking at what a friendship encourages you to practise. Can you ask honest questions, acknowledge mistakes, and support each other's commitments? Choose opportunities to learn from admirable conduct, while remembering that friendship is a relationship you contribute to as well.

### For example

Imagine joining a study group in which people admit when they do not understand a passage and check one another's interpretations. You can practise that same honesty and offer help when you can. The example identifies a useful kind of support without promising automatic improvement.

- **[[Social baseline|Social baseline]]**: Coan and Sbarra propose that trusted relationships reduce expected risk and effort; this is a theoretical account, not a guarantee for every friendship ([2015, "Tentative Conclusions"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4375548/)).

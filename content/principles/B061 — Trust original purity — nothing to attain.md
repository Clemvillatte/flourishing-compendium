> **Principle 61** — Trust original purity: nothing to attain

The Platform Sutra stages the disagreement as two verses on a wall. Shen Hsiu's stanza treats practice as polishing: the body is the Bodhi-tree, the mind a mirror bright, wiped hour by hour so no dust alights. Huineng answers that there is no tree and no stand to polish, and that from the beginning not a thing is, so there is nowhere for dust to gather. Chapter 2 puts the point in prose: the potential for enlightenment is inherent in everyone, and what differs is whether delusion keeps it unrealized. (Platform Sutra.)

> There is no Bodhi-tree,
> Nor stand of a mirror bright.
> Since from the beginning not a thing is,
> Where can the dust alight?
>
> (Huineng's stanza, Platform Sutra, ch. 1, [full text](http://www.zhenhost.net/platform/))

Bankei's lecture makes a parallel claim about the mind's ordinary working. What each person has innately is the Unborn Buddha Mind, unborn and marvellously illuminating, and his proof is hearing: while listening to a talk, you also distinguish the cawing of crows, the chirping of sparrows and the rustling of wind, without having decided to hear them. The instruction is to realize this conclusively, not to assemble anything. (Bankei.)

> "What you all have from your parents innately is the Unborn Buddha Mind alone. There's nothing else you have innately." (Bankei, [Dharma Zen Center](https://www.dharmazen.com/dharma-talks/2025/12/5/a-lecture-from-bankei-yotaku))

**Plain words**
The evidence is in your ears right now: sounds arrive and you tell them apart without managing anything. You can treat that as a running demonstration, and ask what you add on top of it: the commentary, the grading of yourself, the plan to improve. The compendium's exercise is to notice the adding, and try stopping it without stopping the listening.

*For example:* Imagine noticing mid-conversation that you are grading your own performance. The grading is an addition; the listening does not depend on it.

> **Principle 31** — Read anger as blocked striving with a wounded self-regard

In the affect definitions, anger is the desire to repay one who has injured us or someone we love, and its closest allies are emulation and wounded esteem. Anger is not primary energy; it is a secondary emotion over a frustrated conatus with an audience of the self. Spinoza's counsel is not suppression (the remedies explain why that fails) but decomposition: name the blocked striving under the anger, and the anger loses its single-mindedness.

(Ethics III, definitions of Anger and Revenge; P39 scholium on esteem.)

**Plain words** Anger is the desire to repay one who has injured us — a secondary emotion over a frustrated striving, with wounded self-regard as its closest ally. Not primary energy: it's the symptom. Read the anger as blocked striving, and the treatment aims upstream.

*For example:* The employee's fury at the colleague's promotion — surface: repayment-desire; underneath: their own striving, blocked and unacknowledged. The anger dissolves not when it's punished but when the striving gets a channel.

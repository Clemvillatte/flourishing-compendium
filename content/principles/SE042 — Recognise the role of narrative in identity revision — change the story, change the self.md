> **Principle 42** — Recognise the role of narrative in identity revision: change the story, change the self

Because identity is narrative, revising the story revises the self. This is the basis of narrative therapy. The facts of your life may not change, but the meaning, arc, and framing can.

**Plain words** You are a story you keep telling. Change the telling and you change the teller. This is not spin — the facts stay — but which facts you foreground, what you make the plot, decides who you become next. Narrative therapy is just editing with a professional's help.

*For example:* "I'm divorced" can be the story of failure, or the story of two people who learned what they needed and left. Same courthouse, same papers, different next decade.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Narrative therapy|Narrative therapy (White & Epston)]]**, revising life narratives changes identity and well-being (White & Epston, 1990; McAdams, 2001)
- **Re-storying difficult experiences**, reframing the narrative of difficult experiences improves well-being (Adler, 2012; McAdams & McLean, 2013)

## Practice

- Notice the story you are telling about a difficult experience. Is it a story of victimhood, growth, redemption, or stuckness? The facts may not change, but the arc can. Consider re-authoring.

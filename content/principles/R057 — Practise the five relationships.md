> **Principle 319** — Practise the five relationships

[[The doctrine of the mean|The Doctrine of the Mean]] lists duties between sovereign and minister, father and son, husband and wife, older and younger brothers, and friends ([chapter 20, James Legge translation](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf3.htm)). Mencius gives these relations different ethical demands ([IIIA.4](https://ctext.org/mengzi/teng-wen-gong-i)).

> "between father and son, there should be affection; between sovereign and minister, righteousness" (Mencius IIIA.4, [James Legge translation](https://ctext.org/mengzi/teng-wen-gong-i); excerpt).

The historical scheme includes unequal ranks and separate marital functions; a contemporary application need not endorse those arrangements to ask what responsibility a particular relationship gives us ([Mencius IIIA.4](https://ctext.org/mengzi/teng-wen-gong-i)). That last question is an application of the text, not a claim that the ancient roles were egalitarian.

**Plain words** Before offering the help you prefer to give, find out what this person is asking of you. A promise, a need for privacy or a request for advice can each change what being dependable requires.

*For example:* Suppose your parent needs help booking an appointment, while a friend needs someone to listen after a breakup. You offer practical help to one and time to the other, after asking what each actually wants.

## Psychology parallels

- **Relational roles and well-being**: self-determination theory places relatedness alongside autonomy and competence, so its concern for relationships does not validate obedience to a prescribed hierarchy ([Ryan and Deci, 2000 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/)).

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Role ethics|Role ethics]]**: the Doctrine of the Mean relates these duties to cultivating one's own character, not merely evaluating what other people owe ([chapter 20](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf3.htm)).

> **Principle 422** — Practise dwelling (wohnen)

Heidegger reverses the usual order of building and dwelling: "We do not dwell because we have built, but we build and have built because we dwell, that is, because we are dwellers." He then says what dwelling asks of us: "To dwell, to be set at peace, means to remain at peace within the free sphere that safeguards each thing in its nature. The fundamental character of dwelling is this sparing and preserving." The word for building, bauen, also "means at the same time to cherish and protect, to preserve and care for, specifically to till the soil, to cultivate the vine", and "Building in the sense of preserving and nurturing is not making anything" (Heidegger, 1951/1971, "Building Dwelling Thinking," in Poetry, Language, Thought, trans. Hofstadter, [full text](https://www.contentarchive.wwf.gr/images/pdfs/pe/katoikein/Filosofia_Building%20Dwelling%20Thinking.pdf)).

He does not treat dwelling as something we already have. "The real dwelling plight lies in this, that mortals ever search anew for the nature of dwelling, that they must ever learn to dwell" (same essay).

In Being and Time, he contrasts this with a false ease: the "they" brings "tranquillized self-assurance-'Being-at-home', with all its obviousness-into the average everydayness of Dasein", while anxiety brings Dasein into the mode of the "not-at-home" (Heidegger, 1927/1962, §40, Macquarrie and Robinson, [full text](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf)).

**Plain words**
To dwell is to care for the places and people you belong to, and to leave them free to be what they are. A place becomes yours through tending it, whether that is a garden, a street, a shared kitchen or a friendship. It is a practice you keep learning.

**For example**
You have lived in your flat for three years and never learned the names of your neighbours. This month you fix the broken gate, water the shared planter and learn one name a week.

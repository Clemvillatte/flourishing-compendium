> **Principle 18** — Practise deliberate distraction management: know what pulls your attention

Every person has specific, predictable attentional pullers — certain apps, topics, people, or activities. Knowing your specific pullers allows you to design around them rather than fight them with willpower.

**Plain words** You have specific, predictable attentional predators — the apps, topics, people that reliably eat you. Everyone's list is different. Find yours; that knowledge is half the management.

*For example:* The person who "just checks" sports scores loses an hour every time. That's not a mystery; that's a known predator with a known feeding schedule. Fence it.
## Psychology parallels

- **Individual differences in distractibility**, people differ in what captures their attention and how resistant they are to distraction (Forster & Lavie, 2008; Sörqvist et al., 2013)
- **Habitual media use captures attention automatically**, checking behaviours become automatic and capture attention without conscious decision (Ward et al., 2017)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Know thyself** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] includes examined distraction** (Socrates, Apology)
- **[[Environmental design|Environmental design over willpower]]** (Aristotle, Politics)

## Practice

- Keep a distraction log for a week. Note what pulled your attention and when. Identify your top 3 pullers and design around them (remove, block, or restructure).

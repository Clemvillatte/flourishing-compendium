> **Principle 149** — Moderate food, drink, sex, and sleep

Huainanzi 14.39 places food and drink alongside sleep, emotion, movement and stillness in its [[Yangsheng (cultivating vitality)|yangsheng]] instruction. The grouping makes moderation a matter of how several parts of life are arranged together, with each activity given its own term of care: moderation, appropriateness, harmony or suitability.

(Yangsheng literature; Neiye.)

> "To regulate your body and nourish your nature, moderate your sleep and rest, be appropriate in your food and drink, harmonize your happiness and anger, and make suitable your movement and stillness." (Huainanzi 14.39, [Major, Queen, Meyer and Roth trans.](https://archive.org/stream/theessentialhuainanziliuan/The%20Essential%20Huainanzi%20-%20Liu%20An_djvu.txt))

The title includes sex; this particular quotation instead addresses sleep, food, drink, happiness, anger, movement and stillness.

### Plain words

Choose a routine that is not fitting your needs or obligations. Describe what you actually do before calling it excess or discipline. What modest adjustment would you be willing to try? Keep basic needs intact while making the choice.

### For example

Plan two ways to make room for lunch on a busy day: reserve a break or prepare something you can eat without rushing. Compare which fits your duties, then reuse the workable arrangement. This overt-behaviour change protects a basic need instead of turning moderation into restriction.

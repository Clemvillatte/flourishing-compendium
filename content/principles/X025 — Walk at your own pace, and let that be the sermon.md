> **Principle 25** — Walk at your own pace, and let that be the sermon

Thoreau's walking essay and Whitman's loafe-and-observe stance: the deliberate refusal to keep pace with one's companions merely because they walk faster. The distinct contribution is the pacing principle applied to whole lives: the rate of one's own development is a fact, not a failing, and comparison's calendar is a false metric. Practically, name one domain where you are behind by others' calendar (career, family, art, savings) and re-date it by your own.

(Thoreau, "Walking"; Walden's conclusion; Whitman, "Song of Myself.")

**Plain words** Refuse to keep pace with your companions merely because they walk faster — the rate of your own development is a fact, not a failing. Comparison's calendar is a false metric. Name where you're "behind" by others' clock, and re-date it by your own.

*For example:* The novelist who published at 48 and heard about the under-30 lists for years — and whose books, arriving late and whole, are the ones still read.

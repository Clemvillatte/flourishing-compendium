> **Principle 40** — Test the three conventional goods

Spinoza takes up the three things men pursue, money, fame, and sensual pleasure, and examines each. Money is pursued as the means to everything and never the end; fame demands living according to others' understanding of the world; pleasure, taken as the end, chains the person to appetite. None is dismissed; each is demoted from end to possible instrument. The practical exercise is the demotion itself: for each of the three, name what it is a means to, and whether the end can be pursued more directly.

(Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect, sections 4-7.)

**Plain words** The three conventional goods, examined: money — pursued as the means to everything and never the end (a means mistaken for a life); fame — demands living according to others' understanding; pleasure — the body's stimulus, valuable in proportion and deadening beyond it. Test all three against the life you actually want.

*For example:* Someone maps their five-year plan against the three goods: the raise (means, fine), the followers (fame, others' understanding), the indulgences (stimulus, proportion). The plan doesn't have to be torn up — but the parts that were means posing as ends get re-labelled honestly.

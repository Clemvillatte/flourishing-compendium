> **Principle 8** — Use the middle as a vantage point

The person in nepantla sees two worlds and belongs fully to neither, which is an epistemic position, not only a wound: the between-dweller notices what insiders of each side cannot. The tradition's counsel is to use the position: the immigrant child translating between parents and school, the mediator, the scholar between fields, all hold a double vision that monoculture lacks. Practically, in your in-between places, write down what you can see from there that neither side can; the list is your unique yield.

(The nepantla vantage; Anzaldua's "la conciencia de la mestiza," labeled modern.)

**Plain words** The between-dweller sees two worlds and belongs fully to neither — which is an epistemic position, not only a wound: the person in the middle notices what insiders of each side cannot. Use the double vision; it's nepantla's dividend.

*For example:* The immigrant kid translating between parents and school sees exactly how each side misreads the other — a blind spot on both sides, visible only from the bridge.

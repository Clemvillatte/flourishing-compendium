> **Principle 123** — Practice the virtue list as a relational curriculum

The Gita's thirteenth chapter virtue list (humility, unpretentiousness, non-injury, patience, uprightness, service to teacher and elders, purity, steadfastness, self-control, dispassion, absence of egoism, non-attachment to home and family, even-mindedness in desired and undesired, devotion, solitude, distaste for the crowd, steadfastness in self-knowledge) is explicitly social: most clauses name how to be among people. Practically, take one clause per week as a relationship practice, and review Sundays.

(BG 13.7-13.11.)

**Plain words** The Gita's virtue list — humility, unpretentiousness, non-injury, patience, uprightness, service, purity, steadfastness, self-control, dispassion, absence of egoism, even-mindedness — is a relational curriculum: every virtue is tested on someone. Practise the list with people, not in the abstract.

*For example:* Someone "practises patience" alone until it's real — then the toddler throws the bowl, and the list's true testing environment arrives. The virtues were always social; the solitude was just training camp.

> **Principle 242** — End the evening with review

Seneca describes his evening practice: he reviews the day, examining what he did well and what he did poorly, without self-punishment. The review is a training tool, not a tribunal. What did I do today? What did I think? Where did I drift? What will I do tomorrow?

(Seneca, Letters 83; Med. I.1; Disc. III.10, III.23.)

**Plain words** At night, run the day back like game film: what went well, where did you drift, what will you do differently tomorrow. Seneca did this without whipping himself — the point is coaching, not sentencing. Three questions are enough: what did I do, what did I think, what needs fixing?

*For example:* The manager who writes four lines before bed — "lost my temper in the 2pm meeting, listened poorly to Sam, kept my promise on the walk" — and starts noticing the same drift before it becomes a habit.

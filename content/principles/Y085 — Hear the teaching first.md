> **Principle 85** — Hear the teaching first

In the Advaita sequence, hearing the teaching comes first, before reflection and contemplation. The Upadeśasāhasrī opens with the pupil approaching the teacher: "with fuel in his hands he should approach a teacher versed in the Vedas and established in Brahman in order to know the Eternal". The Gita gives the same order of operations: "Learn the Truth by approaching a spiritual master. Inquire from him with reverence and render service unto him" (BG 4.34). The first responsibility is fidelity of reception: get the teaching stated, and stated accurately, before doing anything with it.

(Śaṅkara, Upadeśasāhasrī I.1 §3; BG 4.34.)

> With fuel in his hands he should approach a teacher versed in the Vedas and established in Brahman in order to know the Eternal.
>
> Śaṅkara, Upadeśasāhasrī I.1, §3, translated by Swami Jagadānanda (1930), [online text](https://www.sankaracharya.org/upadesa_sahasri.html).

**Plain words**
You cannot weigh a teaching you have not received. The first step is ordinary: find someone who knows the material, ask, and keep listening until you can state the teaching back without distorting it. Weighing it comes later, and it only means something if the statement you weighed was the one actually given.

**For example**
In an imagined case, a student wants to dispute a teacher's position after one lecture. The teacher asks for the position restated in a paragraph the teacher would accept, and the dispute waits until then.

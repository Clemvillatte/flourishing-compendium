> **Principle 85** — Hear the teaching first

In the Advaita method, the teaching is first received from a teacher. The Upadeśasāhasrī opens with the pupil approaching one, its rule quoted below, and the teacher then explaining the knowledge of Brahman to a pupil with self-control and a tranquil mind. The Gita gives the same order: approach a spiritual master, inquire with reverence, render service, and learn the truth from one who has seen it (BG 4.34). The compendium's reading: the first step is fidelity of reception, getting the teaching stated accurately before anything is done with it.

(Śaṅkara, Upadeśasāhasrī I.1 §3; BG 4.34.)

> Then, with fuel in his hands he should approach a teacher versed in the Vedas and established in Brahman in order to know the Eternal.
>
> Śaṅkara, Upadeśasāhasrī I.1, §3, translated by Swami Jagadānanda (1930), [online text](https://www.sankaracharya.org/upadesa_sahasri.html).

**Plain words**
You cannot weigh a teaching you have not received. The first step is ordinary: find someone who knows the material, ask, and keep listening until you can state the teaching back without distorting it. Weighing it comes later, and it only means something if the statement you weighed was the one actually given.

**For example**
In an imagined case, a student wants to dispute a teacher's position after one lecture. The teacher asks for the position restated in a paragraph the teacher would accept, and the dispute waits until then.

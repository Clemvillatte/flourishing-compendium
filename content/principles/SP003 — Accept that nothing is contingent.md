> **Principle 3** — Accept that nothing is contingent

Things could not have been otherwise. Spinoza demonstrates that nothing in Nature is contingent, and that all things follow by the same necessity as the properties of a triangle follow from its nature. The principle is not fatalism, which would counsel passivity; it is the removal of the counterfactual burden, the exhausting rehearsal of how things should have gone. Practical translation: when something has happened, the question "how could this have been avoided" is useful only once, for learning; after that, you are arguing with geometry.

(Ethics I, P29 and P33, with the scholium on the will.)

**Plain words** Nothing is contingent: things could not have been otherwise — all things follow by the same necessity as a triangle's properties. Not fatalism (which would say effort is pointless): the necessity includes your effort as part of the mechanism. The past is unchangeable because it's complete.

*For example:* Someone replays the divorce for years — "if only I'd said" — and the Spinozist frame lands: given everything that each person was at that moment, it went the only way it could. The replay isn't processing; it's arguing with a finished proof.

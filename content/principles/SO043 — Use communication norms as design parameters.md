> **Principle 43** — Use communication norms as design parameters

Treat communication norms — the rules of discourse in a community (who speaks, who interrupts, how disagreement is expressed, what counts as evidence) — as design parameters. Habermas's ideal speech situation (1981) and the deliberative-democracy literature (Fishkin, 2009) show that norms can be designed to improve the quality of collective judgment.

**Plain words** The rules of who speaks, who interrupts, and what counts as evidence are design choices — not laws of nature. Change the rules, change the outputs. Most meetings and families run on inherited rules nobody ever examined.

*For example:* The team where the loudest voice always wins, redesigned to round-robin turns — same people, same facts, different decisions. The norm was the hidden variable.

## Practice

- In meetings, establish norms: equal turn-taking, evidence-based disagreement, and psychological safety (Edmondson, 1999). In online discourse, design for slow thinking rather than fast reaction.

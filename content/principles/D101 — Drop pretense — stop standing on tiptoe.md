> **Principle 101** — Drop pretense: stop standing on tiptoe

Chapter 24 of the Daodejing opens with two bodily images, then widens the pattern. The one who displays himself does not shine; the one who asserts his own views is not distinguished; vaunting oneself does not get merit acknowledged; self-conceit gets no superiority allowed. The Tao's verdict on all of it: "like remnants of food, or a tumour on the body, which all dislike."

> He who stands on his tiptoes does not stand firm; he who stretches
> his legs does not walk (easily).
>
> (Daodejing 24, Legge)

The compendium applies these comparisons to claims of competence or certainty that exceed what a person can support.

(DDJ 24.)

**Plain words**
Separate what you know from what you want others to think you know. State what you can support, acknowledge what needs checking, and consider whether you are making a claim mainly to impress someone.

*For example:* Imagine a new manager being asked a question she cannot answer. She acknowledges the gap and agrees to check the information before giving an answer.

> **Principle 482** — Practise the hermeneutics of faith

> ... a hermeneutics of suspicion, like that found in Marx, Nietzsche and Freud, which held that nothing ultimately means what it first seems to say.

([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Paul Ricoeur", section 2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ricoeur/), on Ricoeur's account in Freud and Philosophy (1970). Quoted at second hand; the book itself was not consulted.)

The same section sets suspicion against an approach "such as that used in The Symbolism of Evil which sought to recover meaning that was assumed to be already there." The Encyclopedia's phrase for the other side is recovery of meaning. The label "hermeneutics of faith" does not appear in the sources opened here, so this entry's name for it rests on the wider literature.

Ricoeur does not choose one side. The Encyclopedia describes his method as one that "mediates and negotiates rather than removes the conflict of interpretations." Suspicion has a job: interpretation takes "a critical function by exposing repressed or distorted meaning that lies beneath the surface of commonly accepted meaning" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, "Hermeneutics", section 8](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hermeneutics/)). The entry's "hold both in tension" follows that spirit.

**Plain words** Two ways of reading a text, a person or a situation. One asks what is really going on underneath. The other asks what is being said and takes it seriously. Ricoeur's method keeps both, and neither one gets the final word.

*For example:* A friend's compliment can be read as flattery with a motive. It can also be read as kindness. Reading both ways, then checking against what you know of the friend, is better than adopting either reading for good.

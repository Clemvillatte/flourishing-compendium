> **Principle 61** — Treat economic inequality as a health determinant

Pickett and Wilkinson's 2015 review concluded that "the body of evidence strongly suggests that income inequality affects population health and wellbeing". They write that most studies finding no association can be explained by choices of scale, controls, health measures or follow-up ([Pickett and Wilkinson 2015, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25577953/)). The pooled estimates are smaller than the claim sounds. A 2009 meta-analysis of multilevel studies found a relative risk of mortality of 1.08 per 0.05 rise in the Gini coefficient across nine cohort studies ([Kondo et al. 2009, BMJ](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2776131/)). A later causal assessment with almost double the studies found a lower mortality risk of 1.02 (1.00 to 1.04) and notes that Kondo's authors had concluded the effect may be "modest" ([Milbank Quarterly](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1468-0009.12689)).

Individual studies disagree. A Norwegian register study with 58.5 million person-years found that increased income and wealth inequality was not associated with higher mortality risk ([PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10845829/)). A study of older residents of Sao Paulo found no dose-response relationship between Gini values and mortality ([PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3780697/)). Inequality correlates with trust and life expectancy across 33 countries (r = -0.51 and r = -0.74), which is cross-country correlation and not proof of cause ([PMC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2951926/)).

### Scope

The Spirit Level, Snowdon 2010, Marmot 2004 and Kawachi and Berkman 2000 are listed as further reading, not summarized here. The seed's claim that inequality harms people "near the top" was not checked against an opened source, and its example of a richer but more unequal society is not sourced.

**Plain words**
Many studies link larger income gaps with worse health, but the effect sizes are modest and some large studies find none.

**For example**
When you read that a country does well on health, ask how unequal it is as well as how rich.

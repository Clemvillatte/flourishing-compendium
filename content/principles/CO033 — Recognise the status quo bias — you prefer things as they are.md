> **Principle 33** — Recognise the status quo bias: you prefer things as they are

The 1988 paper opens with the observation that most real decisions, unlike those of economics texts, have a status quo alternative, doing nothing or maintaining one's current or previous decision, and its series of decision-making experiments demonstrated that individuals disproportionately stick with the status quo ([Samuelson & Zeckhauser 1988](https://rzeckhauser.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4441/files/rzeckhauser/files/status_quo_bias_in_decision_making.pdf)). The field data matters for the stakes: data on the selections of health and retirement programs by faculty members revealed that the status quo bias is substantial in important decisions ([Samuelson & Zeckhauser 1988](https://rzeckhauser.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4441/files/rzeckhauser/files/status_quo_bias_in_decision_making.pdf)).

> "Most real decisions, unlike those of economics texts, have a status quo alternative—that is, doing nothing or maintaining one's current or previous decision." (Samuelson & Zeckhauser 1988, [Journal of Risk and Uncertainty 1(1): 7-59](https://rzeckhauser.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum4441/files/rzeckhauser/files/status_quo_bias_in_decision_making.pdf))

**Plain words**
Ask whether you favour an option for its features or because you already have it. Try comparing the available options afresh, including any genuine costs of changing. Staying with the current option is not automatically a mistake.

*For example:* An imagined chooser compares two health plans without seeing which one they currently hold, then checks whether learning that detail changes their preference.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Status quo bias|Status quo bias]]**, people prefer the current state even when alternatives are better (Samuelson & Zeckhauser, 1988)
- **Defaults are powerful**, default options (organ donation, retirement savings) dramatically influence outcomes (Thaler & Sunstein, 2008)

## Practice

- When you notice yourself "sticking with" something by default, ask: "if I were choosing fresh today, would I choose this?" If no, the status quo is the reason, not the value.

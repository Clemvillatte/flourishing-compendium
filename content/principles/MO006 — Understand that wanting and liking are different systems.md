> **Principle 6** — Understand that wanting and liking are different systems

The motivation system (wanting, pursuit, dopamine) and the reward system (liking, pleasure, opioids) are dissociable. You can want something you will not enjoy (addiction, compulsive checking) and enjoy something you do not strongly want (a calm walk). This dissociation explains many motivational puzzles.

**Plain words** Wanting and liking are different machines — you can crave things you don't enjoy (scrolling, second drinks, slot machines). The pull isn't proof you'll like it; it's just the pursuit system doing its job. Knowing this breaks half your worst habits.

*For example:* The hand reaching for the phone with no anticipation of joy — you didn't even want it. The wanting was firing on its own. Naming that gap is half the escape.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[The wanting/liking dissociation|Wanting vs liking dissociation (Berridge & Robinson)]]**, dopamine mediates wanting; opioids mediate liking; the two can come apart (Berridge & Robinson, 2016; Berridge, 2009)
- **[[Reward prediction error (Schultz)|Reward prediction error (Schultz)]]**, dopamine responds to the difference between expected and received reward (Schultz et al., 1997; Schultz, 2016)

## Practice

- When you notice wanting something you will not actually enjoy (scrolling, sugar, compulsive behaviour), recognise this is the wanting system, not the liking system. Do not treat the urge as evidence of genuine value.

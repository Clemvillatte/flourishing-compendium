> **Principle 24** — Recognise affect's relationship to motivation; use it, do not obey it

Affect is a signal about value, not a command. Anxiety says "this matters"; sadness says "something was lost"; anger says "a boundary was crossed." But the signal is imperfect, and obeying it uncritically can lead to avoidance, withdrawal, or aggression. The adaptive move is to use the information without being ruled by it.

**Plain words** Affect is a signal about value, not a command. Anxiety says "this matters"; sadness says "something was lost"; anger says "a boundary was crossed." The signal is imperfect — use it, don't obey it. Read the message; don't let the message drive.

*For example:* The anxiety before the launch reads as "abort" if obeyed — but as "this matters, prepare harder" if read. Same signal, two relationships. The people who thrive are mostly the ones who read.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Functional accounts of emotion|Functional accounts of emotion]]**, each emotion carries adaptive information about the environment (Keltner & Gross, 1999)
- **Avoidance maintains anxiety; approach reduces fear over time**, avoidance is negatively reinforcing and maintains anxiety disorders (Craske et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Emotion as judgement (pathē)** (Stoics, Epictetus)
- **[[Right action|Right action over right feeling]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)
- **The passions as signals, not masters** (Hume, Treatise)

## Practice

- When you feel a strong emotion, extract the message ("this matters to me," "a boundary was crossed") and then decide whether the action it suggests is wise.

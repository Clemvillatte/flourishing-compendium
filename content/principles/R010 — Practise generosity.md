> **Principle 272** — Practise generosity

Tutu's description of the praised person puts giving among the marks of ubuntu: when someone is said to have it, "Then you are generous, you are hospitable, you are friendly and caring and compassionate. You share what you have." The sharing is not charity bolted onto a person; it is one of the signs by which a community recognizes a full one. Mbiti's formulation carries the identity underneath: whatever happens to the individual happens to the whole group, and whatever happens to the whole group happens to the individual, so the individual can only say, "I am, because we are; and since we are, therefore I am". What one has is held inside that bundle of life, which is why the giving is free rather than strategic.

> "Then you are generous, you are hospitable, you are friendly and caring and compassionate. You share what you have." (Tutu, No Future Without Forgiveness, 1999, p. 31, [excerpt](https://cepreaching.org/preaching-connections/reading/no-future-without-forgiveness-2/))

> "Whatever happens to the individual happens to the whole group, and whatever happens to the whole group happens to the individual. The individual can only say: 'I am, because we are; and since we are, therefore I am'." (Mbiti, African Religions and Philosophy, 1969, pp. 108-109, [as quoted in this paper](https://scholar.sun.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/2890e98d-77ab-4395-ac16-d4e294d1a8ac/content))

The compendium's application is the wealth reading in the principle: a person's plenty is measured at the point of distribution, so the one who has much and shares is honoured and the one who hoards is the embarrassment. The honour is not payment for the gift; it is the community saying where its life comes from.

**Plain words** Watch what a gift does to the giver's standing, not just the receiver's. The person who quietly covers a colleague's shortfall becomes a name people say when they explain who holds the community together; the person who never does stays comfortable and peripheral. Giving is how membership shows itself. Start where the principle starts: from what you already have, freely, without staging it.

*For example:* The person who quietly pays the deposit for a cousin's course, funds the church roof, covers a colleague's shortfall — and is the first name everyone says when they describe who holds this community together.

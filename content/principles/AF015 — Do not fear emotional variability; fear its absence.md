> **Principle 15** — Do not fear emotional variability; fear its absence

In a 2015 special section of Emotion Review on affect dynamics, Peter Kuppens, commenting on Tom Hollenstein's account of affective flexibility, writes that a central notion of that perspective is flexibility, which is proposed to characterize healthy emotional functioning — a proposal, not an established marker. ([Kuppens, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947)) Separately, two cross-sectional studies of emodiversity report that the variety and relative abundance of the emotions people experience is an independent predictor of mental and physical health over and above mean levels of positive and negative emotion — an association in cross-sectional data. ([Quoidbach et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038025)) The compendium's exercise is an observation without diagnostic interpretation: look back over a week and note whether your feelings varied when your situations did.

> "A central notion in his perspective is that of flexibility, which is proposed to characterize healthy emotional functioning." (Kuppens, P. (2015). It's About Time: A Special Section on Affect Dynamics. Emotion Review, 7(4), 297-300, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947))

**Plain words** The research behind this page is a proposal and an association: one researcher's perspective proposes that flexibility characterizes healthy emotional functioning ([Kuppens, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947)), and cross-sectional studies link greater emotional variety to better health ([Quoidbach et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038025)). The exercise: look back over a week and note whether your feelings changed when your situations did — an observation, not a diagnosis.

**For example (imagined):** A colleague is equally flat at a win and at a layoff. The exercise is to observe whether the feeling changed when the situation changed, and to leave open what the flatness means.
## Psychology parallels

- **Emotional variability is not uniformly maladaptive**, moderate variability associated with context sensitivity is adaptive; very low variability can indicate dissociation or depression (Hollenstein, 2015)
- **Emodiversity, the range and relative abundance of emotions experienced, predicts mental health**, beyond mean positive and negative affect (Quoidbach et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Harmony, not uniformity** (Confucius, Analects)
- **[[Ziran (naturalness)|Ziran (naturalness)]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 25)

## Practice

- Notice whether you feel differently in different situations. If you feel the same "flat bad" across contexts, this is worth investigating.

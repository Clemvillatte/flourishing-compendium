> **Principle 15** — Do not fear emotional variability; fear its absence

The ability to feel differently in response to different situations is a marker of health. Extreme emotional stability (flat affect, low variability) can indicate disconnection or dissociation. The goal is not to eliminate variability but to ensure it is responsive to context.

**Plain words** Feeling differently in response to different situations is a marker of health. Extreme stability — flat affect, low variability — can indicate disconnection or dissociation. Don't fear emotional variability; fear its absence. The goal is responsive, not eliminated.

*For example:* The "unshakeable" colleague who is equally calm at the win and the layoff — and the realisation that the unshakeability isn't mastery, it's distance. The person who reacts appropriately to both is the one who's actually present.
## Psychology parallels

- **Emotional variability is not uniformly maladaptive**, moderate variability associated with context sensitivity is adaptive; very low variability can indicate dissociation or depression (Hollenstein, 2015)
- **Emodiversity, the range and relative abundance of emotions experienced, predicts mental health**, beyond mean positive and negative affect (Quoidbach et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Harmony, not uniformity** (Confucius, Analects)
- **[[Ziran (naturalness)|Ziran (naturalness)]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 25)

## Practice

- Notice whether you feel differently in different situations. If you feel the same "flat bad" across contexts, this is worth investigating.

> **Principle 15** — Do not fear emotional variability; fear its absence

Writing in a 2015 Emotion Review special section on affect dynamics, Peter Kuppens presents the proposal, drawn from Tom Hollenstein's account of affective flexibility, that flexibility may characterize healthy emotional functioning. ([Kuppens, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947)) Separately, two cross-sectional studies report that emodiversity — the variety and relative abundance of the emotions people experience — is associated with mental and physical health over and above mean levels of positive and negative emotion. ([Quoidbach et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038025))

> "A central notion in his perspective is that of flexibility, which is proposed to characterize healthy emotional functioning." (Kuppens, P. (2015). It's About Time: A Special Section on Affect Dynamics. Emotion Review, 7(4), 297-300, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947))

**Plain words** The sources behind this page are one researcher's proposal ([Kuppens, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947)) and a cross-sectional association ([Quoidbach et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038025)). For the proposed diary, choose two moments from the week and record the situation and the feeling you remember. Do not use those entries to classify yourself as healthy or unwell.

**For example (imagined):** A colleague is equally flat at a win and at a layoff. The exercise is to observe whether the feeling changed when the situation changed, and to leave open what the flatness means.
## Psychology parallels

- **Emotional variability is not uniformly maladaptive**, moderate variability associated with context sensitivity is adaptive; very low variability can indicate dissociation or depression (Hollenstein, 2015)
- **Emodiversity, the range and relative abundance of emotions experienced, predicts mental health**, beyond mean positive and negative affect (Quoidbach et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Harmony, not uniformity** (Confucius, Analects)
- **[[Ziran (naturalness)|Ziran (naturalness)]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 25)

## Practice

- Notice whether you feel differently in different situations. If you feel the same "flat bad" across contexts, this is worth investigating.

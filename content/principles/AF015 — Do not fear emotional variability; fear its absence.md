> **Principle 15** — Do not fear emotional variability; fear its absence

In a 2015 special section of Emotion Review on affect dynamics, Tom Hollenstein argued that affective flexibility — the transitions into and out of affective states — characterizes healthy emotional functioning, a characterization his commentator Peter Kuppens drew out explicitly. ([Kuppens, 2015, Emotion Review 7, 297-300](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947)) Related work on emodiversity reports that the variety and relative abundance of the emotions a person experiences predicts mental and physical health over and above mean levels of positive and negative emotion. ([Quoidbach et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038025)) The compendium's application is to notice whether your feelings vary with your situations, rather than treating sameness of mood as steadiness.

> "A central notion in his perspective is that of flexibility, which is proposed to characterize healthy emotional functioning." (Kuppens, P. (2015). It's About Time: A Special Section on Affect Dynamics. Emotion Review, 7(4), 297-300, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1754073915590947))

**Plain words** Feeling different things in different situations looks, in this research, like a sign of a working system rather than a problem to fix. The exercise here is an audit: look back over a week and check whether your feelings changed when your situations did.

**For example (imagined):** A colleague is equally flat at a win and at a layoff. Instead of reading the flatness as calm, they treat it as the audit's starting point and ask whether the feeling changed when the situation changed.
## Psychology parallels

- **Emotional variability is not uniformly maladaptive**, moderate variability associated with context sensitivity is adaptive; very low variability can indicate dissociation or depression (Hollenstein, 2015)
- **Emodiversity, the range and relative abundance of emotions experienced, predicts mental health**, beyond mean positive and negative affect (Quoidbach et al., 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Harmony, not uniformity** (Confucius, Analects)
- **[[Ziran (naturalness)|Ziran (naturalness)]]** (Daoism, Daodejing 25)

## Practice

- Notice whether you feel differently in different situations. If you feel the same "flat bad" across contexts, this is worth investigating.

> **Principle 64** — Engage in spiritual/contemplative behaviour: practices of depth

Goyal and colleagues reviewed 41 trials (2,993 participants) of meditation programs compared with active controls. Mindfulness programs had "moderate" strength of evidence for improving anxiety (effect size 0.40 at 8 weeks), depression (0.32 at 8 weeks) and pain (0.33), and "low" strength of evidence for stress or distress and mental health-related quality of life. For positive mood, attention, substance use, eating, sleep and weight they found "either low SOE of no effect or insufficient SOE of an effect". They "did not find any evidence to suggest that these meditation programs were superior to any specific therapies they were compared with", and only 10 trials had a low risk of bias (Goyal et al. 2014, [AHRQ review on PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24501780/)).

This is evidence about structured programs, mainly mindfulness-based. It says little about prayer, silence or retreats, and the title's "depth" is not something these trials measured.

**Plain words**
Regular meditation practice helps with anxiety, low mood and pain by a modest amount, and about as well as other treatments it was compared with. The evidence is thinner for stress, mood and attention, and it is not a substitute for care if you are unwell. Whether silence and retreat bring depth is a personal question that these trials do not answer.

*For example:* You sit for ten minutes each morning with a mindfulness recording and note, once a week, how anxious you felt. This is an imagined routine in the style of the programs Goyal reviewed, which were typically longer structured courses.

## Psychology parallels

- **Meditation programs and stress-related outcomes**, moderate evidence for anxiety, depression and pain, low for stress and quality of life, and no evidence of superiority over other treatments (Goyal et al., 2014)
- **Mindfulness-based interventions**, MBSR and MBCT (Kabat-Zinn, 1982; Segal et al., 2002)

## Practice

- Engage in contemplative behaviour: daily meditation or contemplative practice, periodic silence or retreat. These practices build regulation, awareness, and equanimity.

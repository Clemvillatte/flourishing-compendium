> **Principle 64** — Engage in spiritual/contemplative behaviour: practices of depth

Contemplative behaviour — meditation, prayer, contemplation, silence, retreat — is associated with reduced stress, improved emotion regulation, and increased well-being. It is a specific behavioural practice.

**Plain words** Contemplative behaviour — silence, retreat, prayer, long walks without input — is a practice of depth, and it correlates with calm, meaning, and groundedness. Not escapism; the opposite. Depth is trained in the quiet.

*For example:* The one weekend a year with no phone, no plans, no noise — terrifying to book, nourishing to live. The silence wasn't empty; it was where the insight was waiting.
## Psychology parallels

- **Meditation and well-being**, contemplative practice reduces stress and improves regulation (Tang et al., 2015; Goleman & Davidson, 2017)
- **Mindfulness-based interventions**, MBSR and MBCT improve outcomes (Kabat-Zinn, 1982; Segal et al., 2002)

## Practice

- Engage in contemplative behaviour: daily meditation or contemplative practice, periodic silence or retreat. These practices build regulation, awareness, and equanimity.

> **Principle 120** — Make activities autotelic: worth doing for their own sake

The concept Csikszentmihalyi builds the book around has an old pedigree. Describing the Taoist ideal of Yu in the Chuang Tzu, he writes:

> "Chuang Tzu believed that to Yu was the proper way to live—without concern for external rewards, spontaneously, with total commitment—in short, as a total autotelic experience." (Csikszentmihalyi, Flow, p. 150, [archive.org text](https://ia801906.us.archive.org/4/items/Csikszenthmihalyi1990/Csikszenthmihalyi-1990_text.pdf))

His own examples are workers. Serafina the weaver, Joe the welder and Ting the cook run through the "Work as Flow" chapter as people who turned constrained jobs into flow:

> "Serafina, Joe, and Ting are examples of people who have developed an autotelic personality. Despite the severe limitations of their environment they were able to change constraints into opportunities for expressing their freedom and creativity." (Csikszentmihalyi, Flow, "Work as Flow," p. 152, [archive.org text](https://ia801906.us.archive.org/4/items/Csikszenthmihalyi1990/Csikszenthmihalyi-1990_text.pdf))

The chapter records what the transformation feels like from inside: "Thus transformed, work becomes enjoyable, and as the result of a personal investment of psychic energy, it feels as if it were freely chosen, as well." (Csikszentmihalyi, Flow, "Work as Flow," p. 152, [archive.org text](https://ia801906.us.archive.org/4/items/Csikszenthmihalyi1990/Csikszenthmihalyi-1990_text.pdf))

The compendium's exercise: take one activity you currently do only for what it produces, and look for what Joe found in welding: a constraint that could be treated as an opportunity for expression. Then protect the activities that already carry their own reason.

**Plain words**
An autotelic activity is one you would keep doing if the reward stopped. The test is not enjoyment exactly; it is whether the doing itself is what holds you. The work has two halves: try to make one forced activity more like a chosen one, and defend the time you already spend on things worth doing for their own sake.

*For example:* A Saturday fixing a friend's bike, unpaid and unasked, is an activity that carries its own reason. A Saturday in a meeting that engages no skill of yours has its reason somewhere else entirely.

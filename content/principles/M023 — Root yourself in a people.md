> **Principle 23** — Root yourself in a people

Britannica describes the calpulli as a "corporate group" of households, translated by the Spaniards as barrio, that was "a physical and territorial unit as well as a socially organized one" and "a unit of land tenure". Its lands "were owned communally but were distributed among various households", and it was "ruled by a council of household heads presided over by a chief" ([Britannica, Pre-Columbian civilizations](https://www.britannica.com/topic/pre-Columbian-civilizations/Agriculture)). It also served "as a unit of taxation to the central government, as a unit of corvée labor, and as a military regiment" (same entry).

(The calpulli structure.)

### What is uncertain

The same article says "the structure of the calpulli is open to question". Some sources call it a kin group, but there is "no evidence ... of either exogamy or unilineal descent", and it shows internal ranking (same entry). So "local kin-neighborhood" is a fair shorthand but not a settled description. A World History Encyclopedia article describes the calpolli as "a collection of families connected either by blood or long association" with its own temple, where members performed religious rituals and festivals together ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/845/aztec-society/)).

These sources describe a hierarchical, taxed and conscripted institution, so the modern principle takes the idea of being tied to named neighbours without the rest of that system.

**Plain words**
You do better with a particular set of people who know you and depend on you, and whom you also depend on. The group can be as small as a few neighbours.

**For example**
Write the names of the five people who would notice within a week if you disappeared. If the list is short, choose one local group, such as a club, a roster or a neighbourhood committee, and attend its next meeting.

> **Principle 36** — Learn to build tolerance for discomfort: approach despite discomfort

The core behavioural skill is the capacity to act despite discomfort — to approach despite fear, engage despite fatigue, show up despite shame. This is not the absence of discomfort; it is action alongside it.

**Plain words** The master skill under all these techniques: act despite the discomfort. Fear present? Act anyway. No motivation? Act anyway. Not recklessness — just refusing to let a feeling hold veto power over a behaviour. Discomfort is a passenger, not the driver.

*For example:* Hands shaking, going in for the injection — the shaking doesn't stop the walking. That gap, between the feeling and the action, is where the whole skill lives.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Distress tolerance (DBT)|Distress tolerance (DBT)]]**, the capacity to act despite discomfort (Linehan, 1993; Leyro et al., 2010)
- **[[Acceptance|Acceptance and commitment (ACT)]]**, willingness to experience discomfort while acting toward values (Hayes et al., 2011)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Courage: action despite fear|Courage: action despite fear]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III)
- **[[Right effort|Right effort: action despite discomfort]]** (Buddhism)
- **[[Amor fati|Amor fati: embrace difficulty]]** (Nietzsche, The Gay Science)

## Practice

- Practise acting despite discomfort: when you feel fear, fatigue, shame, or discomfort, act anyway. Do not wait for comfort; act with the discomfort. This is the core behavioural skill.

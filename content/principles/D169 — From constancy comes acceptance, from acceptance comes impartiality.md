> **Principle 169** — From constancy comes acceptance, from acceptance comes impartiality

Chapter 16 of the Daodejing begins with a cosmology of return: all things alike go through their processes of activity, and then we see them return to their root. That returning to the root is the state of stillness, and stillness may be called a reporting that things have fulfilled their appointed end. The report of that fulfilment is the regular, unchanging rule; to know that rule is to be intelligent, and not to know it leads to wild movements and evil issues. The chapter then gives the chain this principle's title translates: the knowledge of the unchanging rule produces capacity and forbearance; capacity and forbearance lead to a community of feeling with all things; from that community of feeling comes a kingliness of character.

(DDJ 16.)

> "The knowledge of that unchanging rule produces a (grand) capacity and forbearance, and that capacity and forbearance lead to a community (of feeling with all things). From this community of feeling comes a kingliness of character; and he who is king-like goes on to be heaven-like." (DDJ 16, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

The chapter's chain is addressed to the person who would be king-like. The compendium's reading takes the chain's first links for personal practice: the constancy of the principle's title is the knowing of the unchanging rule, and the acceptance is the capacity and forbearance that knowledge produces. The principle's title calls the community of feeling "impartiality"; that equivalence is the compendium's interpretation of Legge's phrase, not a translation.

**Plain words**
The compendium's exercise: before judging someone, pause and try to describe the situation from their side. Ask what you might be missing, then return to your decision. The pause does not settle who is right.

*For example:* The compendium's hypothetical: a judge who pauses before responding to a lawyer's theater — the pause is the exercise, not a guarantee of the verdict.

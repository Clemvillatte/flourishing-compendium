> **Principle 184** — Recognize habit energy

Thich Nhat Hanh uses habit energy to describe familiar tendencies such as rushing toward a future in which one expects happiness. His practice is to recognise the tendency and meet it with mindfulness rather than fight it; he also acknowledges its persistence ([his essay](https://www.lionsroar.com/watering-the-seeds-of-happiness/)):

> Some habit energies are very difficult to transform.

(Thich Nhat Hanh, [How Mindfulness Nurtures Joy](https://www.lionsroar.com/watering-the-seeds-of-happiness/).)

Wood and Neal's psychological model describes habits through learned associations between responses and recurring features of a context. It cautions that such associations do not readily shift with a new goal or an occasional contrary action ([author abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ776723)). This is a separate explanatory model, not evidence that Buddhist habit energy is a measurable substance.

**Plain words** Notice the moment a familiar action starts, and ask what prompted it. If you want to do something else, decide on that next action. Noticing is worth recording even when the old response still follows.

*For example:* Imagine opening the news app whenever you close a work document. You notice the sequence and decide to stand up instead on the next occasion. You can check what happens without assuming that one interruption has erased the habit.

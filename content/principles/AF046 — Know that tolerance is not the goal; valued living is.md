> **Principle 46** — Know that tolerance is not the goal; valued living is

Acceptance and distress tolerance are means, not ends. The goal is not to tolerate suffering indefinitely; it is to build a life worth living even when suffering is present. Tolerance without direction becomes endurance without purpose.

**Plain words** Acceptance and tolerance are means, not ends — the goal is a life worth living even with suffering present. Tolerance without direction becomes endurance, and endurance hardens into despair. Always attach the tolerance to a valued direction.

*For example:* The chronic pain patient who can now tolerate the flares — and the crucial next question: tolerate them FOR what? The answer (the grandkids, the garden, the book) is what turns endurance back into living.
## Psychology parallels

- **Values-based action is the organising principle in ACT**, acceptance serves committed action toward valued ends (Hayes et al., 2011)
- **Valued living predicts well-being beyond affect**, engaging in valued activity is associated with meaning and life satisfaction (Wilson et al., 2010)

## Practice

- Ask: "what do I want my life to stand for, even when I feel bad?" Let this, not the absence of distress, be the criterion for success.

> **Principle 46** — Know that tolerance is not the goal; valued living is

In Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, the acceptance skills are explicitly not ends in themselves: they serve chosen values. ([Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda and Lillis, 2006](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf)) The compendium uses this to frame distress tolerance as a means directed at a life worth living, not as a goal in itself.

(Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda and Lillis, "Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: Model, processes and outcomes," Behaviour Research and Therapy 44(1), 2006; Wilson et al., 2010.)

> "Values are chosen qualities of purposive action that can never be obtained as an object but can be instantiated moment by moment." (Hayes et al., [Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: Model, processes and outcomes](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf), 2006)

The same paper is explicit about the hierarchy: "In ACT, acceptance, defusion, being present, and so on are not ends in themselves; rather they clear the path for a more vital, values consistent life." ([Hayes et al., 2006](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf)) And on action: "Finally, ACT encourages the development of larger and larger patterns of effective action linked to chosen values." ([Hayes et al., 2006](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf))

**Plain words**
In ACT, acceptance processes serve a values-consistent life rather than functioning as ends in themselves. ([Hayes et al., 2006](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf)) For this compendium's exercise, connect an instance of willingness to a chosen direction.

**Reflection exercise:** If discomfort is present, name a valued activity and one feasible step toward it.

## Psychology parallels

- **Values-based action is the organising principle in ACT**, acceptance serves committed action toward valued ends (Hayes et al., 2011)
- **Valued living predicts well-being beyond affect**, engaging in valued activity is associated with meaning and life satisfaction (Wilson et al., 2010)

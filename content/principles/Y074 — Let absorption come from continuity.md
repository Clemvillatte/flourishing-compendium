> **Principle 74** — Let absorption come from continuity

Sutra 3.2 defines the second stage, which Woods translates as contemplation (dhyana): "Focusedness of the presented idea upon that place is contemplation" (trans. Woods, p. 204). The commentary clarifies: "the stream [of presented ideas] of like quality unaffected by any other presented idea" (p. 204). What marks it is an unbroken stream of one kind of idea on the same object.

### How it relates to concentration

The three stages are linked as cause and effect: "fixed-attention and contemplation and concentration are in the relation of cause to effect, and the serial order [of causes and effects] is specified" (p. 203). Contemplation "is to be effected by fixed-attention" (p. 204). A quoted Purana verse describes it as "an uninterrupted succession of presented-ideas single-in-intent upon His form without desire for anything else", and adds that it "is brought about by the first six aids [to yoga]" (p. 204). The next stage, concentration (samadhi), is contemplation "shining forth [in consciousness] as the intended object and nothing more" (3.3, pp. 204-205).

### What the sources do not say

Nothing in this passage says that attention "stops returning and begins resting", that absorption follows after a number of returns, or that one should count returns. The commentary describes a sequence of causes, not a schedule. The practical advice not to chase absorption is the principle's own, and is consistent with a view of contemplation as an effect, not a direct target.

**Plain words** In the text, contemplation is the same attention as in sutra 3.1, held without interruption. It is produced by the earlier stage, so you work on the earlier stage. How long it takes is not stated, so counting returns is a modern device.

*For example:* Imagined example: you hold attention on a candle flame for ten minutes. At first you return to it often. Later the returns are fewer and the flame fills your attention. This is a modern description, not the sutra's.

(Yoga Sutras 3.1-3.3 with Vyasa's Bhasya and Vacaspati Misra's gloss, trans. James Haughton Woods, The Yoga-System of Patanjali (1914), pp. 203-205, Internet Archive, opened 2026-10-21.)

> **Principle 50** — Practise tolerance of uncertainty

Carleton describes intolerance of uncertainty as a "fear of the unknown", and reports that research "has clearly demonstrated that IU is a broad transdiagnostic dispositional risk factor for the development and maintenance of clinically significant anxiety" (Carleton, 2012, Expert Review of Neurotherapeutics, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23002938/)). Ladouceur and colleagues went further than correlation. Using a gambling procedure, they raised intolerance of uncertainty in 21 people and lowered it in another 21. Those whose intolerance was increased "showed a higher level of worry", which gives "some initial clarifications as to the causal nature of the link" (Ladouceur et al., 2000, Behaviour Research and Therapy, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10957827/)).

There is also evidence that the capacity can be trained. Dugas and Ladouceur treated four adults with a programme whose "goal is to help patients become more tolerant of uncertainty", and "treatment outcome was highly related to change in intolerance of uncertainty" (Dugas & Ladouceur, 2000, Behavior Modification, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11036732/)). In a trial of 65 adults with generalized anxiety disorder, therapy built on the intolerance-of-uncertainty model beat a wait-list at posttest, and "only CBT led to continued improvement" over follow-up (Dugas et al., 2010, Behavior Therapy, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20171327/)). Those programmes teach more than waiting, so I did not find evidence for the specific drill of "staying a little longer each time".

**Plain words**
The urge to settle questions fast can feed worry. People who can't stand not knowing tend to worry more, and learning to tolerate it appears to ease worry. If worry dominates your days, ask a clinician.

**For example**
You are waiting on test results and notice the urge to search symptoms again. You write the question down, do something else, and leave the answer until the appointment.

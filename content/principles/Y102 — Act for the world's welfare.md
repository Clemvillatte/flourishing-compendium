> **Principle 102** — Act for the world's welfare

The Gita's social mandate: as the ignorant act with attachment, the wise act for the holding together of the world (lokasangraha); the exemplar king attained perfection by action, not withdrawal. The tradition explicitly forbids using contemplative ideals to abandon necessary work. Practically, hold one role in your life as service-in-form (a committee, a shift, a mentoring hour) and keep it when contemplation tempts you to simplify everything away.

(BG 3.20, 3.25.)

**Plain words** As the ignorant act with attachment, the wise act for the holding-together of the world — lokasangraha. The tradition explicitly forbids using contemplative ideals to abandon necessary work. Withdrawal is not the advanced move; participation is.

*For example:* The person who uses "non-attachment" to justify checking out of the community's problems — versus the one whose practice shows up as the opposite: more present, more useful, better company. The tradition's exemplar king attained perfection by action, not withdrawal.

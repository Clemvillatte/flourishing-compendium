> **Principle 35** — Practise social perspective-taking

When you assume you know what someone thinks or feels, ask instead. Perspective-getting, the practice of asking rather than guessing, consistently outperforms perspective-taking (guessing) for accuracy. The practice builds social cognition, reduces conflict, and improves relationship quality.

(Epley et al., 2004; Kruger et al., 2005.)

**Plain words** Don't assume you know what someone thinks or feels. Ask. Perspective-getting, asking, beats perspective-taking, guessing, for accuracy. It builds social understanding, cuts conflict and improves relationships.

*For example:* Instead of guessing why your teenager is quiet, you say "I noticed you're quiet. What's going on?"

> **Principle 16** — Distinguish realistic from unrealistic optimism

Optimism, when grounded in evidence and calibrated to what one can control, predicts persistence, health, and well-being. Optimism that is unrealistic, that denies real risks or ignores genuine constraints, predicts poor decisions and worse outcomes. The distinction is between optimism as a realistic assessment that things can improve with effort, and optimism as a denial that anything is wrong.

(Scheier and Carver, 1985; Schwarzer, 1994;Armor and Taylor, 2002.)

**Plain words** Optimism that's grounded in evidence and aimed at what you can influence predicts persistence, health and well-being. Optimism that denies risks or ignores real limits leads to worse decisions. The difference is between "things can improve with effort" and "it'll be fine whatever I do."

*For example:* A founder who says "we can fix the cash problem if we change the pricing" is realistic. One who ignores the bank statement is not.

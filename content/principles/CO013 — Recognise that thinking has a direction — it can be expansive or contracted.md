> **Principle 13** — Recognise that thinking has a direction: it can be expansive or contracted

Under threat or stress, thinking narrows (focus on the threat, reduce options). Under safety and positive affect, thinking broadens (more options, more creativity). This is not a flaw; it is an adaptive trade-off. But chronic stress locks thinking into a contracted mode.

**Plain words** Under threat or stress, thinking narrows — focus on the threat, options shrink. Under safety and positive affect, thinking broadens — more options, more creativity. Not a flaw; an architecture. Know which mode you're in before deciding.

*For example:* The person who makes all their big decisions in crisis mode — narrow, fast, options contracted — versus the one who schedules decisions for calm mornings when the full menu is visible. Same brain; different apertures. The aperture is the variable.
## Psychology parallels

- **Stress narrows cognition**, acute stress reduces [[Cognitive flexibility|cognitive flexibility]] and working memory (Arnsten, 2009; Diamond, 2013)
- **Positive affect broadens cognition**, positive emotions expand thought-action repertoires (Fredrickson, 2001; Fredrickson & Branigan, 2005)

## Practice

- When you notice your thinking has become rigid or narrow (only seeing one option, only seeing problems), this is a stress response, not a truth about the world. Regulate the stress first; the thinking will expand.

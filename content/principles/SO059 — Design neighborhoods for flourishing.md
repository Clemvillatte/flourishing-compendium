> **Principle 59** — Design neighborhoods for flourishing

The strongest evidence here concerns safety and social cohesion, with a caution about cause. In Chicago, a survey of 8,782 residents in 343 neighborhoods found that collective efficacy, "social cohesion among neighbors combined with their willingness to intervene on behalf of the common good", was "negatively associated with variations in violence", and that it largely mediated the links of concentrated disadvantage and residential instability with violence ([Sampson, Raudenbush and Earls 1997, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9252316/)). That is an observational study.

A randomised trial supports changing the physical environment. In Philadelphia, 541 vacant lots were randomly assigned to treatment or control. Residents near restored lots reported "significantly increased use of outside spaces for relaxing and socializing (75.7%)", and in neighborhoods below the poverty line gun violence fell by 29.1% ([Branas et al. 2018, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.1718503115)). The reduction across all neighborhoods for gun assaults was smaller (2.7%) in that paper.

On health, Diez Roux and Mair summarise that neighbourhood features "may affect health and contribute to social and race/ethnic inequalities in health", while noting key "conceptual and methodological challenges" ([Diez Roux and Mair 2010](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Neighborhoods-and-health/991020099638704721)). A methods paper argues that causal inference about neighbourhood effects is hard ([Social Science & Medicine](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0277953603004131)).



### Scope

I did not verify the claims about walkability and physical activity, or the Hartig and White nature papers. The sources fit "neighbourhood design can matter", not "your postcode does more than your GP".

**Plain words**
Where you live shapes safety, contact and use of outdoor space. Some of that you can change by joining with neighbours.

**For example**
Pick one shared space on your street or block that people avoid. Ask three neighbours what would make them use it, then take the idea to the council or a local group.

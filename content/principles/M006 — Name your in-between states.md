> **Principle 6** — Name your in-between states

Nepantla is a Nahuatl word for "in the middle". It appears in sixteenth-century dictionaries by Andrés de Olmos (1547) and Alonso de Molina (1571), where it means en medio, "in the middle". The fuller colonial gloss comes from the Dominican Diego Durán (1581). A native convert told him "Padre, no te espantes pues todavía estamos nepantla" ("Father, don't be afraid since we are still nepantla"), and Durán read it as being "the place of nothing (to be neither on one side nor the other) and the place of everything (to be in two incompatible places at the same time)" ([Villaseñor Black, UCLA](https://www.chicano.ucla.edu/files/s7.pdf)).

The modern reading of nepantla as a working concept is Gloria Anzaldúa's:

> Bridges span liminal (threshold) spaces between worlds, spaces I call Nepantla, a Nahuatl word meaning tierra entre medio. Transformations occur in this in-between space, an unstable, unpredictable, precarious always-in-transition space lacking boundaries. ([Anzaldúa and Keating 2002, p. 1, as quoted in a journal article](https://ojs.tnkul.pl/index.php/rh/article/download/6657/6449/))

Anzaldúa also says it is "very awkward, uncomfortable and frustrating to be in that Nepantla because you are in the midst of transformation" ([Heidelberg HeLix, 2025](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/helix/article/download/112141/107933/286299)). So the colonial record shows a painful position, and Anzaldúa builds a method on it. My practical step is its own: when you are between jobs, homes or selves, say aloud that this is nepantla.

(Durán 1581, via Villaseñor Black; Anzaldúa and Keating, eds., this bridge we call home, 2002, p. 1.)

### Plain words

Being between things is uncomfortable, and a name for it helps. Use "nepantla" for the stretch where the old life has ended and the new one has not started. Naming does not make it comfortable. It lets you say where you are.

### For example

Someone has left a job but not yet started another. They say "I am in nepantla" instead of "I am failing at moving on", and use the stretch to do the things only a gap allows.

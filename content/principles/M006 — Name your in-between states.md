> **Principle 6** — Name your in-between states

Nepantla is the Nahuatl term for the condition of being in the middle: between worlds, between identities, between an old life that has ended and a new one not yet formed. The tradition treats it as a real place with its own rules, not as a failure to have arrived. The modern bicultural and migrant experience (Anzaldua's Borderlands, labeled as the modern development) has made nepantla a standing condition of millions, but the principle is ancient: the middle is where transitions happen, and it has a name so it can be worked with. Practically, when you are between things (jobs, homes, selves), name the nepantla aloud; naming converts disorientation into location.

(The Nahua nepantla concept; Anzaldua, Borderlands/La Frontera, as the labeled modern development.)

**Plain words** Nepantla: the in-between — between worlds, identities, an old life ended and a new one not yet formed. It's a real place with its own rules, not a failure to have arrived. The tradition treats the middle as terrain, not error.

*For example:* The recent graduate, the new migrant, the divorcée — all in nepantla, all feeling like they're failing at a race. The tradition's verdict: you're not late; you're in a real place. It has rules. Learn them.

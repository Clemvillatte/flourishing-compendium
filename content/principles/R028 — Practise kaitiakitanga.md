> **Principle 290** — Practise kaitiakitanga

New Zealand law gives the term a statutory definition. The Resource Management Act 1991 defines kaitiakitanga as "the exercise of guardianship by the tangata whenua of an area in accordance with tikanga Māori in relation to natural and physical resources; and includes the ethic of stewardship" (RMA 1991, s 2, as quoted in [New Zealand Treasury, 2018](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2018-09/dp18-11.pdf)). Two things are worth noticing. First, guardianship is exercised by tangata whenua, the people of the area, in accordance with tikanga. Second, the Act adds the ethic of stewardship as an included meaning, and scholars warn against collapsing the two: Kawharu writes that "Māori interpretations of kaitiakitanga as guardianship is perhaps a response to the Crown's need to understand more fully what kaitiakitanga means in terms intelligible to the Crown" (Kawharu, 2000, as quoted in New Zealand Treasury, 2018).

The encyclopaedia account grounds the same idea in worldview rather than statute: "Traditionally, Māori believe there is a deep kinship between humans and the natural world. This connection is expressed through kaitiakitanga – a way of managing the environment" (Te Ahukaramū Charles Royal, Te Ara; page content via search index, direct fetch blocked). Its account of the practice is concrete: temporary bans (rāhui) on taking food from an area, using the lunar calendar (maramataka) to decide when to plant and harvest, and taking only what was needed. Mauri, the life force, "must be protected in forests, rivers, gardens, lakes and the sea".

(Resource Management Act 1991; Te Ara; Kawharu, 2000, as discussed in New Zealand Treasury, 2018.)

**Plain words** The word names a job, not a mood. A kaitiaki is a guardian appointed by the people of a place, and the work is practical: bans when a fishery needs to recover, calendars that keep harvests in rhythm, taking only what is needed. The law translates this as guardianship plus stewardship; the scholarly caution is that the translation is for the Crown's benefit, and the original obligation runs from kinship.

*For example:* Imagine the family that clears the invasive weed from the creek bank each season, with no sign thanking them and nobody paying them.

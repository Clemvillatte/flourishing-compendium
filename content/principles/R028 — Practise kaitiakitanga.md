> **Principle 290** — Practise kaitiakitanga

New Zealand law gives the term a statutory definition. The Resource Management Act 1991 defines kaitiakitanga as "the exercise of guardianship by the tangata whenua of an area in accordance with tikanga Māori in relation to natural and physical resources; and includes the ethic of stewardship" (RMA 1991, s 2, as quoted in [New Zealand Treasury, 2018](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2018-09/dp18-11.pdf)). Two things are worth noticing. First, guardianship is exercised by tangata whenua, the people of the area, in accordance with tikanga. Second, the Act adds the ethic of stewardship as an included meaning, and scholars warn against collapsing the two: Kawharu writes that "Māori interpretations of kaitiakitanga as guardianship is perhaps a response to the Crown's need to understand more fully what kaitiakitanga means in terms intelligible to the Crown" (Kawharu, 2000, as quoted in [New Zealand Treasury, He Ara Waiora](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2018-09/dp18-11.pdf)).

The Treasury discussion of the term adds its context: kaitiakitanga is "most often used in relation to the environment", has been defined as "guardianship, stewardship, trusteeship, trustee" and "stewardship and protection, often used in relation to natural resources", and is "primarily... associated with the environment and sustainability and is taken to mean 'guardianship'" ([New Zealand Treasury, He Ara Waiora](https://www.treasury.govt.nz/sites/default/files/2018-09/dp18-11.pdf)).

(Resource Management Act 1991; Kawharu, 2000, as discussed in New Zealand Treasury, He Ara Waiora.)

**Plain words** The word names a job rather than a mood. The statutory definition has guardianship exercised by the tangata whenua of an area, in accordance with tikanga, and includes the ethic of stewardship. The scholarly caution is that rendering it as guardianship is perhaps a response to the Crown's need for terms it can understand; the definition does not settle what the obligation amounts to from inside.

*For example:* Imagine the family that clears the invasive weed from the creek bank each season, with no sign thanking them and nobody paying them.

> **Principle 69** — Recognise ADHD: interest-based attention, not a deficit

ADHD involves a distinct pattern of attention: it is driven by interest, novelty, and urgency rather than by importance or consequence. This is not a failure of will; it is a different attentional architecture. Understanding it changes how you design attentional life.

**Plain words** ADHD isn't a broken attention system — it's an interest-driven one: driven by interest, novelty, and urgency rather than importance. The strategies that work build the missing spark in, instead of shaming the engine for its design.

*For example:* The ADHDer who can't start the boring report for days, then finishes it in one hyperfocused hour when a deadline lights the fuse. The engine's fine — it runs on different fuel.
## Psychology parallels

- **ADHD as interest-based attention**, attention is captured by interest and novelty rather than directed by importance (Brown, 2013; Barkley, 1997)
- **ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition with evidence-based treatments**, medication and behavioural strategies are effective (Faraone et al., 2021; Brown, 2013)
- **Note: ADHD is not simply "poor attention"**, it involves interest-based regulation, time blindness, and executive function differences (Brown, 2013; Barkley, 1997)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Different, not less** (Grandin, Thinking in Pictures)
- **[[Neurodiversity|Neurodiversity as variation]]** (Singer, 1998)
- **The tree that is not judged by the forest** (Zhuangzi)

## Practice

- If you have ADHD, do not judge your attention by neurotypical standards. Design around interest: make important tasks interesting (novelty, challenge, urgency), use external structure (timers, accountability, visual cues), and consider professional evaluation for treatment.

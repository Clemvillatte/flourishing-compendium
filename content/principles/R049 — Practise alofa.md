> **Principle 311** — Practise alofa

Pratt's Samoan dictionary gives alofa as "love, compassion", and as a second noun sense "a present, a gift" ([Pratt, A Grammar and Dictionary of the Samoan Language](https://dokumen.pub/a-grammar-and-dictionary-of-the-samoan-language.html)). The word covers both feeling and giving. Oranga Tamariki describes alofa as the "Samoan heart", covering "love/compassion, reciprocity, respect, humility and dignity", and says tautua (service) "stems from a philosophy of alofa/love, responsibility, hospitality, caring, and dignity" ([Oranga Tamariki](https://practice.orangatamariki.govt.nz/practice-framework/whai-oranga/vaaifetu/tautua-samoan-cultural-approach)).

The practice side is stated plainly in a New Zealand government speech for Samoan Language Week: alofa "must be manifested and reciprocated", either through formal gifting or as "Alofa Fa'atino", the manifestation of alofa through something tangible ([Beehive](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/speech/launch-samoan-language-week-fale-o-samoa-mangere)). Drawing on Tui Atua (2018), one article says alofa and reciprocity "keep Pasifika culture alive" and that disharmony is addressed by bringing alofa, remorse and forgiveness forward ([PESA Agora](https://pesaagora.com/access-vol-41-2/mobilising-tofa-saili-for-ece-leadership/)). Since alofa is reciprocal, it comes with obligations as well as warmth. The seed's "not merely a feeling" fits these sources.

Lynn Underwood, whose framework the psychology bullet cites, defines compassionate love as "attitudes and actions related to giving of self for the good of the other", and says it is "a self-giving, caring love" ([Underwood chapter](https://www.lynnunderwood.com/wp-content/uploads/cleancompassionatelovebookchapterblackwell.pdf)). Sprecher and Fehr (2005) define it as an attitude containing "feelings, cognitions, and behaviors" of caring and concern, particularly toward people who are suffering or in need ([Sprecher and Fehr](https://ggsc.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Sprecher_Fehr_2005_Compassionate_love_for_close_others_and_humanity.pdf)). This is a close fit for the action side of alofa, but it is a Western research construct and not a translation. The seed cites Tuhiwai Smith (1999), which I did not open. It is a book on Māori research, not a Samoan source.

**Plain words** Alofa is the Samoan word for love, compassion and gift, and the sources stress that it is shown by acts and returned in kind. Psychology's compassionate love is similar, but not the same thing.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. An aunt keeps the freezer stocked for whoever turns up and drives someone to the airport at 5 a.m. Nobody asks whether she loves them.

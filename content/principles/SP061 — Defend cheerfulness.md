> **Principle 61** — Defend cheerfulness

Mirth, Spinoza holds, cannot be excessive and is always good; melancholy is always bad. Mirth (hilaritas) is pleasure in which all parts of the body are affected equally, so it increases power in proportion; it is the whole-body joy the tradition calls cheerfulness. The person living by reason, he says, is most conscious of themselves, of God, and of things, and conscious with joy. Practically, protect your baseline joys (movement, light, friendship, craft) as infrastructure, and audit pleasures by whether they leave more power than they found.

(Ethics IV, P42; V, P42 scholium's portrait of the wise person.)

**Plain words** Mirth cannot be excessive and is always good; melancholy is always bad. Cheerfulness (hilaritas) is pleasure in which all parts of the body are affected equally — the whole-body joy. Defend it. The tradition treats the good mood as an achievement of the system, not a lucky mood.

*For example:* The genuine laughter that ends with the whole body relaxed — versus the stimulation hit that fixes one part and numbs the rest. The first kind increases power in proportion; it can't be overdone. Protect the sources of the first kind.

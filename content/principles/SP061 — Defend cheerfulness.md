> **Principle 61** — Defend cheerfulness

> Mirth cannot be excessive, but is always good; contrariwise, Melancholy is always bad.

(Spinoza, Ethics, Part IV, Proposition 42, trans. R. H. M. Elwes, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800). Elwes renders hilaritas as "Mirth"; the entry's "cheerfulness" follows another translation, which was not opened.)

The proof defines mirth as pleasure that, "in so far as it is referred to the body, consists in all parts of the body being affected equally," so that the body's power of activity is increased "in such a manner, that the several parts maintain their former proportion of motion and rest." Melancholy is the opposite: pain that consists in "the absolute decrease or hindrance of the body's power of activity."

The next proposition supplies the contrast the entry uses: "Stimulation may be excessive and bad." Spinoza defines this "localized pleasure or stimulation (titillatio)" as pleasure in "one or some of its parts being affected more than the rest," which can become "obstinately fixed" and leave the body unable to be affected in other ways (IV, P43).

The closing portrait of the wise person is in Part V, Proposition 42, Note: the wise man "being conscious of himself, and of God, and of things, by a certain eternal necessity, never ceases to be, but always possesses true acquiescence of his spirit." The passage says "acquiescence," not joy, so the entry's "conscious with joy" is its paraphrase. The advice to protect movement, light, friendship and craft is the entry's own. The Fredrickson, Nietzsche and Buddhist parallels were not opened.

**Plain words** Spinoza counts the kind of cheerfulness that touches the whole person as always good, and he counts sadness as always bad, because it lowers your power to act. A strong hit of pleasure in one spot is different: it can take over. So the aim is steady, whole-body gladness and not the loudest pleasure on offer.

*For example:* Laughter that leaves the whole body loose is one thing. A scrolling habit that fixes attention on one small pleasure and numbs the rest is another.

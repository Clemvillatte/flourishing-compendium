> **Principle 159** — Treat your body as a loan, not a possession

In Zhuangzi 22, Shun asks whether he can obtain the Dao and hold it as his own. His attendant Cheng challenges the question by saying that even Shun's body is not his to possess. When Shun asks who holds it, Cheng replies ([Legge](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/knowledge-rambling-in-the-north)):

> It is the bodily form entrusted to you by Heaven and Earth.

(Zhuangzi 22, paragraph 4, [Legge](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/knowledge-rambling-in-the-north).)

Cheng extends this account of entrustment to life, one's nature and one's descendants ([Legge](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/knowledge-rambling-in-the-north)). The compendium takes the body image as a reason for care without an expectation of complete control. That is an application of the dialogue, not a health claim or an instruction to neglect the body.

**Plain words** Ask what looking after your body requires today, without making the answer depend on keeping it unchanged forever. Care can still matter when it offers no guarantee.

*For example:* Imagine feeling disappointed by how your appearance has changed with age. You decide to spend the evening preparing a meal rather than comparing old photographs. The point is the choice to care, not a promised change in how you feel.

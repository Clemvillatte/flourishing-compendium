> **Principle 181** — Stay with boredom

Thich Nhat Hanh describes the Lazy Day as training in not being afraid of doing nothing ([Happiness, 'Lazy Day' section, Parallax Press](https://www.parallax.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Happiness_2020FreeDownload.pdf)):

> When we do not have something to do, we get bored and seek for something to do or for entertainment. We are very afraid of being there and doing nothing. The Lazy Day has been prescribed for us to train ourselves not to be afraid of doing nothing.

(Thich Nhat Hanh, Happiness, 'Lazy Day' section, p. 104, [Parallax Press PDF](https://www.parallax.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Happiness_2020FreeDownload.pdf).)

He continues that it is only when we get bored and become aware that we are seeking entertainment to hide the feelings of loneliness and worthlessness in ourselves that the tension, the depression, the stress begin to dissolve. That is his account of the training, offered for readers to test; it is not a finding about boredom in general.

**Plain words** Before choosing something to fill an idle moment, ask what you hope it will do for you. You might notice an uncomfortable feeling, or simply a wish for something interesting to do.

*For example:* Imagine noticing your hand reach for the phone in a free moment and asking what you want from it. The answer might be a wish for something interesting, or a discomfort with the quiet. Either answer tells you what you are choosing between, and the choice stays yours.

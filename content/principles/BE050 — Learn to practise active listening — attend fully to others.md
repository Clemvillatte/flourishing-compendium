> **Principle 50** — Learn to practise active listening: attend fully to others

Active listening involves attending fully to what another person is saying — listening without planning your response, reflecting back, and asking clarifying questions. It is a specific social behaviour.

**Plain words** Active listening means actually receiving — not planning your reply while they talk. Reflect back what you heard, ask one real question. It's rare enough that doing it makes you memorable. Most "conversations" are two people waiting.

*For example:* "So the worst part was that she didn't even call" — and the listener says that sentence back instead of jumping to their own story. That's the whole skill. It changes what the speaker says next.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Active listening|Active listening (Rogers)]]**, empathic listening improves relationships and outcomes (Rogers, 1951; Rogers & Farson, 1957)
- **Perceived responsiveness**, feeling heard and understood predicts relationship satisfaction (Reis et al., 2017; Gable et al., 2004)

## Practice

- Practise active listening: when someone is speaking, listen without planning your response. Reflect back ("what I hear you saying is..."). Ask questions. Give full attention.

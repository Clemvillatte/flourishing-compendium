> **Principle 50** — Learn to practise active listening: attend fully to others

The term comes from Carl Rogers and Richard Farson's 1957 training pamphlet, and their claim for it was strong:

> "Active listening is an important way to bring about changes in people. Despite the popular notion that listening is a passive approach, clinical and research evidence clearly shows that sensitive listening is a most effective agent for individual personality change and group development." (Rogers & Farson, "Active Listening", University of Chicago Industrial Relations Center, 1957, [PDF reprint](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Rogers_Farson_Active-Listening.pdf))

They described what being listened to does to the person heard:

> "People who have been listened to in this new and special way become more emotionally mature, more open to their experiences, less defensive, more democratic, and less authoritarian." (Rogers & Farson, 1957, [PDF reprint](https://wholebeinginstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/Rogers_Farson_Active-Listening.pdf))

A modern experiment tested the paraphrase-reflect-ask package against advice-giving and simple acknowledgment, with 115 participants paired with trained confederates:

> "Results indicate that participants who received active listening responses felt more understood than participants who received either advice or simple acknowledgements." (Weger, Castle Bell, Minei & Robinson, "The Relative Effectiveness of Active Listening in Initial Interactions", International Journal of Listening 28(1), 13-31, 2014, abstract, [PDF](https://rachel.core2learn.org/modules/en-empatico/content/activities/friendship_bingo/files/the_relative_effectiveness_of_active_listening_in_initial_interactions.pdf))

The same abstract records that the effect sizes for the satisfaction and social-attractiveness differences were small ([Weger et al., 2014, PDF](https://rachel.core2learn.org/modules/en-empatico/content/activities/friendship_bingo/files/the_relative_effectiveness_of_active_listening_in_initial_interactions.pdf)), so the honest summary is: the practice works, modestly, on the feeling of being understood.

**Plain words**
Listening is a skill with a method. Do not rehearse your reply while they talk. Say back the sentence that mattered. Ask one real question. The experiment says speakers feel more understood when this happens; Rogers believed it changed people.

**For example**
"So the worst part was that she didn't even call" - and the listener says that sentence back instead of jumping to their own story. That is the move the experiment tested.

## Practice

- Practise active listening: when someone is speaking, listen without planning your response. Reflect back ("what I hear you saying is..."). Ask questions. Give full attention.

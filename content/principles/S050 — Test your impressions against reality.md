> **Principle 172** — Test your impressions against reality

> Work, therefore to be able to say to every harsh appearance, "You are but an appearance, and not absolutely the thing you appear to be." And then examine it by those rules which you have, and first, and chiefly, by this: whether it concerns the things which are in our own control, or those which are not.

(Epictetus, Enchiridion 1, trans. Elizabeth Carter, [Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html), page opened.)

The test Epictetus names is a question about the impression, asked before assent. It sorts the impression by whether it concerns what is in one's control. This differs from the entry's "check it against the world", which describes an empirical check. Marcus gives a related line in Meditations IV.7: "Take away thy opinion, and then there is taken away the complaint, 'I have been harmed.'" ([Meditations IV, Long trans.](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.4.four.html), opened). Taking away the opinion is the same move as asking whether the impression is what it seems. Neither passage says to gather evidence about the other person, so that step is the compendium's addition. The other cited passages (Discourses I.20 and I.28, Seneca's De Ira III.36) were not opened.

For the psychology parallel, Kahneman and Klein conclude that "subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy" and that the quality of an intuitive judgment depends on how predictable the environment is and on the chance to learn its regularities ([American Psychologist, 2009; PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)). The abstract does not mention prediction logging, which is the entry's label. Bennett-Levy et al. (2004) was not opened.

**Plain words** Epictetus teaches saying to a strong impression, "You are only an appearance," and then examining it before accepting it. The examination he names is about what is in one's control. Checking the facts is a sensible addition.

*For example (imagined):* Thinking the boss is angry, someone waits and notices she spoke to everyone that way this morning, and holds off on the angry reply.

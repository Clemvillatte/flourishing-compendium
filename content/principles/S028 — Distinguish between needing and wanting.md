> **Principle 150** — Distinguish between needing and wanting

You need less than you think. Most of what you want is not needed for flourishing. The Stoic practises the distinction: identify what you need (virtue, reason, the basic necessities) and treat the rest as optional. This does not mean living in poverty; it means living without attachment.

(Disc. I.1, I.25; Ench. 15; Med. III.6, VI.13; Seneca, Letters 2, 110.)

**Plain words** You need less than you think. Identify what you truly need, virtue, reason, the basics, and treat the rest as optional. That doesn't mean poverty. It means living without clinging.

*For example:* Looking at a full wardrobe and realising you wear a fifth of it.

> **Principle 13** — Cultivate accurate self-knowledge: know your traits, values, and patterns

Accurate self-knowledge — knowing your actual traits, values, strengths, weaknesses, and patterns — is a foundation of good decisions and authentic living. It requires both introspection and external feedback.

**Plain words** Accurate self-knowledge — your actual traits, values, strengths, weaknesses, patterns — is the foundation of good decisions and authentic living. It requires both introspection and external data: journals AND feedback. You're too close to see yourself without instruments.

*For example:* The person who introspects for years and concludes "I'm an introvert" — and the behavioural data (they light up at every party, drained only by specific people) that corrects it. The introspection needed the data; the data needed the journal. Both instruments, together.
## Psychology parallels

- **Self-knowledge and well-being**, accurate self-assessment is associated with better decisions and higher well-being (Vazire, 2010; Sedikides et al., 2004)
- **Others know us better in some domains**, close others are more accurate about our traits than we are in some cases (Vazire, 2010; Vazire & Mehl, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Know thyself** (Delphic maxim)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]]** (Socrates, Apology 38a)
- **Self-knowledge as the beginning of wisdom** (Aristotle, Metaphysics)

## Practice

- Seek honest feedback from people who know you well. Use structured self-assessment (strengths inventories, [[Values clarification|values clarification]]). Compare your self-view with others' view of you.

> **Principle 72** — Take the free person's signs as a monthly audit

Part IV closes with an appendix that rearranges the part's practical teaching under heads. Its summary item states the aim: "in life it is before all things useful to perfect the understanding, or reason, as far as we can" (Appendix IV). The last item ends with the equal mind: "we shall bear with an equal mind all that happens to us in contravention to the claims of our own advantage, so long as we are conscious, that we have done our duty" (Appendix XXXII); the same item continues that the power we possess is not sufficient to protect ourselves completely, and that we are a part of universal nature following her order. Between them are things a person can check: the appendix observes that minds are won by love and high-mindedness rather than force (Appendix XI).

The related propositions carry the same audit character: the strong man "hates no man, is angry with no man, envies no man, is indignant with no man, despises no man, and least of all things is proud" (note to IV.73), and mirth "cannot be excessive, but is always good" (IV.42).

As a compendium exercise, take the list as this page's title suggests: read it monthly, one or two items at a time, and mark which items describe you.

> "Yet minds are not conquered by force, but by love and high-mindedness." (Ethics IV, Appendix, XI, Elwes)

**Plain words**
The appendix gathers the Ethics' practical teaching into something you can hold in one view. Read it top to bottom and ask, item by item, whether it describes you. For this exercise, choose one item and describe a recent situation in which you did or did not act that way.

**For example**
In an imagined application, someone reads the appendix on the first Sunday of each month and picks the one item that least describes them as the month's single project.

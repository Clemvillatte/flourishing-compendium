> **Principle 266** — Practise compassion

In the excerpt reproduced by [Breems](https://sociologyandchristianity.org/index.php/jsc/article/download/6/4/20), Tutu connects being human with belonging, participation and sharing, and describes a person with ubuntu as open and available to others, affirming of others. The compendium applies this account of [[Ubuntu|Ubuntu philosophy]] to compassion through an exercise in offering practical help.

> "Ubuntu is very difficult to render into a Western language. It speaks of the very essence of being human… We say, 'A person is a person through other persons.' It is not, 'I think, therefore I am.' It rather says, 'I am human because I belong. I participate, I share.' A person with ubuntu is open and available to others, affirming of others, does not feel threatened that others are able and good…." (Tutu, No Future Without Forgiveness, 1999, p. 33, as quoted in Breems)

**Plain words**
Think of someone who is having a difficult time. Consider one specific task you could help with, and ask whether that help would be useful.

*For example:* Imagine a neighbour preparing for surgery. You offer to bring groceries on Thursday and ask what they need.

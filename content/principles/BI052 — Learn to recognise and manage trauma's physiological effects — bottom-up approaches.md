> **Principle 52** — Learn to recognise and manage trauma's physiological effects: bottom-up approaches

Trauma often shows up in the body: it can produce physiological patterns (hypervigilance, freeze, dissociation) that top-down (cognitive) approaches alone may not reach. Bottom-up (body-based) approaches are one way to work with what words alone don't shift.

**Plain words** Trauma lives in the body — hypervigilance, freeze, dissociation — patterns that top-down reasoning alone may not reach. Bottom-up approaches (breath, movement, body awareness) work on what words alone don't shift. The body holds what the words dropped.

*For example:* The years of insight into the trauma that changed nothing about the startle response — the knowing was top-down; the flinch was bottom-up. The body needed its own therapy.
## Psychology parallels

- **Trauma as physiological (van der Kolk)**, the view that trauma is stored in the body and produces physiological patterns (van der Kolk, 2014; Levine, 1997)
- **Body-based therapies for trauma**, somatic experiencing, sensorimotor therapy, and EMDR address trauma physiologically (Levine, 1997; Ogden et al., 2006; van der Kolk, 2014)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The body keeps the score** (van der Kolk)
- **The wound and the healing** (Nietzsche)
- **[[Post-traumatic growth|Post-traumatic growth]]** (Tedeschi & Calhoun)

## Practice

- If trauma has left physiological patterns (hypervigilance, freeze, dissociation), consider body-based therapies (somatic experiencing, sensorimotor therapy, EMDR), alongside — not instead of — other approaches.

> **Principle 405** — Practise the lived body (Leib)

The distinction this principle turns on is Merleau-Ponty's: the body you are is not one more object in the world. In the Stanford Encyclopedia's exposition, "the body itself is experienced in ways that distinguish it in kind from all other things" — always present in your own perceptual field, doubled back on itself when one hand feels the other, given its own movement from inside. Its relation to space is not a container's: the body relates to space as an "I can" rather than an "I think", and it lives or inhabits space rather than sitting in it. And the perceiver is not a pilot behind controls: "the agent of perception is the pre-reflective and anonymous subjectivity of the body, which remains enmeshed in and 'connatural' with the world that it perceives." The compendium's application is the principle's own instruction: do not treat the body as a mere instrument you have; live it as the ground of your being.

(Merleau-Ponty, 1945/2012, Phenomenology of Perception, Parts I and II; Husserl, 1952/1989, Ideas II, §§35-62.)

**Plain words** Distinguish the body as physical object (Körper) from the body as lived (Leib) — the body you are, not the body you have. The lived body is the medium through which you perceive and act. Don't treat it as a machine you're piloting.

*For example:* A user with a knee injury runs both frames deliberately: the clinical one, where it is a torn meniscus with a protocol, and the lived one, where the stairs have become something to watch and the day quietly reorganises around them.

> **Principle 21** — Treat truth as something you stand in, not only something you hold

The Nahua term for truth, neltiliztli, derives from the root of "to root": the true is the well-rooted, that which has taken root, that which holds. Truth is less a property of propositions than a property of lives: a life is true when it is rooted in what is real, and words are true when they are rooted in lived experience. The epistemology and the ethics are one system. Practically, test your beliefs by their roots: which of your convictions are rooted in your own experience, and which are unrooted borrowings?

(Maffie's neltiliztli chapters, as reconstruction.)

**Plain words** Neltiliztli, truth, comes from the root "to root": the true is the well-rooted, that which holds. Truth is less a property of propositions than of lives — a life is true when rooted in what's real; words are true when rooted in lived experience. Truth is something you stand in, not just something you hold.

*For example:* The person whose stated values and actual calendar have nothing to do with each other — versus the one whose week is a receipt for what they claim to love. Only one of them is telling the truth, in the Nahua sense.

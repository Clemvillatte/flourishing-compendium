> **Principle 11** — Use the broaden-and-build mechanism

The theory in Fredrickson's own sentence:

> "The broaden-and-build theory posits that experiences of positive emotions broaden people's momentary thought-action repertoires, which in turn serves to build their enduring personal resources, ranging from physical and intellectual resources to social and psychological resources." (Fredrickson, "The role of positive emotions in positive psychology," [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11315248/))

The build side has a field test: working adults, half randomly assigned to learn loving-kindness meditation. "Results showed that this meditation practice produced increases over time in daily experiences of positive emotions, which, in turn, produced increases in a wide range of personal resources (e.g., increased mindfulness, purpose in life, social support, decreased illness symptoms). In turn, these increments in personal resources predicted increased life satisfaction and reduced depressive symptoms" ([Fredrickson et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954193/)). The theory proposes a pathway; the trial reported findings consistent with parts of it. The practical implication is the functional framing: moments of joy, interest, and contentment are not just pleasant; they are the widening moments from which resources get built. The compendium's account is [[Broaden-and-build]].

**Plain words**
A good mood is not a reward; it is a doorway. When you feel good, you see more options: the new recipe, the neighbour, the detour. Nothing was added to the world except your range. The build part is the long game: the recipe becomes a skill, the neighbour becomes a friend. You do not need to chase good feelings; when one opens the door, notice what it opened, and walk through.

*For example:* When you're in a good mood you try the new recipe and chat with a neighbour, and a month later you have a new friend.

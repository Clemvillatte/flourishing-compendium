> **Principle 70** — Treat climate change as a sociocultural stressor

Berry and colleagues' framework proposes that climate change may affect mental health directly, by exposing people to trauma, and indirectly, by affecting physical health and community wellbeing, within which eroded physical environments in turn damage social ones. ([Berry et al., 2010](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00038-009-0112-0))

The scale is documented: in a ten-country survey of 10,000 people aged 16 to 25, more than 45% said their feelings about climate change negatively affected their daily life and functioning, and distress was correlated with perceived inadequate government response. ([Hickman et al., 2021](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanplh/article/PIIS2542-5196(21)00278-3/fulltext))

> "Different aspects of climate change may affect mental health through direct and indirect pathways, leading to serious mental health problems, possibly including increased suicide mortality." ([Berry et al., 2010, abstract: Conclusions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00038-009-0112-0))

**Plain words** Take climate-related distress seriously without assuming that the problem lies entirely within the person. Ask what they are worried about and what has changed around them. This principle invites attention to circumstances as well as feelings.

**For example (imagined):** A construction worker loses paid hours during a heatwave and worries about rent. A conversation about the worry also makes room for the practical problem of lost income.

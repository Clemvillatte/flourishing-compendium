> **Principle 107** — Practice austerity of body, speech, and mind

In Arnold's translation of the seventeenth chapter, the Gita names three kinds of discipline. Of the body: "Worship of gods meriting worship; lowly reverence of Twice-borns, Teachers, Elders; Purity, rectitude, and the Brahmacharya's vow, and not to injure any helpless thing,--these make a true religiousness of Act" (Bhagavad-Gita ch. 17, trans. Arnold).

### Speech and mind

"Words causing no man woe, words ever true, gentle and pleasing words, and those ye say in murmured reading of a Sacred Writ,--these make the true religiousness of Speech." Of the mind: "Serenity of soul, benignity, sway of the silent Spirit, constant stress to sanctify the Nature,--these things make good rite, and true religiousness of Mind" (ch. 17, Arnold).

### Judged by motive

The text grades the same discipline by its aim. Kept "with no hope of gain, by hearts devote", it is "perfect work of Sattwan, true belief". "Religion shown in act of proud display to win good entertainment, worship, fame" is of Rajas, "rash and vain", and one "followed by a witless will to torture self, or come at power to hurt another" is of Tamas, "dark and ill". Earlier in the chapter, penance "in self-sufficient, proud hypocrisies" belongs to men Arnold calls "devils devoted, not to Heaven" (ch. 17, Arnold).

### What the principle adds

Arnold's text lists the three domains, but it does not say to adopt one austerity a month or to keep it private. Those are the principle's practical additions, as is the cross-reference to principle 40.

**Plain words** The Gita counts as discipline respect, purity and non-harm in action, true and kind speech, and a quiet, gentle mind. Display or self-torture ruins the practice. A small monthly commitment kept quietly is a sensible way to apply it, though the text does not set any schedule.

*For example:* Imagined example: one month you commit to saying nothing about a colleague that you would not say to them. You do not mention it to anyone.

(Bhagavad-Gita ch. 17, trans. Sir Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388, opened 2026-10-21. Arnold's verses are unnumbered, so the old cite, 17.14-17.19, is not checked against a numbered edition.)

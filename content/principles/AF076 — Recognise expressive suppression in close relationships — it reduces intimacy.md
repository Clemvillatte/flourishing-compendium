> **Principle 76** — Recognise expressive suppression in close relationships: it reduces intimacy

Suppressing emotional expression with a partner or close friend reduces rapport, increases physiological stress in both parties, and is associated with lower relationship satisfaction. In close relationships, expression (appropriately timed and modulated) outperforms suppression.

**Plain words** Suppressing emotional expression with a partner or close friend reduces rapport, increases physiological stress in both parties, and predicts lower relationship satisfaction. In close relationships, suppression costs more than it saves.

*For example:* The couple where one partner's bad week is carried silently — the other one's body reads the tension anyway, and now there are two dysregulated systems and zero information. The unsaid feeling didn't stay private; it went public physiologically.
## Psychology parallels

- **Suppression in close relationships reduces intimacy and increases stress**, suppressing with a partner reduces rapport and is perceived as inauthentic (Butler & Gross, 2009; English & Eldreda, 2020)
- **Emotional expression predicts relationship satisfaction**, the ability to express emotions appropriately is associated with better relationship outcomes (Gottman & Levenson, 2000)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Authenticity|Authenticity in relationship]]** (Buber, I and Thou)
- **The value of honest expression** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV)

## Practice

- With close others, err on the side of expression (with appropriate timing and modulation) rather than suppression. The cost of suppression in intimacy is high.

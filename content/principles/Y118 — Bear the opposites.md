> **Principle 118** — Bear the opposites

The Gita's early training instruction: the contact of the senses (heat and cold, pleasure and pain) comes and goes; they are transient, and the wise learn to bear them. The capacity named is titiksha, tolerance, treated as trainable rather than inborn. Practically, practice small voluntary bearances (cold shower's last thirty seconds, the queue, the hunger before the meal) as reps of the general capacity.

(BG 2.14; Vivekachudamani's titiksha, attributed.)

**Plain words** Heat and cold, pleasure and pain — the contact of the senses comes and goes; they are transient, and the wise learn to bear them. Titiksha, tolerance — trainable, not inborn. Practice small voluntary bears: the cold shower, the hunger gap, the boring hour.

*For example:* Someone trains on the small bears — waits out the itch to check the phone, takes the walk in the rain — and finds the big bears (the illness, the layoff) are met with a capacity that was built at small scale.

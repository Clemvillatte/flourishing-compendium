> **Principle 5** — Treat yourself as part of nature

The preface to Part III of the Ethics opens with a complaint about other writers: they treat human beings as exempt from the order of nature.

> Most of those who have written about the affects and men's way of living write as though their topic was not natural things that follow the common laws of Nature but rather things that are outside Nature. Indeed they seem to think of man in Nature as a kingdom within a kingdom. (Spinoza, Ethics III, Preface; Bennett translation)

He then states his alternative:

> Nothing that happens in Nature can be attributed to any defect in it; for Nature is always the same—the laws and rules of Nature that govern all events are the same at every place and every time. So the affects of hate, anger, envy etc., considered in themselves, follow from the same necessity and force of Nature as other particular things. (Spinoza, Ethics III, Preface; Bennett translation)

The program that follows treats the affects as objects of study rather than targets of sermon: "approaching human actions and appetites in the way I would approach questions about lines, planes, and bodies" (Spinoza, Ethics III, Preface; Bennett translation). Part I had already placed everything within God or Nature: "Whatever exists is in God, and nothing can exist or be conceived without God" (Spinoza, Ethics I, P15; Bennett translation). The compendium's reading of the preface for self-relations: self-hatred treats a fear or jealousy as a personal scandal, which both intensifies it and hides its cause; treating it as nature treats it as a mechanism you can study and steer.

**Plain words** A fear or an appetite is a fact about you, like your height or the day's weather. A fact can be examined. A scandal can only be confessed. Studying your own fear is the first step toward steering it.

*For example (imagined):* A man who has hated himself for years for being "so emotional" starts keeping notes on when the emotion rises and what comes before it, treating it as information about his own system.

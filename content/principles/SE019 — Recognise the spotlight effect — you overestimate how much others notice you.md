> **Principle 19** — Recognise the spotlight effect: you overestimate how much others notice you

People overestimate how much others notice and evaluate them. In reality, others are mostly attending to themselves. This "spotlight effect" creates unnecessary self-consciousness and social anxiety.

**Plain words** The spotlight effect: you overestimate how much others notice and evaluate you. In reality, everyone is mostly attending to themselves — their own spotlight, their own flub. The audience you're performing for is mostly thinking about their own performance.

*For example:* The coffee spill that feels like a public spectacle — and the reality check: ask anyone what they saw that morning. Nobody can name it. They were busy replaying their own awkward email. The spotlight was always rented to each person, for themselves.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Spotlight effect (Gilovich)|Spotlight effect (Gilovich)]]**, people overestimate how much others notice them (Gilovich et al., 2000; Savitsky et al., 2001)
- **[[The illusion of transparency|Illusion of transparency]]**, people overestimate how visible their internal states are to others (Gilovich et al., 1998; Savitsky & Gilovich, 2003)

## Philosophy parallels

- **The fear of judgment as suffering** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)
- **[[Care|Care about the right audience]]** (Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations)
- **The crowd as projection** (Kierkegaard, The Crowd is Untruth)

## Practice

- When you feel scrutinised, remember the spotlight effect: others are mostly attending to themselves, not to you. This reduces social anxiety and self-consciousness.

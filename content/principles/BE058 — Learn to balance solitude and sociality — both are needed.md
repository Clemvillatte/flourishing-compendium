> **Principle 58** — Learn to balance solitude and sociality: both are needed

Both solitude (time alone) and sociality (time with others) are needed. Solitude supports reflection and renewal; sociality supports connection and support. Balancing both is a behavioural skill.

**Plain words** Solitude and sociality are both needs — the person who never sits alone is as lopsided as the one who never sits with anyone. The balance point is personal and seasonal. Know your own mix and schedule both.

*For example:* The weekend with nothing social — bliss for two days, swampy by three — versus the over-packed one. The good life isn't one or the other; it's the right ratio, adjusted monthly.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Solitude|Solitude and sociality (Larson, Winnicott)]]**, both are necessary (Larson, 1990; Winnicott, 1958)
- **Balance predicts well-being**, having both alone time and social time predicts well-being (Larson, 1990; Long & Averill, 2003)

## Practice

- Balance solitude and sociality: schedule both alone time (for reflection, renewal) and social time (for connection, support). Do not sacrifice one for the other.

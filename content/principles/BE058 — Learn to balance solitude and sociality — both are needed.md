> **Principle 58** — Learn to balance solitude and sociality: both are needed

Weinstein and colleagues tested the principle's central idea in a 21-day diary study of 178 adults. They found "no evidence for a one-size-fits-all 'optimal balance' between solitude and social time". On days with more hours alone, people "were lonelier and less satisfied", but "these detrimental relations were nullified or reduced when daily solitude was autonomous (choiceful)", and on those days people "felt less stress and greater autonomy satisfaction" (Weinstein et al. 2023, [Scientific Reports](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38052821/)). The authors caution that the study was correlational and offers no basis for causal claims, and they conclude that "'balance' is not best conceptualized in terms of hours of solitude time" ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10698034/)).

**Plain words**
You need time alone and time with others, but there is no magic ratio to hit. In the diary study, days with more hours alone brought less stress and more freedom, and also more loneliness, and choosing the time alone softened the loneliness. So balance is less about counting hours and more about whether your alone time is something you chose and how lonely you feel afterwards.

*For example:* You block Saturday morning for a solo walk because you chose it, then ring a friend in the evening because you noticed you felt flat. This is an imagined week. It uses the study's finding that chosen solitude carried fewer costs, and nothing in it says three hours alone is the right amount.

## Practice

- Balance solitude and sociality: schedule both alone time (for reflection, renewal) and social time (for connection, support). Do not sacrifice one for the other.

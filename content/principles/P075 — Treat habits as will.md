> **Principle 75** — Treat habits as will

Dewey's claim in *Human Nature and Conduct* is that habits are the self in action, with no separate will standing behind them. In the chapter on habit as will he writes that "a predisposition formed by a number of specific acts is an immensely more intimate and fundamental part of ourselves than are vague, general, conscious choices."

> "All habits are demands for certain kinds of activity; and they constitute the self. In any intelligible sense of the word will, they are will. They form our effective desires and they furnish us with our working capacities. They rule our thoughts, determining which shall appear and be strong and which shall pass from light into obscurity." (John Dewey, *Human Nature and Conduct*, 1922, Part 1 §2, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt))

He rejects the picture of habits as "means, waiting, like tools in a box, to be used by conscious resolve": "they are something more than that. They are active means, means that project themselves, energetic and dominating ways of acting."

His practical conclusion is about circumstances. In Part 1 §1 he writes: "To change the working character or will of another we have to alter objective conditions which enter into his habits." The same logic would apply to yourself, though that step is my extension and not a sentence of his.

**Plain words** A resolution is something you decided once. A habit is what you do on an ordinary morning when you are not thinking about it. Dewey counts the habit as the real will, and treats the resolution as a vague, general choice. If you want to read more, change what your day puts in front of you, such as where the books sit and where the phone charges.

*For example:* Someone who wants to run puts the shoes by the front door the night before and charges the phone in another room. The decision to run is no stronger than before, but the morning now starts differently. This is my illustration, not Dewey's example.

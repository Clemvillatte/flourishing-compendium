> **Principle 370** — Practise deliberative community

Habermas's deliberative theory holds that law is legitimate only if it results from "a free and inclusive democratic process of opinion and will-formation". His "two-track" model links formal bodies (legislatures, courts) with informal public-sphere communication, which is "wild" and not centrally coordinated ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Democracy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/democracy/)). So citizens' talk outside the formal bodies has a role in the theory, which supports the principle. Sandel (2009) and Bohman (1996) were not opened.

Evidence on what deliberation does comes mostly from Deliberative Polls. Fishkin reports significant opinion change on 72% of 58 indices across nine national polls ([Involve](https://www.involve.org.uk/resources/knowledge-base/what-are-effects-deliberation/impact-deliberation-participants-opinions)), with knowledge gains of about 10% ([Fishkin and Luskin 2005](https://moodle.epfl.ch/pluginfile.php/2721132/mod_resource/content/1/Fishkin-Luskin2005_Article_ExperimentingWithADemocraticId.pdf)). A critic finds "no evidence of a normatively desirable gain in informed, autonomous, or considered opinion", only minor knowledge gains ([Critical Review](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08913811.2011.635872)). These are structured events with recruited samples, which differ from a town meeting.

The psychology parallel runs mostly the other way from the seed's wellbeing framing. Flavin and Keane (2012) found, in about 1,300 US citizens, that more satisfied people were more likely to vote and take part in politics ([World Happiness Report 2019](https://files.worldhappiness.report/WHR19_Ch03.pdf)), but only in non-conflictual forms, not protest ([Ghana study summarising it](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/server/api/core/bitstreams/67bed250-32c6-4a2f-a01c-f6ba3af8be8e/content)). It is correlational, and its direction is satisfaction to participation. It does not show that participating improves wellbeing.

**Plain words** Public decisions need ordinary people's reasoning, not just that of officials. Habermas's theory gives informal public talk a role in making law legitimate. Evidence on deliberation shows people learn and change views in structured settings, though one critic doubts that the opinions get better. Whether joining improves your own wellbeing is not shown here.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A parent who rarely speaks at school meetings raises the bus route, and the committee asks for the timetable.

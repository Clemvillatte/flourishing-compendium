> **Principle 346** — Practise infinite responsibility

Levinas's claim is not that you should feel guilty; it is about the order of things. Responsibility, in his account, does not come after freedom:

> "But the other absolutely other—the Other—does not limit the freedom of the same; calling it to responsibility, it founds it and justifies it." (Levinas, Totality and Infinity, [Lingis translation, p. 197](https://wuecampus.uni-wuerzburg.de/moodle/pluginfile.php/2896578/mod_folder/content/0/Levinas,%20Totality%20%20Infinity,%20S.%2048-51,110-114,117-119,197-201.pdf))

So the call to responsibility is not a constraint your freedom reluctantly obeys; it is what founds freedom in the first place: "Thus in expression the being that imposes itself does not limit but promotes my freedom, by arousing my goodness" (p. 200). The two orders are one: "The order of responsibility... is also the order where freedom is ineluctably invoked" (p. 200). And the demand is not optional:

> "Before the hunger of men responsibility is measured only 'objectively'; it is irrecusable." (Levinas, Totality and Infinity, [Lingis translation, p. 201](https://wuecampus.uni-wuerzburg.de/moodle/pluginfile.php/2896578/mod_folder/content/0/Levinas,%20Totality%20%20Infinity,%20S.%2048-51,110-114,117-119,197-201.pdf))

(Levinas, 1961/1969, Totality and Infinity.)

**Plain words** Your responsibility for others isn't a contract you signed — you discover you were already in it. And it's never fully discharged: whatever you've done, the demand isn't exhausted. Not a guilt trip; a description of what a human being is.

*For example:* Imagine the neighbour who helped once and later wonders whether once was ever the right unit of measure for the demand.

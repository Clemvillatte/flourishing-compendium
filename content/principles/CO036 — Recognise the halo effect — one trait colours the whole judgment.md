> **Principle 36** — Recognise the halo effect: one trait colours the whole judgment

In Nisbett and Wilson's study, two videotaped interviews staged with the same instructor, one warm and one cold, showed that "global evaluations of a person can induce altered evaluations of the person's attributes, even when there is sufficient information to allow for independent assessments of them". Viewers of the warm instructor rated his appearance, mannerisms and accent as appealing, and viewers of the cold one rated the same features as irritating. The subjects were also unaware of the influence, and those who saw the cold instructor believed that their dislike of his attributes had lowered their global evaluation of him, which was the reverse of what actually happened ([Nisbett & Wilson 1977](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/biases/35_J_Personality_Social_Psychology_250_(Nisbett).pdf)).

Looks are the best-studied trigger. Dion, Berscheid and Walster reported a "what is beautiful is good" stereotype, with attractive people assumed to have more desirable personalities and better lives ([Dion et al. 1972](http://www.agpolpsy.de/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/dion-berscheid_what-is-beautiful-is-good.pdf)). A later meta-analysis found that the stereotype is "not as strong or general as suggested" by that phrase. The differences were largest for social competence, intermediate for intellectual competence, and "near zero for integrity and concern for others" ([Eagly et al. 1991](https://scispace.com/papers/what-is-beautiful-is-good-but-a-meta-analytic-review-of-4ebqnan72s)).

### Scope

Thorndike 1920 is listed as further reading. The seed's claim that confidence and charisma carry a halo, and its hiring claim, are not checked here. Its claim that the traits "were never actually correlated" is too strong, and it is not tested here.

**Plain words**
A good first impression can lift your rating of everything else about a person, and you probably will not notice it happening. Appearance carries less weight on honesty and kindness than on social skill.

**For example**
When you review candidates, score each criterion across all candidates before you move to the next criterion, so that one strong first impression cannot lift every score.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Halo effect|Halo effect]]**, global evaluations are influenced by one salient trait (Thorndike, 1920; Nisbett & Wilson, 1977)
- **The effect is robust in impression formation**, physical attractiveness drives judgments of competence, intelligence, and morality (Dion et al., 1972; Eagly et al., 1991)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Judging by the whole vs the parts** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **The face is not the character** (Aesop, fables)
- **Do not judge by appearances** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)

## Practice

- When evaluating a person (or yourself), deliberately separate traits. Ask: "what is the evidence for each specific quality, independent of my overall impression?"

> **Principle 36** — Recognise the halo effect: one trait colours the whole judgment

When you perceive one positive trait (attractiveness, confidence, charisma), you assume other positive traits (intelligence, competence, kindness). One negative trait similarly colours everything. This affects hiring, relationships, and self-assessment.

**Plain words** The halo effect: one positive trait — attractiveness, confidence, charisma — colours the whole judgment: you assume intelligence, competence, kindness. One negative trait similarly stains everything. Judge traits separately; the halo is doing your hiring.

*For example:* The tall, confident presenter the room rates as "brilliant" — and the quiet analyst whose numbers were actually right — and the audit a year later. The halo gave the wrong person the project. Separate the traits on purpose; they were never actually correlated.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Halo effect|Halo effect]]**, global evaluations are influenced by one salient trait (Thorndike, 1920; Nisbett & Wilson, 1977)
- **The effect is robust in impression formation**, physical attractiveness drives judgments of competence, intelligence, and morality (Dion et al., 1972; Eagly et al., 1991)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Judging by the whole vs the parts** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics)
- **The face is not the character** (Aesop, fables)
- **Do not judge by appearances** (Buddhism, Dhammapada)

## Practice

- When evaluating a person (or yourself), deliberately separate traits. Ask: "what is the evidence for each specific quality, independent of my overall impression?"

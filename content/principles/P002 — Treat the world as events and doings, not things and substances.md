> **Principle 2** — Treat the world as events and doings, not things and substances

Whitehead's ontology in one line:

> "how an actual entity becomes constitutes what that actual entity is." (Whitehead, Process and Reality [1985: 23], [quoted in the SEP entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/))

Process philosophy takes the fundamental units of the world to be happenings rather than stuff: "actual entities are drops of experience, complex and interdependent" ([P&R 1985: 18, quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)), and each is what its becoming makes it. The principle's own illustration: a "thing" is a name for a pattern that recurs slowly enough to seem permanent, the marriage, not the certificate; the health, not the body; the house, not the deed. Whitehead's named target was the abstraction habit, "the error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete" ([Science and the Modern World, 1925 (1967 ed., p. 51), quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)): treating the noun as the reality and the doings as incidental. The correction changes what you attend to. A certificate can be framed; a marriage has to be maintained. The compendium's psychology parallel is [[Dynamic systems theory]].

**Plain words**
A thing is a slow event. The chair is wood fibres holding a shape this year; the friendship is a history of Tuesdays; your health is what your days are doing lately. Naming the pattern is useful, but the pattern is the happenings, and if the happenings stop, the thing stops. That is why a marriage cannot be stored in a certificate: the paper is a snapshot, and the marriage is the exposure.

*For example:* The house isn't the deed — it's the repairs, the dinners, the arguments resolved, twenty years of small doings holding a shape.

> **Principle 333** — Practise responsibility

The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy summarises Joan Tronto's account. She and Bernice Fisher define care as "a species of activity that includes everything we do to maintain, contain, and repair our 'world' so that we can live in it as well as possible". Tronto identifies four sub-elements of care: "(1) attentiveness, a proclivity to become aware of need; (2) responsibility, a willingness to respond and take care of need; (3) competence, the skill of providing good and successful care; and (4) responsiveness, consideration of the position of others as they see it and recognition of the potential for abuse in care" ([IEP, "Care Ethics"](https://iep.utm.edu/care-eth/), citing Tronto 1994, 126-136).

Responsibility is therefore the second step, after attentiveness. It names a willingness to act, so noticing a need does not complete the task. The same article records criticism that Tronto's definition is "overly broad, counting nearly every human activity as care", and that care ethics risks a "slave morality". I did not open Tronto's own book, so I rely on the encyclopedia's summary and its page citation.

**Plain words**
Noticing a need is the first step. Responsibility is the second: deciding that the response is yours and following through. "Somebody should do something" becomes "I'll be there at three".

**For example**
You see that an elderly neighbour's bins have not gone out. You tell her you'll take them out every week and put a reminder on your phone.

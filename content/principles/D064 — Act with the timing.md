> **Principle 64** — Act with the timing

Two sources carry the principle. The Yinfu Jing states it in its middle section:

> "eat in season and the hundred bones are ordered; move with the pivot and the ten thousand transformations are settled." (Yinfu Jing, Middle Section, [Good Works Translation](https://tianmu.org/good-work-library/daoist/classical-daoist-texts/classic-of-the-hidden-accordance))

Daodejing 8 makes the same point at the end of its water catalogue:

> "The excellence of a residence is in (the suitability of) the place; that of the mind is in abysmal stillness; that of associations is in their being with the virtuous; that of government is in its securing good order; that of (the conduct of) affairs is in its ability; and that of (the initiation of) any movement is in its timeliness." (DDJ 8, Legge trans., [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

In both lists, timeliness is the last-named excellence, attached to the initiation of movement itself. The Yinfu Jing pairs the ordered body with the settled transformations: eating at the right time and moving at the pivot are the same skill pointed at different objects.

(Yinfu Jing; DDJ 8.)

**Plain words** The texts make timing a property of the action, not the actor. The same raise request, apology or launch is a different action in a record quarter than in a layoff week, because the door it must pass through is open or shut. Being ready is your part; the door is not. The practice is to watch the door as closely as you prepare.

*For example:* Imagine the same raise request in a record quarter and in a layoff week. The words are identical; the door is not.

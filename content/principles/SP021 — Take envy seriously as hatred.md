> **Principle 21** — Take envy seriously as hatred

Envy is hatred insofar as it disposes a person to be pained by another's good fortune and to rejoice in another's ill fortune. Spinoza's contribution is to strip the romanticism from it: envy is not a shade of longing but straight hatred with a specific trigger, and it is fed by comparison. Practically, when someone else's success disturbs you, treat that as diagnostic data about where you have placed your worth, and audit the comparison rather than feeding the story.

(Ethics III, the definition of Envy; P24 and its scholium.)

**Plain words** Envy is hatred: being pained by another's good fortune, rejoicing in their ill fortune. Spinoza strips the romance — envy is not a shade of longing but straight hatred with a specific target. Taking it seriously is the beginning of treating it.

*For example:* The "benign envy" that's rebranded every year as motivation — and the test that catches it: did their failure just taste a little good? That taste is the diagnosis. The tradition treats it as hatred, which is why the affirmations never worked.

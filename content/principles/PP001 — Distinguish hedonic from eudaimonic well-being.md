> **Principle 1** — Distinguish hedonic from eudaimonic well-being

Hedonic well-being is the presence of positive affect and the absence of negative affect, the experience of pleasure and life satisfaction. Eudaimonic well-being is the experience of meaning, purpose, growth, and the actualisation of potential. They are correlated but distinct. A life can be high in hedonic well-being and low in eudaimonic, and vice versa. Flourishing requires both, but the eudaimonic dimension is the stronger predictor of long-term outcomes.

(Ryff, 1989; Deci and Ryan, 2000.)

**Plain words** There are two broad ways of doing well. One is feeling good: pleasure, satisfaction, few bad moods. The other is a life with meaning, purpose and growth. They overlap, but they're not the same, and a life can be full of the first and thin on the second.

*For example:* A well-paid job with great holidays can be pleasant for years, and still leave you wondering what any of it is for.

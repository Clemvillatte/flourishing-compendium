> **Principle 15** — Learn sleep hygiene: behaviours that support sleep

The behaviours the principle lists are real supports, and the clinical guideline puts them in their place. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine's 2021 guideline recommends multicomponent cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia as the strong first choice, and conditionally suggests stimulus control, sleep restriction and relaxation as single-component therapies. For sleep hygiene itself the guideline suggests clinicians not use it as a single-component therapy, based on indirect evidence that sleep hygiene was less effective than other treatments when used alone.

So the honest reading is two-layered: the hygiene behaviours are supports worth practising, and chronic insomnia needs more than them. The behaviours with public-education backing: a set schedule, exercise most days but not near bedtime, no caffeine or nicotine late in the day, a dark quiet bedroom without television or smartphones, and getting up to do something else if you cannot sleep.

> "We suggest that clinicians not use sleep hygiene as a single-component therapy for the treatment of chronic insomnia disorder in adults." (Edinger et al. 2021, AASM clinical practice guideline, [JCSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7853203/), Recommendation 6)

**Plain words**
Sleep hygiene is the maintenance work of sleep: steady schedule, dark cool room, no late caffeine, bed for sleeping, get up if sleep does not come. The guideline finding that matters is the ceiling on it: hygiene alone treats chronic insomnia worse than the fuller therapies, so a month of perfect routines plus bad sleep is a referral signal, not a discipline failure.

*For example:* An imagined case: someone with months of insomnia does everything right: dark room, regular hours, no late coffee, phone out of the bedroom, and still sleeps badly. The guideline's conditional recommendation is about exactly this case: hygiene is not the treatment for chronic insomnia, and the next step is a clinician who does CBT-I.

## Practice

- Practise sleep hygiene: consistent sleep and wake times, a dark cool bedroom without screens, no caffeine late in the day, use the bed for sleep, and get up if sleep does not come. For chronic insomnia, the guideline's first choice is multicomponent CBT-I from a clinician, not hygiene alone.

> **Principle 71** — Do not accept the mob's favors

The anchor is IV, P70:

> "The free man, who lives among the ignorant, strives, as far as he can, to avoid receiving favours from them." (Ethics IV, P70, [Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800))

The source says "the ignorant", not crowds as such, and the problem it describes is mismatched valuation, not a scheming giver. The proof: the ignorant benefactor "puts his own estimate upon" the benefit, and "if it appears to be estimated less highly by the receiver, will feel pain", while the free man declines to repay benefits "with others reckoned as of like value", guiding himself instead "by the free decision of reason". A favour accepted binds the receiver into another man's estimate, and the repayment terms follow from it.

The note supplies the qualification the title omits: "it is often necessary to accept favours from them... we must, therefore, exercise caution in declining favours, lest we should have the appearance of despising those who bestow them... and so give ground for offence by the very fact of striving to avoid it." Necessity can require accepting, and then repaying in kind; courtesy governs the occasions of declining. The page's exercise drawn from the account: distinguish gifts from investments in your future compliance, and decline the second kind early, when the price is low.

**Plain words**
A favour accepted puts you in the giver's accounting. They decide what it was worth; if your gratitude does not match their estimate, they take offence, and the repayment terms are theirs. The free person avoids that ledger where he can, and where he cannot, accepts and repays in kind. When he declines, he declines with courtesy, because a rude refusal creates the very offence the rule was meant to avoid.

*For example:* Imagined: a committee hands out a sponsorship with a schedule of expectations attached. The free response reads the schedule before accepting, and if the price is compliance, declines while the price is small, with courtesy so as not to give offence.

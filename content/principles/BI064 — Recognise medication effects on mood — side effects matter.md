> **Principle 64** — Recognise medication effects on mood: side effects matter

Three literatures make the point from three directions. Antidepressants: the largest network meta-analysis found all 21 antidepressants more effective than placebo, with real differences between them in dropout rates ([Cipriani et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477251/)); a Cochrane-methods review of the SSRI trials found SSRIs significantly increased the risk of serious adverse events, with an effect on depressive symptoms below the review's predefined threshold for clinical significance ([Jakobsen et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28178949/)). Corticosteroids: a meta-analysis of their neuropsychiatric effects found glucocorticoid users showing depressive symptoms in 22%, mania in 11%, delirium in 16%, and behavioral changes in 52% ([Koning et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11099480/)).

> "In terms of efficacy, all antidepressants were more effective than placebo, with ORs ranging between 2·13 (95% credible interval [CrI] 1·89-2·41) for amitriptyline and 1·37 (1·16-1·63) for reboxetine." (Abstract, Findings; Cipriani et al., 2018, Lancet 391(10128): 1357-66, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477251/))

> "SSRIs significantly increased the risks of serious adverse events (OR 1.37; 95% CI 1.08 to 1.75; P = 0.009; 44 trials; Trial Sequential Analysis-adjusted CI 1.03 to 1.89)." (Abstract, Results; Jakobsen et al., 2017, BMC Psychiatry 17: 58, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28178949/))

> "For glucocorticoid users, meta-analysis showed a proportion of 22% for depression (95% CI, 14%-33%), 11% for mania (2%-46%), 8% for anxiety (2%-25%), 16% for delirium (6%-36%), and 52% for behavioral changes (42%-61%)." (Abstract; Koning et al., 2024, J Clin Endocrinol Metab 109(6): e1442-e1455, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11099480/))

**Plain words**
Mood has a pharmacist sometimes. The corticosteroid numbers are the clearest case: about one user in five shows depressive symptoms, one in nine manic ones, and half show behavioral changes ([Koning et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11099480/)). Antidepressants help more than placebo on average, and they carry adverse-event risks of their own ([Cipriani et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477251/); [Jakobsen et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28178949/)). The practical discipline is a timeline: when mood, energy or sleep shift, list what changed first, prescriptions included, and take the list to the prescriber rather than to a self-diagnosis.

**For example**
(Imagined) Six weeks into a prednisone course for a chest complaint, someone's family notices agitation and low mood. The timeline, that it started with the prescription, is the useful information; the review appointment is where it goes. The drug is not stopped at home.

## Psychology parallels

- **Antidepressant side effects**, SSRIs increased the risk of serious adverse events in a systematic review of 44 trials ([Jakobsen et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28178949/))
- **Corticosteroid effects on mood**, glucocorticoid users showed 22% depression and 11% mania proportions in a meta-analysis ([Koning et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11099480/))

## Practice

- If you notice mood or energy changes after starting a medication, discuss with your prescriber. Do not stop medications on your own. Medication effects are real and manageable.

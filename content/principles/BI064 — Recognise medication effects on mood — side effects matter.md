> **Principle 64** — Recognise medication effects on mood: side effects matter

Medications (including psychiatric medications) have side effects that can affect mood, energy, and cognition. Recognising medication effects is important for both physical and mental health.

**Plain words** Medications move mood — side effects of the ordinary kind (sleep, energy, weight, flatness) and the psychiatric kind. Reviewing the timeline against the prescription is basic hygiene. The new fatigue might have a name on the bottle.

*For example:* The "depression returning" that tracked the new statin's start date — the mood had a pharmacist, not a psychologist. The review appointment found it in ten minutes.
## Psychology parallels

- **Medication side effects on mood**, many medications affect mood and cognition (Preston et al., 2017; Haddad & Anderson, 2002)
- **Antidepressant side effects**, SSRIs and other antidepressants have side effects (Jakobsen et al., 2017; Cipriani et al., 2018)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Prudence|Prudence in medication]]** (Aristotle)
- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] includes examined medication** (Socrates)
- **Know thy limits** (Delphic maxim)

## Practice

- If you notice mood or energy changes after starting a medication, discuss with your prescriber. Do not stop medications on your own. Medication effects are real and manageable.

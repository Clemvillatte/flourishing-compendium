> **Principle 47** — Use intergroup contact as a prejudice-reduction tool

Use intergroup contact — cooperative, equal-status interaction between members of different groups — as a prejudice-reduction tool. Gordon Allport's contact hypothesis (1954) has substantial meta-analytic support: contact reduces prejudice across a wide range of groups and contexts (Pettigrew and Tropp, 2006). The conditions matter: cooperation, common goals, equal status, and institutional support.

**Plain words** Contact works — cooperative, equal-status interaction across group lines measurably reduces prejudice. Not acquaintance-from-afar; actual working contact. The conditions matter: cooperation, equal footing, common goal. It's the most reliable prejudice-reduction tool we have.

*For example:* The two departments that hated each other until the joint project with shared deadlines — six months later, "those people" became "the folks in analytics, who are actually pretty good at this." Contact did what lectures couldn't.

## Practice

- In intergroup settings, create conditions of cooperative, equal-status contact. In personal life, seek genuine contact across group lines (see principle 33). Recognize that contact alone does not change structures (Dixon et al., 2010).

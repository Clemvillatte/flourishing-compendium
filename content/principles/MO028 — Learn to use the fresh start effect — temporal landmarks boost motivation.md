> **Principle 28** — Learn to use the fresh start effect: temporal landmarks boost motivation

Dai, Milkman and Riis documented the effect they named the fresh start effect across three field studies:

> "We show that Google searches for the term 'diet' (Study 1), gym visits (Study 2), and commitments to pursue goals (Study 3) all increase following temporal landmarks (e.g., the outset of a new week, month, year, or semester; a birthday; a holiday)." (Dai, Milkman & Riis, 2014, PDF p. 1)

Their proposed mechanism is that the landmarks "demarcate the passage of time, creating many new mental accounting periods each year, which relegate past imperfections to a previous period, induce people to take a big-picture view of their lives, and thus motivate aspirational behaviors" (PDF p. 3). These archival studies found increased goal-related activity following temporal landmarks; they do not establish lasting habit formation. The compendium's suggested use: a meaningful date can serve as a prompt when planning a start.

**Plain words** People show more goal-related activity just after boundary dates: a birthday, the first of the month, a Monday. The researchers' account is that the boundary pushes past slips into an old mental period and lets you look at your life from further back. A boundary date can serve as a prompt when planning a start.

*For example (imagined):* Someone planning a return to running labels an upcoming Monday as a fresh start and schedules the first session.

## Practice

- When planning a start, a meaningful date can serve as a prompt: Monday, the first of the month, a birthday, the new year. These findings do not establish that postponing an available opportunity improves the outcome.

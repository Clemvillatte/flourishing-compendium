> **Principle 98** — Accept what destiny brings (li ming)

The sixth chapter of the Liezi stages a debate rather than delivering a doctrine. Endeavour (li) boasts that long life, success, rank and wealth all lie within its reach. Destiny (ming) replies with the historical record: the virtuous Confucius was distressed in Ch'en and Ts'ai, the tyrant Chou sat on the throne, Po Yi and Shu Chi starved on Mount Shou-yang while a bad family grew rich — "if all this is within the reach of your endeavour, why did you give long life to one and early death to the other?" ([Graham 1990, ch. 6, Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/258599406/Graham-A-C-The-Book-of-Lieh-Tzu-A-Classic-of-the-Tao)).

The key move is the last line. Destiny does not claim credit either — "when we say that a thing is destined, how can there be anyone who directs it?" The chapter's real teaching, per the [IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/liezi/), is that neither your effort nor an external controlling hand is in charge. Life, failure, rank and wealth come about of themselves (gu). Accepting this is not fatalism about action; you still act, and it is about where the outcome's authorship sits.

> "When we say that a thing is destined, how can there be anyone who directs it? I push it when it is going straight, let it take charge when it is going crooked. Long life and short, failure and success, high rank and low, wealth and poverty, come about of themselves. What can I know about it? What can I know about it?" (Liezi 6, "Endeavour and Destiny", A. C. Graham translation, [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/doc/258599406/Graham-A-C-The-Book-of-Lieh-Tzu-A-Classic-of-the-Tao))

**Plain words** A poker player can play the hand perfectly and still be beaten by a run of cards. The player who understands the game keeps the skill — and the hand — and keeps the outcome in a different category. If they can control a hand, they control it. If they cannot, they do not read that hand as a verdict on their play. The Liezi's twist is that this is not resignation to fate either — no one is behind the cards. You do your best; you take what comes; you do not waste years trying to figure out who to blame for the hand you did not choose.

*For example:* Two applicants send near-identical resumes to the same role. One gets the call because the hiring manager was in a particular mood on a Tuesday. The other is exactly as qualified. Nothing about the outcome changes what either of them could have done that morning — and the one who does not read the rejection as a personal verdict keeps their skill intact for the next application.

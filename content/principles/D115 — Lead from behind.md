> **Principle 115** — Lead from behind

The best leader: people barely know she exists; the next best, they love and praise; the worst, they despise. Power over is failure mode; power through is the work.

Chapter 17 of the Daodejing ranks rulers by how the people respond to them: at the top, people who did not know there were rulers; below that, rulers they loved and praised, then feared, then despised. The chapter ties the people's faith to the ruler's: "when faith (in the Tao) was deficient (in the rulers) a want of faith in them ensued (in the people)". What the earliest rulers did instead was keep their words few:

> "How irresolute did those (earliest rulers) appear, showing (by their reticence) the importance which they set upon their words! Their work was done and their undertakings were successful, while the people all said, 'We are as we are, of ourselves!'" (DDJ 17 §2, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

Chapter 66 explains the position that produces this:

> "So it is that the sage (ruler), wishing to be above men, puts himself by his words below them, and, wishing to be before them, places his person behind them." (DDJ 66 §1, [Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The rest of the chapter spells out what the position buys: though the sage has his place above them, men do not feel his weight, and though he is before them, they do not feel it an injury to them. Because he does not strive, no one finds it possible to strive with him.

(DDJ 17, 66.)

**Plain words**
Applied to a team, ask which decisions colleagues are equipped to make without your approval. Give them the information and authority those decisions require, and stay available when they need help. When the work is done, people saying it happened of itself is the outcome the chapter describes, and it does not require making yourself absent.

**For example**
A manager agrees a spending limit for routine purchases with her team: colleagues decide within the limit and bring exceptions back for review.

> **Principle 28** — Mattering matters

The experience of mattering, the sense that one is significant to others and that one's life makes a difference, predicts well-being independently of self-esteem and social support. Mattering is not about importance in a grand sense. It is about the experience that one's existence is registered, that one would be missed, that one's presence and absence are noted.

(Rosenberg and McCullough, 1981; George and Park, 2016.)

**Plain words** Mattering is feeling that you're significant to others and that your life makes a difference. It predicts well-being separately from self-esteem and support. It's about being registered: you'd be missed, you're counted on, you're noticed.

*For example:* Being asked by a neighbour to come to the street meeting, because they'd like your view.

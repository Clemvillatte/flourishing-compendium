> **Principle 35** — Use collective intelligence deliberately

Groups can be smart, and how they work together matters more than who is in them. In two studies of 699 people in groups of two to five, Woolley and colleagues found a general collective intelligence factor that "is not strongly correlated with the average or maximum individual intelligence of group members but is correlated with the average social sensitivity of group members, the equality in distribution of conversational turn-taking, and the proportion of females in the group" ([Woolley et al. 2010, Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20929725/)). A later meta-analysis of 22 studies (1,356 groups) found "group collaboration process is more important in predicting CI than the skill of individual members" ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2005737118)). One critique argues there is not yet enough evidence for such a factor ([Credé and Howardson, as summarised in an MIT Sloan paper](https://gusandco.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/SSRN-id3187373.pdf)).

Sharing information can also hurt. In an experiment with 144 participants, "even mild social influence can undermine the wisdom of crowd effect in simple estimation tasks": knowing others' estimates "narrows the diversity of opinions" and boosts confidence "despite lack of improved accuracy" ([Lorenz et al. 2011, PNAS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21576485/)). A published comment argues the same data show individuals benefiting from the information ([Farrell, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1109947108)). Intermittent contact appears to be a design fix: groups with "intermittent social influence" found optimum solutions often while keeping a high mean ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1802407115)).



### Scope

These results come from lab tasks with small groups. I did not open Surowiecki, Sunstein and Hastie, Janis or Stasser and Titus.

**Plain words**
A group does best when people think alone before they compare notes, when quieter members get turns, and when sharing is staged.

**For example**
In your next decision meeting, have everyone write an estimate or recommendation on paper before anyone speaks. Collect the papers, then discuss.

## Practice

- In group decision-making, use structured processes: independent judgment before discussion (to avoid anchoring), devil's advocacy (to avoid groupthink), and diverse membership (to capture hidden profiles). See principle 36 for the failure modes.

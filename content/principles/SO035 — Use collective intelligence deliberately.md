> **Principle 35** — Use collective intelligence deliberately

Use collective intelligence — the ability of groups to solve problems that individuals cannot — deliberately. Groups can outperform their best members (Surowiecki, 2005); they can also fall prey to groupthink, polarization, and information cascades (Sunstein and Hastie, 2015). The adaptive stance is to design group processes that capture the benefit and avoid the failure modes.

**Plain words** Groups can be smarter than their smartest member — or dumber than their dumbest. The difference is design: independence of views, diversity, aggregation. Collective intelligence isn't a vibe; it's an engineering problem.

*For example:* The estimation meeting where everyone guesses privately first, then shares — versus the one where the loudest speaks first and anchors the room. Same people, wildly different output. That's the design.

## Practice

- In group decision-making, use structured processes: independent judgment before discussion (to avoid anchoring), devil's advocacy (to avoid groupthink), and diverse membership (to capture hidden profiles). See principle 36 for the failure modes.

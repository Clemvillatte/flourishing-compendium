> **Principle 74** — Make freedom of thought the safety valve

Chapter 20 of the Theologico-Political Treatise argues that minds cannot be commanded. If men's minds were as easily controlled as their tongues, every ruler would sit safe; but "no man's mind can possibly lie wholly at the disposition of another, for no one can willingly transfer his natural right of free reason and judgment, or be compelled so to do" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)). The object of government "is not to change men from rational beings into beasts or puppets, but to enable them to develope their minds and bodies in security, and to employ their reason unshackled"; the chapter concludes: "In fact, the true aim of government is liberty" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)). The same chapter allows that speech against the authorities is bearable when it comes "from rational conviction, not from fraud, anger, or hatred" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)).

> "the ultimate aim of government is not to rule, or restrain, by fear, nor to exact obedience, but contrariwise, to free every man from fear, that he may live in all possible security; in other words, to strengthen his natural right to exist and work without injury to himself or others." (Spinoza, Theologico-Political Treatise ch. 20, [Elwes translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm))

**Plain words**
A government cannot get inside anyone's head, and pretending it can costs it the outside too: people say what is wanted and plan what is not. Spinoza's answer is to drop the pretense. Let speech be free within the bounds of the peace, and the dissenter has an exit that is not conspiracy. The same holds in a family or a team: the person who is not allowed to disagree does not stop disagreeing, they go quiet.

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: a team lead who says "no complaints" at every meeting and wonders why problems surface first in resignations; the same lead asking each person for the strongest objection to the plan hears the problems while they are still fixable.

(Theologico-Political Treatise, ch. 20.)

> **Principle 74** — Make freedom of thought the safety valve

Chapter 20 of the Theologico-Political Treatise argues that a person cannot wholly transfer, or be compelled to surrender, free reason and judgment: "no man's mind can possibly lie wholly at the disposition of another, for no one can willingly transfer his natural right of free reason and judgment, or be compelled so to do" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)). The object of government "is not to change men from rational beings into beasts or puppets, but to enable them to develope their minds and bodies in security, and to employ their reason unshackled"; the chapter concludes: "In fact, the true aim of government is liberty" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)). Speech against the authorities is allowed within limits: a man "may even speak against them, provided that he does so from rational conviction, not from fraud, anger, or hatred, and provided that he does not attempt to introduce any change on his private authority" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)).

> "the ultimate aim of government is not to rule, or restrain, by fear, nor to exact obedience, but contrariwise, to free every man from fear, that he may live in all possible security; in other words, to strengthen his natural right to exist and work without injury to himself or others." (Spinoza, Theologico-Political Treatise ch. 20, [Elwes translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm))

**Plain words**
Spinoza separates what citizens think from what they are authorized to do. People can disagree while remaining subject to common laws; agreement in action does not require identical judgments. In the democracy he describes, "everyone submits to the control of authority over his actions, but not over his judgment and reason" ([TTP ch. 20, Elwes](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/992/992-h/992-h.htm)).

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: a citizen follows a law while publicly explaining why it should be amended.

(Theologico-Political Treatise, ch. 20.)

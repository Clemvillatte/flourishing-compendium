> **Principle 77** — Want unity of minds, not uniformity of opinion

Spinoza observes that a commonwealth is strongest when its people are bound by common purposes while their judgments are left free; the attempt to compel belief produces only the appearance of unity. The principle scales down to households and teams: agreement on the mission, freedom on the theories. Practically, when conflict is about opinion, re-anchor the conversation in the shared aim, and let the opinions differ.

(Theological-Political Treatise, ch. 20.)

**Plain words** A commonwealth is strongest when people are bound by common purposes while judgments are left free — compelling belief produces only the appearance of unity. Scales down to households and teams: want unity of minds, not uniformity of opinion. The uniform version is brittle.

*For example:* The family that requires matching opinions at every dinner — brittle, quiet, explosive — versus the one bound by common purposes (the farm, the care, the holidays) with opinions roaming free. The second one is the one still talking in ten years.

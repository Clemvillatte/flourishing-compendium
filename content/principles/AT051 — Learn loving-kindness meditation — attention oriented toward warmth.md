> **Principle 51** — Learn loving-kindness meditation: attention oriented toward warmth

Fredrickson and colleagues tested a loving-kindness meditation intervention in a field experiment with working adults. Half were randomly assigned to begin the practice. (Fredrickson et al., 2008.)

> "Results showed that this meditation practice produced increases over time in daily experiences of positive emotions, which, in turn, produced increases in a wide range of personal resources (e.g., increased mindfulness, purpose in life, social support, decreased illness symptoms)." (Fredrickson et al., 2008, [J Pers Soc Psychol 95(5):1045-1062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954193/))

The compendium uses a repeated wish for another person's wellbeing as an attention exercise. The reported positive-emotion findings do not establish that this exercise changes negative attentional bias.

**Plain words**
Bring one person's wellbeing to mind and wish them well. You can include yourself. Notice whether attention stays with the wish or moves elsewhere, and return to it if you choose to continue.

*For example:* Imagine practising with a colleague in mind and noticing your response to repeating the goodwill wish.

## Psychology parallels

- **Loving-kindness meditation and positive emotions**, a randomized field experiment reported increases over time in daily positive emotions among practitioners (Fredrickson et al., 2008)
- **Positive-emotion pathway to personal resources**, the reported increases in positive emotions were associated with increases in personal resources and life satisfaction (Fredrickson et al., 2008)

## Practice

- Practise loving-kindness: 10-20 minutes, silently repeating phrases ("may you be well, may you be happy, may you be free from suffering") toward self, loved one, neutral person, difficult person, all beings.

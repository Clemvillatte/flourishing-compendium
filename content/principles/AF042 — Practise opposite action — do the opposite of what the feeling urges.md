> **Principle 42** — Practise opposite action: do the opposite of what the feeling urges

Opposite action comes from dialectical behavior therapy. Its starting point is that "every emotion comes with an action urge" ([Fassbinder et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5021701/)). Anger pushes toward attack, sadness toward withdrawal, anxiety toward avoidance, shame toward hiding. When the urge does not fit the facts or the person's values, the skill is to act the other way: approach, engage, be kind.

> Opposite action does not intend to suppress an emotion, but to be mindfully aware and accepting of an emotion and its action urge, but to be able to decide to act differently.
>
> Fassbinder et al., Frontiers in Psychology (2016)

The evidence is thinner than the skill's reputation. Linehan's idea is that acting against the urge reduces the emotion's intensity, which a 2019 team called "thought to lead to reductions in its intensity, though this has not been empirically-tested". Their own lab study of 16 people with borderline personality disorder found larger drops with opposite action for sadness and for guilt and shame, but not for anxiety or anger ([Sauer-Zavala et al., 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6467696/)). They say replication outside the lab is needed.

(Linehan, 1993; Sauer-Zavala et al., 2019.)

**Plain words** A feeling tells you what it wants you to do. This skill is to notice that, check whether the feeling fits the situation, and when it does not, do something else on purpose. It is not pretending the feeling is absent. The feeling stays; what you do with it is up to you.

For example (imagined): you feel ashamed after a clumsy presentation and want to skip the next team meeting. The opposite action is to attend and say hello to the people who were in the room. You are choosing against the urge to hide, and you are not trying to feel fine first.

## Psychology parallels

- **Opposite action is hypothesised to reduce the target emotion**, and was tested in a single-case laboratory study of people with borderline personality disorder (Linehan, 1993; Sauer-Zavala et al., 2019)
- **Support is mixed by emotion**, with larger reductions for sadness and guilt or shame than for anxiety or anger in that study (Sauer-Zavala et al., 2019)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as the mean between extremes]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)

## Practice

- When you feel an urge to withdraw, approach. When you feel an urge to attack, step toward. When you feel an urge to hide, show up. Do this deliberately, not as denial but as a corrective to the emotion's bias.

> **Principle 42** — Practise opposite action: do the opposite of what the feeling urges

When angry, the urge is to attack; when sad, to withdraw; when anxious, to avoid; when ashamed, to hide. Opposite action means deliberately doing the opposite — approaching, engaging, moving. This is a core DBT skill and one of the most effective brief interventions for specific emotional patterns.

**Plain words** Opposite action: when angry, the urge is to attack; when sad, to withdraw; when anxious, to avoid; when ashamed, to hide. Do the deliberate opposite — approach, engage, move. The behaviour feeds back into the feeling and unwinds it.

*For example:* The shame that says skip the class, hide the face — and the person who walks in anyway, does the thing the shame forbade — and the shame, starved of its evidence, thins by the third week. The feeling followed the action, not the reverse.
## Psychology parallels

- **Opposite action reduces the target emotion**, acting opposite to the emotion's urge reduces the emotion over time (Linehan, 1993; Rizvi & Linehan, 2005)
- **Opposite action works for anger, sadness, anxiety, shame, and guilt**, with specific actions mapped to each emotion's typical urge (Linehan, 1993)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Virtue|Virtue as the mean between extremes]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Right action|Right action over instinct]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- When you feel an urge to withdraw, approach. When you feel an urge to attack, step toward. When you feel an urge to hide, show up. Do this deliberately, not as denial but as a corrective to the emotion's bias.

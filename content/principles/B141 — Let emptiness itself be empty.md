> **Principle 141** — Let emptiness itself be empty

MMK 13.7-8 are the source for the principle. Verse 7 says the empty exists only in contrast to the non-empty: if a bit of the non-empty existed, a bit of the empty would also exist, and if there did not exist a bit of the non-empty, how could the empty exist? Verse 8 draws the consequence for practice: the Conquerors taught emptiness as the forsaking of all views, and those who view emptiness are taught to be without realisation, incurable. (Nagarjuna, MMK 13.7-8.)

> "The Conquerors taught emptiness as the forsaking of all views. Those who view emptiness are taught to be without realisation" (MMK 13.8, [Batchelor, literal translation](https://www.leighb.com/VfromC.htm))

The compendium's application: emptiness in this verse is a medicine with a use and a dosage, not a position. The warning is aimed at the person who keeps the medicine in the stomach, which is how the verse's tradition frames holding emptiness as a view.

**Plain words**
Emptiness was taught to dissolve fixed positions, so a fixed position about emptiness is the failure mode the verse names. As a compendium exercise, notice when you use the word in an argument: are you loosening a grip, or claiming territory? The test the verse offers is functional: if holding it adds a view rather than removing one, it is being held the wrong way.

*For example:* Imagine someone who lectures about non-attachment for an hour, attached to the lecturing. The tool has become the trophy.

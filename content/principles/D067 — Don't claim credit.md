> **Principle 67** — Don't claim credit

The sage accomplishes and doesn't dwell on it, acts and doesn't take the credit. Credit-hoarding is how good work turns bad.

(DDJ 2, 24, 77.)

**Plain words** Accomplish and don't dwell on it; act and don't take the credit. Credit-hoarding is how good work turns bad — the moment it's about the trophy, the work serves the trophy. Let results speak; they're better at it than you are.

*For example:* The teammate who quietly fixed the bug gets pulled onto every important project after — the fix was the advertisement.

- **[[Leader humility|Leader humility]]**, expressed humility improves team performance and engagement (Owens et al., 2013)
- **Self-serving attribution biases and their relational costs**

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[Anatta|Anatta]]** (Buddhism, Milindapanha), no self to hold the credit
- **[[Humility|Humility]]** (Aquinas, Summa Theologiae II-II, q.161)

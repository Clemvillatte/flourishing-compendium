> **Principle 84** — Build a personal affect-maintenance system: learn, adapt, retain

Gross's extended process model treats emotion regulation as an ongoing cycle: identify what is happening, select a tactic, implement it, and register whether it worked. Regulation "maintenance" is the loop running smoothly; when outcomes stop matching, the cycle revises. On this model, keeping an affect-regulation practice alive means running the loop on the practice itself: keep what still works, replace what stopped.

> "When the action outputs of the three emotion regulation stages are highly similar across cycles, we refer to this as emotion regulation maintenance." (Gross, "Emotion Regulation: Current Status and Future Prospects", Psychological Inquiry 26, 2015, p. 15, [opened full-text PDF](http://www.johnnietfeld.com/uploads/2/2/6/0/22606800/gross_2015.pdf))

The model is built to change across a life. Gross reports that emotion regulation skills continue to develop through adulthood as adults learn the emotional practices tied to the workplace, and that a lifetime of experience with regulation supports high socioemotional functioning for many people in later life (2015, p. 18). The learning is cumulative, which is the reason to retain what works rather than start over each time circumstances shift.

**Plain words**
Your ways of managing mood are not installed once. The habit that carried you through one decade can die quietly in the next. A maintenance system treats each practice as an experiment with a shelf life: notice the effect, keep what still delivers, swap out what stopped.

**For example**
A toolkit built in one's twenties: the Tuesday run, the friend called after bad news, the early night. An annual audit asks which still changes how the day feels. The run still works, the friend has moved, the early night has become a phone-scroll. Keep the run, replace the night.

## Practice

- Keep a simple affect log. Try a regulation practice for 2 weeks; assess whether it works; retain what works, discard what doesn't. Repeat as circumstances change.

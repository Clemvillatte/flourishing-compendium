> **Principle 418** — Practise being-with (Mitsein)

Heidegger's claim in Being and Time §26 is that being-with is not something a person has as a result of other people showing up; it is part of the structure of what it is to be a person at all.

> Being-with is an existential characteristic of Dasein even when factically no Other is present-at-hand or perceived. Even Dasein's Being-alone is Being-with in the world. The Other can *be missing* only in and *for* a Being-with. Being-alone is a deficient mode of Being-with; its very possibility is the proof of this. (Heidegger, Being and Time, §26, trans. Macquarrie and Robinson, [online text](https://dhspriory.org/kenny/PhilTexts/Heidegger/BeingTime03.doc))

The consequence Heidegger draws is that loneliness is not evidence against being-with but a mode of it. You can only be alone relative to others who are somewhere, and the world you are alone in is one that is already structured as shared — the language you think in, the tools you use, the paths you walk are all things other people made and maintain.

What follows practically is a stance toward other people in daily settings. If the shared world is not an obstacle course of separate individuals but the primary given, then treating each person encountered as either an impediment or a resource is a misreading of the situation rather than a neutral reading of it.

**Plain words** The usual picture is that you exist first as a separate person, and then other people enter the scene. Heidegger reverses the order: the "with" is there before the "I." You learn a language someone else spoke, use tools someone else built, and walk paths someone else cleared, before you ever meet them. Being alone works the same way; it only makes sense against a background of people who could be with you.

*For example:* Imagine two drivers on the same motorway. One experiences every other car as something blocking a road that is otherwise his; the other experiences the traffic as many people going somewhere together. The road is the same road; only one of these readings treats it as what it is.

> **Principle 213** — Practise negative visualisation

Imagine losing what you have: your health, your loved ones, your possessions, your life. Not to induce anxiety but to prepare the mind and to appreciate what is present. The Stoic who has rehearsed loss is less devastated when it comes.

(Seneca, Letters 18, 24, 91, 107, 122; Med. III.1, III.6, VI.34; Disc. I.14, III.26.)

**Plain words** Imagine losing what you have: health, loved ones, things, your life. The point isn't anxiety. It's preparing the mind and noticing what's here. The Stoic who has rehearsed loss is less flattened when it comes.

*For example:* Picturing your partner gone for a minute while they're in the next room, then going in and just listening to them.

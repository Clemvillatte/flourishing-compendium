> **Principle 72** — Recognise trauma's effect on attention: hypervigilance

The clinical literature places hypervigilance at the center of post-traumatic attention. In the DSM-5 criteria for PTSD, hypervigilance, heightened startle reaction, and difficulty concentrating sit together in the arousal-and-reactivity cluster (Criterion E), alongside sleep disturbance and irritability. The intrusive side of the pattern (unwanted upsetting memories, nightmares, flashbacks) forms its own cluster (Criterion B).

> "A common symptom that arises from traumatic experiences is hyperarousal (also called hypervigilance). Hyperarousal is the body's way of remaining prepared. It is characterized by sleep disturbances, muscle tension, and a lower threshold for startle responses and can persist years after trauma occurs. It is also one of the primary diagnostic criteria for PTSD." (SAMHSA, [Understanding the Impact of Trauma](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK207191/), Trauma-Informed Care in Behavioral Health Services, TIP 57, Ch. 1)

The same guidance describes how hyperarousal affects response: it "can interfere with an individual's ability to take the necessary time to assess and appropriately respond to specific input, such as loud noises or sudden movements," and "can produce overreactions to situations perceived as dangerous when, in fact, the circumstances are safe." The compendium's reading: this is a nervous system doing its protective job too well, long after the danger — and it is the reason this principle's Practice section points to trauma-specific treatment rather than ordinary attention training.

**Plain words**
Trauma re-tunes attention to sentinel duty: constant scanning, startle at nothing, focus that won't hold because the exits are being watched. That's not distraction — it's a guard that never got relieved. It deserves care, not criticism.

**For example**
As an everyday illustration: the veteran who sits facing the door and can't follow the conversation: not rude, not scattered. The attention is busy keeping everyone alive. That's a job for a clinician, not a productivity app.

## Practice

- If your attention is dominated by threat-scanning and intrusion after trauma, seek trauma-specific treatment (trauma-focused CBT, EMDR). Ordinary attentional training is not sufficient for trauma.

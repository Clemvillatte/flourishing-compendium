> **Principle 72** — Recognise trauma's effect on attention: hypervigilance

Trauma produces a specific attentional pattern: hypervigilance (scanning for threat), difficulty with sustained focus (threat capture), and flashbacks (involuntary attention to trauma memories). This is a distinct pattern requiring trauma-specific treatment.

**Plain words** Trauma re-tunes attention to sentinel duty: constant scanning, startle at nothing, focus that won't hold because the exits are being watched. That's not distraction — it's a guard that never got relieved. It deserves care, not criticism.

*For example:* The veteran who sits facing the door and can't follow the conversation — not rude, not scattered. The attention is busy keeping everyone alive. That's a job for a clinician, not a productivity app.
## Psychology parallels

- **Trauma and attentional patterns**, PTSD involves hypervigilance, threat attention bias, and intrusive memories (APA, 2022; Foa et al., 2007)
- **Trauma impairs sustained attention**, threat scanning and intrusion disrupt concentration (Esterman & Roth, 2018; Steel et al., 2002)

## Practice

- If your attention is dominated by threat-scanning and intrusion after trauma, seek trauma-specific treatment (trauma-focused CBT, EMDR). Ordinary attentional training is not sufficient for trauma.

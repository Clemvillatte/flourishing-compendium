> **Principle 110** — Distinguish need from craving

In Dhammapada 186-187, sensual desires are described as unsatisfied even by abundant wealth, and the paired verses end by praising the destruction of craving ([Buddharakkhita translation](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.14.budd.html)).

> "There is no satisfying sensual desires, even with the rain of gold coins. For sensual pleasures give little satisfaction and much pain." (Dhammapada 186-187, trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita, [text](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.14.budd.html))

The passage warns about seeking satisfaction through sensual pleasures. It does not supply a diagnostic test separating needs from cravings. The compendium uses the distinction as a question for reflection: what are you hoping to obtain, and what would count as enough in the situation you face?

### Plain words

Try separating the problem you need to address from the particular solution you want. Describe what each would do for you, and consider alternatives. This exercise leaves room for practical requirements and preferences; it does not classify an urge by how quickly it disappears.

### For example

Imagine your phone has a cracked screen. Compare repairing it with buying a replacement. If you also want a better camera, consider that preference separately when deciding what to spend.

> **Principle 424** — Practise the distinction between facticity and transcendence

Recognise the two poles of your existence: facticity (the given facts of your situation — your body, your history, your context) and transcendence (your capacity to go beyond the given, to choose, to project). You are neither your facticity alone (determinism) nor your transcendence alone (abstract freedom). You are the tension between them.

(Original references, not consulted: Sartre, 1943/2003, Being and Nothingness, Part IV, ch. 1; de Beauvoir, 1947/2018, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I. Evidence: [SEP, Sartre](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/) §§4.1–4.2.)

Sartre's insight is that a person is never simply what their situation says they are. He glosses the for-itself as a "being which is what it is not and is not what it is" (Sartre 1943 [1956: 79]). The two poles are real at once, and the trouble starts when either one is affirmed to the exclusion of the other: the SEP entry summarizes Sartre's position as that the exclusive affirmation of one pole, or a motivated oscillation between them, constitutes bad faith.

Sartre's café waiter illustrates bad faith: having a role does not mean being reducible to that role. The SEP describes him as a waiter who plays at being a café waiter and attempts to institutionalise himself as this object. In Sartre's own words:

> There is no doubt I am in a sense a café waiter – otherwise could I not just as well call myself a diplomat or reporter? But if I am one, this cannot be in the mode of being-in-itself. I am a waiter in the mode of *being what I am not*. — Sartre, Being and Nothingness [1956: 60], quoted in [SEP, Sartre §4.2 (opened)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)

The practical core is that the given does not dictate its meaning. As the SEP entry puts it, a factual state, even poverty, does not determine how consciousness apprehends it; we choose the significance it has for us.

**Plain words**
Your past cannot be undone, but it does not supply a finished answer to what you will do next. Taking your circumstances seriously is different from treating them as a verdict on your whole future. Choosing a new direction also means working with the circumstances you have, rather than pretending they have disappeared.

*For example:* Someone might treat an anxious temperament as a fixed verdict (collapsing into facticity); someone else might treat every circumstance as freely rewritable (collapsing into transcendence). The workable position names the given and still chooses the project.

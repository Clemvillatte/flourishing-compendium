> **Principle 424** — Practise the distinction between facticity and transcendence

Recognise the two poles of your existence: facticity (the given facts of your situation — your body, your history, your context) and transcendence (your capacity to go beyond the given, to choose, to project). You are neither your facticity alone (determinism) nor your transcendence alone (abstract freedom). You are the tension between them.

(Sartre, 1943/2003, Being and Nothingness, Part IV, ch. 1; de Beauvoir, 1947/2018, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I.)

**Plain words** The two poles of your existence: facticity — the given facts (body, history, context) — and transcendence — your capacity to go beyond the given, to choose, to project. You are neither your facts nor a pure free float; you're the negotiation between them. Don't collapse into either pole.

*For example:* Someone collapses into facticity ("I'm just an anxious person, it's in the family") and another into transcendence ("I can be anyone, watch me") — the workable life is the one that holds both: given the anxiety, the project.

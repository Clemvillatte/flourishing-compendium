> **Principle 198** — Speak truthfully and simply

The Stoic speaks plainly. No pretension, no cleverness for its own sake. Say what you mean. Mean what you say. The simplest speech is often the most effective and the least likely to cause misunderstanding.

(Med. III.5, IV.16, V.9, VI.8, XI.15; Disc. III.21; Ench. 24, 33, 35, 42, 45, 46.)

**Plain words** The Stoic speaks plainly: no pretension, no cleverness for its own sake. Say what you mean and mean what you say. Simple speech is often the most effective and least likely to be misunderstood.

*For example:* Replacing "I was wondering if perhaps we might possibly..." with "Can we move the meeting?"

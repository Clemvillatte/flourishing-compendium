> **Principle 14** — Learn the architecture of sleep: cycles, deep sleep, and REM

Sleep has a structure worth knowing. There are two basic types of sleep, REM and non-REM, and non-REM has three stages; you cycle through them several times a night, with increasingly longer, deeper REM periods later in the session ([NINDS, Brain Basics](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep)). REM first occurs about 90 minutes after falling asleep, and memory consolidation, in the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke's wording, most likely requires both non-REM and REM sleep ([NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep)).

The memory research adds the mechanism. In the review literature, consolidation begins with the reactivation of recently encoded memory representations during slow-wave sleep, transforming them for integration into long-term memory, and the ensuing REM sleep may stabilize the transformed memories ([Rasch & Born 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23589831/)).

> "You cycle through non-REM and REM sleep several times during a typical night, with increasingly longer, deeper REM periods occurring later in the sleep session." (NINDS, [Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep), Sleep Stages)

**Plain words**
A night is not one block of unconsciousness but a repeating pattern, and the pattern is weighted toward the end: the page describes increasingly longer, deeper REM periods occurring later in the sleep session ([NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep)). Ending sleep early may therefore cut into those later REM periods ([NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep)) — the stage the memory research says may help stabilise what the night has consolidated ([Rasch & Born 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23589831/)).

*For example:* An imagined case: someone who sleeps from midnight to six sets an early alarm for a flight and cuts the night shorter still. On the primer's description ([NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep)), what gets cut is weighted toward the later REM periods rather than spread evenly across the night.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Sleep architecture (Walker)|Sleep architecture (Walker)]]**, sleep cycles between non-REM (deep) and REM (dream) stages, each serving distinct functions (Walker, 2017; Carskadon & Dement, 2011)
- **Deep sleep restores the body; REM consolidates memory and emotion**, non-REM is physical restoration; REM is emotional and memory processing (Walker, 2017; Rasch & Born, 2013)

## Practice

- Protect full sleep cycles: do not truncate sleep (both deep and REM are needed); adults should sleep 7 or more hours per night ([AASM/SRS 2015](https://aasm.org/resources/pdf/pressroom/adult-sleep-duration-consensus.pdf)). Do not sacrifice sleep for productivity.

> **Principle 4** — Recognise that thinking serves action, not truth alone

The root sentence of this principle is William James's, from the chapter on reasoning in The Principles of Psychology. James classed his own thinking as always in the service of doing, and gave necessity as his excuse: a finite, practical creature attending to one thing at a time.

> My thinking is first and last and always for the sake of my doing, and I can only do one thing at a time. (James, The Principles of Psychology, vol. 2, ch. XXII, [Project Gutenberg #57634](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57634/pg57634.txt))

The modern study of heuristics lands in the same place from the other side. Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier's review defines heuristics as efficient processes that ignore information, and reports that individuals and organizations often rely on simple heuristics in an adaptive way.

> Heuristics are efficient cognitive processes, conscious or unconscious, that ignore part of the information. (Gigerenzer and Gaissmaier, "Heuristic Decision Making", Annual Review of Psychology 62, 2011, [abstract](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-120709-145346))

The practical consequence: thoughts arrive shaped for action, speed and relevance, not calibrated for truth. The question to ask of a thought is what it is trying to get you to do.

**Plain words**
Your head is not a courtroom; it is a pair of hands looking for the next move. A thought shows up pre-shaped to make you do something: it compresses, it guesses, it picks a target. That is usually a bargain worth taking. The check is to catch the load-bearing thoughts, the ones you are about to act on with real stakes, and slow them down. The certainty that someone did something on purpose is exactly the kind of thought that is built for action and allergic to a second look.

**For example**
A colleague's snide remark reads instantly as deliberate. The thought arrives ready for action: confront, withdraw, complain. The truth question, whether they were distracted or having a bad day, needs the slower second look that the first reading had no appetite for.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Ecological rationality|Ecological rationality]]**, heuristics are adapted to specific environments and succeed by exploiting environmental structure (Gigerenzer & Gaissmaier, 2011; Todd & Gigerenzer, 2012)
- **[[Thinking for doing|Thinking for doing]]**, cognition is organised around action, not representation; the "descriptive" function is secondary to the "prescriptive" one (Gigerenzer, 2007)

## Philosophy parallels

- **Pragmatism: truth as what works** (James, Pragmatism; Dewey, Experience and Nature)
- **[[Will to power|Will to power as organising principle]]** (Nietzsche, Beyond Good and Evil)
- **[[Right intention|Right intention]]** (Buddhism, Eightfold Path)

## Practice

- When you notice a thought, ask: "what is this thought trying to get me to do?" This reveals its function, which is more informative than its content.

> **Principle 62** — Practice removes obstacles; it does not manufacture states

Yoga Sutra 4.3 gives the tradition's mechanism for practice. In Rama Prasada's translation:

> The creative-causes are not-moved-into-action by any incidental-cause; but that pierces-the-obstacle from it like the husbandman. ([YS 4.3, Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216765.html))

Vyasa's commentary spells out the farmer: he does not carry the water by hand to a field on the same or a lower level. He "simply removes the obstacles, and thereupon the water flows down of itself to the other bed." So practice acts as the one who opens the channel. The transformation itself comes from nature. Sutra 4.11 adds that the pattern ends when what holds it together does: "Being held together by Cause, Motive, Substratum and Object they disappear on-the-disappearance of these" ([YS 4.11](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216773.html)).

Vyasa's farmer removes vice as the obstacle to virtue. This book adapts the mechanism into a design rule: when a state such as calm or clarity fails to appear, stop trying to produce it and look for the obstacle. Fatigue, hunger, conflict, an unprocessed emotion and a body that has not moved are candidates. That list is a synthesis, not the source's.

(YS 4.3 and 4.11, trans. Rama Prasada, 1924.)

### Plain words

You can't make calm by willing it. You can find what is blocking it and clear that. The farmer does not grow the crop. He opens the channel.

### For example

Forcing yourself to sleep rarely works. Removing late caffeine, a hot room and the screen often does, since the sleep was already on its way.

> **Principle 62** — Practice removes obstacles; it does not manufacture states

The tradition's mechanism claim about practice: an instrumental cause does not produce the transformation, it removes obstacles to what nature is already doing — like the farmer who does not grow the crop but clears the channel so water reaches it. The same chapter adds that the pattern ends when what maintains it (its conditions and supports) ends. This book's adaptation of the mechanism into a design rule: when a state (calm, clarity, warmth) fails to appear, stop trying to produce it and look for the obstacle — fatigue, hunger, conflict, an unprocessed emotion, a body that has not moved — and clear that; the list is a synthesis, not the source's.

(YS 4.3 with the farmer image; YS 4.11 on removing what maintains.)

**Plain words** The mechanism claim: practice does not produce the transformation — it removes obstacles to what nature is already doing. Like the farmer who doesn't grow the crop but clears the channel so water reaches it. You're not manufacturing states; you're clearing channels.

*For example:* The person who tries to force themselves to sleep — versus the one who removes the obstacles: the late caffeine, the hot room, the screen. The sleep was always coming; the channel was blocked.

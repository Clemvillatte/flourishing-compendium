> **Principle 20** — Treat secular meaning as legitimate

Secular meaning is common, and the evidence on how well it works is mixed. A study using US survey data found that atheists and the religiously unaffiliated were "more likely to indicate that meaning in life was endogenous", meaning self-produced. It also found that this "was not associated with any 'penalty' for overall existential meaning", and the groups did not differ on fatalism or nihilism ([Speed, Coleman and Langston 2018, SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2158244017754238)). The same paper reports that atheists asked for sources of profundity "often list external sources such as nature, the universe, social causes, and humanity".

A review of nonreligiosity and life satisfaction is more cautious. It notes that in a small nonrepresentative sample, "highly religious individuals indicated higher levels of presence of meaning in life than atheists and agnostics, but that this was not related to higher levels of life satisfaction" ([Journal for the Scientific Study of Religion](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jssr.12723)). Steger's review adds that "relationships of one type or another are almost always the most important or most commonly mentioned sources of meaning" ([Steger, Meaning in Life](http://www.drpaulwong.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Steger-Michael-20181223_v2.pdf)).



### What this does and does not show

These studies show that people find meaning in family, work, nature and community without religion, and that no penalty appeared in one large survey. They do not settle every comparison, and "legitimate" is partly a philosophical claim these studies do not test. I did not open Frankl, Ryff and Singer or Keyes.

**Plain words**
Meaning can come from people, projects and the natural world, with or without religion. You do not need to defend your source of meaning to anyone.

**For example**
List three things that made last month feel worth it. Mark each as a relationship, a project or a place. Give the one with the least time in your calendar a slot this week.

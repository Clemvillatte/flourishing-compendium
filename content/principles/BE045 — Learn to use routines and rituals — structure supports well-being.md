> **Principle 45** — Learn to use routines and rituals: structure supports well-being

The evidence on routines is mostly correlational. A meta-analysis of 53 studies (910,503 respondents) found that disrupted daily routines were associated with depressive symptoms (r = 0.13), anxiety symptoms (r = 0.12) and general distress (r = 0.09), but not PTSD symptoms ([BMC Medicine](https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12916-024-03253-x.pdf)). In a cross-sectional study of older adults, social rhythm dysregulation correlated with depressive symptoms (r = 0.41) and lower quality of life (r = -0.39), and the authors read the findings "as associative rather than causal" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13206238/)). One review says the social zeitgeber hypothesis behind this idea has incomplete and circumstantial evidence, though regular routines are part of established treatments for bipolar disorder ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7890582/)). Research on routineness and wellbeing is also inconsistent, with some work linking routine to rigidity ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11075731/)).

For rituals, Norton and Gino ran three experiments in which people who completed rituals after a loss reported lower grief, and a regained feeling of control mediated the effect ([Norton and Gino 2014, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23398180/)). Hobson and colleagues' review proposes that rituals regulate emotions, performance states and social connection ([Hobson et al. 2018](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/jschroeder/Publications/Hobson%20et%20al%20Psychology%20of%20Rituals.pdf)). The widely cited paper claiming that rituals improve performance by decreasing anxiety was retracted ([retraction notice, 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejobhdp/v_3a185_3ay_3a2024_3ai_3ac_3as0749597824000694.htm)), so I do not rely on it.

### Scope

The seed's claim that routines "reduce decision fatigue" was not supported by a source I read. I did not open Lyons 2016 or Zisberg 2007. Norton and Gino 2014 is a set of lab experiments with short-term measures.

**Plain words**
Regular daily patterns go with better mood, but the studies cannot show routines cause it. Rituals after loss can help by restoring a sense of control.

**For example**
Fix one anchor in your week, such as a regular wake time, and note over a month whether your mood is steadier.

## Psychology parallels

- **Routines and well-being**, having regular routines is associated with better mental health (Lyons et al., 2016; Zisberg et al., 2007)
- **Rituals reduce anxiety and improve self-control**, rituals reduce anxiety and improve performance (Norton & Gino, 2014; Hobson et al., 2018)

## Practice

- Build routines and rituals: morning routine, evening routine, weekly rituals, seasonal practices. Structure supports well-being. Do not leave life to chance.

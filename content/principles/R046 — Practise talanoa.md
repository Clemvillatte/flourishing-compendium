> **Principle 308** — Practise talanoa

Timote Vaioleti, a Tongan scholar, defines talanoa as "a personal encounter where people story their issues, their realities and aspirations" and argues that it makes more mo'oni (pure, real, authentic) information available for Pacific research than other methods ([Waikato Journal of Education, 2006](https://wje.org.nz/index.php/WJE/article/download/296/310/1184)). He breaks the word into tala, to tell or inform, and noa, ordinary or nothing in particular, so talanoa is literally talk "about nothing in particular, and interacting without a rigid framework" ([quoted in a University of Auckland paper](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/1298c568-d2a7-475f-bbd9-dd69f357c978/content)). The word is shared across Tongan, Samoan and Fijian ([same paper](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/1298c568-d2a7-475f-bbd9-dd69f357c978/content)).

The same paper says the aim is understanding rather than agreement. The original entry's line "not debate or negotiation" is too strong, though. Sitiveni Halapua introduced talanoa as a political dialogue method after Fiji's 2000 coup (same paper), and at the 2017 climate talks in Bonn the Fijian negotiator said it was a way to "settle difficulties and disputes" ([Fiji Times](https://www.fijitimes.com.fj/lets-talanoa-the-pacific-way/)). The UN note describing the 2018 Talanoa Dialogue says its purpose is "to share stories, build empathy and trust" and that it should not single out parties in "confrontational" discussions ([UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/approach_to_the_talanoa_dialogue.pdf)). So talanoa can sit inside a negotiation without being one. Whether it changed the climate outcomes is not checked here.

For psychology, McAdams (2008) argues that life stories are told in social relationship and vary with the audience ([summary in a book chapter](https://www.irma-international.org/viewtitle/76737/?isxn=9781466629493)). That fits storytelling as relational. It is not evidence that talanoa builds bonds; the chapter itself is cited at second hand.

**Plain words** Most hard conversations run on an agenda: positions, rebuttals, a clock. Talanoa takes the agenda out on purpose. You sit together and tell what you actually lived, nothing in particular to decide, and the telling does the work. The surprise is that this is not soft avoidance. The same practice was taken into Fiji's political reconciliation and into the climate talks, on the reasoning that stories build the empathy and trust that positions cannot. So the move for a stalled meeting is to stop trading conclusions and start trading stories.

*For example:* A team replaces its blame-focused review with one question, "tell the story of what you saw", and people start listening to one another.

> **Principle 5** — Carry the ten modes lightly, as spot-checks

Sextus reports the tradition at the start of the ten-modes chapter: the modes by which suspension is supposed to be brought about are ten in number, and they carry the synonymous names of arguments and positions. The list runs from the variety in animals and the differences in human beings, through the structures of the organs of sense, the circumstantial conditions, positions and intervals and locations, intermixtures, quantities and formations, relativity, and frequency or rarity of occurrence, to the disciplines and customs and laws. Sextus closes the list with a caution: "This order, however, we adopt without prejudice."

As a compendium exercise, take your current strongest impression and run it through three of the modes in one minute: would it look different from another position, to another perceiver, in another quantity?

> "The usual tradition amongst the older skeptics is that the 'modes' by which 'suspension' is supposed to be brought about are ten in number; and they also give them the synonymous names of 'arguments' and 'positions.'" (Outlines of Pyrrhonism I.36, Bury)

**Plain words**
The list is old and long, but the use is quick. Each mode is one question about how something appears: to whom, from where, in what state, next to what, how often. When an impression grabs you, three of those questions usually loosen it. Not because the impression is wrong, but because the question of how it appears has more than one answer.

**For example**
In an imagined application, an insult that stings in a meeting is checked against three modes: from the back of the room it was barely audible, the speaker was running on three hours' sleep, and to a stranger present it read as a joke.

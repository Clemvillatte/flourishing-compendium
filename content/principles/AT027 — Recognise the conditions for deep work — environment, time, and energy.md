> **Principle 27** — Recognise the conditions for deep work: environment, time, and energy

Deep work — sustained, distraction-free concentration on cognitively demanding tasks — requires specific conditions. It cannot be achieved in fragmented, interrupted, or low-energy states. Understanding these conditions allows you to design for deep engagement.

**Plain words** Deep work has conditions: a protected block, no pings, and your best energy hours. It's not a mood, it's a setup. Treat those conditions like a recipe — miss an ingredient and the dish fails.

*For example:* The novelist writes 6–8 a.m. with the router unplugged. Not because she's precious — because the recipe only works with those three ingredients.
## Psychology parallels

- **Deep work requires uninterrupted blocks**, task switching and interruptions destroy concentration (Newport, 2016; Rubinstein et al., 2001)
- **[[Attention residue|Attention residue (Sophie Leroy)]]**, switching tasks leaves a residue that impairs subsequent performance (Leroy, 2009; Leroy & Glomb, 2018)

## Practice

- Protect blocks of time (60-120 minutes) for deep work. Eliminate all distractions (phone, notifications, other tabs). Match your highest-energy time to your most demanding work.

> **Principle 29** — Learn to use temptation bundling: pair unpleasant tasks with pleasures

Temptation bundling involves pairing a task you should do but do not enjoy with a pleasure you enjoy but should limit. For example, only listening to your favourite podcast while exercising. This increases motivation for the desired behaviour.

**Plain words** Temptation bundling: pair the thing you avoid with the thing you love — podcasts only while running, favourite café only while studying. Let pleasure pull the wagon. It's not cheating; it's engineering.

*For example:* The audiobook you're only allowed in the gym — suddenly the gym has a plot. You're not disciplined; you're finding out what happens next.
## Psychology parallels

- **[[Temptation bundling (Milkman)|Temptation bundling (Milkman)]]**, pairing a "should" behaviour with a "want" behaviour increases engagement (Milkman et al., 2014; Schwartz et al., 2014)
- **Bundle-only conditions increase gym attendance**, restricting enjoyable audio to the gym increases exercise (Milkman et al., 2014; Schwartz et al., 2014)

## Practice

- Bundle temptations: pair a "should" (exercise, studying, chores) with a "want" (favourite podcast, music, treat). Make the want contingent on the should.

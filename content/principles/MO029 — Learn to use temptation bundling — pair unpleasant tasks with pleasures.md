> **Principle 29** — Learn to use temptation bundling: pair unpleasant tasks with pleasures


Temptation bundling was introduced and tested by Milkman, Minson and Volpp. They define it as "a method for simultaneously tackling two types of self-control problems by harnessing consumption complementarities" (Management Science 60(2):283-299, 2014, [abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a60_3ay_3a2014_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a283-299.htm)). In their field experiment, participants were randomly assigned to gym-only access to page-turner audiobooks, to encouragement to keep the audiobooks for the gym, or to a control condition.

"Initially, full and intermediate treatment participants visited the gym 51% and 29% more frequently, respectively, than control participants, but treatment effects declined over time (particularly following Thanksgiving)." After the study, "61% of participants opted to pay to have gym-only access", which suggests that people see value in the restriction (same abstract).

A larger test followed. In a four-week exercise programme (N = 6792), giving participants an audiobook with encouragement to bundle "boosted their likelihood of a weekly workout by 10-14% and average weekly workouts by 10-12% during and up to seventeen weeks post-intervention". Relative to an audiobook alone, the encouragement had only "a modest positive effect", possibly because a free audiobook already suggests the idea (Kirgios et al. 2020, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 161:20-35, [abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeejobhdp/v_3a161_3ay_3a2020_3ai_3as_3ap_3a20-35.htm)).

The evidence is for exercise and audiobooks, with effects that fade or stay modest. It does not show that bundling works for every unpleasant task.

**Plain words** Save a treat for the hard task. Allow yourself the audiobook only at the gym. It helps at first, and the gain can shrink, so expect to revisit the arrangement.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A student keeps her favourite podcast for the library hours only. After a few weeks, the trick feels stale, so she switches to a new series that she wants to hear.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Temptation bundling (Milkman)|Temptation bundling (Milkman)]]**, pairing a "should" with a "want" raised gym visits at first in a field experiment (Milkman, Minson & Volpp, 2014) and raised weekly workouts in a larger programme (Kirgios et al., 2020); Schwartz et al. (2014) was not opened
- **Gym-only access to audiobooks**, full treatment 51% and encouragement 29% more visits than control, with effects declining over time (Milkman et al., 2014)

## Practice

- Bundle temptations: pair a "should" (exercise, studying, chores) with a "want" (favourite podcast, music, treat). Make the want contingent on the should.

> **Principle 77** — Build attentional maintenance: ongoing practice and adaptation

Attentional capacity is not a one-time achievement; it requires ongoing maintenance, adaptation to changing circumstances, and retention of what works. This is the variation-selection-retention loop applied to attentional life.

**Plain words** Attention maintenance is gardening forever: seasons change, tools change, life phases change the plan. What worked in your twenties won't fit the toddler years. The practice isn't a program; it's a relationship you keep adjusting.

*For example:* The deep-work routine that survived uni, collapsed with the newborn, and returns in toddler-friendly 40-minute blocks — same gardener, different weather.
## Psychology parallels

- **Attentional skills require ongoing practice**, like physical fitness, attention requires maintenance (Ericsson et al., 1993; Newport, 2016)
- **Regular meditation maintains attentional capacity**, sustained practice is more effective than intensive but brief training (Tang et al., 2015)

## Philosophy parallels

- **[[The examined life|The examined life]] as ongoing practice** (Socrates, Apology 38a)
- **[[The unity of the virtues|Virtue as maintained habit]]** (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II)
- **[[Bhavana (cultivation)|Bhavana (ongoing cultivation)]]** (Buddhism)

## Practice

- Build a maintenance routine: daily attention practice, weekly assessment, periodic environmental cleanup, and adaptation as circumstances change. Retain what works; discard what does not.

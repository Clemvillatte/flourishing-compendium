> **Principle 39** — Learn to limit alcohol: it disrupts sleep and worsens mood

Alcohol disrupts sleep (especially REM), worsens anxiety and depression, and impairs cognitive function. While it may provide short-term relaxation, it worsens mental health over time.

**Plain words** Alcohol trades sleep for sedation — it knocks you out, then fragments the night, murders REM, and bills you the next day's anxiety. "Relaxing" is the down payment; the withdrawal is the interest. The 3 a.m. wake-up is the loan shark arriving.

*For example:* The two wines that "help me sleep" — and the 3 a.m. staring-at-the-ceiling that follows on schedule. The relaxation was rented at a terrible rate.
## Psychology parallels

- **Alcohol and sleep**, alcohol disrupts sleep architecture, especially REM (Ebrahim et al., 2013; Thakkar et al., 2015)
- **Alcohol and mental health**, alcohol worsens anxiety and depression over time (Koob & Le Moal, 2008; Boden & Fergusson, 2011)
- **Alcohol as "liquid anxiety"**, the rebound effect after alcohol worsens anxiety (Koob & Le Moal, 2008; Thakkar et al., 2015)

## Practice

- Limit alcohol: if you drink, do so moderately and not before bed. Notice the effect on sleep and next-day anxiety. Reduce or eliminate if it worsens mood or sleep.

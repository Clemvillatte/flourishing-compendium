> **Principle 25** — Learn the full regulation toolkit: it is larger than "calm down."

Emotion regulation is not one skill; it is a family of processes that can be deployed at different points in the emotion-generating sequence. These include [[Situation selection|situation selection]] (avoiding or seeking situations), situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change (reappraisal), and response modulation (suppression, physiological downregulation).

### The definition

> "Emotion regulation refers to the processes by which individuals influence which emotions they have, when they have them, and how they experience and express these emotions." — James J. Gross, "The Emerging Field of Emotion Regulation: An Integrative Review", Review of General Psychology 2(3), 1998, p. 275 ([PDF](https://emotion.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/1353/2024/11/gross-1998-the-emerging-field-of-emotion-regulation-an-integrative-review.pdf))

### The five points

The process model behind the toolkit: "emotion may be regulated at five points in the emotion generative process: (a) selection of the situation, (b) modification of the situation, (c) deployment of attention, (d) change of cognitions, and (e) modulation of responses" (Gross 1998). The model distinguishes several things you can change: the situation you enter, the situation itself, your attention, your interpretation, and your response.

**Plain words** The kit is bigger than one tool. In a hard conversation you can change the situation (take it out of the kitchen), change the attention (look at what is actually being said), change the meaning (maybe this is not an attack), or change the response (breathe, lower the voice). "Calm down" is one move in that kit.

*For example:* In one difficult conversation, the only move is slowing the breath at the peak; in another, the same person has already chosen the time, reframed the stakes, and decided what to attend to. Same conversation, different kit.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Extended process model of emotion regulation|Extended process model of emotion regulation]]**, five families of strategies operate at different points in the emotion-generation sequence (Gross, 1998, 2015)
- **Each strategy has distinct costs and benefits**, reappraisal reduces negative affect but requires cognitive resources; suppression reduces expression but increases physiological arousal (Gross & John, 2003)

## Practice

- Build a repertoire across all five families. When one strategy fails, you have four others to try.

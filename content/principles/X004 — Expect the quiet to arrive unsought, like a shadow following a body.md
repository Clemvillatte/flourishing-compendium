> **Principle 4** — Expect the quiet to arrive unsought, like a shadow following a body

Sextus tells the story as an accident: the painter Apelles, unable to render the foam on a horse's mouth, "flung at the picture the sponge on which he used to wipe the paints off his brush, and the mark of the sponge produced the effect of a horse's foam" ([Outlines of Pyrrhonism I.12, Bury](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Online/texts/316/Sextus%20Empiricus,%20Pyrrhonism.pdf)). The Sceptics had the same experience: hoping to gain quietude by deciding the questions of sense and thought, "being unable to effect this they suspended judgment; and they found that quietude, as if by chance, followed upon their suspense, even as a shadow follows its substance" ([Outlines of Pyrrhonism I.12, Bury](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Online/texts/316/Sextus%20Empiricus,%20Pyrrhonism.pdf)). The quiet is not pursued; it is what the suspension leaves behind. He adds the limit: the Sceptic "is troubled by things unavoidable," thirst and age among them, but escapes the added belief that these conditions are evil, and with it a large part of the discomfort ([Outlines of Pyrrhonism I.12, Bury](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Online/texts/316/Sextus%20Empiricus,%20Pyrrhonism.pdf)).

> "they found that quietude, as if by chance, followed upon their suspense, even as a shadow follows its substance." (Sextus Empiricus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism I.12, [Bury translation](https://users.manchester.edu/Facstaff/SSNaragon/Online/texts/316/Sextus%20Empiricus,%20Pyrrhonism.pdf))

**Plain words**
The method gives up on calm as a goal, because aiming at it is one more verdict to defend. What it aims at is the balance; the calm is what the balance leaves behind, the way a shadow comes with the body without being chased. The practical difficulty is the checking: asking whether it has worked yet re-opens the question, and the question is what was disturbing you. The sponge story is the honest part: the effect arrived when the attempt stopped.

**For example**
Hypothetical illustration: someone practising the balancing method keeps asking whether they are calm yet, and only gets calm the afternoon they forget to ask.

(Sextus Empiricus, Outlines of Pyrrhonism, Book I, chapter 12, the Apelles passage and the shadow image.)

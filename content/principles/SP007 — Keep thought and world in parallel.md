> **Principle 7** — Keep thought and world in parallel

In the Ethics, Spinoza states the parallelism as a demonstrated proposition: the order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt))

> "PROP. VII. The order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things."

(Ethics II, Prop. VII, Elwes translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt).)

> "So, also, a mode of extension and the idea of that mode are one and the same thing, though expressed in two ways."

(Ethics II, Prop. VII, Note, Elwes translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt).)

The proof appeals to Part I, Axiom IV. The Note describes a mode of extension and its idea as one thing expressed in two ways. It also keeps causal explanations within their respective attributes: thought through thought, extension through extension. ([Ethics II, Prop. VII and Note](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3800/pg3800.txt))

**Plain words** Start with one thing and ask how Spinoza describes it under thought and under extension. Keep that reading exercise separate from checking whether your own description is accurate; this proposition does not supply that check.

*For example:* Imagined: a reader draws two columns, "extension" and "thought", and uses Spinoza's circle example to compare the two descriptions.

> **Principle 14** — Learn the poems of your tradition by heart

The Nahua education (formal and domestic) was built substantially on memorized song and speech: the songs carried the tradition's self-understanding in a form that survives forgetting because it lives in bodies. The principle generalizes to any tradition: the memorized text is available at 3 a.m., in hospital waiting rooms, and in old age, when books and search engines are not. Practically, carry a small repertoire (a poem, a psalm, a song, a passage) that you know by heart and add one piece per year.

(The huehuetlatolli's memorized speeches; the calmecac's memorized songs.)

**Plain words** Nahua education ran on memorized song and speech — carried in bodies, available when books and search engines aren't: at 3 a.m., in the hospital waiting room, in old age. Learn your tradition's poems by heart. The memorized text is the one that shows up when everything else fails.

*For example:* The grandmother with dementia who can't hold the morning — but the hymns and the old poems are still fully there, arriving unprompted, holding her company in the evening.

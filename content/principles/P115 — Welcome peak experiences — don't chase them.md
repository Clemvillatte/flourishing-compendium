> **Principle 115** — Welcome peak experiences: don't chase them

Maslow's description of the peak-experience, from Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences: [BQ below]. The passage marks the experience as an end in itself rather than a means.

> The peak experience is felt as a self-validating, self-justifying moment which carries its own intrinsic value with it. It is felt to be a highly valuable – even uniquely valuable – experience, so great an experience that even to attempt to justify it takes away from its dignity and worth. (Maslow, Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences, [full text](https://academyofideas.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Religions-Values-and-Peak-Experiences.pdf))

The principle's invitation follows from the end-in-itself character: attend to such an experience when it occurs without demanding a particular result from it. The preparations the practice kernels list (rest, gratitude, nature) are ordinary supports, offered as invitations rather than guarantees.

(Maslow, Toward a Psychology of Being, 1962; Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences, 1964.)

**Plain words**
When a moment of wholeness or awe arrives, give it your attention without asking it to prove anything. Afterwards, note what the experience was like rather than how to get it back. The kernels, rest, gratitude, nature exposure, are ordinary practices you can try; they are invitations, not levers that produce the experience.

**For example**
Suppose a walk by the water becomes unexpectedly moving. You could stop and attend to it for a few minutes, then note afterwards what the moment was like; or you could reach for the phone and let it pass. Either way, the moment is not asked to perform.

> **Principle 115** — Welcome peak experiences: don't chase them

Maslow's account of the peak experience, in Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences, is that the moment is "felt as a self-validating, self-justifying moment which carries its own intrinsic value with it" ([Maslow 1964, as quoted in Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religions,_Values,_and_Peak_Experiences)). The principle's practical reading follows from the carrying: what the moment carries with it, it does not borrow from your plans for it.

> "felt as a self-validating, self-justifying moment which carries its own intrinsic value with it." (Maslow, Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences, 1964, [as quoted in Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religions,_Values,_and_Peak_Experiences))

The application the principle takes from this: the preparable part is the conditions (rest, openness, attention) and the receiving part is the noticing. The demand is the compendium's own warning, not Maslow's sentence: treating the moment as an achievement is one of the conditions it does not survive.

(Maslow, Toward a Psychology of Being, 1962; Religions, Values, and Peak-Experiences, 1964.)

**Plain words**
You cannot schedule the moment, but you can prepare the ground it grows in: rest, attention, gratitude. When something arrives, the rain on the bus window, the work is to notice it rather than check the phone. Preparation is the part you own; the arrival is not.

**For example**
The mountaintop moment that happened on a bus, looking at rain on a window — not on the two planned retreats. Peaks aren't scheduled; they're noticed. Conditions, then attention.

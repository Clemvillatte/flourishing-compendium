> **Principle 9** — Recognise that motivation is social: it is influenced by others


Other people shape what we pursue, and some of that happens without any decision on our part. Six goal-contagion studies tested "the automatic adoption and pursuit of goals that others are perceived to strive for". People briefly exposed to behaviour implying a goal went on to pursue it, but not when the observed pursuit was "conducted in an unacceptable manner" (Aarts, Gollwitzer and Hassin 2004, J Pers Soc Psychol 87(1):23-37, [abstract](https://www.ovid.com/journals/jpspy/abstract/00005205-200407000-00002~goal-contagion-perceiving-is-for-pursuing)).

Norms work in the field. Hotel signs saying that "the majority of guests reuse their towels" beat a standard environmental appeal in two field experiments, and the wording closest to the guest's own situation did best (Goldstein, Cialdini and Griskevicius 2008, [abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/35/3/472/1856257)). A German replication found that both message types raised reuse over no message, but norm messages were "not more effective than the standard message" ([Bohner and Schlüter](https://www.socialpsychology.org/download/81001/BohnerSchltertowelreuse20131229SPN.pdf)).

Support helps, with limits. In a randomised weight-loss trial, 95% of people recruited with friends and given extra support completed treatment, against 76% of those recruited alone, and 66% against 24% fully maintained their loss (Wing and Jeffery 1999, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10028217/)). In a trial of 790 students, however, the support-only teams had a lower mean attendance than the control group (16.8 against 20.3 a week, no significance test reported in the abstract), while comparison with other teams raised attendance ([Zhang et al. 2016](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97q266wf/qt97q266wf.pdf)). So the kind of social structure matters.

**Plain words** Other people's goals and habits rub off on you. Pick who you spend time around with that in mind, and add support that suits the goal, because a team that only cheers does not always move people.

*For example:* This is an imagined case. A writer who struggles to start joins a Tuesday-morning writing group. Everyone works silently for an hour. Starting is easier, because starting is what the room does.

## Psychology parallels

- **[[Social influences on motivation|Social influences on motivation]]**, observed goals, descriptive norms and support affect pursuit (Aarts et al., 2004; Goldstein et al., 2008; Festinger, 1954 and Cialdini, 2001 are bare references, not opened)
- **Social support and goal pursuit**, recruiting with friends plus a support programme raised completion and maintenance in one weight-loss trial (Wing & Jeffery, 1999); support-only teams did not raise exercise attendance in another trial (Zhang et al., 2016)

## Practice

- Recognise that motivation is social. Surround yourself with people who support your goals. Use accountability (tell someone, work with others). Do not rely solely on willpower.

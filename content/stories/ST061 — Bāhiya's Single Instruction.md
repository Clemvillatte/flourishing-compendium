*Buddhist biographical narrative · India, 5th century BCE · [Bāhiya Sutta, Udāna 1.10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)*

Bāhiya, revered as a teacher, asks the Buddha for a teaching in brief. The Buddha, seeing his time is short, gives it: in the seen, let there be only the seen; in the heard, only the heard; in the sensed, only the sensed; in the cognised, only the cognised. Bāhiya awakens on the spot. He is killed by a cow moments later, and the Buddha has him cremated as one fully liberated.

The instruction is the tradition's minimalism at its limit. Nothing is added to experience: no commentary, no self standing behind it. The tradition preserved the story knowing most readers would find it too bare to use, which is itself part of the teaching.

*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · Malawi, 2009/2019 · [The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boy_Who_Harnessed_the_Wind)*

William Kamkwamba, 14, is forced out of school in Malawi during the 2001 famine because his family cannot pay the fees. He finds a library book about energy and, in the village junkyard, the materials: a bicycle dynamo, a tractor fan, blue-gum poles, and builds a windmill that powers lights and, eventually, a water pump. The film, directed by Chiwetel Ejiofor, adds the village context: the famine, the politics, and the father's initial refusal to let the family's only bicycle be cannibalised for parts.

The story is filed under ingenuity, and its care ethics is the more precise reading: the windmill is built from the village's own discarded things, by a boy who cannot attend school, to meet his family's and neighbours' actual need, and the intervention that works is the one designed from inside the situation. The book's title is the argument: he did not harness the wind; he cared for his family and the wind was the means.

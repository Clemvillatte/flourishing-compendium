*Zen biographical narrative · China, 7th century CE · [Platform Sutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_Sutra)*

Huineng, an illiterate woodcutter, hears a traveller recite the Diamond Sutra, the line "the mind should be born without dwelling anywhere," and awakens. He goes to the Fifth Patriarch's monastery, is assigned to the rice pounders, and is later chosen over the senior monk Shenxiu with a verse: Bodhi originally has no tree, the mirror has no stand; originally there is not one thing, so where could dust alight?

The story, the only Chinese sutra, is a narrative argument that wisdom is not accumulated. One line, heard with the whole life behind it, does what decades of commentary cannot. The Diamond Sutra line it turns on, "the mind born without dwelling," is the same instruction as Bāhiya's, and Huineng's verse is its philosophy compressed to four lines.

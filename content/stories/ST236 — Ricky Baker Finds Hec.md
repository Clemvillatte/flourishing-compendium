*Film narrative, cross-cultural · New Zealand, 2016 · [Taika Waititi, Hunt for the Wilderpeople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunt_for_the_Wilderpeople)*

Ricky Baker, a city kid who has been through a series of foster placements, is dropped at the remote farm of Bella and Hec. Bella wins him immediately, with kindness and a haiku. Bella dies; Ricky, afraid of being returned, runs into the bush, and Hec goes after him, and child services finds them together and assumes abduction. The two become fugitives in the New Zealand back country, national outlaws, and, gradually, family.

The film's whanaungatanga, kinship as made rather than merely inherited, is its entire plot. Bella and Hec are not Ricky's blood, and the film does not pretend the difference does not matter: what it argues is that kinship is constituted by staying, by shared hardship, and by the accumulated acts of care, and that this is what the word family has always meant when it has meant anything.

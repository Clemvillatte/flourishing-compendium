*Buddhist biographical narrative · India, 5th century BCE · [Aṅgulimāla Sutta, MN 86](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.086.than.html)*

Aṅgulimāla is a serial killer who wears a necklace of fingers. He sees the Buddha walking alone on a forest path and chases him. Running at full speed, he cannot catch a man walking at normal pace. He shouts: stop. The Buddha replies: I have stopped. You stop.

The exchange is the whole of Buddhist psychology in two sentences. The Buddha's stopping is not locomotive; it is the ending of the intention that drives all the rest. Aṅgulimāla drops his weapon, becomes a monk, and the tradition insists he attained liberation. His later life, mocked in the streets by the relatives of his victims, is the sutta's quiet appendix about consequences.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2004 · [Million Dollar Baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_Dollar_Baby)*

Maggie Fitzgerald, a waitress in her thirties, wants Frankie Dunn to train her. He refuses: she is too old, too amateur, a girl. She persists, he relents, and she rises to a title fight in which she is fouled, falls, and is paralysed. She asks Frankie to end her life. He, a Catholic who goes to mass daily, refuses, then does it.

The film is a study of the mean as relative rather than absolute. Protection that was a vice at the start, the refusal to train her, is the same act Frankie performs as a virtue throughout the middle; and the mercy killing, wrong by every rule he lives by, is presented as the final act of a love that has exhausted the rules. Aristotle's point that the virtuous action is relative to person, time, and circumstance is the film's whole moral architecture.

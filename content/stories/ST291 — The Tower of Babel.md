*Biblical/mythological narrative · Middle East, ancient · [Tower of Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Babel)*

Humanity, speaking one language, migrates to a plain in Shinar and decides to build a city and a tower with its top in the heavens, to make a name for themselves and avoid being scattered. God comes down, observes that nothing they propose will be impossible for them, and confuses their language so that they cannot understand one another's speech, and the building stops, and they are scattered.

The myth is usually read as a punishment for hubris, and its deeper content is a claim about communication: the common language is the technology that makes the collective project possible, and its fragmentation is the end of the project, not a moral consequence but a structural one. Its modern descendants are every analysis of the limits of translation, including Ricoeur's late work on translation as the difficult alternative to Babel.

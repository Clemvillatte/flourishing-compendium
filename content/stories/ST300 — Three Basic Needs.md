*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 2000 · [Self-determination theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-determination_theory)*

Deci and Ryan's [[Self-determination|self-determination theory]] identifies three basic psychological needs, the satisfaction of which predicts flourishing across cultures: autonomy, the experience of acting from one's own endorsement; competence, the experience of effectance in the world; and relatedness, the experience of connection to others. The needs are distinguished from goals and motives, which can be intrinsic or extrinsic, and the theory predicts that pursuing extrinsic goals, wealth, fame, image, does not satisfy the needs.

The theory is the most heavily researched framework in motivation psychology, with cross-cultural support, and its practical content is diagnostic: interventions that ignore one of the three needs, or that trade one for another, underperform, and the common failure mode is trading autonomy for competence, as in the tightly managed workplace, or competence for relatedness, as in the depersonalised school.

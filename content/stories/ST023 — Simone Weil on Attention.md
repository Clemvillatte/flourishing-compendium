*Biographical/philosophical, cross-cultural · France, 1942 · [Simone Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Weil)*

Weil, working through war and illness, writes that attention is the rarest and purest form of generosity. Her essay on school studies argues that every time we really attend to something, we are exercising the same faculty that prayer and friendship require. A student who works at solving a geometry problem with real attention is, she says, practising the presence of God whether or not she believes in one.

The claim sounds mystical until you test it against ordinary experience: the people you consider most loving are rarely the most effusive. They are the ones who listen as if the matter were difficult and important, because to them it is.

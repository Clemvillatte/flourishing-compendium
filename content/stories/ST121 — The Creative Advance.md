*Philosophical metaphor · England/United States, 1929 · [Alfred North Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_North_Whitehead)*

Whitehead names the universe's fundamental activity the creative advance into novelty. Each occasion inherits the entire past and adds something not contained in it, and the addition is real: the future is not already rolled up in the present, waiting to unwind. Creativity, for Whitehead, is the category of the ultimate, more basic than any substance.

The metaphysics is demanding, but the ethical upshot is stated plainly: a civilization, a person, a conversation is healthy exactly insofar as it is producing genuine novelty, and ill exactly insofar as it is merely repeating. The criterion is not happiness or comfort; it is whether tomorrow contains anything that was not in yesterday's inventory.

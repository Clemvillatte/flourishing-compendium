*Buddhist biographical narrative · India, 5th century BCE · [Sigālovāda Sutta, DN 31](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.31.0.nara.html)*

The Buddha finds young Sigālaka worshipping the six directions, as his dying father told him to. The Buddha reframes the ritual: the directions are relationships. Parents, teachers, spouse, friends, workers, ascetics. And each direction carries duties running both ways: the parents support and the child honours them; the friend protects what is entrusted and does not abandon one in trouble. The sutta then runs through the whole web in detail.

It is the closest thing in the canon to a householders' manual, and its move is characteristic: the Buddha does not abolish the inherited practice, he decodes it. The ritual was pointing at the real structure of a life all along.

*Stoic parable · Greece/Rome, 1st century CE · [Stoicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoicism)*

In a storm at sea, the passengers panic and the sailors work. The storm is identical for both; the difference is the story each has about it. For the passengers, the storm is a threat to everything they value and cannot control; for the sailors, it is a professional condition with known procedures, and their competence converts the same event into a different experience.

The parable, in various forms, runs through the tradition, and its point is often misread as "the sailors are brave." The Stoic reading is epistemic: the sailors know what is theirs to do and what is not, and their calm is a by-product of that sorting, not a character trait. The passengers' terror is also a by-product, of having no such sorting.

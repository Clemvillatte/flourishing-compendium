*Philosophical metaphor · Germany, 1932 · [Karl Jaspers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Jaspers)*

Jaspers describes Grenzsituationen, limit situations: the structures of human existence that cannot be avoided or changed, death, chance, guilt, and struggle. In these situations, he says, the ordinary categories fail, the self is thrown back on itself, and the response is not a solution but an illumination, since limit situations cannot be resolved, only clarified. We are, in his phrase, always in situations, and we cannot live without struggling against them, and we cannot fundamentally change them.

Jaspers, a psychiatrist turned philosopher, excluded from his university post by the Nazis and reinstated after the war, developed the concept in his massive systematic philosophy. Its clinical and literary descendants are the whole literature of crisis, and its practical content is a reorientation: the limit situation is not a problem to be solved but the occasion of the self's clarification, and the choice is not whether to meet it but how.

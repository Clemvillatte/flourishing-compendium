*Film narrative · Australia, 2013 · [Rolf de Heer, Charlie's Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie%27s_Country)*

Charlie, an elderly Yolngu man in Arnhem Land, is caught between two systems. The police confiscate his spear as a weapon; the welfare system assesses his house; he cannot hunt the way he was raised because hunting is regulated and his tools are contraband. He leaves the community for the bush, lives on Country as he was taught, becomes ill, and is taken into hospital and then custody. He returns, and the film ends with him teaching a young man, in a cage in a correctional facility, the songs of the land.

The film, co-written with its star David Gulpilil, is the clearest cinematic statement of the Aboriginal concept of Country as health: not property but the relational ground of wellbeing, such that separation from it, and the prohibition on living in the traditional manner, is itself a form of illness. The film's refusal to romanticise the bush, where Charlie nearly dies, is part of the argument: the relationship is real, and it has been made nearly impossible to live.

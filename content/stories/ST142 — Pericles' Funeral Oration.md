*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 431 BCE · [Pericles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericles)*

At the funeral for the first Athenian dead of the Peloponnesian War, Pericles does not praise the fallen as individuals; he describes the polis that made them. Athens, he says, is the school of Hellas, a democracy where the prizes go to merit rather than birth, where citizens participate in public affairs, and where private life is graceful. The soldiers' excellence and the city's excellence are the same excellence seen at two scales.

The oration, as Thucydides records it, is the ancient world's clearest statement that flourishing is not private. The function argument, which Aristotle later systematises, is already operating here: a human being's good is the good performance of human activity, and the activity is civic before it is anything else.

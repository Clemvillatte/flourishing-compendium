*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2006 · [The Pursuit of Happyness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pursuit_of_Happyness)*

Chris Gardner, homeless and raising a small son alone in San Francisco, competes for an unpaid internship at a stock brokerage, one of twenty candidates for one job. He sleeps in subway station bathrooms, arrives each day in the one suit he owns, and makes more calls than anyone, selling his bone-density scanners at night to survive. He wins the position. The film is based on Gardner's memoir, and the misspelled title is his, from the daycare's sign.

The film is usually filed under inspiration, but its structure is the pragmatist theory of action. Gardner's advantage is not confidence or vision; it is the daily performance of the work under conditions that make it nearly impossible, and the film's honesty is in showing how much of flourishing is just showing up completely, repeatedly, with nothing in reserve.

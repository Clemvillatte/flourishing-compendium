*Song dynasty, 1079–1082 · from the Huangzhou exile poems of Su Shi, translated by Stephen Owen*

In 1079 the poet Su Shi was put on trial for verses his enemies read as mockery of the emperor's reforms. He survived, was stripped of office, and in 1080 was sent into exile at Huangzhou, a minor post with a nominal title and no stipend. A court career built over decades had reversed in a season, and the reversal left him poor enough to farm. With a friend's help he built a small residence on a parcel of land in 1081 and worked a plot called Dongpo, the Eastern Slope, a name he then took for himself. The exile that was meant to end him became his most prolific period; many of the poems considered his best were written there.

Two years in, walking in the spring rain, he wrote the short song lyric the Chinese still know by heart. The rain beats through the woods. Everyone else hurries for shelter. He keeps strolling, chanting, letting the storm soak him, and notices the storm has somehow stopped mattering.

> Pay no heed to those sounds, piercing the woods, hitting leaves--
> why should it stop me from whistling or chanting and walking slowly along?
> With my bamboo cane and sandals of straw I move more free than on horse.
> Who's afraid?
> Let my life be spent with a raincoat in the misty rain.
>
> A biting chill in the spring breeze blows me sober from wine,
> A bit cold,
> but the sunshine that sinks on the hilltop comes back to welcome me.
> Turn your head to where you just were, where the winds were howling,
> go back--
> on the one hand, it's not a storm; on the other, not clear skies.

The last line does the teaching quietly. He turns to look back at the place of howling and finds it is neither storm nor clear sky. The weather that seemed to define the day has no fixed character of its own once he stops bracing against it. What changed on the walk is not the rain. It is the man, who by 1082 had already lost nearly everything the trial could take and was still producing his best work in the mud of the Eastern Slope.

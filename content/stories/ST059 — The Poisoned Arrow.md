*Buddhist parable · India, 5th century BCE · [Cūḷa-Māluṅkyovāda Sutta, MN 63](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.063.than.html)*

A monk demands answers before he will practise: is the universe eternal or not, is the soul the same as the body, what happens after death. The Buddha replies with a parable. A man is shot with a poisoned arrow. Before he will let anyone treat him, he wants to know who shot it, from which clan, of what wood the bow was made. He will be dead before the interrogation ends.

The sutta is the tradition's charter for its own practical bias. Some questions are real and some are anaesthesia dressed as rigour. The distinction is whether answering them would remove the arrow.

*Philosophical metaphor · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

Aristotle's founding claim about moral development: as we become builders by building and lyre-players by playing the lyre, so we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts. The child does not first understand justice and then act; the acting, repeated under guidance, produces the disposition.

The sentence is the whole habituation theory in one line, and it is why the Ethics devotes two books to it. Its consequence is the reversal of the modern intuition that you must mean it first: Aristotle's claim is that the meaning is produced by the practice, and that waiting for authentic motivation is like waiting to be musical before touching the instrument.

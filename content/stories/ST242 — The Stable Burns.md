*Historical/biographical narrative · China, 5th century BCE · [Analects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analects_(Confucius))*

When the stable burned, Confucius asked: was anyone hurt? He did not ask about the horses. The Analects records this in a single line, and the line's economy is the point: the order of the two questions is the whole of the ethics, and the Analects preserves it as the master's spontaneous conduct rather than as a stated principle.

The detail that gives the anecdote its force is the economic reality: horses were expensive property, and the loss of a stable's horses was a serious material blow. Confucius asks after the grooms, the lowest-status people in the compound. The tradition treats this as ren, humaneness, operating without deliberation, which is the Confucian definition of the cultivated person: the right concern arrives first, unbidden.

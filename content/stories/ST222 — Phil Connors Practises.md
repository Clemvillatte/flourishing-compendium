*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1993 · [Groundhog Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundhog_Day)*

Phil Connors is trapped in a repeating day, and the film is the most complete popular depiction of philosophical exercise ever made. His curriculum is a Stoic's: first the indulgences fail, then the despair, then the manipulation, then, in the film's long middle, the daily practice, piano, French, ice sculpture, the saving of lives and the small kindnesses, done with no audience and no reward, since the day resets. The film's theological and Buddhist readings are deserved, but its mechanics are askesis: character is built by repetition, and the repetition is available in a day identical to this one.

The film's final movement is the release, and it comes without explanation, which is the point. The day never released its grip because Phil earned it; the practice simply became what he was, and the loop, with nothing left to teach, ended.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2000 · [Erin Brockovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Brockovich)*

Erin Brockovich, an unemployed single mother with no legal training, is doing clerical work at a small law firm when she notices medical records in a real-estate file. She investigates: Pacific Gas and Electric has leaked hexavalent chromium into Hinkley's water, told residents it was fine, and bought the sick families' silence with small payments. She interviews hundreds of plaintiffs and becomes the case's engine, and the settlement, $333 million, was the largest of its kind in US history.

The film's Aristotelian content is equity as a discovery procedure. The law as it stood had produced a systematic wrong; the correction came from outside the profession, through a person without standing reading the files no lawyer had read, which is epieikeia in its least glamorous form.

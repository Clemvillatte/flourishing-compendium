*Biographical/cultural narrative, cross-cultural · Japan, 16th century · [Sen no Rikyū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sen_no_Riky%C5%AB)*

Rikyū perfected the Japanese tea ceremony as a discipline of poverty and attention: a hut approached by crouching, a single flower rather than a display, utensils chosen for their understatement. A famous story has him trimming the garden to perfection, then shaking a tree so that a few leaves fell scattered, completing it. The aesthetic he defined, wabi, finds beauty in the worn, the irregular, and the unfinished.

His influence survives in every minimalism since, and his death is part of the story: ordered by his lord to commit ritual suicide, he did. The tradition remembers both, the quiet room and the cost of it, and refuses to separate them.

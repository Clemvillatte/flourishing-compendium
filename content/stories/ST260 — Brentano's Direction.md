*Historical/biographical narrative · Austria, 1874 · [Franz Brentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Brentano)*

Brentano, in Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint, revives the medieval scholastic term intentionality for a modern purpose: every mental act is directed at an object. Hearing is of a sound, fearing is of a danger, judging is of a state of affairs. There is no state of consciousness that is not about something, and this directedness is the mark of the mental.

The thesis is the foundation on which Husserl built phenomenology and, through Brentano's students, which included Freud, Husserl, and Meinong, a surprising share of twentieth-century thought. Its practical content is the inseparability of mind and world: a mind is not a container with contents but a set of directions, and to examine a mind is to examine what it is aimed at.

*Māori mythological narrative · New Zealand · [Māori mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_mythology)*

In the Māori creation traditions, the atua Tāne fashions the first woman, Hine-ahu-one, from the earth at Kurawaka, and breathes life into her. She is the ancestress of humanity, and the genealogies, whakapapa, descend from her. In the traditions' most famous continuation, her daughter Hine-tītama becomes Hine-nui-te-pō, the guardian of the night and of the dead, the place to which all people return.

The narrative's content is the whakapapa system itself: identity as descent, relation, and continuity with land and ancestors, rather than as individual substance. A person is a point in a living line, and the first woman's being made of earth is the tradition's statement that the line runs through the land.

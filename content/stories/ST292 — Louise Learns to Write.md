*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2016 · [Arrival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrival)*

Louise Banks, a linguist, is asked to communicate with aliens whose writing is circular, whole at once, with no tense. The film's science, based on the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis in its strong form, is contested, and its narrative consequence is the point: as Louise learns the language, she begins to experience time as the aliens do, and she finds herself with memories, or foreknowledge, of a daughter who will die young. She tells her husband about the future loss, and he leaves. She has the child anyway.

The film is the limit situation, the certain foreknowledge of loss, made literal by a change in language. Its content is the existential thesis in its most compressed form: the value of a choice is not changed by knowing its outcome, and the affirmation of a life that contains the loss is not optimism but the only available freedom.

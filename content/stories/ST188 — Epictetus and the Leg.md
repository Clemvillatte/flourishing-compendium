*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece/Rome, 1st century CE · [Epictetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epictetus)*

Epictetus, born a slave, is reported by later sources to have been lamed by his master, with the story that he said, as the leg was being broken, that he was telling him it would break, and then, when it did, that he had told him so. The anecdote's historicity is uncertain; the tradition preserved it because it dramatised the teaching, and Epictetus himself discusses his lameness without self-pity.

The story's content is not stoicism as endurance but the dichotomy stated in the flesh. The leg, the master's violence, the social status: not up to him. The response, the speech, the character: up to him, and nothing else is. The tradition's most famous teacher is a man whose body was the site of his philosophy's proof.

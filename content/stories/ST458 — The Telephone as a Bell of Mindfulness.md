Dharma talk · Thich Nhat Hanh, Plum Village, July 30, 1998; a practice described in its own words, with the telephone, the clock, and the watch as bells

The talk opens by naming the practice: "Every time you hear the bell, you shouldn't do anything, you shouldn't think of anything, you shouldn't say anything. You have to go back to your in-breath and out-breath, and listen very deeply." ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm))

The practice extends to the most ordinary sound in the building. Nhat Hanh says: "We have been practicing telephone meditation for, I think, fourteen years now in Plum Village." He adds, "The sound of the telephone does not irritate us anymore, because we can consider it to be a bell of mindfulness." ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm)) The sequence he describes is a working instruction for the breath: "When the first sound is heard, we stop talking, we stop thinking, we enjoy our in-breath and our out-breath, and we smile." The third ring changes the activity without ending the breathing: "When the third sound is heard, you can stand up, and you can walk in the direction of the telephone, but you do it calmly, breathing and smiling while walking" ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm))

The talk extends the trigger to other sounds. "In every dining hall here in Plum Village there is a clock, and when the clock starts to play music, everyone stops eating, and just listens to the clock very deeply, just as they listen to the bell or the voice of the Buddha." A watch works the same way; and the practice runs into ordinary work: "They have learned how to breathe in and out three times before each phone call," said of business people who make "five or six or seven telephone calls in a row" ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm)).

The talk also proposes the practice for the household: "It would be very helpful if everyone in the family signed a treaty, that every time the telephone rings, everyone in the family would stay still and enjoy breathing in and breathing out, for at least two rings of the telephone" ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm)).

The ringing telephone becomes a reminder to pause and notice breathing before answering. Nhat Hanh describes a practice for ordinary life, rather than evidence establishing how reliably it produces calm. ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm))

**Principles:**
- [[B190 — The breath is always available|B190 The breath is always available]]: the talk's telephone, clock, and watch passages illustrate returning attention to breathing during ordinary activities; they do not establish that the breath is available in every circumstance, and the talk's description of the practice is not treated as evidence of its outcomes. ([Nhat Hanh, 1998](https://www.abuddhistlibrary.com/Buddhism/G%20-%20TNH/TNH/Cultivating%20Mindfulness%20in%20a%20Sangha/Dharma%20Talk%20given%20by%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh%20on%20July%2030%201998.htm))

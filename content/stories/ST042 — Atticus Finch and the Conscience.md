*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1960 · [To Kill a Mockingbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Kill_a_Mockingbird)*

Atticus Finch agrees to defend Tom Robinson, a Black man falsely accused in Depression-era Alabama. He knows he will lose; he tells Scout that before he can live with other folks, he has to live with himself, and the one thing that doesn't abide by majority rule is a person's conscience. He takes the case and, in the novel's most quietly radical act, is unfailingly courteous to the men who come to lynch his client.

The principle the character carries is non-contention without capitulation. He neither wins nor yields. He keeps his own conduct intact in a losing cause, and the story's power comes from refusing to console us about the loss.

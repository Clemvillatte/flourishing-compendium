*Scientific narrative · United States, 2010 · [Julianne Holt-Lunstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianne_Holt-Lunstad)*

Holt-Lunstad, Smith, and Layton's meta-analysis, covering 308,849 participants across 148 studies, found that the presence of social relationships predicted mortality: people with stronger social relationships had a 50 per cent increased likelihood of survival, an effect comparable to smoking cessation and exceeding the effects of physical inactivity and obesity. The mechanisms, including stress regulation and health behaviour, are studied separately.

The finding's reception, including the UK's appointment of a minister for loneliness and the WHO's commission, is part of its history. Its content for this volume is the convergence of two traditions: the relational philosophies' claim that a person is constituted in relation, and the empirical finding that the relation is not merely good for the person but a condition of the person's continuing.

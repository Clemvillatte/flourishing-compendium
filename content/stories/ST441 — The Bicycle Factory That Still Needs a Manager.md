Contemporary ACT · David Gillanders, The Bicycle Factory Metaphor

A bicycle factory is running well. Its manager sees the whole operation, catches developing problems and keeps the work supplied. Gillanders then asks what would happen if the manager took six months away precisely because everything seemed fine. The imagined holiday raises questions about what would happen to the factory's orders and supplies ([author's text](https://contextualscience.org/files/The%20Bicycle%20Factory%20Metaphor.docx)).

Gillanders offers a less demanding alternative: the manager can work part time while still giving the factory regular attention. He uses this image near the end of therapy to discuss continuing the skills that have been useful ([author's text](https://contextualscience.org/files/The%20Bicycle%20Factory%20Metaphor.docx)).

> by their continuous attention they keep things running well.
>
> Gillanders, The Bicycle Factory Metaphor ([author's text](https://contextualscience.org/files/The%20Bicycle%20Factory%20Metaphor.docx)).

An editorial application is to choose one practice worth maintaining, such as a weekly conversation with a friend. Try a manageable frequency, notice whether it supports the relationship, and adjust it before treating a missed week as a verdict on the whole friendship. Motivation and overt behaviour are the dimensions to examine here.

The manager's part-time option matters for this application. Continuing a practice can include reducing its burden, asking for help or changing its form. The metaphor gives no reason to demand endless vigilance or to blame someone when an illness persists.

**Principles:**
- [[P162 — Take committed action — behavior is where values become real]]: the factory adds the maintenance angle, when a practice must continue after its first apparent success.

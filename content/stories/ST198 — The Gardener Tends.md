*Stoic metaphor · Greece/Rome, 1st century BCE · [Cicero, De Finibus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero)*

The Stoic sage, Cicero explains, does not withdraw from public life; the natural affections and the social structure of the cosmos pull him toward it. He tends the garden of the commonwealth, and if the harvest fails, if the drought comes, the failure of the crop was never in the gardener's power. His work was the tending.

The image's practical content is the refusal of both withdrawal and attachment. The sage is not a monk and not a striver; he performs his role, family, city, craft, completely, and the outcome is the weather's. It is the discipline of desire and the discipline of action in one picture: care completely about the work, not at all about the harvest.

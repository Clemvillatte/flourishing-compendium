*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United Kingdom, 1981 · [Chariots of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chariots_of_Fire)*

Two men prepare for the 1924 Olympics. Harold Abrahams, a Jew at Cambridge, runs because of antisemitism, to be someone against those who would keep him no one; he runs with clenched jaw and hires a professional coach, an ethics scandal for the amateur code. Eric Liddell, a Scot bound for missionary work, runs because he feels God's pleasure when he runs; when the heats are scheduled on a Sunday, he refuses, and switches to an event he had not trained for, and wins it.

The film's Aristotelian structure is exact: the same activity, excellence at the highest level, is a different activity depending on the reason for which it is done. Abrahams' running, as he admits, is compensation; Liddell's is an expression of a complete life. Both win gold, and the film does not pretend the difference does not matter.

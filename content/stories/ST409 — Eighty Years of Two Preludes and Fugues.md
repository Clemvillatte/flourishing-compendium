Biographical account · Pablo Casals (1876-1973), the Catalan cellist who brought the Bach Cello Suites back into the world's repertoire, told through his own memoir passages and the record of his return to the White House at 84.

Casals reached for most of a century. He performed in the United States almost yearly from 1904 to 1928 ([White House Historical Association](https://www.whitehousehistory.org/distinguished-cellists-in-the-white-house)). Then came the Spanish Civil War, and he spent the rest of his life in self-imposed exile, refusing to play in countries that recognized Franco's Spain; he had not performed in the United States since fleeing in 1939, apart from a 1958 United Nations concert ([White House Historical Association](https://www.whitehousehistory.org/distinguished-cellists-in-the-white-house)). Years of silence in the French village of Prades sat inside that span. What carried him through it was not a grand project. "For the past eighty years I have started each day in the same manner... I go to the piano, and I play two preludes and fugues of Bach. I cannot think of doing otherwise. It is a sort of benediction on the house" ([CelloBello, from Joys and Sorrows](https://cellobello.org/legacy-cellists/pablo-pau-casals-core-principles/)).

The return was the instrument's own light reverting to its source. "It is a rediscovery of the world of which I have the joy of being a part. It fills me with awareness of the wonder of life, with a feeling of the incredible marvel of being a human being" ([CelloBello](https://cellobello.org/legacy-cellists/pablo-pau-casals-core-principles/)). And the source never went stale: "The music is never the same for me, never. Each day is something new, fantastic, unbelievable. That is Bach, like nature, a miracle!" ([CelloBello](https://cellobello.org/legacy-cellists/pablo-pau-casals-core-principles/)).

The consequence of a daily return is a different kind of strength. "Each day I am reborn. Each day I must begin again" ([CelloBello](https://cellobello.org/legacy-cellists/pablo-pau-casals-core-principles/)). At 84, Casals played the East Room of the White House for President Kennedy, his first appearance there since Theodore Roosevelt's administration in 1904 ([New York Times, 14 Nov 1961](https://www.nytimes.com/1961/11/14/archives/casals-plays-at-white-house-last-appeared-there-in-1904-casals.html)). The program held Mendelssohn, Schumann and Couperin, and "the program was entirely serious, even if Señor Casals did not play any of his beloved Bach" ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/1961/11/14/archives/casals-plays-at-white-house-last-appeared-there-in-1904-casals.html)). That is the shape of D065: the public light was for the day's reaching; the private one, returned to every morning, was what kept it burning.

Not carried: DDJ 52's metaphysics of the mother and the sons; the morning ritual here is a musician's, not the principle's meditation check.

**Principles:**
- [[D065 — Use the light, return to the insight]]: Casals used his light by reverting daily to its source — two preludes and fugues each morning, a benediction that renewed the reaching of a long life through exile and silence, so that at 84 the public light still burned.

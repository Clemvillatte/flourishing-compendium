Biographical account · George Fox (1624-1691), founder of the Society of Friends, from his own Journal.

The set-up is a mind pulled in every outward direction. Fox had left home at nineteen and tried every authority the age offered: "I had forsaken the priests... so I left the separate preachers also, and those esteemed the most experienced people; for I saw there was none among them all that could speak to my condition. When all my hopes in them and in all men were gone, so that I had nothing outwardly to help me, nor could I tell what to do" ([Journal, ch. I](https://ccel.org/ccel/fox_g/autobio/autobio.vi.html)). That is the Qingjing Jing's pulled mind, seeking its quiet in the wrong direction.

What stopped the pulling was an opening. "I saw, also, that there was an ocean of darkness and death; but an infinite ocean of light and love, which flowed over the ocean of darkness. In that also I saw the infinite love of God, and I had great openings" ([Journal, ch. I](https://ccel.org/ccel/fox_g/autobio/autobio.vi.html)). The light was already flowing over the darkness when he saw it. Fox also reports the voice: "There is one, even Christ Jesus, that can speak to thy condition'; and when I heard it, my heart did leap for joy" ([Journal, ch. I](https://ccel.org/ccel/fox_g/autobio/autobio.vi.html)).

His instruction to others kept the same shape. To troubled people he prescribed standing still: "their first step to peace, even to stand still in the Light that showed them their sins and transgressions" ([Journal, ch. VI](https://ccel.org/ccel/fox_g/autobio/autobio.xi.html)). The stillness was what remained when the seeking outward stopped.

The consequence gathered into a movement. In 1652 he climbed Pendle Hill: "From the top of this hill the Lord let me see in what places he had a great people to be gathered" ([Journal, ch. VI](https://ccel.org/ccel/fox_g/autobio/autobio.xi.html)). The people he gathered became the Society of Friends; Quakers in Britain describe their worship today: "We first gather together in silence to quiet our minds – we don't have set hymns, prayers or sermons" ([Quakers in Britain](https://www.quaker.org.uk/faith/our-faith/how-quakers-worship)). The compendium reads Fox for D041 by comparison: the ocean of light over the darkness plays, in the Qingjing Jing's terms, the stillness already present beneath the pull, and the silent worship the tradition describes is the standing still that lets it act.

Not carried: the Qingjing Jing's plain-words steps for household use (toss out three, pick up one), and its cosmology of clear and turbid qi; Fox's opening is devotional and Christ-shaped, not metaphysical.

**Principles:**
- [[D041 — Practice purity and stillness (qingjing)]]: Fox found the stillness already given — light flowing over darkness, a voice that spoke to his condition — and taught standing still in it rather than forcing quiet; the gathered people's silent worship is that stillness made communal.

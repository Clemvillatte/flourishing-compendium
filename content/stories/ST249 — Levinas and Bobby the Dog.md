*Biographical narrative · France, 20th century · [Emmanuel Levinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Levinas)*

Levinas, a French Jewish prisoner of war held in a German labour camp, describes the dog named Bobby who greeted the prisoners each day as they marched to work. The guards did not distinguish the prisoners as persons; Bobby did, and the prisoners, in their ununiformed rags, were recognisable to the dog as people. Levinas's essay "The Name of a Dog, or Natural Rights" names Bobby, in a deliberate provocation, the last Kantian in Nazi Germany.

The essay is a fragment of Levinas's larger argument that ethics precedes philosophy, that the face of the other places a demand on me before any reasoning. The dog's place in it is precise: Bobby's greeting demonstrated that recognition is a fact about the recognizer, and that the guards' failure to see persons was not an intellectual error but the failure of a relation.

*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1901-1902 · [Wright brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_brothers)*

Having watched their gliders perform far worse than the published tables predicted, Wilbur and Orville Wright do not proceed by talent. They build a six-foot wind tunnel in their bicycle shop and, over about two months, test roughly 200 miniature wing shapes and 38 airfoil designs, publishing a new table of lift coefficients that corrected the work of the era's leading authorities. Their 1902 glider, built on the corrected numbers, flew as calculated.

The wind tunnel is a small object with a large lesson. The brothers' advantage over better-funded competitors was not courage or genius; it was their refusal to trust either authority or their own impressions when a cheap experiment could answer the question. The aeroplane is downstream of the tunnel.

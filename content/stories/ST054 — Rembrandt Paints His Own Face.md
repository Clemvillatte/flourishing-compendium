*Artistic/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · Netherlands, 1660s · [Rembrandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rembrandt)*

Rembrandt painted, etched, and drew his own face roughly eighty times across his life. The late self-portraits, painted after bankruptcy and the death of nearly everyone he loved, are the reason the self-portrait series is studied: he records the face as it actually is, sagging, reddened, unflattered, with an attention so level it has been described as X-ray-like. There is no vanity left and no self-punishment either. He just looks.

Most portraiture before and since negotiates with the sitter's vanity. The late Rembrandts are what remains when the negotiation is abandoned, and their stillness is the rarest thing in the history of pictures: a person looking at himself without argument.

*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 2009 · [James C. Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_C._Kaufman)*

Kaufman and Beghetto's "Beyond Big and Little" proposes a four-level model of creativity: mini-c, the novel and personally meaningful learning of a child or student; little-c, everyday creativity, the everyday problem-solving and expression; Pro-c, the professional expertise of the developed practitioner; and Big-C, the eminent achievement that changes a field. The model's point is that creativity is not one thing reserved for geniuses, and that the everyday forms are real, distinct, and developmentally connected.

The paper's practical content is a claim about the ordinary person's relation to the creative: mini-c, the learning that involves interpretation and originality, is available to anyone, and the path from mini-c to the higher levels is a developmental question, not a talent question. The model is now the field's standard framework for creativity's scope.

*Zhuangzi parable · China, 4th century BCE · [Zhuangzi, ch. 17, "Autumn Floods"](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/the-floods-of-autumn)*

Zhuangzi fishes at the Pu River when two officials arrive with an offer from the King of Chu to become a minister. Zhuangzi asks: I have heard of a sacred tortoise, dead three thousand years, kept in a box in the temple, honoured. Would that tortoise rather be dead and honoured, or alive, dragging its tail in the mud? The officials say alive, in the mud. Zhuangzhou says: go away, I'll drag my tail in the mud.

The refusal is not piety or laziness. It is a judgement about what vitality is worth, and what usually gets traded for prestige. The tortoise in the temple has been exchanged for a box.

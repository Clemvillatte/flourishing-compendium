*Therapeutic metaphor · 2009 · retold from Russ Harris, ACT Made Simple*

The weather today may be terrible — a thunderstorm, a gale, a week of grey. And the sky above it is exactly as large as it was before the weather started. The sky does not fight the weather; it has room for all of it, and it is still sky when the storm has passed.

Your thoughts and feelings are the weather: they arrive without permission, they are often dark, and they change. The part of you that notices them is the sky. The image offers a way to notice a feeling without being only the feeling — to be aware of sadness without the awareness itself being sad.

Sometimes clouds cover everything and you forget the sky exists. The forgetting is weather too.

Retold from Harris (2009, p. 175), as presented in Stoddard & Afari, *The Big Book of ACT Metaphors* (2014), pp. 120–121.

*Historical narrative · Europe, medieval and early modern · [Guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guild)*

The apprentice in a medieval guild does not begin by making the masterpiece. He sweeps the shop, holds the tools, watches the master work, and performs fragments of the craft for years before being trusted with a whole one. The guild's structure, with its progression from apprentice to journeyman to master, was built on the assumption that skill is acquired through graded, supervised repetition.

The system is the institutional form of habituation, and its survival for centuries is evidence of its effectiveness. Its modern descendants, the medical residency, the judicial clerkship, the trade certification, all preserve the same logic: the practice comes before the licence, because the licence certifies a disposition, not a knowledge.

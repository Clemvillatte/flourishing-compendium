*Documentary narrative, cross-cultural · Japan/United States, 2011 · [Jiro Dreams of Sushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiro_Dreams_of_Sushi)*


Jiro Ono, 85, is regarded by many as the world's greatest sushi chef. His restaurant, in a Tokyo subway station, seats ten, and holds three Michelin stars. He has made sushi for over seventy years, and describes his approach in the film: I do the same thing over and over, improving bit by bit, and there is always a yearning to achieve more. His elder son, Yoshikazu, is 50 and still has not taken over, and the film's most quietly painful thread is the son's lifetime of preparation.

The documentary is the single most-watched study of mastery, and its content is the engagement literature in biography: the deepening is not accumulation but refinement, the same act performed with increasing discrimination, and the flow state, for Jiro, is not a peak but the ordinary condition of a life organised around the deepening of craft.

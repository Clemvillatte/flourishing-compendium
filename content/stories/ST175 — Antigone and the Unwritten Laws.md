*Literary/mythological narrative · Greece, 5th century BCE · [Sophocles, Antigone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigone)*

Creon forbids the burial of Antigone's brother as a traitor. Antigone buries him, is caught, and defends herself with the line that has structured natural-law thinking since: his edict was not the law she answers to, since no mortal proclamation can override the unwritten laws of the gods, which have no yesterday and no tomorrow. Creon holds; Antigone hangs herself; Creon's son and wife follow her.

The play is the founding text of the tension between legal justice and a higher order, and its Aristotelian content is epieikeia, the virtue that corrects the law where the law, being general, errs in the particular. Creon is not stupid or wicked in the ordinary way; he is a man applying the rule, and Sophocles' achievement is making that the tragedy.

*Scientific narrative · Italy, 2025 · [Character strengths](https://www.viacharacter.org/character-strengths)*

Casali and Feraco's meta-analysis synthesised 130 studies with a combined sample of 275,007 participants, examining the associations between character strengths and well-being. The findings: all strengths except humility showed meaningful associations with well-being, and the strongest correlates were hope, zest, curiosity, and love of learning. The strengths' relationships to different well-being outcomes varied, with some strengths predicting life satisfaction more strongly than others.

The analysis is the largest to date, and its content is the field's current best answer to the question of which virtues matter most for flourishing. The finding's interest for this volume is the convergence: hope and zest, the top correlates, are the strengths the Stoic and existential traditions respectively emphasise, and the study's humility anomaly, the one strength not predicting well-being by self-report, is a known measurement issue, since humility is the strength least visible to its owner.

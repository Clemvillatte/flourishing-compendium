*Historical/biographical narrative · Austria, 1946 · [Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)*

Man's Search for Meaning, the memoir of the camps and the theory of logotherapy, argues that the will to meaning is the primary human motivation, and that its frustration is a clinical condition, which Frankl treated in his post-war practice. The book's famous Nietzschean line, that he who has a why to live can bear almost any how, is quoted more than any other in the modern well-being literature, and Frankl's own use of it is careful: meaning does not remove suffering, it changes the relation to it.

The book's clinical legacy is meaning-centred therapy and, in the research literature, the finding that purpose in life predicts health outcomes longitudinally. Its content for this volume is the bridging claim: the existential tradition's limit situations and the positive psychology of meaning are the same subject, approached from the two ends.

Educational narrative · John Dewey, The School and Society, chapter I

A group of children spends thirty minutes removing cotton fibres from their seeds and gets less than an ounce. Dewey places this small result within a sequence of textile lessons: the children handle materials, compare their properties and work out how tools help. For P042, the learning lies in noticing what an action produces and using that observation to guide the next attempt. ([Dewey, pp. 34-37](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1907/Dewey_1907a.html))

Dewey introduces the episode as actual school work. Boys and girls work with flax, cotton and wool as it comes from the sheep. The subject might look like narrow preparation for sewing and mending, but he wants the activity to open questions about the materials, mechanisms and social arrangements involved in making cloth. ([Dewey, pp. 33-34](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1907/Dewey_1907a.html))

Removing cotton seeds by hand gives the group a difficulty to examine. The small quantity they obtain makes the labour of preparation apparent. They compare cotton and wool, including differences in the fibres and how readily they hold together when spun; Dewey credits their work with the materials, while naming the help they receive: "The children worked this out for themselves with the actual material, aided by questions and suggestions from the teacher." ([Dewey, pp. 34-35](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1907/Dewey_1907a.html))

The sequence continues into making tools. Dewey says they devise a frame for carding wool from two boards with sharp pins, then reconstruct simple ways of spinning. A weight draws out the wool as it turns; another method uses a top spun on the floor while the children hold the wool and let it wind onto the top. ([Dewey, pp. 35-36](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1907/Dewey_1907a.html))

From these attempts, the lessons move through further inventions towards the loom, examining what each mechanism does and what it changes. Dewey describes the occupation widening into the study of growing regions, distribution, machinery and history. The handwork becomes a means of investigating how people make and obtain their clothing. ([Dewey, pp. 36-37](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1907/Dewey_1907a.html))

Earlier in the chapter, he distinguishes the school's purpose from the economic value of its products: the aim is "the development of social power and insight." Read alongside P042, the modest cotton trial matters because its consequences can be examined and connected to further questions. The account keeps the teacher's guidance and the children's reflection inside the activity. ([Dewey, pp. 32 and 35-37](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1907/Dewey_1907a.html))

**Principles:**
- [[P042 — Learn by doing|P042 Learn by doing]]: direct work with materials produces consequences the learners compare, discuss and use in further experiments.

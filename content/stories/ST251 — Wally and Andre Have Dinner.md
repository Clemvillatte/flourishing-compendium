*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1981 · [My Dinner with Andre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Dinner_with_Andre)*

The entire film is a dinner conversation. Andre Gregory, a theatre director recently returned from years of experimental and spiritual quests, talks; Wallace Shawn, an actor worried about money and sceptical of the quests, listens, questions, and pushes back. The topics include a Scottish forest workshop, a birthday party staged as a ritual, and whether Shawn's comfortable New York life is a kind of sleep. They part, and Shawn, walking home in the rain, narrates his intention to tell his wife everything.

The film's premise, two men talking for two hours, was considered unreleasable, and it became a cult classic precisely because the premise is the content. The dialogue is the relation: no position is adopted, no argument is won, and the film's meaning is produced by the encounter itself, which is Buber's and Marcel's thesis in cinematic form, that the conversation is not a means of exchange but a place where two realities touch.

*Philosophical metaphor · France, 1889 · [Henri Bergson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)*

Bergson asks us to listen to a melody. The notes are not heard as discrete points; the first is still sounding, changed, when the second arrives, and they interpenetrate. This is durée, lived duration, which is not clock time. Clock time, spatialised and divisible, is a convenience for science; it is not what a life is made of.

The distinction sounds soft until it is applied. In Time and Free Will Bergson uses it to argue that the self we measure, the self of schedules and inventories, is a flattened version of the one that lives in duration, and that much modern unhappiness is the confusion of the two.

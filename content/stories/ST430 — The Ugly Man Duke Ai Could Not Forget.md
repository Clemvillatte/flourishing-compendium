Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 5, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Duke Ai of Lu tells Confucius about a man in Wei named Aitai Tuo. "Frightening" to look at, he never took the lead in a conversation: "he just agreed with other people." He held no office that could save anyone from death and had no wealth to fill anyone's belly. Yet people who were with him could not leave, and in Eno's telling a dozen women had begged their parents to let them be his concubine rather than the wife of anyone else ([Eno, §5.4](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). Legge has it that his father-in-law could not be away from him and that his wife was ready to be his concubine. In Legge, Duke Ai says he was "ugly enough, moreover, to scare the whole world" ([Legge, ch. 5 §4](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The duke summoned him to court. Within a month he felt something special about him, and within a year he had complete faith in him. When the chief minister's post fell vacant, he offered it to Aitai Tuo. He answered only after sitting in silence, then accepted so casually that it seemed almost like declining. The duke took this for humility and handed over the state. Soon Aitai Tuo left. The duke was "overcome with depression, as though someone close to me had died" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Confucius answers with piglets, which in Eno's version he saw in Chu, suckling at a dead sow. After a little they all awoke with alarm and fled. "What they had loved in their mother was not her form but the thing that moved her form." Aitai Tuo, he says, is trusted before he speaks. His abilities are whole because he does not let fate's turns (death, wealth, slander, hunger) enter the "spirit storehouse." "Nothing is so level as water at rest, and it can serve as a model for man: preserve what is within and let nothing outside stir you" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

The compendium reads this beside D096 and D163. The duke's grief shows that what drew him was not what he could see.

Limits: Aitai Tuo is seen only through the duke's report and Confucius' reading, and never speaks. The text does not say why he leaves, or what happened to the state. Eno has Chu where Legge has Qi for the piglets' sighting, and the two differ on who is attracted to him. The story also repeats a trope of the unusual body as the sign of unusual virtue, which the compendium does not endorse as a rule.

**Principles:**
- [[D096 — Don't let the outer displace the inner]]: the duke's disquiet when appearance and effect diverge.
- [[D163 — Carry jade in coarse cloth]]: a man with nothing visible, who is offered a state and leaves.

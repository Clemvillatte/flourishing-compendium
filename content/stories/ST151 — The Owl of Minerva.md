*Philosophical metaphor · Germany, 1820 · [Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Wilhelm_Friedrich_Hegel)*

Hegel closes the Preface to the Philosophy of Right with the image: the owl of Minerva spreads its wings only with the falling of the dusk. Philosophy, he means, comprehends a form of life only when it is finished. Understanding arrives, always, after.

The sentence is usually quoted as a complaint about philosophy's timing, but its practical interest is different. It is the honest limit of intellectual virtue as a guide to living: understanding is retrospective, and the person who demands full comprehension before acting has misunderstood the human situation. Wisdom here includes knowing that the dusk is when the wisdom arrives.

*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 6th century BCE · [Solon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon)*

Croesus, king of Lydia, shows Solon his treasury and asks who is the happiest of men, expecting to hear his own name. Solon names Tellus of Athens: he lived in a prosperous city, had fine children, saw grandchildren, and died fighting for his city. Pressed for second place, he names two young men who died with honour after carrying their mother. Call no man happy until he is dead, Solon concludes. Count no man happy until you have seen his end.

Herodotus frames the story with Croesus' later capture and near-execution on a pyre, where he calls Solon's name. The tale is the earliest and most durable warning against measuring a life before it is finished, and Aristotle treats exactly this as the reason [[Eudaimonia|eudaimonia]] must be a whole-life property.

*Zhuangzi parable · China, 4th century BCE · [Zhuangzi, ch. 18, "Perfect Happiness"](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/perfect-enjoyment)*

Zhuangzi sees a skull, dry and hollow, and pokes it with his riding crop, asking whether it brought itself to this through greed, ambition, or shame. That night the skull appears in his dream. There is no ruler above or servant below in the land of the dead, he says, no labour and no seasons. The happiness of a king on his throne does not compare. Zhuangzi offers to restore him to life; the skull declines with the look of a man refusing a promotion.

Read carefully, the story is not an argument that death is better. It is an argument against the assumption, made without inspection, that aliveness is the only valuable state.

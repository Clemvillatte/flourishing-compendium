*Film narrative, cross-cultural · Japan, 1997 · [Hayao Miyazaki, Princess Mononoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Mononoke)*

Ashitaka, a young prince cursed by a demon boar, travels west to find the source of the iron that poisoned the boar's rage. He finds a war: Irontown, the refuge and workshop of Lady Eboshi, cutting the forest for iron, against the forest gods, led by the wolf-girl San, raised by wolves and at war with all humans. Ashitaka does not take a side. He saves Eboshi's workers and is shot by San's wolves, and his stated project is to see with eyes unclouded by hate. The Forest Spirit's death, the decapitation of the deer god by Eboshi's gun, produces the film's apocalyptic climax, and the head is returned, and the forest begins to grow again, and the film ends without a victory for either side.

Miyazaki's refusal of the villain is the film's philosophy. Eboshi is cruel to the forest and the only person in the story who employs lepers and buys the freedom of brothel women; San is fierce and unbalanced. The ecological relation, the film argues, is not a problem to be solved by identifying the bad party; it is a tension to be lived, and the seeing of it without hate is the only available starting point.

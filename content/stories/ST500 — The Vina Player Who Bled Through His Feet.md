India, trad. 5th century BCE · sutta narrative from the Sona Sutta (AN 6.55), translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu, with the principle file in the compendium working tree

Ven. Sona had been doing walking meditation until, in the translation's bracketed note, "the skin of his soles was split & bleeding", and his mind had not reached release ([AN 6.55, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)). Secluded, he counted his options out loud to himself: "Of the Blessed One's disciples who have aroused their persistence, I am one", and still nothing. "Now, my family has enough wealth that it would be possible to enjoy wealth & make merit. What if I were to disavow the training, return to the lower life, enjoy wealth, & make merit?" ([AN 6.55, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)). The plan was to quit.

The Buddha, perceiving the train of thought, appeared before him in the Cool Wood and repeated it back to him ([AN 6.55, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)). Then he asked a householder's question: "Before, when you were a house-dweller, were you skilled at playing the vina?" Sona had been. "And what do you think: when the strings of your vina were too taut, was your vina in tune & playable?" No, lord. "And what do you think: when the strings of your vina were neither too taut nor too loose, but tuned to be right on pitch, was your vina in tune & playable?" Yes, lord ([AN 6.55, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)).

The application is one sentence: "In the same way, Sona, over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness, overly slack persistence leads to laziness. Thus you should determine the right pitch for your persistence, attune the pitch of the [five] faculties [to that], and there pick up your theme" ([AN 6.55, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)). The sutta then reports the part a modern reader might miss: the Buddha did not stay to supervise. "So after that, Ven. Sona determined the right pitch for his persistence, attuned the pitch of the [five] faculties [to that], and there picked up his theme", and dwelling alone, heedful and resolute, he became "another one of the arahants" ([AN 6.55, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)).

The compendium's reading for B036: the Buddha moved the question from how hard to how tuned. Sona's problem was not too little effort; his feet were bleeding. The instruction names two ways to fail and hands the determination of pitch back to the practitioner, whose own former craft supplies the standard. The psychology parallel on the principle page, the inverted-U of optimal arousal, is not carried by this story.

**Principles:**
- [[B036 — Tune, don't force — the lute string|B036 Tune, don't force: the lute string]]: the Sona Sutta's narrative frame shows the simile given to a man whose effort was already past the breaking point; the adjustment is his own act after instruction, and the two failure modes and the pitch standard are the sutta's own terms.

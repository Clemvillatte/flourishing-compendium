*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States/China, 2019 · [Lulu Wang, The Farewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Farewell)*

Billi, a Chinese-American writer in New York, learns that her grandmother, Nai Nai, in Changchun, has been diagnosed with terminal cancer, and that the family has decided not to tell her. The diagnosis is delivered to the family, and a wedding is hastily organised as the pretext for everyone to gather and say goodbye. Billi, raised on American norms of autonomy and disclosure, is told: this is a Chinese family's way; the burden is shared so the patient can live her remaining time in peace.

The film is the single best study in cinema of the conflict between relational and individualist conceptions of the person. The family's withholding is not depicted as paternalism or deception but as a different account of who the illness belongs to, and the film, which is based on Wang's own family story, refuses to resolve the question for the audience.

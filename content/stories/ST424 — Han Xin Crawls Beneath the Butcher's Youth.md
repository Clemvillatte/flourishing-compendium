Historical biography · Sima Qian, Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian), ch. 92

A poor young man who carries a sword is stopped in the market of Huaiyin and given a public choice: stab me, or crawl beneath me. He bends down and crawls while the whole market laughs. Years later, as King of Chu, he summons that same challenger and gives him an officer's post. The biography of Han Xin opens with this humiliation and closes its account of it without a blow. ([Shiji ch. 92, Chinese Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

The biography first presents him as a commoner with no reputation for conduct, who could not be recommended for office and ate at other people's tables until most were tired of him. Among the women washing at the city wall, one fed him for weeks and scolded him when he promised repayment. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

Then comes the challenge from a youth of the butcher's quarter: "信能死，刺我；不能死，出我袴下。" — if you dare to die, stab me; if not, crawl out beneath my crotch. Han Xin looked closely at him, then stooped and crawled beneath his legs while the whole market laughed at him for a coward. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

The biography then follows his rise through the war, and records that in the fifth year of Han, "漢五年正月，徙齊王信爲楚王，都下邳" — Han Xin was moved from King of Qi to King of Chu. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

Back in his own country he settles the old accounts in order. The woman who had fed him receives a thousand gold; the pavilion head receives a hundred coins and a rebuke; and then this: "召辱己之少年令出胯下者以爲楚中尉。告諸將相曰：『此壯士也。方辱我時，我寧不能殺之邪？殺之無名，故忍而就於此。』" — he summoned the youth who had made him crawl out beneath his legs and made him a commandant in Chu, telling his generals and ministers: this is a brave man; when he humiliated me, could I not have killed him? To kill him would have gained no name; so I endured, and came to this. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

This page reads Han Xin's restraint as yielding in a particular confrontation. His reported explanation connects his refusal to kill with what he subsequently achieved; appointing the man resolves that earlier encounter without revenge. The episode offers no guarantee that yielding secures a person's lasting safety. The English renderings are this page's own translations of the quoted classical Chinese; the Chinese text is the primary source. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

**Principles:**
- [[D068 — Yield in conflict to remain whole|D068 Yield in conflict to remain whole]]: Han Xin gives up the street fight and keeps the life that carries his future; his later explanation connects his refusal to kill with what he subsequently achieved.
- [[D069 — Don't meet force with force|D069 Don't meet force with force]]: the youth tries to force a duel; Han Xin declines the terms of the fight itself, refusing the provocation without adopting its aggression.

*Zen parable, cross-cultural · Japan, recorded 20th century · [Zen Flesh, Zen Bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zen_Flesh,_Zen_Bones)*

A professor visits the Zen master Nan-in to learn about Zen. He talks, and Nan-in pours the tea, and keeps pouring, and the cup overflows onto the table. The professor shouts that the cup is full, no more will go in. Nan-in says: like this cup, you are full of your own opinions. How can I show you Zen unless you first empty your cup? The story is recorded in Paul Reps's collection of Zen stories, and its provenance is a modern literary one.

The parable is the phenomenological reduction told as a joke: the bracketing of what one already knows, the [[Epoché|epochē]], as a physical act. Its interest is that it identifies the obstacle precisely, not ignorance but fullness, and the professor's talking, which seemed like engagement, is the overflow.

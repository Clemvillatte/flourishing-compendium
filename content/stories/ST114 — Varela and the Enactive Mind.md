*Scientific/biographical narrative · France/United States, 1991 · [Francisco Varela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Varela)*

Varela, Thompson, and Rosch argue in The Embodied Mind that cognition is not representation. An organism does not build an internal picture of a pre-given world; it enacts a world through its embodied activity. The frog's eye does not mirror reality; it evolved to detect flies, so the frog's world literally contains fly-detections. Cognition is sense-making, and sense-making is biological.

The book also imported Buddhist practice into cognitive science, arguing that meditative experience was data about the mind, not noise. Its practical consequence for psychology is the shift from correcting beliefs to examining the activity of sense-making itself, which enactive and 4E approaches have since made a research programme.

*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1996 · [Posttraumatic growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posttraumatic_growth)*

Tedeschi and Calhoun, studying people who had experienced major life crises, found a phenomenon the pathology framework did not predict: a significant minority reported positive change after trauma, in five domains, appreciation of life, relationships with others, new possibilities, personal strength, and spiritual change. Their Posttraumatic Growth Inventory measures the reported change, and the finding has been replicated across cultures and event types.

The concept's critical history, including the debate over whether growth is real or a coping narrative, is part of the honest account, and the researchers themselves note that growth and distress co-occur. Its content for this volume is the convergence with the Stoic and Daoist traditions: adversity, the traditions claim, is not merely a threat to be survived, and the empirical finding is that a subset of people, for reasons partly studied and partly not, emerge from it changed in ways they value.

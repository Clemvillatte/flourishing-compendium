*Historical/biographical narrative · South Africa, 1995-1998 · [Desmond Tutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desmond_Tutu)*

The Truth and Reconciliation Commission, chaired by Desmond Tutu, offered amnesty in exchange for full public disclosure of political crimes. Perpetrators testified to the commission, in public, and victims testified, and the two streams were recorded in the same archive. Tutu's own behaviour in the chair, his weeping, his laughter, his occasional fury, is part of the record, and his account, No Future Without Forgiveness, argues that the alternative, Nuremberg-style trials, would have traded truth for punishment in a country that could have survived neither.

The commission's design is ubuntu as institutional architecture: the wrong is understood as a rupture in a web that includes both parties, and the repair must be public, witnessed, and relational, not adjudicated between strangers. The model has been exported and contested, and both the export and the contest are part of its history.

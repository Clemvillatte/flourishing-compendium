*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece/Rome, 1st century CE · [Epictetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epictetus)*

Epictetus's iron lamp is stolen. He replaces it with an earthenware one, cheaper, and no one steals it. In Discourses I.18, he recounts this with the observation that the thief paid a price for the lamp: he made himself a thief, which is the worse bargain. The loss of the lamp was an indifferent; the thief's character is the thief's own.

The anecdote is preserved because it is the dichotomy applied to property without solemnity. Epictetus is not performing indifference; he is doing the accounting, and the accounting shows the thief came out behind. The earthenware replacement is the punchline: the man who actually lost nothing bought the cheaper lamp.

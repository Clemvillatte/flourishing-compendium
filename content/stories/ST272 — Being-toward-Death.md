*Philosophical metaphor · Germany, 1927 · [Martin Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heidegger)*

Heidegger's analysis: death is not an event at the end of a life; it is the possibility that each of us is, the ownmost possibility, which cannot be outstripped, and which no one can take over for me. The average everyday response is to treat death as something that happens, an event among events, and the authentic response is to own it as one's own, which has the effect of pulling one's other possibilities into focus.

The analysis is the source of every later existential treatment of mortality, including Frankl's and Yalom's, and its practical content is the claim that death is the horizon that gives a life's choices their weight. The inauthentic mode, treating death as a rumour, is described with a chill of recognition: one dies, we say, as though it were an error of grammar.

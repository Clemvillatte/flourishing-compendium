*Buddhist parable · India, 1st century BCE · [Milindapañha, Questions of King Milinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milindapa%C3%B1ha)*

King Milinda asks the monk Nagasena his name and then pounces: if there is no self, who is this "Nagasena"? Nagasena asks how the king came. By chariot. Is the axle the chariot? The wheels? The frame, the yoke, the reins? The king concedes each part and then the whole: "chariot" is a convention, a name for an assembly in working order. So too with "Nagasena."

The argument is the founding text of Buddhist reductionism about persons. Its force is not that you do not exist; it is that what exists is a functioning assembly, and the assembly is what you actually have to work with. The chariot metaphor became the tradition's standard image and travelled far beyond it.

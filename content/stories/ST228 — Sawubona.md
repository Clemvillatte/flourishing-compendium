*Cultural practice narrative · Southern Africa · [Ubuntu philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubuntu_philosophy)*

In Zulu, the greeting is "Sawubona," literally "I see you," and the response "Ngikhona," "I am here." The exchange, in its traditional form, is a mutual act of recognition: not a pleasantry but a declaration that the other's presence has been registered, and that one is, in turn, present. Related forms exist in other Nguni languages, and the practice is often cited by Tutu and others as the linguistic carrier of the ubuntu worldview.

The greeting's interest is that it makes recognition a daily, repeated, formal act. The philosophy is not stated; it is performed, at every encounter, and the tradition's claim is that the performing constitutes the relating. It is the smallest possible demonstration that a culture's metaphysics can live in its hello.

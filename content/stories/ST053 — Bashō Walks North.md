*Literary/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · Japan, 1689 · [Matsuo Bashō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matsuo_Bash%C5%8D)*

In middle age, Bashō sells his house and walks north into Japan's interior with a travelling companion, months of rain, poor inns, and illness. The Narrow Road to the Deep North records the journey in prose interleaved with haiku. Each poem is small, exact, and empty of commentary: a frog, a war helmet buried in grass, silence. "The journey itself is my home," he wrote elsewhere.

Bashō's refinement is subtraction carried into a life. He stripped the poem to seventeen syllables and the life to what could be carried, and the walking was the discipline that kept both honest. His poems are still the most reassigned texts in Japanese schooling, usually to children who have not yet walked anywhere.

*Philosophical metaphor · France, 20th century · [Emmanuel Levinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Levinas)*

Levinas describes the encounter with the face of the other person. The face is not an object among objects, not a bundle of features; it is the mode in which the other addresses me, and the address is a command: thou shalt not kill. The other's vulnerability, the nakedness of the face, places me under obligation before any contract, before any reasoning about consequences.

Totality and Infinity is the century's central work on ethics as first philosophy, and its claim is the inversion of the tradition: the relation to the other is not one topic among others; it is the condition of meaning itself. The practical content is a description of what is actually encountered in another person's face, which is not a surface but an demand, and which we spend much of our social machinery avoiding.

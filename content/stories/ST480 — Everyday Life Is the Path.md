Tang-dynasty China, set in a 13th-century koan collection · Mumonkan (The Gateless Gate), case 19 · a Zen koan, Senzaki and Reps translation (1934)

A monk asked his master what the path is, and every answer he got made the asking look like the problem. The exchange is the classical statement of what B060 calls practicing without seeking, and the koan's closing comment adds the part a seeker most wants to skip: even after the breakthrough, the practice continues. ([Mumonkan 19](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/glg/glg19.htm))

The case, as Senzaki and Reps give it ([Mumonkan 19](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/glg/glg19.htm)):

> Joshu asked Nansen: "What is the path?"
>
> Nansen said: "Everyday life is the path."
>
> Joshu asked: "Can it be studied?"
>
> Nansen said: "If you try to study, you will be far away from it."
>
> Joshu asked: "If I do not study, how can I know it is the path?"
>
> Nansen said: "The path does not belong to the perception world, neither does it belong to the nonperception world. Cognition is a delusion and noncognition is senseless. If you want to reach the true path beyond doubt, place yourself in the same freedom as sky. You name it neither good nor not-good."
>
> At these words Joshu was enlightened.

Mumon's comment on the case ([Mumonkan 19](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/glg/glg19.htm)):

> Nansen could melt Joshu's frozen doubts at once when Joshu asked his questions. I doubt though if Joshu reached the point that Nansen did. He needed thirty more years of study.

My reading: the koan stages B060's trap from inside it. Joshu's questions are good questions, and each one makes the path an object to be located, studied and known. Nansen's replies remove the object three times: the path is everyday life, so it is not somewhere else; trying to study it moves it away, so the seeking is the distance; and it is not in cognition or noncognition, so there is no audit of progress to run. The enlightenment at the end is the koan's trap for the reader, because it looks like the prize the whole exchange said not to reach for, and Mumon's comment closes that door too: Joshu was enlightened, and he needed thirty more years of study. Read alongside B060, the story carries the principle's arc whole: seeking named as the problem, the path restored to ordinary life, and the practice continuing past attainment without a terminus. It gives no instruction on posture or method, which another story can carry.

**Principles:**
- [[B060 — Practice without seeking|60. Practice without seeking]] — the koan names seeking as what moves the path away, returns the path to everyday life, and has the practice continue for thirty more years after enlightenment

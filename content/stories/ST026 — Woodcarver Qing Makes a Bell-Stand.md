*Zhuangzi parable · China, 4th century BCE · [Zhuangzi, ch. 19, "The Full Understanding of Life"](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/the-full-understanding-of-life)*

Woodcarver Qing's bell-stands are so beautiful people assume they are the work of spirits. Asked how, he describes a preparation that is mostly forgetting: he fasts to still his mind, and by the third day he has forgotten reward; by the fifth, praise; by the seventh, that he has four limbs and a body. Only then does he enter the mountain forest. When he sees the right tree, the stand is already there, and he puts his hand to it.

The detail worth holding is that the skill is not the carving. The carving is the last step. The work is the long subtraction of the audience from the room.

*Stoic metaphor · Greece, 2nd century CE · [Hierocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierocles_%28Stoic%29)*

Hierocles describes the self as surrounded by concentric circles: the body at the centre, then parents, siblings, extended family, community, city, nation, and, outermost, the whole human race. The task, he says, is to draw the outer circles inward, treating the distant as one treats the near, without collapsing the distinctions.

The image survives as the tradition's clearest map of moral development, and it is a practice, not a picture: the exercise consists of deliberately moving people from outer circles to inner ones. Its modern descendants are contact-hypothesis research and every deliberate community-building practice, and the metaphor's endurance owes to its precision about where the work is: at the boundaries, not the centre.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1994 · [The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shawshank_Redemption)*

Andy Dufresne, wrongly convicted, spends nineteen years in Shawshank. He does not rage; he builds. He improves the library, does the guards' taxes, teaches a young man to read, and carves a tunnel through his cell wall with a rock hammer, hidden behind posters, one grain of wall at a time. He crawls through five hundred yards of sewage pipe and emerges in the rain, and the film's most quoted line is Red's: get busy living or get busy dying.

The film is the discipline of action as cinema. Every act Andy performs is within his power and done with excellence, and the outcomes, the acquittal, the vindication, are never in his power and never pursued directly. The tunnel is the stoic paradox made literal: the man who wanted out did the work of the man who had accepted he might never leave.

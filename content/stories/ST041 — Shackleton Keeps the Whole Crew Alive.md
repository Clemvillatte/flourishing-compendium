*Historical/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · Antarctica, 1914-1916 · [Ernest Shackleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Shackleton)*

The Endurance is crushed by ice in the Weddell Sea, leaving twenty-eight men stranded on floes with no radio, a thousand miles from help. Shackleton's leadership is unheroic in the best sense: routine, shared rations, shared work, and an almost punctilious attention to morale. He gives up his own gloves, takes the worst sleeping spot, and decides who sits in the boats. After 634 days, all twenty-eight survive.

What the story shows is not courage but a form of care organised as logistics. Shackleton understood that in extremity the leader's real task is the maintenance of each person's sense of mattering, and he did it by structure, not speeches.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · South Korea, 2003 · [Kim Ki-duk, Spring, Summer, Fall, Winter... and Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring,_Summer,_Fall,_Winter..._and_Spring)*

A child monk lives with an old master on a floating temple in a lake. In spring he torments animals for fun and learns cruelty's cost when the master ties a stone to his back. In summer, love. In fall, jealousy and murder and return. In winter, penance: he drags a millstone up the mountain, and a woman comes to leave a baby. The master, weeping, rows out and burns himself on the raft. The film ends with spring again, the cycle restarted with a new child.

The film's structure is the Buddhist claim about lives told as weather. Nothing in the cycle is optional; the cruelty, the love, the crime, and the penance are all seasons of the same person. What the master teaches is not avoidance of the cycle but how to be in each phase without being destroyed by it.

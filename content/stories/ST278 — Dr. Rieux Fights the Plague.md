*Literary narrative · France, 1947 · [Albert Camus, The Plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Plague_(novel))*

In the quarantined city of Oran, the plague, which everyone initially refuses to name, kills its thousands. Dr. Rieux, the physician narrator, does not offer a theory of the plague's meaning. He treats the sick, day after day, and his stated reason is plain: it's the only thing a man can do in the face of suffering. The novel's other figures, the priest who preaches that the plague is punishment, the journalist who escapes, the clerk who dedicates himself to writing a perfect sentence, are tested against the same condition.

The novel, read as an allegory of the French occupation, is also a pure statement of the revolt against the absurd: Rieux does not fight the plague because he believes he will win. He fights it because it is an injustice done to people, and the fighting is the meaning, which is the essay's conclusion in narrative form. Read this way the doctor's plainness is the whole argument: solidarity, not victory, is what a lucid person does with a plague.

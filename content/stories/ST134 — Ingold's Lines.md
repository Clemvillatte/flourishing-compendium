*Scientific/biographical narrative · United Kingdom, 2007 · [Tim Ingold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Ingold)*

Ingold argues that the world is not a network of connected points but a meshwork of lines: people, animals, winds, and waters trace paths of movement, and the places we treat as fixed points are knots in these lines. Life, he says, is lived along, not at, and knowledge is grown through participation in these lines rather than assembled from views.

Lines: A Brief History is a quiet book with a large consequence for how flourishing is modelled. If a person is a line of movement rather than a point with properties, then the questions to ask of a life are questions of direction, rhythm, and correspondence with other lines, which is what Ingold's later anthropology of making and dwelling works out.

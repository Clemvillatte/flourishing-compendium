*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United Kingdom, 2010 · [The King's Speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_King%27s_Speech)*

Prince Albert, Duke of York, has a severe stammer. His doctors prescribe smoking to relax the throat and marbles to place in the mouth, a cure dating to Demosthenes. He improves instead under Lionel Logue, an Australian actor with no medical credentials, who insists on calling him Bertie, treating him in a shabby office, and probing the childhood origins of the stammer. The therapy works when the relationship does.

The film is popularly read as an underdog story, but its content is the phronesis critique of technique. The royal doctors have the credentials and the rules; Logue has the reading of this particular man, and the film's argument, in Aristotle's terms, is that the reading is the treatment.

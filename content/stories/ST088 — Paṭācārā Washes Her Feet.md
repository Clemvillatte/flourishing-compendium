*Buddhist biographical narrative · India, 5th century BCE · [Therīgāthā 5.8](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/thig/thig.05.08.than.html)*

Paṭācārā loses her husband, both children, and her parents in quick succession, and goes mad with grief, wandering naked. The Buddha meets her and says: sister, recover your presence of mind. He gives her an instruction drawn from the daily act of washing the feet: as the water runs down, so everything arises and passes away. She steadies, enters the order, and becomes one of the great teachers of the tradition.

The instruction's brilliance is its scale. She could not take a doctrine of impermanence; she could watch water run downhill off her own feet, and the doctrine walked in by itself. The Therīgāthā preserves her verses, and she went on to console hundreds of grieving women with the same method.

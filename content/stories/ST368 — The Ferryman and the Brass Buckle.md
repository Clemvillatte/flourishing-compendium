Daoist literary narrative · Zhuangzi, Book XIX (Da Sheng), in James Legge's 1891 translation ([Legge, Zhuangzi, METIS](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Yan Yuan tells Confucius that when he crossed the gulf of Shang-shen, "the ferryman handled the boat like a spirit", and that when he asked whether such handling could be learned, the ferryman answered that good swimmers learn it quickly, while divers can manage a boat without ever having seen one ([Zhuangzi 19.4, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Confucius explains the answer: "Good swimmers acquire the ability quickly - they forget the water (and its dangers)", and divers look on the watery gulf as a hill-side, with the upsetting of a boat no more disturbing than a carriage turning back ([same passage](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Then he turns to what ruins such ability ([same passage](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)):

> "He who is contending for a piece of earthenware puts forth all his skill. If the prize be a buckle of brass, he shoots timorously; if it be for an article of gold, he shoots as if he were blind. The skill of the archer is the same in all the cases; but (in the two latter cases) he is under the influence of solicitude, and looks on the external prize as most important." ([Confucius, in Zhuangzi 19.4, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The passage closes with his judgment: "All who attach importance to what is external show stupidity in themselves" ([same passage](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

For D073, the compendium reads the contrast as an image of presence disrupted by concern for an outcome. In the dialogue, the archer's skill stays the same while his performance worsens as the prize becomes more valuable ([Zhuangzi 19.4, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The practical question is whether attention is with the task or with the prize.

**Principles:**
- [[D073 — Don't over-plan — act from presence]]: the archer's underlying skill is the same in all the cases while his performance worsens as the prize becomes more valuable, and the ferryman's ability is internalized familiarity that has forgotten the water; both images turn on whether attention is with the task or with the prize ([Zhuangzi 19.4, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

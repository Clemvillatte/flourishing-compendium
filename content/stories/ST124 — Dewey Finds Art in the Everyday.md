*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1934 · [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey)*

Dewey opens Art as Experience with a complaint: we have put art in museums and thereby severed it from the experiences it comes from. The meal cooked with attention, the conversation held with presence, the problem solved with craft, have the same structure as the painting: doing and undergoing, tension and resolution, a whole that is consummated. The aesthetic is not a department of life; it is life at its most integrated.

The book's practical claim is large: aesthetic experience is available in ordinary activity, and its conditions can be studied and improved. It became the philosophical foundation for the entire field of everyday aesthetics, and for Csikszentmihalyi's flow, which Dewey anticipated almost point for point.

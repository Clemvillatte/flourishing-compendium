*Stoic metaphor · Rome, 1st century BCE · [Cicero, De Finibus III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicero)*

Cicero, expounding Stoic ethics, uses the archer: the archer does everything in his power, selecting the arrow, the stance, the aim, the release. Once the arrow leaves the bow, it is no longer his. The hitting is not his telos; the aiming is. The Stoic, likewise, pursues preferred outcomes, health, success, safety, while treating them as selected indifferents, not goods.

The image is the tradition's clearest device for the dichotomy of control, and its precision is often missed. The archer does not stop caring about the target; he trains for it. What changes is where the verdict on his performance is located, and the Stoic claim is that relocating it is the whole difference between freedom and anxiety.

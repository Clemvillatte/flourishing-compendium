*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1989 · [Carol Ryff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Ryff)*

Carol Ryff, in "Happiness Is Everything, or Is It?", attacks the equation of well-being with happiness and proposes a six-dimensional model drawn from Aristotle, John Stuart Mill, and humanistic psychology: autonomy, environmental mastery, personal growth, positive relations with others, purpose in life, and self-acceptance. Her scales, the Ryff measures, remain the standard instruments for psychological well-being as distinct from subjective happiness.

The paper's significance is its restoration of the eudaimonic tradition to empirical psychology, and its practical yield is a distinction that now organises the field: hedonic well-being, how you feel, and eudaimonic well-being, how your life is going, correlate only moderately, and interventions that raise one do not necessarily raise the other. The six dimensions are the operational descendants of the function argument.

*Modern biographical narrative · Vietnam/France, 20th century · [Thích Nhất Hạnh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%ADch_Nh%E1%BA%A5t_H%E1%BA%A1nh)*

In The Miracle of Mindfulness, written to students during the Vietnam War, Thích Nhất Hạnh teaches a young monk that washing the dishes is not a task to get through in order to have a cup of tea. There are two ways to wash dishes: to have them done, and to wash them. The instruction continues through the ordinary day: washing, peeling vegetables, drinking tea, each named as sufficient.

He would go on to coin "engaged Buddhism," insisting the practice was for soldiers and refugees, not retreatants. The dishwashing teaching is the root of it: if the practice cannot live in the sink, it cannot live anywhere, because the sink is where a life actually happens.

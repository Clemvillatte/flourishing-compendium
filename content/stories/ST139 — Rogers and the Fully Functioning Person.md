*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1961 · [Carl Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Rogers)*

Rogers describes the outcome of good therapy not as contentment but as a process he calls the fully functioning person: open to experience, living existentially in the present, trusting the organism's own valuing process, and, crucially, unfinished. The good life, he writes, is not a state of being; it is a direction, not a destination.

On Becoming a Person is the humanistic tradition's answer to the question of what therapy is for, and its answer is process metaphysics in clinical clothing. There is no endpoint to reach, and Rogers treats this as good news: a person who has stopped becoming has not arrived, they have stopped.

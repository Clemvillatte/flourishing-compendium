Philosophical parable · Zhuangzi, chapter 20, section 1

A tree is spared because its wood is useless; later, a goose is killed because it cannot cackle. Zhuangzi's disciples ask which condition he would choose. His answer challenges both a fixed strategy and a supposedly safe midpoint, giving D156 an angle on flexibility in judgement: a rule that protects in one setting may fail in the next. ([Zhuangzi 20.1](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

Walking on a mountain, Zhuangzi sees a great tree with spreading branches and thick foliage. A woodcutter is resting beside it but leaves it untouched. Asked why, the man says it is of no use for anything. Zhuangzi tells his disciples that its worthless wood will allow it to live out its natural years. ([Zhuangzi 20.1, tree and woodcutter](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

After leaving the mountain, he stays with an old friend. Pleased to see him, the host orders a servant to kill and boil a goose. The servant asks which one: one can cackle and the other cannot. The host answers, "Kill the one that cannot cackle." ([Zhuangzi 20.1, visit and goose](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

The next day, the disciples put the incidents together. Yesterday, lack of usefulness promised the tree a full life; now the same lack has brought the goose's death. Where would their teacher place himself? Zhuangzi laughs and considers a position between usefulness and its absence. He immediately qualifies it: that position would seem right, but would still leave him involved in trouble. ([Zhuangzi 20.1, disciples' question and midpoint](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

He then describes a different orientation: taking one's ease with Dao and its Attributes, beyond praise and blame. Such a person can rise like a dragon or keep low like a snake; "he is transformed with the (changing) character of the time, and is not willing to addict himself to any one thing." He describes harmony with surroundings rather than permanent allegiance to either useful or useless status. ([Zhuangzi 20.1, transformation with the times](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

The speech closes by listing reversals: union leads to separation, success to overthrow, honour to criticism. Zhuangzi asks where unchangeableness can be found among these conditions and tells the disciples to make their abode in Dao and its Attributes. ([Zhuangzi 20.1, concluding exhortation](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

Read beside D156, the story extends flexibility to the rules someone adopts for self-protection. Keeping the tree episode without the goose would lose the very counterexample that brings the disciples' question into view. The full exchange also keeps the easy compromise from becoming the final answer. ([Zhuangzi 20.1, complete exchange](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/tree-on-the-mountain))

**Principles:**
- [[D156 — Stay flexible — rigidity is death's companion|D156 Stay flexible: rigidity is death's companion]]: a rule of uselessness, or a fixed compromise, gives way to responsiveness rooted in Dao.

United States, 1907 · lecture anecdote from William James's Pragmatism, Lecture II, "What Pragmatism Means", delivered at the Lowell Institute and Columbia University, with the text at the Mead Project (Brock University) and the principle file in the compendium working tree

The 1907 book that gave pragmatism its name opens its second lecture with a story James told on himself. "Some years ago, being with a camping party in the mountains, I returned from a solitary ramble to find everyone engaged in a ferocious metaphysical dispute" over a squirrel ([James 1907, Lecture II](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/James/James_1907/James_1907_02.html)). A live squirrel clings to one side of a tree trunk; a human being stands against the opposite side and circles the tree to catch sight of it, but the squirrel circles too, keeping the trunk between them. "Does the man go round the squirrel or not? He goes round the tree, sure enough, and the squirrel is on the tree; but does he go round the squirrel?" The party had worn the question threadbare, and the sides were even; each appealed to James to make a majority ([James 1907, Lecture II](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/James/James_1907/James_1907_02.html)).

James did not pick a side. "Mindful of the scholastic adage that whenever you meet a contradiction you must make a distinction, I immediately sought and found one,": it depends on what you practically mean by going round. Pass north, east, south, west of the squirrel and back, and the man plainly does go round him; mean being in front, right, behind, left and in front again, and he equally plainly does not, since the squirrel keeps its belly toward him throughout. "Make the distinction, and there is no occasion for any farther dispute. You are both right and both wrong according as you conceive the verb 'to go round' in one practical fashion or the other" ([James 1907, Lecture II](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/James/James_1907/James_1907_02.html)). One or two hotter disputants called this "a shuffling evasion", wanting plain honest English round; the majority thought the dispute assuaged ([James 1907, Lecture II](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/James/James_1907/James_1907_02.html)).

The anecdote matters because James used it to introduce the method itself: "The pragmatic method is primarily a method of settling metaphysical disputes that otherwise might be interminable", by asking "What difference would it practically make to anyone if this notion rather than that notion were true?" ([James 1907, Lecture II](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/James/James_1907/James_1907_02.html)). The compendium's reading for P020: the audit is not a refusal to answer; it is the discovery that some questions carry no difference worth having. James's theory of truth, the Lecture VI material on verification, is not carried by this story.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2016 · [Kenneth Lonergan, Manchester by the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_by_the_Sea)*

Lee Chandler, a handyman in Boston, returns to his hometown when his brother dies, and through flashbacks we learn his secret: through his own negligence, a fire he started by leaving the fire screen off, his three children died. His wife divorced him; he attempted suicide; he survived. In the present, he is named guardian of his teenage nephew, and the film's arc is his inability to accept the role and return to a town where everyone knows. He does not forgive himself. He arranges for the nephew to stay, and leaves.

The film's existential content is its refusal of catharsis, which is a formal choice: there is no scene of redemption, no confrontation that heals, and the final conversations are about logistics. The film's claim is that some experiences are not resolved, that care is not the same as repair, and that the honest response to what one has done may be a life lived at a reduced radius, permanently, without the consolation of meaning.

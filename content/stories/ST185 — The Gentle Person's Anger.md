*Philosophical narrative · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

The gentle-tempered person, Aristotle says in NE IV.5, is praised not for never being angry but for being angry in the right way: at the right things, with the right people, for the right duration, in the right manner. The vice is not the emotion but its bad aim: the irascible are angry too quickly, at the wrong things, for too long, and the inirascible, who never get angry, are foolish and slavish.

The passage is the founding treatment of emotions as aimable rather than suppressible. It underwrites every modern emotion-regulation protocol and contradicts all of them slightly: the goal is not down-regulation of anger but the training of its aim, and Aristotle treats the person who cannot be angered by injustice as having a defect, not a virtue.

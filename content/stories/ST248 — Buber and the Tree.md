*Philosophical narrative · Europe, 20th century · [Martin Buber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Buber)*

Buber, in I and Thou, distinguishes two primary relations. I-It: the tree as object, measured, classified, used. I-Thou: the tree as presence, addressed, met. The same tree can be encountered either way, and Buber's claim is that the I itself is different in each, since the I-It relation produces an I that possesses and the I-Thou produces an I that is present. All real living, he says, is meeting.

The book's influence on psychotherapy, education, and theology is immense, and its practical content is a diagnostic for attention: in this conversation, am I relating to the person or to my idea of the person. Buber's later essays, including "The Education of Character," work out the pedagogical consequence, that the teacher's relation to the student is the medium of the teaching.

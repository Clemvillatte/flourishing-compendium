*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2011 · [Moneyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moneyball)*

Billy Beane, general manager of the Oakland Athletics, has the lowest payroll in baseball and a team of scouts who trust looks, speed, and pedigree. He hires Peter Brand, an economics graduate who argues that the market misprices players: the statistics that predict runs, on-base and slugging percentage, are undervalued because scouts prefer the wrong story. The 2002 Athletics, assembled from players everyone else discarded, win 20 consecutive games.

The film is the pragmatic maxim dramatised. The scouts are not stupid; they are using a vocabulary whose cash value has collapsed. Beane's insight is not that statistics are true and eyes are false; it is that the test is scoring runs, and on that test the traditional descriptions had stopped buying anything.

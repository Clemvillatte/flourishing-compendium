*Scientific/biographical narrative · Sweden/United States, 1993 · [K. Anders Ericsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Anders_Ericsson)*


Ericsson, Krampe, and Tesch-Römer's studies of expert musicians, violinists at a Berlin conservatory, found that the difference between the best and the rest was not talent but accumulated hours of a specific kind of practice: deliberate practice, focused on weaknesses, with immediate feedback, at the edge of ability, and not inherently enjoyable. The best had accumulated thousands more hours of it by the same age.

The finding's popularisation, Malcolm Gladwell's "10,000 hours," distorted it, and Ericsson spent years correcting the record, and the meta-analytic history, including Macnamara's finding that deliberate practice explains a real but moderate share of variance across domains, is the honest version. What survives is the structure: expertise is built through structured difficulty, not repetition, and the practice is designed, not merely done.

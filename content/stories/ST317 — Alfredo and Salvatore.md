*Film narrative, cross-cultural · Italy/France, 1988 · [Cinema Paradiso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_Paradiso)*

In a small Sicilian village, the boy Salvatore befriends Alfredo, the projectionist at the Cinema Paradiso, who teaches him to run the projector and cuts the kisses from the films for the priest's censorship. Alfredo, near the end, tells Salvatore to leave: don't come back, don't give in to nostalgia, keep working, and the scene is the film's centre. Salvatore becomes a famous director in Rome. Thirty years later, he returns for Alfredo's funeral, and finds a reel: all the cut kisses, spliced together, and he watches them alone in a cinema.

The film's content for this volume is the relationship's shape over time: the mentor's gift is the release, the pushing away, and the final reel is the accumulated record of the relationship, held in trust. The film's argument, which it does not state, is that the relation that shapes a life is not always the one that endures, and that the letting-go is part of the love.

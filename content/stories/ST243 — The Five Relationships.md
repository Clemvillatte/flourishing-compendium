*Confucian philosophical narrative · China, 5th century BCE · [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism)*

Confucian ethics is organised around the wulun, the five relationships: ruler and minister, parent and child, husband and wife, elder and younger sibling, friend and friend. Each relationship is defined by reciprocal duties rather than by unilateral authority, and the tradition's ideal, the junzi, the cultivated person, is one who performs each role's duties excellently. The system is hierarchical, and the tradition's own correction, in Mencius, is that rulers who fail their duties forfeit the mandate.

The structure's interest is its refusal of the general other. Where universalist ethics addresses humanity in the abstract, the Confucian system addresses the specific persons one is actually bound to, with specific duties, and the tradition's claim is that the general benevolence is built out of the particular ones, not the reverse.

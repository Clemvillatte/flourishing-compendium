*Scientific/philosophical narrative · France, 1945 · [Maurice Merleau-Ponty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Merleau-Ponty)*

The patient who has lost an arm still feels the hand. He reaches with it, gestures with it, and the surgeon's knife cannot remove it, because it is not in the arm. Merleau-Ponty uses the phantom limb as one of his central exhibits: the body schema, the system of motor possibilities by which we inhabit space, does not dissolve when a part is lost. The world is still organised around the missing hand.

Phenomenology of Perception is the century's central work on embodiment, and the phantom limb is its sharpest evidence: the body is not the physical object, the Körper, but the lived structure, the Leib, and the two can come apart. Every patient who has felt a missing limb knows something that no physiology of the arm can contain.

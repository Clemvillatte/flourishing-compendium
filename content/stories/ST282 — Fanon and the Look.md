*Philosophical/biographical narrative · France/Martinique, 1952 · [Frantz Fanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frantz_Fanon)*

Fanon, a Black psychiatrist from Martinique practising in France, describes the experience of being seen by a white child: "Look, a Negro!" The look fixes him, he writes, seals him into a crushing objecthood, and he is made aware, in the phrase's most quoted form, of his being-for-others. The body schema, the quiet, taken-for-granted being-in-the-world, collapses under the look, and he is forced to assume a racial epidermal schema.

Black Skin, White Masks is the century's central work on the phenomenology of colonised experience, and its method is Sartre's, turned to a different material. Its content is the recognition structure under asymmetric power: the recognition demanded by Hegel's dialectic is precisely what the colonial relation refuses, and the pathology Fanon describes, in his clinical practice, is the internalisation of the refusal. Read next to principle E068 the case sharpens the point: the look is not a mood but an ontological structure, and its asymmetry is what a practice of solidarity has to work against.

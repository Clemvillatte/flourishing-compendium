*Literary/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1854 · [Henry David Thoreau, Walden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walden)*

Thoreau moves to Walden Pond to front only the essential facts of life. He builds the cabin himself, grows beans he mostly sells rather than eats, and keeps meticulous accounts: the entire experiment costs $28.12. The ledger is half the book. The other half is what the economy buys: mornings with nothing to do but notice.

The bean field draws the line clearly. Thoreau is not against comfort; he is against the confusion of means with ends, the way a lifetime can be spent maintaining a life no one has time to live. "A man is rich," he writes, "in proportion to the number of things which he can afford to let alone."

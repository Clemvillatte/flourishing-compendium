*Buddhist parable · India, 5th century BCE · [Sallatha Sutta, SN 36.6](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn36/sn36.006.than.html)*

A well-taught person struck by an arrow feels one arrow. An untaught person struck by the same arrow feels two. The first is the physical pain. The second is the story: why me, this is unfair, what will become of me. The sutta then notes drily that the taught person feels the pain without adding grief and lamentation, and that the first arrow is not optional.

This is the most quoted psychological text in the Pali canon and the clearest anticipation of modern pain psychology and acceptance-based therapies. Its precision matters: the teaching is not that pain is unreal. It is that the second arrow is manufactured, and manufacturing it is optional.

*Philosophical narrative · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

In Nicomachean Ethics VI, Aristotle compares practical wisdom to the craft of the builder or the doctor. The doctor does not apply a rule to a body; he brings together knowledge of the particular patient, the disease, the season, the available means, and adjusts. Craft knowledge and practical wisdom differ, but both share the structure: general knowledge plus the ability to see what is relevant here.

The passage is where Aristotle's epistemology of action is stated most plainly. The intellectual virtues are not contents; they are capacities for discrimination in context, which is why they cannot be taught the way facts can, only cultivated the way skills are.

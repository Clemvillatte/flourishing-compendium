*Philosophical narrative · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

In Nicomachean Ethics X, Aristotle sorts the competing theories of the good life: the many say pleasure, and they are not to be dismissed lightly, since pleasure completes activity the way bloom completes youth. His resolution is that pleasure is not the end but accompanies the end: the pleasure of an activity is a sign the activity is unimpeded, which is why the virtuous person's pleasures and the base person's pleasures are not the same pleasures.

The passage is the tradition's most careful treatment of enjoyment, and its practical yield is a diagnostic: pleasure that comes with the activity, unforced, is information about the activity's fit. Pleasure pursued as the target, extracted from activity, is the hedonist's error, and it produces less pleasure, not more.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States/UK, 2010 · [127 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/127_Hours)*

Aron Ralston, canyoneering alone in Utah, dislodges a boulder that pins his right arm against the canyon wall. He has a litre of water, a multitool with a dull knife, and no one who knows where he is. The film, based on Ralston's memoir, covers the 127 hours: the rationing, the video diary, the hallucinations, and finally the decision, after five days, to break his own arm and cut it off with the knife. He walks out and is found by a family.

The film's Stoic content is the situation's perfect smallness of the controllable: the boulder is not movable, the water is finite, the rescue is not coming. What remains, the film shows in unflinching detail, is still enormous: the choice of what to do with the situation, including the choice that requires doing the unthinkable to oneself with a dull blade.

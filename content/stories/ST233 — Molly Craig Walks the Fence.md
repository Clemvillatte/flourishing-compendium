*Film narrative, cross-cultural · Australia, 2002 · [Rabbit-Proof Fence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabbit-Proof_Fence)*

In 1931, Molly Craig, her sister Daisy, and her cousin Gracie are taken from their families in Jigalong, Western Australia, under the policy of removing mixed-descent children, and transported 1,600 kilometres to the Moore River Native Settlement. Molly leads the escape, and the three girls follow the state's rabbit-proof fence, the longest fence in the world, as a navigational line home. Gracie is recaptured; Molly and Daisy arrive at Jigalong after nine weeks of walking, pursued.

The film is based on Molly's daughter Doris Pilkington's book, and the historical Molly's later life, including her own removal and that of her daughters, is part of the record. Its relational content is the fence itself: the same structure that was built to contain the country's ecology is used by a child as the thread of connection to kin and Country, and the walk is the argument that the connection is not an idea but a fact that can be followed on foot.

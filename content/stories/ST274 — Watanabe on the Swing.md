*Film narrative, cross-cultural · Japan, 1952 · [Akira Kurosawa, Ikiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikiru)*

Kanji Watanabe, a Tokyo bureaucrat with thirty years of stamped documents, learns he has stomach cancer. The film's structure is the classic existential arc: the denial, the hedonistic attempt, the despair, and then the discovery of a project, pushing a playground through the bureaucracy, that connects him to other people's lives. He dies in the snow, on a swing in the playground, singing the song about the brief life, and the film's second half is his wake, where the mourners reconstruct and debate what happened.

The film's existential content is the confrontation with death as clarification. Watanabe does not become brave or wise; he becomes a man with a project, and the project is relational, for mothers and children in a neighbourhood he will never live in. The wake's argument, in which his colleagues vow to change and return to their desks, is the film's honest statement about how rare the clarification is.

Contemporary ACT · experiential visualisation · Russ Harris, Observing Thoughts

Imagine a stream carrying leaves. As a thought appears, place it on a leaf and let it float past. Harris's instructions include what to do when the exercise is interrupted: notice that a thought has caught you, gently disengage and continue. The moving image gives a way to practise observing thinking ([workshop handout, p. 9](https://www.actmindfully.com.au/upimages/2008_Introductory_ACT_Workshop_Handout_-__Russ_Harris.pdf)).

> Looking at thoughts, rather than from thoughts
>
> Harris, defusion notes, p. 8 ([handout](https://www.actmindfully.com.au/upimages/2008_Introductory_ACT_Workshop_Handout_-__Russ_Harris.pdf)).

An editorial variation includes the thought that the exercise is going badly. That thought can also be noticed. The image offers no requirement to make the stream run perfectly or to finish without distraction.

For an editorial application, briefly notice one recurring thought before returning to a chosen task, such as reading a paragraph carefully. Compare whether that response helps attention return; keep it if useful, and try another approach if the image becomes a distracting chore. Cognition and attention are the dimensions being varied. Whether the thought contains an important warning still needs ordinary examination.

The Buddhist comparison has a boundary. MN 20 directs practitioners to evaluate unskillful thoughts and offers several responses, including changing the theme and a final forceful method. Its full teaching cannot be reduced to watching thoughts drift away ([MN 20, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)).

**Principles:**
- [[P159 — Defuse — see thoughts as thoughts]]: the leaf gives thinking an observable form.
- [[B074 — Thoughts are not facts]]: a limited modern comparison for examining a thought before following it.
- [[P161 — Return to the present — contact what is here]]: noticing that attention has wandered allows a return to the current activity.

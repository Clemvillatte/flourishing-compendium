Early Buddhist discourse, simile · Majjhima Nikaya 21, in Bhikkhu Sujato's translation.

At Savatthi lived a housewife named Vedehika with a good reputation: "gentle, placid, and calm." Her bonded maid, Kali, was deft and tireless, and she began to wonder: "But does she actually have anger in her and just not show it?" Or was it only that Kali's work was so well organised that there was nothing to be angry about? She decided to test her mistress ([Sujato, MN 21](https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato)).

First Kali got up during the day. Vedehika asked what was the matter with her, and when Kali said "Nothing," the housewife was angry and upset: she "scowled." The next day Kali got up later, and Vedehika "blurted out angry words." The third day Kali got up even later, and Vedehika "grabbed a door-pin and hit Kāḷī on the head, cracking it open." The sutta repeats the same sentence at each step, "Angry and upset," and changes only what the anger does. At the first step, the anger is visible only in a face ([Sujato](https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato)).

Kali, bleeding, denounced her mistress to the neighbours: "See, ladies, what the gentle one did!" The housewife's reputation turned to "fierce, ill-tempered, and not calm at all." The Buddha applies it to monks: "it’s when they encounter disagreeable criticism that you’ll know whether they’re really gentle, placid, and calm." Gentleness that depends on comfortable conditions has not yet been tested ([Sujato](https://suttacentral.net/mn21/en/sujato)).

The compendium reads the scowl as the moment the principles B116 and B105 are about. By the time of the door-pin there is little to dissect. The sutta does not say what Vedehika felt, or whether she saw the scowl.

Limits: this is a simile told by the Buddha to teach monks, and it is a story about a mistress and a bonded maid. The sutta takes no position on how Kali is treated, and the maid's test costs her a broken skull. It tells us what anger does when unnoticed but offers no method for the person who does notice. Only Sujato's translation was used here.

**Principles:**
- [[B116 — Catch irritation early — sparks before fires]]: the scowl, the words, then the blow.
- [[B105 — Dissect anger when it arises]]: the maid's test makes the anger and its triggers visible.

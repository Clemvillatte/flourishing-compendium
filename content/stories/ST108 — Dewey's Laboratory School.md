*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1896 · [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey)*

Dewey founds a school at the University of Chicago in which children do not memorise and recite. They cook, which requires measurement, chemistry, and botany; they build, which requires geometry and physics; they garden, spin wool, and form a miniature society with its own occupations. The curriculum is the method: knowledge is what inquiry produces when a genuine problem needs solving, and the school's job is to supply genuine problems.

The Laboratory School is the practical answer to the question of what education is for. Dewey's answer, worked out in the building rather than the essay, was growth itself, and the school remains the reference point for every project-based and experiential curriculum since.

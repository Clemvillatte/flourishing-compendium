Traditional Chan parable cycle with verses · verses by Kakuan (Kuoan Shiyuan, 12th-century China); paintings by Yokoo Tatsuhiko; commentary by Kubota Ji'un; Cusumano and Sato translation

A twelfth-century Chinese Zen master, Kakuan, supplied the ten pictures of a herdsman and an ox with verses; the opened edition pairs his verses with modern paintings by Yokoo Tatsuhiko and a contemporary commentary by Kubota Ji'un. The cycle is a story told in images: "Here, our essential self is compared to an ox. We seek the ox, grasp it, tame it and finally the self which has always been seeking becomes completely one with the ox", the page's introduction explains, and "this also is forgotten so that we now simply carry on our ordinary lives" ([Terebess, Ten Ox-herding Pictures](https://terebess.hu/english/oxherding.html)). In the early pictures the herdsman chases the animal deeper into the mountains; by the middle pictures he has caught it and is riding the ox home; by the eighth, the commentary says, "person and ox both forgotten".

The turn the principle needs is the ninth picture, titled "Returning to the Source". Its verse gives away nothing that was hidden: "Having come back to the origin and returned to the source, you see that you have expended efforts in vain. What could be superior to becoming blind and deaf in this very moment? Inside the hermitage, you do not see what is in front of the hermitage. The water flows of itself and the flowers are naturally red" ([Terebess](https://terebess.hu/english/oxherding.html)). The commentator's warning guards the same point: "the state after enlightenment is exactly the same as that of before enlightenment", and it is "totally wrong to maintain from the very beginning that practice and enlightenment are of no use. Such an attitude is called \"inactive zen\"" ([Terebess](https://terebess.hu/english/oxherding.html)). And the tenth picture is not a hermitage: it shows Hotei, the big-bellied monk, "barefoot without shoes", who "enters the marketplace. Without giving any thought to what others may be thinking of him, he opens himself completely with no premeditation and acts and talks to them spontaneously" ([Terebess](https://terebess.hu/english/oxherding.html)).

Read beside D065, the cycle can be taken as the principle told as a journey, with the mapping marked as this compendium's reading rather than the cycle's own claim. The seeking and the taming are the light in use: skill, effort, the outward progression of the early pictures. The ninth picture is the return: the source was never elsewhere, and the commentary's "unnecessary" is said only after the process, never before it. But the cycle does not end at the source; it ends barefoot in the market, which is the shape that makes the return a re-entry and not a retreat. The principle's specific purpose-check between tasks is not narrated in the cycle and is not claimed here; it stays in the principle's own practice section. ([D065](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/D065%20%E2%80%94%20Use%20the%20light%2C%20return%20to%20the%20insight.md))

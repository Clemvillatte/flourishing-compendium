*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 2004 · [George Bonanno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bonanno)*

George Bonanno's research programme, beginning with "Loss, Trauma, and Human Resilience," challenged the clinical assumption that the normal response to loss and trauma is severe distress requiring intervention. His findings, across bereavement, disaster, and loss studies: the most common outcome is resilience, a stable trajectory of healthy functioning, and the majority of people exposed to potentially traumatic events do not develop chronic distress. Grief, in his data, is often brief and functional.

The finding's clinical consequence is a correction to the PTSD model's universalism, and the debate it provoked, including critiques of the measurement of resilience, is part of the record. Its content for this volume is a convergence with the contemplative traditions: impermanence, the traditions claim, is workable, and the empirical finding is that most people, most of the time, work it.

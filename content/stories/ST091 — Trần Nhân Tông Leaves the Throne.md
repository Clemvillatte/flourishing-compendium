*Vietnamese biographical narrative · Vietnam, 13th century CE · [Trần Nhân Tông](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tr%E1%BA%A7n_Nh%C3%A2n_T%C3%B4ng)*

Trần Nhân Tông leads Vietnam to victory over two Mongol invasions, governs with distinction, then abdicates to his son and retires to Yên Tử mountain, where he founds the Trúc Lâm Zen school as a king turned monk. His verse is the school's whole teaching: "Dwelling in the world, joyful in the Way, hunger we eat, rest we sleep," and the tradition he founded is defined by this refusal to separate contemplative life from ordinary obligation.

He did not flee the throne from disgust. He had already engaged it completely, including the war, and the tradition reads the sequence as deliberate: full engagement, then release. Trúc Lâm remains Vietnam's principal indigenous Buddhist school.

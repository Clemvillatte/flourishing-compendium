*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2007/2014 · [Still Alice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Alice)*

Alice Howland, a 50-year-old linguistics professor at Harvard, is diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimer's. The film tracks the losses with clinical patience: the missed word in a lecture, the disappearance of her running route, the plan for suicide by pills with questions she must be able to answer to take them, and the arrival of the day she can no longer answer them. The film ends with her daughter reading to her, and Alice, unable to follow the plot, following the sentence.

The film's existential content is the dissolution of the narrative self, which is the self that the hermeneutic and narrative-identity traditions hold we are. The suicide plan's failure mechanism is the film's precise thesis: the self that made the decision, and the self who would execute it, are not the same self, and the continuity we assume between them is the continuity that the disease breaks. What remains, the film's ending argues, is not the narrative but the relation.

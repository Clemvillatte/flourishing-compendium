*Confucian parable · China, 4th century BCE · [Mencius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mencius)*

Mencius asks his interlocutor to imagine: anyone who suddenly saw a child about to fall into a well would feel alarm and distress. Not to win the gratitude of the parents, not to win the praise of neighbours, not from any calculation at all, but because everyone has a heart that cannot bear the suffering of others. The reaction is the evidence; it precedes any reasoning about what to do.

The passage is the founding text of Confucian moral psychology, and its claim is empirical rather than normative: Mencius argues that the sprouts of virtue, the emotional responses, are native equipment, and that the entire project of self-cultivation is the extension of this immediate response outward, from the child in front of you to the family, the state, and the world.

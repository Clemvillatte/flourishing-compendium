*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1934 · [George Herbert Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Herbert_Mead)*

Mead describes the self emerging in two stages. In play, the child takes single roles: mother, doctor, cowboy, one at a time. In the game, the child must take the role of everyone at once, because shortstop only means something given the whole field. The self, Mead argues, is this internalised conversation with the generalised other; it is a social process that happens to have a body attached.

Mind, Self and Society was assembled from lecture notes after his death, and it remains the clearest statement of the process view of persons. There is no self prior to interaction waiting to be expressed. There is interaction, and the self is one of its products.

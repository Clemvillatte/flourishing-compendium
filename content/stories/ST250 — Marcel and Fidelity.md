*Philosophical narrative · France, 20th century · [Gabriel Marcel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Marcel)*

Gabriel Marcel distinguishes the problem, something solvable by technique and inspection, from the mystery, something in which the inquirer is themselves involved, and argues that human life is made of mysteries, not problems. Fidelity, in his account, is not the keeping of a past promise but the continuous renewal of presence: to be faithful to a person is to be present to them, now, in a way that is not exhausted by the commitment's history.

Being and Having, from which this comes, is Marcel's argument against treating persons as possessions, and its practical content is a critique of the completed relationship: the marriage that has become an asset, the friendship that has become a habit. Fidelity, for Marcel, is a work, and the work is presence, which cannot be banked.

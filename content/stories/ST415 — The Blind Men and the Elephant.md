Canonical Buddhist parable · the Tittha Sutta, Udana 6.4, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation

After monks report sectarians quarrelling, the Buddha tells of a king who has people blind from birth shown different parts of an elephant. Each group describes the whole animal from its part; the conflicting descriptions lead to a fistfight. Read alongside D080, the story shows how a partial view can harden into certainty and conflict. ([Ud 6.4, opening frame and parable](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html))

The king has ordered an attendant to gather the people and show them the elephant. One group is shown its head, another its ear; others are shown its tusk, trunk, body, foot, hindquarters, tail or the tuft at the tail's end. Asked to describe the animal, each group answers from the part it had been shown. ([Ud 6.4, demonstration and answers](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html))

> The blind people who had been shown the elephant's head said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like a jar."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's ear said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like a winnowing basket."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's tusk said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like plowshare."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's trunk said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like the pole of a plow."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's body said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like a granary."

These are the first five answers in [Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation of Ud 6.4](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html).

The rest answer from the foot, hindquarters, tail and tuft: a post, a mortar, a pestle and a broom. Insisting on their conflicting answers, they start hitting one another. ([Ud 6.4, remaining answers and fistfight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html))

> Saying, "The elephant is like this, it's not like that. The elephant's not like that, it's like this," they struck one another with their fists. That gratified the king.

The fistfight passage is quoted from [Ud 6.4](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html).

The Buddha compares the sectarians to the people in the story. His closing verse speaks of attachment to views and ends "people seeing one side." ([Ud 6.4, concluding comparison and verse](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html))

The comparison with D080 is limited to the danger of clinging to one side. The sutta itself retains a distinction between what is beneficial and harmful, and between Dhamma and non-Dhamma; its frame calls the sectarians ignorant of both. No disputant reconsiders their answer in the narrated ending. Those limits matter when bringing a Buddhist parable into a Daoist discussion. ([Ud 6.4, frame and ending](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.6.04.than.html))

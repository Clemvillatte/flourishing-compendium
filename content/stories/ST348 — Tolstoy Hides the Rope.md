*Russia, 19th century · Leo Tolstoy's A Confession, written after the crisis of his late forties; William James used the case in The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902)*

In his own account Tolstoy was "not yet fifty," with, as he tells it, a good wife, good children, a large estate that improved and increased without much effort on his part, and a famous name; none of it explained the crisis ([Part 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-4.html)). The page tells a collapse and a reconstruction; the reconstruction matters. Part 4 describes the man he was:

> I, a man favored by fortune, hid a cord from myself lest I should hang myself from the crosspiece of the partition in my room where I undressed alone every evening, and I ceased to go out shooting with a gun lest I should be tempted by so easy a way of ending my life. ([Part 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-4.html))

The standstill was inner and total ([Part 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-4.html)):

> My life came to a standstill. I could breathe, eat, drink, and sleep, and I could not help doing these things; but there was no life, for there were no wishes the fulfillment of which I could consider reasonable. ... The truth was that life is meaningless.

He told it as an Eastern fable: a man drops into a dry well to escape a beast, finds a dragon at the bottom, and hangs from a twig that two mice gnaw through; he insisted it was "not a fable but the real unanswerable truth intelligible to all" ([Part 4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-4.html)). Honey on the leaves, family and art, no longer consoled him. In Part 7: "I found that for people of my circle there were four ways out" ([Part 7](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-7.html)): ignorance, epicureanism while it lasts, suicide, or weak clinging. Reason, he thought, pointed to suicide: "I saw that this was the worthiest way of escape and I wished to adopt it." What stopped him was "some dim consciousness of the invalidity of my thoughts," and a craving he could call "by no other name than that of a thirst for God" ([James, Lectures VI-VII](https://www.authorama.com/varieties-of-religious-experience-6.html)), against the whole movement of his reasoning.

The turn came from below: he turned to the lives and faith of the Russian working people, finding a meaning his own reasoning had not supplied. As he writes ([Part 13](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-13.html)):

> The simplest laboring people around me were the Russian people, and I turned to them and to the meaning of life which they give. ... This meaning was clear to me and near to my heart.

His relation to faith had changed: "My relations to faith before and after were quite different" ([Part 13](https://www.marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/1882/a-confession/part-13.html)). In James's telling ([James, Lecture VIII](https://www.authorama.com/varieties-of-religious-experience-7.html)), the returning energy "was nothing new. It was my ancient juvenile force of faith, the belief that the sole purpose of my life was to be BETTER"; he gave up "the life of the conventional world, recognizing it to be no life, but a parody on life," and has "felt right and happy, or at least relatively so, ever since."

The compendium reads it as the twice-born route told from inside. James contrasts people whose basic confidence in life remains intact with those who must rebuild a way of living after that confidence breaks. Tolstoy illustrates the second route: the fall, the four exits, the turn, and a second birth in which the renewed willingness to live did not erase his awareness of suffering. In James's terms ([James, Lecture VIII](https://www.authorama.com/varieties-of-religious-experience-7.html)), the once-born live in a "one-storied" world and the twice-born in a "double-storied" one, where "renunciation and despair of it are our first step in the direction of the truth." These are contrasting outlooks, not fixed kinds; James calls actual people "intermediate varieties and mixtures."

**Principles:**
- [[P184 — Distinguish the once-born from the twice-born: two routes through suffering|P184 Distinguish the once-born from the twice-born]] — Tolstoy's collapse and reconstruction illustrate the twice-born route.

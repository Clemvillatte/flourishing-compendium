*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 2001 · [Dan McAdams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_McAdams)*

Dan McAdams's research programme collects life narratives and codes them. The central finding: the stories people tell about their lives, not just the events, predict well-being, and the pattern that predicts best is the redemptive sequence, in which suffering is narrated as leading to growth, insight, or contribution. His work distinguishes contamination sequences, the reverse, and the two patterns are, he argues, the deep structure of American life stories, with religious and political correlates.

The programme is the operationalisation of Ricoeur's narrative identity, and its practical content is a finding the narrative therapies rely on: the same life can be told in either pattern, the telling is not a report on the events but a shaping of them, and the shaping is learnable. The finding's cross-cultural limits, since the redemptive preference is not universal, are part of the honest account.

*Historical/biographical narrative · Vietnam/United States, 1965-1973 · [James Stockdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Stockdale)*

James Stockdale, a naval aviator, is shot down over Vietnam and spends seven and a half years as a POW, four in solitary confinement, repeatedly tortured. He credits Epictetus, whom he had studied at Stanford, with his survival: the Enchiridion's teachings on what is and is not in one's power were, he said, literally a manual for the cell. He organised prisoner resistance, enforced a code of unity, and was awarded the Medal of Honor.

Stockdale's formulation, from his later reflections, is precise: you must never confuse faith that you will prevail in the end with the discipline to confront the most brutal facts of your current reality. The optimists, he observed, the ones who expected release by Christmas, were the ones who died of broken hearts. The Stoic is not the optimist; the Stoic is the one who has stopped needing a forecast.

*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1879 · [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison)*

Searching for a lamp filament, Edison's laboratory tests thousands of materials: platinum, then carbonised cotton thread, then, famously, bamboo from a Japanese fan, after sending scouts to collect samples worldwide. His notebooks record the failures as data. The frequently quoted line, "I have not failed, I've found 10,000 ways that won't work," is a paraphrase, but it is a faithful one: the lab ran as a systematic elimination of hypotheses.

Edison is an awkward figure for this compendium, but the filament search is the purest available case of variation, selection, and retention as a research method. The insight was not in any single test; it was the refusal to stop generating variants until one survived.

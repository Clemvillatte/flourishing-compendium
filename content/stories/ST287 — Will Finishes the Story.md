*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1998/2003 · [Big Fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Fish)*

Edward Bloom has told his son Will fantastic stories his whole life: the giant, the witch with the glass eye, the too-big fish, the Siamese twins, the town of Spectre. Will, a journalist, does not believe any of them and wants to know who his father really was before he dies. At the deathbed, Will realises that the stories are the father, and finishes the last one himself: he carries Edward to the river, where he becomes the big fish, and the funeral, in the film's ending, is attended by the giants, the twins, and the circus ringmaster, who turn out to be real people, ordinary-sized.

The film is the narrative-identity thesis in its most popular form: the self is not a set of facts but a story, and the son's reconciliation is the moment he stops auditing the story and reads it. The detail worth holding is that the funeral's "real" people are ordinary; the film's claim is not that the stories were literally true, but that the truth of a life is the story's, and the auditing of it against the record is a category error.

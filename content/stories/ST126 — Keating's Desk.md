*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1989 · [Peter Weir, Dead Poets Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Poets_Society)*

John Keating, an English teacher at a conservative prep school, has the boys stand on his desk to see the room differently, rips the introduction out of their poetry textbook, and tells them carpe diem: the world looks different from up there, and so will the years. The film ends with a suicide and a firing, and the boys standing on their desks in salute.

The film is usually quoted for the seizing of days, but its actual argument is Mead's and Dewey's: the self is a perspective, and perspectives can be physically practised. Keating's classroom is a laboratory school for the perception of possibility, and the film is honest about what it costs the teacher.

*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2014/2019 · [Bryan Stevenson, Just Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Mercy)*

Bryan Stevenson, a young Harvard-trained lawyer, moves to Alabama to represent death-row prisoners. His most famous client, Walter McMillian, was convicted of murder by a jury that included none of his peers, on the testimony of a witness who recanted repeatedly, in a town where the murder had taken place while McMillian was at a church fish fry, in front of dozens of witnesses. Stevenson wins the exoneration, and McMillian walks free after six years.

The memoir's title is Aristotle's distinction made literal: mercy is not a relaxation of justice but its completion, the correction of a legal process that produced a legally valid and actually false result. Stevenson's argument throughout is that the opposite of poverty is not wealth but justice, and that each wrongfully condemned person is a diagnostic of the system that produced them.

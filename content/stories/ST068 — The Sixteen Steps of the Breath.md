*Buddhist practice narrative · India, 5th century BCE · [Ānāpānasati Sutta, MN 118](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.118.than.html)*

The Buddha teaches mindfulness of breathing in sixteen steps, moving through four tetrads: knowing long and short breaths; experiencing the whole body and calming it; experiencing joy and the mind; seeing impermanence, fading away, cessation, and relinquishment. The instruction is precise about sequence: attention is trained on the breath before it is pointed at the subtler layers.

This is the most detailed meditation manual in the early canon and the root text of every breath meditation taught today, from vipassana retreats to hospital mindfulness programs. Its interest is the progression: nothing is skipped, and each step is verifiable in the body.

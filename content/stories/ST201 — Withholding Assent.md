*Stoic practice narrative · Greece/Rome, 1st century CE · [Epictetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epictetus)*

Epictetus's core instruction: you are not disturbed by things, but by your judgements about things, and the judgement is yours to withhold. An impression arrives, "he insulted me," and the impression is not yet assent. The training consists of catching impressions at the door, asking whether they are true, whether they are useful, and whether they are yours to ratify.

The Enchiridion's first line states it: some things are in our power and some are not, and the things in our power are judgements, impulses, desires. The discipline of assent is the middle discipline, the one between desire and action, and it is the tradition's most original contribution: the discovery of a gap between impression and belief, and the training of that gap.

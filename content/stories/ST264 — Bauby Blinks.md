*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · France, 1997/2007 · [The Diving Bell and the Butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Diving_Bell_and_the_Butterfly)*

Jean-Dominique Bauby, editor of Elle, suffers a massive stroke at 43 and wakes with locked-in syndrome: his mind intact, his body paralysed except for his left eyelid. He dictates a memoir by blinking as an assistant reads the alphabet, letter by letter, and the book is written in the first person, present tense, about the life of the mind inside the diving bell. He describes the imagination's escapes, the memory's voyages, the visits of his children, and the fact that he could end it all by simply not cooperating with his physical therapy.

The book, published days before his death, is the century's most remarkable document on the [[The lived body|lived body]]. Its claim is not triumph over adversity but a phenomenological report: the body's failure changed the world, shrank it to a hospital room, and the consciousness, the butterfly, did not shrink with it. The distinction between the two, the entire theme of embodiment, has rarely been written from inside.

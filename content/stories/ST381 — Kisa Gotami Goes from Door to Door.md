Buddhist commentary legend · Kisa Gotami, in the Dhammapada commentary as translated by Eugene Burlingame (1921).

Kisa Gotami is a poor girl who marries into a rich family and has a son. The boy dies as soon as he can walk. She had never seen death before, and when people came to carry the body away for burning she would not let them. "Placing the dead child on her hip, she went from house to house," asking for medicine ([Burlingame, Buddhist Legends 2, Book 8 story 13](https://archive.org/download/buddhistlegends02burluoft/buddhistlegends02burluoft_djvu.txt)). People told her she was mad. A wise man told her that the Teacher would know.

The Teacher asked her for a pinch of white mustard seed from a house where, in Burlingame's words, "nor son nor daughter nor any other hath yet died" ([Burlingame](https://archive.org/download/buddhistlegends02burluoft/buddhistlegends02burluoft_djvu.txt)). She went to the first house and got the seed. Then she asked, "in the house wherein ye dwell hath son or daughter yet died?" The answer was: "As for the living, they be few; only the dead be many." She gave back the seed and went on to the next door.

By evening the errand had done something else. "I thought 'twas I alone had lost a child, but in every village the dead are more in number than the living." The commentary adds that "hard became the heart the which erewhile was soft with mother's love." Then she laid the child down in a forest and went back to the Teacher with empty hands ([Burlingame](https://archive.org/download/buddhistlegends02burluoft/buddhistlegends02burluoft_djvu.txt)). The Teacher said she had been wrong to think she alone had lost a child, and spoke the verse on the Prince of Death, who "doth take and bear away" a man who has set his heart on sons and herds. She entered the Order of nuns. Later, watching the flames of a lamp, she took their flaring and going out as her meditation, and the Teacher's apparition answered her with Dhammapada 114.

Her own verses in the Therigatha end with a different image: "With dart removed am I, the burden laid down, done what was to be done" ([Hecker and Khema, Thig 10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/thig/thig.10.01.hekh.html)).

The compendium uses this story for B108. She is not told her son is not dead, nor that she should stop. She is given a task that takes the grief into the village, where the common fate gets said in the neighbours' own words.

Limits: the mustard-seed episode comes from the commentary tradition, and the canonical verses excerpted at Access to Insight do not contain it. The story is also cut short on the mother's later grief, and it does not say that others' deaths made her own lighter.

**Principles:**
- [[B108 — Grieve with presence]]: the errand lets a grieving mother carry her child, say what she has lost to others, and find her own loss held among theirs.

*Philosophical metaphor · Germany, 1913 · [Edmund Husserl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Husserl)*

Husserl's instruction is to look at an apple, not to eat or classify it, but to attend to how it appears. You see the front. The back is not seen, and yet it is not absent: it is intended, given as there, as the apple's other side. Walk around it and the new profiles arrive to confirm the intending. Consciousness, Husserl concludes, is always consciousness of something, and the object is given through adumbrations, never all at once.

The example is the front door of phenomenology, the method of describing experience rather than explaining it. Its practical yield is the discovery that perception is not a passive receipt but an intending, a reaching, and that most of what we call seeing is the intending's confidence in profiles we have not checked.

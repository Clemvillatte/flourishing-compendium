*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1877 · [Charles Sanders Peirce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sanders_Peirce)*

"The Fixation of Belief" catalogues the ways people settle their beliefs. Tenacity: hold the belief and ignore doubt. Authority: let the institution settle it. The a priori method: believe what is agreeable to reason, which Peirce notes is really what is agreeable to fashion. And science: submit the belief to a reality that can push back, because only an external check has self-correcting power.

The essay's quiet insight is that the first three methods all work, in the sense of producing settled belief. That is exactly their danger. The scientific method is not the most satisfying; it is the only one whose errors are not permanent by design, and Peirce's argument is that we should prefer it for that reason alone.

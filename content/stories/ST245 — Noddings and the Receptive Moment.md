*Philosophical narrative · United States, 1984 · [Nel Noddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nel_Noddings)*

Nel Noddings, in Caring, describes the caring relation as beginning with a receptivity: the teacher or nurse or parent receives the other's reality, the student's struggle, the patient's fear, and responds to it as it is, not as the professional category predicts. The caring is not a deduction from a moral principle; it is engrossment, a being-filled-with-the-other, followed by a response that stays accountable to the other's actual condition.

The book's significance is its challenge to principle-based ethics from inside education, and its practical yield is a description of what the caring person actually does: attends first, asks what the other's situation really is, and resists the professional reflex of applying the protocol before the receiving. The one-caring and the one-cared-for, Noddings insists, are not two roles but one relation.

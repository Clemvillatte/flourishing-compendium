*Literary narrative · Russia, 1880 · [The Brothers Karamazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brothers_Karamazov)*

In the novel's central chapter, Dostoevsky's Ivan tells Alyosha a parable set in Seville during the Inquisition. Christ returns and is arrested by the Cardinal, the Grand Inquisitor, who visits him in his cell. The Cardinal's accusation: by refusing the three temptations, Christ gave humanity freedom, and humanity cannot bear freedom. The church has corrected the work, taking away the freedom and giving instead miracle, mystery, and authority, and the people are grateful. The chapter ends with the Cardinal waiting for Christ to defend himself, and Christ kisses him on the lips, and the old man shudders and lets him go.

The parable is the novel's philosophical centre, and its existential content is the confrontation between freedom and the systems, religious, political, therapeutic, that manage it on people's behalf. Ivan's own fate, the rebellion against a God he cannot accept and the breakdown that follows, is Dostoevsky's own commentary on the cost of the freedom the parable describes.

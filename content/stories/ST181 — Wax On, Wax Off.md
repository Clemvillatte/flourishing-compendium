*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1984 · [The Karate Kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Karate_Kid)*

Daniel LaRusso wants to learn karate. Mr. Miyagi sets him to waxing cars, sanding floors, and painting fences, saying nothing for days. Daniel explodes; Miyagi demonstrates that the chores were the blocks and strikes, and that Daniel's body has been learning the entire time. The film ends with Daniel winning the tournament, but the scene that matters is the demonstration.

The film is the single most-watched dramatisation of Aristotle's habituation claim, and its mechanism is exactly the ancient one: the practice precedes the understanding, the understanding arrives as a recognition of what has already been installed. Daniel's fury is the natural reaction of a mind that believes learning must look like explanation.

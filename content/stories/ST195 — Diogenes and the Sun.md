*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Diogenes of Sinope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes_of_Sinope)*

Exiled from his native Sinope, Diogenes lives in a jar in Athens. Asked how exile could be borne, he replies that wherever he goes, the sun shines, and that the agora of Athens is as good as the agora of Sinope. The Cynics, whom the Stoics adopted as rough ancestors, made this argument physical: with almost no possessions, no house, and no status, there was nothing left to exile.

The answer's structure matters more than its sass. Diogenes has arranged his desires so that their objects are available everywhere: sunlight, a place to sleep, conversation. The Stoic version, less theatrical, is the same operation: reduce the number of things you need to the number of things are always within your power.

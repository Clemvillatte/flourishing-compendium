*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1996 · [Tim Kasser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Kasser)*

Kasser and Ryan's research programme measures what people value, and finds a reliable pattern: people who prioritise intrinsic goals, personal growth, relationships, community, report higher well-being than people who prioritise extrinsic goals, money, image, fame. The Aspiration Index, the instrument, has been used across cultures, and the findings, including Kasser's book The High Price of Materialism, connect materialistic values to lower well-being and higher ecological impact.

The research's interest is its precision: it does not say money does not matter, since income matters enormously below sufficiency. It says that the relative priority of money as a goal, controlling for actual income, predicts worse outcomes, and the effect holds across the countries studied. The Daoist and Stoic claims about the economy of want are here operationalised as measurable differences in what people are aiming at.

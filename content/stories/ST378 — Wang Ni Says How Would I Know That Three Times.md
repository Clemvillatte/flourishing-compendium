Philosophical dialogue · Zhuangzi, chapter 2, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Nie Que asks Wang Ni whether he knows something that everyone agrees on. "How would I know that?" Wang Ni answers. Does he know what he does not know? "How would I know that?" Then does he know nothing at all? Again, "How would I know that?" ([Eno, §2.17](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). In Legge's rendering, Nie Que asks the second question "again, and he got the same reply" ([Legge, ch. 2 §11](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Wang Ni does not stop there. "Nevertheless, let me state this. How do I know that what I term knowledge is not in fact ignorance? How do I know that what I term ignorance is not in fact knowledge?" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). He then asks about animals. A man who sleeps in a damp place gets a bad back, but does a loach? A man in a tree trembles, but does an ape? Men eat grain-fed beasts, deer eat grass, centipedes relish snakes, owls and crows like mice. Which has the right taste? The beauties Lady Li and Lady Mao made fish dive, birds fly off and deer bolt ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Wang Ni concludes: "As I see things, the sprouts of ren and righteousness, the paths of what is so and what is not, are all hopelessly confused." He cannot tell how to separate them ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Nie Que has an objection: if Wang Ni cannot tell benefit from harm, can the True Man? Wang Ni says the True Man is "spirit-like" and unmoved by burning lakes or frozen rivers, and that death and life do not change him ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

The compendium reads the scene beside D100. "I don't know" here is not an apology or a dead end. Wang Ni stays steady while he says it and uses it to open the next question, and each example shows that the question was built on one creature's idea of what is proper.

Limits: this is a skeptical set-piece, and Wang Ni's calm is a literary effect. The text does not show anyone living by "I don't know" under pressure. The True Man at the end is a mythic figure and no method.

**Principles:**
- [[D100 — Rest in unknowing]]: Wang Ni says "How would I know that?" three times and keeps his footing.

*Buddhist parable · India, 5th century BCE · [Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, DN 22](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.22.0.than.html)*

A monkey, the simile runs, ranges across a whole forest until it is caught and tied to a post; then it stays by the post. The mind tied to a single object, says the commentary tradition, does the same. The training is not to cage the monkey but to give it a post worth staying near.

The image survives in the term "monkey mind," now the most exported Buddhist phrase in English. Its original point is more hopeful than the colloquial one: the monkey is not broken. It just needs somewhere to stand.

*Therapeutic metaphor · 2006 · retold from Aidan Hart, as presented in The Big Book of ACT Metaphors*

You are going somewhere you have wanted to go for as long as you can remember. At the station there are two trains, and both carry signs for your destination. One is odd-looking — hard seats, a bit dirty. The other is familiar and comfortable: air-conditioning, a cinema car, free French cuisine. Obviously, you think, I will take the good train. I could not possibly travel like that.

So you wait. The odd-looking train departs. Another one comes, and departs. You keep waiting for the comfortable train — and here is the question the metaphor turns on: what if the comfortable train never leaves the station?

The station is a life, and the odd train is valued action taken while the feelings are wrong. It leaves on schedule, whether or not you board. The comfortable train is the wait for a better state of mind before the life starts — and waiting, however pleasant, is not travel. The question it leaves you with: is there a step toward what matters that you could take before you feel ready?

Retold from Hart (2006), as presented in Stoddard & Afari, *The Big Book of ACT Metaphors* (2014), ch. 8 (printed pp. 161–162).

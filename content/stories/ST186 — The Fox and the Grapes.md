*Fable, cross-cultural · Greece, 6th century BCE · [The Fox and the Grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fox_and_the_Grapes)*

A fox, unable to reach the grapes, walks away saying they were probably sour anyway. Aesop's tag is: it is easy to despise what you cannot get, and the story is the earliest treatment of what cognitive science now calls cognitive dissonance and motivated devaluation: the reduction of desire by re-describing its object as worthless.

The fable is usually filed under hypocrisy, but its Aristotelian content is subtler. The sour-grapes move is an emotion-regulation strategy, it works, and it is cheap. The question the fable actually raises is whether the strategy is a vice or a skill: whether the fox's problem is the self-deception or the fact that he wanted something and quit, and Aesop leaves it to the reader.

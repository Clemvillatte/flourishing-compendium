Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 5, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

In the state of Lu there is a man named Wang Tai whose foot was cut off as a punishment. Those who follow him are as many as those who follow Confucius. Chang Ji asks Confucius about this: Wang Tai does not teach when he stands or expound when he sits, "yet they go to him empty and return home full" ([Eno, §5.1](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). Legge has the same line: "But they go to him empty, and come back full" ([Legge, ch. 5 §1](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Confucius, who here speaks for the author's view, does not defend his own standing. In Legge: "This master is a sage. I have only been too late in going to him." He says he plans to lead everyone under heaven to him. Chang Ji pushes for the method, and the answer is that life and death can bring no change to Wang Tai, that he sees what unites things where others see differences, and that his mind "roams in the harmony of virtue" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Chang Ji asks again why others would gather around a man occupied only with his own mind. The answer is an image: "No man takes flowing water as his mirror, he observes himself in still water." People see themselves in stillness, and that is why they come. Seeing unity where others see loss, Wang Tai "viewed losing his foot as he would a clod of dirt" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

The compendium reads this beside D004 and D127. The mark of a person's de, here, is what happens to others around them: they arrive empty and leave full, without a lesson. And the person whom a status-minded observer would pass over is the one a famous teacher defers to.

Limits: Confucius is Zhuangzi's spokesman here, and the praise is a literary device. The amputation was a legal punishment, and the story does not say what the man went through. The idea that he treated the loss "as a clod of dirt" should not be used as an expectation for anyone who has lost a limb. Eno has "a foot"; Legge has Wang Tai lose "both his feet." The story says nothing of what Wang Tai says or does, which is the point and also the gap.

**Principles:**
- [[D004 — Cultivate de — your embodied character-power]]: a following built by presence alone.
- [[D127 — No one is worthless]]: the punished man is the one Confucius defers to.

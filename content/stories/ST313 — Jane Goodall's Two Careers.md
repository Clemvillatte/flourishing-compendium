*Biographical narrative · United Kingdom/Tanzania, 1960-present · [Jane Goodall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Goodall)*

Jane Goodall arrives at Gombe Stream in Tanzania in 1960, at 26, with no formal training, and her discoveries, tool use in chimpanzees, hunting, long-term bonds, redefine the boundary between human and animal. She spends decades in the field. In 1986, at a conference on the chimpanzees' conditions, she sees the scale of the destruction, and leaves fieldwork permanently, becoming an activist who travels roughly 300 days a year, and founding Roots and Shoots, the youth programme now in dozens of countries.

The biography's structure is the purpose literature's central finding in life form: purpose is not a single thing but an evolving fit between one's capacities and the world's needs, and the deepest transition is not from less purpose to more but from one's own work to the enabling of others'. Her line, "every individual matters, every individual has a role to play," is the programme's thesis, and she has lived it into her nineties.

*Biographical narrative · United States, 1949 · [Aldo Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Leopold)*

Leopold, in "Thinking Like a Mountain," describes shooting a wolf with his party in the early years of his forestry career, and reaching the dying animal in time to see what he calls a fierce green fire dying in her eyes. He was young, he says, and full of trigger-itch, and believed fewer wolves meant more deer and a hunter's paradise. Since then, he writes, he has seen state after state extirpate its wolves and watch the deer overbrowse the mountain, and he sensed that neither the wolf nor the mountain agreed with him.

The essay is the founding text of the land ethic, the extension of the moral community to soils, waters, plants, and animals, or collectively, the land. Its method is autobiography as ecology: the change in Leopold's view is not an argument's conclusion but the record of a perception, the green fire, that would not fit the professional framework.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · India, 2013 · [Ritesh Batra, The Lunchbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lunchbox)*

In Mumbai, the dabbawalas deliver home-cooked lunches from wives to husbands, with an error rate that management scholars have studied. Ila's lunchbox, intended for her distant husband, is delivered to Saajan Fernandes, a widowed accountant about to retire. She writes a note about the food; he writes back. The correspondence grows into the film's entire relationship, and the two never meet, and the film ends with the possibility of a meeting left suspended in a stairwell.

The film's intersubjective content is the medium: the two selves are constituted entirely for each other, through letters, without the confirming look that Sartre describes as the trap. The relation is built on recognition without observation, and the film's restraint, refusing the meeting, is its thesis: the encounter that would confirm the relation would also, inevitably, re-make it. Set beside principle E070 the film reads as an ethics-of-ambiguity parable: the freedom of each person is disclosed in and by the situation the letters have made, and the suspended stairwell is that situation held open.

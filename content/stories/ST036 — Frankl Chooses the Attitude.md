*Historical/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · Austria, 1946 · [Viktor Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)*

Frankl, a psychiatrist, is imprisoned in Auschwitz and other camps for three years. He observes, carefully and without romance, that prisoners who retained something to live for, a person to return to or a work to finish, endured differently. He does not claim meaning guaranteed survival; most of those he described died. What he reports is a freedom that remained uncapturable even when everything else was taken: the choice of one's own attitude toward circumstances.

The claim is modest and enormous at once. It is not that attitude changes the world. It is that the last piece of territory, the response, cannot be confiscated, and some people were visibly living from it.

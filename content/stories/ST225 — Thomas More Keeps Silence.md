*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United Kingdom, 1966 · [A Man for All Seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Man_for_All_Seasons)*

Thomas More, Lord Chancellor, is asked to sign an oath recognising Henry VIII's supremacy over the Church. He refuses to sign and, crucially, refuses to say why: his silence, he argues, is the law's, and his conscience his own. His family, his friends, and the court press him; the Duke of Norfolk begs him to sign for friendship's sake. He is executed, and his final words on the scaffold are that he dies the king's good servant, but God's first.

Bolt's More is the Stoic figure in Christian dress, and his method is the discipline of assent: the demand is for a public statement, and More's refusal is precisely a withholding, not a counter-claim. The film's law-office scenes, in which More explains to his protégé why the law must be protected even for the devil, are the same principle at a lower temperature: the structures that protect everyone cannot be broken for one good cause, even this one.

*Buddhist metaphor · India, 5th century BCE · [Dhammapada, vv. 46-48](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.04.than.html)*

One who knows the Dhamma, says the Dhammapada, is like a person who looks into a mirror: seeing the smudge, they wash the face. The teaching is a mirror in the sense that it has no content to give; its whole function is to make visible what is already there. The verses then warn of the person who, seeing clearly, does nothing, like a merchant counting another man's cattle.

The metaphor is the tradition's answer to the question of what a teaching actually is. It does not add information. It occasions a seeing, and the seeing is only half; the washing is the practice.

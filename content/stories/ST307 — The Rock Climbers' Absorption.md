*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1975 · [Flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_(psychology))*


Csikszentmihalyi's original studies, collected in Beyond Boredom and Anxiety, began with rock climbers, chess players, surgeons, and dancers, and the interviews produced a consistent phenomenology: complete absorption, loss of self-consciousness, distorted time, and the experience of the activity as autotelic, an end in itself. The conditions were also consistent: clear goals, immediate feedback, and a balance between challenge and skill.

The research programme's later development, including the experience-sampling method that paged participants at random moments, is the source of the field's understanding of everyday engagement, and the original finding has held: the most satisfying hours of a life are not the most pleasant but the most absorbed, and absorption is a condition that can be designed for, in activities and in institutions.

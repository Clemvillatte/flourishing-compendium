*Literary/mythological narrative · Greece, 5th century BCE · [Euripides, Medea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medea)*

Medea, abandoned by Jason for a royal marriage, plans the murder of her children as revenge. Euripides gives her a monologue that remains the definitive portrait of akrasia: I know what evil I am about to do, she says, but passion is stronger than my deliberations. She debates herself, sees the wrong clearly, and does it.

The speech is why Aristotle discusses Medea in his treatment of weakness of will, and why the problem she poses is still unsolved. Her knowledge is not ignorance; it is fully present. The failure is in the machinery that connects knowledge to action, and Euripides' innovation, the self as a contested parliament, is still the best description of it we have.

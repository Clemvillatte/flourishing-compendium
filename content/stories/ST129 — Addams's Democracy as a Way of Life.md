*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1902 · [Jane Addams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Addams)*

In Democracy and Social Ethics, Addams argues that democracy is not a form of government but a way of life, requiring that the people affected by decisions have a real voice in them. The book's cases are drawn from Hull-House: the charity visitor who learns that her standards are one class's habits, the employer who discovers that her workers' perspective is information she lacks.

Addams is the pragmatist tradition's proof that pluralism is an operational requirement, not a mood. The argument is epistemic: any single standpoint is systematically blind to something, and the democratic inclusion of standpoints is the only correction mechanism a society has.

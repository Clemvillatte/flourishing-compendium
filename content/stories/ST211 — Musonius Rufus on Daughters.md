*Historical/biographical narrative · Rome, 1st century CE · [Musonius Rufus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musonius_Rufus)*

Musonius Rufus, Epictetus's teacher, argues that women should study philosophy. His reasoning is not modern sentiment but Stoic logic: virtue is the human excellence, and women are human; the question of whether they should practise it is like asking whether they should have rational souls. He includes daughters, and notes that some men oppose the education of their own daughters, and asks what kind of father this is.

The lectures survive in fragments preserved by Stobaeus, and their interest here is as evidence of what the tradition's core principle does when applied honestly. The Stoic cosmopolis, the city of reason, has no gender requirement, and Musonius simply followed the argument where the culture would not.

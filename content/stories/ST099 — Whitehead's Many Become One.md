*Philosophical metaphor · England/United States, 1929 · [Alfred North Whitehead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_North_Whitehead)*

Whitehead's formula for the deepest fact of the universe is grammatical: the many become one and are increased by one. Each momentary occasion takes in the whole of its past, integrates it, and adds itself as a new datum for what follows. Nothing persists as a substance; everything is an act of prehension, a taking-in, done once.

Process and Reality is one of the most difficult books in the tradition, but the formula is simple enough to hold: reality is occurrences, not things. The implication for a life is direct. If you are a series of integrations rather than a stored essence, then what you repeatedly take in is not your biography; it is your self.

*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 2004 · [Active-constructive responding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_psychology)*

Gable, Reis, Impett, and Asher's research, "What Do You Do When Things Go Right?", identifies four ways people respond to a partner's good news: active-constructive, enthusiastic engagement; passive-constructive, quiet understatement; active-destructive, pointing out the problem; and passive-destructive, changing the subject. The finding: the response to good news predicts relationship quality as strongly as, and in some studies more strongly than, the response to bad news, and the active-constructive response is the strongest predictor.

The research's practical content is a protocol, and it is teachable: when someone shares good news, the first response is the relationship's business. The finding is the field's most direct operationalisation of the rejoicing-in-others' good, the Buddhist mudita, and the measure is the enthusiasm of the response, which is a skill and not merely a feeling.

*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1982 · [Carol Gilligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Gilligan)*

Carol Gilligan, working in Lawrence Kohlberg's moral-development research programme, notices that the tests of moral reasoning systematically scored women lower, and asks whether the fault is in the women or the instrument. In a Different Voice argues the latter: the scoring privileged justice-based reasoning, rules, rights, and universals, while the women in her interviews tended to reason in terms of relationships, context, and care, and the two orientations are not two levels of one scale.

The book's reception, celebration, and criticism, including questions about its empirical grounding and its gender essentialism, is part of its history, and Gilligan later clarified that the voice is not biological but positional. Its enduring contribution is the insistence that care reasoning is a complete moral logic, with its own rigour, and that a psychology that cannot hear it will mistake it for immaturity.

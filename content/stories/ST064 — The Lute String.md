*Buddhist parable · India, 5th century BCE · [Soṇa Sutta, AN 6.55](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)*

Soṇa, a monk from a wealthy family, strains so hard in meditation that his feet bleed and he considers giving up. The Buddha knows he was a lute player and asks: were the strings best when too tight or too slack? Neither, says Soṇa, when neither too tight nor too loose. So with the energy, says the Buddha, and Soṇa soon awakens.

The exchange is brief and famous because it is the tradition's clearest statement about effort itself. Neither the striver's tension nor the relaxer's slack will do; the instrument is tuned, not forced, and the tuning is checked constantly, not once.

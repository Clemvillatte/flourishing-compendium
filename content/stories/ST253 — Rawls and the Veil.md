*Philosophical metaphor · United States, 1971 · [John Rawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rawls)*

Rawls asks what principles of justice people would choose if they did not know their own place in society: not their class, wealth, talents, race, gender, or conception of the good. Behind this veil of ignorance, he argues, rational choosers would not gamble on principles that privilege their actual position; they would protect the worst-off, since they might be them, and they would secure equal basic liberties as the first principle.

A Theory of Justice revived political philosophy from decades of logical-positivist quiet, and its device is the thought experiment as a social relation: the veil forces the chooser to reason from everyone's position at once, since their own is hidden. The communitarian critique, that no real person can be thus abstracted, is part of the story, and Rawls's later work, conceding parts of it, is too.

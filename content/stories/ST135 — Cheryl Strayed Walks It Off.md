*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2012/2014 · [Cheryl Strayed, Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild:_From_Lost_to_Found_on_the_Pacific_Crest_Trail)*

After her mother's death, Strayed's life disintegrates: divorce, heroin, the loss of nearly everything. At 26, with no backpacking experience, she hikes 1,100 miles of the Pacific Crest Trail alone. Her boots are too small and she loses them down a mountainside; her pack, "Monster," is absurdly heavy; she runs out of water and meets rattlesnakes. She keeps walking, and the memoir is the record of a self being rebuilt step by step.

The book's structure is the enactivist claim in narrative form. Strayed does not think her way to a new self; the walking is the thinking, and the trail, with its refusal to negotiate, is the therapist. The transformation is produced by transaction with an environment that does not care about her story.

*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 1990 · [Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihaly_Csikszentmihalyi)*

In Flow, Csikszentmihalyi extends the climbers' finding to ordinary life. He had thousands of participants paged at random moments, and the pattern held: the most satisfying hours were not the most pleasant but the most absorbed, times when a person's skills were fully engaged by a challenge that matched them. The book's chapters then apply the finding to work, leisure, relationships, and the shaping of an entire life.

The popular reading of flow is as peak performance. Csikszentmihalyi's own emphasis, in this book, is different and more demanding: the default state of the unengaged mind is mild dysphoria, and the quality of a life is largely decided by what one does about that, one activity at a time.

*Philosophical narrative · Germany, 1883 · [Thus Spoke Zarathustra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thus_Spoke_Zarathustra)*

Zarathustra's opening speech describes three metamorphoses of the spirit. The camel: the beast of burden, who says "thou shalt" and kneels to be loaded, and carries the weight of inherited duties into the desert. The lion: who says "I will" and destroys the old values, but the lion cannot create new ones, only clear the ground. The child: who says "yes," who is a new beginning, a self-propelled wheel, a sacred yes-saying, and only the child creates.

The parable is Nietzsche's account of the sequence of self-overcoming, and its content is a theory of psychological development: the load-bearing stage, the critical stage, and the creative stage, in that order, with the lion unable to skip to creation and the child unable to skip the critique. The figure of the child is not innocence but the capacity to begin, which is the hardest thing the sequence asks.

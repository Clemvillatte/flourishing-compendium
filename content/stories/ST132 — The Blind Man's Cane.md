*Philosophical metaphor · France, 1945 · [Maurice Merleau-Ponty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Merleau-Ponty)*

Merleau-Ponty describes the blind man's cane. After enough use, the cane stops being an object the man holds and becomes part of his body schema: the man feels the street at its tip, not the vibrations in his palm. The boundary of the body is not the skin; it is wherever the perceptual system currently ends.

The example, from Phenomenology of Perception, is now the standard reference point for extended and embodied cognition. Its significance for practice is direct: tools are not aids added to a fixed body; they are incorporations, and one chooses, deliberately, what to incorporate.

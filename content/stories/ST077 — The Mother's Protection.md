*Buddhist parable · India, 5th century BCE · [Karaṇīya Metta Sutta, Sn 1.8](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.1.08.than.html)*

As a mother would risk her own life to protect her only child, says the sutta, so toward all beings cultivate a boundless heart. The instruction then extends the circle deliberately: above, below, and across, without obstruction, hostility, or enmity, whether standing, walking, seated, or lying down.

The image is chosen with care. It does not say "love everyone as much as your mother loves you," which would be sentimentality. It says: take the strongest protective love you have actually experienced, and practise widening its object until nothing is outside it. Mettā is not a feeling one waits for; the sutta calls it a cultivation, a discipline of direction.

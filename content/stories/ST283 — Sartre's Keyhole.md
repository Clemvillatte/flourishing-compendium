*Philosophical metaphor · France, 1943 · [Jean-Paul Sartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Sartre)*

Sartre's example: a man crouching at a door, listening through the keyhole, absorbed in the scene, conscious of nothing but what he is hearing. Footsteps in the hall. Someone is watching. The man's world transforms: he becomes aware of himself as an object, a shameful thing caught in the act, and this being-seen is a new dimension of his being that his own consciousness cannot produce.

The example, from Being and Nothingness, is the century's sharpest analysis of shame, and its content is the intersubjective structure of selfhood: I am, for myself, a subject, a freedom; but for the other, I am an object, and the other's look is the mechanism by which this dimension is imposed. Sartre's conclusion is that human relations are fundamentally haunted by this asymmetry, which is the darker reading of the recognition dialectic.

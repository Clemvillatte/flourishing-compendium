*Historical/philosophical narrative, cross-cultural · North Africa, 4th century CE · [Augustine of Hippo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo)*

In Book XI of the Confessions, Augustine asks what time is and nearly despairs: if no one asks him, he knows; if he must explain, he cannot. His solution is that the present is not a point but a triple structure: a present of things past (memory), a present of things present (attention), and a present of things future (expectation). The future and the past exist in the mind, as present intentions and present memories.

The analysis is the ancestor of Bergson's duration and of every modern account of temporal experience. Its quiet conclusion is that a life is lived entirely in the attention that joins the other two, which makes attention the whole of the available territory.

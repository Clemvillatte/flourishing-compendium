*Television narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2016-2020 · [The Good Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Place)*

Eleanor Shellstrop dies and is sent, by error, to "the Good Place," a bespoke heaven she did not earn. She is, by her own account, not a good person, and the show's four seasons are a philosophy seminar in sitcom form: can people change, what makes an action good, and does moral philosophy's rule-following capture anything. The finale's answer, in which the afterlife is redesigned as a place where people can complete and then leave, has been the subject of actual philosophical essays.

The show's content is the flourishing-science question in narrative form: is well-being a reward for correct conduct or a practice of growth, and the show's answer, argued by its writers with philosophy consultants, is that the practice is the point, and that no fixed score captures a person. Its ethics consultant, Pamela Hieronymi, has written about the collaboration, and the show became the most-assigned philosophy text in modern universities' first-year courses.

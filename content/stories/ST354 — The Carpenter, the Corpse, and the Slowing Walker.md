Canonical Buddhist teaching with five embedded similes · MN 20, The Relaxation of Thoughts, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu.

MN 20 gives a monk five ways to respond when thoughts of desire, aversion or delusion persist, changing the method when the preceding one has not worked ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)). Its images move from a carpenter's small peg to a stronger man's force, with several different responses in between ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)).

First comes substitution: attend to another theme connected with what is skillful, as a carpenter uses a small peg to knock out a large one ([MN 20, first theme](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)). If the thoughts still arise, scrutinize their drawbacks; the image is a person fond of adornment recoiling from a snake's, dog's or human's carcass hung around the neck ([MN 20, second theme](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)).

If they persist, pay them no attention, like someone closing their eyes or looking away from an unwanted sight ([MN 20, third theme](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)). The next method relaxes thought-fabrication: a man asks why he is walking quickly, slows down, stands, sits, and finally lies down, giving up successively grosser postures ([MN 20, fourth theme](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)).

> "In this way, giving up the grosser posture, he takes up the more refined one." ([MN 20, slowing-walker simile](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

If the thoughts persist despite relaxing thought-fabrication, the fifth response is severe: with teeth clenched and tongue against the roof of the mouth, the monk is instructed to "beat down, constrain, and crush his mind with his awareness"; the image is a stronger man seizing and overpowering a weaker one ([MN 20, fifth theme](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)). Each successful method has the stated result of steadying and concentrating the mind, and the discourse concludes by describing mastery of thought sequences and the ending of craving, fetters and suffering ([MN 20, repeated result and conclusion](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)).

The compendium reads the sequence as a corrective to treating one response as the answer to every condition. The small peg is only the first of five images, and force appears last; these are separate similes, not five incidents in one person's biography ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)).

This reading needs a limit: the source explicitly calls the thoughts blameworthy, so it cannot establish that Buddhist training avoids moral evaluation ([MN 20, second theme](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html)). Its monastic instructions also do not establish B084's modern everyday prescriptions or a clinical treatment for intrusive thoughts.

**Principles:**
- [[B084 — Use antidotes skillfully]]: the five-method sequence changes the response when unskillful thoughts persist, and pairs each method with a concrete image and stated consequence.

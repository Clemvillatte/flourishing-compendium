*Pacific cultural practice narrative · Hawaii, documented from the early 19th century · [Ho'oponopono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho%27oponopono)*

Ho'oponopono, literally "to make right," is the Hawaiian practice of family reconciliation. When conflict disrupts a household, the family gathers with a facilitator, and each person speaks in turn until the wrong is identified, acknowledged by the wrongdoer, and set right; the session closes with a shared meal and, traditionally, a religious sealing. The practice is documented in Hawaiian-language newspapers and court records of the nineteenth century, and it treats relational rupture as a real thing with structure: it must be worked through together, not privately wished away.

The practice's interest is its refusal of the private apology. A wrong between two members of a family, the tradition holds, is a wrong in the whole family's vā, and the repair must be witnessed by everyone it affected. Modern self-help versions of the four phrases, popularised from a later, simplified lineage, are its descendants at a great distance.

Canonical Buddhist narrative · Udana 4.1, Meghiya Sutta

Meghiya finds a mango grove that looks ideal for meditation and repeatedly asks to practise there. Alone beneath a tree, he is troubled by thoughts of sensuality, ill will and harm; he returns to ask the Buddha about them. For B026, the episode distinguishes wanting to exert oneself from knowing what to cultivate, what to abandon and which conditions support that work. ([Ud 4.1](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.4.01.than.html))

Meghiya is serving as the Buddha's attendant. After his alms round, he walks beside the Kimikala River and notices the grove. Back with the Buddha, he asks permission to go; the answer is, "As long as I am still alone, stay here until another monk comes." Meghiya asks again, explaining that he still has work to do in his training. On the third request, the Buddha lets him go. ([Ud 4.1, discovery and requests](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.4.01.than.html))

He goes deep into the grove and sits at the root of a tree. The three kinds of unskillful thoughts occupy him for much of his stay. He is astonished that they can overpower him even though he entered the homeless life through faith; in the late afternoon, he returns and reports what happened. ([Ud 4.1, seclusion and return](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.4.01.than.html))

The Buddha names five qualities that mature an immature release of awareness: admirable companions, virtuous conduct, access to helpful conversation, persistent effort to abandon unskillful qualities and take on skillful ones, and discernment of arising and passing away. He gives friendship a particular role, explaining how admirable companions support the other four qualities. ([Ud 4.1, five supports and companionship](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.4.01.than.html))

Once grounded in those qualities, the monk is to develop further practices: contemplation of the unattractive for passion, good will for ill will, mindfulness of breathing for thinking, and perception of inconstancy for the conceit "I am." One instruction is especially direct: "He should develop good will so as to abandon ill will." ([Ud 4.1, four additional practices](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.4.01.than.html))

The account ends with teaching about understanding and releasing thoughts, without reporting a later successful retreat. Read alongside B026, its contribution is a change in the question: which quality needs attention, and what will help its development? The grove has supplied seclusion; the response supplies a training with different tasks and social supports. ([Ud 4.1, final instruction and verse](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.4.01.than.html))

**Principles:**
- [[B026 — Apply right effort — the four efforts|B026 Apply right effort: the four efforts]]: Meghiya's troubled retreat leads to instructions for abandoning particular unskillful states and developing their supports.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1997 · [Good Will Hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Will_Hunting)*

Will Hunting, a janitor at MIT with a genius for mathematics, can quote any text on love but has never been out of Boston. Sean Maguire, his therapist, tells him about his own dead wife, the dirty jokes she told, the cancer he sat through. Then the line: if I asked you about love, you'd quote me a sonnet, but you can't tell me what it smells like in the Sistine Chapel. You've never actually stood there and looked up at that ceiling.

The scene is the pragmatist critique of representation, delivered as therapy. Will's knowledge is real and empty, because knowledge that has not been verified in experience has, in James's sense, no cash value, and the therapy consists of sending him to get some.

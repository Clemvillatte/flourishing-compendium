*Film narrative, cross-cultural · France, 2001 · [Amélie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9lie)*

Amélie Poulain, a shy waitress in Montmartre, has a catalogue of small pleasures: dipping her hand into sacks of grain, cracking the caramel crust of a crème brûlée with a teaspoon, skipping stones on the Canal Saint-Martin. She decides to bring happiness to others, anonymously, engineering reunions and small justices, and the film's plot is her slow discovery that she cannot give what she will not receive, and her final step, running after the man she has engineered into her life, is the film's whole argument.

The film is popularly filed under whimsy, and its content is the broaden-and-build theory in narrative form. Amélie's small pleasures are the attentional repertoire that broadens her world, and her anonymous good deeds, the film shows, are partly a strategy for avoiding the risk of being known, which is the resource she has not built.

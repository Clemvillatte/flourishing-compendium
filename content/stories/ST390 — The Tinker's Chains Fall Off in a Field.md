Puritan spiritual autobiography · John Bunyan, Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners

He writes from prison, a tinker by his own aside, of "a low and inconsiderable generation". ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) An old copy of Luther's comment on Galatians had fallen into his hand, and "I found my condition in his experience so largely and profoundly handled, as if his book had been written out of my heart." ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) The comfort did not hold: came, he writes, "a more grievous and dreadful temptation than before." ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654))

The temptation came "not so little as a hundred times together": "Sell Christ for this, or sell Christ for that", and resistance took his body: "my very body would be put into action or motion, by way of pushing or thrusting with my hands or elbows; still answering, as fast as the destroyer said, Sell Him; I will not, I will not, I will not, I will not". ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) His peace ran its own weather: "my peace would be in it, and out, sometimes twenty times a day; comfort now, and trouble presently; peace now, and before I could go a furlong, as full of fear and guilt as ever heart could hold." ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654))

One day he was "passing in the field, and that too with some dashes on my conscience", when the sentence fell upon his soul: "Thy righteousness is in heaven". ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) He saw "with the eyes of my soul, Jesus Christ at God’s right hand: there, I say, was my righteousness", and "it was not my good frame of heart that made my righteousness better, nor yet my bad frame that made my righteousness worse; for my righteousness was Jesus Christ Himself". ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) Then: "Now did my chains fall off my legs indeed; I was loosed from my afflictions and irons; my temptations also fled away". ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) At home he could not find that exact sentence in scripture, and says his heart began to sink again; he then connected it with 1 Corinthians 1:30. ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) The graces still green on him were "but like those cracked groats and fourpence-halfpennies that rich men carry in their purses, when their gold is in their trunks at home: Oh! I saw my gold was in my trunk at home!" ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654))

This page compares Bunyan's account with P184's twice-born route: peace sought through suffering and inner conflict. ([P184](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/P184%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20the%20once-born%20from%20the%20twice-born%3A%20two%20routes%20through%20suffering.md)) His earlier distress is not presented as a once-born stage. ([P184](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/P184%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20the%20once-born%20from%20the%20twice-born%3A%20two%20routes%20through%20suffering.md)) Bunyan attributes his relief to Christ's righteousness, which he says his changing inner condition could neither improve nor diminish. ([Bunyan](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/654)) That is the form his reported resolution takes, not a mechanism P184 requires of every twice-born experience. ([P184](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/P184%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20the%20once-born%20from%20the%20twice-born%3A%20two%20routes%20through%20suffering.md)) The comparison illustrates one route without prescribing suffering, assigning readers a type, or ranking their worth by what they have endured. ([P184](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/P184%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20the%20once-born%20from%20the%20twice-born%3A%20two%20routes%20through%20suffering.md))

**Principles:**
- [[P184 — Distinguish the once-born from the twice-born: two routes through suffering|P184 Distinguish the once-born from the twice-born]]: Bunyan's retrospective account illustrates peace sought amid severe inner conflict; it does not narrate the once-born route or prescribe the same resolution to others.

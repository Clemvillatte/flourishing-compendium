*Philosophical narrative · Germany, 1807 · [Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Wilhelm_Friedrich_Hegel)*

The Phenomenology's most famous passage: two self-consciousnesses meet, and each demands recognition from the other, since selfhood requires being acknowledged by another self. The struggle that follows produces the master-slave relation, and Hegel's analysis of the asymmetry, that the master's recognition is worthless because it comes from a slave who is not recognised as a self, is the engine of the whole dialectic.

The passage is the source of Marx's class analysis, Fanon's colonial analysis, Beauvoir's analysis of the sexes, and the modern recognition theories of Honneth. Its content is a claim about the structure of selfhood: it is not a possession but a social achievement, and its achievement requires a symmetric relation, which the master-slave relation by definition cannot provide. Beauvoir's use of the dialectic in The Second Sex is the one this wiki carries forward: a woman is positioned as the Other, given immanence instead of transcendence, and the struggle is over the terms of that positioning.

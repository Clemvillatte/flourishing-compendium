*Buddhist biographical narrative · India, 5th century BCE · [Kesamutti (Kālāma) Sutta, AN 3.65](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)*

The Kālāmas tell the Buddha that teachers pass through their town contradicting each other, each claiming sole truth. They do not know whom to trust. The Buddha's answer is the tradition's most quoted and most misquoted: do not go by scripture, tradition, rumour, or even logical inference. Know for yourselves which things, when you undertake them, lead to harm and which to welfare.

The sutta is not a licence for opinion. It is a protocol: test teachings in your own conduct and observe the results. It predates pragmatism by twenty-four centuries and states its criterion more plainly.

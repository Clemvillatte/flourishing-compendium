*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2006 · [Little Miss Sunshine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Miss_Sunshine)*

The Hoover family drives from Albuquerque to Redondo Beach, California, so that seven-year-old Olive can compete in the Little Miss Sunshine pageant. The family: a failing motivational speaker father, a heroin-using grandfather, an uncle who has just attempted suicide, a brother who has taken a vow of silence, and a mother holding it together. The van's clutch fails, and the family must push-start it, running alongside and jumping in, and this becomes the film's central image, repeated. When Olive performs the striptease her grandfather taught her, and the pageant organisers are horrified, the family joins her on stage.

The film's content for the personalisation literature is the fit question, dramatised: the family that cannot function by any standard model, and that the film does not idealise, turns out to be exactly the environment in which each member, the father's failure, the brother's silence, the uncle's despair, becomes workable. The film's argument, which is not sentimental, is that the standard is the thing that does not fit, and the family's dysfunction, pushed along at fifteen miles an hour, is the vehicle.

*Buddhist jātaka narrative · India, ancient · [Jātaka tales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jataka_tales)*

A bodhisattva walking in the forest finds a tigress too weak from hunger to feed her cubs, who is about to eat them. He reasons that a body is a vehicle for suffering anyway, and lies down where she can reach him, ending his life so that hers and the cubs' continue. The tale is one of the tradition's most famous jātakas, stories of the Buddha's previous lives.

The story is hard to read literally, and most commentary does not ask us to. Its function is to mark the far end of a spectrum: compassion is not a feeling but a willingness to be spent. The tradition places the extreme case at the edge of the map on purpose, to orient the ordinary ones.

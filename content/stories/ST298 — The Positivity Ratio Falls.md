*Scientific narrative · United States, 2013 · [Positive psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_psychology)*

The claim, influential in the 2000s, held that flourishing required a ratio of positive to negative emotion above about 3:1, a "tipping point" derived from non-linear dynamics. In 2013, Nicholas Brown, Alan Sokal, and Harris Friedman published "The Complex Dynamics of Wishful Thinking," demonstrating that the mathematics behind the ratio was unfounded: the equations were not applicable to the data, and the number itself was an artefact. Fredrickson and Losada retracted the empirical application, and Losada's earlier modelling was separately discredited.

The episode is the field's most instructive failure, and its inclusion here is deliberate. The claim was popular because it was actionable, and it was wrong because the maths did not support it, and the correction came from outside the field. The principle that survives the debunking is more modest and better supported: the ratio of positive to negative affect predicts some outcomes, some of the time, and is not a threshold.

*Scientific narrative · United States, 1995 · [Belongingness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belongingness)*

Baumeister and Leary's review, "The Need to Belong," synthesises decades of evidence for the claim that the need to form and maintain relationships is a fundamental human motivation, not a derived preference: people form bonds readily, resist breaking them, suffer when they break, and the suffering is specific, not just general distress. The review covers attachment, groups, loneliness, and grief, and concludes that belongingness is like a need, with deprivation and satiation effects.

The paper is one of the most cited in the field's history, and it is the bridge between the relational philosophies and the empirical literature. Its content is a claim about the architecture of the human being, that social bonds are not instrumental goods but constitutive ones, and the later findings, including the mortality meta-analysis below, are its consequences.

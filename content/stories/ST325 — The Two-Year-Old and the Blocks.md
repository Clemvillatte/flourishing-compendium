*Phenomenological/scientific narrative · contemporary · [Child development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_development)*

A two-year-old stacks blocks. The tower falls. She tries again, and the second tower falls, and she adjusts the base, widening it, and the tower stands. The observation is the most ordinary thing in the world, and it is the developmental foundation of the creativity and play literature: the child is not following a rule, not imitating a model, and not yet self-conscious about failure. She is engaged in the cycle, attempt, observe, adjust, that every definition of creativity describes.

The vignette's content for this volume is the claim that the mini-c creativity is native equipment, not a taught skill. The play literature, including the research on pretend play's relation to executive function, converges on the finding that the cycle is present before language, and the adult creativity question is what happens to it.

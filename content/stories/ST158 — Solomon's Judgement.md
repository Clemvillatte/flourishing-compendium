*Mythological/historical narrative, cross-cultural · Middle East, traditionally 10th century BCE · [Judgement of Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judgement_of_Solomon)*

Two women who shared a house both claim a surviving infant after a second child dies in the night. Solomon orders the child cut in two and half given to each. One woman says give it to her, do not kill it. The other says let it be neither mine nor yours. Solomon awards the child to the first.

The story is the canonical case of practical wisdom as diagnosis: the rule is a device, not a verdict, and its function is to force the truth about the parties into the open. Solomon's judgement is not the sword, which is theatre; it is the reading of the two reactions, which no interrogation had produced.

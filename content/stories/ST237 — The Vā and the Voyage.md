*Pacific cultural narrative · Polynesia · [Samoan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_culture)*

In Samoan and Tongan thought, the vā is the relational space between people, and it is not empty. It is the sacred, maintained space that must be kept clear through respect, reciprocity, and care, and the concept governs everything from seating arrangements to the order of speakers at a gathering. The ocean-going canoe is the vā at its largest: a small vessel in which survival depended absolutely on the maintenance of the relational space among the crew.

The concept is the Pacific's contribution to the world's relational philosophies, and its practical content is precision about maintenance: relations are not a state but a kept space, and the keeping is a daily practice with techniques, from the Tongan faka'apa'apa, respect behaviours, to the protocols of the kava circle. Neglect the space, and it does not stay neutral; it fills.

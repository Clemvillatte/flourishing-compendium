*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States/China, 2019 · [Lulu Wang, The Farewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Farewell)*

Billi, a Chinese-American writer, learns that her grandmother, Nai Nai, has terminal cancer, and that the family has decided not to tell her, organising a wedding as the pretext for a final gathering. Billi, raised in the American framework of individual autonomy and disclosure, is told that in China the family bears the burden so the patient can live her time in peace. She keeps the secret, and the film's ending, in which Nai Nai waves cheerfully at the departing family, is deliberately unresolved about whether the secret was right.

The film is the single best study in cinema of a conflict between two of [[Self-determination|self-determination theory]]'s basic needs: the autonomy of the patient's right to know, and the relatedness of the family's shared burden. The film's argument, which is not a resolution, is that the two needs are not always jointly satisfiable, and that the choice between them is not made by theory but by the family, in its own language.

Instructional illustration, quoted from a practice manual · Bhante Gunaratana's sequence of what happens when a meditator notices a distraction; with the sutta's own instruction on the breath

The Satipatthana Sutta opens with the claim that the four frames of reference are the direct path for the purification of beings ([MN 10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)), and its first instruction for the body is on the breath: "Always mindful, he breathes in; mindful he breathes out." ([MN 10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html))

What the sutta states as instruction, Gunaratana's chapter narrates as a sequence, and the sequence is the working shape of the attention the sutta asks for. "Watch the sequence of events: Breathing. Breathing. Distracting thought arises. Frustration arising over the distracting thought. You condemn yourself for being distracted. You notice the self condemnation. You return to the breathing. Breathing. Breathing. It's really a very natural, smooth-flowing cycle, if you do it correctly." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The noticing in that sequence is the whole point, and the book says so in its own words. "if you want to change something, the first thing you have to do is see it the way it is." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html)) The function is named and given a working description: "Mindfulness is a function that disarms distractions, in the same way that a munitions expert might defuse a bomb." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html)) The sorting is what matters, not the object: "It actually makes rather little difference what the object of mindfulness is." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The chapter's larger claim is stated plainly: "The purpose of meditation is to achieve uninterrupted mindfulness. Mindfulness, and only mindfulness, produces Enlightenment." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html)) The claim that makes the faculty trainable runs the same way: "Mindfulness grows by the exercise of mindfulness. It is like exercising a muscle. Every time you work it, you pump it up just a little. You make it a little stronger." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html)) Gunaratana describes the stakes: "Vipassana meditation is not a game. Clear awareness is more than a pleasurable pastime. It is a road up and out of the quagmire in which we are all stuck, the swamp of our own desires and aversions." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The chapter quoted is meditation instruction, and the story is the book's instructional illustration of that instruction. This illustration concerns returning attention during formal meditation. It does not demonstrate an everyday-life application, and it does not establish Gunaratana's claim that mindfulness alone produces enlightenment. ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

**Principles:**
- [[B011 — Make mindfulness the master tool (sati)|B011 Make mindfulness the master tool (sati)]]: the chapter's sequence of breathing, distraction, frustration, self-condemnation, noticing, and return carries the sutta's attention-training instruction in working form, and the book's own passages carry sati as the primary, trainable faculty; the chapter does not give an everyday-life application, and the claims about what mindfulness produces are quoted as assertions. ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

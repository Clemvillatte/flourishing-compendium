*Zen biographical narrative · Japan, 13th century CE · [Dōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dgen)*

Dōgen returns from training in Song China and teaches that zazen is not a means to enlightenment but enlightenment itself. His instructions for sitting, the Fukanzazengi, are procedural to the point of tedium: the posture, the hands, the breath, the eyes. His great essay Genjōkōan then states the view: to study the self is to forget the self; to forget the self is to be actualised by the ten thousand things.

The two texts together are the answer to the student's eternal question, what does practice produce? Dōgen's answer is grammatically strange and exactly meant: the sitting is not for anything. It is the life of the thing it was supposed to produce.

*Scientific/biographical narrative · United States, 2001 · [Barbara Fredrickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Fredrickson)*

Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory argues that positive emotions do something different from negative ones: negative emotions narrow attention and behaviour to the threat, while positive emotions broaden the repertoire of thought and action, building physical, intellectual, and social resources over time. Joy, the theory holds, prompts play; interest prompts exploration; contentment prompts [[Savoring|savouring]], and each builds durable assets.

The theory is the field's central account of why positive emotion is not merely pleasant but functional, and it displaced the simple view that positive affect is just the absence of negative affect. The research's subsequent history, including critiques of the lab's methods and the correction of specific findings, is part of the record, and the core mechanism, the broadening effect on attention and cognition, remains well-supported.

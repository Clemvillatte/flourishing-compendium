*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1730s · [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin)*

In his autobiography, Franklin describes, at about 20, a project to arrive at moral perfection. He identified thirteen virtues, gave each a short precept, and made a little book with a page per virtue and columns per day of the week, marking every fault with a black spot. He focused on one virtue per week, cycling through four times a year. He never became perfect, and the book's punchline is his conclusion that the practice, not the perfection, was the gain.

Franklin's chart is the first self-tracking protocol on record, three centuries before apps. Its interest is structural: he discovered in practice what Aristotle argued in theory, that a disposition is built by attending to its instances one at a time, and that the tracking itself is the habituation.

*Sporting/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · Switzerland, 1998-present · [Roger Federer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Federer)*

For two decades, opponents and commentators described Federer's game with the same word: effortless. The strokes look simple, the movement looks unhurried, and the ball arrives earlier and heavier than it should. His rivals hit harder and ran more. What Federer seemed to do was remove movements, arriving already balanced where others scrambled.

The Daoist reading is that his genius is subtractive. Where others add power, he removes friction: early preparation, economical footwork, no wasted rotation. David Foster Wallace, watching him, wrote that watching Federer at his best was a religious experience because you were seeing a human being do something almost impossible, and doing it lightly.

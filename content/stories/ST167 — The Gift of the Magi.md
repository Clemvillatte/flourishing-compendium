*Literary narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1905 · [The Gift of the Magi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gift_of_the_Magi)*

A young wife sells her hair to buy a chain for her husband's watch. The husband sells the watch to buy combs for her hair. Each gift is now useless, and O. Henry names them the two foolish children who were also the wisest of all who give gifts, for in giving they saw the other as the whole point.

The story is a Christmas-card staple, and its logic is Aristotle's, exactly. The gifts fail on the plane of utility, the second-best kind of exchange, and succeed completely on the plane of mutual giving, the highest, in which the point of the transaction is the other person rather than the object.

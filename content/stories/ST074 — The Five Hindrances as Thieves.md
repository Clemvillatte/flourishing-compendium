*Buddhist parable · India, 5th century BCE · [Sabbāsava Sutta, MN 2](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.002.than.html)*

The Buddha catalogues five hindrances that obscure the mind: sensual desire, ill will, sloth and drowsiness, restlessness and worry, and doubt. The sutta's instruction is not to fight them but to know them: to notice when a hindrance is present, when it is absent, and how it arose and passed away.

The inventory is the tradition's diagnostic framework, and it is the direct ancestor of every modern protocol for monitoring one's own states. Its key move is grammatical: the hindrance is an object of observation, not an identity. One does not say "I am restless"; one notices restlessness arising. That shift is the practice.

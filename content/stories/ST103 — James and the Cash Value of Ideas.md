*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1907 · [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James)*

James popularises the maxim with a metaphor that stuck and annoyed: true ideas are those we can assimilate, corroborate, and verify; the cash value of an idea is what it buys in experience. He is careful to say the metaphor is about verification, not profit: the question is what difference the idea makes when lived.

Pragmatism's critics heard "whatever works is true." James's actual point is narrower and harder: an idea that makes no difference to any possible experience is empty, and one that consistently leads you into walls is false regardless of how elegant it is. The metaphor of currency was chosen to insist that ideas are instruments, not mirrors.

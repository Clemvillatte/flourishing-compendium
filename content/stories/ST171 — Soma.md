*Literary narrative, cross-cultural · England, 1932 · [Aldous Huxley, Brave New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brave_New_World)*

In the World State, citizens receive soma, a euphoric with no hangover, and are conditioned from birth to consume it at any discomfort. Negative emotion has been eliminated, and with it art, science, love, and religion: "You've got to choose between happiness and what people used to call high art," the World Controller explains. "We have the welfare of the whole world at heart. We're happy."

The novel's argument is aimed at exactly the Aristotelian target: the elimination of suffering is not the same as the cultivation of flourishing, and a life engineered for pleasantness is compatible with the total absence of anything worth doing. The Savage's demand, "I'm claiming the right to be unhappy," is the book's whole thesis in five words.

*Philosophical fragment · Greece, c. 500 BCE · [Heraclitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraclitus)*

The surviving fragment says it plainly: upon those stepping into the same rivers, different and again different waters flow. The river is not the same river; the person is not the same person. Heraclitus offers no consolation and no advice; he makes a claim about the basic character of things, and it is a claim about flow rather than substance.

The fragment matters here because everything downstream in this Part depends on it. If the person is a process rather than a thing, then a psychology of fixed traits is studying the river's name rather than the water, and every flourishing practice becomes a question of steering rather than of being.

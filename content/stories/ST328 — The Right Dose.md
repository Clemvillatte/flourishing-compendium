*Scientific narrative · United States, 2013 · [Kennon Sheldon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennon_Sheldon)*

Sheldon and colleagues' research on the practice of gratitude found a dose and frequency effect: participants who practised gratitude once a week showed larger increases in well-being than those who practised daily, and the mechanism is habituation, the adaptation to a repeated stimulus that reduces its effect. The finding, part of the positive-activity intervention literature, is a corrective to the more-is-better assumption.

The study's content for this volume is the personalisation principle in its most concrete form: the intervention's design, not merely its content, determines the effect, and the design includes the frequency, the timing, and the person's habituation curve. The contemplative traditions' warnings about rote practice, the Daoist and Buddhist critiques of mechanical ritual, are here measured as a real effect.

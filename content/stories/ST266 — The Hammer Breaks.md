*Philosophical metaphor · Germany, 1927 · [Martin Heidegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Heidegger)*

Heidegger's central example: when we hammer, the hammer is not an object with properties that we contemplate; it is ready-to-hand, transparent, absorbed in the task, invisible. It becomes present-at-hand, an object with features, only when it breaks. The world of everyday activity is a web of such equipment, and its usual mode is withdrawal.

Being and Time is the century's most influential philosophy, and its critics and the author's political history are part of any honest account. The hammer example, however, has travelled far beyond its origin, into design, ergonomics, and the psychology of tools: the best technology disappears in use, and it becomes visible, as an object, only in failure.

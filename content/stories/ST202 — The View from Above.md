*Stoic contemplative exercise · Rome, 2nd century CE · [Marcus Aurelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Aurelius)*

Marcus practises and prescribes an exercise: imagine looking down at the earth from above, the harvests, the marriages, the deaths, the courts, the wars, all at once, the whole ant-colony of human affairs. The exercise appears in the Meditations and has antecedents in Cicero's Dream of Scipio. Its purpose is not nihilism but scale: seen from there, the insult, the loss, the reputation, do not change size, but their apparent weight does.

The exercise is the tradition's most portable practice, and modern distance-perspective research on reappraisal has studied its descendants. Its mechanism is stated plainly in the Meditations: nothing that happens to you is new under the sun, and the regular contemplation of that fact is not depressing but steadying.

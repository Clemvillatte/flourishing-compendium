*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2016 · [Moana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moana_(2016_film))*

Moana, the daughter of a Polynesian chief, is drawn to the ocean against her father's insistence that the island is sufficient. The crops fail; the fish leave. She leaves, with the demigod Maui, to restore the stolen heart of Te Fiti, the island goddess, and the voyage, guided by the wayfinding her people had abandoned, restores both the goddess and the tradition. The film's story consultants included the master navigator Nainoa Thompson, and the wayfinding depicted, reading swells, stars, and birds, is real technique.

The film's relational content is the diagnosis: the island's sickness and the ocean's sickness are one sickness, the severance of the relationship between people, sea, and tradition, and the cure is a voyage, not a harvest. Moana's identity is restored not by leaving her people but by returning to them with the tradition restored, which is the film's statement of what a self is.

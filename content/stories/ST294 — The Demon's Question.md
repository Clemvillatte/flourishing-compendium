*Philosophical metaphor · Germany, 1882 · [The Gay Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gay_Science)*

Nietzsche's most famous thought experiment: suppose a demon crept after you into your loneliest loneliness and said that this life, as you now live it and have lived it, you will have to live again, innumerable times, with every pain and every joy, in the same sequence, and nothing new in it. Would you curse the demon and gnash your teeth, or would you say: you are a god, and never have I heard anything more divine?

The passage, from The Gay Science §341, precedes Zarathustra's doctrine of eternal recurrence, and Nietzsche's framing is careful: the question is a test, not a cosmology. The criterion it supplies for every choice is the only one the passage offers, and it has become the tradition's sharpest question: would you live this again. The answer is the measure of the life.

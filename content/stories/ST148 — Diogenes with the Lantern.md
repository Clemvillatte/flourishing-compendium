*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Diogenes of Sinope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diogenes_of_Sinope)*

Diogenes walks through Athens in full daylight carrying a lit lantern. Asked what he is doing, he says he is looking for a human being. The anecdote is one of the best attested of the Cynic repertoire, and its sting is usually missed: he is not saying humans are rare. He is saying that a human being, the thing whose function is reason exercised in practice, is what he has never actually seen.

The gesture is a Socratic experiment turned street theatre. It presumes the function argument: there is something a human being is for, most people are not doing it, and the lantern makes the gap visible at noon.

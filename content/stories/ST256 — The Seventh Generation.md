*Indigenous cultural narrative · North America · [Haudenosaunee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee)*

The Haudenosaunee Great Law of Peace includes the instruction that the lords of the confederacy, in all their deliberations, must consider the impact of their decisions on the seventh generation to come. The principle, cited in the nations' constitution and by modern Haudenosaunee delegates in public testimony, including to the United Nations, extends the relational web through time, not just space: the people affected by every decision include those not yet born.

The principle's influence on modern environmental thought, including the mention of future generations in international law and the rights-of-nature movement, is well documented. Its philosophical content is the refusal of the present tense as the boundary of the community: a decision is made among the living, the dead, and the unborn, and the tradition treats the unborn as parties to the deliberation.

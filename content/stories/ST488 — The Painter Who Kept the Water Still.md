Twentieth-century life, documented · Agnes Martin's 1967 departure from New York, the camping years and the return to painting, from the Art Canada Institute biography and her Writings (ed. Dieter Schwarz).

In late 1967, Agnes Martin burned all of the art left in her New York studio, gave away her art supplies, and left ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)). She had finally established the career she had pursued for two decades ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)). Later, she offered different explanations for going, including: "I thought I would experiment in solitude, you know. Simple living." ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)) She also wrote, "I left New York in 1967 because every day I suddenly felt I wanted to die and it was connected with painting. It took me several years to find out that the cause was an overdeveloped sense of responsibility." ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography))

She spent the proceeds of a $5,000 arts grant on a Dodge pickup and an Airstream trailer, then spent a year and a half camping across Canada and the American West ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)). "I drove all in the West and up in Canada and couldn't make up my mind where to stop," she recalled ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)). What stopped her was odder than any plan: "I had a vision of an adobe brick," she said. "And I thought, that means I should go to New Mexico." ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)) She leased fifty acres on Mesa Portales near Cuba, New Mexico, lived in her trailer and began building an adobe house ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)).

In "The Current of the River of Life Moves Us," Martin described stillness as part of her working practice ([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf)):

> "To live and work by inspiration you have to stop thinking."

([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf))

She added that you "have to hold your mind still" in order to hear inspiration ([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf)). And in her Reflections she insisted that the waiting itself is not a malfunction: "To feel insufficient, to experience disappointment and defeat in waiting for inspiration is the natural state of mind of an artist" ([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf)).

In her "Lecture at Cornell University," she wrote ([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf)):

> "Inspiration is there all the time. For everyone whose mind is not clouded over with thoughts whether they realize it or not."
>
> "Of course we know that an untroubled state of mind cannot last. So we say that inspiration comes and goes but really it is there all the time waiting for us to be untroubled again."

([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf))

In 1974 Martin built a thirty-five-foot-square log-cabin-style studio on the property and began painting again ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)). Her first exhibition at Pace Gallery opened on March 1, 1975 ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)).

### Action

When your work goes dark, ask first whether the water has been stirred. Before adding effort, try two minutes of doing nothing at all, and see what becomes audible.

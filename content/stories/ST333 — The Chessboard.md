*Therapeutic metaphor · 1999 · retold from Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy*

Your inner life is a chess game: some thoughts and feelings are played as white, some as black, and the war between them can feel like the most important thing happening to you. A normal day is a series of moves and counter-moves — a burst of confidence attacked by a memory, a hope defended against a prediction.

The switch the metaphor asks for is this: you are not any of the pieces. You are the board. The pieces fight on you, and the fight is real, and the board holds all of it — white and black, attack and loss — without being any of it.

The point is a shift in perspective, not a claim that the game does not matter. From the position of a piece, you can only win or lose the current exchange. From the position of the board, you can notice the whole game — including that it is a game — and then choose your next move.

Retold from Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson (1999, pp. 190–192), as presented in Stoddard & Afari, *The Big Book of ACT Metaphors* (2014), pp. 118–119.

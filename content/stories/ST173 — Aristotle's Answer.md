*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

Asked what he had gained from philosophy, Aristotle is reported to have answered: to do without being ordered what others do from fear of the laws. The anecdote is preserved by Diogenes Laertius, and its brevity is the point: it is the shortest complete statement of virtue as second nature.

The sentence contains the whole habituation theory. The many behave when watched; the person of practical wisdom behaves identically when no one is watching and nothing is threatened, because the good act has become what they most want to do. The freedom Aristotle describes is not political; it is the freedom of not needing the enforcement.

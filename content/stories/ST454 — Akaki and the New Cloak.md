Nineteenth-century fiction · Nikolai Gogol, The Overcoat (Shinel); the Claud Field translation ([Gutenberg ebook 36238](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt))

In Gogol's tale a copy clerk who loves his work is drawn, by the ruin of his old cloak and the price of a new one, into months of decisions with a tailor, and the narrator marks how much livelier he becomes. Beside P193, the cloak months are the stretch of his life densest with chosen acts ([Field](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

Akaki Akakievitch copies, and loves it: "He worked with a passionate love of his task", and while he copied "a world of varied beauty rose before his eyes" ([Field, copying](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). Changing headings and pronouns exhausts him; he begs: "No! Please give me again something to copy." ([Field, copying](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt))

The turn arrives through the cloak. Petrovitch the tailor refuses to repair the old one; a new one costs eighty roubles, and his habit of saving a kopeck for every rouble has already put half aside ([Field, savings](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). A larger bonus than hoped lets him buy the cloth sooner, and Petrovitch sews the cloak over fourteen days and brings it finished before the office ([Field, savings](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). The narrator marks the change: "From that time he became more lively", and "All that was indecisive and vague in his gait and gestures had disappeared" ([Field, anticipation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

After a celebration evening, men rob him on a dark square ([Field, theft](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). At the first official's door, when the clerks ask his business, "Then for the first time in his life Akaki displayed an energetic character", though he leaves not knowing whether anything will be done ([Field, reprimand](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). The important personage he is sent to next rebukes him for coming directly instead of following procedure, and the scolding frightens him nearly unconscious: "disgraceful to see young people so rebellious towards their superiors" ([Field, reprimand](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). Fever follows, with his thoughts "continually revolving round a cloak"; he dies, and "The great city of St Petersburg continued its life as though he had never existed." ([Field, death](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt))

A coda follows: a ghost strips cloaks from passers-by "without any regard for rank or title" and ends by taking the rebuking personage's cloak: "I need your cloak. You did not trouble about me when I was in distress" ([Field, ghost](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). Afterwards that personage no longer scolds subordinates without first hearing them ([Field, ghost](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

The compendium's reading for P193: the principle locates consciousness in choice, and Akaki's cloak months are the stretch of his life densest with chosen acts; Gogol marks the change in gait and gestures. Two limits are kept: the copying was loved, so routine here is not absence of awareness, and after the theft Akaki acts with energy. The tale is fiction; the page does not carry the principle's everyday account of attention.

**Principles:**
- [[P193 — Consciousness is choice; where there is no choice, there is mechanism|P193 Consciousness is choice]]: the cloak months give Akaki a run of chosen acts, and the narrator marks him livelier for it; the reading stays within what the tale shows.

*Literary narrative, cross-cultural · Russia, 1886 · [Leo Tolstoy, The Death of Ivan Ilyich](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/861/861-h/861-h.htm)*

Ivan Ilyich's dying is described with a precision that has not been improved on. The pain, the failing treatments, the family's performance of hope, and above all the fear, which is not of death in the abstract but of the thing he cannot name, the wrongness of a life that everyone around him insists was correct. His scream lasts three days. It stops when he stops resisting, and the last hour, Tolstoy writes, was the most terrible and the most important.

The novella is the tradition's most complete study of the deathbed audit, and its existential content is the discovery: the wrongness was not a moral failure but an [[The unexamined life|unexamined life]], assembled from other people's examples, and the fear is the self's protest at the end of a life it did not choose. The text is a set text in medical education for exactly this reason.

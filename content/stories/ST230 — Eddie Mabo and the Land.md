*Historical/biographical narrative · Australia, 1982-1992 · [Mabo v Queensland (No 2)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mabo_v_Queensland_(No_2))*

Eddie Koiki Mabo, a Meriam man from Murray Island in the Torres Strait, works as a gardener at James Cook University, where he learns that Australian law considers his ancestral land crown land, his people's ownership extinguished by the doctrine of terra nullius, land belonging to no one. He and fellow plaintiffs spend ten years in the High Court, and he dies five months before the 1992 decision that bears his name, which recognised native title for the first time and overturned the doctrine that had justified the continent's seizure.

The case's interest for this volume is its relational conception of land: the claim was not to acreage but to a system of belonging in which land, law, ancestry, and identity are one thing. Mabo's own account of the case, and the documentary and writing it generated, describe a lawsuit as the continuation of a relationship by other means.

*Historical/biographical narrative · Rome, 1st century BCE · [Cato the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_the_Younger)*

Cato, seeing Caesar's victory inevitable at Thapsus, chooses to die rather than accept Caesar's pardon, which he reads as a performance that would make him a prop in Caesar's theatre. Plutarch records the deliberation: he reads Plato's Phaedo through the night, twice, discusses the argument with his friends, and then, after the Stoic manner, takes his own life. His death became the Roman republic's image of the freedom that tyranny cannot reach.

The tradition has always been ambivalent about this passage, and the ambivalence is instructive. Stoics celebrated Cato as the sage's final act; others, including later Christian readers, saw pride. What is not disputed is that Cato's death, more than his life, became the tradition's most cited example, and that Caesar, by pardoning everyone else, made the exception tell.

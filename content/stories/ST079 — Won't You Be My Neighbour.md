*Biographical/cultural narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1968-2001 · [Mister Rogers' Neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mister_Rogers%27_Neighborhood)*

For thirty-three years, Fred Rogers ended each episode by feeding the fish and asking, "Won't you be my neighbour?" The question was not a sign-off. Rogers, an ordained Presbyterian minister, built the show's entire theology into the practice of wishing goodwill toward whoever was actually there: the difficult neighbour, the invisible person, the child who feels unlovable. He insisted the show address children's real experiences, including assassination and divorce, at the pace of care.

Mettā is usually taught as a seated practice. Rogers built a career demonstrating it as a television format, one episode at a time, addressed to strangers.

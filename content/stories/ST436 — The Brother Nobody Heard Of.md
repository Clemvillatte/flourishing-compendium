Warring States China · Heguanzi, chapter 16 · a transmitted literary anecdote, reproduced by Henry Buchtel citing Science & Civilization in China VI:6

A Warring States anecdote explains why the best physician can be the one nobody has heard of. In the story, relayed by Phang Hsüan to a king, Duke Wên of Wei asks the great physician Pien Chhüeh (Bian Que) which of the three physicians — Bian Que and his two brothers — is the best. The answer inverts the question: "The eldest is the best, then the second, and I am the least worthy of the three" ([Heguanzi ch. 16, as reproduced by Buchtel](https://www.dynamicpoints.com/p/bian-ques-older-brothers-and-treating-illness-before-it-occurs)). The duke asks to hear how that can be. The explanation, as the reproduction has it:

> "My eldest brother, in dealing with diseases, is attentive to the spirit (shên). Before [any symptoms] have formed, he has already got rid of it. Thus his fame has never reached beyond our own clan. My next brother treats disease when its signs are most subtle, so his name is unknown beyond our own village. As for myself, I use stone needles on the blood vessels, prescribe strong drugs, and fortify the skin and the flesh. Thus my name has become known among all the feudal lords." ([Heguanzi ch. 16, as reproduced by Buchtel](https://www.dynamicpoints.com/p/bian-ques-older-brothers-and-treating-illness-before-it-occurs))

The account contrasts the brothers' practices and reputations. The eldest treats the ailment before it has taken any form; the second treats it when its signs are most subtle; Bian Que, in the account's own words, uses stone needles on the blood vessels, prescribes strong drugs, and fortifies the skin and the flesh. The account gives the least fame to the earliest care.

My reading: the eldest brother illustrates D061, acting before things arise. The Daodejing's own reasoning is that a thing is easiest to handle before it shows any sign of itself: "Action should be taken before a thing has made its appearance; order should be secured before disorder has begun" ([Daodejing 64, Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)). The awkward part, as the principle's plain words put it, is that the early action looks like effort spent on nothing. Read alongside D061, the anecdote asks the reader to value work done before trouble becomes conspicuous.

*Scientific metaphor · England/United States, 1972 · [Gregory Bateson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Bateson)*

Bateson asks where the blind man's mind ends. Not at the skin, and not at the cane's tip: the relevant unit is the whole circuit, cane, ground, sound, brain, adjustment. Mind, for Bateson, is not a thing in the head but a pattern of organisation that can span organisms and their worlds. He then draws the conclusion he is remembered for: the unit of survival is organism-plus-environment, and we have been destroying the environment as if it were outside the circuit.

Steps to an Ecology of Mind is the bridge between cybernetics and ecology. Its core move is the dissolution of the boundary question: asking where I end and the world begins is like asking where the whirlpool ends and the river begins.

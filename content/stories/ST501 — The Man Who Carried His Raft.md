India, trad. 5th century BCE · sutta narrative from the Alagaddupama Sutta (MN 22), translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu, with the principle file in the compendium working tree

The raft simile of MN 22 is not a free-floating image; it was given as a correction. A monk named Ariṭṭha, formerly of the vulture killers, had taken up the view that "As I understand the Dhamma taught by the Blessed One, those acts the Blessed One says are obstructive, when indulged in, are not genuine obstructions" ([MN 22, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)). After attempts to reason him out of it, the Buddha's rebuke was direct: "you, worthless man, through your own wrong grasp [of the Dhamma], have both misrepresented us as well as injuring yourself and accumulating much demerit for yourself, for that will lead to your long-term harm & suffering" ([MN 22, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)). The teaching about holding teachings follows from there.

"Monks, I will teach you the Dhamma compared to a raft, for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of holding onto" ([MN 22, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)). A man traveling along a path sees "a great expanse of water, with the near shore dubious & risky, the further shore secure & free from risk, but with neither a ferryboat nor a bridge going from this shore to the other" ([MN 22, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)). He gathers grass, twigs, branches and leaves, binds a raft and crosses, making an effort with his hands and feet. The fork in the story comes after the crossing. The traveler might think the raft so useful that he should hoist it on his head and carry it wherever he goes; asked whether that would be doing what should be done with the raft, the monks answer no, lord. Dragging it onto dry land or sinking it in the water, and going on unburdened, is what should be done ([MN 22, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)).

The application reaches past the parable: "In the same way, monks, I have taught the Dhamma compared to a raft, for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of holding onto. Understanding the Dhamma as taught compared to a raft, you should let go even of Dhammas, to say nothing of non-Dhammas" ([MN 22, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)). The compendium's reading for B014: the view that caused the trouble in the first scene, Ariṭṭha's, is itself a raft carried past the water. The principle's wider applications, to judgments, grievances and practices, are not carried by this story.

**Principles:**
- [[B014 — Use views as tools, not possessions (the raft)|B014 Use views as tools, not possessions (the raft)]]: the Alagaddupama Sutta gives the raft simile inside a story about a monk whose grip on a teaching had already gone wrong; the two fates of the raft after crossing, and the instruction to let go even of Dhammas, are the sutta's own terms.

*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2012/2014 · [Wild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild:_From_Lost_to_Found_on_the_Pacific_Crest_Trail)*

Cheryl Strayed, after her mother's death and her own disintegration, hikes 1,100 miles of the Pacific Crest Trail, having never backpacked, with a pack, "Monster," that experienced hikers gawk at, and boots that turn out to be too small, and that she loses, kicking them off a mountain in one of the book's best scenes. The book's structure is the fit question in narrative form: every piece of gear, every mile, every encounter with other hikers, is a test of what actually works, and the learning is the plot.

The story's content for the personalisation principle is its honesty about the discovery's cost: the fit between the person and the intervention, the trail, the boots, the pace, is not computed in advance but found through trial, error, and the willingness to be a beginner in front of strangers. The memoir's popularity owes to its refusal to pretend the discovery was efficient.

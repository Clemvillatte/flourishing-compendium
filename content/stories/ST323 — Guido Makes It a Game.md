*Film narrative, cross-cultural · Italy, 1997 · [Life Is Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Is_Beautiful)*

Guido, a Jewish Italian bookseller, is sent to a concentration camp with his young son, Giosuè. To protect the child, Guido tells him the camp is a game: the rules are harsh, the points are awarded for silence and hiding, and the first to 1,000 points wins a real tank. He turns every humiliation into a challenge, translates the guard's instructions into game rules, and hides his fear. In the film's final movement, marched off to be shot, he passes his hidden son and gives him a wink and a comical march. The son survives, and the tank comes.

The film is famously contested, criticised for making comedy of the camps, and the criticism is part of its history. Its content for this volume is the resilience literature's hardest case: meaning-making within suffering, the deliberate construction of a narrative, the game, that protects another person, and the film's honesty is that the construction is a full-time act of the father's whole being, and that it does not save the father.

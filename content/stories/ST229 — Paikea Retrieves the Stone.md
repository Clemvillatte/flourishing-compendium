*Film narrative, cross-cultural · New Zealand/Germany, 2002 · [Whale Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_Rider)*

Paikea, born a twin whose brother and mother die at the birth, is the first-born of a Māori chiefly line in a community where leadership passes to males. Her grandfather Koro rejects her and trains a class of boys in the traditional arts. Paikea learns in secret. When Koro throws his sacred pendant into the bay in despair, and none of the boys can retrieve it, Paikea dives and brings it back, and offers it to him. The film ends with the community, led at last by Koro's reconciliation, celebrating her.

The film is usually read as feminist, and is also a precise demonstration of relational identity: Paikea's claim is not individual merit but genealogical and communal, and the story's resolution is not her leaving but her being received. Her power, throughout, is that she does not want to replace the tradition; she wants to be admitted to it, and that is the difference between a rebellion and a restoration.

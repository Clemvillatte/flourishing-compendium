*Literary narrative, cross-cultural · England, 1871-1872 · [George Eliot, Middlemarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlemarch)*

Dorothea Brooke, an idealistic young woman, marries Edward Casaubon, a desiccated scholar, believing she will serve his great work. The marriage is a study in misjudgement; Casaubon dies embittered, and Dorothea's life, by the standards of her era's romances, is a disappointment. Eliot's final judgement is the novel's gift: "the growing good of the world is partly dependent on unhistoric acts; and that things are not so ill with you and me as they might have been, is half owing to the number who lived faithfully a hidden life, and rest in unvisited tombs."

The sentence is the Aristotelian account of flourishing against the heroic one. Dorothea's ergon turns out not to be the great achievement but the faithful, largely invisible activity, and Eliot, who read Aristotle closely, structures the whole novel as an argument about where lives are actually made.

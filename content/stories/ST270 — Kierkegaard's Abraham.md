*Philosophical/biblical narrative · Denmark, 1843 · [Søren Kierkegaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B8ren_Kierkegaard)*

Fear and Trembling retells the binding of Isaac, and Kierkegaard's question is not whether Abraham is right but what kind of act it is. Abraham believes he must kill his son; he does not tell Sarah, does not justify himself to anyone, and sets out at dawn. Kierkegaard calls this the teleological suspension of the ethical: an act that cannot be justified in universal moral terms, whose justification, if there is one, is in the absolute relation to God.

The book's pseudonymous author, Johannes de silentio, ends by admitting he cannot make the movements of Abraham's faith. Its enduring content is the analysis of the individual's relation to the universal: the knight of faith, Kierkegaard says, is not the hero of ethics, and the ethical life's categories cannot contain every human possibility, which is the founding claim of existential thought.

Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 6, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Yan Hui comes to Confucius with a puzzle. When Mengsun Cai's mother died, he wailed but shed no tears, "He was not grieved at heart," and he conducted the funeral without mourning. Yet he is renowned in Lu for the excellence of his observance. Can one gain a reputation "without the slightest substance behind it?" ([Eno, §6.6](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). Legge's Yan Hui says that during the rites he "exhibited no sorrow" ([Legge, ch. 6](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Confucius, speaking for the author, says Mengsun Cai "exhausted all that there was to do." He does not know which comes first, birth or death, and so, in Eno's words, "It is as if he had been transformed into a thing and simply awaits his next transformation all unaware" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). In Legge's wording death was to him "like the issuing from one's dwelling at dawn" ([Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Why, then, does he wail? Eno: "When others wail, he wails". Legge: "When they wailed, he also wailed, having in himself the reason why he did so." And Confucius adds that Mengsun Cai "alone has awakened" while he and Yan Hui have "not yet begun to wake from our dream." Legge puts it as "He was more awake than others were." Then comes a turn to the dream of being a bird or a fish: you "can’t be sure when you’re talking about it now whether you’re the wakened one or the dreamer." The passage ends with laughter and pushing things aside as better than rushing about ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content); [Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The compendium reads this beside D090. Acceptance of death need not look like anything. Mengsun Cai's outward conduct is plain, and the story leaves it to the reader whether that is composure or something else.

Limits: Mengsun Cai never speaks. His inner state is asserted by Confucius, who is Zhuangzi's spokesman here, and by Yan Hui's reading of his outward behaviour. A person who does not weep after a death is not thereby awake, and the text offers no way to tell. Eno also notes that the passage reprises themes from earlier in the chapter. The story should not be used to pressure a mourner to feel less.

**Principles:**
- [[D090 — Accept death as transformation]]: a mourner who follows the rites and "awaits his next transformation."

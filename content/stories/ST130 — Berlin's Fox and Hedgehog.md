*Historical/literary narrative · England, 1953 · [Isaiah Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Berlin)*

Berlin takes a line from the Greek poet Archilochus, "the fox knows many things, but the hedgehog knows one big thing," and turns it into the most quoted typology in twentieth-century thought: thinkers who relate everything to a single system versus those who pursue many ends, often unrelated, without a unifying principle. Berlin's own sympathies are famously with the foxes.

The essay matters here for its warning. The hedgehog's single lens is not an error of intelligence; it is a temperament that buys coherence at the price of the evidence, and Berlin's career was largely an argument that the price is too high for human affairs.

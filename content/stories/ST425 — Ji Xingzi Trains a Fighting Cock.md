Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 19, in James Legge's translation, cross-read with Herbert Giles's.

Ji Xingzi is rearing a fighting cock for the king. After ten days the king asks whether it is ready. "Not yet; he is still vain and quarrelsome, and relies on his own vigour" ([Legge, ch. 19 §9](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Giles has the first answer as a stage of "seeking fiercely for a foe" ([Giles, ch. 19](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt)).

Ten days later: "Not yet; he still responds to the crow and the appearance of another bird." After ten days more, "Not yet. He still looks angrily, and is full of spirit." Giles's second and third answers are that the bird is "still excited by the sounds and shadows of other cocks" and that "the sight of an enemy is still enough to excite them to rage." Each check names a quieter layer of the same agitation: first the bird's own pride, then its reaction to a sound, then its glare ([Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf); [Giles](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt)).

At the fourth check Ji Xingzi says it is nearly ready. "Though another cock crows, it makes no change in him. To look at him, you would say he was a cock of wood. His quality is complete. No other cock will dare to meet him, but will run from him." Giles has "To look at them one might say they were of wood. Their virtue is complete" ([Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf); [Giles](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt)).

The compendium reads this beside D183 and D070. The training is a removal: of vanity, of reaction to noise, of the angry stare. The cock does nothing more at the end than it did at the start, and what it has stopped doing is what the others see.

Limits: the subject is a bird bred for fighting, the goal is the king's, and the story does not show the cock fighting. Nothing says a person can be trained the same way, or that stillness should be used to intimidate. Giles adds the gloss "illustrating the value of internal concentration," which is his, not the text's.

**Principles:**
- [[D183 — Keep the inside quiet when the outside is loud]]: "Though another cock crows, it makes no change in him."
- [[D070 — Let stillness precede mastery]]: the order of the four checks, from vanity to reaction to glare to the "cock of wood."

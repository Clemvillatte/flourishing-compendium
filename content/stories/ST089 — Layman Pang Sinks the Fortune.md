*Zen biographical narrative · China, 8th century CE · [Layman Pang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Layman_Pang)*

Layman Pang, a wealthy merchant, empties his house and fortune into the river and lives as a layman with his wife and two children, all practising. He makes his living weaving bamboo sieves. The recorded sayings of the family are among the most beloved texts in Zen: asked the deepest meaning of the Way, Pang replies, well-dried shit is the deepest meaning. His daughter Lingzhao out-koans him repeatedly.

Pang's importance in the tradition is structural: he is the proof case that awakening does not require monasticism. The marketplace, the family, and the bamboo sieve turned out to be sufficient material, and the tradition made him a fixture of the literature precisely because he stayed ordinary.

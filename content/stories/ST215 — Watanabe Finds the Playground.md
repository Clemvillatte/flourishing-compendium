*Film narrative, cross-cultural · Japan, 1952 · [Akira Kurosawa, Ikiru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikiru)*

Kanji Watanabe, a Tokyo bureaucrat, learns he has stomach cancer and less than a year. He has spent thirty years stamping documents and avoiding decisions. He tries the pleasures; they do not work. He tries his son, who wants the money. The pivot comes when he finds a file: a group of mothers have petitioned for a playground, blocked for years by bureaucratic pass-the-parcel. Watanabe pushes the file through, against the resistance of the entire machine, and dies in the snow, on a swing, in the playground.

The film's structure is deliberately Stoic: the confrontation with death is treated not as tragedy but as information, and the information is that the time was always this short. The famous scene at his wake, where colleagues reconstruct his last months and resolve to change, and then return to their desks, is the film's honest coda about how hard the lesson holds.

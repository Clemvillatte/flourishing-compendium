*Historical/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 1969 · [Mister Rogers' Neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mister_Rogers%27_Neighborhood)*

In 1969, Fred Rogers appears before a hostile Senate subcommittee weighing a $20 million cut to public broadcasting. Senator Pastore, impatient, tells him to talk fast. Rogers does not argue. He describes his show's work with children's emotions and recites the words of his song about what to do with the mad you feel. Pastore, visibly moved, says he thinks it's wonderful and approves the funding.

The exchange, preserved on video, is often shown as a triumph of gentleness. The mechanism is worth naming: Rogers refused the adversarial frame entirely and simply told the truth slowly, in a room built for speed and combat. Nothing he said could be used against him because nothing he said was a move.

*Film narrative, cross-cultural · France/Austria, 2012 · [Michael Haneke, Amour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amour)*

Georges and Anne, retired music teachers in their eighties, live a cultivated Parisian life. Anne has a stroke, then a second, and the film settles into the apartment with them as her body fails: the feeding, the washing, the refusal of the hospital, the daughter's offers of management from outside, and Georges's steady, exhausted, occasionally despairing presence. There is no music swelling, no redemption, and no cut away from the difficult moments.

The film is the phenomenology of care in its most literal form: the lived experience of caring for a failing body, presented without the narrative devices that convert it into meaning. Critics called it unwatchable and a masterpiece, often in the same review, and the film's refusal of consolation is its method: this is what it is like, the film says, and the like is the whole content.

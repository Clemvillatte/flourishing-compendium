*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1909 · [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James)*

In his Hibbert Lectures, James argues that the universe may not be a single closed system under one law. There is no guarantee that everything is one thing; the world may be a "strung-along" affair, many things in genuine connection, and our knowledge of it partial from every position. The monist's demand for one perspective is not a cognitive requirement; it is a temperament.

A Pluralistic Universe is the least read and most relevant of James's books. Its practical core is the claim that there is no view from nowhere, that every perspective reveals some things and conceals others, and that the demand for the single total view is the demand for the end of inquiry.

*Parable, cross-cultural · Middle East, 1st century CE · [Parable of the Prodigal Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parable_of_the_Prodigal_Son)*

A younger son demands his inheritance, leaves, and destroys it. Destitute, he returns, rehearsing a speech, and his father runs to meet him and throws a feast. The elder son, who stayed and worked, refuses to come in: all those years I served you, and you never gave me a goat. The parable ends on his resentment, unresolved.

The story is best known for the father's forgiveness, but its doctrine-of-the-mean interest is the elder son, whose virtue has curdled into scorekeeping. He is the illustration of Aristotle's warning that the virtues are means, not maxima: loyalty without generosity is a vice of a different kind, and the parable leaves him outside the party.

*Philosophical metaphor · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

Aristotle's recurring image for the practically wise person is the navigator or the helmsman. The navigator does not control the wind or the sea. What the navigator has is the capacity to read the conditions, adjust the sails, hold or change course, and reach port anyway. The excellence is in the reading and the adjusting, not in the conditions.

The image is [[The doctrine of the mean|the doctrine of the mean]]'s companion: there is no rule for sailing a ship, only the continuous application of judgment to changing conditions. Aristotle's point is that this capacity, not rule-following, is what virtue actually consists of, and that it is why the virtuous person, not the rule, must be the final measure.

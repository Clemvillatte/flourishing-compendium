*Historical/biographical narrative · Austria, 1946 · [Viktor Frankl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Frankl)*

Man's Search for Meaning is two texts: the memoir of three years in Auschwitz and other camps, and the theory, logotherapy, drawn from it. Frankl's observation is precise and modest: the prisoners who retained a reason to live, a person to return to or a work to finish, endured differently from those who did not, and he does not claim meaning guaranteed survival, since most of those he described died. The book's famous formulation is that the freedom to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances is the last of the human freedoms.

The book's endurance, in dozens of languages and millions of copies, owes to its refusal of both despair and uplift. Frankl does not argue that the camps were meaningful; he argues that the response to them was the only remaining territory, and that this territory is always available, which is the existential thesis in its most tested form.

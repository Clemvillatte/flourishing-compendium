*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1878 · [Charles Sanders Peirce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Sanders_Peirce)*

Peirce asks what the meaning of a concept is and answers with a maxim: consider what practical effects we conceive the object of our conception to have, and our conception of those effects is the whole of our conception of the object. Two ideas with no conceivable practical difference are not two ideas.

"How to Make Our Ideas Clear" is the founding document of pragmatism, and the maxim is less a theory of truth than a test for mental clutter. Applied to a personal belief, it is ruthless: what would be different in experience if this were false? If nothing, the belief is decorative, and Peirce would like you to know that.

*Literary/film narrative, cross-cultural · Canada, 2001/2012 · [Yann Martel, Life of Pi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_of_Pi)*

Pi Patel, 16, survives a shipwreck and drifts across the Pacific for 227 days, in a lifeboat, with a Bengal tiger named Richard Parker. The survival is a discipline of practice: the whistle, the territory markings, the fishing, the diary. Rescued, Pi is interviewed by investigators who do not believe the story, and he offers a second version, with the tiger replaced by the cook, the sailor, and his mother, in which the same events are a story of human savagery. Which story do you prefer, he asks. The tiger, say the investigators. Pi says: thank you. And so it goes with God.

The novel's argument is the narrative-identity thesis in its most accessible form: the facts are underdetermined, the choice between interpretations is not a choice about truth, and the choice of the better story is itself the act by which a life is constituted. The novel does not hide that the choice is also a choice about what one can bear.

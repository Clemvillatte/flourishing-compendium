*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 399 BCE · [Socrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates)*

At his trial, offered the chance to propose his own punishment, Socrates suggests free meals for life. Condemned, he tells the jury that the unexamined life is not worth living for a human being. In prison, with escape arranged, he refuses it: one should never do injustice, not even in return for injustice, and he owes the laws of the city his compliance.

The sequence is the foundation text of eudaimonism. Socrates' claim is not that [[The examined life|the examined life]] is pleasant, safe, or rewarded; it is that without it, the other goods of a life are not goods at all. He then pays for the claim with the life itself, which is why the tradition treats him as the standard.

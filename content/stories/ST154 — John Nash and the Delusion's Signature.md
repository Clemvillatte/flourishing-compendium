*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2001 · [A Beautiful Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Beautiful_Mind)*

John Nash, a brilliant mathematician, develops schizophrenia. The film renders his hallucinations as fully realised characters: a roommate, a government agent, a child. Hospitalised and medicated into fog, he stops taking the medication. His solution is diagnostic: the little girl never ages, therefore she is not real. He returns to teaching, and to the hallucinations, as permanent companions, and continues his work.

The film is understood to take liberties with the biography, and its real content is not Nash's but the structure of intellectual virtue under damage. His genius does not cure the delusions; it is turned on them, as a tool for tracking their signatures, and the management becomes a daily discipline rather than a cure.

*Zhuangzi metaphor · China, 4th century BCE · [Zhuangzi, ch. 6, "The Great and Venerable Teacher"](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/the-great-and-venerable-teacher)*

The true man of old, says the text, breathed from his heels. Everyone else breathes from the throat. Those crushed and defeated in argument gasp out their words as if retching.

The image is physiological rather than mystical. It names a recognisable fact of the body: there is a way of breathing that belongs to a settled nervous system, deep in the torso, and there is a gasping, high, throat-held breath that belongs to conflict and alarm. The text treats the breath as a diagnostic of the whole person, centuries before anyone measured heart-rate variability.

*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1890 · [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James)*

In the Principles chapter on habit, James writes that we are born with a nervous system that shapes itself to what it repeatedly does, and issues the warning: "Could the young but realise how soon they will become mere walking bundles of habits, they would give more heed to their conduct while in the plastic state." He then advises acting on resolve at the first opportunity, never letting an exception occur, and seizing the initial moment, since the first act of a habit is the one that counts.

The chapter is practical, in James's sense: he gives rules. Its famous consequence is that character is not given but deposited, one repetition at a time, and that the deposit compounds. James treated this as good news, since it puts the lever of the whole life inside the reach of any given Tuesday.

*Philosophical metaphor · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle)*

Aristotle notes that the doctor does not prescribe the same regimen to everyone: what is healthful for the athlete may harm the convalescent, and the dose that restores one patient destroys another. The mean, therefore, is not a mathematical midpoint between excess and deficiency; it is what is right for this person, in this condition, at this time.

The example is brief in the text but it carries the whole anti-formalist force of the ethics. If virtue were a formula, the mean could be computed once and for all. Because it is relative to the person and circumstance, it requires a perceiver, and the education of that perceiver is the entire project of the Ethics.

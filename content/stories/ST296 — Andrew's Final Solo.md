*Film narrative, cross-cultural · United States, 2014 · [Damien Chazelle, Whiplash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiplash_%282014_film%29)*

Andrew Neiman, a jazz drummer, is pushed by his conductor, Fletcher, past every limit, and the film has already shown the wreckage: the bleeding hands, the failed relationship, the car crash answered with a demand to play. In the final scene, at a jazz festival, Fletcher, who has been fired and has set Andrew up for humiliation, sabotages him with the wrong chart. Andrew, humiliated, returns to the stage without permission, seizes the tempo, and leads the band through an extended solo, and Fletcher, watching, is won over, and the film ends inside the music.

The film's Nietzschean content is its refusal to answer its own question. Is this self-overcoming or self-destruction, creation or mere wreckage, and the final scene's exhilaration is designed to be indistinguishable from the damage. Nietzsche's test, the value-creating act, requires the destruction of what one was, and the film's achievement is that the audience cannot tell whether it is watching the child or only the lion.

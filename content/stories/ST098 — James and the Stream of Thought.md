*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1890 · [William James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_James)*

In The Principles of Psychology, James argues that consciousness is not a chain of beads but a stream: it feels continuous, it is always about something, and it selects. "The same thought never returns," he writes, because the thinker has moved. He chose the word stream carefully, over the objections of colleagues who preferred the atomic model.

The chapter is the birth of process psychology. Its practical consequence, which James drew in his later work on habit and will, is that you do not change a mind by replacing an object in it. You change the direction of the water.

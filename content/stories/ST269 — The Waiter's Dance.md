*Philosophical narrative · France, 1943 · [Jean-Paul Sartre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Paul_Sartre)*

Sartre describes a café waiter whose movements are too exact, too quick, too waiter-like, his back slightly bent forward, his eyes alert, his gestures a little too precise. He is playing at being a waiter, and the excess is the giveaway. This is bad faith: the attempt to become the role, to be a waiter in the way that a table is a table, and thereby escape the freedom of being a person for whom the role is a choice.

Being and Nothingness is built on examples like this, drawn from the Paris of the 1940s, and the waiter is its most famous. Its practical content is a diagnostic for self-deception: the performance of a role with too much precision is usually the flight from the responsibility of having chosen it, and one can catch oneself mid-gesture.

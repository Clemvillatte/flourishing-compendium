*Biblical/philosophical narrative · Middle East, ancient · [Book of Job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Job)*

Job, righteous and prosperous, loses everything: children, property, health. His three friends arrive to comfort him and instead explain, at length, that his suffering must be a punishment for sin. Job refuses the explanation, insisting on his integrity and demanding an answer from God himself. God answers, at last, from the whirlwind, with a speech that is not an explanation but a series of questions: where were you when I laid the earth's foundations. Job, silenced, withdraws his case, and the book ends with his restoration, and with the friends rebuked for not speaking of God what is right, as Job has.

The book is the oldest and most severe text on theodicy, and its content is the refusal of every available explanation, including the religious ones. God's speech from the whirlwind is not an answer to the question of suffering but a reorientation of the questioner, and the friends, with their tidy theories, are the book's real targets.

*Historical/biographical narrative · Greece, 6th century BCE · [Thales of Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_of_Miletus)*

Thales, walking at night studying the stars, falls into a well. A Thracian servant girl laughs at him: so eager to know what is in the heavens that he cannot see what is at his feet. Plato records the anecdote in the Theaetetus with a point attached: the philosopher's absent-mindedness is the mark of a life spent elsewhere, and it looks like folly to the practical.

The story survived because it is funny and because it states the cost of the theoretical life in one image. Aristotle's version of the defence, in the Politics, is that Thales later cornered the olive presses and made a fortune to prove philosophers could, which only sharpened the question of why they usually don't bother.

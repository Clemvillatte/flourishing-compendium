*Historical/biographical narrative · Rome, 1st century CE · [Seneca the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca_the_Younger)*

De Brevitate Vitae opens with the observation that everyone complains life is short, and everyone is wrong. Life is not short; it is long, and people squander it. The person who spends their time in banquets, gossip, and the pursuit of others' business has not lived a short life; they have existed for a long time without living. Seneca's advice is not carpe diem but the audit: gather your days and see what you actually have.

The essay is the tradition's most quotable treatment of time, and its diagnosis has not aged: the complaint about shortness, Seneca says, comes from the feeling of having nothing to show, which is not a property of the lifespan but of the attention. Life, he concludes, is long, if you know how to use it.

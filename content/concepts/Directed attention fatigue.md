Directed attention fatigue is Stephen Kaplan's name for mental fatigue affecting effortful, voluntarily controlled attention ([Kaplan, The Urban Forest as a Source of Psychological Well-Being, p. 102](https://web.archive.org/web/20240803032059/https://4sharing.wulfenfoo.org/images/d/dc/Kaplan1995UrbanForest.pdf), 1995).

Directed attention is the effortful kind: the attention "we call upon when working in a distracting surrounding or when trying to make a decision about a complex situation. It takes effort, and it is susceptible to fatigue" (Kaplan, p. 102). Its counterpart is fascination, which is effortless and hard to tear away from. Kaplan names the syndrome after what actually fatigues:

> Directed attention is effortful, it is subject to voluntary control, and it is susceptible to fatigue. Fascination, by contrast, is at the opposite pole on each of these dimensions. In terms of our analysis of "mental fatigue" then, it is more useful to name the syndrome in terms of what is actually becoming fatigued -- hence we call it Directed Attention Fatigue. (Kaplan, p. 102)

The costs Kaplan lists are cognitive and social: "It leads to difficulty in sustaining a line of thought; one is readily deflected or distracted. There is limited ability to analyze, to plan, to decide" (Kaplan, p. 102), with irritability, impatience and impulsivity alongside, and sufferers "resistant to helping others in need" (Kaplan, p. 103). On Kaplan's restoration account, rest comes through the other attention: "Fascination, the other form of attention, is itself resistant to fatigue and permits directed attention to rest", and quiet fascination in natural settings offers "an opportunity for reflection" (Kaplan, p. 103).

In their 2010 review, Kaplan and Berman propose a shared resource linking executive functioning and self-regulation. They report studies in which natural-environment interventions improved executive-functioning performance and self-regulation effectiveness, treating those findings as support for the proposal ([Kaplan and Berman, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162062/), 2010).

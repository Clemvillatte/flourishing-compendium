Skillful coping is Hubert Dreyfus's name for absorbed practical activity, and the account is stated in his paper "Intelligence without Representation - Merleau-Ponty's Critique of Mental Representation" (Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences 1: 367-383, 2002):

> According to Merleau-Ponty, in absorbed, skillful coping, I don't need a mental representation of my goal. Rather, acting is experienced as a steady flow of skillful activity in response to one's sense of the situation.

(Hubert L. Dreyfus, "Intelligence without Representation," 2002, p. 378, [PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~shaslang/mprg/DreyfusIWR.pdf))

The paper's central terms are the intentional arc and maximal grip. The intentional arc names the tight connection between body and world, such that, as the active body acquires skills, those skills are "stored," not as representations in the mind, but as dispositions to respond to the solicitations of situations in the world. Maximal grip is the body's tendency toward the optimal relationship with the situation: the same tendency that has you step closer to a painting, or hold the object so it vouchsafes most of itself.

The same paper gives the claim in its plainest form: "skillful coping does not require a mental representation of its goal at all. It can be purposive without the agent entertaining a purpose." And the body side: "rather, in skillful coping we experience the situation as drawing the movements out of us."

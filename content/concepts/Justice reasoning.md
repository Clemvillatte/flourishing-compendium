“Justice reasoning” — Kohlberg, 1981.

Justice reasoning names the core of Lawrence Kohlberg's research program in moral development. Drawing from the literature of moral philosophy, he hypothesized that justice-as-fairness was the central moral concept, then presented experimental subjects with moral conflicts and cooperation scenarios, recording their strategies for resolving the dilemmas involved (["Moral Development", Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/moraldev/)).

> Drawing from the literature of moral philosophy, Kohlberg hypothesized that justice-as-fairness was the central moral concept, also that conflict resolution and fostering mutual cooperation were its chief aims and marks of adequacy. ([Moral Development, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/moraldev/))

Kohlberg's model describes six stages grouped into three levels, summarized in the IEP's account of the findings:

> The results showed a six-stage sequence of such stages ranging from (a) a pre-conventional level in which children think egoistically or instrumentally, using each other to get what they want, through (b) a conventional level in which conformity to the institutional practices of one’s peer group and society are key toward maintaining group solidarity and stability, to (c) a post-conventional level at which morality is seen as a mutually created institution serving certain shared and elevated purposes—some achieved, some still being pursued. (["Moral Development", Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/moraldev/))

The IEP's critical assessment notes that Kohlberg's claim that justice and rights are the central concepts of morality is his strongest and most criticized philosophical claim (["Moral Development", IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/moraldev/)).

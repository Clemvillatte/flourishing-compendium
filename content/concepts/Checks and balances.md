Checks and balances is the constitutional device of setting power against power, on the theory that self-interest is the most reliable fuel an institution can run on. Madison's statement in Federalist 51:

> "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition. The interest of the man must be connected with the constitutional rights of the place." (Madison, Federalist 51, [Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed51.asp))

### What the device carries

The design problem is stated in the same paper: "In framing a government which is to be administered by men over men, the great difficulty lies in this: you must first enable the government to control the governed; and in the next place oblige it to control itself." The reasoning is not optimism about human nature but the reverse: "If men were angels, no government would be necessary. If angels were to govern men, neither external nor internal controls on government would be necessary." The device therefore builds the failure into the machinery, each branch's appetite for power made the other branches' brake. The compendium principle SP078 carries the general reading: read institutions as arrangements of power, asking not who holds a role but what restrains them, since an unrestrained role attracts the worst use of it. A compendium exercise drawn from the device is to take any group one belongs to and name what, concretely, checks the person with the most power in it. What the device does not give: gridlock as a good; the aim is control with function, and a check that stops everything has traded one failure for another.

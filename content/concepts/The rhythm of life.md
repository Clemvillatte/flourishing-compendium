The Neiye (Inward Training), the earliest Daoist text on breath cultivation, presents the vital breath as what becomes regular when the mind is tranquil: the Way abides in the excellent mind, and when the vital breath is regular the Way can be halted there. ([Neiye V, Roth translation](https://archive.org/stream/original-tao-inward-training-nei-yeh-and-the-foundations-of-taoist-mysticism-tra/Original%20Tao%20Inward%20Training%20(Nei-yeh)%20and%20the%20Foundations%20of%20Taoist%20Mysticism%20(Translations%20from%20the%20Asian%20Classics)%20by%20Harold%20David%20Roth%20(z-lib.org)_djvu.txt))

> "The Way has no fixed position;
> It abides within the excellent mind.
> When the mind is tranquil and the vital breath is regular,
> The Way can thereby be halted." (Neiye V, Harold D. Roth translation, [archive.org full text](https://archive.org/stream/original-tao-inward-training-nei-yeh-and-the-foundations-of-taoist-mysticism-tra/Original%20Tao%20Inward%20Training%20(Nei-yeh)%20and%20the%20Foundations%20of%20Taoist%20Mysticism%20(Translations%20from%20the%20Asian%20Classics)%20by%20Harold%20David%20Roth%20(z-lib.org)_djvu.txt))

A later chapter gives the practice the image serves: just let a balanced and aligned breathing fill your chest and swirl and blend within your mind, which confers longevity. ([Neiye XXI, Roth translation](https://archive.org/stream/original-tao-inward-training-nei-yeh-and-the-foundations-of-taoist-mysticism-tra/Original%20Tao%20Inward%20Training%20(Nei-yeh)%20and%20the%20Foundations%20of%20Taoist%20Mysticism%20(Translations%20from%20the%20Asian%20Classics)%20by%20Harold%20David%20Roth%20(z-lib.org)_djvu.txt))

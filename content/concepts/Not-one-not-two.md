Not-one-not-two is Nagarjuna's way of refusing both halves of a dichotomy: things are neither identical nor separate. The Mulamadhyamakakarika's tenth chapter works the point through fire and fuel:

> "If firewood were fire, actor and act would be one. If fire were other than wood, it would occur even without wood." (MMK 10.1, [translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/mulamadhyamaka-karika-english/d/doc79724.html))

### The refutation

Both options fail. If fire simply were the wood, the burning and the burned would collapse into one thing. If fire were wholly other than wood, a fire could burn with nothing to burn. The chapter closes the case in verse 14:

> "Wood itself is not fire; fire is also not something other than wood. Fire does not possess wood; wood does not exist in fire; that (fire) does not exist in it."

Verse 15 then names what the example is for: "Through fire and wood is explained without exception all the stages of self and the grasped and at the same time jugs, cloth and so on." The self and its aggregates, like fire and fuel, are neither one thing nor two.

The seed's wording needs a limit. The sources opened for this page show that harsh self-criticism is associated with psychopathology, and Gilbert's paper is a theoretical and clinical account, not a prediction study. The word "predicts" should be read as "goes with" unless a study shows otherwise over time.

### Gilbert's account

In compassion-focused therapy, people with high shame and self-criticism are described as having difficulty being kind to themselves.

> "In compassion-focused therapy it is hypothesised that this affect regulation system is poorly accessible in people with high shame and self-criticism, in whom the 'threat' affect regulation system dominates orientation to their inner and outer worlds." (Gilbert 2009, Advances in Psychiatric Treatment 15:199-208, [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/introducing-compassionfocused-therapy/ECBC8B7B87E90ABB58C4530CDEE04088))

Gilbert also writes that problems of shame and self-criticism "are often rooted in histories of abuse, bullying, high expressed emotion in the family, neglect and/or lack of affection". He treats self-criticism as something to understand before giving it up: "it is important to understand the functions of self-critical thinking and the fears people may have of giving it up."

### What the associations look like

A meta-analysis of 135 studies (42,952 participants) found a medium positive link between self-criticism and disordered eating (r = .37), with subgroups by measure ranging from r = .20 to .52. ([Paranjothy & Wade 2024, International Journal of Eating Disorders, PubMed 38366726](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38366726/))

A separate meta-analysis of the link between self-compassion and psychopathology found a large effect of r = -0.54 (95% CI -0.57 to -0.51) across 20 samples from 14 studies, all using one self-compassion scale. ([MacBeth & Gumley 2012, Clinical Psychology Review, PubMed 22796446](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22796446/)) That is evidence about self-compassion, which is related to self-criticism but not the same construct.

### Does self-criticism come first?

Most of this evidence is cross-sectional. A search excerpt from a recent longitudinal study reports that self-criticism "did not prospectively predict depression or social anxiety" in a cross-lagged model, which cautions against a simple causal reading. ([Current Psychology 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-025-08264-2)) That study was seen as an excerpt only.

The community of occasions is Alfred North Whitehead's way of saying that the world is not a collection of things but a company of happenings. In his process metaphysics the basic units are actual occasions, momentary events of experience that arise, take in their world, and perish; what we call a thing, a stone or a person, is a society of such occasions holding a common form. His summary sentence from Process and Reality:

> "'Actual entities' — also termed 'actual occasions' — are the final real things of which the world is made up." (Whitehead, [Process and Reality](https://www.informationphilosopher.com/solutions/philosophers/whitehead/), ch. II)

### What the community adds

The occasions are not windowless. Each one inherits from the settled past, and the world is the ongoing achievement of all of them together. Whitehead's phrase for the same idea in Religion in the Making: "The actual temporal world can be analyzed into a multiplicity of occasions of actualization. These are the primary actual units of which the temporal world is composed. Call each such occasion an 'epochal occasion.' Then the actual world is a community of epochal occasions." The community is the point: an occasion is what it is by taking account of others, and the others are changed by its having been. To exist is to contribute.

The compendium's use is ecological. If every enduring thing is a temporary arrangement in a shared process, then the boundary between self and world is a management decision rather than a fact, and the care that follows is care for the community of life rather than for one's private fragment of it.

Pragmatism is a philosophical tradition that, in the SEP's words, "understands knowing the world as inseparable from agency within it". It began in the United States around 1870, and its classical figures are Charles Sanders Peirce (1839 to 1914), William James (1842 to 1910) and John Dewey (1859 to 1952), according to the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/).

### The maxim

The core is Peirce's pragmatic maxim, a rule for clarifying ideas by tracing their practical consequences. His 1878 wording, as the SEP quotes it, reads:

> "Consider what effects, which might conceivably have practical bearings, we conceive the object of our conception to have. Then, our conception of these effects is the whole of our conception of the object." (Peirce, 1878, [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/))

### Inquiry as a shared, fallible project

Peirce describes inquiry as replacing doubt with settled belief. The SEP lists three other ways of fixing belief that he contrasts with the method of science: refusing to consider contrary evidence (tenacity), accepting an institution's dictates (authority), and adopting the most coherent or elegant-seeming set of beliefs (the a priori method). Against the lone thinker he writes:

> "We individually cannot reasonably hope to attain the ultimate philosophy which we pursue; we can only seek it, therefore, for the community of philosophers." (Peirce, quoted in the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/))

### James and Dewey

The SEP says James presented pragmatism as a "method for settling metaphysical disputes that might otherwise be interminable" (James, 1907). Unless some practical difference would follow from one side being right, the dispute is idle.

Dewey describes inquiry as starting from a problem in an unsettled situation. The SEP quotes him: the aim is "the controlled or directed transformation of an indeterminate situation into one that is so determinate in its constituent distinctions and relations as to convert the elements of the original situation into a unified whole". The SEP adds that the pragmatists, Dewey most of all, challenge the sharp line between theoretical belief and practical deliberation: in some sense, all inquiry is practical.

### What it is not

Pragmatism is not the slogan that truth is whatever is useful, and it is not wishful thinking. It is a position about what claims mean and how they are tested. Peirce and James disagreed over questions of truth, which is why Peirce renamed his own view pragmaticism.

### Reading it for practice

When an abstract question has you stuck, ask what concrete difference each answer would make to how you live, and where you can, run a small experiment instead of arguing further. This is an application of the maxim, not a rule laid down in the sources above.

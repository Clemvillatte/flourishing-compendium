“Leisure” — Pieper, Leisure: The Basis of Culture.

Leisure, in Josef Pieper's account, is not the pause between work but a condition of the soul that receives reality. He defines it by stillness:

> Leisure is a form of that stillness that is the necessary preparation for accepting reality; only the person who is still can hear, and whoever is not still, cannot hear. (Pieper, [Leisure: The Basis of Culture, p. 4, iwcinsb.org PDF](https://iwcinsb.org/studygroups/2024%20readings/Leisure.pdf))

The stillness is not mere silence: "the soul’s power, as real, of responding to the real – a co–respondence, eternally established in nature – has not yet descended into words", and leisure itself is "the disposition of receptive understanding, of contemplative beholding, and immersion – in the real". Pieper is careful to distinguish leisure from both idleness and inactivity: it "is not the same as the absence of activity; it is not the same thing as quiet, or even as an inner quiet". Its ground is affirmation: leisure "is only possible in the assumption that man is not only in harmony with himself [whereas idleness is rooted in the denial of this harmony], but also that he is in agreement with the world and its meaning. Leisure lives on affirmation."

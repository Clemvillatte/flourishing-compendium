Niko Tinbergen's four questions are the ethologist's test for a complete explanation of any behaviour: function, history, development, and mechanism. The PBT manual adopts them as the check that an evolutionary account of a change process is well-rounded:

> "In a well-rounded evolutionary account, these concepts are applied to any phenomenon using Niko Tinbergen’s (1963) four central questions of function, history, development, and mechanism." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, 2021, Learning Process-Based Therapy, Chapter 3)

Applied to a stuck pattern, the four questions spread the inquiry: what is this behaviour for now (function), what selected it over time (history), how did it develop in this person (development), and what carries it moment to moment (mechanism). The book's standard is the well-rounded account; a case description that answers only one of the four leaves the other questions open, and the model treats that openness as the next thing to investigate.

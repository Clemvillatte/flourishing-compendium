**Life history theory** — evolved strategies trade off reproduction timing and parenting effort against growth and survival; environments shift the trade.

Life history theory came from evolutionary biology: natural selection shapes the schedule of survival, development and reproduction, and strategies combine those schedules ([Stott et al., 2024, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39003192/)). A psychology review introduces what the theory offers the study of persons:

> "This review is organized in five parts, starting with an overview of key concepts in LHT, which clarifies the relationship among LH strategy, LH-related traits, and the fast-slow paradigm of LH variation. We proceed to review theoretical and empirical work related to four basic LH trade-offs, summarized by an integrated descriptive model of LH trade-offs that shape different LH strategies in humans. We then explain the effects of four aspects of environmental risks (morbidity-mortality threats, competition, resource scarcity, and unpredictability) on human LH strategy." (Yang, Zhu, Lu and Chang, [Environmental risks, life history strategy, and developmental psychology, Psych Journal 11 (2022), pp. 433-447, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35599317/))

### The fast-slow axis and its limits

The familiar summary of the variation is the fast-slow continuum, a negative association between time allocated to reproduction and development versus survival ([Stott et al., 2024, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39003192/)). The same paper adds the field's current caution: higher-quality, more representative data and analyses have revealed that life history variation cannot be fully accounted for by this single continuum, and studies often operate as exploratory exercises rather than testing theory ([Stott et al., 2024, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39003192/)). In humans, the review's authors emphasize the environmentally sensitive developmental processes that contribute to variation and plasticity in life-history traits ([Yang et al., 2022, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35599317/)).

"Survivorship bias" — statistics.

Survivorship bias is the error of drawing conclusions from the people, companies or objects that made it through some selection process while overlooking the ones that did not, because the failures are no longer there to be counted. Its founding case is Abraham Wald's wartime work: armor placement for aircraft, decided by reading the damage on planes that came home.

> "Survivorship bias is the error of drawing conclusions from the people, companies or objects that made it through some selection process while overlooking the ones that did not, because the failures are no longer there to be counted." (Psychology.com, [Survivorship Bias](https://psychology.com/biases/survivorship-bias))

### The founding case, carefully told

In the Second World War, the Statistical Research Group at Columbia University, which included Wald, was asked where to add armor to aircraft. The data were counts of hits on planes that had returned from missions. Wald's 1943 memoranda started from the point that the returning planes were a biased sample: a hit that brought a plane down could not appear in the data. His method estimated, from the damage on survivors and the number of planes lost, how fatal a hit on each part of the aircraft was; the memoranda were later reprinted by the Center for Naval Analyses and restated by Mangel and Samaniego in 1984. The popular version of the story — red-dot diagrams, an officer proposing to armor the holes — is, on the sources behind this page, a later growth in the retelling; what is documented is the method and the principle that an absent observation can carry more information than a present one.

### Why a writer on flourishing would care

Advice on living well is the most survivorship-biased literature there is: the people whose choices failed do not write the books. My use of the concept is as a standing check: when reading a success story, ask who is missing from the sample. The page's earlier source list named Merton & Barber (2004) and Taleb (2007); the sources behind this page document the Wald case and the definition.

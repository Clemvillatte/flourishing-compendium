“Fundamental stance” — Jaspers, Psychology of Worldviews.

Karl Jaspers used the psychology of worldviews to describe the underlying postures from which a whole life thinks and sees. The term marks his central insight: before any particular opinion, there is a stance one has taken toward the world, and it decides what can and cannot be seen from there.

> "Jaspers's first intervention in philosophical debate, Psychology of World Views, constructed a typology of mental attitudes, which, close to Weber's model of ideal types, was intended to provide an interpretive account of basic psychological dispositions." — [Thornhill & Miron, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Karl Jaspers", §4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaspers/)

### Why stances harden

The diagnosis Jaspers attached to the typology is its lasting value. World views "commonly take the form of objectivized cages (*Gehäuse*), in which existence hardens itself against contents and experiences which threaten to transcend or unbalance the defensive restrictions which it has placed upon its operations." A stance taken for safety becomes a stance defended against life. The remedy is not to have no stance — that too is one — but to hold it as revisable, which is what the seed's links to gratitude as a fundamental stance and to the originary choice both imply: a stance can be chosen again.

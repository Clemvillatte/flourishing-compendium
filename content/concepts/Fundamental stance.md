“Fundamental stance” — Jaspers, Psychology of Worldviews.

Karl Jaspers used the psychology of worldviews to describe the underlying postures from which a whole life thinks and sees. "Fundamental stance" is this wiki's label for what Jaspers called attitudes (Einstellungen) and world views: the settled position from which everything else is seen.

> "Jaspers's first intervention in philosophical debate, Psychology of World Views, constructed a typology of mental attitudes, which, close to Weber's model of ideal types, was intended to provide an interpretive account of basic psychological dispositions." — [Thornhill & Miron, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Karl Jaspers", §4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaspers/)

### Why stances harden

The diagnosis Jaspers attached to the typology is its lasting value. World views "commonly take the form of objectivized cages (*Gehäuse*), in which existence hardens itself against contents and experiences which threaten to transcend or unbalance the defensive restrictions which it has placed upon its operations." A stance taken for safety becomes a stance defended against life.

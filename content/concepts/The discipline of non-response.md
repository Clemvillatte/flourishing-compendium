The discipline of non-response is the Stoic practice of not adding a reflexive answer to an impression. The technique is stated in the Enchiridion's most quoted line: "Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take of them" (Enchiridion 5; [buboquote collection](https://www.buboquote.com/en/author/61-epictetus)). Between the impression and the response there is a judgment, and the judgment is the only movable part.

### The training

The exercise Epictetus gives is a redirect of wanting rather than a suppression of feeling: "Don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish that they happen as they do happen, and you will go on well" (Enchiridion 5, multiple translations compared at [enchiridion.tasuki.org](https://enchiridion.tasuki.org/display:Code:ec,twh,pem,sw/section:5)). The same point in the earlier sections of the handbook: "If you regard that... which is not your own as being your own, you'll have cause to lament, you'll have a troubled mind, and you'll find fault with both gods and human beings" (Enchiridion 1; [The Collector](https://www.thecollector.com/5-ways-to-be-happy-epictetus/)). Non-response is not numbness — the disturbance is diagnosed as a wrong attribution of ownership, and the training is to catch the attribution at the moment it forms.

### Why it is a discipline

The practice requires repetition because impressions arrive with the force of facts. The research-side account — response prevention, the deliberate blocking of a compulsive reply — is the same structure studied under experimental conditions. The Stoic version adds the diagnostic layer: before withholding the response, ask what the impression claimed to be yours.

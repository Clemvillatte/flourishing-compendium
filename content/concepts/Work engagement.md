"Work engagement" — Schaufeli et al., 2002, as quoted in [Schaufeli's chapter](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/414.pdf).

### The definition

Schaufeli and colleagues define the construct in its own right, as a positive work-related state defined independently of burnout rather than as its mere absence ([Schaufeli, What is Engagement?](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/414.pdf)).

> "Work engagement, in this view, is defined as a concept in its own right: 'a positive, fulfilling, work related state of mind that is characterized by vigor, dedication, and absorption' (Schaufeli, Salanova, González-Romá, and Bakker, 2002: 74), whereby vigor refers to high levels of energy and mental resilience while working, the willingness to invest effort in one's work, and persistence even in the face of difficulties; dedication refers to being strongly involved in one's work, and experiencing a sense of significance, enthusiasm, inspiration, pride, and challenge; and absorption refers to being fully concentrated and happily engrossed in one's work, whereby time passes quickly and one has difficulties with detaching oneself from work."
>
> — Schaufeli, [What is Engagement?](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/414.pdf), chapter 1, reproducing the nested definition from Schaufeli, Salanova, González-Romá and Bakker (2002), p. 74

The chapter distinguishes two views of engagement and burnout. Maslach and Leiter's view treats them as the positive and negative endpoints of a single continuum, so that anyone high on engagement is inevitably low on burnout; Schaufeli's chapter takes the second view, that engagement is a distinct concept negatively related to burnout ([Schaufeli, What is Engagement?](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/414.pdf)).

### The measurement

The definition is operationalized in the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale (UWES), which Schaufeli describes as a brief, valid and reliable self-report questionnaire based on the definition of work engagement as a combination of vigor, dedication, and absorption, with a subscale for each of the three dimensions ([Schaufeli, What is Engagement?](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/414.pdf)).

A caution from the same chapter concerns consultancy engagement. Schaufeli reports a correlation of r = .91 between Gallup's Q12 engagement questionnaire and a single item that taps job satisfaction, and argues that the Q12 assesses perceived job resources, which he treats as antecedents of engagement rather than the experience of engagement itself ([Schaufeli, What is Engagement?](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/414.pdf)).

### Relation to burnout and flow

Vigor and dedication are the core dimensions, the direct positive opposites of burnout's exhaustion and cynicism, whereas absorption resembles flow, a state of optimal experience in Csikszentmihalyi's sense, and seems to act as a consequence of engagement ([Schaufeli's later paper on burnout and engagement](https://www.wilmarschaufeli.nl/publications/Schaufeli/237.pdf)).

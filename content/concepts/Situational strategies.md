“Situational strategies” — Duckworth et al., 2016.

The paper's move is to take self-control out of the head and put it in the room. Enacting self-control is not always difficult, it argues, particularly when it takes the form of proactively choosing or changing situations in ways that weaken undesirable impulses or potentiate desirable ones — the partygoer who sits far from the drinks, the student who goes to the library without a phone. The examples share a timeline logic: because impulses tend to grow stronger over time, strategies that intervene early, before the impulse develops, prevent struggle rather than win it.

> "Because impulses tend to grow stronger over time, situational self-control strategies—which can nip a tempting impulse in the bud—may be especially effective in preventing undesirable action." (Duckworth, Gendler & Gross, 2016, Perspectives on Psychological Science 11(1), 35–55, [abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691615623247/))

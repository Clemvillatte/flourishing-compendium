Insight problem-solving is solving by sudden reinterpretation, not by step-by-step search. Kounios and Beeman define it: "Insight occurs when a person suddenly reinterprets a stimulus, situation, or event to produce a nonobvious, nondominant interpretation. This can take the form of a solution to a problem (an 'aha moment'), comprehension of a joke or metaphor, or recognition of an ambiguous percept." ([Kounios and Beeman, 2014, Annual Review of Psychology 65, 71-93, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24405359/))

### Restructuring

An earlier overview by the same authors says "insights are often the result of the reorganization or restructuring of the elements of a situation or problem, though an insight may occur in the absence of any preexisting interpretation". It contrasts insight with "deliberate, conscious search strategies", since "insights occur when a solution is computed unconsciously and later emerges into awareness suddenly" ([Kounios and Beeman, The Aha! Moment, Northwestern-hosted PDF](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/a/699/files/2015/11/Aha-The-cognitive-neuroscience-of-insight-2895xqe.pdf)).

Ohlsson's representational change theory explains why restructuring is needed. A review in PMC describes it: prior knowledge and experience are "postulated to hamper (instead of promote) the generation of solutions since they easily turn into constraints", and "an impasse... is the precondition for a representational change that results in an insight" ([Intuition and Insight: Two Processes That Build on Each Other or ..., PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5020639/)). In the nine-dot problem, the realisation that moves beyond the imagined square are allowed "may lead to the relaxation of the perceptually driven boundary constraints".

### What the brain does

Kounios and Beeman report "insight-related coarse semantic coding in the right hemisphere and internally focused attention preceding and during problem solving", and that people who tend to solve insightfully "are associated with different patterns of resting-state brain activity" (PubMed abstract). Insight solutions were linked to a gamma-band burst "starting about 300 milliseconds before the button-press", and the authors conclude that insight "is the culmination of a series of brain states and processes operating at different time scales" (Northwestern PDF). In a 2006 study, neural activity before a problem appeared "predicted which problems they would subsequently solve with, versus without, self-reported insight" ([The prepared mind, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17100789/)).

### Open disputes

The definition is not settled. A PMC article notes that insight is variously taken to involve shifts of attentional focus, novel conceptual recombination, or "reinterpretation or relaxation of the problem's understood constraints" ([The irreconcilability of insight, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10907412/)). The same review adds a result on the feeling itself: in a study by Danek and colleagues, "the more constraints had to be relaxed, the less aha-experiences were reported", which the review reads as a multi-step restructuring reducing the sense of suddenness. The experience is also uneven between people: a Cambridge chapter says insight "is not the same for all types of individuals or in all circumstances" ([Why My "Aha!" Is Your "Hmm...", Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/emergence-of-insight/why-my-aha-is-your-hmm/18DB35E0026E19CE2C20B29776E7E616)).

### Scope for the essays

The seed's phrase "hypothesis generation" is not in the Kounios and Beeman abstract; restructuring and reinterpretation are. Claims about meditation or koans belong to other pages and are not supported by these sources.

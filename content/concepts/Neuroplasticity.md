Neuroplasticity is the brain's capacity to reorganize its structure and function in response to experience, a property that extends past the developmental years. A review by Bogdan Draganski and Arne May, authors of the seed's 2004 training study, states the core claim:

> "Structural and functional brain reorganisation can occur beyond the developmental maturation period and this was recently recognised as an intrinsic property of the human central nervous system." (Draganski & May 2008, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18378330/))

The same abstract names what drives the reorganisation: "Brain injury or altered afferent input due to environmental changes, novel experience and learning new skills". The field's stated open question is "to understand the behavioural consequences and cellular mechanisms underlying training-induced neuroanatomic plasticity".

The seed's source is Draganski et al.'s 2004 Nature report "Neuroplasticity: changes in grey matter induced by training" (Nature 427:311-2); its PubMed record ([14737157](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14737157/)) carries the citation but no abstract text, so the 2008 review by the same first author is this page's opened carrier.

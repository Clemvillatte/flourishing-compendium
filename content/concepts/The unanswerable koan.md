The unanswerable koan is a question given to a Zen student that no answer can close. The seed names the Zen tradition and the Gateless Gate, the thirteenth-century collection of Mumon.

### Joshu's dog

The collection opens with the koan most often taken as the type of the whole genre:

> A monk asked Joshu, a Chinese Zen master: "Has a dog Buddha-nature or not?" Joshu answered: "Mu." (Mumon, The Gateless Gate, case 1, trans. Nyogen Senzaki and Paul Reps, 1934, [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Gateless_Gate/Joshu%27s_Dog))

"Mu" is not the answer to the question; it is the refusal of the terms in which the question can be answered. Mumon's own comment says what the refusal is for:

> "To realize Zen one has to pass through the barrier of the patriarchs. Enlightenment always comes after the road of thinking is blocked." (same page)

The unanswerable koan is a machine for blocking that road. It is not a riddle whose solution ends it, but an object the student is told to become, usually in dialogue with a teacher, until the distinction between questioner and question gives way.

The body-subject is Maurice Merleau-Ponty's account of the body as the perceiving, acting subject of experience, and not an object among objects. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes the body's typical mode of existence in the Phenomenology of Perception as "being-toward-the-world", "a pre-objective orientation toward a vital situation that is explicable neither in terms of third-person causal interactions nor by explicit judgments or representations" ([SEP, Merleau-Ponty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)).

### The "I can"

The body's orientation toward the world "is essentially temporal", involving "a dialectic between the present body (characterized, after Husserl, as an 'I can') and the habit body, the sedimentations of past activities that take on a general, anonymous, and autonomous character" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)). The body is also experienced differently from other things: it is "a permanent part of one's perceptual field", and it has "double sensations", such as one hand touching another ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)).

### Body schema and space

Kinesthetic awareness depends on "a pre-conscious system of bodily movements and spatial equivalences" that Merleau-Ponty calls the "body schema". Against the "positional spatiality" of things, the body has a "situational spatiality" oriented toward "actual or possible tasks". The body's relation to space is intentional "as an 'I can' rather than an 'I think'", so that "the body is not 'in' space but lives or inhabits it" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)). The case of Schneider, whose injury disrupted the "intentional arc", is the entry's illustration ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)).

### The body in the world

The entry quotes the simile "One's own body is in the world just as the heart is in the organism" (Phenomenology of Perception, p. 245/209). It adds that the body's expressive unity "extends to the sensible world" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)).

### Scope

The quotations come from one encyclopedia entry. The Phenomenology of Perception itself was not read, and the entry's quoted passages do not show the hyphenated term "body-subject" in the sections consulted. The page therefore describes the idea under the entry's own vocabulary. Whether practices built on it improve wellbeing is a separate empirical question that this source does not address.

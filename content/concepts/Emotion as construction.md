"Emotion as construction" — Nietzsche, The Gay Science.

"Emotion as construction" is this compendium's label, not Nietzsche's phrase; it names a reading of The Gay Science on which emotional life is something a person works on rather than something that merely happens. The anchor is aphorism 290, "One thing is needful," where Nietzsche describes the self-work he calls a great and rare art:

> "To 'give style' to one's character — a great and rare art! It is practiced by those who survey all the strengths and weaknesses of their nature and then fit them into an artistic plan until every one of them appears as art and reason and even weaknesses delight the eye." (Nietzsche, [The Gay Science §290, Kaufmann translation](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/blattnew/intro/gs290.html))

The passage is about character, but the same method applies to the case of feeling. The material is what is already there; the work is re-fitting it:

> "Here a large mass of second nature has been added; there a piece of original nature has been removed — both times through long practice and daily work at it. Here the ugly that could not be removed is concealed; there it has been reinterpreted and made sublime." ([§290](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/blattnew/intro/gs290.html))

The passage mentions no discharge of what cannot be removed: it is concealed or "reinterpreted and made sublime" ([§290](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/blattnew/intro/gs290.html)). On this reading an emotion is not a finished product delivered by nature but raw material for a practice, and the practice is slow: "long practice and daily work at it" ([§290](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/blattnew/intro/gs290.html)).

In the compendium the idea backs [[P113 — Express by shaping, not venting]], and the compendium reads it as a forerunner of the framing in [[AF009 — Understand that affect is constructed, not discovered]].

Modeling is learning by watching another person act and seeing what follows. A Springer reference entry defines observational learning as "the acquisition of new behaviors that occurs when an individual, or observer, watches a model, or actor, perform the target behavior", and says it "was in stark contrast to the behavioral movement which emphasized the role of first-hand experience of reinforcement in learning" ([Observational Learning, Springer Nature](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-24612-3_1801)).

### Four processes

The Springer entry says the model has four components, "all of which must occur for an observer to learn: attention, retention, motor reproduction, and motivation". It quotes Bandura (1977, p. 24): "Attentional processes determine what is selectively observed". Bandura (1989) is quoted in an ERIC paper: "observational learning is governed by four component subfunctions", and "retention involves an active process of transforming and restructuring the information conveyed by modeled events into rules and conceptions for memory representation" ([Bandura and the Bobo Doll, ERIC PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED499095.pdf)).

OpenStax puts it plainly: "we don't learn a behavior simply by observing a model". It describes motivation as depending on "what happened to the model", with vicarious reinforcement raising the wish to copy and vicarious punishment lowering it ([OpenStax Psychology, 6.4](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-4-observational-learning-modeling)).

### Learning versus performing

The ERIC paper reports that Bandura (1989) distinguished acquisition from performance, "since people do not perform everything they learn", and that a learner "could comprehend the information being modeled but not be able to actually perform the behavior". It names three incentive sources: direct, vicarious and self-produced. Vicarious reinforcement is "particularly effective if students witness successes of individuals who are similar to themselves" (Bandura, 1986, as cited there). Britannica adds that people can learn from behaviour without imitating it ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/science/observational-learning)).

### A use in practice

A medical-education study of learning from clinical teachers found that students "evidenced the powerful impact of direct and vicarious reinforcement" ([Springer, BMC Medical Education](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-018-1251-x?error=cookies_not_supported&code=dfae7289-7823-43ea-b1b7-02b9c7ffd29f)).

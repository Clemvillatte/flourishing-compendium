The Noble Eightfold Path (Pali ariya aṭṭhaṅgika magga) is the fourth Noble Truth: the Buddha's summary of the training, given in the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11) as [[Right view|right view]], [[Right intention|right intention]], right speech, right action, right livelihood, [[Right effort|right effort]], right mindfulness, and right concentration. "Right" (sammā) does not mean morally correct so much as complete or whole — each factor is a skill to be developed, not a rule to be obeyed.

The path is traditionally grouped into three trainings: wisdom (view and intention), ethics (speech, action, livelihood), and concentration (effort, mindfulness, concentration) — principle B020. The compendium's reading is that the eight factors are not a sequence to complete but a set to hold together: view guides action, action steadies concentration, concentration clarifies view. The [[Gradual training|gradual training]] (B029) is the same path paced for an ordinary life.

The Eightfold Path is the Buddhist member of a family of structured disciplines of transformation: Aristotle's habituation toward virtue, the Stoic curriculum of logic, physics, and ethics, and the classical progymnasmata of rhetorical training. Its cross-tradition equivalent in spirit is any curriculum that treats wisdom as a trainable skill rather than an insight that arrives whole.

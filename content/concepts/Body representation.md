“Body representation” — Gallagher, 2005.

Body representation is the term cognitive science and philosophy use for the internal structures that carry information about the body. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on bodily awareness gives the minimal definition:

> "According to a minimal definition, a body representation is an internal structure that has the function to track the state of the body and encode it, that can misrepresent it and that can be decoupled from it." (De Vignemont, [SEP: Bodily Awareness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bodily-awareness/))

### The schema/image distinction

The entry distinguishes body schema from body image:

> "It is commonly defined as the representation of the body for action." (De Vignemont, [SEP: Bodily Awareness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bodily-awareness/))

and records the distinction's standing:

> "Furthermore, the distinction between the body schema and the body image has become the stock in trade of much work in cognitive neuroscience and philosophy." (De Vignemont, [SEP: Bodily Awareness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bodily-awareness/))

The entry also notes a controversy: whether the body schema should be conceived as a specific type of mental representation at all, with Merleau-Ponty and Gallagher arguing it should be conceived as a sensorimotor function, devoid of intentional content.

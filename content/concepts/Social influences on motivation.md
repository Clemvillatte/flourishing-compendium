Social influences on motivation are the ways other people, their norms, and the support or pressure they provide change what a person wants to do and how strongly. The seed attributes this to Festinger (1954) and Cialdini (2001). Three sources I opened bear on it, and each covers a different part.

> "Human beings can be proactive and engaged or, alternatively, passive and alienated, largely as a function of the social conditions in which they develop and function." (Ryan and Deci, 2000, American Psychologist 55(1):68-78, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/))

### Social conditions and self-motivation

Ryan and Deci's abstract says research guided by self-determination theory has focused on "the social-contextual conditions that facilitate versus forestall the natural processes of self-motivation and healthy psychological development." It reports three innate psychological needs, competence, autonomy and relatedness, which "when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being" ([Ryan and Deci, 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/)). This is the support side of the seed sentence.

### Comparison with others

Festinger's 1954 paper begins from the hypothesis that "there exists, in the human organism, a drive to evaluate his opinions and his abilities," and adds that where objective, non-social means are unavailable, "people evaluate their opinions and abilities by comparison respectively with the opinions and abilities of others." His third hypothesis is that the tendency to compare with a specific person "decreases as the difference between his opinion or ability and one's own increases." He also derives pressure toward uniformity in groups, and that pressure rises with the importance of the opinion or ability ([Festinger, 1954, Human Relations 7(2):117-140](https://www.humanscience.org/docs/Festinger%20(1954)%20A%20Theory%20of%20Social%20Comparison%20Processes.pdf)). The paper is about self-evaluation and group uniformity. It does not use the word motivation as a topic, so the link to motivation is an inference from it.

### Norms and goals

For the norms side I opened Cialdini and Goldstein's 2004 Annual Review article, not a 2001 work. Its abstract says targets of influence are motivated "to form accurate perceptions of reality and react accordingly, to develop and preserve meaningful social relationships, and to maintain a favorable self-concept," and that the review stresses influence processes that are "subtle, indirect, and outside of awareness" ([Cialdini and Goldstein, 2004, Annu Rev Psychol 55:591-621](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14744228/)).

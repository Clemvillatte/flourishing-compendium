“Alterity” — Levinas, Totality and Infinity.

Alterity is Levinas' word for the otherness of the other person: what remains outside my comprehension rather than what I constitute as an object. In Totality and Infinity, thinking the stranger is "to do more or better than think".

> "To think the infinite, the transcendent, the Stranger, is hence not to think an object. But to think what does not have the outlines of an object is in reality to do more or better than think." (Levinas, Totality and Infinity, TI 49, Lingis translation, quoted in the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Emmanuel Levinas"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/levinas/))

### What it rules out

The Stanford article's gloss: Levinas aligns transcendence "with exteriority, in the sense of what lies outside myself but eludes my comprehensive knowledge: the other person (TI 49)". We can and do constitute the other as an alter ego, the article notes, but such constitution "never exhausts his fundamental difference".

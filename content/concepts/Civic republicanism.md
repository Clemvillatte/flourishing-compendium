“Civic republicanism” — Sandel, Democracy's Discontent.

Civic republicanism is a tradition in political thought that puts freedom first, but understands freedom differently from the liberalism it grew up arguing with. A free person, on this view, is not merely someone nobody happens to interfere with; it is someone who does not live at the mercy of another's arbitrary power. The Stanford Encyclopedia marks out the contemporary school:

> "According to republicans in this second sense (sometimes called 'civic republicans' or 'neo-republicans'), the paramount republican value is political liberty, understood as non-domination or independence from arbitrary power."

> "It defines freedom as a sort of structural independence—as the condition of not being subject to the arbitrary or uncontrolled power of a master." ([Lovett, "Republicanism", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/))

The difference from the standard liberal view is easiest to see in the entry's example of the slave with a kindly master:

> "The slave lacks freedom because he is vulnerable to the arbitrary power of his master; whether his master happens to exercise that power is neither here nor there." (same entry, section 1.3)

The tradition reaches back through Roman law's distinction between free citizens and dependent slaves, Machiavelli, and the English republicans, with Harrington's "empire of laws and not of men" as its classic slogan; the contemporary revival is tied to Quentin Skinner's historical work and Philip Pettit's philosophical development, in which a person is free to the extent that no one has "the capacity to interfere in their affairs on an arbitrary basis" (same entry, sections 1.2 and 3.1). Civic virtue and participation matter to it as the maintenance work of a free society: virtue is "instrumentally useful both in bringing about the right sorts of laws, institutions, and norms, and in ensuring their durability and reliability" (same entry, section 4.3). For the compendium, this is the tradition behind [[R108 — Practise deliberative community]]: a community that deliberates together is practicing the independence that the tradition says freedom is.

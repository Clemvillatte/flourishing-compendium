Socialized intelligence is Dewey's term in The Public and Its Problems for what a democracy produces and an oligarchy cannot: intelligence that is pooled, tested, and distributed. His statement of the division of labor that makes it work:

> "No government by experts in which the masses do not have the chance to inform the experts as to their needs can be anything but an oligarchy managed in the interests of the few. [...] The world has suffered more from leaders and authorities than from the masses."

([Dewey, The Public and Its Problems, ch. VI, Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/the-public-and-its-problems/text/chapter-6).)

### What the pooling requires

The chapter's division of labor is the concept's structure: "Inquiry, indeed, is a work which devolves upon experts. But their expertness is not shown in framing and executing policies, but in discovering and making known the facts upon which the former depend" ([same](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/the-public-and-its-problems/text/chapter-6)). And the many's role is judging rather than investigating: "It is not necessary that the many should have the knowledge and skill to carry on the needed investigations; what is required is that they have the ability to judge of the bearing of the knowledge supplied by others upon common concerns" ([same](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/the-public-and-its-problems/text/chapter-6)).

### Why intelligence is social

Dewey's own summary of the epistemic claim: "effective intelligence is not an original, innate endowment. No matter what are the differences in native intelligence [...] the actuality of mind is dependent upon the education which social conditions effect" ([same](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/the-public-and-its-problems/text/chapter-6)). And the pointed line against the expert class: "The notion that intelligence is a personal endowment or personal attainment is the great conceit of the intellectual class, as that of the commercial class is that wealth is something which they personally have wrought and possess" ([same](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/the-public-and-its-problems/text/chapter-6)).

My principles on communal inquiry and pooling intelligence run on this ground: the reason to solve problems together is epistemic as much as procedural, since the separated classes get one-sided knowledge ([Dewey, Democracy and Education, MW9:354, quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/Entries/dewey-political/)).

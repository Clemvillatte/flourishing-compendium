In Friedrich Nietzsche's sense, genealogy is a method: instead of asking what a moral value is, ask under what conditions it was invented and what it has done to the lives lived under it. The Preface to On the Genealogy of Morals (1887) sets the two questions side by side — a question about origins, then a question about effects on human well-being.

> "Under what conditions did Man invent for himself those judgments of values, 'Good' and 'Evil'? And what intrinsic value do they possess in themselves? Have they up to the present hindered or advanced human well-being? Are they a symptom of the distress, impoverishment, and degeneration of Human Life? Or, conversely, is it in them that is manifested the fulness, the strength, and the will of Life, its courage, its self-confidence, its future?" — [Nietzsche, On the Genealogy of Morals, Preface §3, full text via Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52319/52319-h/52319-h.htm)

### Why the method matters here

Those are the questions of this compendium as well. A value is examined for what it does to a life: whether it evidences flourishing or exhaustion. The diagnostic posture joins an inquiry into origins with an evaluation of effects — noticing what an inherited answer has actually done for the person holding it.

In Friedrich Nietzsche's sense, genealogy is a method: instead of asking what a moral value is, ask under what conditions it was invented and whose life it served. On the Genealogy of Morals (1887) traces values such as "good" and "evil" back to historical sources in which the strong named their own strength good, and in which the weak, resenting that strength, answered with a revaluation that branded it evil. The point is not to refute the values but to dislodge their air of being timeless.

> "Under what conditions did Man invent for himself those judgments of values, 'Good' and 'Evil'? And what intrinsic value do they possess in themselves? Have they up to the present hindered or advanced human well-being? Are they a symptom of the distress, impoverishment, and degeneration of Human Life? Or, conversely, is it in them that is manifested the fulness, the strength, and the will of Life, its courage, its self-confidence, its future?" — [Nietzsche, On the Genealogy of Morals, Preface §3 (Samuel translation, 1913)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52319/52319-h/52319-h.htm)

### Why the method matters here

Those are the questions of this compendium as well. A value is examined for what it does to a life: whether it evidences flourishing or exhaustion. The same diagnostic posture appears wherever a practice asks to be checked against its fruits — in self-overcoming, in the revaluation of values, and in the discipline of noticing what an inherited answer has actually done for the person holding it.

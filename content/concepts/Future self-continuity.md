Future self-continuity is Hal Hershfield's construct for how connected a person feels to their own future self. The research question behind it is why people treat their future selves almost like strangers, and the measured answer is that the felt sense of continuity predicts patience.

> "In three experiments, we tested a \"future self-continuity\" hypothesis that individual differences in the perception of one's present self as continuous with a future self would be associated with measures of saving in the laboratory and everyday life. Higher future self-continuity (assessed by a novel index) predicted reduced discounting of future rewards in a laboratory task, more matches in adjectival descriptions of present and future selves, and greater lifetime accumulation of financial assets (even after controlling for age and education)." — [Ersner-Hershfield, Garton, Ballard, Samanez-Larkin & Knutson, Judgment and Decision Making 4(4) (2009), 280–286, abstract via the author's research page](https://www.halhershfield.com/research-blog/tag/Considering%20The%20Future%20Self)

### What continuity does

The three-experiment finding is a chain: people whose adjectival descriptions of present and future selves matched discounted future rewards less in the lab and had accumulated more financial assets in life. The compendium's principle turns this into temporal ethics: owning your becoming means treating the person you will become as you rather than as a stranger to be taxed.

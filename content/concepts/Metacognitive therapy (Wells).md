treating metacognitive beliefs ("worrying is useful") reduces anxiety and depression (Wells, 2009).

Adrian Wells' metacognitive therapy (MCT) shifts the target of treatment from the content of thoughts to beliefs about thinking. A review in Cureus summarizes the approach:

> "Metacognitive therapy (MCT) is a novel and promising transdiagnostic psychotherapy intervention based on the Self-Regulatory Executive Function model of conceptualizing emotional disorders. It was developed by Adrian Wells in 2009. Its therapeutic response occurs by reducing dysfunctional metacognitive beliefs regarding worry and rumination, often seen in patients with psychiatric disorders." ([Sharma, Sagar, Kaloiya and Mehta, Cureus, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9030663/))

### The syndrome and the lever

The model beneath the treatment holds that a particular style of thinking, not any particular thought, keeps disorder going: "According to this model, a particular style of repetitive thinking called cognitive-attentional syndrome (CAS) contributes to occurrence, persistence, and relapse in most psychological disorders." What maintains it, on the review's account, is metacognitive belief, and MCT works by reducing those beliefs rather than by disputing the worries themselves.

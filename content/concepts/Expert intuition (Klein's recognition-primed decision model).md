The compendium uses this heading for Gary Klein's account of how experienced decision-makers act fast under pressure. The recognition-primed decision model:

> "Recognition-primed decision (RPD) is a model of how people make quick, effective decisions when faced with complex situations." ([Wikipedia, Recognition-primed decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_primed_decision))

### One option at a time

"In this model, the decision maker is assumed to generate a possible course of action, compare it to the constraints imposed by the situation, and select the first course of action that is not rejected" ([Wikipedia, Recognition-primed decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_primed_decision)). The model "has been described in diverse groups including trauma nurses, fireground commanders, chess players, and stock market traders" ([Wikipedia, Recognition-primed decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recognition_primed_decision)).

### When intuition can be trusted

Kahneman and Klein examine the conditions under which professional intuition is likely to be accurate:

> "They conclude that evaluating the likely quality of an intuitive judgment requires an assessment of the predictability of the environment in which the judgment is made and of the individual's opportunity to learn the regularities of that environment." (Kahneman and Klein 2009, abstract, [Am Psychol 64(6):515-526](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/))

The same abstract states that "Subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy" (Kahneman and Klein 2009, [PubMed 19739881](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)).

Overgeneral autobiographical memory (OGM) is a tendency to retrieve general summaries instead of specific events when cued to recall. Sumner's review states that it "has been found to be an important cognitive phenomenon with respect to depression and trauma-related psychopathology (e.g., posttraumatic stress disorder)" ([Sumner, The mechanisms underlying overgeneral autobiographical memory, Clinical Psychology Review 2012, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22142837/)).

### The CaR-FA-X model

The leading explanation is the model of Williams and colleagues (2007), which "proposes that three processes influence OGM: capture and rumination, functional avoidance, and impaired executive control" (Sumner, above). A PMC illustration from an adolescent study gives an example of capture: to the cue word happy, a person thinks "Why can't I be as happy as others seem to be", is "captured" by the thought, and the retrieved output is an overgeneral memory tied to the rumination ([Adolescent over-general memory, life events and mental health, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4743605/)). Functional avoidance is described there as "the tendency to passively avoid any episodic material (even positive material) that might reactivate memory of past difficulties or trauma".

### Evidence

Sumner's evaluation of 38 studies found "robust support for associations between OGM and both rumination and impaired executive control", and that OGM "also appears to be a cognitive avoidance strategy", with avoidance of specific memories reducing distress after an aversive event "at least in the short term". Unresolved issues include "the nature of the capture phenomenon, the role of trauma in functional avoidance, and the developmental nature of functional avoidance" (PubMed). Williams's own review notes substantial evidence for links between OGM and current or past depression, trauma history, and suicidal ideation and behaviour "independent of depression" ([Williams, CaRFAX, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26529222/)).

### Limits on the seed claim

A meta-analysis of 15 studies found that, though "the effects are small", memory specificity predicted the course of depression: r = -.10 for specific memories and r = .13 for categoric or overgeneral memories ([Sumner, Griffith and Mineka, OGM as a predictor of the course of depression, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2878838/)). The adolescent paper adds that the associations were stronger for people depressed at assessment, which "questioned the role of OGM as a predisposing vulnerability factor in the absence of pre-existing depression" (PMC4743605). A test in 29 adolescents with elevated depressive symptoms found that the model was "partially supported", with differences in rumination and working memory but not in inhibition or functional avoidance ([A test of the CaR-FA-X mechanisms and depression in adolescents, Memory](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09658211.2018.1518457)). So "associated with" is the safe phrase; causation is not established.

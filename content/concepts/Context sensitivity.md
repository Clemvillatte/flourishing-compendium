Context sensitivity is one of the four evolutionary principles the Extended Evolutionary Meta-Model applies to every dimension and level of change processes: behaviour is shaped by the context that selects it. The PBT manual defines it clinically:

> "Increasing a client’s context sensitivity means making them more aware of the shifting demands of their context so they can flexibly adjust their response to it in an adaptive way." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, 2021, Learning Process-Based Therapy, Chapter 7)

The principle has an ancestor in functional analysis: behaviour makes sense relative to the situation that occasions and reinforces it. PBT generalises that insight into a whole-grid principle. The EEMM classifies change processes into "six key psychological dimensions (affect, cognition, attention, self, motivation, and overt behavior), nested into two additional levels of selection (biophysiological and sociocultural)", and holds that "in each of these dimensions and levels, variation, selection, retention, and context are central" (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, 2021, Learning Process-Based Therapy, Chapter 3). Context sensitivity is the client-facing face of that last term: on this grid, a stuck pattern can be maintained by an unexamined context, and the definition above makes awareness of that context the clinical target.

The definition also says what the work is not: it is not insight for its own sake. The aim is awareness that feeds adjustment — noticing the shifting demands well enough to "flexibly adjust" the response. A client who can name their contexts but cannot change what they do inside them has half the skill.

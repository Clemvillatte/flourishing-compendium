Gabriel Marcel's name for the mode of being in which one is involved in what one is, rather than standing before it as an examiner. The concept belongs to his The Mystery of Being, and it rests on his distinction between problems, which one can lay siege to from outside, and mysteries, which one is inside of.

### The sphere where inside and before lose their meaning

> "A problem is something which I meet, which I find completely before me, but which I can therefore lay siege to and reduce. But a mystery is something in which I am myself involved, and it can therefore only be thought of as a sphere where the distinction between what is in me and what is before me loses its meaning and initial validity." — [Marcel, Being and Having (1949), as reproduced in Treanor & Sweetman, SEP, "Gabriel Marcel"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/)

Participation is the fitting stance toward what is mystery in this sense. Marcel's own words reach for it directly: "I aspire to participate in this being, in this reality—and perhaps this aspiration is already a degree of participation, however rudimentary" ([The Philosophy of Existentialism, quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/)). Its social face is what he calls availability, disponibilité: to be present to another "with the whole of himself", where for the unavailable person the other is merely an object ([Marcel 1995, pp. 39-40, in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marcel/)). Participation does not exclude reflection; Marcel's secondary reflection is itself a participatory thinking, since the thinker includes his own involvement in what it examines.

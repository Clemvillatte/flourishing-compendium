Two people can score equally high on a self-esteem scale and still differ in how that self-regard behaves under pressure. Michael Kernis argued that high self-esteem comes in a fragile form and a secure form. His summary, from the abstract of his 2003 target article:

> High self-esteem can be fragile or secure depending upon the extent to which it is defensive or genuine, contingent or true, unstable or stable, and discrepant or congruent with implicit (nonconscious) feelings of self-worth.

(Kernis, 2003, Psychological Inquiry 14(1): 1-26, [abstract](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/TARGET-ARTICLE:-Toward-a-Conceptualization-of-Kernis/90e6fe5d604dee8d9662f988780b056c3989d13e).)

### Four dimensions

The abstract names four pairs: defensive or genuine, contingent or true, unstable or stable, and discrepant or congruent with implicit feelings of self-worth. It does not unpack them, and the article body was not available in an open copy for this page, so the compendium should not lean on specifics such as measurement scales or effect sizes from here.

### Where it leads

Kernis says optimal self-esteem is "characterized by qualities associated with genuine, true, stable, and congruent (with implicit self-esteem) high self-esteem", and that optimal and high self-esteem are different things. He then proposes authenticity, with four components (awareness, unbiased processing, action, relational), as the way to describe what makes the optimal form adaptive ([abstract](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/TARGET-ARTICLE:-Toward-a-Conceptualization-of-Kernis/90e6fe5d604dee8d9662f988780b056c3989d13e)). The abstract also says he discusses what the fragile versus secure distinction means for narcissism, defensive processing and cross-cultural views of self-esteem.

### A caution from Kernis himself

In his reply to commentators, Kernis clarifies that "My use of the term contingent is not meant to imply that secure or optimal self-esteem is completely independent (i.e., noncontingent) of contextual events and significant others, especially during the formative years." He also writes that authenticity and secure high self-esteem are likely reciprocally related ([Kernis, 2003, "Optimal Self-Esteem and Authenticity: Separating Fantasy From Reality"](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2003_Kernis_Optimal.pdf)). So his "secure" does not mean independent of other people or circumstances, and essays should not present it as a case for self-sufficiency. In the same reply he adds that he believes various forms of fragile self-esteem tap into an aspect of the "narcissistic experience" without being the same as narcissism.

“Facing the fear” — Kierkegaard; Kierkegaard, The Concept of Anxiety.

Facing the fear is the thread Kierkegaard pulled through The Concept of Anxiety, written under the pseudonym Vigilius Haufniensis ([SEP, "Søren Kierkegaard"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/)). On the entry's reading, the book probes the nature of human choice: anxiety belongs to the human condition itself rather than being only a pathological symptom, and it is an awareness of freedom ([SEP, ibid.](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/)). In anxiety we feel a "sympathetic antipathy" or an "antipathetic sympathy", drawn toward and repelled by a possibility at once (CA 42; [SEP, ibid.](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/)).

> "anxiety is the dizziness of freedom, which emerges when the spirit wants to posit the synthesis and freedom looks down into its own possibility, laying hold of finiteness to support itself." (Kierkegaard, The Concept of Anxiety, CA 61/SKS 4, 365, quoted in [Lippitt and Evans, "Søren Kierkegaard", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/))

### Learning to be anxious the right way

The pseudonym's cure matters for the compendium's theme: it is not the elimination of anxiety. The entry's reading of the book's close is that what matters is learning to be anxious properly, and it carries the line: "Whoever has learned to be anxious in the right way has learned the ultimate" (CA 155; [SEP, ibid.](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/)). A morally free action comes from a leap, not from necessity (CA 48-51; [SEP, ibid.](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/)).

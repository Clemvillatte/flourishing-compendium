Psychological well-being, in Carol Ryff's formulation, is the theory-guided answer to a gap she found in the field: the reigning measures of well-being had little theoretical grounding, so her 1989 study defined the essential features of positive functioning by integrating the clinical, humanistic and life-span theories of what it means to be well, then built scales for them. The result is a six-dimension model: self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, and personal growth.

> Reigning measures of psychological well-being have little theoretical grounding, despite an extensive literature on the contours of positive functioning. Aspects of well-being derived from this literature (i.e., self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, and personal growth) were operationalized. — Ryff, ["Happiness Is Everything, or Is It?" (1989), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 57(6), 1069-1081](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf)

### What the study found

Ryff's critique targeted two prior indexes: Bradburn's affect balance, built for a study of social change rather than to define well-being's structure, and life-satisfaction and morale scales with acknowledged theoretical gaps. The paper also carries the compendium's key eudaimonia point: Bradburn's team had cited Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics, where the highest of all goods achievable by human action is eudaimonia, but translated the term as happiness; Ryff, following Waterman, argues the translation matters, and that had eudaimonia been translated as realization of one's true potential rather than as happiness, the previous twenty years of research might well have taken different directions. In the study itself, 321 adults rated themselves on the six new scales alongside the six prominent prior instruments; positive relations with others, autonomy, purpose in life, and personal growth were not strongly tied to the prior assessment indexes, supporting the claim that key aspects of positive functioning had not been represented in the empirical arena.

Encoding strategies are the ways a person works on new material while taking it in, and the research behind the idea says that the way you work on it shapes how long you remember it. The source usually cited is Craik and Lockhart's 1972 paper on levels of processing. They propose that "trace persistence is a function of depth of analysis, with deeper levels of analysis associated with more elaborate, longer lasting, and stronger traces" ([Craik and Lockhart, 1972](https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych205/papers/CraikLockhart72.pdf)).

### Depth and elaboration

In their account, "greater 'depth' implies a greater degree of semantic or cognitive analysis". They add that after a stimulus is recognized, "it may undergo further processing by enrichment or elaboration", and give an example: "after a word is recognized, it may trigger associations, images or stories on the basis of the subject's past experience with the word" ([Craik and Lockhart, 1972](https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych205/papers/CraikLockhart72.pdf)). This is the part of the paper closest to my phrase "connecting to prior knowledge".

The authors also explain why they moved attention to the learner's own activity. Faced with inconsistencies in storage models, they found it "more useful to focus on the encoding operations themselves" ([Craik and Lockhart, 1972](https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych205/papers/CraikLockhart72.pdf)). Simply repeating material is, in their terms, "maintaining information at one level of processing", which is different from analysing it more deeply.

### What later work adjusted

Lockhart and Craik's 1990 retrospective notes that research since 1972 "has enabled the original formulation of depth of processing to be refined in various ways", with elaboration and distinctiveness as examples. They keep both ideas: "depth referring to qualitatively different types of processing and elaboration referring to rich or impoverished processing within any one qualitative domain". They also report a criticism they take seriously, the "danger of circular reasoning", meaning "the tendency to define depth in terms of the memory outcome" ([Lockhart and Craik, 1990](http://www.psychologyib.com/uploads/1/1/7/5/11758934/_ib_psychology_-_craik_and_lockhart_1972.pdf)). Their conclusion is that "many of the original ideas of levels of processing have survived" ([1990](http://www.psychologyib.com/uploads/1/1/7/5/11758934/_ib_psychology_-_craik_and_lockhart_1972.pdf)).

### Scope

The 1972 paper describes a framework and does not offer a list of study techniques. My three-part list of elaboration, organisation and connecting to prior knowledge is a practical summary. The paper supports elaboration and depth. A separate claim about organisation as a strategy does not appear in the passages checked. The experiments are on word lists, which is far from learning a way of living.

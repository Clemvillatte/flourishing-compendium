"Pre-understanding" — Heidegger, Being and Time, §32.

Heidegger's argument in §32 is that interpretation never starts from zero. It is founded on a fore-structure, fore-having, fore-sight, fore-conception, and the appeal to what "stands there" in a text turns out to be an appeal to what the interpreter brought along.

### The three fores

The three fores each do a distinct job, in Heidegger's own sequence:

> "In every case this interpretation is grounded in something we have in advance—in a fore-having." (Heidegger, Being and Time §32, [Macquarrie and Robinson translation, p. 191, beyng.com](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.191.html))
>
> "In every case interpretation is grounded in something we see in advance—in a fore-sight. This fore-sight 'takes the first cut' out of what has been taken into our fore-having, and it does so with a view to a definite way in which this can be interpreted." (Heidegger, Being and Time §32, [Macquarrie and Robinson translation, p. 191, beyng.com](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.191.html))
>
> "In either case, the interpretation has already decided for a definite way of conceiving it, either with finality or with reservations; it is grounded in something we grasp in advance—in a fore-conception." (Heidegger, Being and Time §32, [Macquarrie and Robinson translation, p. 191, beyng.com](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.191.html))

Heidegger then aims the point at anyone who claims to read only what the text says:

> "If, when one is engaged in a particular concrete kind of interpretation, in the sense of exact textual Interpretation, one likes to appeal [beruft] to what 'stands there', then one finds that what 'stands there' in the first instance is nothing other than the obvious undiscussed assumption [V ormeinung] of the person who does the interpreting. In an interpretative approach there lies such an assumption, as that which has been 'taken for granted' ["gesetzt"] with the interpretation as such—that is to say, as that which has been presented in our fore-having, our fore-sight, and our fore-conception." (Heidegger, Being and Time §32, [Macquarrie and Robinson translation, p. 192, beyng.com](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.192.html))

Read as a unit, the three fores describe one movement: what a reader already holds, the angle cut into it, and the concept ready to receive it. Heidegger's claim is not that interpretation is doomed but that it is always already underway. The consequence is a shift in intellectual honesty: the question becomes not whether to bring assumptions, but which ones, held openly enough to be revised by the thing itself. That is the hinge Gadamer later turns into a defense of prejudice: you cannot understand anything without prejudgments, so the work is to know them rather than to pretend to have none.

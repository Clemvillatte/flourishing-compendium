“Judging by where one rests” — Confucius, Analects 2.10.

Analects 2.10 gives Confucius's method for seeing through a person. The Master said:

> See what a man does. Mark his motives. Examine in what things he rests. How can a man conceal his character? How can a man conceal his character? (Analects 2.10, Legge translation, [Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/analects/wei-zheng/zh))

The verse stacks three observations: what a person does, the motives they follow, and where they come to rest. The last is this page's title. An action can be performed and a reason rehearsed; the place a person settles into when nothing is demanded is the observation the closing question leans on. Read a person there, the verse argues, and concealment runs out: how can a man conceal his character?

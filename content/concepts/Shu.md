Shu is the Confucian word for reciprocity: using one's own likes and dislikes as the measure for how to treat others. The Analects gives it in a scene where a disciple asks for one word to live by.

> "Tsze-kung asked, saying, 'Is there one word which may serve as a rule of practice for all one's life?' The Master said, 'Is not RECIPROCITY such a word? What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others.'" (Confucius, [The Analects, Book XV, James Legge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3330))

The formulation is negative, the silver rule: it does not command doing good so much as forbearing from what one would not accept. The Wikipedia article on Confucius records that he "championed the Silver Rule, or a negative form of the Golden Rule, advising, 'Do not do unto others what you do not want done to yourself.'" ([Wikipedia, Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius)).

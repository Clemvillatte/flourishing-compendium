This entry uses the label "Magnanimity" for the character discussed as "pride" in Book IV, chapter 3 of Ross's translation of the Nicomachean Ethics ([Aristotle, IV.3](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html)).

> "Pride seems even from its name to be concerned with great things; what sort of great things, is the first question we must try to answer. It makes no difference whether we consider the state of character or the man characterized by it. Now the man is thought to be proud who thinks himself worthy of great things, being worthy of them; for he who does so beyond his deserts is a fool, but no virtuous man is foolish or silly." (Aristotle, [Nicomachean Ethics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html), Book IV, chapter 3, paragraph beginning "Pride seems even from its name…", W. D. Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive)

Aristotle calls someone vain if he claims great things without deserving them ([Aristotle, IV.3](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html)). He describes someone who claims less than his deserts as unduly humble ([Aristotle, IV.3](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html)).

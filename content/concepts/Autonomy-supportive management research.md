“Autonomy-supportive management research” — Deci and Ryan, 2000.

Autonomy-supportive management is the cluster of supervisory behaviours that self-determination theory identifies as supporting the employee's basic psychological needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness. The behaviours include taking the employee's internal frame of reference, providing rationale and choice, and minimising controls used to motivate.

### What the meta-analysis found

Slemp, Kern, Patrick and Ryan reported a meta-analytic review of perceived leader autonomy support drawn from work settings.

> "Results showed LAS correlated strongly and positively with autonomous work motivation, and was unrelated to controlled work motivation. Correlations became increasingly positive with the more internalized forms of work motivation described by self-determination theory. LAS was positively associated with basic needs, well-being, and positive work behaviors, and was negatively associated with distress." (Slemp, Kern, Patrick & Ryan, 2018, [Motivation and Emotion, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6133074/))

The same record reports the database as 754 correlations across 72 studies, 83 unique samples, with N = 32,870, and notes that correlations were not moderated by the source of autonomy support, country of the sample, publication status, or the operationalisation of autonomy support.

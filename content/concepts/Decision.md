A decision, in the compendium's sense, is the act by which a person settles a course of action and takes responsibility for it. The seed comes from Jaspers' Philosophy, vol. 2, where decision belongs to existential freedom: the self is not given in advance but is realized in choosing ([Tepley 2019, quoting Jaspers P2](https://existenz.us/volumes/Vol.14-2Tepley.pdf)).

For Jaspers, the freedom of a decision has two sides. One is breadth before the choice: he writes that "I become free by incessantly broadening my world orientation, by limitlessly visualizing premises and possibilities of action, and by allowing all motives to speak to me and to work within me" (Philosophy, vol. 2, p. 157, [quoted in Tepley 2019](https://existenz.us/volumes/Vol.14-2Tepley.pdf)). The other is holding after it: "the import of resolution is that once I have made a choice I will unconditionally stick to it" (p. 159, [ibid.](https://existenz.us/volumes/Vol.14-2Tepley.pdf)). A decision, on this account, is not the moment of picking but the whole arc of surveying, committing, and standing behind what was chosen.

### In the compendium

Three principles put the concept to work in different traditions:

- [[E046 — Practise resoluteness (Entschlossenheit)]] — Heidegger's version of the committed side: choosing within one's actual situation
- [[P076 — When habits conflict, let deliberation do its work]] — Dewey's version of the surveying side: deliberation as a rehearsal of competing lines of action
- [[X002 — Use no more this than that as a working formula]] — Sextus's version of what precedes decision: suspending judgment when the reasons balance

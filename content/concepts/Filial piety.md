Filial piety (xiao, 孝) is the Confucian virtue of reverence and care for parents and ancestors. The Analects treats it as the first school of human relationship: the place a person first learns to take another's needs as their own concern. Youzi's summary makes it the root of the rest:

> "The superior man bends his attention to what is radical. That being established, all practical courses naturally grow up. Filial piety and fraternal submission! - are they not the root of all benevolent actions?" ([Confucius, Analects 1.2, trans. Legge](https://ctext.org/analects/xue-er))

### More than support

The tradition is explicit that the virtue is not material support. Asked what filial piety is, Confucius answered:

> "The filial piety nowadays means the support of one's parents. But dogs and horses likewise are able to do something in the way of support; - without reverence, what is there to distinguish the one support given from the other?" ([Confucius, Analects 2.7, trans. Legge](https://ctext.org/analects/wei-zheng))

The same chapter presses the point inward: when the young take on the elders' troublesome affairs and serve them first with wine and food, that alone is not yet filial piety, "the difficulty is with the countenance" (2.8). The face, not the ledger, is where reverence shows.

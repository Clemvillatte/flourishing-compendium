This page approaches self-deception through Sartre's discussion of bad faith (mauvaise foi) in Being and Nothingness (1943). The café-waiter example concerns treating a social role as a fixed identity; the passage below presents bad faith as a permanent possibility of consciousness.

> "If bad faith is possible, it is because it is an immediate, permanent threat to every project of the human being; it is because consciousness conceals in its being a permanent risk of bad faith. The origin of this risk is the fact that the nature of consciousness simultaneously is to be what it is not and not to be what it is" (Being and Nothingness, quoted in a [University at Buffalo course handout](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~degray/CP05/Sartre%20-%20Nothingness-Bad-Faith.pdf)).

### The waiter

Sartre's worked example is a café waiter who throws himself into his role. The failure is not the role itself but the treating of it as an essence: "the waiter in the café can not be immediately a café waiter in the sense that this inkwell is an inkwell, or the glass is a glass" (quoted in the Buffalo handout). A person can play at being a waiter, rehearse the gestures, let the role absorb the attention; what they cannot do is be one the way an inkwell is an inkwell. In this example, bad faith takes the form of treating a social role as though it fixed what the person is.

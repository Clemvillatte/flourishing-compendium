Metacognition is thinking about thinking, and John Flavell's 1979 article is where much of the field starts. He proposed that "the monitoring of a wide variety of cognitive enterprises occurs through the actions of and interactions among four classes of phenomena: (a) metacognitive knowledge, (b) metacognitive experiences, (c) goals (or tasks), and (d) actions (or strategies)" ([Flavell, 1979, American Psychologist, course copy](https://jwilson.coe.uga.edu/EMAT7050/Students/Wilson/Flavell%20(1979).pdf)). The article appears in [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ217109) as American Psychologist 34(10), 906-911.

### The four parts

Metacognitive knowledge is "that segment of your (a child's, an adult's) stored world knowledge that has to do with people as cognitive creatures and with their diverse cognitive tasks, goals, actions, and experiences". It covers three kinds of variable: person, task, and strategy. Metacognitive experiences are "any conscious cognitive or affective experiences that accompany and pertain to any intellectual enterprise", for example "the sudden feeling that you do not understand something another person just said".

Flavell separates the strategies too: "Cognitive strategies are invoked to make cognitive progress, metacognitive strategies to monitor it". Paraphrasing aloud what someone told you, to check you understood, is his example of a metacognitive strategy. Experiences can "lead you to establish new goals and to revise or abandon old ones", and can add to, delete from or revise your metacognitive knowledge.

### Why it began with children

The ERIC record says the paper "holds that young children are limited in their knowledge about cognitive phenomena ('metacognition') and do relatively little monitoring of their own memory, comprehension, and other cognitive enterprises" ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ217109)). In the study Flavell cites, older children studied "and usually were" ready, whereas the younger ones "usually were not" ([Semantic Scholar excerpt](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Metacognition-and-Cognitive-Monitoring:-A-New-Area-Flavell/ee652f0f63ed5b0cfe0af4cb4ea76b2ecf790c8d)).

### Later usage

A recent review describes metacognition as "the capacity to reflect on, evaluate and control first-order cognitive processes such as decision-making, memory and perception", and distinguishes stable beliefs about one's capacities from local evaluations of performance ([The future of metacognition research, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11139654/)). A mathematics-education review quotes a later definition by Flavell, Miller and Miller (2002): "any knowledge or cognitive activity that takes as its object, or regulates, any aspect of any cognitive activity" ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11858-010-0245-x.pdf?error=cookies_not_supported&code=03ad0a30-4dcd-4d55-8ee4-8b97d1f9a935)).

### A caution on the seed gloss

The earlier line on this page, "noticing that even your framework is a mental event", is an interpretive reading, not Flavell's wording. In the text I opened, Flavell is describing monitoring of cognitive tasks, not the status of frameworks. Treat the gloss as the wiki's bridge to contemplative themes and keep the two apart in essays.

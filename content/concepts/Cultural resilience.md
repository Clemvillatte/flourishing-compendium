Cultural resilience carries two linked senses in the research literature. At the individual level it "denote[s] the role that culture may play as a resource for resilience in the individual"; at the collective level it is the resilience of whole communities and cultural systems ([Fleming & Ledogar 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2956753/)).

The definition the review endorses for the collective sense, quoting Healy (2006):

> "community or cultural resilience is the capacity of a distinct community or cultural system to absorb disturbance and reorganize while undergoing change so as to retain key elements of structure and identity that preserve its distinctness." ([Fleming & Ledogar 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2956753/))

The move the concept makes is from resilience as an individual trait to resilience as something carried by structure and identity — language, kinship, storytelling, shared practices — that lets a community absorb disturbance without dissolving. That is close to the intuition in MacIntyre's After Virtue: practices and living traditions are what carry the resources a person draws on in misfortune, which is why the original entry source attached the term there.

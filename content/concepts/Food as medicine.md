Food as medicine is the popular label for the Hippocratic idea that diet belongs to the treatment of disease. The famous slogan "let food be thy medicine and medicine be thy food" is a misquotation: a review of the works on food and diet in the Corpus Hippocraticum found the phrase nowhere, and concluded that for Hippocrates "the concept of food was not confused with that of medication" (Cardenas 2013).

What the corpus does argue is that medicine began as the search for the right food. On Ancient Medicine reasons that if the same diet suited the sick and the healthy, medicine would never have been looked for, and ends its account of the discovery of bread and prepared food with the words:

> "To such a discovery and investigation what more suitable name could one give than that of Medicine? since it was discovered for the health of man, for his nourishment and safety, as a substitute for that kind of diet by which pains, diseases, and deaths were occasioned." (Hippocrates, [On Ancient Medicine](https://classics.mit.edu/Hippocrates/ancimed.3.3.html), part 3, Francis Adams translation)

Ambiguity is Beauvoir's name for the human condition's two-foldness, though the concept's fullest development is her own Ethics of Ambiguity rather than Merleau-Ponty's Phenomenology. Her opening claim: "As long as there have been men and they have lived, they have all felt this tragic ambiguity of their condition, but as long as there have been philosophers and they have thought, most of them have tried to mask it" ([Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, ch. 1, Marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)).

### What the ambiguity is

Her own instruction is to stop masking it: "Since we do not succeed in fleeing it, let us therefore try to look the truth in the face. Let us try to assume our fundamental ambiguity. It is in the knowledge of the genuine conditions of our life that we must draw our strength to live and our reason for acting" ([ch. 1](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)). The two-foldness is the subject/object one: "to say that the human being is ambiguous is to say that one is both a subject and an object, that one is both mind and body, that one is both a being for-itself and a being for-others" ([Daigle, "Beauvoir's Politics of Ambiguity"](https://www.cpsa-acsp.ca/papers-2009/Daigle.pdf)).

### The key distinction

Her ethics rests on separating ambiguity from absurdity:

> "To declare that existence is absurd is to deny that it can ever be given a meaning; to say that it is ambiguous is to assert that its meaning is never fixed, that it must be constantly won."

([ch. 3, Marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm).) Absurdity leaves no room for ethics; a finished rationalization of the real leaves no room either. "It is because man's condition is ambiguous that he seeks, through failure and outrageousness, to save his existence" ([ch. 3](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm)). The distinction is the compendium's reason for holding the concept: to stop a person treating the un-fixedness of meaning as evidence there is none.

Exposure processes are the mechanisms by which exposure therapy, the repeated approaching of feared stimuli, produces its effects. Craske and colleagues present an inhibitory-learning account: exposure adds learning that a feared cue does not predict the feared outcome, while the original fear association remains.

> "The more the expectancy can be violated by experience, the greater the inhibitory learning." (Craske et al. 2014, strategy (1) Expectancy Violation, [Maximizing exposure therapy: an inhibitory learning approach](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4114726/), Behav. Res. Ther. 58: 10-23)

### What the model says

The review discusses strategies including expectancy violation, removal of safety signals and varying exposure conditions. Table 6 also recommends maintaining attention on the feared cue during exposure. The authors distinguish this account from approaches that use fear reduction within an exposure trial as the criterion for therapeutic change.

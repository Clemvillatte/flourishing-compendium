Moral exemplar research studies people recognized for exceptional moral action and asks what, psychologically, distinguishes the sampled exemplars from comparison participants. The anchor study compared two contrasting types: 50 Canadian awardees for either exceptional bravery or caring, alongside 50 comparison participants, assessed with personality questionnaires and a life-review interview (Walker and Frimer, [Moral personality of brave and caring exemplars, JPSP 93(5), 2007, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17983304/)).

> "moral exemplars were distinguished from the comparison groups by themes embodied in their life narratives. Specifically, moral exemplars had stronger motivational themes of both agency and communion, were more likely to construe critical life events redemptively, more frequently identified helpers in early life, and reported more secure attachments." (Walker and Frimer, [2007, PubMed record 17983304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17983304/), abstract)

### Two profiles of moral maturity

The abstract reports differences in life-narrative themes and says that personality variables added to moral cognition in predicting exemplary action: as it puts it, "Personality variables were found to substantially augment moral cognition in the prediction of exemplary action." The two exemplar types also diverged from each other. The personality of caring exemplars was more nurturant, generative, and optimistic than that of brave exemplars, and the authors read the divergent profiles as implying "multiple ideals of moral maturity" (Walker and Frimer, 2007, abstract).

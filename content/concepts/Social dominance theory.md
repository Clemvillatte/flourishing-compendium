**Social dominance theory** — Sidanius, Pratto — societies create group-based hierarchy sustained by legitimising myths; hierarchy-enhancing and -attenuating.

## Field

Sociocultural · Psychological science

The theory's own one-sentence statement, from Sidanius and Pratto's 2011 overview: "Stated most simply, social dominance theory (SDT) argues that intergroup oppression, discrimination, and prejudice are the means by which human societies organize themselves as group-based hierarchies, in which members of dominant groups secure a disproportionate share of the good things in life (e.g., powerful roles, good housing, good health), and members of subordinate groups receive a disproportionate share of the bad things in life (e.g., relatively poor housing and poor health)" ([Sidanius and Pratto 2011, quoted in the AWL paper](https://awl-ojs-tamu.tdl.org/awl/article/download/417/310)).

### The legitimizing myths

The maintenance mechanism is cultural rather than coercive. Legitimizing myths are "attitudes, values, beliefs, stereotypes, and ideologies that provide moral and intellectual justification for the social practices that distribute social value within the social system" ([Sidanius and Pratto 1999, p. 45, quoted in the Lausanne model paper](https://api.unil.ch/iris/server/api/core/bitstreams/5a07182c-18e6-4cd6-85ba-239e4e7737aa/content)). The theory splits them by function: "Hierarchy-enhancing legitimizing myths help maintain or increase group-based inequality, and hierarchy-attenuating legitimizing myths decrease group-based inequality and promote egalitarian relations among social groups" ([Pratto, Encyclopedia of Peace Psychology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9780470672532.wbepp253)). The hierarchy-enhancing case includes "the thesis of the divine rights of kings, the doctrine of meritorious karma, or the negative stereotypes associated with the status of subordinates," all sharing the structure that "members of subordinate groups are said to deserve their subordinate status, and members of dominant groups deserve their dominant status" ([Sidanius and Pratto 2001, quoted in the Emerald discussion](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/emre.12671)).

### The asymmetry that stabilizes the hierarchy

The theory's distinctive claim is that subordinate-group members can participate in the maintenance: "group-based hierarchies and inequalities are reproduced and maintained in part through compliance with dominant power by some members of subordinate groups" ([tpmap.org framing paper](https://www.tpmap.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/26.3.3.pdf)). The hierarchy is thus stabilized by consensus rather than force, which is what the myths are for.

"The Axial Age" — Jaspers, The Origin and Goal of History.

The Axial Age is Karl Jaspers' name for the period roughly 800 to 200 BCE, when major intellectual, philosophical and religious systems emerged across the globe in Greece, India, China and the Near East, shifting thought away from localized concerns toward transcendence. Jaspers' book The Origin and Goal of History (Vom Ursprung und Ziel der Geschichte, 1949) introduced the term, playing on the image of a turn, as if on an axis.

> "The Axial Age, roughly spanning from 800 to 200 BCE, was a transformative period where major intellectual, philosophical, and religious systems emerged across the globe. This era saw a shift from localized concerns toward concepts of transcendence, focusing on the cosmos, the nature of thought, and humanity's relationship with the divine and the universe." (Encyclopedia Britannica, [The Axial Age](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Axial-Age))

### Why a writer on flourishing would care

The compendium's own subject matter — the wisdom traditions that ask what a good life is — is largely an inheritance of this period. Britannica's inventory of the developments runs from Socrates and Plato on reason and truth, through karma and liberation in India and Confucian and Daoist guidance for human life in China, to Zoroastrianism's cosmic struggle between good and evil and the Hebrew prophets' creator God ([Britannica, The Axial Age](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Axial-Age)). The concept lets a writer see Buddhism, Confucianism, Greek philosophy and the Hebrew prophets as one era's shared turn of the human mind toward questions that transcend local custom, rather than as isolated curiosities. The compendium's principle E096 carries the practical reading.

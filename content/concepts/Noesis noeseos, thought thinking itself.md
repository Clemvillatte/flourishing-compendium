Noēsis noēseōs, "thinking of thinking," is Aristotle's description of what the primary god does. In the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Aristotle's Metaphysics, Cohen and Reeve give the argument from Metaphysics Λ.9 and its conclusion, quoted here in their translation:

> "It is itself, therefore, that it understands, if indeed it is the most excellent thing, and the active understanding is active understanding of active understanding (hê noêsis noêseôs noêsis)." (Aristotle, Metaphysics Λ.9, 1074b22-35, as translated in Cohen and Reeve, "Aristotle's Metaphysics," [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/))

### Why the god thinks itself

The argument runs by elimination. The primary god either understands itself or something else, and if something else, some other object would be more estimable than the understanding. The entry quotes Aristotle asking whether there are "certain things that it would be absurd for it to think of." He concludes that the best thing cannot take as its object anything lower than itself.

### Where the Nicomachean Ethics comes in

Cohen and Reeve describe this self-understanding as a state of consciousness in which subject and object disappear, "fully and truly attentive, fully alive and joyous," and note that Aristotle defends the same account of blessedness in Nicomachean Ethics X.6-8. The seed's reference to X.7 falls inside that range. This page does not claim that the phrase itself occurs there.

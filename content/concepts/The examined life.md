“The examined life” is Socrates' name for a life held up to questioning — his answer, at his trial, to how a human being should live. The dictum comes from Plato's *Apology* 38a, where Socrates tells the jury that no man should calculate risks of life and death when deciding how to act, only whether he acts justly or unjustly: “the unexamined life is not worth living for a human being” (ὁ δὲ ἀνεξέταστος βίος οὐ βιωτὸς ἀνθρώπῳ) ([Apology 38a, trans. Center for Hellenic Studies](https://chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/plato-the-apology-of-socrates-sb/)). Examination, for Socrates, is not an intellectual hobby but the activity he calls the greatest good for a man: daily discussion of virtue and the examined life itself, tested in conversation against anyone who claims to know how to live ([Apology 38a](https://chs.harvard.edu/primary-source/plato-the-apology-of-socrates-sb/)).

The method is Socratic interrogation: take a claim about how to live (“courage is standing firm”), ask what it really means, watch it fail under cases, and hold the revised claim more lightly than before. Socrates' profession of ignorance — he knows that he does not know — is what keeps the examination honest; it is the ancestor of [[Fallibilism]].

The distinction that keeps this livable: examination is not rumination. Rumination circles repetitively and changes nothing; examination is structured, answerable to evidence, and aimed at living better — which is why the compendium pairs it with metacognition (thinking about one's own thinking) and with practice rules that give it a shape: begin the day with intention and end it with review ([[B192 — Begin the day with intention; end it with review]]), turn experience over ([[P048 — Think reflectively — turn experience over]]), make self-study a discipline ([[Y038 — Make self-study a discipline]]).

An example of the practice at its smallest: each evening, five minutes with three questions — What did I do today? What did it serve? What will I do differently? — written, so that tomorrow's self can examine today's. This is the “as ongoing review” facet: the life is examined the way an experiment is, with data ([[SE013 — Cultivate accurate self-knowledge — know your traits, values, and patterns]]).

The question has a twin entry in this library — [[The unexamined life]] — and its kin across traditions include the Delphic self-knowledge at the root of [[Self-as-context]].

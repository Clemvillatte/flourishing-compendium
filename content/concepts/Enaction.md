"Enaction" — Varela, Thompson and Rosch, The Embodied Mind.

Enaction is the name Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson and Eleanor Rosch gave, in The Embodied Mind (1991), to their proposal for cognitive science: cognition is not the processing of representations of a pre-given world, but the bringing forth of a world by an organism acting in it. The word names the doing. A perceiver does not first receive and then interpret; perceiving and moving are one continuous activity, and the world the perceiver works with is shaped by that activity.

The book's most quoted passage defines the embodiment half of the claim:

> "By using the term embodied we mean to highlight two points: first that cognition depends upon the kinds of experience that come from having a body with various sensorimotor capacities, and second, that these individual sensorimotor capacities are themselves embedded in a more encompassing biological, psychological and cultural context." (Varela, Thompson & Rosch, [The Embodied Mind, pp. 172-173](https://iclc.toplap.org/2015/meta/72_Embodiment_of_code.pdf), quoted in Wilkie, "Embodiment of code")

The enactive program itself can be summarized as two claims: perception consists in perceptually guided action, and cognitive structures emerge from the recurrent sensorimotor patterns that enable action to be perceptually guided ([Varela, Thompson & Rosch, ch. 8](https://academic.oup.com/mit-press-scholarship-online/book/15075/chapter-abstract/169493399)). Neither claim leaves the organism passive, and neither treats the world as finished in advance: what is perceived depends on what the perceiver's embodiment affords doing.

Here the idea stands behind [[P005 — You are an organism in transaction with an environment]] and [[P142 — Own that you enact your world]], which state the claim as a practical stance.

The compendium uses "Monastic hours" for the fixed times of prayer that structure the monastic day. The Wikipedia article on the concept:

> "In the practice of Christianity, canonical hours mark the divisions of the day in terms of fixed times of prayer at regular intervals." ([Wikipedia, Canonical hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_hours))

### Structure for daily life

The article describes the role of the cycle: "As the form of fixed-hour prayer developed in the Christian monastic communities in the East and West, the Offices grew both more elaborate and more complex, but the basic cycle of prayer still provided the structure for daily life in monasteries" ([Wikipedia, Canonical hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_hours)). It also records the Benedictine contribution: "In 525, Benedict of Nursia set out one of the earliest schemes for the recitation of the Psalter at the Office" ([Wikipedia, Canonical hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_hours)).

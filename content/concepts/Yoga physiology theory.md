**Yoga physiology theory** — postures, breath and meditation act on autonomic and endocrine systems to reduce stress reactivity.

## Field

Biophysiological · Psychological science

### The physiological claim

The theory holds that yoga's practices shift autonomic balance toward parasympathetic dominance, measured most cleanly as heart rate variability (HRV). The review literature states the claim carefully: "The reviewed studies suggest that yoga can affect cardiac autonomic regulation with increased HRV and vagal dominance during yoga practices. Regular yoga practitioners were also found to have increased vagal tone at rest compared to non-yoga practitioners" ([Tyagi and Cohen, "Yoga and heart rate variability: a comprehensive review," Int. J. Yoga 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4959333/)).

The same review gives the honest limit of the evidence in its own words: "It is premature to draw any firm conclusions about yoga and HRV as most studies were of poor quality, with small sample sizes and insufficient reporting of study design and statistical methods" ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4959333/)). A more recent psychoneuroimmunology review frames the mechanism set: yoga's outcomes "may result from the synergistic effects of multiple psychoneuroimmunological mechanisms, including (a) increased interoception, (b) improved metabolism, (c) improved parasympathetic tone, (d) HPA axis downregulation, (e) reduced inflammation and (f) augmented neuroplasticity" ([PMC review, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11489066/)).

### What carries the effect

The breath is the best-supported lever. Breathing practices that extend the exhalation "have the most potent effect on parasympathetic tone," and slow breathing "activates the parasympathetic nervous system" leading to reduced heart rate and blood pressure ([PMC review, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11489066/); [pranayama RCT meta-analysis, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13316001/)). A meta-analysis of pranayama RCTs found a significant reduction in heart rate (SMD -0.43) with modest HRV improvements ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13316001/)). Postures and meditation contribute, but the breath is where the physiology is clearest.

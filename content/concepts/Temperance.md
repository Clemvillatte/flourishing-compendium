"Temperance" is one of the four cardinal virtues: the right ordering of pleasure and appetite. Plato's Republic defines it as "a kind of beautiful order and a continence of certain pleasures and appetites," and says it bears "more likeness to a kind of concord and harmony than the other virtues did" ([Republic IV.430e, Shorey translation](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0168%3Abook%3D4%3Asection%3D430e)). Aristotle locates it on the map: "temperance is a mean with regard to pleasures" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.10-12, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### The strange phrase

Plato flags the ordinary phrase for it as a riddle: "the phrase 'master of himself' is an absurdity... for he who is master of himself would also be subject to himself." The riddle is the teaching: self-rule cannot be one part of the self defeating another, for the defeated part would still be the self that is ruled. That is why the definition prefers order to combat, and why temperance, more than the other virtues, resembles concord.

### What it is about

Aristotle narrows the terrain: temperance "must be concerned with bodily pleasures, but not all even of these," specifically "touch and taste," the pleasures "that the other animals share in." The temperate person desires moderately "the things that, being pleasant, make for health or for good condition"; the self-indulgent person "craves for all pleasant things...at the cost of everything else," and is pained by the craving itself — "it seems absurd to be pained for the sake of pleasure" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.10-12](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### In the compendium

The body principles give the modern instances: BI040 on caffeine, BI015 on sleep hygiene, BE042 on nutrition for mood and BE044 on limiting substances. The compendium pairs the virtue with the Stoic dichotomy of control: appetite's arising is not up to one, the response of judgment is. Habituation is the vehicle — a trained disposition rather than a repeated decision.

Being and Time sets its task as "the concrete working out of the question of the 'meaning' (Sinn) of 'being'" (SZ 1). The question is the meaning of Being, and it is not about what things exist: beings, the things that are, are distinguished from Being itself, glossed as "that which determines entities as entities" (GA20: 195; SZ 1).

> "Why are there beings at all instead of nothing?"—this is obviously the first of all questions. (Heidegger, Introduction to Metaphysics, trans. Gregory Fried and Richard Polt, 2nd ed., [beyng.com text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/IntroductiontoMetaphysics2/IntroductiontoMetaphysics2.001.html))

The Introduction to Metaphysics gives the question its most famous form, calling it, in the Fried-Polt translation, "the first of all questions." Heidegger notes that it looms in "great despair," when all weight tends to dwindle away from things and the sense of things grows dark.

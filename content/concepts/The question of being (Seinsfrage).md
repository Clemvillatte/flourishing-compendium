Heidegger's driving question is the question of the meaning of Being: not what things exist, but what it means for anything to be. Being and Time announces its aim as "the concrete working out of the question of the 'meaning' (Sinn) of 'being'" (SZ 1). The question rests on a distinction Heidegger treats as basic: beings (the things that are) differ from Being itself, glossed as "that which determines entities as entities."

> "Why are there beings at all instead of nothing?"—this is obviously the first of all questions. (Heidegger, Introduction to Metaphysics, trans. Gregory Fried and Richard Polt, 2nd ed., [beyng.com text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/IntroductiontoMetaphysics2/IntroductiontoMetaphysics2.001.html))

The Introduction to Metaphysics gives the question its most famous form and calls it the fundamental question of metaphysics. It surfaces, Heidegger observes, in great despair, when the weight of things dwindles and their sense grows dark.

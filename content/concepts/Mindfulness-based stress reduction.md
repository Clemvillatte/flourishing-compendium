Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) is the eight-week course in secular mindfulness training that Jon Kabat-Zinn founded at the University of Massachusetts hospital in 1979. In his own account of the origins: "I started what was originally called the Stress Reduction and Relaxation Program in September 1979," and the program was set up "under the auspices of ambulatory care in the hospital, which would take the form of an eight-week course to which physicians would refer patients they were seeing who they felt were not responding to their treatments, and were in some sense, falling through the cracks of the healthcare system" ([Kabat-Zinn 2011](https://jonkabat-zinn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JonKabat-Zinn_SomeReflectionsOriginsMBSR.pdf)). The name changed in the early 1990s: "it increasingly felt that something more was needed to differentiate our approach from many programmes that also used the term stress reduction or stress management but that had no dharma foundation whatsoever" ([Kabat-Zinn 2011](https://jonkabat-zinn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JonKabat-Zinn_SomeReflectionsOriginsMBSR.pdf)).

The training itself was adapted from Buddhist practice for medical patients. Kabat-Zinn describes it as "relatively intensive training in Buddhist meditation without the Buddhism (as I liked to put it), and yoga," intended for "those who would never come to a place like IMS or a Zen Center" ([Kabat-Zinn 2011](https://jonkabat-zinn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JonKabat-Zinn_SomeReflectionsOriginsMBSR.pdf)). The working definition of the attention being trained appeared in several variants over the years, most compactly "paying attention in a particular way: on purpose, in the present moment, and non-judgmentally" (Kabat-Zinn 1994, quoted in [Kabat-Zinn 2011](https://jonkabat-zinn.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JonKabat-Zinn_SomeReflectionsOriginsMBSR.pdf)).

The first published results came from the program's chronic-pain patients, trained in a ten-week version of the course. The 1982 paper reports that "the practice of mindfulness meditation was used in a 10-week Stress Reduction and Relaxation Program to train chronic pain patients in self-regulation," that the meditation "facilitates an attentional stance towards proprioception known as detached observation," and that this "appears to cause an 'uncoupling' of the sensory dimension of the pain experience from the affective/evaluative alarm reaction and reduce the experience of suffering via cognitive reappraisal" ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)). Of 51 patients who had not improved with traditional medical care, "at 10 weeks, 65% of the patients showed a reduction of greater than or equal to 33% in the mean total Pain Rating Index (Melzack) and 50% showed a reduction of greater than or equal to 50%," with large reductions in mood disturbance that were relatively stable at follow-up ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)). The paper presents these as preliminary findings and notes that the limitations of the present uncontrolled study are discussed ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)). The ten-week format reported in 1982 differs from the eight-week course Kabat-Zinn describes in his retrospective account, which does not state when the duration changed.

### Definition

An organism-environment transaction is the whole event in which a living being and its surroundings form one system. There is no bird without air, no seeker without what is sought, no learner without the problem that teaches. Dewey spent a lifetime dissolving the wall between inner mind and outer world, and with Arthur Bentley he gave the idea its final form in Knowing and the Known (1949). Their famous triad marks the move:

> Self-action: where things are viewed as acting under their own powers. Inter-action: where thing is balanced against thing in causal interconnection. Trans-action: where systems of descriptions and naming are employed to deal with aspects and phases of action, without final attribution to "elements" or other presumptively detachable or independent "entities," "essences," or "realities," and without isolation of presumptively detachable "relations" from such detachable "elements." ([Dewey & Bentley, as reprinted in Bentley, Inquiry into Inquiries](https://archive.org/stream/inquiryintoinqui00bent/inquiryintoinqui00bent_djvu.txt))

In a transaction, the terms exist only together. A buyer is a buyer only inside a sale; a knower is a knower only inside an inquiry.

### Origin

Dewey first signalled the direction in "The Reflex Arc" (1896), where stimulus and response are not two events joined but one continuous circuit. Experience and Nature (1925) widened the claim: experience is the interaction of organism and environment. Knowing and the Known (1949) then fixed the vocabulary, presenting transaction as the most advanced level of inquiry, beyond self-action and interaction. The transactional approach, they wrote, claims "the right to see together, extensionally and durationally, much that is talked about conventionally as if it were composed of irreconcilable separates."

### A real-life example

A child learns to catch a ball. The ball, the grass, the small hands, the widening eyes: none of these makes sense alone. Her "environment" for that half hour is not the street in the abstract but the whole moving situation, and she changes with every catch. Her skill and her world grow together, in one loop.

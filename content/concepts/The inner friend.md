Seneca's Letters to Lucilius records the Stoic ideal of making peace with oneself. In Letter VI he passes on a line from Hecato that gives the concept its name.

> "What progress, you ask, have I made? I have begun to be a friend to myself." (Hecato, quoted in Seneca, Letters to Lucilius VI, Gummere translation, [Internet Archive text](https://archive.org/details/AdLuciliumEpistulaeMoralesVolI))

### What the letter adds

Seneca calls the line "indeed a great benefit", and gives two reasons: "such a person can never be alone" and "such a man is a friend to all mankind". The self-relation is treated as a skill with progress markers, not a fixed trait, and it is written into a letter about sharing what one learns with others.

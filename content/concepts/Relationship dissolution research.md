The label for Steve Duck's research line on how relationships end, begun in the early 1980s with the paper the wiki's catalogue lists as "Duck, 1982". Its core artifact is a phase model in which dissolution is a process with named concerns and thresholds, not a single event.

> "Grave-dressing Phase: Getting over it all and tidying up" (Duck 1984, p. 169, reprinted in [Bridges Not Walls](http://people.uncw.edu/mcdaniela/Break-up%20table.pdf))

Duck's summary table runs from a breakdown stage through four phases, each with a threshold that moves the process along: "I can't stand this anymore" (breakdown), "I'd be justified in withdrawing" (intrapsychic), "I mean it" (social), "It's now inevitable" (grave-dressing). Each phase has its own concerns and its own repair focus, which is what makes the model double as a repair manual: the intrapsychic phase's concern is the partner's "faults and inadequacies", and the dyadic phase brings confrontation and the possibility of reformulating the relationship.

The model's point for this wiki's purposes: the ending is communication-shaped. The intrapsychic phase is private brooding; the dyadic phase is confrontation; the social phase takes the distress public; grave-dressing is the post-mortem in which each partner builds a usable account of what happened. In the compendium, A075 uses the line for the plain fact that friendships change over time, with the model supplying the process view of how.

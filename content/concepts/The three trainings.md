The three trainings — virtue, concentration and wisdom — is the Buddhist account of the path in its most compressed form. Pāli: sīla, samādhi, paññā. Every other practice in the tradition is usually placed under one of the three, and the order is part of the teaching, not a detail of presentation.

> "There are these three trainings. Which three? The training in heightened virtue, the training in heightened mind, the training in heightened discernment."
> — [Sikkhā Sutta (AN 3.88), trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, Access to Insight](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.088.than)

Each of the three is defined in the sutta by what it actually consists in. Heightened virtue is conduct and restraint. Heightened mind is the sequence of absorptions, the four jhānas, entered in order. Heightened discernment is direct knowledge of the four noble truths: stress, its origin, its cessation, and the path.

### The order is the teaching

The sequence runs outward in, or rather conduct first, then steadiness, then seeing. It is not a claim that a person must perfect virtue before they can meditate. It is a claim about what holds what up. The tradition treats the relation as one of dependence: unsteady conduct keeps a person in situations that scatter attention, and a scattered attention does not see things as they are. In the stock passage repeated through the canon, concentration cultivated with virtue brings great fruit, and wisdom cultivated with concentration brings great fruit — each stage matures the next.

The mapping onto the noble eightfold path is given in the Cūḷa Vedalla Sutta, in the nun Dhammadinnā's answer to Visākha:

> "The three aggregates are not included under the noble eightfold path, friend Visakha, but the noble eightfold path is included under the three aggregates. Right speech, right action, & right livelihood come under the aggregate of virtue. Right effort, right mindfulness, & right concentration come under the aggregate of concentration. Right view & right resolve come under the aggregate of discernment."
> — [Cūḷa Vedalla Sutta (MN 44), trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, Access to Insight](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.044.than.html)

The eightfold path, in other words, is the three trainings unfolded.

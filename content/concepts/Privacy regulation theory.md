**Privacy regulation theory** — Altman — privacy is dynamically regulated by opening and closing boundaries; optimal levels differ across people and cultures.

Privacy regulation theory is Irwin Altman's 1975 account of privacy as an ongoing boundary process rather than a state of withdrawal. A communication-research review states the core:

> "Whereas Westin regarded privacy as resulting from withdrawal, Altman (1975) defined privacy as 'selective control of access to the self' (p. 18). He used privacy synonymously with interpersonal boundary control through which individuals or larger social units aim to achieve a temporarily desired level of interpersonal contact." (Teutsch, Masur & Trepte, [Privacy in Mediated and Nonmediated Interpersonal Communication, 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2056305118767134))

### Openness and closedness

The same article notes that Altman "emphasized the dialectical nature of the process that involves the regulation of input and output": the person opens and closes the self to others, and the desired level of contact moves with person, situation and culture.

**Lazarus and Folkman's Transactional Theory of Stress and Coping** — stress is a person-environment transaction; harm depends on appraisal, and coping can change it.

Richard Lazarus and Susan Folkman's 1984 book Stress, Appraisal, and Coping gave the model its name. The stressor is not the event alone: harm depends on what the person makes of it, and the person acts back on the situation. The model's working parts are the two appraisals, defined in the theory's own research abstract:

> "This study uses an intraindividual analysis of the interrelations among primary appraisal (what was at stake in the encounter), secondary appraisal (coping options), eight forms of problem- and emotion-focused coping, and encounter outcomes in a sample of community-residing adults." (Folkman, Lazarus, Dunkel-Schetter, DeLongis and Gruen, [Dynamics of a stressful encounter: cognitive appraisal, coping, and encounter outcomes, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 50 (1986), pp. 992-1003, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3712234/))

### What the transaction means

Primary appraisal asks what is at stake; secondary appraisal asks what can be done about it ([Folkman et al., 1986, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3712234/)). In the theory, coping is defined as constantly changing cognitive and behavioural efforts to manage demands appraised as taxing or exceeding the person's resources, divided into problem-focused and emotion-focused forms ([Lazarus and Folkman's definition as quoted in a later review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21703432/)). The same study found coping strongly related to appraisal: the forms used varied depending on what was at stake and the options for coping ([Folkman et al., 1986, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3712234/)). Later empirical work largely confirmed the model's structure in patients with psychosomatic conditions, while noting its inner mechanisms, including appraisal, are hard to capture with self-report data ([Hiebel et al., 2021, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33746820/)).

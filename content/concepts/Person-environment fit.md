### Definition

Person-environment fit holds that what a person feels and achieves depends on the match between who they are and where they are. Behaviour, on this view, is never a property of the person alone; it is what Kurt Lewin compressed into the formula that experience and behaviour are "a function of the person and of his environment" (Lewin 1951, p. 239). The modern statement of the construct, as Edwards and Shipp put it, is:

> Person-environment (P-E) fit has been conceptualized as "the congruence, match, similarity, or correspondence between the person and the environment" (Edwards and Shipp, 2007, p. 211) that, on the whole, is assumed to lead to positive outcomes with respect to job satisfaction, job performance, organizational commitment, and well-being. ([Brandstätter, Job & Schulze 2016, Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4981689/))

### Origin

The idea descends from Lewin's field theory and grew up inside vocational and organisational psychology, maturing in reviews by Kristof (1996), Edwards and Shipp (2007) and Kristof-Brown and Guay (2011). Kristof-Brown, Zimmerman and Johnson's 2005 meta-analysis consolidated the evidence that congruence predicts satisfaction, performance and retention.

### Core claims

Two complementary directions of fit carry the load. Demands-abilities fit runs from the world to the person: "the fit between the demands of the environment and the abilities of the person". Needs-supplies fit runs from the person to the world: "the fit between the needs of the person and the supplies available in the environment" (Kristof, 1996; Edwards and Shipp, 2007, as summarised in [Brandstätter et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4981689/)). Fit is therefore not flattery of the self but complementarity: either the setting feeds what the person needs, or the person supplies what the setting demands, and at best both.

### How it explains suffering and growth

Misfit, not defect, is a root of distress. A capable person in the wrong role burns out; a needing person in a starving environment withers. Fit theory reframes many "personal problems" as placement problems, which is hopeful, since environments are easier to change than characters. Growth then has two levers: choose ground that matches you, and shape your ground until it does. Both beat grinding against a misfit.

### A real-life example

A nurse who thrives on teamwork takes a night-shift documentation post for the pay. Within a year she is exhausted and blames herself. The demands-abilities fit is fine; the needs-supplies fit is broken, and no affirmation course will fix it. A transfer to a day-team role restores her within weeks.

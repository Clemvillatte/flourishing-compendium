Non-striving is the Daoist refusal of forcing, the attitude the Daodejing places in the Dao itself. Chapter 37 states it in Legge's translation:

> "The Tao in its regular course does nothing (for the sake of doing it), and so there is nothing which it does not do." (Daodejing 37, [Legge translation, Gutenberg #216](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The chapter then moves the rule from the Dao to rulers and to desire: "If princes and kings were able to maintain it, all things would of themselves be transformed by them", and "If this transformation became to me an object of desire, I would express the desire by the nameless simplicity". Its closing verse runs: "Simplicity without a name / Is free from all external aim. / With no desire, at rest and still, / All things go right as of their will."

### What the rule asks

The paradox is the point. The Dao "does nothing" for the sake of doing, yet nothing is left undone. Striving, in this picture, is not extra effort added to results; it is effort that substitutes itself for the way things transform themselves. The practice is the stillness of having no external aim, with the work still happening.

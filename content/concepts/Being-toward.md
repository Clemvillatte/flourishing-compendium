"Being-toward" is this compendium's label for Heidegger's being-toward-death (Sein-zum-Tode) — the linked principles use it that way (E043, B055, D090), and E023 links it under care (Sorge). Dasein is structurally directed at its possibilities; the SEP entry locates the death-relation in that structure: temporality is "Dasein's temporal structure, in virtue of which we are simultaneously ahead-of-ourselves, already-being-in (a world) and being-amidst (entities encountered in the world) (SZ 327)" (§2.5) ([Wrathall, "Martin Heidegger", SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)). Being-toward-death is the death-relation itself (§2.4.1):

> Death proper befalls not us qua persons, but us qua Dasein. In distinguishing death from demise, Heidegger defines death as "the possibility of the absolute impossibility of Dasein" (SZ 250, emphasis supplied; see also SZ 329; SZ 262; SZ 306).
> — [Wrathall, "Martin Heidegger", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)

The entry explains what anticipatory being-toward-death does: "The anticipation of death, Heidegger argues, 'individualizes Dasein down to itself' (SZ 263)" — anxiety discloses the finitude of one's possibilities, inauthentic existence flees it, and authentic existence "will anticipate or run forward toward death (SZ 266)" — not brooding over death, but "actively taking up and pursuing my finite existence as finite" ([SEP, §2.4.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)).

Mindful attention is attention deliberately trained on present experience. Jon Kabat-Zinn's operational definition, from the Stress Reduction Clinic's work published as Full Catastrophe Living (1990), is the field's touchstone: mindfulness is "the awareness that emerges through paying attention on purpose, in the present moment, and nonjudgmentally to the unfolding of experience moment by moment" ([Kabat-Zinn, Mindfulness-Based Interventions in Context, Clinical Psychology: Science and Practice](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1093/clipsy.bpg016); [Wherever You Go definition, quoted by Cambridge](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/74699/excerpt/9781107074699_excerpt.pdf)).

### Two traditions of the term

The word names two distinct research traditions, and the compendium uses both. The meditative tradition, Kabat-Zinn's, grounds attention in the body: self-regulation of attention toward present experience with an accepting attitude ([Cambridge introduction](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/74699/excerpt/9781107074699_excerpt.pdf)). The social-cognitive tradition, Ellen Langer's from Mindfulness (1989), defines mindfulness as "the simple process of noticing new things and drawing novel distinctions," the opposite of mindlessness, "relying on previously established categories" ([Langerian mindfulness in schools, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5018476/); [Frontiers validation of the Langer scale](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00468/full)). The two converge on the present: "actively drawing these distinctions keeps us situated in the present" ([Langer and Moldoveanu, quoted in mind-wandering research](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2014/2014-An-Antidote-for-Wandering-Minds.pdf)).

### The self-compassion facet

The seed's second facet has its own trial base. Ferrari and colleagues' 2019 meta-analysis of 27 randomized controlled trials found self-compassion interventions improved 11 psychosocial outcomes, with large effects for rumination (g = 1.37), moderate effects for self-compassion (g = 0.75), stress (g = 0.67), depression (g = 0.66) and mindfulness itself (g = 0.62), stronger for group than individual delivery, with the caveat that publication bias may be present ([Ferrari et al., Mindfulness 10 (2019), full text](https://self-compassion.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Ferrari2019.pdf)).

### In the compendium

The principles distribute the practice. R070 practises attentiveness and P097 treats each moment as fresh, Langer's facet as a principle; B038's mindfulness of breathing and B041's walking meditation are the classical exercises, BI060 the movement forms, R123 the ecological extension, and P122 turns maintenance itself into art, the Langerian move applied to housework.

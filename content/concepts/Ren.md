Ren (仁, humaneness) is Confucius's cardinal virtue: the humane disposition toward others. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy renders it "benevolence" or "humaneness" and notes Peter Boodberg's "co-humanity"; the character joins the graphs for person and two, though the entry cautions that the how-two-people-treat-each-other reading is a folk etymology ([IEP Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). The Analects gives ren its practice: "the man of perfect virtue, wishing to be established himself, seeks also to establish others; wishing to be enlarged himself, he seeks also to enlarge others" (Analects 6.30, [Legge translation](https://ctext.org/analects/yong-ye)).

### Love, ritual, reciprocity

Three passages set ren's edges. Asked to define it, Confucius says "it is to love all men" (12.22). Asked how, he answers "to subdue one's self and return to propriety" (li), "look not at what is contrary to propriety...make no movement which is contrary to propriety" (12.1, [Legge translation](https://ctext.org/analects/yan-yuan)). And asked for one word to live by, he chooses shu, reciprocity: "What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to others" (15.24, [Legge translation](https://ctext.org/analects/wei-ling-gong)). Ren is a disposition trained through ritual and exercised as consideration: love with a method.

### Rare and demanding

The bar is high. Confucius grants it to his best student alone, and only provisionally: "Such was Hui that for three months there would be nothing in his mind contrary to perfect virtue. The others may attain to this on some days or in some months, but nothing more" (6.7). The IEP adds D. C. Lau's distinction that ren is "an attribute of agents, while yi...is an attribute of actions," and that the junzi manifests ren "in his person" (4.5). Mencius later read human nature as congenitally disposed toward ren and cultivated through li, an interpretation the entry notes goes beyond what Confucius himself said.

### In the compendium

R051 "Practise ren (humanity/benevolence)" makes ren the supreme Confucian virtue, the root the others grow from, citing the Analects, Mencius 2A6 and the Doctrine of the Mean. S085's kindness and A018's philia friendliness are its Greek cousins; AF065's compassion and AT051's loving-kindness meditation give it a modern practice. The Ubuntu pairing marks the cross-tradition resonance: personhood constituted through relation.

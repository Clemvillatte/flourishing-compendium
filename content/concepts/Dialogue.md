Dialogue is the exchange in which each person turns to the other as a Thou rather than treating them as an It. Martin Buber's I and Thou, Hans-Georg Gadamer's Truth and Method and Gabriel Marcel's The Mystery of Being are the classic statements of the idea; Buber's book supplies the words below.

> "All real living is meeting. The relation to the Thou is direct." (Buber, I and Thou, trans. Ronald Gregor Smith, [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/details/IAndThou_572))

For Buber, the two primary words are I-Thou and I-It: the first "can only be spoken with the whole being", and the second "can never be spoken with the whole being". Dialogue is what happens between two people who speak the first word to each other; the I-It relation experiences and uses rather than meets. Gadamer's version of the idea makes understanding itself conversational, a coming-together of horizons, and Marcel's makes presence a matter of availability to another.

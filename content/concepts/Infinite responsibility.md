“Infinite responsibility” — Levinas, Totality and Infinity.

For Emmanuel Levinas, responsibility for another person is not one duty among others, balanced against duties to oneself. It is prior, unlimited, and one-directional: the other's claim arrives before any question of what the other owes back.

> "The intersubjective relation is a non-symmetrical relation. In this sense, I am responsible for the other without waiting for reciprocity, were I to die for it. Reciprocity is *his* affair … I am responsible for a total responsibility, which answers for all in the others, even for their responsibility. The I always has one responsibility *more* than all the others." — [Levinas, Ethics and Infinity (1985), quoted in Van Niekerk, Verbum et Ecclesia 44(1) (2023), full text](https://verbumetecclesia.org.za/index.php/ve/article/view/2924/6891)

### What the asymmetry is for

The infinite in the name is the point: because the claim does not wait for reciprocity, no response can discharge it as settled. Levinas's starker formulations speak of substitution — standing where the other stands, responsible even for the other's responsibility. The idea earns its place in this compendium as a corrective rather than a rule of life: against the bookkeeping instinct that keeps accounts and withdraws care when the ledger goes negative. Read alongside the practice principles in the seed links, it marks the difference between a responsibility one has and a responsibility one is.

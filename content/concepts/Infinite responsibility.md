“Infinite responsibility” — Levinas, Totality and Infinity.

For Emmanuel Levinas, responsibility for another person is not one duty among others, balanced against duties to oneself. It is prior, unlimited, and one-directional: the other's claim arrives before any question of what the other owes back.

> "The intersubjective relation is a non-symmetrical relation. In this sense, I am responsible for the other without waiting for reciprocity, were I to die for it. Reciprocity is his affair … I am responsible for a total responsibility, which answers for all in the others, even for their responsibility. The I always has one responsibility more than all the others." — Levinas, Ethics and Infinity (1985), pp. 98–99, as quoted in [Van Niekerk, Verbum et Ecclesia 44(1) (2023), full text](https://verbumetecclesia.org.za/index.php/ve/article/view/2924/6891)

### What the asymmetry is for

The infinite in the name is the point: because the claim does not wait for reciprocity, no response can discharge it as settled. Levinas's starker formulations, in the same interview, speak of substitution — responsibility that goes as far as answering for the other's responsibility. For Levinas the relation is not a contract: care that waits for reciprocity is not yet responsibility in his sense.

Ego dissolution, in the psychedelic research literature, is the reported diminishing or loss of the sense of being a self distinct from the rest of the world. The 2017 review of the phenomenon:

> "Users of psychedelic drugs often report that their sense of being a self or 'I' distinct from the rest of the world has diminished or altogether dissolved. Neuroscientific study of such 'ego dissolution' experiences offers a window onto the nature of self-awareness." (Letheby and Gerrans, [Self unbound: ego dissolution in psychedelic experience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6007152/), Neuroscience of Consciousness 2017(1), 2017)

### The paper's conclusion

The review's account of what the experiences show: "Ego dissolution experiences reveal that the self-model plays an important binding function in cognitive processing, but the self does not exist." On outcomes, the paper reports that "ego dissolution experiences often occur in the context of mystical states", and that "there is evidence that mystical states are important for the therapeutic effects of psychedelics (Garcia-Romeu et al. 2016)".

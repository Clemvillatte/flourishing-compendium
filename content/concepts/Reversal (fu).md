"Reversal (fu)" — Daoism, Daodejing 40.

The Daodejing states the pattern in its fortieth chapter as a property of the Dao itself: the Dao moves by contraries, and weakness marks the course of its mighty deeds. The concept collects every place the old text treats a condition carried to its extreme as the seed of its opposite, so that a full vessel spills, a kept sharpness goes blunt, and fortune and misery sit close enough to trade places. Chapter 58 applies the same rule to human affairs, where happiness and misery are set side by side and neither can be counted on to stay what it is; the delusion of expecting otherwise, the text says, has subsisted for a long time.

> "The movement of the Tao / By contraries proceeds; / And weakness marks the course / Of Tao's mighty deeds." (Daodejing 40, [Legge translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

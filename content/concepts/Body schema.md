The body schema, as introduced by the neurologists Henry Head and Gordon Holmes in 1911, is the unconscious postural model against which all changes of posture are measured before they enter consciousness ([Vallar 2025, J Neuropsychol, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)). Modern summaries keep the structure: an unconscious, on-line, real-time plastic representation of one's own body in space, derived mainly from proprioceptive inputs and interacting with motor systems for action, while the body image is its conscious counterpart ([Vallar 2025, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)).

> "For this combined standard, against which all subsequent changes of posture are measured before they enter consciousness, we propose the word 'schema'." — [Head & Holmes 1911, quoted in Vallar 2025, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)

> "By means of perpetual alterations in position we are always building up a postural model of ourselves which constantly changes. Every new posture or movement is recorded on this plastic schema, and the activity of the cortex brings every fresh group of sensations evoked by altered posture into relation with it. Immediate postural recognition follows as soon as the relation is complete." — [Head & Holmes 1911, quoted in Vallar 2025, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)

### Tools join the schema

Head and Holmes observed that the schema extends past the skin: anything that participates in the conscious movement of our bodies is added to the model of ourselves. The review's own example is a woman's power of localization extending to the feather in her hat ([Vallar 2025, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)).

> "Anything which participates in the conscious movement of our bodies is added to the model of ourselves and becomes part of these schemata: a woman's power of localization may extend to the feather in her hat." — [Head & Holmes 1911, quoted in Vallar 2025, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)

Later writers put the point in plainer cases: "the body-image of a motorist, an air-pilot, an equestrian temporarily includes part at least of the automobile, the aircraft, or the horse. A surgeon with his probe, a blind man with his white stick for the time being are endowed with an extension of corporeal awareness" (Critchley 1979, p. 94, quoted in [Vallar 2025, PMC11923729](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923729/)).

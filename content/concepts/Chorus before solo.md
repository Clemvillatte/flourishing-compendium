Chorus before solo is the structure Greek tragedy gave its art: the collective voice of the chorus is not a warm-up act for the individual actors but a constitutive part of the drama, and the individual emerges out of it rather than replacing it. [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]]'s judgment of the two styles:

> "The Chorus too should be regarded as one of the actors; it should be an integral part of the whole, and share in the action, in the manner not of Euripides but of Sophocles." (Aristotle, Poetics XVIII, tr. Butcher, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1974/1974-h/1974-h.htm))

### What the order carries

The chorus is the community's voice inside the play, singing and dancing as a body, and its presence keeps the individual character's suffering answerable to the whole. When tragedy later reduced the chorus to interlude, Aristotle's verdict registers the loss: a chorus that merely fills between the acts has become decoration. The cultural parallel the page carries is general: in most human traditions the communal form comes first and the soloist is its exception, a child learns the lullaby before the recital, the work song before the aria. My principle M015 draws the applied reading: let art be communal, not only private, since the chorus is the form in which beginners are actually carried into the practice. A compendium exercise drawn from the order is to join the group version of something one wants to learn, singing in the choir before the solo, and to notice how the collective holds the beginner's attention. What the order does not give: a rule against solo work; the point is the sequence, that the solo is grown out of the chorus rather than attempted instead of it.

Micro-moments of connection is Barbara Fredrickson's name for the brief, warm exchanges she redefines love around in Love 2.0 (2013). As a Greater Good summary puts her view: "In her book Love 2.0, Barbara Fredrickson describes love as a momentary emotion that swells up and expands your awareness of your surroundings, your sense of self, and your ability to truly see another person. We experience a \"positivity resonance\" comprising three events: \"shared positive emotions, biobehavioral synchrony, and mutual care\" with the other person, writes Fredrickson, a psychology professor at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill" ([Greater Good Science Center, UC Berkeley](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/what_is_love_scientists_have_answersbut_they_dont_all_agree)).

### The small scale is the point

The moments are momentary by definition, and they are not reserved for intimates: the same article reports that "studies on people directed to have meaningful interactions with the barista while buying coffee, greet and thank strangers, and chat with strangers found that these interactions increased their happiness and positivity" ([same article](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/article/item/what_is_love_scientists_have_answersbut_they_dont_all_agree)). The compendium's seed line, that these brief present-to-present interactions build relational health, is the claim's everyday form; Fredrickson's own research program tests the accumulation through studies of positive emotions and vagal tone ([Fredrickson's Positive Emotions and Psychophysiology Lab](https://peplab.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/18901/2019/06/FredricksonJoiner2019PPSupwardspirals.pdf)).

Reduction as return is Edmund Husserl's phenomenological reduction, read with its Latin root in view: re-ducere, to lead back. The epoché suspends the natural attitude's assumption that the world is simply there, and the reduction is the movement this suspension opens, a leading back to the field of experience in which the world appears at all. A summary in the phenomenological literature puts it in one line: the epoché "suspends the general thesis and brackets the external world, thereby achieving a 'reduction' — a leading back — to pure consciousness."

> "We put out of action the general thesis which belongs to the essence of the natural standpoint, we place in brackets whatever it includes respecting the nature of Being: this entire natural world therefore which is continually 'there for us', 'present to our hand', and will remain there, is a 'fact-world' of which we continue to be conscious, even though it pleases us to put it in brackets." (Husserl, Ideas I, §32; quoted in [Giraud, Nishida's Concept of Pure Experience from the Standpoint of the Husserlian Reduction](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnpa/9/0/9_187/_pdf/-char/ja))

### Why the return matters

The word reduction misleads if heard as diminution. Husserl's operation takes nothing away from the world; it declines to use the world's existence as a premise while inquiry looks at how the world is given. The suspended world, as the passage says, remains "a 'fact-world' of which we continue to be conscious." What the epoché bars is the use of "any judgement that concerns spatio-temporal existence" (§32). The return, once made, is also repeatable rather than final: the bracket goes on whenever the natural attitude reasserts itself, which for a finite perceiver is always.

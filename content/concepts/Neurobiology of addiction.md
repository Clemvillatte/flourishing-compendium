Neurobiology of addiction is the subject of George Koob and Nora Volkow's 2016 review, "Neurobiology of addiction: a neurocircuitry analysis" (Lancet Psychiatry, 3(8), 760-773). Its abstract states the three-stage framework the review is known for: "Drug addiction represents a dramatic dysregulation of motivational circuits that is caused by a combination of exaggerated incentive salience and habit formation, reward deficits and stress surfeits, and compromised executive function in three stages" ([PMID 27475769](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475769/)).

### The three stages

The abstract maps the stages to their circuitry. In the binge/intoxication stage, "the rewarding effects of drugs of abuse, development of incentive salience, and development of drug-seeking habits... involve changes in dopamine and opioid peptides in the basal ganglia". In the withdrawal/negative affect stage, "the increases in negative emotional states and dysphoric and stress-like responses... involve decreases in the function of the dopamine component of the reward system and recruitment of brain stress neurotransmitters, such as corticotropin-releasing factor" ([both from the abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475769/)).

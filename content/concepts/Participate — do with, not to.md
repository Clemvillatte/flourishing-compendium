"Participate: do with, not to" is Dewey's rule for how one human being should enter another's activity. His example is the horse: "the horse does not really share in the social use to which his action is put. Some one else uses the horse to secure a result which is advantageous... He is not a partner in a shared activity" ([Democracy and Education, 1916](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)). Doing to is using; doing with is partnering.

### The completing step

"In many cases — too many cases — the activity of the immature human being is simply played upon to secure habits which are useful. He is trained like an animal rather than educated like a human being" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)). The alternative is that "he really shares or participates in the common activity... the same ideas and emotions are aroused in him that animate the others." Dewey's formula: "Making the individual a sharer or partner in the associated activity so that he feels its success as his success, its failure as his failure, is the completing step" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)).

### Communication, not transmission

The same structure governs speech. "Communication is a process of sharing experience till it becomes a common possession. It modifies the disposition of both the parties who partake in it" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)). "To be a recipient of a communication is to have an enlarged and changed experience. One shares in what another has thought and felt" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)). A message delivered is done to; a communication is done with.

### Democracy's mode

The stakes are political: "A democracy is more than a form of government; it is primarily a mode of associated living, of conjoint communicated experience" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)). In shared activity, "the young have to refer their way of acting to what others are doing and make it fit in," and "this common understanding of the means and ends of action is the essence of social control. It is indirect, or emotional and intellectual, not direct or personal" ([Democracy and Education](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/852/852-h/852-h.htm)).

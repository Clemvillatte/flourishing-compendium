### Definition

The physiological sigh is the breathing pattern the body uses on its own when it needs to reset: a full inhale, a short second inhale to pop the lungs' remaining air sacs open, then a long, slow exhale. The Stanford trial studied it as a daily practice called cyclic sighing, "which emphasizes prolonged exhalations" ([Balban et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/)). Five minutes a day was the whole dose.

### Origin

The 2023 Cell Reports Medicine study, run remotely by Yilmaz Balban, Spiegel, Huberman and colleagues, compared three daily five-minute breathwork exercises against mindfulness meditation for a month. Its summary states the design and headline result:

> Here, we report a remote, randomized, controlled study (NCT05304000) of three different daily 5-min breathwork exercises compared with an equivalent period of mindfulness meditation over 1 month. ([Balban et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/))

> Using a mixed-effects model, we show that breathwork, especially the exhale-focused cyclic sighing, produces greater improvement in mood (p < 0.05) and reduction in respiratory rate (p < 0.05) compared with mindfulness meditation. ([Balban et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/))

Note the careful scope: the trial tested a month of daily practice, not an instant cure. "Daily 5-min cyclic sighing has promise as an effective stress management exercise" is the authors' closing line, and "promise" is the right word.

### A real-life example

Before a difficult conversation, thirty seconds of the pattern: big nasal inhale, quick top-up inhale, and a mouth exhale that lasts twice as long as the whole intake. The shoulders drop a little. The words come out slower. Nothing about the situation has changed; the breathing has.

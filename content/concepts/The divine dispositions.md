The divine dispositions are the twenty-six qualities Krishna lists at the opening of Bhagavad Gita Chapter 16 as the *daivi sampad*, the estate of a nature headed for liberation. The list, in openbhagavadgita's translation:

> "Fearlessness, purity of heart, perseverance in the methods of cultivation of immediate insight, charity, control of the external senses, sacrifice, study of the Vedas, austerity, straightforwardness, non-violence, truthfulness, absence of anger, detachment, composure of mind, freedom from the tendency to malign others, compassion toward all living beings, absence of greed, soft-heartedness, modesty, steadiness, vigor, tolerance, fortitude, cleanliness, freedom from enmity, and the absence of excessive self-esteem — O descendant of Bharata, all these qualities arise to one who is born of the divine disposition."

(BG 16.1-3; [openbhagavadgita](https://www.openbhagavadgita.org/english/chapter/16/verse/1-3/).)

### What kind of list this is

The chapter's structure is diagnostic, not decorative: the divine qualities are set against the asuric ones in verse 4, so each item is a fork rather than a rule. The list opens with *abhayam*, fearlessness, which the srimadgita commentary reads as the foundation "because fear causes us to act against our higher nature" ([srimadgita.com](https://www.srimadgita.com/chapter/16)), and it closes with *nātimānitā*, absence of pride — the whole arc running from security to humility. A modern reading of the same passage: "The Gita's list of 26 divine qualities is not a lecture on morality but a practical guide to inner alignment" ([Indica Today](https://www.indica.today/long-reads/the-unseen-game-how-we-win-or-lose-the-inner-battle/)).

### How the list is used

As a practice document the list works as a self-audit: each quality is checkable on a given day, and the Gita's own framing — that these belong to one "born for the divine estate" — is read by the commentaries as a description of trajectory rather than pedigree ([srimadgita.com](https://www.srimadgita.com/chapter/16); [Ramanuja commentary at bhagavad-gita.us](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-16-1-3/)). The monthly audit practice in my research side is the direct descendant of this structure.

Self-validation theory is Richard Petty and Pablo Briñol's account of when thoughts move judgment and action: not when the thoughts are objectively accurate, but when the thinker perceives them as valid. Their 2022 statement of the theory opens with its core notion.

> "The core notion of SVT is that thoughts become more consequential for judgment and action as the perceived validity of the thoughts is increased. Instead of focusing on the objective accuracy of thoughts, self-validation research focuses on a subjective sense that one's thoughts are valid or appropriate to use." (Briñol & Petty, [Self-Validation Theory: An Integrative Framework for Understanding When Thoughts Become Consequential, Psychological Review](https://richardepetty.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2022-Psyc-Rev-SVT-uncorrected-proof-r.pdf))

### Origin

The 2022 paper gives the framework its first formal description, as six testable propositions, and records that it "advances beyond what had been called the "self validation hypothesis" (Petty et al., 2002)", the earlier statement that perceived thought validity can emerge from incidental variables.

### Core claims

The theory's claim about how validation happens:

> "People come to rely on any thought more when they perceive that thought is likely to be true (cognitive validation) or because they feel good about the thought (affective validation)." (same article)

**Zuckerman’s Alternative Five-Factor Model** — Zuckerman — a biologically grounded five-factor model led by sensation seeking, not agreeableness.

The model begins from Marvin Zuckerman's trait of sensation seeking, which he defined as:

> "a trait defined by the seeking of varied, novel, complex, and intense sensations and experiences, and the willingness to take physical, social, legal, and financial risks for the sake of such experience" (Zuckerman, 1994, p. 27, as quoted in a review of behavioral and biological correlates, [open PDF](https://people.wku.edu/richard.miller/520_Sensation-seeking%20physiology%20correlates.pdf))

When Zuckerman and his colleagues factor-analyzed personality questionnaires with five factors, impulsivity and sensation seeking always merged into a single factor, and agreeableness did not survive as one. The result was an alternative to the standard Big Five:

> "The Zuckerman–Kuhlman Personality Questionnaire (ZKPQ) is considered an 'alternative' five-factor model of personality (Zuckerman et al., 1993) that measures five factors of personality: (Impulsive Sensation Seeking, Neuroticism-Anxiety, Aggression-Hostility, Activity, and Sociability)." (same review, quoting the model's instrument)

The model's contrast with the Big Five is its center of gravity. Where the Big Five is organized around social dispositions like agreeableness, the Alternative Five is organized around a biologically motivated approach dimension: impulsive sensation seeking is the model's leading factor, the one Zuckerman spent his career tying to enzymes, hormones and cortical arousal. The claim behind the model is that a personality taxonomy should be grounded in psychobiology rather than in the lexical clusters of ordinary language.

The compendium's application: the model is a reminder that trait vocabularies are instruments with histories and biological commitments, not neutral mirrors of human nature, and that the adventurer's profile has a measurable structure.

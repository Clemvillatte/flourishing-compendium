Approach versus avoidance motivation is the valence split in the science of motivation, given its modern statement in Andrew Elliot's hierarchical model. The older achievement-motivation dichotomy, hope of success versus fear of failure, becomes two independent goal systems, each with its own dynamics. Elliot's definition of the two systems:

> "Approach motivation is the energization of behavior by, or the direction of behavior toward, positive stimuli (objects, events, possibilities), whereas avoidance motivation is the energization of behavior by, or the direction of behavior away from, negative stimuli (objects, events, possibilities)." (Elliot 2006, [abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9028-7))

### Two systems, different fuel

Approach goals organize behavior around positive possibilities; avoidance goals organize it around negative ones. In the affect research the same split appears. A review of the evidence concludes that "anger relates to an appetitive or approach motivational system, whereas anxiety relates to an aversive or avoidance motivational system" (Carver & Harmon-Jones 2009, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19254075/)). Avoidance of real danger is not weakness; the research question is about chronic posture. Higgins' regulatory focus is the cousin distinction, promotion versus prevention ([[Regulatory focus theory]]).

### In everyday life

One goal reframed: "avoid failing the exam" becomes "master these three topics". (Illustrative reframe, not a reported intervention.)

### Its contemplative cousin

Its contemplative cousin is [[Right effort]], the Buddhist training that is explicitly double-barreled, preventing the unskillful and developing the skillful, and the Stoic movement toward virtue rather than merely away from vice.

## Other formulations in this library

- all motivation can be organised around approaching desired end-states or avoiding undesired ones (Elliot)
- achievement motivation splits into approach of success and avoidance of failure, with different antecedents and outcomes (Elliot)
- approach goals are associated with better outcomes than avoidance goals (Elliot & Church, 1997; Elliot, 2006)
- the different trajectories of promotion and prevention goals (Higgins, 1997, the regulatory-focus framework)

Approach versus avoidance motivation is the valence split in the science of motivation, given its modern statement in Andrew Elliott's hierarchical model: approach motivation is the energization of behavior toward a positive or desirable end-state (possibilities, success); avoidance motivation is the energization of behavior away from a negative or undesirable end-state (threats, failure) ([Elliot, "The Hierarchical Model of Approach-Avoidance Motivation" (2006)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-006-9028-7)). The older achievement-motivation dichotomy — hope of success versus fear of failure — becomes two independent goal systems, each with its own dynamics.

The mechanism: the two systems run on different fuel. Approach goals organize attention around opportunity and mastery; avoidance goals organize it around threat and self-protection — and the performance and well-being findings favor approach goals: they predict better achievement and positive affect, while avoidance goals predict anxiety, self-handicapping, and worse performance ([[MO019 — Learn to set approach goals, not avoidance goals]]; the anger research is the same split — approach-motivated mobilization ([[AF050 — Understand anger's function — it signals a violated boundary or blocked goal]]).

The distinction to hold: avoidance is not weakness — avoiding real danger is sane. The claim is about chronic posture: a motivational life organized around threat narrows attention and makes every outcome self-relevant. Higgins' regulatory focus is the cousin distinction — promotion versus prevention ([[Regulatory focus theory]]).

An example at its smallest: one goal reframed — "avoid failing the exam" becomes "master these three topics".

Its contemplative cousin is [[Right effort]], the Buddhist training that is explicitly double-barreled — prevent the unskillful, develop the skillful — and the Stoic movement toward virtue rather than merely away from vice.

the different trajectories of promotion and prevention goals (Higgins, 1997, the regulatory-focus framework).

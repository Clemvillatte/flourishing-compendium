Interoceptive inference is Anil Seth's predictive-coding account of emotion and the embodied self. The idea extends the brain-as-prediction-machine view from the outside senses to the inside of the body: feelings are not read off raw bodily signals but inferred from generative models of what those signals most likely mean.

> "Here, I describe a predictive, inferential perspective on interoception: 'interoceptive inference' conceives of subjective feeling states (emotions) as arising from actively-inferred generative (predictive) models of the causes of interoceptive afferents." — [Seth, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 17(11) (2013), 565–573, full text](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Interoceptive%20inference%20emotion%20and%20the%20embodied%20self..pdf)

### From feelings to selfhood

The model generalizes appraisal theories that treat emotions as cognitive evaluations of physiological change, and it carries the account up to the sense of being a body. As Seth summarizes: "emotion and embodied selfhood are grounded in active inference of those signals most likely to be 'me' across interoceptive and exteroceptive domains." The compendium's principles use this twice over: feelings become information about the organism's transactions rather than noise, and the lived body becomes something the brain is continuously modeling rather than simply inhabiting.

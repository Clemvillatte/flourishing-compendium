Feng shui is a traditional Chinese form of geomancy concerned with choosing and orienting buildings and burial sites according to beliefs about qi. Its name literally means wind-water. Yuan Ding describes kanyu as the term used in classical texts.

> "a superstition in ancient China, according to which the surrounding situation of residences and graves can decide the fortune of the residents and the offspring of the dead" (Ci-hai lexicon definition, as reproduced in Yuan Ding, [Kanyu (Feng-shui): A Case of Re-interpretation of the Spirit of Space](https://publ.icomos.org/publicomos/jlbSai?html=Bur&base=technica&ref=41507&file=146.pdf&path=77-y1bV-92.pdf), PDF p. 1)

### Siting and orientation

Historically, and in much of the contemporary Chinese world, feng shui has been used to determine the orientation of buildings, dwellings and tombs. The Form branch is the oldest; it was originally concerned with the location and orientation of tombs and later took up homes and other buildings. The Compass branch uses the luopan, a disc marked with formulas in concentric rings around a magnetic compass.

Yuan Ding describes the aim of kanyu as finding the spot where qi is held to concentrate. He reads the traditional rule of sitting north and facing south as a device for obtaining the best advantage of sun and wind.

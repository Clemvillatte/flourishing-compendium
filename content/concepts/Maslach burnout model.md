Christina Maslach's model of burnout as a three-part syndrome arising from chronic interpersonal and emotional stress at work.

> "Maslach defined burnout in the 1970s as "a syndrome of emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and reduced personal accomplishment that can occur among individuals who do 'people work' of some kind" ([1], p. 3)." (Loera, Converso & Viotti, [Evaluating the Psychometric Properties of the Maslach Burnout Inventory-Human Services Survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4264862/), PLoS ONE, 2014, Introduction, quoting Maslach 1982, p. 3)

The model treats burnout as a psychological response to chronic work-related stress of an interpersonal and emotional nature that appears in professionals working directly with clients, patients, or other recipients ([Loera et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4264862/)). The three dimensions divide the syndrome: emotional exhaustion, depersonalization, and a reduced sense of personal accomplishment, measured by the Maslach Burnout Inventory ([Loera et al., 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4264862/)).

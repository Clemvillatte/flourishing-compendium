Self-cultivation is the deliberate work of forming one's own character, given its classical shape in the Confucian text The Great Learning. The text makes cultivation the hinge between inner work and outer life: a person made right is the precondition for a family regulated, a state governed, a kingdom at peace.

> "From the Son of Heaven down to the mass of the people, all must consider the cultivation of the person the root of everything besides" (The Great Learning, Legge trans., [sacred-texts](https://sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf2.htm)).

### The chain

The text sets cultivation inside a sequence, and runs the sequence in both directions. Backwards, toward its conditions:

> "Wishing to cultivate their persons, they first rectified their hearts. Wishing to rectify their hearts, they first sought to be sincere in their thoughts. Wishing to be sincere in their thoughts, they first extended to the utmost their knowledge. Such extension of knowledge lay in the investigation of things" (The Great Learning, Legge trans.).

Forward, toward its effects: "Their hearts being rectified, their persons were cultivated. Their persons being cultivated, their families were regulated. Their families being regulated, their states were rightly governed. Their states being rightly governed, the whole kingdom was made tranquil and happy" (The Great Learning, Legge trans.). Cultivation is not private polish; on this account the state of the person is the root the rest of social life grows from, and a neglected root cannot yield a well-ordered growth.

The Confucian term of art for this practice is covered at [[Moral cultivation (xiushen)]].

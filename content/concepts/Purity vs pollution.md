“Purity vs pollution” — Douglas, Purity and Danger.

Mary Douglas's classic move is to treat purity rules not as primitive hygiene but as classification at work. What counts as dirt varies by the scheme doing the classifying.

> "As we know it, dirt is essentially disorder. There is no such thing as absolute dirt: it exists in the eye of the beholder. If we shun dirt, it is not because of craven fear, still less dread of holy terror. Nor do our ideas about disease account for the range of our behaviour in cleaning or avoiding dirt. Dirt offends against order. Eliminating it is not a negative movement, but a positive effort to organize the environment." (Douglas, Purity and Danger, p. 2, [quoted in Andrews 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/00380385211024448))

Douglas's own term for dirt elsewhere in the book is "matter out of place": the same item can be clean inside one ordering and polluting inside another.

“The tool-being of equipment” — Heidegger, Being and Time, the analysis of Zuhandenheit.

The tool-being of equipment is Heidegger's account, in Being and Time (1927), of what tools are when we work with them. Equipment is not first a thing with properties that we later put to use; it is from the start a for-something, and it shows itself in use rather than under inspection. Macquarrie and Robinson's translation puts the relational point at the start of section 15:

> "Taken strictly, there 'is' no such thing as *an* equipment. To the Being of any equipment there always belongs a totality of equipment, in which it can be this equipment that it is. Equipment is essentially 'something in-order-to ...' [\"etwas um-zu ...\"]." (Heidegger, Being and Time §15, Macquarrie & Robinson trans., [p. 97](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.097.html))

### What equipment is

The p. 97 text supplies its own examples: "Equipment—in accordance with its equipmentality—always is *in terms of* [*aus*] its belonging to other equipment: ink-stand, pen, ink, paper, blotting pad, table, lamp, furniture, windows, doors, room." An item of equipment is what it is in terms of that belonging. Heidegger adds that this is what makes equipment available in the first place:

> "Only because equipment has *this* 'Being-in-itself' and does not merely occur, is it manipulable in the broadest sense and at our disposal." (Being and Time §15, Macquarrie & Robinson trans., [p. 98](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.098.html))

The companion idea is readiness-to-hand (Zuhandenheit), the mode of being equipment has while it is in use, discussed on its own page.

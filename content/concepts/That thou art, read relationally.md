"That thou art, read relationally" names the construal of Chandogya 6.8.7 in which the sentence tat tvam asi is not an identity claim that dissolves the listener into the absolute, but an address: you and that stand in a real relation. The seed's line credited "Shankara's tradition"; that needs correcting, since Shankara's Advaita is the identity reading. The relational reading belongs to Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita, which read the sentence as coordinate predication (samanadhikaranya): "the same one God is named twice, once as the world-cause and once as the inner Self of the soul, so the soul is real and distinct yet wholly dependent." ([Sanatana Rahasya, "Tat tvam asi: three readings"](https://www.sanatanarahasya.com/philosophy/darshanas/tat-tvam-asi-three-readings/))

### What the reading holds

The schools divide on one sentence: "identity (Shankara), qualified oneness (Ramanuja), and difference (Madhva)." On Ramanuja's construal the payoff is pastoral rather than dissolving: "The soul is not an illusion to be seen through. It is a real self in a real and permanent relationship of dependence and love with a personal God. Liberation is the soul coming to live consciously in that relationship." Read this way, the mahavakya does not erase the person it addresses; it tells the person what their addressor is. The sentence is heard as spoken, and its force depends on keeping both parties real.

The compendium's use is a reading practice: a great statement can be taken as a doctrine to be applied or as a relation to be entered, and the choice changes what practice follows — whether the path is mainly knowledge or mainly love.

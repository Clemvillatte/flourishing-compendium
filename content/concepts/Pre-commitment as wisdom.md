"Pre-commitment as wisdom" — Stoics, Seneca.

Seneca's counsel for dealing with emotion is timing. An emotion is weak at the threshold and strong once it has entered, so the wise act happens early: refuse the encounter rather than win the fight. The emblem he reaches for is Ulysses and the mast.

> "These are voices you should avoid just as Ulysses did; he wouldn't sail past them until he was tied to the mast. They're no less powerful." (Seneca, Letters 123, [On the Conflict Between Pleasure and Virtue](https://www.lettersfromastoic.net/letter-123-on-the-conflict-between-pleasure-and-virtue/))

> "Every emotion at the start is weak. Afterwards, it rouses itself and gains strength by progress; it is more easy to forestall it than to forgo it." (Seneca, Letters 116, [Gummere translation, Monadnock](https://monadnock.net/seneca/116.html))
>
> "Let us therefore resist these faults when they are demanding entrance, because, as I have said, it is easier to deny them admittance than to make them depart. Therefore, knowing our weakness, let us remain quiet. Let us not expose this unstable spirit to the temptations of drink, or beauty, or flattery, or anything that coaxes and allures." (Seneca, Letters 116, [Gummere translation, Monadnock](https://monadnock.net/seneca/116.html))

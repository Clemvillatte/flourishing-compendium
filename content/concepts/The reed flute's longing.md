The reed flute's longing is the image Rumi opens the Mathnawi with: a reed cut from its bed, made into a flute, whose music is complaint. The whole poem, twenty-five thousand couplets, begins as an instrument speaking about being severed:

> "Listen to the reed (flute), how it is complaining! It is telling about separations (Saying), 'Ever since I was severed from the reed field, men and women have lamented in (the presence of) my shrill cries. (But) I want a heart (which is) torn, torn from separation, so that I may explain the pain of yearning.'" (Rumi, Mathnawi I:1–3, trans. Ibrahim Gamard after R. A. Nicholson, [Dar-al-Masnavi](https://www.dar-al-masnavi.org/n-I-0001.html))

### What the image carries

The reed's condition is double: it is wounded, and it is musical only because of the wound. The opening lines run on to the reed's secret being inseparable from its lament, the body not veiled from the soul though none is permitted to see the soul, and the sound being fire rather than breath. Read as a phenomenology of longing, the passage says three things at once: that longing is the signature of having been severed from a source; that it is communicable, men and women lament at the reed's cry; and that it wants an answering wound, a heart torn the same way, to be understood at all. The compendium principle SP086 trusts the love that cannot become hate as the mark of the real thing; the reed's longing is the same trust in image form, a love whose whole evidence is its persistence in absence. A compendium exercise drawn from the opening is to listen for the complaint inside a piece of music or a person's talk, and to hear it as reed-song rather than as noise to fix. What the image does not give: an argument that all longing is for a divine source; that is the Sufi reading the poem builds toward, and a reader may hold the phenomenology without the metaphysics.

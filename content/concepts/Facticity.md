"Facticity" — Sartre, Being and Nothingness.

- de Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity

Facticity is Jean-Paul Sartre's name in Being and Nothingness (1943) for the given, unchosen side of a human life: the body, the place, the past, the class and era one did not pick. His consciousness, the for-itself, is free; but it is free only as a nihilation of something, and that something is facticity. The book's definition:

> "This perpetually evanescent contingency of the in-itself which, without ever allowing itself to be apprehended, haunts the for-itself and reattaches it to being-in-itself — this contingency is what we shall call the facticity of the for-itself." (Sartre, [Being and Nothingness, Barnes translation](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.69160/2015.69160.Jean-paul-Sartre-Being-And-Nothingness_djvu.txt))

The definition is deliberately haunted rather than solid: facticity is never an object one can inspect, only the contingency that keeps a freedom from being nowhere. The passage leading into it reads: "Thus the for-itself is sustained by a perpetual contingency for which it assumes the responsibility and which it assimilates without ever being able to suppress it" ([same text](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.69160/2015.69160.Jean-paul-Sartre-Being-And-Nothingness_djvu.txt)). One can take up one's facticity in projects, and one can neither choose it away nor be it.

Here the concept pairs with [[E022 — Practise thrownness (Geworfenheit)]] and with [[Situated freedom]], where the same structure is worked out as a practice rather than an ontology.

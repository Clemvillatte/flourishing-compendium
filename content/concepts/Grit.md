Grit is a personality trait introduced to psychology by Angela Duckworth and colleagues in 2007. The article defines it by what it sustains: effort and interest kept up over years, through failure, adversity, and plateaus in progress.

> "We define grit as perseverance and passion for long-term goals." (Duckworth, Peterson, Matthews and Kelly, [Grit: Perseverance and Passion for Long-Term Goals](https://www.timeandlearning.org/files/-duckwort/images/grit%20jpsp.pdf), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 92(6), 2007, p. 1087)

### What the studies found

Across six studies, the 2007 article reports that grit accounted for an average of 4% of the variance in success outcomes, including educational attainment in two adult samples, grade point average among Ivy League undergraduates, retention among West Point cadets, and ranking in the National Spelling Bee. The effect is real but modest, and the measure was designed to predict achievement in difficult domains beyond talent measures such as IQ.

The trait has two proposed facets: consistency of interest and perseverance of effort. The first holds a goal steady over years; the second keeps working at it through setbacks.

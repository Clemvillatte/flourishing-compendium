Hostility research is the body of studies that asks whether chronic hostility and anger predict coronary heart disease. One quantitative synthesis is Chida and Steptoe's 2009 meta-analysis of prospective evidence:

> "Anger and hostility were associated with increased CHD events in the healthy population studies (combined hazard ratio [HR]: 1.19; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.05 to 1.35, p = 0.008) and with poor prognosis in the CHD population studies (HR: 1.24; 95% CI: 1.08 to 1.42, p = 0.002)." (Chida and Steptoe, [The Association of Anger and Hostility With Future Coronary Heart Disease](https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2008.11.044), Journal of the American College of Cardiology 53(11), 2009, abstract)

The authors' own summary of the field: "The current review suggests that anger and hostility are associated with CHD outcomes both in healthy and CHD populations."

### What the evidence does and does not show

The association survived the review's main analyses, but the picture has a caveat the researchers themselves flagged. In a subgroup analysis of studies that controlled for behavioral covariates such as smoking, physical activity, body mass index, socioeconomic status and disease treatment, the association of anger and hostility with CHD was no longer statistically significant, "suggesting that the major pathway between anger and hostility and CHD might be behavioral risk factors" ([American College of Cardiology press release](https://www.acc.org/about-acc/press-releases/2010/02/22/13/35/9-2)). The pathway remains an open question, and the press release presents it as a possibility, not a finding.

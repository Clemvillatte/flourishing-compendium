Place attachment is the emotional bond between people and the places that matter to them. Scannell and Gifford open their 2010 review with it: "Place attachment, the bonding that occurs between individuals and their meaningful environments, has gained much scientific attention in recent years (e.g., Giuliani, 2003; Low & Altman, 1992)" ([Scannell & Gifford, 2010, Journal of Environmental Psychology 30, 1-10, text copy](https://docslib.org/doc/2163388/defining-place-attachment-a-tripartite-organizing-framework)). Low and Altman (1992) is thus one of the sources they cite for the idea.

> "Place attachment has been researched quite broadly, and so has been defined in a variety of ways. The various definitions of the concept are reviewed and synthesized into a three-dimensional, person-process-place organizing framework." (Scannell & Gifford, 2010, abstract, [text copy](https://www.hse.ru/data/2019/03/04/1196347392/Gifford_2.pdf))

### The three dimensions

In the framework, "the person dimension of place attachment refers to its individually or collectively determined meanings," the psychological dimension "includes the affective, cognitive, and behavioral components of attachment," and the place dimension "emphasizes the place characteristics of attachment, including spatial level, specificity, and the prominence of social or physical elements."

### What attachment does

The paper reviews proposed functions. "Speculated upon in the literature, the most common include survival and security, goal support, and temporal or personal continuity." Under goal support and self-regulation, the authors say that "place attachment serves self-regulation because favorite places have restorative qualities" (citing Korpela, 1989). They add that the list "is by no means exhaustive" and name belongingness as a further function. The text opened here does not state outright that attachment raises well-being, so the compendium's facet "and well-being" is a reasonable reading of the restorative and security functions but not a result this review reports.

### In the compendium

The linked principles apply the concept to practice, for example M022 (Root yourself in a place) and D035 (Live within your ecosystem's bounds). Several, such as E025 and E030, come from Heidegger's thought on spatiality and dwelling, which is a different tradition from the psychological research above, so the two should not be treated as one argument.

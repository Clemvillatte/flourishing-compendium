Natural and necessary is Epicurus's term for the desires that must be met for a happy life, and the half of his classification that the other halves are judged against. The Letter to Menoeceus states it: "We must also reflect that of desires some are natural, others are groundless; and that of the natural some are necessary as well as natural, and some natural only. And of the necessary desires some are necessary if we are to be happy, some if the body is to be rid of uneasiness, some if we are even to live" ([Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus 127, MIT translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html)). The aim of the sorting is "health of body and tranquillity of mind, seeing that this is the sum and end of a happy life" ([Letter to Menoeceus](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html)).

### The three classes

The classification runs three ways. Natural and necessary desires look to happiness, physical well-being or life itself: food, drink, shelter, clothing, basic medicine ([SEP, Epicurus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/)). Natural but unnecessary desires are for variations of pleasure, sweet odors, fine food and drink, which can contribute joy but produce no pain when unfulfilled; Principal Doctrine 26 makes this the test, such desires are "easy to dispel when they seem hard to achieve or to produce harm" ([UC Davis lecture notes, with PD 26](https://hume.ucdavis.edu/phi143/epieth.htm)). Empty desires are for things "designated by empty sounds, such as immortality, which cannot exist for human beings," and for crowns, statues, wealth and power ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/); [UC Davis notes](https://hume.ucdavis.edu/phi143/epieth.htm)). Principal Doctrine 29 gives the compressed version: "Of our desires, some are natural and necessary, some are natural and not necessary, and some are neither natural nor necessary, but are produced by empty opinion" ([Epicurus, Principal Doctrine 29, via the classification literature](https://www.untroubled.blog/p/aphorisms-on-the-classification)).

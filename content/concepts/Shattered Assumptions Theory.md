Shattered Assumptions Theory is Ronnie Janoff-Bulman's account of psychological trauma: trauma does its damage by breaking the taken-for-granted beliefs a person lives by. A narrative review of PTSD theories states its structure.

> "This theory outlines three positive and rigid schematic assumptions: the world is meaningful (e.g., people get what they deserve), the world is benevolent (e.g., people are more good than not), and the self is worthy (e.g., I am good, so bad things could never happen to me)." (Strachan, Paulik & McEvoy, [A narrative review of psychological theories of post-traumatic stress disorder, voice hearing, and other psychotic symptoms](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10084244/), Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy, 2022)

### Origin

The theory was set out in Janoff-Bulman's 1992 book, cited in the review via Brewin and Holmes (2003); the review also cites earlier work from 1983 and 1989.

### Core claims

The review summarizes the causal claim: the assumptions "are proposed to afford the individual a feeling of invulnerability and that the world is predictable, which produces a sense of safety and security" ([same review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10084244/)). A traumatic event that cannot be integrated into these schemas shatters them, producing feelings of extreme vulnerability, defencelessness and distress. The theory holds that the more strongly a person holds the assumptions, or if they have never been challenged before, the more sudden the shattering will be. Coping strategies such as denial, self-blame and intrusive symptoms are read as attempts at re-building the schematic structure.

### Standing criticism

The theory has been challenged empirically. The review records the objection that people with multiple traumas, whose positive schemas should already have changed, are more likely to experience PTSD symptoms; Janoff-Bulman's responses to this are also summarized there. Clifton's study reports Kaler et al. (2008), which found that "in only about a quarter of those recently traumatized was there any reliable change in world beliefs" (Clifton, [Testing If Primal World Beliefs Reflect Experiences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7327086/), Frontiers in Psychology, 2020), so, as that paper puts it, "despite the popularity of shattered assumptions theory, there is little evidence much shattering happens".

### In everyday life

Illustrative scenario, not a reported study: a driver who has always assumed careful drivers are safe is rear-ended at a red light. On the theory's account the accident confronts her with information the assumption cannot absorb, and her heightened caution afterward is the schema being rebuilt around new information.

George Kelly's 1955 theory rests on one fundamental postulate: "A person's processes are psychologically channelized by the ways in which he anticipates events" (Kelly 1955, p. 46, quoted in the [Springer Nature Link entry, Fundamental Postulate (Kelly)](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-24612-3_977?error=cookies_not_supported&code=f223088d-9543-4b3f-82f3-4dc58e6361cc)). The entry describes it as "meant as a tentative statement so basic in its nature that it antecedes everything said in the system it supports", elaborated by 11 corollaries and grounded in constructive alternativism: "all of our present interpretations of the universe are subject to revision or replacement".

### Anticipation, not drives

The pcp-net encyclopaedia says the person "is not determined by inner drives as in Freudian psychoanalysis, or by environmental stimuli like behaviourism, or by a potential for self-realisation as in humanistic psychologies. Rather the person, like a scientist, seeks prediction: he/she formulates hypotheses about the world and him/herself (by construing events), and verifies them by means of behaviour". On this view behaviour "is a way of posing a question", an experiment that can validate or invalidate what the person anticipates ([Chiari and Nuzzo, Anticipation, pcp-net](https://www.pcp-net.org/encyclopaedia/anticipation.html)). The seed's "like naive scientists" therefore reflects Kelly's own comparison, as summarised there, rather than a later gloss.

### Constructs and the repertory grid

The Construction Corollary states: "A person anticipates events by construing their replications" ([Kihlstrom, Cognitive Approaches to Personality, Berkeley](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~jfkihlstrom/PersonalityWeb/Ch12Cognitive.htm)). An ERIC summary says constructs are abstractions "possessing both similarities and contrasts in their elements", which is the sense of the seed's bipolar pairs ([George Kelly: The Theory of Personal Constructs, ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED397368)). The pairs kind-cruel and safe-dangerous are the seed's examples; none of the sources I opened lists them. Kelly's Role Construct Repertory Test, the basis of the grid, asks people to name role figures, compare them and rate each on the constructs elicited. The Wiley excerpt calls the grid "an idiographic means of understanding a person's psychological space in terms of the patterns between constructs and elements" ([Exploring Personal Construct Theory, Wiley excerpt PDF](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9780470986547.excerpt.pdf)).

### How well it holds up

Kihlstrom reports that test-retest studies indicate most normal individuals have fairly stable construct systems, and that responses on the test permitted prediction of actual behaviour. He adds that stability "is an open question in Kelly's theory, and should not be assumed in advance". I did not open evidence on therapeutic outcomes, such as fixed-role therapy.

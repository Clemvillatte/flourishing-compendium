The skandhas (Sanskrit; Pali khandha) are the five aggregates — form (rūpa), feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), mental formations (saṅkhāra), and consciousness (viññāṇa) — the five streams that compose experience. The Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta (SN 22.59) examines each and finds none to be a self: each aggregate is inconstant and stressful, and each is to be seen with right discernment as "This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am."

> "Thus, monks, any form whatsoever that is past, future, or present; internal or external; blatant or subtle; common or sublime; far or near: every form is to be seen as it actually is with right discernment as: 'This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am.'" (Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta, SN 22.59, [trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu, AccessToInsight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn22/sn22.059.than.html))

### The clinging that hurts

My reading (principle B120): experience comes as five streams, with no single owner inside them. There is no pilot; there is weather. The qualifier that matters is the one the Khandhasaṁyutta adds: SN 22.48 distinguishes the five aggregates from the five aggregates subject to clinging (upādānakkhandhā), and SN 56.11 names the five clinging-aggregates as stressful.

### The family it keeps

The skandha analysis is the Buddhist member of a family of deconstructive accounts of the person: Hume's bundle of perceptions and the [[The narrative self|narrative self]] of psychology. Its job in the teaching is diagnostic: it is the basis on which [[Anatta]] gets argued, and its cross-tradition work is to dissolve the sense of a solid self without denying experience.

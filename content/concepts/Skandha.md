The skandhas (Sanskrit; Pali khandha) are the five aggregates — form (rūpa), feeling (vedanā), perception (saññā), mental formations (saṅkhāra), and consciousness (viññāṇa) — the five streams that compose experience. The Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta (SN 22.59) examines each and finds none to be a self: none obeys the command "let this be thus", each leads to affliction, each is to be seen as "this is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self".

The compendium's reading (principle B120): experience is five streams, not one owner. There is no pilot; there is weather. The Khandhasaṁyutta (SN 22) adds the crucial qualifier — it is the five aggregates subject to clinging (upādānakkhandhā) that are dukkha. The aggregates are not the problem; the grasping is.

The skandha analysis is the Buddhist member of a family of deconstructive accounts of the person: Hume's bundle of perceptions, the narrative self of psychology, and the Five Aggregates' role as the diagnostic basis for anatta. Its cross-tradition work is to dissolve the sense of a solid self without denying experience.

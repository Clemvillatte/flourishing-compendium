In Hull's account, drive reduction provides reinforcement, while drive activates learned behavioural potentials into performance ([EBSCO Research Starters, Drive theory](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/psychology/drive-theory), section "Hull's drive, reinforcement, and learning"). Drives push, habits guide.

## Field

Biophysiological · Psychological science

### Drive and habit

Hull's system gives the drive two jobs in behavioural activation: "According to Hull, a drive has at least two distinct functions as far as behavioral activation is concerned: without drives, there could be no reinforcement and thus no learning, because drive reduction is the reinforcement; and without drives, there could be no response, for a drive activates behavioral potentials into performance" ([EBSCO Research Starters, Drive theory](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/psychology/drive-theory), section "Hull's drive, reinforcement, and learning").

EBSCO describes H as an acquired stimulus-response relationship: "Hull uses D to symbolize drive and sHr (H is commonly used to denote this, for convenience) to symbolize a habit that consists of an acquired relationship between stimulus (S) and response (R). It represents a memory of experience in which certain environmental stimuli and responses were followed by a reward." The two combine multiplicatively: "A simplified version of the Hullian formula for a behavior would be 'behavior = HD' or 'performance = behavioral potential energizer.'... This is a multiplicative relationship; that is, when either H or D is zero, a specific performance cannot occur" (both from the same [EBSCO Research Starters article](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/psychology/drive-theory), section "Hull's drive, reinforcement, and learning").

### Need and drive

The theory separates the bodily need from the drive that connects it to behaviour: "Drive theory maintains that a state named 'drive,' or D, is a necessary condition for behavior to occur, but D is not the same as the bodily need." The two can diverge: "the intensity of behavior is not always related to the intensity of need," and "Need can be defined as a state of an organism attributable to deprivation of a biological or psychological requirement, related to a disturbance in the homeostatic state" (same [EBSCO Research Starters article](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/psychology/drive-theory), section "Hull's drive, reinforcement, and learning").

### Drive theory in Freud

EBSCO's page description also draws a parallel with Freud's account of the id: "Sigmund Freud's concept of the id parallels drive theory, depicting instinctual drives as fundamental sources of behavior, managed by the ego in response to external realities" ([EBSCO Research Starters, Drive theory](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/psychology/drive-theory), page description; the article's section "Freud's 'Id'" develops the parallel).

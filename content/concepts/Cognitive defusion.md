Cognitive defusion is the ACT practice of seeing thoughts as thoughts, mental events and constructions passing through awareness, rather than as commands, facts, or the self. Where cognitive change strategies argue with a thought's content, defusion changes one's *relationship* to it: "I'm a failure" becomes "I'm having the thought that I'm a failure," and the thought loses its authority. A critical review of decentering and its kin quotes the two definitional statements:

> "cognitive defusion has been described as the process that enables a person to 'to see thoughts and feelings for what they are (i.e., a verbally entangled process of minding) rather than what they advertise themselves to be (e.g., the world understood, structured reality)'" (Hayes, Strosahl & Wilson 1999, p. 150, quoted in [Bernstein et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5103165/)).

> "The DDS is used to measure defusion, which is defined as 'the ability to achieve psychological distance from internal experiences such as thoughts and feelings'" (Forman et al. 2012, p. 55, quoted in [Bernstein et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5103165/)).

### The mechanism

On the compendium's reading, language fuses with experience by default: the word "failure" is the experience until attention steps back. Defusion inserts the gap by noticing the thought as a thought, without disputing it, often by thanking the mind, singing it, or labelling the process ("there's the catastrophising again").

### Not reappraisal

The distinction to hold: defusion is not reappraisal. [[Cognitive reappraisal]] replaces the thought's content with a better reading; defusion leaves the content untouched and loosens its grip. It is useful when the thought is sticky, when disputing feeds it, or when there is no time.

### In everyday life

The same sentence written twice on a card, "I am worthless" and "I am having the thought that I am worthless", and one minute spent noticing how differently the two land. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

The cross-tradition cognates are exact: [[Anatta]] and [[Self-as-context]] are the Buddhist and ACT accounts of the observing perspective defusion trains, and [[Right concentration]] is the practice through which thoughts are watched arising and passing without being followed.

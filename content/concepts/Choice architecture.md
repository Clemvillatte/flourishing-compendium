Choice architecture seeks to influence decisions by designing their physical, social or psychological context while preserving freedom of choice ([Mertens et al. 2022, PNAS 119(1):e2107346118](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)). The PNAS meta-analysis describes Thaler and Sunstein's book Nudge as widely introducing this approach, which aims to change behavior by redesigning the environment in which people make decisions while preserving their freedom of choice ([Mertens et al. 2022, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)). The approach draws on the finding that boundedly rational decision makers are susceptible to contextual influences such as how information is presented or structured, and treats that susceptibility as a lever: simple changes to the choice architecture can influence decisions ([Mertens et al. 2022, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)).

> "Choice architecture interventions aim to nudge people toward personally and socially desirable behavior through the design of choice environments." — [Mertens, Herberz, Hahnel & Brosch 2022, PNAS, Significance statement](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)

### The evidence

A meta-analysis of more than 200 studies reporting over 440 effect sizes (n = 2,148,439) found that choice architecture interventions promote behavior change with a small to medium effect size (Cohen's d = 0.43), with decision-structure interventions (arranging the alternatives, e.g. defaults) consistently outperforming decision-information and decision-assistance interventions, and food choices especially responsive ([Mertens et al. 2022, PNAS, abstract](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)).

> "Our results show that choice architecture interventions overall promote behavior change with a small to medium effect size of Cohen's d = 0.43 (95% CI [0.38, 0.48])." — [Mertens et al. 2022, PNAS, abstract](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)

The same analysis records the main limit: "Our analysis further reveals a moderate publication bias toward positive results in the literature" ([Mertens et al. 2022, PNAS, abstract](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2107346118)).

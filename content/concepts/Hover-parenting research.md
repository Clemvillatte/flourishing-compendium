Studies of overprotective, over-involved parenting and its measured associations with college students' autonomy and well-being.

Schiffrin and colleagues surveyed 297 college students, measuring over-controlling, so-called helicopter parenting:

> "Students who reported having over-controlling parents reported significantly higher levels of depression and less satisfaction with life. Furthermore, the negative effects of helicopter parenting on college students’ well-being were largely explained by the perceived violation of students’ basic psychological needs for autonomy and competence." (Schiffrin et al., [Helping or Hovering? The Effects of Helicopter Parenting on College Students’ Well-Being](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10826-013-9716-3.pdf), Journal of Child and Family Studies 23, 2014, abstract)

The study is a survey, so it reports associations as students described them. The authors interpret the well-being associations in terms of the students' perceived autonomy and competence, and they frame the results through self-determination theory, which holds that autonomy and competence are basic needs ([Schiffrin et al., 2014](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10826-013-9716-3.pdf)).

**Relational Models Theory** — Fiske — all social life is built from four relational models: communal sharing, authority ranking, equality matching, market pricing.

Relational Models Theory (RMT) is Alan Fiske's account of the elementary structures of social life, introduced in his 1991 book and 1992 Psychological Review article. A review quotes his summary directly:

> "People construct complex and varied social forms using combinations of these models implemented according to diverse cultural rules. People's chief social conceptions, concerns, and coordinating criteria, their primary purposes and their principles, are usually derived from the four models; they are the schemata people use to construct and construe relationships." (Fiske, 1992, p. 689, quoted in Cacioppo & Cacioppo, [Social Relationships and Health, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4021390/))

### The four models

The four models are communal sharing (equivalence and undifferentiated sharing within a group), authority ranking (linear hierarchy, with prerogatives above and protection below), equality matching (balanced, one-for-one reciprocity), and market pricing (proportionality and ratios). The same review notes that the models combine: people mix them, implemented according to diverse cultural rules, rather than living in only one at a time.

Beatitudo, blessedness, is for Spinoza the active joy that accompanies the third kind of knowledge — not a prize handed out afterward. The intellectual love of God generates it directly: in that love "consists not a passionate joy but an active one, even blessedness itself" ([SEP Spinoza](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)). The SEP is explicit that this is a state of understanding rather than of reward: the key to experiencing God is "philosophy and science, not religious awe and worshipful submission," which "leads to enlightenment, freedom and true blessedness (i.e., peace of mind)" ([SEP Spinoza](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)).

Blessedness is also democratic. "True piety and blessedness are universal in their scope and accessible to anyone, regardless of their confessional creed" ([SEP Spinoza](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)) — the mind's greatest good is not reserved for the metaphysician. The required love "must arise not from fear of possible penalties or hope for any rewards, but solely from the goodness of its object" ([SEP Spinoza](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/)).

For the flourishing project, blessedness names the endpoint where virtue and well-being coincide: the agent whose understanding has quieted the passions is not paid for virtue but fulfilled in it. It is the intellectual equivalent of grace, achieved from the inside.

Scheitern is Karl Jaspers' word for the failure that belongs to human existence as such, not to particular plans. In Way to Wisdom he ties it to the ultimate situations: "The ultimate situations — death, chance, guilt, and the uncertainty of the world — confront me with the reality of failure" (p. 21, trans. Manheim). The English rendering "shipwreck" was proposed to catch the word's force, since ordinary "failure" sounds like an event that happens to a project.

> "Gabriel Marcel and Paul-Louis Landsberg, in the course of a discussion of Jaspers's philosophy, rightly remarked that the word 'failure' perhaps does not evoke enough of the activity (relation to the subject) and of the objectivity (relation with the stumbling block) contained in the word Scheitern. This would rather be stranding, the fact of being beached, the shipwreck." (Jean Wahl, "The Problem of Choice: Existence and Transcendence in the Philosophy of Jaspers", note 67, [2016 translation](https://www.jffp.org/ojs/jffp/article/download/717/702))

### What the attitude decides

Jaspers' point is not that shipwreck is secretly good. What decides, on his account, is "his attitude toward failure: whether it remains hidden from him and overwhelms him only objectively at the end or whether he perceives it unobscured as the constant limit of his existence" (Way to Wisdom, p. 22). A person can snatch at "fantastic solutions and consolations" or face the limit "honestly, in silence before the unfathomable", and "the way in which man approaches his failure determines what he will become". Even despair, "by the very fact that it is possible in the world, points beyond the world" (p. 22).

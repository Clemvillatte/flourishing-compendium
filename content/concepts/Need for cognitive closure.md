Need for cognitive closure is a motive to have a definite answer to a question rather than to stay in doubt. Arie Kruglanski introduced the construct in 1989; the 1996 paper with Donna Webster, "Motivated closing of the mind: 'Seizing' and 'freezing,'" in Psychological Review 103(2), sets out the framework. Its abstract defines the term:

> "The need for closure is a desire for definite knowledge on some issue. It represents a dimension of stable individual differences as well as a situationally evocable state." (Kruglanski and Webster, Psychological Review 103(2), 1996, 263-83, abstract, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8637961/))

### Seizing and freezing

The abstract says the consequences of the need for closure derive from two tendencies. The urgency tendency is an inclination to reach closure as soon as possible, and the permanence tendency is an inclination to maintain it for as long as possible. The paper's title names these as "seizing" and "freezing." The authors report evidence of effects on impression formation, stereotyping, attribution, persuasion, group decision making and language use in intergroup contexts.

### Trait and state

Because it is both a stable individual difference and a state that situations can evoke, the same person can vary in this motive across circumstances. The abstract does not say which situations raise it, and this page makes no claim about them.

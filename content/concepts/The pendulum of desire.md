The pendulum of desire is Schopenhauer's image for the structure of wanting, stated in The World as Will and Representation (1819). The argument is that desire is born of lack, so wanting is pain; satisfaction removes the lack but leaves nothing to do, so possession tips into boredom; a new desire starts the cycle. Life therefore swings between two poles, and neither pole is the good.

### The argument in his own words

The diagnosis of wanting comes first: "Willing or striving may be compared to an unquenchable thirst. Every act of willing presupposes a want. The basis of all willing is need or deficiency. The nature of man, therefore, is subject to pain originally and through its very nature" ([Schopenhauer, WWR, quoted in Beer, Schopenhauer, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/47136/pg47136-images.html)). Then the other pole: "If, on the other hand, man lacks objects of desire, being deprived of them by too easy satisfaction, then a terrible emptiness and sense of boredom, comes over him" ([same](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/47136/pg47136-images.html)).

The pendulum sentence, in its most-circulated form:

> "Thus life swings like a pendulum backwards and forwards between pain and boredom."

([Schopenhauer, WWR, quoted in Beer, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/47136/pg47136-images.html).)

### Why the image matters

The point is structural, not a mood report. Schopenhauer's claim is that no single desire, however good, can stabilize the swing, because the will that runs the system is a restless force and not a negotiator with a finite list of demands. Satisfaction is therefore not the end of the process but one of its positions.

The compendium keeps the concept for two reasons. First, it is the sharpest statement of a problem several traditions address: Buddhist dependent arising, Stoic detachment and Schopenhauer's own prescription, the denial of the will, are all attempts to get off the pendulum. Second, it gives the affective sciences a target: if wanting is structured as a push-pull between two negative states, then the research question is what breaks the cycle, and the answer is not more satisfaction but a different relation to wanting. See [[Y012 — Read the fragility of chasing as signal]] for the compendium's own version, and Read joy and sadness as power signals (SP011) for the Stoic-Spinozist reading of the affects involved.

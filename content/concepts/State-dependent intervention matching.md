Niederlohmann proposes that a patient's momentary state can constrain productive use of a psychotherapy task. ([Niederlohmann, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13200463/))

> "Psychotherapy does not fail only because techniques are poorly chosen or alliances are weak; it may also fail because the patient’s momentary state does not support productive use of the task." ([Niederlohmann, 2026, Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13200463/))

### What the model proposes

The paper says plainly what kind of work it is: "In this hypothesis-and-theory paper, I propose a state-dependent translational model of psychotherapeutic processing." Autonomic regulation is "treated not as a binary switch but as a constraint on whether three proposed functional conditions are jointly available in clinically usable form: affective salience, contextual memory access, and reflective working capacity". It matters "especially in psychotherapy and psychosomatic care, where bodily distress, interoceptive threat, shame, and autonomic dysregulation can narrow affective, mnemonic, and reflective workability" (Niederlohmann, 2026, [Frontiers in Psychiatry, via PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13200463/)).

The paper states its limits: "The aim is not to offer a complete neuroscience of psychotherapy, but a clinically teachable and empirically testable model of psychotherapeutic workability". It also says that some therapies "rely on this logic implicitly".

### What it is not

This is not an established finding. It is a recent proposal that has been put forward as testable, and the compendium cites it for the idea that the match between a practice and the person's present state is a question worth asking before choosing the practice. It does not show that matching improves outcomes.

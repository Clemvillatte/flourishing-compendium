This line of research asks what a child's worth gets staked on. Its sharpest instrument is the study of parental conditional regard: acceptance that switches on when the child meets expectations. Assor, Roth, and Deci found that the practice buys compliance at a price.

> "Overall, the two studies showed that although perceived parental conditional regard was related to behavioral enactment in several domains, it was also associated with negative affective consequences such as feelings of internal compulsion, short-lived satisfaction, shame after failure, fluctuations in self-esteem, poor coping skills, low self-worth, a sense of being disapproved of by parents, and resentment toward parents." (Assor, Roth & Deci 2004, Journal of Personality 72(1), [full text at selfdeterminationtheory.org](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2004_AssorRothDeci.pdf))

### What autonomy support does

The comparison the seed points to was run directly. Roth, Assor, Niemiec, Ryan, and Deci compared conditional positive regard, conditional negative regard, and autonomy support as parenting practices and concluded: "even parents' use of conditional positive regard as a socialization practice has adverse emotional and academic consequences, relative to autonomy support" ([Developmental Psychology, 2009](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2009_RothAssorNiemiecRyanDeci.pdf)). The compendium's use is plain: a child whose worth is not held hostage learns self-regulation without resentment, which is why respecting another's nature counts as a flourishing practice rather than permissiveness.

The ethical is Kierkegaard's middle stage of existence, set out in the second volume of Either/Or through the letters of Judge Wilhelm. The judge's own formula for the difference: "the aesthetical in man is that by which he is immediately what he is, the ethical is that whereby he becomes what he becomes" (Either/Or II, quoted in [PhilArchive, Morality's Place](https://philarchive.org/archive/PILMPK)). And on choice: "The act of choosing is essentially a proper and stringent expression of the ethical. Whenever in a stricter sense there is [a] question of an either/or, one can always be sure that the ethical is involved" (EO, 170; [same](https://philarchive.org/archive/PILMPK)).

## Facets

- **demand** — Løgstrup's separate concept: the ethical demand is the unspoken claim the other's life places on you. The two uses of "ethical" are distinct; Kierkegaard's is about becoming a self, Løgstrup's about what we owe before we choose anything.

### Choosing oneself

The decisive act is what the judge calls choosing oneself: "But what is it then, that I choose — is it this or that? No, for I choose absolutely... I choose the absolute, and what is the absolute? It is myself in my eternal validity" (Either/Or II, quoted in [sorenkierkegaard.nl paper](https://www.sorenkierkegaard.nl/artikelen/Engels/048.%20despair%20in%20Kierkegaard's%20Either%20Or.pdf)). Marriage is the judge's worked example: commitment gives the self temporal continuity, and "in the ethical the aesthetic is not abolished but ennobled" ([Britannica, Kierkegaard](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Soren-Kierkegaard/Stages-on-lifes-way)). The stage is not the last word — the religious both annuls and preserves it ([SEP, Kierkegaard](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2016/entries/kierkegaard/)) — but the ethical is the first place a person becomes anyone in particular.

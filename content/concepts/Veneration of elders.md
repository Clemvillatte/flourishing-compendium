Veneration of elders is the Confucian practice the Analects builds around filial piety (xiao). Its core test is not obedience but reverence: "The filial piety of now-a-days means the support of one's parents. But dogs and horses likewise are able to do something in the way of support; — without reverence, what is there to distinguish the one support given from the other?" ([Confucius, Analects 2.7, Legge translation, Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/confucius/analects/james-legge/text/single-page)).

### What the practice asks

The Analects gives the ritual shape of the duty: "That parents, when alive, be served according to propriety; that, when dead, they should be buried according to propriety; and that they should be sacrificed to according to propriety" ([Analects 2.5, Legge](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/confucius/analects/james-legge/text/single-page)). And it gives the more demanding instruction for a son who disagrees: "In serving his parents, a son may remonstrate with them, but gently; when he sees that they do not incline to follow his advice, he shows an increased degree of reverence, but does not abandon his purpose" ([Analects 4.18, Legge](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/confucius/analects/james-legge/text/single-page)). Reverence and remonstrance are not opposites here; the second is done inside the first.

My own principle on listening to elders holds the same structure from the Ubuntu tradition: elders tell stories of their youth, and younger people consider what those experiences mean for life now, while older age alone is not treated as proof of wisdom ([IEP, Hunhu/Ubuntu, section 6](https://iep.utm.edu/hunhu-ubuntu-southern-african-thought/)).

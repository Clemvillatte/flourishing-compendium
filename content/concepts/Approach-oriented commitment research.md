Approach-oriented commitment research is the experimental literature on what commitments are made of. Its core contrast: an approach goal moves toward a desired outcome, an avoidance goal moves away from an aversive one. A recent review paper in the line states the pattern plainly.

> "Psychological research on human motivation repeatedly observed that approach goals (i.e., goals to attain success) increase task enjoyment and intrinsic motivation more strongly than avoidance goals (i.e., goals to avoid failure)." (Sakaki et al. 2024, [Cognitive, Affective & Behavioral Neuroscience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11078806/))

### Why avoidance drains

Avoidance goals can hold behavior in place; what the research tracks is their cost. Elliot, Sheldon, and Church followed participants' personal goals over a semester and found that "avoidance regulation proved deleterious to both retrospective and longitudinal subjective well-being (SWB), as participants with a greater proportion of avoidance goals reported lower SWB over the course of the semester and evidenced a decrease in SWB from the beginning to the end of the semester" ([Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 23, 1997](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0146167297239001)). Path analyses validated perceived progress as a mediator of those relationships: how much movement a goal portfolio yields is bound up with what it does to the person carrying it.

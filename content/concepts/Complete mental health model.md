“Complete mental health model” — Keyes, 2002.

In his 2002 study, Keyes operationalized mental health through positive feelings and positive functioning. His 2005 study investigated a complete-state model of health and found support for treating mental health and mental illness as separate but correlated dimensions. The 2002 paper states its own program:

> This paper introduces and applies an operationalization of mental health as a syndrome of symptoms of positive feelings and positive functioning in life. — Keyes, 2002, *J Health Soc Behav* 43(2):207–22, [PubMed abstract (opened)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/)

The two ends of the continuum have names. The presence of mental health is described as flourishing; the absence of mental health is characterized as languishing. The diagnosis was applied to the 1995 Midlife in the United States study of adults aged 25 to 74 (n = 3,032), and the paper reports the depression risk by category:

> The risk of a major depressive episode was two times more likely among languishing than moderately mentally healthy adults, and nearly six times greater among languishing than flourishing adults. — Keyes, 2002, [PubMed abstract (opened)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/)

The compendium builds on the model's core distinction: mental health is not just the absence of illness, and feeling good is a different question from doing well.

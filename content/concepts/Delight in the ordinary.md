Delight in the ordinary is the capacity to find sufficiency and freshness in plain, repeated things: food, clothes, work, the people and places already at hand. Its root text is Daodejing 80, whose small community is to "savour their food, find beauty in their clothes, feel secure in their homes, and delight in their customs" ([Daodejing 80](https://wudang.org/dao/dao-de-jing/full-text/chapter-80)). The chapter is not a programme against tools, in the page's own commentary, but a counter-image to restless expansion: a life rich enough in meaning that technology, travel and novelty do not become compulsions.

### The Zen completion

The Buddhist cousin makes the same point about enlightenment rather than society. Layman P'ang, a Tang-dynasty layman whose recorded sayings date his death to 808, summed up his realization in a verse: his "supernatural power and marvelous activity" is "drawing water and carrying firewood" ([Recorded Sayings of Layman P'ang, Sasaki translation](https://terebess.hu/zen/LaymanPang.pdf)). Another verse has him "an ordinary man who has settled his affairs." The awakened life does not leave the ordinary behind; it returns to it.

### Why it is hard

The compendium's principle D180 states the tension plainly: "Ecstasy is cheap; contentment is rare. The chase for peak experiences is how people end up missing the entire baseline." Craving keeps the baseline invisible, and boredom is what the untrained mind feels when the stimulation stops. The skill is attention, not circumstance. Psychology names a piece of it: in Fredrickson's account, contentment's urge is to savour current life circumstances and integrate them into new views of self and world ([Fredrickson 2001](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Frederickson_Broaden-and-build-2001.pdf)).

### In the compendium

D180 carries the concept directly, with Zhuangzi 18 and Daodejing 80 as its sources. K023 makes the Kyoto School's endpoint the return to dishes, work and family, the "ordinary mind" whose marks are patience, warmth and availability. B166 practises P'ang's chop wood and carry water, B181 stays with boredom, and SP073 guards cheerfulness under loss. ST306's Tim learns to live each day as if he had already come back to it, which is the same skill.

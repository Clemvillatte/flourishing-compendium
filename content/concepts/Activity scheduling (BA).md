Activity scheduling is one of the behavioral strategies used in behavioral activation (BA) ([Dimidjian, Barrera, Martell, Muñoz and Lewinsohn, 2011](https://docslib.org/doc/11508311/the-origins-and-current-status-of-behavioral-activation-treatments-for-depression)). In the University of Washington training protocol described below, patients plan activities for the coming week and rate their satisfaction after doing them ([Meta-Competency 7-d: Behavioral Activation](https://bhss-wa.psychiatry.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/MC7D-Behavioral-Activation.pdf)).

> "weekly activity schedule: a schedule created by the patient for the next week that aims to include at least one rewarding activity each day (e.g., an enjoyable, social, or physical activity) over and above their routine. There is space next to each activity for the patient to rate their level of satisfaction after doing the activity." (University of Washington, [Meta-Competency 7-d: Behavioral Activation](https://bhss-wa.psychiatry.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/MC7D-Behavioral-Activation.pdf))

### What the approach aims at

The 2011 review defines the approach the procedure belongs to:

> "We define BA as a structured, brief psychotherapeutic approach that aims to (a) increase engagement in adaptive activities (which often are those associated with the experience of pleasure or mastery), (b) decrease engagement in activities that maintain depression or increase risk for depression, and (c) solve problems that limit access to reward or that maintain or increase aversive control." ([Dimidjian, Barrera, Martell, Muñoz and Lewinsohn, 2011, available text excerpt](https://docslib.org/doc/11508311/the-origins-and-current-status-of-behavioral-activation-treatments-for-depression))

The review lists activity scheduling among the strategies used to pursue these aims.

### The procedure

The clinical training description breaks the practice into steps ([Meta-Competency 7-d](https://bhss-wa.psychiatry.uw.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/MC7D-Behavioral-Activation.pdf)). Patients first brainstorm activities that have been rewarding or meaningful, including ones they did before experiencing depressive symptoms. They then make specific plans for the coming week: "what each step may involve, when they would like to begin each activity, for how long, and with whom." After each activity the patient rates satisfaction. The patient's activity plans from the prior week are reviewed at the start of the next session, and barriers are problem-solved into a new weekly activity schedule.

## In plain words

Allostasis theory is Peter Sterling and Joseph Eyer's account of how the body stays stable: not by holding every parameter at a fixed point, but by predicting what will be needed and shifting physiology in advance. Their founding statement reads: "To maintain stability an organism must vary all the parameters of its internal milieu and match them appropriately to environmental demands. We refer to this principle as allostasis, meaning 'stability through change'" ([Sterling & Eyer 1988, quoted at INHN](https://inhn.org/inhn-projects/ebooks/default-title-4)).

## Where it comes from

The term appeared in Sterling and Eyer's 1988 chapter in the Handbook of Life Stress, Cognition and Health, as a rival to Walter Cannon's homeostasis, which corrects errors after they occur. Bruce McEwen then built the health consequences on top of it.

## How it works

Where homeostasis reacts, allostasis anticipates: blood pressure rises on waking so the brain keeps its blood supply on standing. McEwen's definition of the mechanism: "the term 'allostasis' was introduced by Sterling and Eyer to refer to the active process by which the body responds to daily events and maintains homeostasis (allostasis literally means 'achieving stability through change')" ([McEwen 2006](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.31887/DCNS.2006.8.4/bmcewen)).

## What it explains

The theory explains why adaptation itself has a cost. When the shifting runs too often or never switches off, the bill comes due: "we introduced the term 'allostatic load or overload' to refer to the wear and tear that results from either too much stress or from inefficient management of allostasis, eg, not turning off the response when it is no longer needed" ([McEwen 2006](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.31887/DCNS.2006.8.4/bmcewen)).

## Everyday life

A week of poor sleep and looming deadlines keeps the predictive machinery running hot; you feel wired at midnight and flat by morning. Allostasis theory reframes this: nothing is broken, the system is doing its job too often. The remedy is not calm but rhythm, letting the response complete and switch off, which is why recovery, sleep and downtime are medical acts, not luxuries.

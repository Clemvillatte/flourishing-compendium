Learning by doing is John Dewey's name for the claim that knowledge grows out of activity and its consequences, not from the passive reception of information. The doctrine runs through Democracy and Education (1916) and Experience and Education (1938), and its core sentence is Dewey's definition of experience as "an active and a passive element peculiarly combined": "When we experience something we act upon it, we do something with it; then we suffer or undergo the consequences" ([Democracy and Education, ch. 11, JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7312/dewe21010); [Hanover excerpts](https://history.hanover.edu/courses/excerpts/165dewey.html)).

### Trying and undergoing

The learning happens in the connection between the two phases. "To 'learn from experience' is to make a backward and forward connection between what we do to things and what we enjoy or suffer from things in consequence. Under such conditions, doing becomes trying; an experiment with the world to find out what it is like; the undergoing becomes instruction, discovery of the connections of things" ([Democracy and Education, ch. 11](https://history.hanover.edu/courses/excerpts/165dewey.html)). Dewey's caveat is the part most often lost: "Mere activity does not constitute experience," and change without the "return wave of consequences" being consciously connected is "meaningless transition" ([Democracy and Education, ch. 11, discussed in Journal of Philosophy of Education](https://academic.oup.com/jope/article/52/2/287/6821430)). The slogan form, "we learn from reflecting on experience," carries the same point ([Sophia tutorial, Dewey](https://app.sophia.org/tutorials/john-dewey-experiential-learningpragmatism?msockid=3d6eb3d9ee1d62cd0519a428efc063ca)).

Dewey's pedagogy follows: schools should let students discover that "success in something he is interested in doing depends on ability to use numbers," rather than lecturing on remote benefits ([Democracy and Education quotes](https://libquotes.com/john-dewey/works/democracy-and-education)), and "whether we permit chance environments to do the work, or whether we design environments for the purpose makes a great difference" ([Wiley, Dewey and education for democracy](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/edth.12567)).

### In the compendium

The compendium treats the principle as an epistemology of practice. P042 states it directly and CO008 gives it the constructivist reading, understanding built rather than received. D072's Daoist form says learn by doing, not only by reading, and D003's wu wei supplies the non-forcing that keeps the doing honest; ST027's Mr. Miyagi turns chores into the curriculum itself; A092 finds the joy in learning that fuels the trying; A072's suzēn extends it to living together; K252's moral exemplar modelling is the doing of character; SE024 warns that the credit-and-blame distortion can corrupt the undergoing phase; and ST254's George Bailey ledger is the reflection that shows what the doing meant.

## In plain words

The approach-inhibition theory of power is Dacher Keltner, Deborah Gruenfeld and Cameron Anderson's account of what power does to a person. Its core claim, from the abstract: "Elevated power is associated with increased rewards and freedom and thereby activates approach-related tendencies. Reduced power is associated with increased threat, punishment, and social constraint and thereby activates inhibition-related tendencies" ([Keltner, Gruenfeld & Anderson 2003](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/dacherkeltner/docs/keltner.power.psychreview.2003.pdf)). Power does not just change what you can do; it changes how you think and feel.

## Where it comes from

The theory appeared in Psychological Review in 2003 as an integrative review, "Power, Approach, and Inhibition" (110(2), 265-284). As the authors put it, they "derive predictions from recent theorizing about approach and inhibition" and review the relevant evidence ([Keltner, Gruenfeld & Anderson 2003](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/dacherkeltner/docs/keltner.power.psychreview.2003.pdf)).

## How it works

The mechanism is the environment power creates. The powerful live in reward-rich surroundings with freedom to act, which activates approach; the powerless live among threats, punishments and social constraints, which activates inhibition. The theory's inventory: "power is associated with (a) positive affect, (b) attention to rewards, (c) automatic information processing, and (d) disinhibited behavior. In contrast, reduced power is associated with (a) negative affect; (b) attention to threat, punishment, others' interests, and those features of the self that are relevant to others' goals; (c) controlled information processing; and (d) inhibited social behavior" ([Keltner, Gruenfeld & Anderson 2003](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/dacherkeltner/docs/keltner.power.psychreview.2003.pdf)).

## What it explains

The theory organises a wide range of findings under one heading. On its account, the powerful show positive affect, attention to rewards, automatic information processing and disinhibited behaviour, while the powerless show negative affect, attention to threat, punishment and others' interests, controlled information processing and inhibited social behaviour ([Keltner, Gruenfeld & Anderson 2003](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/dacherkeltner/docs/keltner.power.psychreview.2003.pdf)). The authors note that these are relative tendencies with moderators and consequences discussed in the review, not fixed traits.

## Everyday life

As an illustration of the pattern: notice who in the room asks questions freely and who gauges reactions before speaking. The theory would read this as one possible effect of position, not of character: freedom loosens, constraint tightens. For anyone holding power, the theory's implication is to install their own checks, since the position itself will not supply them.

Prospect theory is [[T022 — Kahneman, Daniel|Kahneman]] and Tversky's 1979 descriptive model of choice under risk. Its own summary of what it does:

> "This paper presents a critique of expected utility theory as a descriptive model of decision making under risk, and develops an alternative model, called prospect theory." ([Kahneman & Tversky, 1979, Econometrica 47(2), abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecm/emetrp/v47y1979i2p263-91.html))

The value function is, in the abstract's words, "normally concave for gains, commonly convex for losses, and is generally steeper for losses than for gains" ([1979 abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecm/emetrp/v47y1979i2p263-91.html)). On the certainty effect, the authors write: "This tendency, called the certainty effect, contributes to risk aversion in choices involving sure gains and to risk seeking in choices involving sure losses" ([1979 abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ecm/emetrp/v47y1979i2p263-91.html)).

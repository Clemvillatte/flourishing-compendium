A positivity portfolio is a personal collection of photos, letters, quotes, songs or objects, built to bring back one particular positive emotion when you look at it. Barbara Fredrickson describes it in her book Positivity (2009) as part of a toolkit for raising positive emotion in daily life. A teaching page from the Proof Positive project puts it plainly: it is "a portfolio or collection of items, memories, songs or pictures that trigger you to feel specific positive emotions" ([Proof Positive](https://proofpositive.org/skills/positivity-portfolios/learn/)).

### How it is used

The same page lists the ten emotions the portfolios are organised around: joy, love, inspiration, serenity, amusement, awe, pride, interest, gratitude and hope. The steps it gives are:

- Spend several weeks building the portfolio.
- Collect items, memories, songs or pictures tied to a chosen emotion.
- Spend about 15 minutes a day savoring what you have collected.

I read this description second hand. The page names Fredrickson's book as its source, and the book itself was not opened for this wiki. A copy of the book text turned up in search, but it was not an authorised source, so nothing here is quoted from it.

### The theory behind it

The portfolio is an application of Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory. Her 2001 paper says that positive emotions "broaden people's momentary thought-action repertoires, which in turn serves to build their enduring personal resources", and that the capacity for them "may be a fundamental human strength central to the study of human flourishing" ([Fredrickson, 2001, American Psychologist](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3122271/)). The portfolio is a practical way to put a person in front of those moments more often. That link is mine, and Proof Positive draws the same connection.

### What the evidence does and does not show

The Proof Positive page lists many benefits of regularly feeling positive emotions, but it gives no specific study, sample or statistics for the portfolio itself. I found no trial of the portfolio on its own. Treat it as a reasonable, low-cost practice that rests on the broader positive-emotion literature, not as a tested intervention. The broaden-and-build paper reviews preliminary evidence and lists open questions, which is a reminder to stay modest about how far the theory reaches.

Eidos is the Greek term Husserl sets beside the word essence, for what we grasp when we see what kind of thing something is, as distinct from seeing this individual thing. In §3 of Ideas I (Kersten translation), he argues that this is a kind of seeing in its own right: "Of whatever sort intuition of something individual may be, whether it be adequate or inadequate, it can take the turn into seeing an essence." He continues: "The essence (Eidos) is a new sort of object. Just as the datum of individual or experiencing intuition is an individual object, so the datum of eidetic intuition is a pure essence" ([Husserl, Ideas I §3, PDF of the Kersten translation](https://www.finophd.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Husserl-Ideas-First-Book.pdf)).

### Seeing an essence is intuition

Husserl insists that this is no metaphor: "Seeing an essence is also precisely intuition, just as an eidetic object is precisely an object." He describes the relation as "not a merely external analogy but a radical community" ([§3](https://www.finophd.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Husserl-Ideas-First-Book.pdf)).

### Seen in imagination

In §70 he adds a method point. Seizing an essence, he says, "can be effected on the ground of a mere presentiation of exemplificative single particulars". Imagining an example, if it is clear enough, "makes possible a perfect seizing upon essences and a perfect eidetic insight." He still gives perception its place, because external perception has "its primacies over all kinds of presentiation" as a foundation for findings about factual being and for eidetic findings ([§70](https://www.finophd.eu/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Husserl-Ideas-First-Book.pdf)).

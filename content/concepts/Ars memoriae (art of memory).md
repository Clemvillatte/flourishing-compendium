The art of memory is a trained method for holding facts in mind by placing images in remembered places. Its founding story, told by Cicero in De Oratore, credits the poet Simonides of Ceos: called outside a banquet just before the roof fell, he identified the crushed dead for burial by remembering where each guest had been reclining. The discovery he drew from it is the method's whole engine.

> "He inferred that persons desiring to train this faculty must select localities and form mental images of the facts they wish to remember and store those images in the localities, with the result that the arrangement of the localities will preserve the order of the facts, and the images of the facts will designate the facts themselves, and we shall employ the localities and images respectively as a wax writing tablet and the letters written on it." (Cicero, De Oratore II.354, trans. E.W. Sutton, [UT Austin selection](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/memoria/Cicero.html))

### What the story argues

Cicero frames the discovery as a truth about order rather than about imagery: "the best aid to clearness of memory consists in orderly arrangement" (same page). The places supply sequence, the images supply content, and the two together act as tablet and letters. I pair the method with modern retrieval practice rather than treating it as a curiosity: both claim that memory improves when the act of remembering is given structure.

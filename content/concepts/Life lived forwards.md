Kierkegaard's journal entry from 1843: understanding looks backward, living goes forward, and there is no moment in which the two can be held together.

> It is really true what philosophy tells us, that life must be understood backwards. But with this, one forgets the second proposition, that it must be lived forwards. A proposition which, the more it is subjected to careful thought, the more it ends up concluding precisely that life at any given moment cannot really ever be fully understood; exactly because there is no single moment where time stops completely in order for me to take position [to do this]: going backwards.

(Søren Kierkegaard, journals, JJ:167, 1843, Søren Kierkegaards Skrifter vol. 18, p. 306, quoted at [University of Iowa provenance page](https://homepage.divms.uiowa.edu/~jorgen/kierkegaardquotesource.html))

The line usually quoted, that life can only be understood backwards but must be lived forwards, is a shortened paraphrase of this entry. The entry's own twist is the closing move: life never becomes fully understandable even in retrospect, because the backward view is taken from inside a life that is still moving. The compendium's D181 pairs it with not grieving the past or chasing the future, and the narrative principles read lives backward exactly as Kierkegaard says they are read.

Planning research gives this page's claim two legs, one from how people actually plan and one from what happens to a plan that meets an uncertain future.

The human leg is Barbara Hayes-Roth and Frederick Hayes-Roth's opportunistic planning model. Watching people plan a day of errands, they found planning that jumps between big decisions and small ones as chances arise, and the research group drew a strong conclusion from it:

> "We believe that the flexibility embodied in the opportunistic control regime is both a more accurate model of the variability we observe in human planning behavior and a more powerful approach to planning in general." (Hayes-Roth, Hayes-Roth, Rosenschein and Cammarata, [Modeling planning as an incremental, opportunistic process](https://escholarship.org/content/qt413217pr/qt413217pr.pdf))

The same paper says what the freedom buys:

> "Opportunism provides planners freedom to examine various aspects of a problem, investigate alternative plan configurations, etc. This enables them to discover solutions that more rigid approaches would obscure." (same paper)

The engineering leg tests rigid against adaptive directly. Kwakkel, Walker and Marchau compared a dynamic adaptive plan for airport infrastructure with a static rigid implementation across a wide spectrum of simulated futures, and the rigid plan won in only a small part of that spectrum. The adaptive plan also showed a narrower spread of outcomes, which the authors read as less exposure to uncertainty about its future performance ([Kwakkel, Walker and Marchau 2012, Environment and Planning B, abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/envirb/v39y2012i3p533-550.html)).

My reading, and the limit worth holding: the Kwakkel result is a simulation of one infrastructure case, so it shows the direction rather than a field-measured size. And "adaptive" in this literature means plans with re-planning built in, triggers and signposts included, so the finding favors plans that keep a direction while changing their steps. It does not favor drifting without a direction.

Abhyāsa (Sanskrit, "practice") is the Yoga tradition's term for sustained, repeated effort directed at steadying the mind. The Yoga Sutra defines practice in sūtra 1.13 and the conditions under which it becomes firm in sūtra 1.14 ([Yoga-Sutra 1.13, Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216612.html)).

> "Of these, practice is the effort to secure steadiness." (Yoga-Sutra 1.13, [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216612.html))

### How practice becomes firm

Sūtra 1.14 gives the conditions:

> "And this is firmly rooted, being well-attended to for a long time without interruption and with devotion." (Yoga-Sutra 1.14, [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216613.html))

Bhagavad Gita 6.35 also pairs practice with renunciation:

> "Śrī Bhagavān said: O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, undoubtedly the mind is restless and most difficult to control, but by constant practice and renunciation, it can be checked." (Bhagavad Gita 6.35, [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shrimad-bhagavad-gita/d/doc419954.html))

“Abhyasa” — Bhagavad Gita 6.35; Yoga Sutra 1.13-14; Samkhya.

Abhyāsa (Sanskrit, "practice") is the Yoga tradition's term for sustained, repeated effort directed at steadying the mind. The Yoga Sutra names practice and detachment as the two means of stilling the mind's fluctuations, then defines practice in sutra 1.13 and its conditions in sutra 1.14 ([Yoga Sutra 1.13-14, Rama Prasad translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216612.html)).

> "Of these, practice is the effort to secure steadiness." (Yoga Sutra 1.13, [Rama Prasad translation, Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216612.html))

### How practice becomes firm

Sutra 1.14 gives the conditions under which practice holds: "And this is firmly rooted, being well-attended to for a long time without interruption and with devotion." ([Yoga Sutra 1.14](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-commentaries/d/doc1216613.html)) Vyasa's commentary adds that the practice reaches firmness gradually, because the outgoing habits of the mind oppose it, and that it should not be given up.

The Bhagavad Gita states the pairing of the two means at 6.35, where Krishna answers Arjuna's complaint that the mind is restless and hard to control:

> "Śrī Bhagavān said: O mighty-armed son of Kuntī, undoubtedly the mind is restless and most difficult to control, but by constant practice and renunciation, it can be checked." (Bhagavad Gita 6.35, [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shrimad-bhagavad-gita/d/doc419954.html))

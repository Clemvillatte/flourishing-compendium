Hope is the forward-facing emotion: expectation of a good that has not arrived and is not guaranteed. The compendium's seed names its two poles, the psychological one in Snyder's hope theory and the philosophical one in Bloch's The Principle of Hope and Marcel's Mystery of Being, and the principle that carries it, R087, draws the line that organises both: hope is not optimism, and it is the refusal to despair while staying honest about the odds.

### Snyder's theory: goals, pathways, agency

C.R. Snyder defined hope as "a positive motivational state that is based on an interactively derived sense of successful (1) agency (goal-directed energy) and (2) pathways (planning to meet goals)" (Snyder, Irving, and Anderson 1991, p. 287, quoted in [Oxford Hope Theory chapter](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28153/chapter/212948318)). Pathways thinking is the perceived ability to generate routes to a goal, and alternates when one is blocked; agency is the motivational self-talk that sustains movement, "I can do this." In the 2002 target article Snyder reported that higher hope relates consistently to better outcomes in academics, athletics, physical health, psychological adjustment and psychotherapy, and that on his definition "no evidence is found for 'false' hope" ([Snyder, Hope Theory: Rainbows in the Mind, Psychological Inquiry 13 (2002)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/S15327965PLI1304_01)). The clinical extension treats hope as a common factor: therapies work because they help people identify goals, specify routes and motivate their use ([hope enhancement meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/2211-1522-1-5)).

### Bloch's principle: the Not-Yet

Ernst Bloch's three-volume Principle of Hope starts from the daydream: "Everybody's life is pervaded by daydreams: one part of this is just stale, even enervating escapism... but another part is provocative, is not content just to accept the bad which exists, does not accept renunciation" ([Marxists Internet Archive, introduction](https://www.marxists.org/archive/bloch/hope/introduction.htm)). His category is the Not-Yet, in consciousness and in reality: "Expectation, hope, intention towards possibility that has still not become," which for Bloch is, "concretely corrected and grasped, a basic determination within objective reality as a whole" ([Marxists Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/bloch/hope/introduction.htm)). The ontological formula is "S is not yet P," and the practical upshot is concrete utopia, hope "set on its feet and connected to the Real-Possible," as against abstract utopias set outside history ([SEP, Ernst Bloch](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bloch/); [Thesis Eleven](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14409917.2024.2431789)).

### Marcel's disposition

Gabriel Marcel's distinction, carried in R087, separates hope from prediction: hope is the orientation toward a future that is not guaranteed but possible, "not optimism (the belief that things will turn out well) but the refusal to despair in the face of uncertainty" (the compendium's R087, citing Marcel, The Mystery of Being).

### In the compendium

R087 practises hope as a disposition and R125 applies it to the ecological future, where the test case lives: working for a future that is not guaranteed, planting trees under whose shade one will never sit. The Stoic caution in the seed's citation list, Epictetus on the passions, marks the classical worry that hope is a passion to be examined; Spinoza's Ethics III and Aquinas's theological virtue carry the classical analyses, and Camus's Sisyphus the limit case.

One-pointedness is the Buddhist description of a gathered mind, the English rendering of the Pali term cittas' ekaggata. The Wikipedia article on samadhi, quoting the scholar Richard Shankman, gives the rendering and its double sense:

> "the related term cittas' ekaggata may be rendered as 'one-pointedness,' fixated on a single object, but also as 'unification of mind,' in which mind becomes very still but does not merge with the object of attention, and is thus able to observe and gain insight into the changing flow of experience." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi))

The article records the same double sense for samadhi itself, which "may be viewed as 'an exclusive focus on a single object'", but also as "a broader state of awareness in which the mind remains steady and unmoving, yet aware of a wide range of phenomena around the meditation object".

### What the term asks

One-pointedness is not tunnel vision on this rendering. The narrower sense fixes the mind on a single object; the wider sense describes a mind that becomes very still, does not merge with what it attends to, and observes the changing flow of experience. The same term carries both, which is why the rendering matters: a reader who takes only the first sense hears a narrowing, and a reader who takes the second hears an opening.

The article's sentence about samadhi makes the same point at one remove: it describes the state either as "an exclusive focus on a single object", or as a steadiness of mind that remains "aware of a wide range of phenomena around the meditation object". A practice instruction built on the word can mean either of these, and the difference between them is the difference between a mind that is narrowed and a mind that is gathered.

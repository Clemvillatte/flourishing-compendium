The working memory model is Alan Baddeley and Graham Hitch's 1974 multicomponent account of how the mind holds and manipulates information over seconds ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/)). Its four-part shape is phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive and, from 2000, episodic buffer.

> "We proposed the multicomponent model as a deliberately simplistic interpretation. It assumes the extra component is a limited capacity 'central executive,' responsible for attention-demanding control processes necessary in many aspects of cognition. We characterised the STM component as an 'articulatory loop' in which stored information decays rapidly but can be refreshed by subvocal rehearsal." (Hitch, Allen and Baddeley, [The multicomponent model of working memory fifty years on](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology 78(2):222-239, published online 8 November 2024, DOI 10.1177/17470218241290909, section "Origins of the multicomponent model")

### The components

The verbal store they called an articulatory loop, later the phonological loop, holds speech-based information that decays rapidly unless refreshed by subvocal rehearsal ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), section "Origins of the multicomponent model"). A second store was needed for visual material: "there already existed a growing body of evidence indicating the need to include at least one other component storing visuospatial information" ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), section "Developing the multicomponent model"). Above both sits the central executive, "a novel concept about which little was said initially beyond the claim that its limited capacity resources could be allocated flexibly between control processes and temporary storage" ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), section "Origins of the multicomponent model").

### The episodic buffer

The model grew a fourth component through an argument about capacity ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), section "Developing the multicomponent model"). The review's words: "the immediate recall of a series of words vastly exceeds the capacity of the phonological loop when they form a meaningful sentence", which "suggests some form of integrated storage, in this case drawing on LTM". "These considerations led to a major extension of the multicomponent model to include an episodic buffer, seen as a limited capacity store for integrated, multimodal representations, accessed by conscious awareness" ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), section "Developing the multicomponent model").

### The honest limit

The authors call the model "a deliberately simplistic interpretation" and record that the central executive "was wide open to criticism for being little more than a label for the homunculus", while noting in its defence that it "did succeed in emphasising the importance of internally driven attentional control processes in working memory and, in so doing, setting the agenda for further investigation" ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), section "Origins of the multicomponent model").

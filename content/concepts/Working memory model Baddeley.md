The working memory model is Alan Baddeley and Graham Hitch's 1974 multicomponent account of how the mind holds and manipulates information over seconds. Its four-part shape, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, central executive and, from 2000, episodic buffer, has been the field's reference model for fifty years ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/)).

> "We proposed the multicomponent model as a deliberately simplistic interpretation. It assumes the extra component is a limited capacity 'central executive,' responsible for attention-demanding control processes necessary in many aspects of cognition. We characterised the STM component as an 'articulatory loop' in which stored information decays rapidly but can be refreshed by subvocal rehearsal." (Hitch, Allen and Baddeley, [The multicomponent model of working memory fifty years on](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/), Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology, 2024)

### The components

The verbal store they called an articulatory loop, later the phonological loop, holds speech-based information that decays rapidly unless refreshed by subvocal rehearsal. A second store was needed for visual material: "there already existed a growing body of evidence indicating the need to include at least one other component storing visuospatial information" ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/)). Above both sits the central executive, "a novel concept about which little was said initially beyond the claim that its limited capacity resources could be allocated flexibly between control processes and temporary storage".

### The episodic buffer

The model grew a fourth component when the two stores proved too small for what people can recall. The immediate recall of words "vastly exceeds the capacity of the phonological loop when they form a meaningful sentence", which "suggests some form of integrated storage, in this case drawing on LTM". "These considerations led to a major extension of the multicomponent model to include an episodic buffer, seen as a limited capacity store for integrated, multimodal representations, accessed by conscious awareness" ([Hitch, Allen and Baddeley 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11783980/)).

### The honest limit

The authors call the model "a deliberately simplistic interpretation", and the fifty-years review is candid about what the executive is not: the framework has stayed stable while the specific models built inside it kept changing. The components are functional labels sustained by dissociation experiments, not localized brain organs, and rival single-capacity accounts remain active competitors.

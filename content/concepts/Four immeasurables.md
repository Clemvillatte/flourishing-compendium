The four immeasurables (Pali appamaññā; Sanskrit apramāṇa, "boundlessness, infinitude, a state that is illimitable") are the Buddhist set of four virtues and the meditations that cultivate them: loving-kindness (metta), compassion (karuna), sympathetic joy (mudita) and equanimity (upekkha) ([Wikipedia, Brahmavihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmavihara)). They are also called the brahmavihārā, "divine abidings," attitudes the god Brahma is said to dwell in ([Insight Meditation Center](https://www.insightmeditationcenter.org/books-articles/the-four-faces-of-love-the-brahma-viharas/)). The Karaṇīyametta Sutta gives them their canonical image: "even as a mother protects with her life her child, her only child, so with a boundless heart should one cherish all living beings" ([Abhayagiri Monastery](https://www.abhayagiri.org/reflections/1138-brahma-viharas)).

### Four stances, one system

Each immeasurable is a stance toward a condition. Metta is active goodwill that wishes wellbeing; karuna is metta meeting pain, the wish that suffering end; mudita is metta meeting good fortune, joy in another's happiness even when one contributed nothing to it; upekkha is even-mindedness where one has no role in the other's welfare ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmavihara); [Insight Meditation Center](https://www.insightmeditationcenter.org/books-articles/the-four-faces-of-love-the-brahma-viharas/)). The cultivated state has a canonical formula: "extensive, expanded, limitless, free from hatred and ill-will" ([Insight Meditation Center](https://www.insightmeditationcenter.org/books-articles/the-four-faces-of-love-the-brahma-viharas/)).

The tradition treats them as trainable rather than inborn, each with its own practice, and names their opposites: mudita is "the opposite of jealousy or envy," and the direct enemy of metta is anger, of karuna cruelty ([Buddhanet, Metta and the Other Brahma Viharas](https://www.buddhanet.net/mettab5/)). Karuna and mudita balance each other, one facing the darker side of life and one the brighter.

### Why immeasurable

The name marks scope, not intensity: the attitudes are extended toward an immeasurable range of beings, with no cap on the number of objects ([Free Buddhist Audio, Brahmaviharas retreat notes](https://www.freebuddhistaudio.com/texts/othertexts/Tejananda/FBA86_Brahmaviharas_Retreat_-_Notes.pdf)). The Metta Sutta ties their full cultivation to rebirth in the Brahma realm ([Britannica, brahmavihāra](https://www.britannica.com/topic/brahmavihara)); the Mahayana traditions fold them into bodhicitta, the mind set on awakening for the sake of all beings.

### In the compendium

The compendium's carrier is Y065, "Cultivate the fourfold stance toward people," which teaches the closely parallel yogic formulation, "friendliness toward the happy, compassion toward the unhappy, delight toward the virtuous, and even-minded disregard" toward the hostile, a comparison between traditions rather than a single doctrine. Y114 applies it: match the stance to the other's condition, friendliness against envy, compassion against pity, delight against competitive comparison. AF055 works with envy, mudita's enemy, by reading it as a signal of value; SP022 pairs gratitude with the repayment of love; SP073 guards cheerfulness under loss; SO001, M042, K362 and A003 carry the four into social flourishing, giving, courage and virtue.

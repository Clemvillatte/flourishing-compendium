The exemplary person is the Analects' term for a person formed by virtue rather than by birth, office, or learning alone. This page quotes the translation opened here, which renders the term "superior man".

### The contour of the character

The Analects draws the figure through small scenes rather than definitions:

> "Of 'the superior man,' the Master observed, 'In him there is no contentiousness. Say even that he does certainly contend with others, as in archery competitions; yet mark, in that case, how courteously he will bow and go up for the forfeit-cup, and come down again and give it to his competitor. In his very contest he is still the superior man.'" (Confucius, Analects, in [Chinese Literature, ed. Epiphanius Wilson, Project Gutenberg #10056](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/10056/pg10056.txt))

And through what the person carries with them everywhere:

> "Not even whilst he eats his meal will the 'superior man' forget what he owes to his fellow-men. Even in hurried leave-takings, even in moments of frantic confusion, he keeps true to this virtue." (same work)

The same stretch of the text adds the social corollary: "Virtue dwells not alone: she must have neighbors." The exemplary person is not a solitary perfectionist; the practice is always owed to others. The scenes are small ones, and the smallness is the method: an archery match, a meal, a hurried departure. My reading is that virtue shows in the handling of forfeits and leave-takings before it shows in anything grand, which is what makes the figure imitable rather than remote.

### Here

I read the figure as the answer to the question "who should I become?", and pairs it with its counterparts in other traditions.

- [[A043 — Develop phronesis — practical wisdom]] — Aristotle's practical wisdom, the capacity defined by its work in the Nicomachean Ethics
- [[Y116 — Be the portrait]] — the Gita's portrait of the person dear to Krishna
- [[A120 — Understand emulation (zelos)]] — the Rhetoric's account of emulation, the pain that good fortune is not one's own
- [[S126 — Practise the contemplation of exemplary lives]] — Marcus's ledger of debts to his teachers

Loneliness is the subjective, distressing experience of feeling alone or socially disconnected: a perceived gap between one's desired and actual social relationships. It is distinct from objective social isolation: one can be alone without feeling lonely, and lonely within a crowd.

### The signal and its costs

As a biological and social signal, loneliness evolved to motivate reconnection, much as hunger motivates eating: it is an aversive state that promotes survival by keeping individuals within protective groups. Chronic loneliness, however, dysregulates stress physiology (HPA-axis activation, elevated cortisol), impairs sleep, and carries mortality risk. The Holt-Lunstad meta-analysis gives the scale:

> "Across 148 studies (308,849 participants), the random effects weighted average effect size was OR = 1.50 (95% CI 1.42 to 1.59), indicating a 50% increased likelihood of survival for participants with stronger social relationships. ... These findings indicate that the influence of social relationships on the risk of death are comparable with well-established risk factors for mortality such as smoking and alcohol consumption and exceed the influence of other risk factors such as physical inactivity and obesity." (Holt-Lunstad et al. 2010, [PLoS Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2910600/))

### The treatment finding

Research distinguishes emotional loneliness (absence of an intimate attachment) from social loneliness (absence of a broader engaging network), and highlights that the quality of relationships matters more than their quantity. The Masi meta-analysis of interventions identified four strategies (improving social skills, enhancing social support, increasing opportunities for social contact, and addressing maladaptive social cognition), and its verdict on the randomized comparison studies:

> "Among studies that used the latter design, the most successful interventions addressed maladaptive social cognition." (Masi et al. 2011, [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20716644/))

The "latter design" is randomized comparison: interventions that work with the heightened threat-vigilance and negative self-appraisals lonely individuals carry into interactions outperform those that merely increase social contact.

### The contemplative contrast

Cross-traditionally, solitude is often reframed: contemplative traditions distinguish involuntary loneliness (dukkha, a form of suffering) from chosen solitude as a condition for reflection and depth. The examined response is neither denial nor surrender but honest acknowledgment of the signal, corrective action where possible, and [[Equanimity|equanimity where not]].

### In everyday life

One honest look per week at the gap: which relationships are wanted, which are had, and one small message sent to the truest gap. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

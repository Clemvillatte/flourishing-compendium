Seriousness, in Simone de Beauvoir's The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947), names an evasion: the attitude that treats values as ready-made things found in the world, so that one's freedom never has to found them.

> "the characteristic of the spirit of seriousness is to consider values as ready-made things" (de Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, [ch. II](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch02.htm)).

### What the serious man does

The mechanism is a trade: security for freedom. "The serious man gets rid of his freedom by claiming to subordinate it to values which would be unconditioned. He imagines that the accession to these values likewise permanently confers value upon himself. Shielded with 'rights,' he fulfills himself as a being who is escaping from the stress of existence" (The Ethics of Ambiguity). The disguise reaches down: "The thing that matters to the serious man is not so much the nature of the object which he prefers to himself, but rather the fact of being able to lose himself in it" (The Ethics of Ambiguity). And the attitude requires upkeep: "He chooses to live in an infantile world, but to the child the values are really given. The serious man must mask the movement by which he gives them to himself, like the mythomaniac who while reading a love-letter pretends to forget that she has sent it to herself" (The Ethics of Ambiguity).

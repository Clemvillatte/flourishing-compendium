The interconnected good is the Stoic teaching, worked out in [[T014 — Marcus Aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]]'s Meditations under the name sympatheia, that the world is one woven system and that a person's good is therefore never a private possession. The emperor's own formulation:

> "All things are implicated with one another, and the bond is holy; and there is hardly anything unconnected with any other thing. For things have been co-ordinated, and they combine to form the same universe [order]." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 7.9, trans. George Long, [Lexundria](https://lexundria.com/m_aur_med/7.9/lg))

### What the chapter carries

The passage continues: "For there is one universe made up of all things, and one god who pervades all things, and one substance, and one law, [one] common reason in all intelligent animals, and one truth." The interconnection is not an ornament on the ethics; it is the physics from which the ethics follows. An earlier book gives the practice instruction: "Frequently consider the connection of all things in the universe and their relation to one another. For in a manner all things are implicated with one another, and all in this way are friendly to one another; for one thing comes in order after another, and this is by virtue of the active movement and mutual conspiration and the unity of the substance" (Meditations 6.38, trans. George Long, [Lexundria](https://lexundria.com/m_aur_med/6.38/lg)). My principle B157 carries the same logic: protecting others and protecting yourself are one act, because the parties are parts of one body. What the passages do not give: empirical measurement; the claim is a metaphysical picture from which practice follows, and its modern testing is a matter for research on connectedness, not for Marcus.

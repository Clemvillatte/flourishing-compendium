A mediator, in the research sense process-based therapy leans on, is the variable a treatment actually moves that then produces the outcome: the mechanism through which therapy works. The PBT manual quotes the classic definition from Baron and Kenny's 1986 paper:

> "the generative mechanism through which the focal independent variable is able to influence the dependent variable of interest" (Baron & Kenny, 1986, p. 1173, quoted in Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, 2021, Learning Process-Based Therapy, Chapter 3)

PBT's research program is organised around a mediational question: not "did the package beat the control condition?" but "which process did the intervention change, and did changing that process carry the outcome?" The book's kernel standard asks that a change method be linked to evidence-based processes of change — a mediation path is what such linkage looks like when the evidence supports it.

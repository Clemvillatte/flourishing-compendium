“Faith seeking understanding” — Anselm, Proslogion.

Fides quaerens intellectum was the original title Anselm gave the Proslogion. The formula is often read as a quarrel between faith and reason, but its own wording is a description of how understanding starts: with a commitment already made, held open, and asking to be understood more fully.

> "For I do not seek to understand that I may believe, but I believe in order to understand. For this also I believe, --that unless I believed, I should not understand." — [Anselm, Proslogium, ch. I (Deane translation, 1903)](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/basis/anselm-proslogium.asp)

### What the formula claims

The preceding lines frame the whole: "I do not endeavor, O Lord, to penetrate your sublimity, for in no wise do I compare my understanding with that; but I long to understand in some degree your truth, which my heart believes and loves." Belief is not the rival of inquiry but its fuel — the believer already cares about the object, and caring is what sustains the long work of understanding. The structure generalises past theology. A person who commits to a practice, a person, or a tradition before fully understanding it has not stopped thinking; they have taken the posture in which thinking about it can go deep at all. The maxim's honest reading requires its companion warning: faith that refuses all correction stops being a seeker.

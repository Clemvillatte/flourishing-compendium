Incubation is the effect in problem solving where setting a problem aside, rather than grinding on it, improves the chance of a solution. The phenomenon is old in anecdote and hard to pin down in the lab, which is what the meta-analytic verdict below settles.

> "The authors identified a positive incubation effect, with divergent thinking tasks benefiting more than linguistic and visual insight tasks from incubation. Longer preparation periods gave a greater incubation effect, whereas filling an incubation period with high cognitive demand tasks gave a smaller incubation effect." — [Sio & Ormerod, Psychological Bulletin 135(1) (2009), 94–120, full text](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/2009-sio.pdf)

### What the conditions are

The moderator pattern carries the practical instruction my principle draws. Incubation is real but conditional: it helps divergent thinking most, it works better after a longer preparation period, so the break should follow real work rather than replace it, and the break should be filled with something undemanding rather than another demanding task. A walk beats a spreadsheet.

Self-regulated learning theory is Barry Zimmerman's account of how effective learners manage their own learning: they plan before, monitor during, and reflect after. Zimmerman's compact statement of what the capacity is appears in his 2002 overview.

> "Self-regulation is not a mental ability or an academic performance skill; rather it is the self-directive process by which learners transform their mental abilities into academic skills." (Zimmerman, [Becoming a Self-Regulated Learner: An Overview](http://www.johnnietfeld.com/uploads/2/2/6/0/22606800/zimmerman_2002.pdf), Theory Into Practice 41(2), 2002, p. 65)

### The cycle

The theory gives the process a three-part loop.

> "Psychologists view the structure of self-regulatory processes in terms of three cyclical phases. The forethought phase refers to processes and beliefs that occur before efforts to learn; the performance phase refers to processes that occur during behavioral implementation, and self-reflection refers to processes that occur after each learning effort." (same article)

The cycle is what makes it a learning system rather than a set of study tips: self-reflections from earlier efforts feed the next round of forethought, and learners who fail to set specific goals or self-monitor fall into reactive self-regulation instead. Zimmerman also frames the learner's stance in one line: learning is "an activity that students do for themselves in a proactive way rather than as a covert event that happens to them in reaction to teaching".

The Dreaming is the term for the Aboriginal Australian account of the world's origin and ongoing order, and W.E.H. Stanner's 1953 essay is the standard English-language treatment. His own summary of the concept:

> "A central meaning of The Dreaming is that of a sacred, heroic time long ago when man and nature came to be as they are; but neither 'time' nor 'history' as we understand them is involved in the meaning. [...] One cannot 'fix' The Dreaming in time: it was, and is, everywhen."

([Stanner, "The Dreaming," White Man Got No Dreaming, pp. 23-24, quoted in the IRWLE essay on Kim Scott](https://www.worldlitonline.net/issues/vol-9-no-2/articles/magic-realism-kim-scott-true-country/).)

### The everywhen

"Everywhen" is Stanner's coinage for the temporal structure: not a distant past but a timeless reality where past, present, and future coexist. The Australian Dictionary of Biography's summary of his work: "In 1979 Stanner published White Man Got No Dreaming — an eloquent exposition of Dreaming understanding — that included the powerful 'it was, and is, everywhen'" ([ADB, Stanner biography](https://adb.anu.edu.au/biography/stanner-william-edward-bill-15541)). Stanner's fuller statement of the concept's content:

> "Clearly, The Dreaming is many things in one. Among them, a kind of narrative of things that once happened; a kind of charter of things that still happen; and a kind of logos or principle of order transcending everything significant for Aboriginal man. [...] It is a cosmogony, an account of begetting the universe, a study about creation. It is also a cosmology, an account or theory of how that was created became an ordered system. To be more precise, how the universe became a moral system."

([Stanner 1979, pp. 23-24, 28, quoted in the IRWLE essay](https://www.worldlitonline.net/issues/vol-9-no-2/articles/magic-realism-kim-scott-true-country/).)

### The translation limit

Stanner gives the caveat in his own text: the word "suffers badly by translation into our dry and abstract language," and he records the elder Muta's formulation of the gap: "White man got no dreaming, Him go 'nother way. White man, him go different. Him got road belong himself" ([quoted in the Taylor & Francis paper](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504622.2025.2527895)). The compendium's principles on connection to spirituality and respect for the Dreaming hold the concept with that limit in view: the English rendering is Stanner's scholarly account, not the concept itself.

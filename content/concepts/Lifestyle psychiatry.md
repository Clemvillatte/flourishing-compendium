Lifestyle psychiatry names the use of health behaviors, exercise, diet and sleep, as levers for preventing and treating psychiatric disorders. Joseph Firth, Philip Ward and Brendon Stubbs introduce the field:

> "As the interface between physical and mental health becomes more widely acknowledged, the role of 'lifestyle factors' (i.e., health behaviors, such as physical activity, diet, and sleep) in the onset and treatment of psychiatric disorders is also gaining increasing interest." ([Firth, Ward and Stubbs, Frontiers in Psychiatry, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6717907/))

### The evidence lines

The editorial, which popularized the term from Noordsy, points to meta-analytic support on each front: "The role of physical activity in psychiatry is particularly well researched, with a number of recent meta-analyses showing that physical activity can aid in the prevention and management of multiple symptoms of mental illness." On diet: "the potential impact of nutrition on mental health is also gaining increasing recognition due to large-scale meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) showing that both dietary interventions and certain nutrient supplements can significantly reduce symptoms of various psychiatric disorders." The field's later meta-review consolidated the evidence for targeting physical activity, smoking, diet and sleep across depression, anxiety, schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and ADHD.

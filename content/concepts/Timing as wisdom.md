Timing as wisdom is the Daoist claim that knowing when matters as much as knowing what. Daodejing 64 grounds the claim in an observation about things: at rest they are easy to maintain; situations that have not yet emerged are easy to plan for ([Robert Eno, Daodejing ch. 64](https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Daodejing-Eno.pdf)).

> "When things are at rest they are easy to maintain; when situations have not yet emerged they are easy to plan for. When brittle, things are easy to split; when minute things are easy to disperse. Deal with things before they occur; order things before they are disordered."
> — [Robert Eno, Daodejing ch. 64](https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Daodejing-Eno.pdf)

The same chapter follows with the failure of late action: "When people pursue an undertaking, it is always at the point of success that they ruin it. Attend at the end as you did at the start and you will have no failures" ([Robert Eno, Daodejing ch. 64](https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Daodejing-Eno.pdf)). Wisdom here is not more cleverness about outcomes; it is knowing which phase of the situation you are in.

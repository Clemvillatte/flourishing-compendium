Iris Mauss and colleagues' 2011 paper asked whether wanting happiness too much can undermine it. Their finding: the more people value happiness, the more likely they are to feel disappointed when their feelings fall short, and the worse they fare precisely when circumstances are good.

> "Paradoxically, therefore, valuing happiness may lead people to be less happy just when happiness is within reach." (Mauss, Tamir, Anderson & Savino, "Can seeking happiness make people unhappy? Paradoxical effects of valuing happiness", Emotion 11(4) 2011, [abstract via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21517168/))

### What the studies found

In the paper's first study, female participants who valued happiness more reported lower happiness under conditions of low, but not high, life stress. In the second, participants induced to value happiness reacted less positively to a happy, but not a sad, emotion induction, an effect mediated by disappointment at their own feelings. The abstract states the mechanism plainly: "the more people value happiness, the more likely they will feel disappointed", particularly "in positive situations, in which people have every reason to be happy".

**Bayesian brain** — the brain is a prediction machine that continuously updates a generative model of the world from sensory error.

### What the hypothesis says

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on cognitive science summarises the Bayesian approach:

> "The Bayesian approach assumes that cognition is approximately optimal in accord with probability theory, especially Bayes' theorem, which says that the probability of a hypothesis given evidence is equal to the result of multiplying the prior probability of the hypothesis by the conditional probability of the evidence given the hypothesis, all divided by the probability of the evidence." (Thagard, [SEP: Cognitive Science](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cognitive-science/))

The same entry notes that Bayesian methods have had impressive applications to learning, vision, motor control, language and social cognition, but that their psychological plausibility is debatable because of the assumptions about optimality and probability-theoretic computation.

### The predictive-processing version

The Bayesian-brain idea is most often worked out as predictive processing. The SEP entry states the version most relevant here:

> "Predictive processing is an approach to theoretical neuroscience that views the brain as constantly generating and updating models of the environment in order to predict the results of perceptions and actions." (Thagard, [SEP: Cognitive Science](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cognitive-science/))

The entry adds that active inference is a version of predictive processing that hypothesises the brain uses Bayesian calculations to minimise free energy, consisting of discrepancies between expectations and actual observations, and that organisms survive when brains reduce prediction errors by changing their models of the environment or by changing the environment through action. The entry also records challenges: whether brain functioning is really Bayesian updating rather than connectionist constraint satisfaction or deep reinforcement learning, and whether the account subsumes pattern recognition, explanation, emotional evaluation, memory and communication.

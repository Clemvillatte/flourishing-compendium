Boundary situations are Karl Jaspers' term (Grenzsituationen) for the fixed limits of human life: death, suffering, struggle, chance and guilt. Unlike ordinary situations, they cannot be solved, managed or outgrown; they remain "essentially the same even when their momentary aspect changes," so that the work of a life is not removing them but taking a stance toward them. Jaspers' summary of the two possible reactions:

> "But to ultimate situations we react either by obfuscation or, if we really apprehend them, by despair and rebirth: we become ourselves by a change in our consciousness of being." (Jaspers, Way to Wisdom, ch. II, tr. Manheim, p. 20, in [Epimelea](https://epimelea.com/library/jaspers-boundary-situations/))

### What the situations carry

The list is deliberately unglamorous: I must die, I must suffer, I must struggle, I am subject to chance, I cannot avoid becoming guilty. These are not problems with solutions but conditions of being a person among persons, and Jaspers' claim is that a philosophy which refuses them stays shallow, while one that faces them can convert the collision into self-knowledge — the "despair and rebirth" of the quoted line. The situations are boundary in the geographic sense: what lies beyond them cannot be surveyed, only marked. My principle E091 carries the practical reading: practise limit situations, meaning deliberate exposure to the facts one ordinarily keeps out of sight, since the obfuscation reaction is the default and costs more than the facing does. A compendium exercise drawn from the concept is to name one boundary situation one is currently managing rather than facing, and to write down what the management is protecting. What the concept does not give: a promise that facing the limit ends the pain; Jaspers' claim is about becoming oneself, not about relief.

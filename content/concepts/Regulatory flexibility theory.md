Regulatory flexibility theory is George Bonanno's account of what makes coping healthy: the capacity to deploy strategies flexibly and switch them when they fail. The theory has a named target, the assumption it was built against: "the tendency to assign a value judgment about the consistent efficacy or consistent lack of efficacy of a particular regulatory strategy as the fallacy of uniform efficacy" ([Bonanno and Burton, Regulatory Flexibility, Perspectives on Psychological Science 2013](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/gab38/faculty-profile/files/2013_Bonanno_Burton_REGULATORY_FLEXIBLITY.pdf)). No strategy is always right. The same expressive suppression that costs intimacy in one relationship is the courtesy that carries a person through a funeral. What predicts adjustment is the fit between strategy and situation, and the skill of noticing when the fit has broken.

### The three-part sequence

The theory breaks flexibility into three sequential components. First, context sensitivity: "the ability to perceive impinging demands and opportunities from the situational context as they emerge over and above the normative background of ongoing regulatory concerns and processes and to determine the most appropriate regulatory strategy in response to those demands or opportunities." Second, repertoire: "the ability to utilize a wide range of regulatory strategies that might accommodate divergent contextual demands and opportunities." Third, feedback: "the ability to monitor and use feedback about the efficacy of a chosen regulatory strategy over time so as to adjust or correct behavior when needed" (all Bonanno and Burton 2013).

### The evidence

The anchor study measured both directions of expression. Participants "viewed emotionally evocative photos" while an observer in another room tried to guess their emotions, under three rotating instructions: behave normally, enhance expression, or suppress it. The result: "Controlling for baseline distress, the ability to enhance emotional expression and the ability to suppress emotional expression each independently predicted reduced distress 2 years after 9/11" ([Bonanno and Burton 2013](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/gab38/faculty-profile/files/2013_Bonanno_Burton_REGULATORY_FLEXIBLITY.pdf)). Being able to do either one, as the occasion asked, beat being good at either one alone.

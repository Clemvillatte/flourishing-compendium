Intolerance of uncertainty is a dispositional incapacity to endure the aversive response triggered by uncertain situations. Dugas and colleagues' model gives intolerance of uncertainty a key role in the development and maintenance of worry. A 2016 article describing the model states:

> The Intolerance of Uncertainty Model (IUM) of Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) attributes a key role to Intolerance of Uncertainty (IU), and additional roles to Positive Beliefs about Worry (PBW), Negative Problem Orientation (NPO), and Cognitive Avoidance (CA), in the development and maintenance of worry, the core feature of GAD.
>
> (Bottesi, Ghisi, Carraro, Barclay, Payne and Freeston, "Revising the Intolerance of Uncertainty Model of Generalized Anxiety Disorder", Frontiers in Psychology, 2016, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5088195/))

The article defines IU as the "individual's dispositional incapacity to endure the aversive response triggered by the perceived absence of salient, key, or sufficient information, and sustained by the associated perception of uncertainty" (Carleton 2016, quoted in Bottesi et al. 2016), and reports that individuals high in IU "find situations that are uncertain threatening, upsetting, and undesirable, regardless of the actual probability of a negative event to occur (Dugas et al., 1998)." The same article reports "a fast growing interest in the role of IU as a shared vulnerability factor for emotional disorders" (Bottesi et al. 2016).

### Why uncertainty produces worry

The mechanism the model proposes is a coping substitution: "Negative beliefs about uncertainty usually interfere with the individual's ability to effectively deal with these situations and promote the use of worry as a dysfunctional strategy to cope with or prevent feared outcomes (Dugas et al., 1998; Behar et al., 2009)" (Bottesi et al. 2016). The shared-vulnerability evidence has grown beyond GAD: "evidence supporting its involvement in several psychopathologies has been reported" across the emotional disorders (Bottesi et al. 2016).

### How well the model holds

The revision test came back mixed: "the original conceptualization of the model, claiming that IU contributes to worry and anxiety through PBW, NPO, and CA is substantially supported but only in the Italian undergraduate sample, whereas findings emerged from the UK one are controversial" (Bottesi et al. 2016). The authors' refinement "provide[s] support to the current refinement of the original Laval model, where the IUM components are better conceptually integrated" (Bottesi et al. 2016). The clinical upshot: "understanding how people of different cultures understand and express uncertainty and IU may be central in our ability to target it effectively in therapy" (Bottesi et al. 2016).

## Facets

- **scale** — the original IUS (Freeston et al., 1994) and the IUS-12 (Carleton et al., 2007), "a 12-item self-report measure" whose items "were derived from the original IUS" (Bottesi et al. 2016)
- **as a risk factor** — the key role the IUM gives IU in the development and maintenance of worry (Bottesi et al. 2016)
- **as a transdiagnostic risk factor** — Bottesi et al., 2016

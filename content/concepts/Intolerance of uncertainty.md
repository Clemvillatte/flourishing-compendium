Intolerance of uncertainty is a dispositional incapacity to endure the aversive response triggered by uncertain situations. In the model developed by Dugas and colleagues it is the central process behind chronic worry. A later review of the model states:

> The Intolerance of Uncertainty Model (IUM) of Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) attributes a key role to Intolerance of Uncertainty (IU), and additional roles to Positive Beliefs about Worry (PBW), Negative Problem Orientation (NPO), and Cognitive Avoidance (CA), in the development and maintenance of worry, the core feature of GAD.
>
> (Bottesi, Ghisi, Carraro, Barclay, Payne and Freeston, "Revising the Intolerance of Uncertainty Model of Generalized Anxiety Disorder", Frontiers in Psychology, 2016, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5088195/))

The article defines IU as the "individual's dispositional incapacity to endure the aversive response triggered by the perceived absence of salient, key, or sufficient information, and sustained by the associated perception of uncertainty" (Carleton 2016, quoted in Bottesi et al. 2016), and reports that individuals high in IU "find situations that are uncertain threatening, upsetting, and undesirable, regardless of the actual probability of a negative event to occur (Dugas et al., 1998)." The same article notes a growing interest in IU "as a shared vulnerability factor for emotional disorders," which is the transdiagnostic reading the facets below record.

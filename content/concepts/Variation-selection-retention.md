Variation, selection and retention are the evolutionary engine that process-based therapy borrows from biology and applies to psychological change: behaviours and mental patterns vary, contexts select some variants, and the selected ones are retained as habits. The PBT manual compresses the scheme into an acronym:

> "six key concepts needed in an evolutionary approach, which can be expressed in the acronym VRSCDL (pronounced as the word “versatile”), which stands for Variation and Retention of what is Selected in Context at the right Dimension and Level." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, 2021, Learning Process-Based Therapy, Chapter 3)

Read the acronym as a checklist rather than a slogan. Each letter names one thing to examine: is the client's repertoire still producing variants (variation), what is the environment currently reinforcing (selection), which variants have hardened into habit (retention), and in what setting does all this unfold (context)? The last two letters place the examination on the EEMM's grid: its six psychological dimensions (affect, cognition, attention, self, motivation, overt behavior) and, nested around them, what the book calls "two additional levels of selection (biophysiological and sociocultural)" — dimensions and levels mark where in the person or the situation a pattern and its selection pressures are to be looked for. The clinical reading this invites is investigative: when a pattern is rigid, the questions the scheme poses are whether variation around it has collapsed, whether the context keeps selecting it, and whether retention has consolidated it. Change, on this model, means reopening variation, shifting what the context selects, and helping retention work for the adaptive variant instead — hypotheses to test in the case, not conclusions to draw from rigidity alone.

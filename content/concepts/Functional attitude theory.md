Functional attitude theory asks what an attitude is for. Daniel Katz's 1960 paper opens its abstract with the answer:

> "At the psychological level the reasons for holding or for changing attitudes are found in the functions they perform for the individual, specifically the functions of adjustment, ego defense, value expression, and knowledge." ([Katz, 1960, Public Opinion Quarterly](https://academic.oup.com/poq/article-abstract/24/2/163/1884959?login=false))

### Two classic taxonomies

Katz named four functions: knowledge, utilitarian, ego-defensive and value-expressive. Smith, Bruner and White (1956) proposed object appraisal, externalization and social adjustment. A need-based persuasion paper explains that "the object-appraisal can be seen as a combination of Katz' knowledge function and utilitarian function; and the externalization function is very similar to Katz's ego-defensive function" ([University of Hong Kong repository](https://hub.hku.hk/bitstream/10722/127187/1/Content.pdf?accept=1)). Most current researchers, a social-psychology lecture summary says, treat "knowledge, ego-defensive, value- expressive, social-adjustive, and utilitarian" as the main functions ([CRISP, University of Iowa](https://crisp.org.uiowa.edu/sites/crisp.org.uiowa.edu/files/2020-04/5.21.pdf)).

### What each function does

The Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Psychology gives the plain reading:

- The utilitarian function "focuses on maximizing rewards and minimizing punishments from attitude objects".
- The ego-defensive function "serves to protect an individual's self-concept against internal conflict".
- The social adjustment function "helps people to identify with others they like and to distance themselves from others they dislike".
- The value-expressive function "indicates that attitudes express an individual's self-concept and values" ([Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Psychology, "Attitudes"](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62246/chapter/550953525)).

### Changing attitudes means matching the function

Katz's abstract states that "The conditions necessary to arouse or modify an attitude vary according to the motivational basis of the attitude". For ego-defensive attitudes he lists "removal of threat, catharsis, and self-insight" as ways to change them. Expressive attitudes can be changed "by showing the appropriateness of the new or modified beliefs to the self-concept". The later prediction drawn from this, called the matching hypothesis, is that persuasion works better when it matches the functional basis of the targeted attitude (University of Hong Kong repository, above).

### One behaviour, different functions

Clary and colleagues put the core functionalist point this way: "people can and do perform the same actions in the service of different psychological functions", so the same attitude can serve different functions for different people ([Clary et al., Journal of Personality and Social Psychology](https://users.nber.org/~rdehejia/!@$DRM/Lecture%2009/supplemental/Clary%20et%20al.pdf)).

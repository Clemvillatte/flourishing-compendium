Response inhibition is the stopping of an action that has been triggered or planned. Verbruggen and Logan call it "a hallmark of executive control" and study it with the stop-signal paradigm: subjects perform a go task, and occasionally the go stimulus is followed by a stop signal that instructs them to withhold the response ([Verbruggen and Logan, Response inhibition in the stop-signal paradigm, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2008, PDF](http://www.psy.vanderbilt.edu/faculty/logan/verbruggen_logan_2008_tics.pdf)).

### The race model

Logan and Cowan (1984, "On the ability to inhibit thought and action: A theory of an act of control", Psychological Review 91, 295-327) modelled the result as a race. "When the stop process finishes before the go process, the response is inhibited; when the go processes finishes before the stop process, the response is emitted" (Verbruggen and Logan 2008). A theoretical review adds that "numerous studies have shown that the independent horse-race model of Logan and Cowan (1984) accounts for the data very well" ([Models of Response Inhibition in the Stop-Signal and Stop-Change Paradigms, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2696813/)). The delay between go and stop signals lets researchers estimate the stop-signal reaction time, where "shorter SSRTs are associated with more efficient inhibition" ([Inhibition of Action, Thought, and Emotion, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2396584/)).

### Brain systems

That neurobiological review says response inhibition "depends upon fronto-basal ganglia networks" and that "the right VLPFC is especially critical to inhibition of motor responses", based on patients with right frontal damage showing longer SSRTs. Another review notes the right inferior frontal gyrus is activated and that the patterns have been read as "the engagement of motoric stopping processes, like a brake" ([Understanding and Supporting Inhibitory Control, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11230644/)).

### Is the pause trainable?

The seed says the pause between impulse and act is trainable and that mindfulness strengthens it, citing Logan and Cowan (1984) and Aron (2007). None of the sources I opened tests training or mindfulness. Logan and Cowan's paper is a model of an act of control, so treat the training claim as unverified until a mindfulness or training study is opened. I did not open Aron (2007) directly; a PMC paper cites it (Aron et al., 2007a,b) for the idea that the inferior frontal gyrus may implement stopping via a hyperdirect pathway to the subthalamic nucleus ([Rational Decision-Making in Inhibitory Control, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3103997/)).

Formative assessment is assessment used to change teaching and learning while it is still under way. Paul Black and Dylan Wiliam's 1998 review defined it for their purposes as "all those activities undertaken by teachers, and/or by their students, which provide information to be used as feedback to modify the teaching and learning activities in which they are engaged" (Black and Wiliam, 1998, pp. 7-8, [full text](https://www.gla.ac.uk/t4/learningandteaching/files/PGCTHE/BlackandWiliam1998.pdf)).

### What the 1998 review found

Its abstract says "Several studies show firm evidence that innovations designed to strengthen the frequent feedback that students receive about their learning yield substantial learning gains" ([Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Assessment-and-Classroom-Learning-Black-Wiliam/16141e08389aff8546421d3276c4c33a65c69944)). The authors say the gains "appear to be quite substantial, and as noted above, amongst the largest ever reported for educational interventions" (p. 61). They also report that an emphasis on formative assessment "is of particular benefit to the disadvantaged and low-attaining students".

### Marks and comments

One study the review describes compared feedback types. For the "comments only" group, "the scores improved by about one standard deviation between the first and second sessions". The "comments with grades" group "showed a significant decline in scores across the three sessions". The review also notes that "those given grades had the highest expectations of success, even though they had been significantly less successful than the 'comments' group". So the useful variable is what the feedback tells a learner to do next, not how often a mark is given.

### Is it a theory?

Black and Wiliam later wrote that their 1998 article "was a review of 250 research articles", that it "did not start from a pre-defined theoretical base" and "did not aim to formulate any over-arching theory". They cite their own 1998 conclusion that "building coherent theories, adequate descriptions, and firmly grounded guides to practice, for formative assessment is a formidable undertaking". In 2018 they proposed that any theory of formative assessment be embedded in a wider theory of pedagogy ([Black and Wiliam, 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0969594X.2018.1441807)). The label "theory" in this wiki is therefore a convenience for a body of research, not one agreed theory.

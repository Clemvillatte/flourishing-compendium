Circadian science studies the endogenous near-24-hour clocks that time physiology and behavior. Its human landmark is the finding that the electrically lit constructed environment is associated with reduced daytime sunlight exposure, increased light after sunset, and a delayed circadian clock compared with a week of summer camping under a natural light-dark cycle ([Wright et al. 2013, Current Biology 23(16):1554-1558, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23910656/)).

> "Here we show that electrical lighting and the constructed environment is associated with reduced exposure to sunlight during the day, increased light exposure after sunset, and a delayed timing of the circadian clock as compared to a summer natural 14 hr 40 min:9 hr 20 min light-dark cycle camping." — [Wright et al. 2013, abstract, PubMed 23910656](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23910656/)

### The reset finding

After exposure to only natural light, the internal circadian clock synchronized to solar time: the beginning of the internal biological night occurred at sunset and its end before wake time, just after sunrise ([Wright et al. 2013, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23910656/)). Later chronotypes showed larger circadian advances under natural light, making their internal timing more similar to earlier chronotypes ([Wright et al. 2013, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23910656/)).

> "Furthermore, we find that after exposure to only natural light, the internal circadian clock synchronizes to solar time such that the beginning of the internal biological night occurs at sunset and the end of the internal biological night occurs before wake time just after sunrise." — [Wright et al. 2013, abstract, PubMed 23910656](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23910656/)

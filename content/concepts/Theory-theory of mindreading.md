**Theory-theory of mindreading** — Gopnik, Wellman — children construct a naive theory of mind and revise it like a scientific theory.

The theory-theory holds that reading other minds runs on a body of tacit knowledge rather than direct access. As the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's survey puts it, in this literature the term "theory" refers to "a 'tacit' or 'sub-doxastic' structure of knowledge, a corpus of internally represented information that guides the execution of mentalistic capacities," and the approach treats other agents "as the bearers of unobservable psychological states and processes, and to anticipate and explain the agents' behavior in terms of such states and processes" ([IEP, "Theory of Mind"](https://iep.utm.edu/theomind/), opened).

On the child-scientist version associated with Gopnik and Wellman:

> "According to a first version of TT, 'the child (as little) scientist theory,' the body of internally-represented knowledge that drives the exercise of mentalistic abilities has much the same structure as a scientific theory, and it is acquired, stored, and used in much the same way that scientific theories are: by formulating explanations, making predictions, and then revising the theory or modifying auxiliary hypotheses when the predictions fail." (same source)

The developmental evidence is the false-belief literature: "in general we can confidently say that children begin to successfully perform the ('verbal') false-belief tasks at around 4 years," and on Wellman's reading children pass because they move from an elementary "copy" theory of mind to a fully "representational" theory of mind, which lets them acknowledge the explanatory role of false beliefs. Three-year-olds, who typically fail the tasks, are not deficient in caring but still using the earlier theory.

The compendium's application: the view models understanding other people as a conceptual achievement with a revision history, not a gift. The same humility applies to one's own mind: on this account, one's grasp of one's own inner life is itself theory-mediated, acquired from one's culture rather than read off introspection.

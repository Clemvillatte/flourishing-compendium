Meaning-making is the research tradition's name for the process by which people respond to events that violate what they believe and want: revising the event's meaning, or the global meaning it violated, until the gap between them closes. The organizing model comes from Crystal Park's integrative review of the literature (2010), built on Park and Folkman (1997).

> "The Meaning Making Model is discrepancy-based, that is, it proposes that people's perception of discrepancies between their appraised meaning of a particular situation and their global meaning (i.e., what they believe and desire) (Park, 2010a) creates distress, which in turn gives rise to efforts to reduce the discrepancy and resultant distress." (Park, "The Meaning Making Model," European Health Psychologist 15(2), [PDF](https://www.ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/download/ehp.v15.i2.p40/1041))

### The two levels

The model "identifies two levels of meaning, global and situational (Park & Folkman, 1997)" ([Park, European Health Psychologist](https://www.ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/download/ehp.v15.i2.p40/1041)). Global meaning is the orienting system of beliefs and goals; situational meaning is the appraised significance of a particular event. The work itself is defined as "approach-oriented intrapsychic efforts to reduce discrepancies between appraised and global meaning" ([Park, European Health Psychologist](https://www.ehps.net/ehp/index.php/contents/article/download/ehp.v15.i2.p40/1041)).

### The narrative branch

The page's facets mark the tradition's other streams. The narrative one runs through McAdams's life-story model: narrative identity is "a person's internalized and evolving life story, integrating the reconstructed past and imagined future to provide life with some degree of unity and purpose" (McAdams and McLean 2013, [Narrative Identity](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721413475622)). The page's remaining facets — the durable resource, post-traumatic growth, the creative response to mortality — are kept as inherited citation lines (Calhoun and Tedeschi 2004; Cozzolino 2006; Pennebaker et al. 1997), with their claims made only where opened sources support them.

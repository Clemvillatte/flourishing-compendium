In chapter 6 of the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna describes the yogi's equal vision: the one united in yoga sees the Self in all beings and all beings in the Self ([6.29](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-6-29/)), and the one who sees the Supreme everywhere and everything in the Supreme is never lost to it ([6.30](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-6-30/)). The sequence ends with the test the Gita gives for this vision: recognizing others' happiness and suffering by comparison with one's own. ([Bhagavad Gita 6.29-32](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-6-32/))

> "He is a perfect yogi who, by comparison to his own self, sees the true equality of all beings, in both their happiness and their distress, O Arjuna!" (Bhagavad Gita 6.32, [translation with classical commentaries](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-6-32/))

Shankara's commentary on the page reads the verse through one standard applied to oneself: as happiness is agreeable to me and sorrow unwelcome to me, so they are to all creatures; the yogi who judges all beings this way is the best among yogis. ([commentary of Sri Adi Shankaracharya on the same page](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-6-32/))

Hermeneutic listening is the kind of listening that Gadamer's philosophical hermeneutics makes central to understanding. In Truth and Method he gives the experience of understanding a discipline of its own, and the sentence has become a summary of the whole posture:

> "has its own rigour: that of uninterrupted listening" (Gadamer, Truth and Method, 2nd revised edition, trans. Weinsheimer and Marshall, p. 461, as quoted in [The Hermeneutic Priority of the Question: Cultivating the Hermeneutic Ear](https://apcz.umk.pl/SPI/article/download/SPI.2015.003/8246/23946))

The quote is taken from an open journal article whose footnote 12 cites that page of Truth and Method directly. The fuller sentence there reads: "The hermeneutical experience", Gadamer writes, "has its own rigour: that of uninterrupted listening". The same article elaborates that to re-enact the question of a text, one must first learn to listen perceptively for what is said in it, or for what an interlocutor is speaking about. The listening runs toward the matter itself, and it stays with what the text or the person keeps saying, past the point where you already think you have it.

The compendium's principle on deep listening is B147.

Hermeneutic listening is the kind of listening that Gadamer's philosophical hermeneutics makes central to understanding: not waiting for your turn to speak, but staying open to what a text or another person is actually saying. In Truth and Method he gives the experience of understanding a discipline of its own:

> "The hermeneutical experience", Gadamer writes, "has its own rigour: that of uninterrupted listening." (Gadamer, Truth and Method, 2nd revised edition, trans. Weinsheimer and Marshall, p. 461, as quoted in [The Hermeneutic Priority of the Question: Cultivating the Hermeneutic Ear](https://apcz.umk.pl/SPI/article/download/SPI.2015.003/8246/23946))

The quote is taken from an open journal article that cites the Truth and Method page directly; the same article elaborates that to re-enact the question of a text, one must first learn to listen perceptively for what is said in it, or for what an interlocutor is speaking about.

What makes the listening hermeneutic rather than merely acoustic is that it goes both ways. You listen to the text or the person, and you let what you hear revise the expectations you brought. Interruption here means what interrupts understanding: talking over the other, or hearing only what you already believe. The compendium's principle B147, listen deeply, is the practice this page grounds.

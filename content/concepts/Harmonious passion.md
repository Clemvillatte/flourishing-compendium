"Harmonious passion" — Vallerand et al., 2003: one of the two forms of passion in the dualistic model of passion.

In Robert Vallerand's dualistic model of passion, harmonious passion is the form that comes from an autonomous internalization of the activity into identity: the person freely accepts the activity as important, engages in it voluntarily, and the activity stays in harmony with the rest of life. The scale's own items carry the two marks of the construct, control and integration: "My passion for playing football allows me to live a variety of experiences"; "My passion for music is in harmony with other things that are part of me".

> "Harmonious passion (HP) refers to an autonomous internalization that leads individuals to choose to engage in the activity that they like." (Vallerand et al. 2003, [abstract, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14561128/))

The model pairs it with obsessive passion, which comes from controlled internalization and brings an uncontrollable urge to engage. The 2003 paper's abstract states the contrast in outcomes: "HP promotes healthy adaptation whereas OP thwarts it by causing negative affect and rigid persistence."

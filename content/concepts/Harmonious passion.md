Harmonious passion is one of the two forms of passion in Robert Vallerand's dualistic model of passion: the form that comes from an autonomous internalization of the activity into identity, where the person freely accepts the activity as important, engages in it voluntarily, and the activity stays in harmony with the rest of life. The 2003 founding paper's abstract gives the definition:

> "Harmonious passion (HP) refers to an autonomous internalization that leads individuals to choose to engage in the activity that they like." (Vallerand et al. 2003, [abstract, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14561128/))

Passion itself, in the model, is "a strong inclination toward an activity that people like, that they find important, and in which they invest time and energy" ([same abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14561128/)). A later review states the mechanism in full:

> "Harmonious passion results from an *autonomous* internalization of the activity in the self, in such a way that self-initiation, volition, and willingness drive harmoniously passionate individuals' behaviors." (Briki 2017, [Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5447058/))

### The contrast with obsessive passion

The model pairs harmonious passion with obsessive passion, which comes from a controlled internalization and brings an internal pressure to engage. The 2003 abstract states the contrast in outcomes:

> "HP promotes healthy adaptation whereas OP thwarts it by causing negative affect and rigid persistence." (Vallerand et al. 2003, [abstract, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14561128/))

The model's illustrative examples carry the two marks of the construct, control and integration: "My passion for playing football allows me to live a variety of experiences" and "My passion for music is in harmony with other things that are part of me" ([Briki 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5447058/)); the scale itself asks for agreement with items like "My activity is in harmony with other activities in my life" ([same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5447058/)).

### In everyday life

The runner who misses a day without agitation and returns without ceremony. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

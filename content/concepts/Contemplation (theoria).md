Contemplation (theoria) is the activity Aristotle argues, in Nicomachean Ethics X.7-8, is valued for itself and most closely connected with happiness: the activity of thought that is prized for itself alone ([Aristotle, NE X.7-8, Ross translation, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html)).

### The elimination argument

The argument of Book X works by elimination: strip away action, and still more production, from a living being, and what remains is contemplation ([NE X.7-8, Ross](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html)).

> "Now if you take away from a living being action, and still more production, what is left but contemplation? Therefore the activity of God, which surpasses all others in blessedness, must be contemplative; and of human activities, therefore, that which is most akin to this must be most of the nature of happiness." — [Aristotle, NE X.7-8, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html)

> "Happiness extends, then, just so far as contemplation does, and those to whom contemplation more fully belongs are more truly happy, not as a mere concomitant but in virtue of the contemplation; for this is in itself precious. Happiness, therefore, must be some form of contemplation." — [Aristotle, NE X.7-8, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html)

### The moderation caveat

Aristotle keeps the conclusion human-sized: being a man, one will also need external prosperity, since our nature is not self-sufficient for contemplation; the body must be healthy and must have food; but noble acts do not require ruling earth and sea, and one can with moderate possessions do what one ought ([NE X.7-8, Ross](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html)). Anaxagoras also seems to have supposed the happy man not to be rich nor a despot, and would not be surprised if the happy man seemed to most people a strange person, since they judge by externals ([NE X.7-8, Ross](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.10.x.html)).

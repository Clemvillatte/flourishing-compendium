The compendium attaches "ecological mindfulness" to Joanna Macy and Molly Brown's Coming Back to Life, whose central practice is the Work That Reconnects. The Work That Reconnects Network describes it as "an interactive group process" developed by Macy "in cooperation with many colleagues, over several decades", drawing on "Systems Thinking, Deep Ecology and Deep Time, Spiritual Traditions and Undoing Oppression" ([Work That Reconnects Network](https://workthatreconnects.org/what-is-the-work-that-reconnects/)).

### The Spiral

The network's page, "adapted from Coming Back to Life, by Joanna Macy and Molly Brown", presents the practice as a four-stage Spiral: Coming from Gratitude, Honoring Our Pain for the World, Seeing with New/Ancient Eyes, and Going Forth. Gratitude "quiets the frantic mind and helps to ground us". Honoring pain is where "we learn the true meaning of compassion: to suffer with", and what "had isolated us in private anguish now opens outward". In the third stage "we can sense how intimately and completely we are related to all that is". In Going Forth, people "move into the actions that call each of us, according to our situation, gifts, and limitations" ([Work That Reconnects Network](https://workthatreconnects.org/what-is-the-work-that-reconnects/)).

The page describes the Spiral as open: "every journey through the process changes us", and it is helpful "to experience the Work That Reconnects Spiral over and over again" ([Work That Reconnects Network](https://workthatreconnects.org/what-is-the-work-that-reconnects/)).

### Scope

The page above does not use the phrase "ecological mindfulness". It presents a group process for facing grief, gratitude and action, so the label is the compendium's and the link to mindfulness research is not made by this source. The page is the organisation's own description, not independent evidence that the practice improves wellbeing or behaviour. Coming Back to Life was not read directly.

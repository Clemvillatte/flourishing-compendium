The holographic whole is Leibniz's picture, in the Monadology, of a universe in which every part mirrors the whole: each simple substance expresses all the others, so that the whole is present, from its own angle, in each piece. His formulation:

> "Now this connection or accommodation of all created things to each and of each to all the others, means that each simple substance has relations that express all the others, and, consequently, that it is a perpetual living mirror of the universe." (Leibniz, Monadology §56, trans. Robert Latta, rev. Donald Rutherford, [UCSD](https://philosophyfaculty.ucsd.edu/faculty/rutherford/Leibniz/translations/Monadology.pdf))

### What the doctrine carries

The mirroring is perspectival, not total. Leibniz continues: "And just as the same town looked at from different sides appears completely different, and as if multiplied in perspective, so through the infinite multitude of simple substances, it is as if there were so many different universes, which nevertheless are only perspectives on a single universe, according to the different point of view of each monad" (§57). And the view each monad has is clearest about what is nearest it: "although each created monad represents the whole universe, it represents more distinctly the body which is specially assigned to it" (§62). Since "every monad is, in its own way, a mirror of the universe, and... the universe is regulated according to a perfect order, there must also be an order in that which represents it" (§63). The compendium pairs this with the Tiantai teaching that each thought-moment contains the whole (B063): the two traditions, independently, make the part a window on the whole rather than a fragment of it. A compendium exercise drawn from the image is to take one small state, a mood or a habit, and read it as a perspective on the whole of a life rather than an isolated event. What the doctrine does not give: empirical support; the mirroring is a metaphysical claim about the structure of substances, argued from order, not observed.

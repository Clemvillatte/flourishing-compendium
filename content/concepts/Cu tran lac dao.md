Cư trần lạc đạo, often rendered "Living in the world, joyful in the Way", is both the title of a Vietnamese Zen poem and the name usually given to its closing four lines. The poem, Cư trần lạc đạo phú, is attributed to Trần Nhân Tông, founder of the Trúc Lâm Zen school ([Dot Chuoi Non, translation by Trần Đình Hoành](https://dotchuoinon.com/2023/11/26/living-in-the-world-joyful-in-the-way/)).

### The closing verse

In the Hán-Việt transcription the lines read: "Cư trần lạc đạo thả tùy duyên, Cơ tắc xan hề khốn tắc miên. Gia trung hữu bảo hưu tầm mịch, Đối kính vô tâm mạc vấn thiền." One English rendering by Trần Đình Hoành reads: "Living in the world, joyful in the way, let's go with causation flow. When hungry, eat; when sleepy, sleep. Jewels in the home, search no more. Facing things, no mind, why ask for Zen" ([Dot Chuoi Non](https://dotchuoinon.com/2023/11/26/living-in-the-world-joyful-in-the-way/)). Another version, by Kevin Bowen and Nguyễn Bá Chung, titled "Joyful Practice", has: "Living in the world, practice quietly with joy. When hungry eat. When tired sleep. Don't look for it elsewhere, the jewel lies within" ([Đạo Phật Ngày Nay, on Zen Poems from Early Vietnam](https://www.daophatngaynay.com/vn/dien-dan/tam-tinh-doc-gia/32624-doc-zen-poems-from-early-vietnam-ban-dich-tho-thien-ly-tran.html)). The renderings differ, which is a reminder that the verse is compressed.

### How commentators read it

One commentary explains the title as "living in the dust of the world while rejoicing in the Way", with the spirit of "not abandoning worldly life to seek the Way somewhere far away, but practicing the Way right in the midst of the world" ([Nhật Nguyệt](https://nhatnguyet.org/en/kien-thuc/bai-viet/cu-tran-lac-dao-phu-tran-nhan-tong-noi-gi-e857cc)). A Vietnamese provincial government page glosses tùy duyên as doing what needs doing at the right time without clinging, and relying on one's own inner resources instead of looking outward ([Bắc Giang portal](https://bacgiang.gov.vn/web/huyen-yen-dung-tinh-bac-giang/chi-tiet-tin-tuc/-/asset_publisher/St1DaeZNsp94/content/-cu-tran-lac-ao-phu-ang-van-nom-mang-tinh-than-phat-giao-truc-lam-/22815)).

### Scope

The sources are a translator's website, commentaries and a government cultural page, and no scholarly edition was consulted. The attribution to Trần Nhân Tông and the dating come from those secondary sources. The verse is not a rule to ignore effort or learning. Its own longer text, in the translator's version, speaks of keeping precepts and examining body and mind.

ERG theory is Clayton Alderfer's 1969 reworking of Maslow's hierarchy of needs: five levels compressed into three groups, Existence, Relatedness and Growth. Existence covers "the basic material existence requirements of humans", relatedness "the desire for maintaining important interpersonal relationships", and growth "the desire for personal development", taking in Maslow's "intrinsic component" of esteem and his self-actualization ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ERG_theory)).

### Not a ladder

The reworking's point is structural. As a Penn State course summary puts it, "these three needs are organized on a concreteness continuum rather than a hierarchy", and "needs do not have to operate in a strict, hierarchical fashion (Alderfer, 1969)": where Maslow's fulfillment progression went only one way up, ERG "allows for a back and forth movement from one need category to another".

The back-and-forth has a name: frustration-regression. "When needs are not satisfied, people may regress back to a more concrete need" (Penn State); in Wikipedia's phrasing, "when needs in a higher category are frustrated, an individual will invest more efforts in the lower category". Blocked growth sends effort back down toward relationships and material security.

Two asymmetries mark the theory's edges. "Failure to fulfill existence needs leads to greater existence needs": without food, the need for food grows. But "fulfillment of growth needs leads to greater growth needs": writing a good book increases the need to develop further. Growth is the one appetite that grows by being fed.

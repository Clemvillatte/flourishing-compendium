Teotl is the Nahua concept of a single, sacred, self-generating energy that is the cosmos. James Maffie's account in the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy gives the thesis at the heart of Nahua philosophy:

> "At the heart of Nahua philosophy stands the thesis that there exists a single, dynamic, vivifying, eternally self-generating and self-regenerating sacred power, energy or force: what the Nahuas called teotl." (Maffie, [Aztec Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/aztec-philosophy/), IEP)

### Motion, not stuff

Teotl is not a thing that moves. "Teotl is properly understood as ever-flowing and ever-changing energy-in-motion — not as a discrete, static entity," and the becoming of the world is teotl's own activity: "The continual becoming of the cosmos and its myriad aspects are teotl's shamanic self-masking and self-disguising." On this picture, asking what the world is made of gets the question backwards; the world is a performance of teotl, and every thing is a temporary, moving arrangement of it.

The human question follows from the picture. The Nahua sages, the tlamatinime or "knowers of things," treated as defining of human existence the question "How can humans maintain their balance upon the slippery earth?", and their answer was that "humans must conduct every aspect of their lives wisely." The practice the compendium draws from teotl is this balance, the middle way the Nahuas called nepantla ([[Nepantla|in-betweenness]]): to live with the motion rather than against it, treating the world's constant shifting as the medium of wisdom rather than its obstacle.

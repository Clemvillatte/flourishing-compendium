"Right mindfulness" is the seventh factor of the Buddhist Noble Eightfold Path: the deliberate keeping of attention on present experience, steady and undefended. Its root text, the Satipatthana Sutta, calls the four establishments of mindfulness "the direct path for the purification of beings...for the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation": body, feelings, mind and mental qualities, each observed "in and of itself" ([MN 10, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)).

### The refrain that does the work

What makes the practice right is less the objects than the posture toward them. Every exercise in the sutta carries the same refrain: "ardent, alert, and mindful — putting aside greed and distress with reference to the world." The observation is not detached watching; it is warm, effortful and free of grasping. The compendium's Y026 kernel gives the everyday version: bring the mind back, without drama.

### What the trials show

The clinical translation is mindfulness-based stress reduction and its relatives, and the evidence is real but modest. A meta-analysis of 47 randomized trials with active controls found mindfulness programs produced small to moderate reductions in anxiety, depression and pain, with low or insufficient evidence for effects on positive mood, attention, sleep and other outcomes — and no evidence that meditation programs were better than active treatments such as drugs, exercise or behavioral therapies ([Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24395196/)). Mindfulness earns its place alongside other treatments, not above them.

### In the compendium

The attention principles operationalise it: AT007 on attention shaping experience, AT024 on attentional presence, AT034 on metacognitive awareness of attention, SE028 on mindful self-awareness without over-identifying. PP060 adds the honest caveat: match the practice to the person. ST071's wind-and-flag story gives it its koan: it is not the wind or the flag that moves — it is the mind.

Non-seeking is Shunryu Suzuki's term for practice without a "gaining idea", the wish that meditation or practice will buy you something. In Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind he sets the rule and its limit in one breath:

> "We say our practice should be without gaining ideas, without any expectations, even of enlightenment. This does not mean, however, just to sit without any purpose. This practice free from gaining ideas is based on the Prajna Paramita Sutra." (Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind, "Nothing Special")

### What the rule asks

The point is not inaction. Suzuki is explicit that the practice still does something; it just refuses to make the doing conditional on a reward. Two passages from the same chapter carry it:

> "If you continue this simple practice every day you will obtain a wonderful power. Before you attain it, it is something wonderful, but after you obtain it, it is nothing special."

> "When you give up, when you no longer want something, or when you do not try to do anything special, then you do something. When there is no gaining idea in what you do, then you do something."

The chapter's summary line: "The most important thing is to forget all gaining ideas, all dualistic ideas."

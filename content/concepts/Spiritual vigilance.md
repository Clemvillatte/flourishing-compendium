Spiritual vigilance is Pierre Hadot's name for the watchful attention the ancient Stoic philosopher kept over his own mind, hour by hour. The Greek term behind it is prosoche, attention to oneself, and Hadot makes it the center of his reading of Stoicism as philosophy lived rather than taught. His formulation, quoted in a later study of attention:

> "Attention (prosoche) is the fundamental Stoic spiritual attitude. It is a continuous vigilance and presence of mind, self consciousness which never sleeps, and a constant tension of the spirit. Thanks to this attitude, the philosopher is fully aware of what he does at each instant, and he wills his actions fully. Thanks to his spiritual vigilance, the Stoic always has 'at hand' (procheiron) the fundamental rule of life: that is, the distinction between what depends on us and what does not." (Hadot, Philosophy as a Way of Life, p. 84, as quoted in ["Attention as an Existential Hub in Heidegger and Christian Mysticism" (open PDF via German National Library)](https://d-nb.info/1377040321/34))

### What the vigilance watches for

For Hadot, this attention is not a mood of anxiety but the working posture of the philosophical life. It keeps the rule of life ready to hand: the Stoic distinction between what depends on us and what does not, so that the passions have less to grip. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's account of Hadot places prosoche among the practices "demanding daily or continuous repetition: practices of attention (prosoche), meditations (meletai), memorizations of dogmata, self-mastery (enkrateia), the therapy of the passions..." ([IEP, Pierre Hadot](https://iep.utm.edu/hadot/)). The same article notes that monasticism later took over this posture, refiguring prosoche as attention to oneself and one's thoughts "in constant remembrance of god" ([IEP, Pierre Hadot](https://iep.utm.edu/hadot/)).

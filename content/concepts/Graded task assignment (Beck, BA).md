Graded task assignment is the practice of breaking a task into steps ordered from least to most demanding, so that each completed step makes the next more likely. The technique comes from Aaron Beck's cognitive therapy for depression and survives in behavioral activation; the seed's second source, Bandura and Schunk's 1981 experiment, supplies the mechanism: proximal subgoals build competence, self-efficacy and interest where distal goals do nothing.

### The clinical technique

Beck's version sits inside the activity-scheduling toolkit: tasks the patient avoids are divided into "stages that are more concrete and less formidable to achieve," and the patient performs them one by one, attending to the success each generates ([Beck, Cognitive Therapy of Affective Disorders, excerpts](https://www.scribd.com/document/458269758/Cognitive-Therapy-of-Affective-Disorders-Beck)). Graded task assignment "orders the sequences in which tasks are attempted, from least demanding to most demanding, in order to maximize the individual's probability of initiating and completing any given sequence" ([cognitive therapy treatment description](https://scholarworks.uni.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3626&context=grp)). The behavioral activation manual states the reinforcing logic plainly: "the reinforcing qualities of success and mastery over a given component will increase the likelihood of completing the other components," and if a component proves too challenging, the task is broken down further ([Jacobson et al., BA treatment description](https://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/CBT_Readings/CPSP_Jacobson_et_al_BA.pdf)).

### The experimental anchor

Bandura and Schunk tested the mechanism with children who showed "gross deficits and disinterest in mathematical tasks," pursuing self-directed learning under proximal subgoals, distal goals or no goals. Under proximal subgoals the children "progressed rapidly in self-directed learning, achieved substantial mastery of mathematical operations, and developed a sense of personal efficacy and intrinsic interest," while "distal goals had no demonstrable effects" ([Bandura and Schunk, JPSP 41 (1981), abstract](https://www.digitalgreensboro.org/record/8817?v=pdf)). Goal proximity also fostered "veridical self-knowledge of capabilities," high congruence between efficacy judgments and later performance ([abstract](https://www.digitalgreensboro.org/record/8817?v=pdf)).

Curiosity is the drive to know. Aristotle placed it at the start of philosophy: wonder at difficulties, on his account, is what first moved people to philosophize. Modern psychology models the same pull in information-gap terms, as the awareness of a gap in one's knowledge.

> "For it is owing to their wonder that men both now begin and at first began to philosophize; they wondered originally at the obvious difficulties, then advanced little by little and stated difficulties about the greater matters, e.g. about the phenomena of the moon and those of the sun and of the stars, and about the genesis of the universe." (Aristotle, Metaphysics I.2, 982b, W. D. Ross translation, [ELLOPOS full text](https://www.ellopos.net/elpenor/greek-texts/ancient-greece/aristotle/metaphysics.asp?pg=5))

### The information gap

The psychological model of the same pull: "In a review of the psychological literature on curiosity, one of the authors of this paper (Loewenstein, 1994) introduced the concept of an information gap and proposed that curiosity occurs when an individual becomes aware of a gap in his or her knowledge that could potentially be filled by information. That is, a gap opens when a question becomes activated but the answer is not known with certainty" (Golman and Loewenstein, [Curiosity, Information Gaps, and the Utility of Knowledge](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/golman_loewenstein_curiosity.pdf), author-hosted paper, 2014).

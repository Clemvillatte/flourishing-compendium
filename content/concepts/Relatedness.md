“Relatedness” — Deci and Ryan, 2000.

- Deci & Ryan, 2000
- Ryan & Deci, 2017

Relatedness is the sense of belonging and connection to other people, groups or a culture. In self-determination theory it is one of three innate psychological needs, alongside competence and autonomy: Ryan and Deci describe "the innate needs for competence, autonomy, and relatedness" (Contemporary Educational Psychology 25(1), 2000, p. 57, [open PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf)).

### Why it is more than a comfort

SDT assigns relatedness a role in motivation. Uninteresting but necessary behaviours get internalized, made one's own, when the people who value them are people one feels connected to:

> This suggests that the groundwork for facilitating internalization is providing a sense of belongingness and connectedness to the persons, group, or culture disseminating a goal, or what in SDT we call a sense of relatedness. (Ryan and Deci, "Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations: Classic Definitions and New Directions," Contemporary Educational Psychology 25(1), 2000, p. 64, [open PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf))

Ryan and Deci suggest that feeling respected and cared for supports students' willingness to accept classroom values. They report that Ryan, Stiller and Lynch (1994) found relatedness to teachers and parents was associated with greater internalization of school-related behavioral regulations (ibid., p. 64). Note this reports their account of the 1994 study, not the study itself.

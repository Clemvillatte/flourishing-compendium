**Nudge theory Thaler** is Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein's account of how small changes in choice architecture steer behaviour while preserving freedom to choose. The book's definition, from its first chapter:

> "A nudge, as we will use the term, is any aspect of the choice architecture that alters people's behavior in a predictable way without forbidding any options or significantly changing their economic incentives. To count as a mere nudge, the intervention must be easy and cheap to avoid. Nudges are not mandates. Putting the fruit at eye level counts as a nudge. Banning junk food does not." (Thaler & Sunstein, Nudge, ch. 1)

### The idea

The agent of the theory is the "choice architect": "A choice architect has the responsibility for organizing the context in which people make decisions." Because context shapes choices whether or not anyone designs it, the theory's proposal is to design it deliberately — the stance the authors call "libertarian paternalism": steer toward the chooser's own good, without closing any doors.

The theory sits on the behavioural finding that defaults, orderings and framings move choices that pure deliberation was supposed to settle. Its power source is the same mechanism its name downplays: the nudge works only because choices are context-dependent.

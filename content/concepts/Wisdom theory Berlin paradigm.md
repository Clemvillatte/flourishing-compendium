The Berlin wisdom paradigm is the research program a group led by Paul Baltes built at the Max Planck Institute for Human Development in Berlin, commonly known as the "Berlin group" ([Dong, Weststrate and Fournier 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10336627/)). It treats wisdom as a form of expertise: "an expert knowledge system concerning the fundamental pragmatics of life" ([Baltes and Staudinger 2000](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2102711/component/file_2102710/content)). By "fundamental pragmatics of life" they mean "knowledge and judgment about the essence of the human condition and the ways and means of planning, managing, and understanding a good life" ([Baltes and Staudinger 2000](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2102711/component/file_2102710/content)).

### How wisdom is measured

Participants think aloud about difficult hypothetical problems of life planning, life management and life review; one example prompt is "A 15-year-old girl wants to get married right away" ([Baltes and Staudinger 2000](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2102711/component/file_2102710/content); [Dong et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10336627/)). Trained raters score the protocols against a family of five criteria: rich factual and procedural knowledge, lifespan contextualism, relativism of values and life priorities, and recognition and management of uncertainty ([Baltes and Staudinger 2000](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2102711/component/file_2102710/content)). The review of the field notes that in addition to factual and procedural knowledge about the human condition and how to live a good life, expertise in this domain requires the consideration of context, tolerance of different values, and the ability to manage uncertainty ([Dong et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10336627/)).

### What the model claims

The authors conclude with a further characterization: wisdom as a "cognitive and motivational metaheuristic (pragmatic) that organizes and orchestrates knowledge toward human excellence in mind and virtue, both individually and collectively" ([Baltes and Staudinger 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392856/)). The paradigm measures general wisdom, a person's insight into life in general; the later Bremen paradigm was developed to assess personal wisdom along an analogous set of criteria ([Dong et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10336627/)).

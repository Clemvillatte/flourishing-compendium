The Daoist idea, central to the Zhuangzi, that nature is the first teacher: heaven and earth display their order without saying anything, and the sage's knowledge comes from attending to that display rather than to doctrine.

> Heaven and earth have their great beauties but do not speak of them; the four seasons have their clear-marked regularity but do not discuss it; the ten thousand things have their principles of growth but do not expound them. The sage seeks out the beauties of Heaven and earth and masters the principles of the ten thousand things. Thus it is that the Perfect Man does not act, the Great Sage does not move—they have perceived [the Way of] Heaven and earth, we may say.

(Zhuangzi, ch. 22 "Knowledge Wandered North," Watson translation, [26reads](https://www.26reads.com/library/92138-zhuangzi/22))

Because the teaching has no words, it is available anywhere the natural order can be watched: seasons, water, trees, animals. The compendium connects it to wu wei (D003) and, on the empirical side, to attention restoration and nature contact (AT038, BE041).

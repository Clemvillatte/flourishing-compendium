Peer influence, in the compendium's sense, is the fact that people around you shape what you do, and not mainly by argument. The scientific base is Albert Bandura's Social Learning Theory (1977), whose most quoted passage is about the economy of learning from others:

> Learning would be exceedingly laborious, not to mention hazardous, if people had to rely solely on the effects of their own actions to inform them what to do. Fortunately, most human behavior is learned observationally through modeling: from observing others one forms an idea of how new behaviors are performed, and on later occasions this coded information serves as a guide for action.

(Bandura, Social Learning Theory, 1977, p. 22, as quoted in [principlesoflearning](https://principlesoflearning.wordpress.com/dissertation/chapter-3-literature-review-2/the-social-perspective/social-cognitive-learning-theory-albert-bandura-1977/))

Modeling runs on four component processes: attentional ("People cannot learn much by observation unless they attend to, and perceive accurately, the significant features of the modeled behavior," p. 24), retention, motor reproduction, and motivation. The last one is the lever: "People do not enact everything they learn. They are more likely to adopt modeled behavior if it results in outcomes they value" (p. 28).

The compendium's [[S079 — Associate with people who make you better]] is the practical application: if behavior is copied more than argued, then choosing your models is choosing your future behavior.

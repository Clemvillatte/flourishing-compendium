Choice Theory is William Glasser's proposal that behaviour is chosen to satisfy five basic needs, and that the psychology of external control is the main source of human misery. The premise is stated in one line:

> "The basic premise for Choice Theory is to understand that the only person's behavior you can control is your own." ([Little Book of Choice Theory, Glasser Institute](https://wglasser.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Little-Book-of-Choice-Theory-2019.pdf))

### What the theory carries

The needs are five and genetic: "According to Choice Theory, there are five Basic Needs: Survival, Love & Belonging, Power, Freedom, Fun." They are not stacked in a hierarchy but act together, each person's profile differing in strength, so the same situation is a different need-threat to different people. The choice framing extends to the fourth need: "Everyone has a basic need to make their own choices and direct the course of their own lives." What Glasser called external control psychology is the standing temptation to run other people instead: coercion, nagging, rewards used as levers, all built on the false premise that another's behaviour can be directly produced. The misery follows from the premise failing: the controller never gets compliance, only resistance or pretence. The compendium principle P069 carries the kinship on the believing side: the will to believe operates at the living edge, where the agent's own choice is the only lever there is. A compendium exercise drawn from the theory is to take one recurring conflict and restate it as a need on each side rather than a fault on one. What the theory does not give: an argument that all choices are wise; "chosen" means self-generated toward a need, not good, and Glasser's therapy existed to improve the choosing.

more options degrade satisfaction and decision quality (Iyengar and Lepper, 2000; Schwartz, 2004).

### The jam study

The original demonstration was a field study in a Californian supermarket. A review summarises the design and the finding:

> "A famous field study conducted by Iyengar and Lepper (2000) in a Californian supermarket demonstrated that too much choice decreases customers' motivation to buy as well as their post-choice satisfaction. Tasting booths were set up in two different areas of the supermarket, one of which displayed 6 different jars of jam while the other displayed 24 options, with customers free to taste any of the different flavors of jam." (Misuraca et al., 2024, [Frontiers in Psychology, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11111947/))

The same review gives the numbers:

> "Indeed, 60% of passers-by stopped at the table displaying 24 different options, whereas only 40% of the passers-by stopped at the table displaying the small variety of 6 jams. This finding was expected given that more choice options are appealing. However, out of the 60% of passers-by who stopped at the table with more choices, only 3% of them decided to buy jam. Conversely, 30% of the consumers who stopped at the table with only 6 jars of jam decided to purchase at least one jar." (Misuraca et al., 2024, [Frontiers in Psychology, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11111947/))

The same review notes that a more recent meta-analytic work, Chernev et al. 2015, has shown that choice overload occurs only under certain conditions.

"I and Thou" (*Ich und Du*, 1923) is Martin Buber's name for the two basic attitudes a person can take: the relation of *I-Thou*, meeting another being in their wholeness, present to them without using them, and the mode of *I-It*, in which the other becomes an object of experience, classification, and use ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/martin-buber/); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2016/entries/buber/)). The IEP's summary of the contrast:

> "An 'I-It' relation *experiences* a detached thing, fixed in space and time, while an 'I-Thou' relation *participates* in the dynamic, living process of an 'other'. ... in an 'I-Thou' relation both participants exist as polarities of relation, whose center lies in the between (*Zwischen*)." ([IEP, "Martin Buber"](https://iep.utm.edu/martin-buber/))

And the pure-encounter formulation:

> "The 'I-Thou' relation is the pure encounter of one whole unique entity with another in such a way that the other is known without being subsumed under a universal." ([IEP, "Martin Buber"](https://iep.utm.edu/martin-buber/))

The claim is not that the I-It mode is evil (science, administration, and much of daily life run on it) but that a life conducted entirely in I-It, including with people, thins into management: the Thou is *met*, not known, and only in meeting does the I itself become real.

### The practice of presence

The concept grounds a practice of presence rather than a theory: to listen to someone without simultaneously preparing the reply, formulating them, or finishing their sentence is to treat them as a Thou for a moment. That is why the compendium pairs it with attentional presence in relationship and [[Deep listening|deep listening]], and against the framing and possessing moves that quietly convert persons into objects.

### The distinction to hold

I-Thou is not intimacy or agreement. One can meet an opponent as a Thou (the meeting is the mode, not the warmth), and one can use a lover as an It. Buber's late extension, that every particular Thou "glimpses" the eternal Thou, is what keeps the concept from being mere interpersonal technique.

### In everyday life

In the next conversation, one full minute of listening with nothing prepared: no question held in reserve, no lesson drawn, then noticing what changed in the speaker. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

The Confucian cousin is [[Ren]], relation rather than the isolated individual as the unit of ethics.

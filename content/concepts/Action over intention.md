"Action over intention" is the label this compendium gives to the right action factor of the Buddhist Noble Eightfold Path. In Pali the factor is sammā kammanta, and the sutta that defines the path factor by factor is the Magga-vibhanga Sutta (SN 45.8).

> "Abstaining from taking life, abstaining from stealing, abstaining from unchastity: This, monks, is called right action." (SN 45.8, translated by [Thanissaro Bhikkhu](http://www.dhammatalks.net/accesstoinsight/html/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html))

### How it sits in the path

Right action is the fourth of the eight path factors and belongs to the virtue division of the path ([Access to Insight, "Right Action"](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/ptf/dhamma/sacca/sacca4/samma-kammanto/index.html)). The same sutta defines the neighboring factor, right resolve, as "being resolved on renunciation, on freedom from ill will, on harmlessness" — a state of mind. Right action is defined instead by what a person refrains from doing: three bodily deeds.

That is the contrast the concept label marks. The path does not train intention alone; it names conduct as its own factor, defined by abstention from taking life, stealing and unchastity, and trains it alongside the resolve that precedes it.

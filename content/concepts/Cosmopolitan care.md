Cosmopolitan care is Sarah Clark Miller's name for a moral position: care owed to people as such, wherever they are. Her 2010 article sets out its foundation: "I develop the foundation for cosmopolitan care, an underexplored variety of moral cosmopolitanism" ([Miller 2010, article abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17496535.2010.484258)). Miller critiques the moral foundations of cosmopolitan justice from a feminist philosophical perspective, arguing "the need for an alternative account of moral cosmopolitanism as cosmopolitan care" (Miller 2010). The account combines care ethics with Kantian ethics to generate "a duty to care", and its distinctive feature is the theory of obligation it endorses, which Miller applies to gender violence in conflict zones (Miller 2010).

Virginia Held supplies the global reach of the care tradition. Where "such other moral theories as Kantian morality and utilitarianism demand impartiality above all, the ethics of care understands the moral import of ties to families and groups" ([Oxford Academic, book abstract](https://academic.oup.com/book/2881)). Held argues that "a globalization of caring relations would help enable people of different states and cultures to live in peace, to respect each others' rights, to care together for their environments, and to improve the lives of their children" ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, quoting Held](https://iep.utm.edu/care-ethics/)).

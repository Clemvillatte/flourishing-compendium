Qi (氣, also written ch'i) is the Chinese word for the breath-like, moving stuff that early thinkers took everything to be made of: weather, breath, bodies, moods. It is often translated as "vital energy", although Legge, translating the Daodejing in the nineteenth century, rendered it as breath.

The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy says that "the most enduring interpretation of yinyang in Chinese thought is related to the concept of qi", with yin and yang taken as two forms of qi at work in the universe. On the same page it relays an early account of how human life began: "When qi moved, the clear and light rose to be heaven and the muddy and heavy fell to become earth. When these two qi interacted and attained the stage of harmony (he), human life began." (IEP, Yinyang, [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/))

### Breath as the handle

Chapter 10 of the Daodejing treats it as something to be trained, and Legge's translation reads:

> "When one gives undivided attention to the (vital) breath, and brings it to the utmost degree of pliancy, he can become as a (tender) babe." (Daodejing 10, Legge translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/216))

The parenthesis "(vital)" is Legge's own addition, a reminder that the English word is already an interpretation. Chapter 42 gives the cosmic version, ending with things "harmonised by the Breath of Vacancy" (Legge, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/216)).

### What it is not

Qi is not a substance that modern physics or physiology has measured. In its original frame it is a way of describing change, vitality and the relation between body, breath and surroundings. Claims that qi has been shown to be a specific physical energy go beyond what these texts say.

### Reading it for practice

The practical idea needs no metaphysics: vitality is partly cultivated. Rest, breathing, movement and food are the medium of a working life, and energy gets depleted by overexertion and restored by recovery, not drawn from an endless reserve.

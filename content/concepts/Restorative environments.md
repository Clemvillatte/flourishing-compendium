Restorative environments are the settings that let a tired attentional system recover. Stephen Kaplan's Attention Restoration Theory, in the abstract to his 1995 paper:

> "Directed attention plays an important role in human information processing; its fatigue, in turn, has far-reaching consequences. Attention Restoration Theory provides an analysis of the kinds of experiences that lead to recovery from such fatigue. Natural environments turn out to be particularly rich in the characteristics necessary for restorative experiences." (Stephen Kaplan, "The restorative benefits of nature", [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0272494495900012))

### Why quiet and nature

The claim is about effort. Concentrating blocks out distraction; that blocking is work, and the work fatigues. Quiet and natural settings are the ones whose fascination comes cheap — leaves in wind, water, distant birds — so the blocking effort can stand down while the mind stays gently engaged.

The clarity mechanism sits in the first component of the two-part operational definition of mindfulness that Bishop and colleagues proposed at the Toronto meetings:

> "The first component involves the self-regulation of attention so that it is maintained on immediate experience, thereby allowing for increasing recognition of mental events in the present moment. The second component involves adopting a particular orientation or attitude toward one's experience in the present moment, an orientation that is characterized by curiosity, openness and acceptance" ([Bishop et al., 2004, p. 232, as quoted in Rejeski, The Gerontologist, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3632420/))

### Where the clarity comes from

On this model, clarity is a byproduct of sustained, immediate contact. Attention held on experience as it arrives produces increasing recognition of mental events as mental events, thoughts seen as thoughts rather than as the world. The second component keeps the seeing from turning harsh: the same events met with curiosity, openness and acceptance.

The compendium's contemplative principles describe the same settling from the other side of the tradition: the mind as a clear lake, practice that removes obstacles rather than manufacturing states, and purity and stillness in the Daoist register.

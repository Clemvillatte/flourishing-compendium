Indra's net is the image the Huayan school of Chinese Buddhism uses to picture mutual dependence: the god Indra has a net with a jewel at every crossing of strands, and each jewel reflects every other jewel. The image comes from the Avatamsaka Sutra (Huayan jing), the Flower Garland Sutra that gives the Huayan school its name.

> "According to pre-Buddhist myth, the god Indra has a net with a jewel at the intersection of every two strands. Each jewel is so bright that it reflects every other jewel in the net." ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Huayan Buddhism, §1.3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buddhism-huayan/))

### What the image carries

The point is the reflections, not the jewelry: each thing exists and is what it is through everything else. The Stanford Encyclopedia gives the school's reading, that each thing "is dependent for both its existence and its identity upon every other thing that exists". Huayan's distinctive doctrines are that one is all and all is one, and that realizing this leads to universal compassion.

Fazang (643-712), the third patriarch and the school's great systematizer, illustrated "one is all and all is one" with the relationship between a rafter and the building it is part of. He also made the doctrine tangible for lay audiences with devices such as mirror halls.

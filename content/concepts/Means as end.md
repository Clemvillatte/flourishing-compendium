"Means as end" is the seed's name for a way of thinking John Dewey works out in Experience and Education (1938): the things done on the way to a result are part of the result, so a means is never merely a pass-through. His own sentence comes from the chapter on freedom:

> "But the fact still remains that an increased measure of freedom of outer movement is a means, not an end. The educational problem is not solved when this aspect of freedom is obtained. Everything then depends, so far as education is concerned, upon what is done with this added liberty." ([Dewey, Experience and Education (1938), ch. 5, extract PDF](https://lcl.media.mit.edu/resources/readings/experience-and-education.pdf))

### What the book says around it

The chapter's argument runs on: "freedom of outward action is a means to freedom of judgment and of power to carry deliberately chosen ends into execution" ([same text](https://lcl.media.mit.edu/resources/readings/experience-and-education.pdf)). The book's last chapter is titled "Experience—The Means and Goal of Education", and its opening sentence states the principle the seed points at: "education in order to accomplish its ends both for the individual learner and for society must be based upon experience--which is always the actual life-experience of some individual" ([Dewey 1938, ch. 8, full text](http://ruby.fgcu.edu/courses/ndemers/colloquium/experienceducationdewey.pdf)).

The minimal self is the thinnest sense of being a subject: the first-person character that every experience has, before any reflection or life story. Shaun Gallagher and Dan Zahavi define it as "the immediate and first-personal givenness of experience", explained as pre-reflective self-consciousness, "present whenever I am living through or undergoing an experience, e.g., whenever I am consciously perceiving the world, remembering a past event, imagining a future event, thinking an occurrent thought, or feeling sad or happy, thirsty or in pain" ([Gallagher and Zahavi, 2021, quoted in a PhilArchive paper](https://philarchive.org/archive/FARAIS-3)).

### Two senses inside it

An article in Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences summarises Gallagher's view: the minimal self has two separable modalities, "(1) a sense of ownership or the sense that I am the one who is undergoing an experience and (2) a sense of agency or the sense that I am the one who is the initiator or source of the action" ([Reconstructing the minimal self, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-009-9148-0)). In ordinary voluntary action the two are "intimately intertwined and often indistinguishable", but in involuntary action such as anarchic hand syndrome they "can come apart" ([PhilArchive paper on self-awareness](https://philarchive.org/archive/LPESAA-3)).

### What pre-reflective means

Pre-reflective self-awareness "does not require one to explicitly reflect on one's own mental states, or to otherwise take them as objects of attention", and on this view all conscious experience involves "an implicit awareness of oneself as its subject" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Self-Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/FALL2017/entries/self-consciousness/index.html)). Another paper puts it as "the ineliminable (pre-reflective) awareness of the very fact of being conscious", not awareness of oneself as an object ([The We in Me, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11245-018-9564-2)). Zahavi holds that this minimal self is foundational for the narrative self, since a narrator must be able "to differentiate between self and non-self" and so needs a first-person perspective ([ScienceDirect review of Zahavi](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810008000366)).

### Debate

The view is contested. Stanford's entry points readers to criticism of pre-reflective self-awareness and notes that it is "often associated" with a sense of ownership or mineness. A recent critical assessment examines the claim that "for-me-ness" is present "whenever anything is consciously experienced" ([For-me-ness: a critical assessment, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10339-026-01383-z?error=cookies_not_supported&code=369bbcc7-2a80-44d3-9695-1656d4072d85)). A reviewer of Zahavi adds that to say experiences are self-possessed "is not to say they are necessarily self-conscious" ([Wiley review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2008.00228.x)).

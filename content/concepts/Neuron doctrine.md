The neuron doctrine is "the concept that the nervous system is made up of discrete individual cells, a discovery due to decisive neuro-anatomical work of Santiago Ramón y Cajal and later presented by, among others, H. Waldeyer-Hartz" ([Wikipedia, Neuron doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron_doctrine)). The seed credits Cajal alone; the sources show a shared effort.

### Who said what

Waldeyer coined the term "neuron" in 1891. Bock's article quotes his conclusion: "The nervous system is made up of innumerable nerve units (neurons) which are anatomically and genetically independent of each other" ([Bock, Cajal, Golgi, Nansen, Schäfer and the Neuron Doctrine, PDF](https://www.hobertlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/BOCK_Cajal_Golgi_Nansen_Schafer_and_the_Neuron_Doctrine_2013.pdf)). A history in Frontiers notes the doctrine was "enunciated in 1886-1887 by anatomists such as Wilhelm His, August Forel, Fridtjof Nansen and boosted by Cajal's indefatigable work since 1888", then "officially promulgated in 1891" by Waldeyer ([Golgi's original slides, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6388087/)).

### The rival reticular theory

Golgi's silver stain of 1873 let researchers see whole nerve cells, yet Golgi himself favoured the reticular theory, in which the nervous system is a continuous network. Cajal used the same stain; Wikipedia says he reported in 1888 that nerve cells were not continuous in bird brain. Golgi and Cajal shared the 1906 Nobel Prize, and in his Nobel lecture "Golgi defended the going-out-of-favour Reticular Theory" while Cajal argued the neurons are independent of one another (Bock, above).

### Synapses and the end of the dispute

Cajal described gaps between cells across which communication took place, summed up as contiguity, not continuity. The name "synapse" came from Charles Scott Sherrington in 1897 ([Psychology Today, Story of Camillo Golgi](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/psychiatry-a-history/202209/the-story-of-camillo-golgi-and-the-visualization-of-the-neuron)). The dispute was resolved in the 1950s, when electron microscopy showed that nerve cells were individual cells interconnected through synapses (Wikipedia).

### Limits

The Psychology Today piece says electron microscopy left no doubt about "the vast majority of neurons", so the doctrine has recognised exceptions; I did not open a source listing them. Cajal's own late summary named six principles, calling each neuron an anatomical, genetic, functional, trophic, nosological and electrophysiological unit, per a [Waldeyer biography in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8591077/).

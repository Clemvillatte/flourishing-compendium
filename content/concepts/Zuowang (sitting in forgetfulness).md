Zuowang (坐忘, literally "sitting forgetting") is the meditation practice named in chapter 6 of the Zhuangzi ([Wikipedia, Zuowang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuowang)). The term comes from a short dialogue in which Yan Hui reports his progress to Confucius, and the third report gives the practice its name:

> "My connexion with the body and its parts is dissolved; my perceptive organs are discarded. Thus leaving my material form, and bidding farewell to my knowledge, I am become one with the Great Pervader. This I call sitting and forgetting all things." (Zhuangzi 6, Book VI section 9, trans. James Legge, [ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

### What Yan Hui says he drops

The passage names what falls away, not a technique: the felt connexion to the body, the perceptive organs, material form and knowledge. Confucius answers that one so united with the Great Pervader is "free from all likings" and "become impermanent", and asks leave to follow in Yan Hui's steps ([Zhuangzi 6, trans. James Legge, ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

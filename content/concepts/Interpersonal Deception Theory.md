Interpersonal Deception Theory (IDT) is a theory of deceptive communication published by David B. Buller and Judee K. Burgoon in 1996 ([Buller & Burgoon, 1996](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2885.1996.tb00127.x)). Its defining move is to treat deception as an interaction rather than a one-way act: the theory merges interpersonal communication and deception principles to account for deception in interactive contexts, presenting key definitions and 18 general propositions from which testable hypotheses can be derived ([Buller & Burgoon, 1996](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2885.1996.tb00127.x)).

> "Interpersonal deception theory (IDT) represents a merger of interpersonal communication and deception principles designed to better account for deception in interactive contexts." (Buller & Burgoon, [Interpersonal Deception Theory](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2885.1996.tb00127.x), Communication Theory 6, 1996, pp. 203-242, abstract)

### What makes it interpersonal

IDT treats deceptive exchanges as interactions in which participants adjust their behaviour in response to one another. It groups intentional deception into falsification (lying), concealment (omitting material facts) and equivocation (skirting issues by changing the subject or responding indirectly) ([Wikipedia, Interpersonal deception theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_deception_theory)).

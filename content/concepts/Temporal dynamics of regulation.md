## What the chapter argues

Emotions unfold over stages, and regulation can step in at any of them ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)). Gal Sheppes and James Gross made timing the center of their account of what works when, and the account rests on two distinct time comparisons that are easy to run together.

The first comparison is between strategies, and the clearest version of it pits reappraisal against suppression.

> The basic logic of this comparison is that reappraisal which alters the emotional trajectory early on, should be a more effective regulation strategy than suppression, which intervenes later, and thus has to combat a higher intensity emotional response.
> — Sheppes & Gross, "Emotion Regulation Effectiveness: What Works When," Handbook of Psychology, 2nd ed., 2012, "Empirical Tests of the Process Model's Predictions" section ([author copy](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf))

On the evidence the chapter reviews, suppression has carried relative costs in affective, cognitive and social domains ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)). Studies cited there found that suppressing did not reduce negative emotional experience the way reappraisal did, and increased sympathetic nervous system arousal ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)). That is the finding behind the common shorthand that an emotion is easier to head off than to hold down.

The second comparison is between intensities, and it complicates the first. Here the two senses of early and late need separating. In the chapter's account of how emotions are generated, reappraisal works earlier than suppression ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)). In its account of information processing, distraction works earlier than reappraisal, blocking the information at the start, while reappraisal takes it in and works a meaning onto it before damping it down ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)). That late work suits a mild response ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)). At high intensity the same operation becomes costly and effortful, and distraction does better there ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)), though the chapter notes its own limitation: relief that comes cheaply in the short term can prove maladaptive over the long run ([Sheppes & Gross, 2012](https://people.socsci.tau.ac.il/mu/galsheppes/files/2016/11/Sheppes-Gross-2012.pdf)).

## In the compendium

The principle on regulating early, not late states the compendium's own counsel: intervening early in an emotional episode beats intervening at the peak. The principle on the temporal structure of affect supplies the frame: emotions have intensity, duration, variability and recovery. The "more necessary" phrasing for late suppression belongs to the seed and to that principle, and this page does not present it as a finding of the research.

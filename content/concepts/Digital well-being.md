Digital well-being, in the definition used by Burr, Taddeo and Floridi's thematic review, is the impact digital technologies have on a life that is good for a human being:

> "The expression 'digital well-being' refers in this article to the impact of digital technologies on what it means to live a life that is *good for* a human being in an information society." (Burr, Taddeo and Floridi, [The Ethics of Digital Well-Being: A Thematic Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7417400/), Science and Engineering Ethics, 2020)

### The active and passive distinction

Within this field, the Verduyn review's distinction separates two ways of using a social network site. In its words, passive use "refers to the monitoring of other people's lives without engaging in direct exchanges with others" (Verduyn et al., 2017, p. 281, as quoted in Calvin et al., [Computers in Human Behavior Reports](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13341170/), 2025). The model's hypothesis, as the same article summarizes it:

> "Researchers hypothesize that passive use elicits ill-being through instigating feelings of envy and social comparison while active social media use elicits feedback and social connection, subsequently increasing well-being (Verduyn et al., 2017)." (Calvin, Chen and Selkie, 2025)

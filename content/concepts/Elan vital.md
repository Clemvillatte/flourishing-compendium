"Elan vital" — Bergson, Creative Evolution.

## Facets

- **read as creativity** — Bergson, Creative Evolution

The élan vital is Henri Bergson's name, introduced in Creative Evolution (1907), for an impetus of life. The term is usually left untranslated. Bergson arrives at it through the character of conscious life: a conscious being never merely repeats itself, and each moment of a life modifies the person living it. In chapter 1, he writes:

> "for a conscious being, to exist is to change, to change is to mature, to mature is to go on creating oneself endlessly." (Bergson, Creative Evolution, ch. 1, [Mitchell 1911 translation, Brock University](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Bergson/Bergson_1911a/Bergson_1911_01.html))

The chapter then asks "Should the same be said of existence in general?" ([ch. 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Bergson/Bergson_1911a/Bergson_1911_01.html)) and answers with the concept this page is about: life, too, as a movement that passes between the generations:

> "So we come back, by a somewhat roundabout way, to the idea we started from, that of an original impetus of life, passing from one generation of germs to the following generation of germs through the developed organisms which bridge the interval between the generations." ([same text](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Bergson/Bergson_1911a/Bergson_1911_01.html))

## In plain words

Competing response theory is the engine inside habit reversal training: a habit is not argued with or suppressed, it is replaced. You train an incompatible response, performed at the habit's trigger, so the old movement cannot run.

## Where it comes from

Nathan Azrin and R. Gregory Nunn introduced the habit reversal technique in 1973 as "a method of eliminating nervous habits and tics," and habit reversal training "is a 'multicomponent behavioral treatment package originally developed to address a wide variety of repetitive behavior disorders'" ([Wikipedia, Habit reversal training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habit_reversal_training)).

## How it works

The treatment package "consists of five components: awareness training, competing response training, contingency management, relaxation training, and generalization training" ([Wikipedia, Habit reversal training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habit_reversal_training)). For tics, the components "are intended to increase tic awareness, develop a competing response to the tic, and build treatment motivation and compliance," and the approach "is based on the presence of a premonitory urge, or sensation occurring before a tic" ([Wikipedia, Habit reversal training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habit_reversal_training)). The competing response is trained to that early warning signal, so it fires before the habit does.

## What it explains

The theory explains the replacement logic inside the package: an incompatible action gives the urge somewhere else to go, and practice welds it to the trigger. The evidence base is strong for the package as a whole: research on HRT "have produced consistent, large effect sizes (approximately 0.80 across the disorders)," and it "has met the standard of a well-established treatment for stuttering, thumb sucking, nail biting, and TMJ disorders" ([Wikipedia, Habit reversal training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habit_reversal_training)), with tics, trichotillomania and skin picking among the treated disorders. The article does not isolate the competing-response component's separate effect.

## Everyday life

The package's template, as described in the article: awareness training so the habit's early signal is caught, then a competing response trained to that signal. The article does not prescribe specific responses for specific habits.

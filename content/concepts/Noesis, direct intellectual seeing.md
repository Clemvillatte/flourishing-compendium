Noesis (noēsis) is the top of the divided line in Plato's Republic VI: the grasp of the Forms, set over by the faculty Allan Silverman's Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry calls Nous, "Knowledge or Understanding." The page title's wording, "direct intellectual seeing," is a gloss that the entry does not use. What the entry does say is that the topmost segment of the line is "clear enough":

> "The objects are Forms and the faculty set over Forms is Nous, Knowledge or Understanding." (Silverman, "Plato's Middle Period Metaphysics and Epistemology," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, section 13, [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-metaphysics/))

### How it differs from reasoning from hypotheses

The same section quotes Republic 510b on the segment just below. There the soul, "using as images the things that were imitated before, is forced to investigate from hypotheses, proceeding not to a first principle, but to a conclusion." In the upper segment, by contrast, the soul "makes its way to a first principle that is not a hypothesis, proceeding from a hypothesis, but without the images used in the previous subsection, using Forms themselves and making its investigation through them."

Silverman adds that the objects and nature of the third section, dianoia, are "matters of great controversy." The entry is therefore a reading of the line, and the middle of the line is disputed.

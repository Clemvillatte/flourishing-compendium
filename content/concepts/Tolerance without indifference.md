Tolerance without indifference is my name for William James's position in "On a Certain Blindness in Human Beings" (1899): the argument for tolerance is not the claim that all values are equally valid, but the claim that no single observer sees enough to judge. James builds the case from the blindness itself, "the blindness with which we all are afflicted in regard to the feelings of creatures and people different from ourselves," and concludes with a command rather than a shrug:

> "It absolutely forbids us to be forward in pronouncing on the meaninglessness of forms of existence other than our own; and it commands us to tolerate, respect, and indulge those whom we see harmlessly interested and happy in their own ways, however unintelligible these may be to us. Hands off: neither the whole of truth nor the whole of good is revealed to any single observer, although each observer gains a partial superiority of insight from the peculiar position in which he stands. Even prisons and sick-rooms have their special revelations."

([James, "On a Certain Blindness in Human Beings," monadnock.net full text](https://monadnock.net/james/blindness.html).)

### What makes it tolerance without indifference

The passage's structure is the concept's structure. The ground for tolerance is epistemic, not relativist: each observer has partial insight, not no insight, and "even prisons and sick-rooms have their special revelations" ([same](https://monadnock.net/james/blindness.html)). James elsewhere makes the same point about his own commitments: he combined "this humility and tolerance" with strong opinions, speaking out hard for his convictions while holding that no one has a monopoly on truth ([History News Network on James](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/2012-politics-and-william-james)). The tolerance does not dilute the conviction; it disciplines the pronouncing.

The passage's last sentence carries the balance: "It is enough to ask of each of us that he should be faithful to his own opportunities and make the most of his own blessings, without presuming to regulate the rest of the vast field" ([same](https://monadnock.net/james/blindness.html)). James in a letter called this essay "the perception on which my whole individualistic philosophy is based" ([SEP, William James](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2017/entries/james/)).

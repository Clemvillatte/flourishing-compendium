Skills training is the teaching of specific behavioural skills, such as emotion regulation or interpersonal skills, as an organised programme. The seed cites Linehan (1993) and Kelly (2004). The best-documented case in the sources opened for this page is the skills training component of dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT), run from a manual by Linehan; the title also names CBT, but those sources describe DBT only.

### Where it sits in DBT

A 2020 study describes standard DBT as a 12-month treatment with four modes: individual therapy, group skills training, phone coaching and team consultation.

> "In its standard form, DBT is a 12-month intervention comprising four modes of treatment: individual therapy, group skills training, phone coaching and team consultation [12]." (Kells et al. 2020, [PMC6993331](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6993331/))

The same study delivered the skills component in 2.5-hour weekly sessions, teaching mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation and interpersonal effectiveness skills, organised into three modules. A scoping review adds that skills training has traditionally run as a weekly face-to-face group in cycles of 12 to 30 weeks, following Linehan's manual (Lakeman et al. 2022, [PMC9305106](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9305106/)).

### How well it works alone

Valentine and colleagues report that DBT skills training is being given as a stand-alone treatment in many clinical settings, with little empirical support for that use so far. Their review covers 17 trials of skills training given in the absence of the other modalities.

> "While the literature reviewed provides preliminary evidence of the utility of DBT skills training to address a range of mental health and behavioral problems, methodological limitations of published studies preclude us from drawing strong conclusions about the efficacy of skills training as a stand-alone treatment." (Valentine et al. 2015, [PubMed 25042066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25042066/))

Faculties of the mind is Kant's term for the mind's distinct powers of knowing. In the Critique of Pure Reason the two that supply knowledge are sensibility, the receptivity through which objects are given to us, and understanding, the spontaneity through which they are thought.

> "Our knowledge springs from two main sources in the mind, first of which is the faculty or power of receiving representations (receptivity for impressions); the second is the power of cognizing by means of these representations (spontaneity in the production of conceptions)." (Kant, [Critique of Pure Reason](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4280/4280-h/4280-h.htm), Transcendental Logic, Introduction I; Meiklejohn translation)

### How the faculties cooperate

Kant's rule is that neither faculty can do the other's work: understanding cannot intuit, the sensuous faculty cannot think. His summary line is that "thoughts without content are void; intuitions without conceptions, blind." A third faculty sits above the two: knowledge begins with sense, proceeds to understanding, and ends with reason, beyond which Kant says nothing higher can be discovered in the human mind for elaborating the matter of intuition.

Co-regulation names the ways partners in a close relationship regulate each other's affect rather than only their own. Emily Butler and Ashley Randall's account gives it a precise shape: a dyadic emotional system, not two side-by-side solo systems.

> "Coregulation refers to the process by which relationship partners form a dyadic emotional system involving an oscillating pattern of affective arousal and dampening that dynamically maintains an optimal emotional state." (Butler and Randall, [Emotional coregulation in close relationships](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/55917f64e4b0cd3b4705b68c/t/5cd262650d92978bc2bbc629/1557291622593/butler.randall.2013.pdf), Emotion Review 5(2):202-210, 2013)

### The operational definition

The paper's proposed working definition is narrower: "a bidirectional linkage of oscillating emotional channels between partners, which contributes to emotional stability for both partners" ([Butler and Randall 2013](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/55917f64e4b0cd3b4705b68c/t/5cd262650d92978bc2bbc629/1557291622593/butler.randall.2013.pdf)). The channels can be subjective experience, expressive behaviour or autonomic physiology; the linkage runs both ways, and its signature is dampening — oscillations that settle back toward a stable state rather than amplifying away from it.

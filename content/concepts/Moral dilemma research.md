Moral dilemma research is the psychology of how people judge hard cases where every option violates something. The seed's sources are Kohlberg, 1981 and Haidt, 2001; the opened carrier is Haidt's 2001 paper, which opens by naming the field's dominant assumption:

> "Research on moral judgment has been dominated by rationalist models, in which moral judgment is thought to be caused by moral reasoning." ([Haidt, 2001, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/))

His counter-hypothesis, in the same abstract:

> "moral reasoning is usually a post hoc construction, generated after a judgment has been reached." ([Haidt, 2001, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/))

### The intuitionist turn

The social intuitionist model Haidt presents states that moral judgment is generally the result of quick, automatic evaluations, intuitions, and deemphasizes the private reasoning done by individuals in favour of social and cultural influences ([2001 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/)). For a compendium on practical wisdom this matters directly: if the judgment comes first and the reasons after, then examining which reasons are honest and which are decoration is part of the practice. The compendium's principles use the research that way: A052 (Know when to depart from rules) and A105 (Recognise the limits of law) both list the concept among their psychology parallels.

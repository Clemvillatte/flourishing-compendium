Effortless attention is attention that sustains itself inside an absorbing activity, without the felt effort that ordinary concentration costs (Bruya (ed.), Effortless Attention, 2010; Bruya and Tang, [2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.704443/full)).

Bruya's 2021 follow-up paper names the construct: "a third kind of attention—fluid attention (also called postvoluntary attention), which is goal-directed and selective, like voluntary attention, but also effortless and drawn to its source, like involuntary attention" (Bruya and Tang, [2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.704443/full)). The term postvoluntary originates in the Russian-language work of Nikolaj Dobrynin, which the paper introduces to the English-language literature.

The paper's recovery of William James gives the mechanism its shape. James, quoted in the paper: "There is no such thing as voluntary attention sustained for more than a few seconds at a time. What is called sustained voluntary attention is a repetition of successive efforts which bring back the topic to the mind". The transition happens when the topic takes over: "the topic once brought back, if a congenial one, develops; and if its development is interesting, it engages the attention [involuntarily] for a time" (James, quoted in Bruya and Tang, [2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.704443/full)).

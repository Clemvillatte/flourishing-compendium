political and moral beliefs processed as identity threats (Kahan, 2013).

Identity-protective cognition names a way of reasoning in which the question "is this true?" is answered, without the reasoner noticing, by the question "does this mark me as one of us?" Dan Kahan's research program treats political conflict over evidence — climate, guns, vaccines — not as a failure of information or intelligence but as the mind protecting its standing in groups its life depends on.

> "The 'Science Comprehension Thesis' (SCT), which identifies defects in the public's knowledge and reasoning capacities as the source of such controversies; and the 'Identity-protective Cognition Thesis' (ICT), which treats cultural conflict as disabling the faculties that members of the public use to make sense of decision-relevant science." — [Kahan et al., Motivated Numeracy and Enlightened Self-Government, working paper (2013), full text](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/540b4ffa-9bea-499b-bee6-cf7a74a73731/content)

### Why more thinking can make it worse

The unsettling result of the 2013 study is that the bias is not a shortcut of the lazy mind. Subjects who scored highest in cognitive reflection were the ones most likely to display ideologically motivated cognition; being good at reasoning supplied better tools for reaching the group's conclusion. The same paper notes that cultural cognition is "simply the term used to designate the species of identity-protective cognition that accounts for a wide variety of risk-controversies." The remedy this suggests is not more facts but rearranged incentives: beliefs stop being badges before they stop being wrong.

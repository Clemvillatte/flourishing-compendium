In Gabriel Marcel's philosophy, fidelity is more than keeping a promise. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy sets out the starting problem: "The 'problem' posed by fidelity is that of constancy." It then adds the turn that matters:

> "fidelity—a belief in someone—requires presence in addition to constancy over time, and presence implies an affective element." ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Gabriel (-Honoré) Marcel, section 12](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2025/entries/marcel/))

### Creative fidelity

The encyclopedia ties fidelity to what Marcel calls disponibilité, usually translated "availability". That term "refers to the measure in which I am available to someone, the state of having my resources at hand to offer". Being available in this way is to "believe in the other, to place myself at her disposal and to maintain the openness of disponibilité".

On this reading, "'Creative fidelity' consists in actively maintaining ourselves in a state of openness and permeability, in willing ourselves to remain open to the other and open to the influx of the presence of the other." And: "The truest fidelity is creative, that is, a fidelity that creates the self in order to meet the demands of fidelity."

### Fidelity and hope

The same section links fidelity with hope: "Thus, creative fidelity invariably touches upon hope."

### Reading Marcel carefully

The sources opened here support reading Marcel's fidelity as presence and openness to another person, kept up over time. They do not, on their own, support reading it as a technique or a duty of consistency.

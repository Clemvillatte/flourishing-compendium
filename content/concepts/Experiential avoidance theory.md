**Experiential avoidance theory** — Hayes — psychological suffering compounds when one refuses contact with unwanted inner experience.

## Field

Affect · Psychological science

The theory comes out of Steven Hayes and colleagues' contextual behavioral tradition, which introduced experiential avoidance as a named process in 1996. Its core claim is functional: what maintains suffering is not the unwanted inner experience itself but the unwillingness to stay in contact with it, together with the steps a person takes to alter its form or frequency. Those steps can spread from the avoided event to the contexts that occasion it, narrowing where a person goes and what they do.

> "Although the avoidance of unwanted inner experiences may alleviate distress in the short-term, it paradoxically exacerbates distress over longer periods of time." (Fernández-Rodríguez et al. 2018, [Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6129770/), summarizing earlier findings)

The theory comes from the same tradition as Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT, Hayes et al. 1999), which the paper describes as teaching clients "to contact psychological experiences, directly and fully" rather than avoid them. The construct is measured with the Acceptance and Action Questionnaire-II (AAQ-II, Bond et al. 2011), which the paper treats as focused on experiential avoidance. The empirical study of the construct is collected on [[Experiential avoidance research]].

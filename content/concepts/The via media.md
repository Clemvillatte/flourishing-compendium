Via media is Latin for a middle way, and the English noun is defined as "A third way; a middle course, a compromise" ([Wiktionary, "via media"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/via_media)). The phrase names a course taken between two poles:

> "The quip highlighted the extent to which the philosophes were seeking a via media between what they saw as the morally and intellectually impoverished tradition of Christian revelation and its embattled polar opposite, a sceptical materialism [...]." (Colin Jones, The Great Nation, 2002, quoted in [Wiktionary, "via media"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/via_media))

The compendium's seed names Bernstein's Beyond Objectivism and Relativism, where the two poles are objectivism and relativism. The relativist side of the dilemma, as Bernstein states it: "There is no substantive overarching framework in which radically different and alternative schemes are commensurable" (Bernstein, Beyond Objectivism and Relativism, pp. 11-12, quoted in the [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/cognitive-relativism-truth/)).

“Intergenerational transmission” — Fivush et al., 2011.

The research program behind this concept studies how families hand down more than genes: narratives, styles of remembering, and the shapes of a life story. Robyn Fivush and colleagues open their review with the integrative claim:

> "Autobiographical memory is a uniquely human form of memory that integrates individual experiences of self with cultural frames for understanding identities and lives." ([Fivush, Habermas, Waters and Zaman, 2011, International Journal of Psychology 46(5)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00207594.2011.596541))

### What parents hand to children

The mechanism they document is conversational. The abstract's summary of the evidence: "Parents who structure more elaborated coherent personal narratives with their young children have children who, by the end of the preschool years, provide more detailed and coherent personal narratives, and show a more differentiated and coherent sense of self." The style of family reminiscing, in other words, becomes part of the child's equipment for selfhood. In adolescence, the process continues: parents help structure life narratives during coconstructed reminiscing, and adolescents draw on parents and families as a source for their own autobiographical content and structure.

### The wider frame

The transmission is not only intra-family. Adolescents also take up culturally available canonical biographical forms, life scripts and master narratives in constructing a life story, so that what passes between generations carries a culture inside it. The review's closing argument gives the concept its importance for this compendium:

> "Ultimately, we argue that autobiography is a critical developmental skill; narrating our personal past connects us to our selves, our families, our communities, and our cultures." ([Fivush et al., 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00207594.2011.596541))

The compendium's principles pick up the three directions of that sentence: practising cultural continuity keeps the channel open, treating cultural traditions as living keeps what passes through it from hardening, and treating intergenerational trauma as a real mechanism names the case where what is transmitted harms.

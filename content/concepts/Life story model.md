The life story model is Dan McAdams's theory that identity is a story. In Power, Intimacy, and the Life Story (1985) and The Stories We Live By (1993), McAdams proposed "that what Erikson called an identity is really an inner story or personal myth that integrates a person's life in time (past, present, and future) and social space (the manifold roles a person plays in society)" ([McAdams, Narrative Identity and the Redemptive Self](https://www.erudit.org/en/journals/nw/2022-v11-nw09057/1108951ar.pdf)).

### The story's anatomy

The life story is "organized into life chapters and punctuated by key scenes like high points, low points, and turning points" (nuclear episodes), situated in an "ideological setting" of values and assumptions, and moving toward an envisioned ending, the generativity script, an ending that "ideally gives birth to new beginnings" ([McAdams autobiography](https://www.erudit.org/en/journals/nw/2022-v11-nw09057/1108951ar.pdf)). The model's current form layers personality into three lines: the social actor with dispositional traits, the motivated agent with goals and values, and the autobiographical author who constructs the narrative identity in the adult years ([McAdams autobiography](https://www.erudit.org/en/journals/nw/2022-v11-nw09057/1108951ar.pdf); [McAdams and Olson, Annual Review](https://www.imprs-life.mpg.de/25664/052_mcadams_olson.pdf)).

### The key findings

The model's most-cited empirical result concerns story shape. Highly generative adults disproportionately tell redemption sequences, arcs where a clearly negative scene turns positive, while contamination sequences, good scenes that end badly, track the opposite ([Conn College paper](https://digitalcommons.conncoll.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1014&context=psychfacpub); [McAdams, First We Invented Stories](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/McAdamsFirstWeStoriesThenTheyChangedUs2019.pdf)). The redemptive self, the full pattern of early advantage, witnessed suffering, moral steadfastness, redemption and prosocial goals, predicts generativity and relates to well-being. The cultural caveat is McAdams's own: stories of agency and redemption "tend to be highly favored, at least in American society" ([McAdams 2019](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/McAdamsFirstWeStoriesThenTheyChangedUs2019.pdf)).

The compendium treats the model as the psychology of self-authorship. The stories carry cases: ST172's Thales falling in the well, ST003's useless tree, ST082's Huineng hearing one line, ST047's McCandless and the bus, ST056's Matisse cutting paper, each a nuclear episode with its own emplotment; P129's sympathetic understanding is the reader's stance toward other people's stories.

The Stoic theory of how concern grows. The Greek term names a process of coming-to-belong, and it grounds Stoic [[Cosmopolitanism]] and the [[Preferred indifferents|indifferents]] doctrine.

> "the Stoics rely on their doctrine of oikeiôsis ('appropriation' or 'affiliation'; the Greek term suggests a process whereby objects come to be recognized as oikeion – as 'appropriate' or 'affiliated' or 'belonging' to the creature in question)" (SEP, [Stoicism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/), §4.6)

The doctrine starts at birth: "straight from birth, every creature seeks to preserve its own constitution, even when this is painful", and its own constitution is "the first thing each creature sees as oikeion to it and, hence, as an object of concern". Reason then reorganises the process: with adulthood "reason supervenes as the craftsman of impulse", and concern widens to offspring, community, and finally, in virtue of shared rationality, to the cosmopolis of all rational beings. Cicero states the extension plainly: "it could not be consistent for nature both to desire the production of offspring and not to be concerned that offspring should be loved" (all from the same SEP section, with the entry's ancient locators).

In the compendium, S010 practises oikeiosis as appropriate self-love: the same mechanism that begins as self-preservation is what, matured by reason, turns care outward.

**Affective disposition theory** — Zillmann — media enjoyment depends on judgments of characters: we like seeing liked characters succeed and disliked ones suffer.

## Field

Sociocultural · Psychological science

The theory, first laid out by Dolf Zillmann and Joanne Cantor, holds that enjoyment comes from moral judgments about characters and what happens to them. Raney's summary of its three components: "1) the dispositions formed and held toward a character, 2) the emotional reactivity to the plight of that character, and 3) the viewer's hedonic response to the ultimate resolution of the narrative" ([PMC, Model of Individual Difference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11391638/)).

### How it works

The dispositions are built by moral judgment. "Morally good and appropriate judgments lead to positive affective dispositions, whereas behavior appraised as morally wrong leads to negative affective dispositions. Positive affective dispositions lead to hope for a positive outcome for the character, whereas negative affective dispositions lead to contrasting anticipation and apprehension" ([PMC, Model of Individual Difference](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11391638/)). Zillmann's own later formulation describes viewers as "untiring moral monitors" who continually render judgments about characters' behavior throughout the experience ([Zillmann 2000, p. 54, as quoted in the Chapman dissertation](https://digitalcommons.chapman.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1038&context=comm_articles)). The classic experiment: Zillmann and Cantor manipulated sympathy for a protagonist and found participants enjoyed a rewarding outcome if the protagonist had acted benevolently, but not if the protagonist had misbehaved ([Oxford Research Encyclopedia, Media Entertainment and Emotions](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61798/chapter/546237004)).

The enjoyment structure this produces is the one the compendium flags: "viewers (even those who are not generally vindictive) are allowed to wish for terrible things to happen to characters in proportion to the immorality of the character" ([VU disposition development paper](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/3102030/disposition%20development%20in%20drama%20IJART.pdf)). Suspense is what the delay of the hoped-for outcome produces, and excitation transfer explains why the distress of suspense can convert to enjoyment at the resolution ([LMU PDF on cognitive and affective challenge](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/42717/1/0093650214565921.pdf)).

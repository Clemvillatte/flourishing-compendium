The corruption of amour-propre is Rousseau's account, in the Discourse on the Origin of Inequality, of how a natural passion becomes a social vice. Amour-propre is self-love in its comparative form: caring how one stands against others. In Cole's translation of Rousseau's footnote it appears as "egoism", set against self-respect (amour de soi), the plain wish to preserve oneself.

> "Egoism must not be confused with self-respect: for they differ both in themselves and in their effects. Self-respect is a natural feeling which leads every animal to look to its own preservation, and which, guided in man by reason and modified by compassion, creates humanity and virtue. Egoism is a purely relative and factitious feeling, which arises in the state of society, leads each individual to make more of himself than of any other, causes all the mutual damage men inflict one on another, and is the real source of the \"sense of honour.\"" (Rousseau, Discourse on the Origin of Inequality, footnote to Part One, Cole translation, [Project Gutenberg #46333](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46333/46333-0.txt))

### How society corrupts it

The Discourse then shows the corruption happening once people gather. In the older anonymous translation: "Every one begins to survey the rest, and wishes to be surveyed himself; and public esteem acquires a value." Talent and appearance become rank, and the outcome is "on one side vanity and contempt, on the other envy and shame". Esteem becomes something each person can claim, "and it was no longer safe for any man to refuse it to another", so civility itself begins as a response to this new danger. Once standing is a good that other people control, managing their opinion of you becomes the natural next move, and scheming is that move made a habit.

“The enframing (Gestell)” — Heidegger, "The Question Concerning Technology".

### What it is

In "The Question Concerning Technology", Heidegger argues that the essence of modern technology is not a machine or an instrument but a way of revealing: it summons everything to show up as orderable stuff. He names that summons Gestell, enframing: "We now name that challenging claim which gathers man thither to order the self-revealing as standing-reserve: 'Ge-stell' [Enframing]" (Heidegger, "The Question Concerning Technology", trans. Lovitt, p. 19, [online text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/QCT/QCT.019.html)). His definition: "Enframing means the gathering together of that setting-upon which sets upon man, i.e., challenges him forth, to reveal the real, in the mode of ordering, as standing-reserve. Enframing means that way of revealing which holds sway in the essence of modern technology and which is itself nothing technological" (p. 20, [online text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/QCT/QCT.020.html)).

### Where it comes from

The essay was first delivered as lectures in 1949-1953 and published in 1954, drawing on the later Heidegger's account of truth as unconcealment. Older craft revealed the world by bringing things forth; modern technology reveals by challenging, and the difference is visible in what shows up: "Everywhere everything is ordered to stand by, to be immediately at hand, indeed to stand there just so that it may be on call for a further ordering... We call it the standing-reserve [Bestand]" (p. 17, [online text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/QCT/QCT.017.html)). An airliner "stands on the taxi strip only as standing-reserve, inasmuch as it is ordered to ensure the possibility of transportation" (p. 17, [online text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/QCT/QCT.017.html)).

### Core claims

Enframing is a destiny of revealing, not a human invention that could simply be voted away. When that destiny reigns, it is the danger at its height: "Yet when destining reigns in the mode of Enframing, it is the supreme danger" (p. 26, [online text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/QCT/QCT.026.html)). The danger is that the orderable view of things becomes the only view, so that man too is finally taken as standing-reserve. Heidegger closes the movement with Hölderlin: "But where danger is, grows / The saving power also", adding that "if there is truth in Hölderlin's words, then the rule of enframing cannot exhaust itself solely in blocking all lighting-up of every revealing, all appearing of truth" (p. 28, [online text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/QCT/QCT.028.html)).

### How it explains the technology principle

E101 practises the critique of technology by asking what a tool reveals and what it conceals. Enframing supplies the diagnosis: the spreadsheet, the productivity app and the "human resources" department all belong to a way of revealing in which things, and people, show up as stock. The compendium's practical reading is that the critique is not of any machine but of a habit of seeing, and that noticing the habit is already the beginning of the saving power Heidegger names.

### An everyday example

A hiker checks her watch, her route, her heart rate and the crowd forecast before deciding whether the mountain is worth climbing. The mountain has not changed; it now shows up as a bundle of metrics. The enframing is not in the devices but in the glance that arrives at the ridge already priced.

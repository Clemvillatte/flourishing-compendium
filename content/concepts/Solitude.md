Solitude is the state of being alone, which can be chosen or imposed, and which is distinct from loneliness. This page covers three points: the line between solitude and loneliness, one experimental study of what being alone does to mood, and Thoreau's account in Walden.

### Solitude and loneliness

Wikipedia's Solitude article describes the state as one of seclusion with effects that "can be either positive or negative, depending on the situation". Its footnote quotes Paul Tillich's The Eternal Now: "Our language has wisely sensed these two sides of being alone. It has created the word 'loneliness' to express the pain of being alone. And it has created the word 'solitude' to express the glory of being alone." The quotation is taken from that footnote, not from Tillich's book. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solitude))

### What an experiment found

Nguyen, Ryan and Deci (2018) ran four studies on solitude and mood. Being alone generally lowered both positive and negative high-arousal feelings, and this did not depend on whether people were reading.

> "Finally, in Study 4, we found that solitude could lead to relaxation and reduced stress when individuals actively chose to be alone." (Nguyen, Ryan & Deci 2018, [PubMed 29072531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29072531/))

So the benefit in this study was tied to choice. The authors do not claim that solitude itself is beneficial for everyone. The abstract frames the work as clarifying why earlier findings differed.

### Thoreau on the uses of solitude

Thoreau's Walden has a chapter titled "Solitude", where he separates solitude from physical distance.

> "I find it wholesome to be alone the greater part of the time. To be in company, even with the best, is soon wearisome and dissipating. I love to be alone. I never found the companion that was so companionable as solitude." (Thoreau, Walden, "Solitude", [Gutenberg #205](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/205))

A few lines later he adds: "Solitude is not measured by the miles of space that intervene between a man and his fellows."

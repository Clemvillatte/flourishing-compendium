**Relational psychoanalysis** — Mitchell, Greenberg — the self forms through relationships; therapy examines the mutual therapeutic relationship.

Relational psychoanalysis is the tradition founded on Jay Greenberg and Stephen Mitchell's 1983 synthesis. The Sigmund Freud Museum's account states its origin and core:

> "Jay Greenberg and Stephen Mitchell coined the term 'relational' in their work 'Object Relations in Psychoanalytic Theory', published in 1983. Here they formulate their intention to combine interpersonal psychoanalysis, which goes back to Erich Fromm, Clara Thompson, and Harry Stack Sullivan, with object relations theory." ([Relational Psychoanalysis, Sigmund Freud Museum Vienna](https://www.freud-museum.at/en/analysis-interminable/articles/relational-psychoanalysis))

### The relational matrix

The same account states what the tradition rejects and what it puts in place: "The Freudian perception of the drive is rejected by relational psychoanalysis as monadic; instead, the concept of the 'relational matrix' as the primary structure of relationships becomes the focal point." The clinical consequence follows: "The analyst is no longer an external, judgmental, interpretative observer, but always also a co-constituent of the psychoanalytic setting."

Deep listening is listening with the sole intention of understanding: attention as generosity, in the ear's register.

### The psychological statement

Its psychological statement is Carl Rogers' empathic understanding:

> "To sense the client's private world as if it were your own, but without ever losing the 'as if' quality—this is empathy, and this seems essential to therapy." (Rogers 1957, [full-text reprint](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf))

The "as if" is the check that keeps understanding from becoming takeover.

### The contemplative statement

Its contemplative statement is Thich Nhat Hanh's:

> "Deep listening is the kind of listening that can help relieve the suffering of another person. You can call it compassionate listening. You listen with only one purpose: to help him or her to empty his heart." (Thich Nhat Hanh, [in conversation with Oprah Winfrey, Plum Village](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/oprah-talks-to-thich-nhat-hanh))

Even when the speaker's words are full of wrong perceptions and bitterness, the listener does not interrupt or argue, because "one hour like that can bring transformation and healing" ([same interview](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/oprah-talks-to-thich-nhat-hanh)).

### What makes it deep

The mechanism: what makes listening deep is what it withholds. Advice, rebuttal, and one-upping each replace the speaker's experience with the listener's; deep listening holds those moves in abeyance so that the speaker can hear themselves, often for the first time.

The distinction to hold: deep listening is not agreement and not passivity. The listener's full attention is an act, and understanding a view is not endorsing it; the compassion is in the receiving, not in the verdict.

### In everyday life

The smallest version: twenty minutes, one person, no advice, no rebuttal, the sole intention being understanding. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

Its contemplative cognates are Buber's I–Thou meeting ([[I and Thou]]) and the Buddhist practice of listening deeply.

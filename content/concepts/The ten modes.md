The Pyrrhonian patterns of argument (discrepancies, relativity, infinity, hypothesis, circularity, etc.) used to produce equipollence and suspension ([Wikipedia Pyrrhonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhonism)).

The ten modes are Aenesidemus's argument forms for setting appearances against each other. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy introduces them:

> "Aenesidemus is famous for having developed Ten Modes or Tropes—forms of argument by which the sceptic puts appearances and thoughts into opposition." (Vogt, [SEP: Ancient Skepticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skepticism-ancient/))

### The argument structure

The modes work through a fixed sequence:

> "The basic elements of Aenesidemus' skepticism are: the skeptic puts appearances and thoughts into opposition; this generates equipollence (isostheneia, lit. 'of equal weight') between several appearances and/or thoughts; suspension of judgment follows; with it comes tranquility; and the skeptic leads a life according to appearances (DL 9.62, 78, 106–7)." (Vogt, [SEP: Ancient Skepticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skepticism-ancient/))

### The modes themselves

The SEP entry lists them: arguments from the differences between kinds of animals; differences among human beings; differences between the senses; states and dispositions of the perceiver; positions, distances and places; mixtures; the composition of the perceived object; relativity; constancy or rarity of occurrence; and ways of life, customs and laws. As the entry notes, the relativity mode "comprises at least 10–1 to 10–7, or all Ten Modes" ([SEP: Ancient Skepticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skepticism-ancient/)), and the modes "offer strategies for suspension of judgment on both kinds of questions."

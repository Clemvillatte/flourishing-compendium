Aristotle's virtue of friendship, treated across Books VIII and IX of the Nicomachean Ethics. He opens the longest single topic in the work with its stakes:

> "friendship is a kind of virtue or implies virtue. It is also indispensable to life. For nobody would choose to live without friends, although he were in possession of every other good." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.1, 1892 English edition, [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/cu31924012537001))

The three kinds of friendship differ in what the friendship is for. Friendships of utility seem "especially to occur among old people, as in old age we look to profit rather than pleasure", while "the friendship of the young is based upon pleasure", and so is quick to form and quick to end, "for the friendship changes with the object which pleases them" (VIII.3).

The best philia is the third kind:

> "the perfect friendship or love is the friendship or love of people who are good and alike in virtue; for these people are alike in wishing each other's good, in so far as they are good, and they are good in themselves. But it is people who wish the good of their friends for their friend's sake that are in the truest sense friends, as their friendship is the consequence of their own character, and is not an accident." (VIII.4)

In the compendium, A063 carries the necessity-for-flourishing claim, A064 the three-kinds teaching, and [[Eudaimonia]] is the life friendship serves.

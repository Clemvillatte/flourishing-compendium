Dialogism is Mikhail Bakhtin's view that meaning and selfhood arise between voices, in answering and being answered, and are never settled by one speaker alone. The Filosofia encyclopedia of Russian thought describes it as "an architectonics of utterances", and pairs it with polyphony, "a decentered authorial stance that grants validity to all voices", both "exemplified by the prose of Dostoevsky" ([Filosofia, "Bakhtin, Mikhail"](https://filosofia.dickinson.edu/encyclopedia/bakhtin-mikhail/)).

### Voices that stay independent

In Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics, Bakhtin contrasted "monologic" novels with Dostoevsky's "dialogic" ones. A review summarises the point: the reader meets "a plurality of independent and unmerged voices and consciousnesses" (p. 6 of the book), not a single authorial voice ([Project MUSE review](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/417186/summary)). The encyclopedia's author, Caryl Emerson, adds that the book "declares itself a formal enterprise: its concern is the working of words", including "the double-voiced word", in the narrator's zone as well as in the characters' speech ([Filosofia](https://filosofia.dickinson.edu/encyclopedia/bakhtin-mikhail/)).

### Why many languages help

Emerson reads dialogism outwards into "heteroglossia", the many languages of a society. "The more languages there are, the more modest and inadequate each is shown to be (this is healthy for all speakers), the more angles of approach to a topic or a person become available." On her reading, Bakhtin treats unity as compatible with difference: "every part is essential to the whole, yet every part is different from every other" ([Filosofia](https://filosofia.dickinson.edu/encyclopedia/bakhtin-mikhail/)).

### Limits and cautions

Emerson notes that the Dostoevsky book mutes the moral side of the plots, and that Bakhtin "comes close to exempting Dostoevskian heroes from moral responsibility". That reading of polyphony "has stimulated vigorous debate". She also warns that "today, dialogue is altogether too easy—too easy to start, to fake, to fritter our selves away on", and that private feeds have produced "a decentered, exclusionary dialogic situation" ([Filosofia](https://filosofia.dickinson.edu/encyclopedia/bakhtin-mikhail/)).

### Scope

The page relies on one encyclopedia entry and one review, not on Bakhtin's own text. Quotations from the book are as reported by the review.

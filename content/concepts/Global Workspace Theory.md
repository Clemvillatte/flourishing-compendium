Global workspace theory says conscious access happens when information is made available to many brain systems at once. Bernard Baars's 2005 review traces the idea to cognitive science: it "emerged from the cognitive architecture tradition", where Newell and co-workers "were the first to show the utility of a GW or 'blackboard' architecture in a distributed set of knowledge sources, which could cooperatively solve problems that no single constituent could solve alone" ([Baars, 2005, Progress in Brain Research, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16186014/)).

### The neuronal version

Stanislas Dehaene and Jean-Pierre Changeux's 2011 review states the neuroscientific version. In the Global Neuronal Workspace (GNW) model, "conscious access occurs when incoming information is made globally available to multiple brain systems through a network of neurons with long-range axons densely distributed in prefrontal, parieto-temporal, and cingulate cortices" ([Dehaene and Changeux, Neuron, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21521609/)). They name three measures of conscious access: "late amplification of relevant sensory activity, long-distance cortico-cortical synchronization at beta and gamma frequencies, and 'ignition' of a large-scale prefronto-parietal network".

The review limits its scope to "how an external or internal piece of information goes beyond nonconscious processing and gains access to conscious processing", a transition marked by "a reportable subjective experience". The review confines itself to that question.

### Put to an adversarial test

In 2025 the Cogitate Consortium published a preregistered test that pitted the global neuronal workspace theory against integrated information theory. With 256 participants and fMRI, MEG and intracranial EEG, they "found information about conscious content in visual, ventrotemporal and inferior frontal cortex". The results "align with some predictions of IIT and GNWT, while substantially challenging key tenets of both theories". For the workspace theory, "GNWT is challenged by the general lack of ignition at stimulus offset and limited representation of certain conscious dimensions in the prefrontal cortex" ([Cogitate Consortium, Nature 642, 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40307561/)).

The consortium's own summary was that both theories made predictions that were partly supported and partly challenged by the data ([ARC-COGITATE](https://x.com/ArcCogitate/status/1917608094642721019)). The University of Oxford's account says the prefrontal cortex responded to a general category but not to details such as an object's direction or identity ([Oxford Psychology](https://www.psych.ox.ac.uk/news/a-landmark-experiment-puts-leading-theories-of-consciousness-to-the-test)).

### What the essays can say

Global workspace theory is a leading, testable account of conscious access. The 2025 results challenged key tenets of it and of its rival, and settled neither.

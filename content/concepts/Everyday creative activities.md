Everyday creative activities are the ordinary acts of making things, such as crafting, cooking, writing or making music, studied as a daily behavior with well-being consequences. The study opened for this page, Conner, DeYoung and Silvia's daily-diary study of young adults, examined whether creative activity on one day predicts affect and flourishing on the next.

> "Lagged multilevel models revealed that people felt higher activated PA and flourishing following days when they reported more creative activity than usual. The other direction – PA predicting next-day creative activity – was not supported, suggesting that the cross-day effect was specific to creative activity predicting well-being." (Conner, DeYoung & Silvia, "Everyday creative activity as a path to flourishing", Journal of Positive Psychology 13(2), 181-189, 2018, [APA record](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2018-01905-009))

### The study behind the finding

The sample was 658 young adults keeping a 13-day daily diary, reporting each day how much time they spent on creative activities, their daily positive and negative affect, and their daily flourishing. The cross-day effect ran from making to feeling, not the reverse; the study is observational, so it links the two without showing that assigning creative homework would lift anyone's flourishing.

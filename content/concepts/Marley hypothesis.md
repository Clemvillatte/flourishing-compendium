Nelson, Adams and Salter's account of why perceptions of racism differ across racial groups: denial of racism reflects ignorance of history.

> "The Marley hypothesis suggests that racial differences in perceptions of racism stem from privileged group members' (e.g., White Americans) lack of knowledge about Black history (Nelson et al., 2013). These authors found that White (compared to Black) Americans lacked knowledge about Critical Black History (i.e., Black history of injustice), which explained their inability to recognize racism, relative to Black Americans." (Martin & Johnson, [You Can't Dismantle What You Don't Recognize](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972773/), Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2023, "Learning Critical Black History", summarizing Nelson, Adams & Salter 2013)

The hypothesis locates a disagreement about facts, rather than a difference in values or sensitivity, behind much denial of racism ([Martin & Johnson, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10972773/)).

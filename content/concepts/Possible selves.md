The paper that introduced the concept frames it as a complement to self-knowledge: possible selves are the future-oriented contents of the self-concept, and they do two kinds of work. They function as incentives for future behavior — selves to be approached or avoided — and they provide an evaluative and interpretive context for the current view of self.

> "Possible selves represent individuals' ideas of what they might become, what they would like to become, and what they are afraid of becoming, and thus provide a conceptual link between cognition and motivation." (Markus & Nurius, 1986, American Psychologist 41(9), 954–969, abstract)

The authors give the categories concrete faces:

> "The possible selves that are hoped for might include the successful self, the creative self, the rich self, the thin self, or the loved and admired self, whereas the dreaded possible selves could be the alone self, the depressed self, the incompetent self, the alcoholic self, the unemployed self, or the bag lady self." (Markus & Nurius, 1986, p. 954)

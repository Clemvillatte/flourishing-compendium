**Interdependence Theory** — Thibaut, Kelley — relationship outcomes are judged by comparison level and comparison level for alternatives; structure shapes behaviour.

Thibaut and Kelley's 1959 book defined the two standards that carry the theory:

> "The first of these, called the comparison level (or CL), is the standard against which the member evaluates the 'attractiveness' of the relationship or how satisfactory it is. The second, called the comparison level for alternatives (or CLalt), is the standard the member uses in deciding whether to remain in or to leave the relationship." (Thibaut and Kelley 1959, The Social Psychology of Groups, ch. 2, [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/download/socialpsychology00thib/socialpsychology00thib_djvu.txt), OCR spacing normalised)

The comparison level itself is calibrated by the person's sense of entitlement:

> "CL is a standard by which the person evaluates the rewards and costs of a given relationship in terms of what he feels he 'deserves'." (Thibaut and Kelley 1959, ch. 2, [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/download/socialpsychology00thib/socialpsychology00thib_djvu.txt), OCR spacing normalised)

The book's other founding idea is structural: the outcomes of a dyad are jointly determined, so much of what a person gets from a relationship is not produced by that person alone. The pair of standards exists, the authors note, because circumstances may require a person to remain in a relationship he regards as unsatisfactory, which is why satisfaction and staying are two different verdicts. The theory's questions — what you deserve, what the alternative is, who controls what — are therefore questions about the situation before they are questions about the person in it. The CL standard is built from what the person believes other people achieve and from his own past outcomes, which makes it movable by both comparison and memory.

**Plain words** Two numbers decide how a relationship feels: what you are getting, against what you think you deserve; and what you could get elsewhere, against whether leaving is possible. The first says whether you are satisfied; the second says whether you stay.

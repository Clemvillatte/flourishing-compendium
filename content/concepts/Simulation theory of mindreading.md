Simulation theory of mindreading holds that we understand other minds not by applying a theory about people, but by using our own mental machinery to model theirs and reading off the result. Matteo Mameli and Lisa Bortolotti state the general account:

> "This consists in using one's own psychological mechanisms to model the psychological mechanisms of another person." (Mameli & Bortolotti, [Animal rights, animal minds, and human mindreading](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2563326/), Journal of Medical Ethics, 2006)

Vittorio Gallese's later development of the account, embodied simulation, states its proposal:

> "It proposes that our social interactions become meaningful by means of reusing our own mental states or processes in functionally attributing them to others." (Gallese, [Bodily selves in relation: embodied simulation as second-person perspective on intersubjectivity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4006180/), Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 2014)

### Origin

The account was linked to mirror-neuron research in Gallese and Goldman's 1998 paper "Mirror neurons and the simulation theory of mind-reading" (Trends in Cognitive Sciences 2(12), 493-501); Gallese's later work frames it as embodied simulation, whose origin the 2014 article records in the discovery of mirror mechanisms.

### Core claims

The general theory's model of the mechanism:

> "the mindreader takes a part of her own psychological system "off line", she pretends that she is in the situation of the other person, she observes the mental states that this off line use of her own psychological system provokes in her, and finally she attributes to the other person the mental states provoked by the simulation." (Mameli & Bortolotti, [same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2563326/))

Gallese's embodied version conceives of simulation differently:

> "simulation is conceived of as a non-conscious, pre-reflective functional mechanism of the brain–body system, whose function is to model objects, agents and events." ([Gallese, same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4006180/))

The same paper records the theory's origin in the discovery of mirror mechanisms: "The discovery of mirror mechanisms for action, emotions and sensations led to the proposal of an embodied approach to intersubjectivity—embodied simulation (ES) theory" ([Gallese, same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4006180/)).

### In everyday life

Illustrative scenario, not a reported study: watching a child reach for a jammed door, a parent feels the pull in her own arm and understands what the child is trying to do before either says anything. On the embodied-simulation account, that felt understanding is the mechanism doing the understanding.

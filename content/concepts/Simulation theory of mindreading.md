Simulation theory of mindreading holds that we understand other minds not by applying a theory about people, but by using our own mental machinery to replicate their states and reading off the result. Vittorio Gallese's later development of the account, embodied simulation, states its proposal:

> "It proposes that our social interactions become meaningful by means of reusing our own mental states or processes in functionally attributing them to others." (Gallese, [Bodily selves in relation: embodied simulation as second-person perspective on intersubjectivity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4006180/), Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 2014)

### Origin

The account was linked to mirror-neuron research in Gallese and Goldman's 1998 paper "Mirror neurons and the simulation theory of mind-reading" (Trends in Cognitive Sciences), which argued that mirror activity — the same circuits firing for observed and executed actions — accords with simulation theory and would not be predicted by theory theory. Gallese's later work frames it as embodied simulation.

### Core claims

The theory's model of the mechanism:

> "simulation is conceived of as a non-conscious, pre-reflective functional mechanism of the brain–body system, whose function is to model objects, agents and events." (same article)

The same paper records the theory's origin in the discovery of mirror mechanisms: "The discovery of mirror mechanisms for action, emotions and sensations led to the proposal of an embodied approach to intersubjectivity—embodied simulation (ES) theory".

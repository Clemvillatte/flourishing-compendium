The examined economics is the Apology read as a pricing problem: Socrates's [[The unexamined life|unexamined life]], priced. Socrates, offered his life on the condition of silence, declines — and the sentence he puts on the other side is not rhetorical:

> "I say that this is the greatest good for a human being — to have discussions every day about virtue and the other things about which you hear me conversing and examining myself and others; and that the unexamined life is not worth living for a human being."

(Plato, *Apology* 38a; [askaphilosopher, The examined life](https://askaphilosopher.org/2018/02/26/the-examined-life/); Greek ὁ δὲ ἀνεξέταστος βίος οὐ βιωτὸς ἀνθρώπῳ, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_unexamined_life_is_not_worth_living).)

### The price in the text

The trial scene itself does the arithmetic. When the jury asks him to propose a counter-penalty, Socrates prices the offense, not the life: "Had I money I might have estimated the offence at what I was able to pay... perhaps I could afford a mina, and therefore I propose that penalty: Plato, Crito, Critobulus, and Apollodorus, my friends here, bid me say thirty minae, and they will be the sureties" (*Apology* 38b-c, [Jowett translation](https://wordsmith.org/board/ubbthreads.php?ubb=showflat&Number=55088)). The exchange is the point: everything in the courtroom can be priced in minae, from the offense to the fine to the sureties, except the one thing Socrates has, which he declines to sell. His counter-offer of maintenance in the Prytaneum is the same joke from the other direction.

### The echo across traditions

The cross-tradition echo is the same ledger kept elsewhere: traditions that rank goods end up implying an exchange rate between them, and the Stoic dichotomy of control is a pricing rule of exactly this kind, assigning everything external a market tag and leaving only assent unpriced. The principle is one the compendium's research side states in economic language: to live deliberately is to price things, and the examined life is the asset whose price, when you finally quote it honestly, comes out to everything you have.

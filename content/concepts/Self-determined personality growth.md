"Self-determined personality growth" is the wiki's label, not a term I found in the sources opened. The seed cites "Deci and Ryan, 2000", which probably points to the self-determination theory (SDT) paper listed in PubMed as Ryan and Deci (2000), in American Psychologist: "Human beings can be proactive and engaged or, alternatively, passive and alienated, largely as a function of the social conditions in which they develop and function." Research "has focused on the social-contextual conditions that facilitate versus forestall the natural processes of self-motivation and healthy psychological development". The abstract postulates "three innate psychological needs--competence, autonomy, and relatedness--which when satisfied yield enhanced self-motivation and mental health and when thwarted lead to diminished motivation and well-being" ([Ryan and Deci, Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/)). Whether this is the exact paper the seed meant is unconfirmed, because the seed gives no title.

### Growth over the lifespan

Sheldon and Kasser's cross-sectional study of 108 adults aged 17 to 82 found that older people listed more strivings concerning generativity and ego integrity, fewer concerning identity and intimacy, and that "measured maturity mediated the relationship between age and well-being" ([Sheldon and Kasser, Getting Older, Getting Better? Developmental Psychology 2001, ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ637760)). A later paper found that older participants reported greater relative autonomy, and that the effect of autonomy on well-being was modest: "other factors besides increased autonomy must also be taken into account" ([Sheldon, Houser-Marko and Kasser, Does autonomy increase with age? Journal of Research in Personality 2006, PDF](http://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_SheldonHouserKasser_JRP.pdf)).

### Caution for essays

Both are correlational designs in specific samples, and the authors describe age differences, not proven personality growth caused by any practice. Use SDT for the claim that supportive conditions foster growth, and use these studies only for the claim that autonomy and maturity tend to be higher in older adults.

**Social Impact Theory** — Latané's 1981 model of how other people affect an individual. The theory's core claim, in the original abstract: "when other people are the source of impact and the individual is the target, impact should be a multiplicative function of the strength, immediacy, and number of other people." ([Latané, American Psychologist 36(4), 1981](https://scispace.com/papers/the-psychology-of-social-impact-rmbkpyfw8x)) Strength is who the sources are, immediacy is how close they stand, number is how many; because the combination is multiplicative, zero on any one cancels the rest.

### Division of impact

The theory also predicts how impact spreads when others stand beside you as fellow targets: "when other people stand with the individual as the target of forces from outside the group, impact should be divided such that the resultant is an inverse power function of the strength, immediacy, and number of persons standing together." And the effect of number shows diminishing returns: "impact should take the form of a power function, with the marginal effect of the Nth other person being less than that of the (N — l)th." The tenth shout barely adds to nine. Latané's review draws the evidence from a deliberately wide shelf — conformity and imitation, stage fright and embarrassment, news interest, bystander intervention, tipping, productivity in groups, even crowding in rats — to show one force law under all of them. The compendium's use: pressure that feels personal can be read structurally — a function of who, how near, and how many — which makes it countable, and therefore placed.

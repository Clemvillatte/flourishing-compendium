Cooperative learning is the classroom application of social interdependence theory, the research program David Johnson and Roger Johnson built on foundations from Kurt Lewin and Morton Deutsch. "Cooperative learning is an educational approach which aims to organize classroom activities into academic and social learning experiences" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative_learning)). The premise of the underlying theory is that "how participants' goals are structured determines the ways they interact and the interaction pattern determine the outcomes of the situation" (Wikipedia).

The Johnsons' meta-analysis of the classroom evidence reports that "students in cooperative learning settings compared to those in individualistic or competitive learning settings, achieve more, reason better, gain higher self-esteem, like classmates and the learning tasks more and have more perceived social support" (Wikipedia). The approach is structural rather than just grouping: "There is much more to cooperative learning than merely arranging students into groups" (Wikipedia). Their conditions for effective group work are positive interdependence, individual accountability, promotive interaction, taught social skills and group processing (Wikipedia).

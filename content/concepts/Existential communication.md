"Existential communication" — Jaspers, Philosophy.

Existential communication is Karl Jaspers' term, developed across the second volume of his Philosophy (1932), for the kind of exchange in which two people address each other as selves rather than as cases. Jaspers' word for the authentic self is Existenz, and his claim is that this self does not exist prior to the exchange: "I am only in communication with another" (Jaspers, quoted in [Sollie, Existenz 2/1](https://www.existenz.us/volumes/Vol.2Sollie.html)).

The communication is not ordinary conversation, and Jaspers says so with his own emphasis:

> "Communication in the idea, and in its realisation by Existenz, will move a man closer to his fellow-man than will the intellect or a purpose, or primitive community." (Jaspers, quoted in [Salamun, Existenz 1/1](https://www.existenz.us/volumes/Vol.1Salamun.pdf))

Jaspers' phrase for the relation inside such communication is a "loving struggle" ([Salamun](https://www.existenz.us/volumes/Vol.1Salamun.pdf)): the partners contend with each other, in truth, without the aim of winning. The point is not agreement but selfhood. Here the idea stands behind [[E093 — Practise existential communication]].

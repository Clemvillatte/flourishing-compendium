Phenomenological methods in psychology are the research designs that take first-person experience as data: the epoché and reduction of the Husserlian tradition, adapted into descriptive research. Experience sampling is the survey-based cousin with a different family tree: the history of the method traces it to a 1977 proposal by Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi, Reed Larson and Susan Prescott to page people at random moments in daily life, building on naturalistic and idiographic research traditions ([Conner, Tennen, Fleeson and Barrett, Experience Sampling Methods, PMC2773515](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2773515/)). What the two arms share is the question, asked of a person in the moment rather than afterward: what is happening in experience now. The modern arm's flagship anchors the case in Killingsworth and Gilbert's 2010 Science paper, whose method paragraph states it: "The most reliable method for investigating real-world emotion is experience sampling, which involves contacting people as they engage in their everyday activities and asking them to report their thoughts, feelings, and actions at that moment" ([Killingsworth and Gilbert, A Wandering Mind Is an Unhappy Mind, Science 330](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/everything/killingsworth2010a.pdf)).

### What the flagship study did

The iPhone application contacted participants at random moments during waking hours with three questions, a happiness rating on a sliding scale from very bad to very good, the current activity, and "Are you thinking about something other than what you're currently doing?" The database reached nearly a quarter of a million samples from about 5,000 people in 83 countries, and the analyzed sample was 2,250 adults. The findings: "Mind wandering occurred in 46.9% of the samples," people "were less happy when their minds were wandering than when they were not," and time-lag analyses suggested the wandering "was generally the cause, and not merely the consequence, of unhappiness." The conclusion is quotable to one line: "a human mind is a wandering mind, and a wandering mind is an unhappy mind" ([Science 330, full text](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/files/papers/others/everything/killingsworth2010a.pdf)).

### The older method behind it

The phenomenological arm is older than the smartphone. The compendium's E-principles carry it as a practice: E001 and E003 practise the reduction and the natural attitude's suspension, E006 the epoché in daily life, E012 the reduction as ongoing rather than once-for-all, E004 the lifeworld the reduction discloses; ST260's Brentano gives the direction of intentionality the whole method rests on; B043's vipassana is the contemplative first-person observer; P004 starts from experience rather than theory and A044 learns from it; P190's precariousness-and-stability is the kind of structure the method finds.

The Aspiration Index is Kasser and Ryan's instrument for measuring what a person's life goals are — and the finding it carries is that the materialistic set predicts worse well-being. The theory's own site describes the construct and the measure:

> "Aspirations refer to people's life goals, and SDT research on aspirations has focused on the relative strength of intrinsic aspirations (viz., meaningful relationships, personal growth, and community contributions) versus extrinsic aspirations (viz., wealth, fame, and image)... The Aspiration Index was developed to assess people's aspirations. There are 7 categories of aspirations with five specific items within each category. The seven categories include: the extrinsic aspirations of wealth, fame, and image; the intrinsic aspirations of meaningful relationships, personal growth, and community contributions; and the aspiration of good health which turned out not to be clearly either extrinsic or intrinsic."
> — [Self-Determination Theory site, "Aspirations Index (AI)" page (theory's official instrument description)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/aspirations-index/)

Respondents rate each aspiration for "(1) the importance to themselves of each aspiration, (2) their beliefs about the likelihood of attaining each, and (3) the degree to which they have already attained each" ([SDT site](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/aspirations-index/)). The well-being finding behind the seed line is summarized in Ryan and Deci's SDT review: "They found, first, that placing strong relative importance on intrinsic aspirations was positively associated with well-being indicators such as self-esteem, self-actualization, and the inverse of depression and anxiety, whereas placing strong relative importance on extrinsic aspirations was negatively related to these well-being indicators" ([Ryan & Deci 2000, American Psychologist 55(1):68-78](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

“The stimulus-response gap” — Epictetus, Enchiridion, the doctrine of the impression.

Here, "the stimulus-response gap" is an interpretive label for Epictetus's account of the impression: between what happens and how we respond there is a judgement, and the judgement — not the event — carries the disturbance. The Encheiridion's fifth chapter states it:

> "Men are disturbed not by the things which happen, but by the opinions about the things; for example, death is nothing terrible, for if it were it would have seemed so to Socrates; for the opinion about death that it is terrible, is the terrible thing." (Epictetus, [Encheiridion V, George Long translation, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm))

### The practice

The same chapter turns the doctrine into an instruction:

> "When then we are impeded, or disturbed, or grieved, let us never blame others, but ourselves—that is, our opinions. It is the act of an ill-instructed man to blame others for his own bad condition; it is the act of one who has begun to be instructed, to lay the blame on himself; and of one whose instruction is completed, neither to blame another, nor himself." (Epictetus, [Encheiridion V, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm))

And the first chapter supplies the underlying division of what is and is not ours:

> "Of things some are in our power, and others are not." (Epictetus, [Encheiridion I, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm))

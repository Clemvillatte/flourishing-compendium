“Abgeschiedenheit” — Meister Eckhart.

Abgeschiedenheit is the detachment that Meister Eckhart, the early fourteenth-century German Dominican, places above every other virtue. In his treatise on detachment, transmitted among his German works (DW V:400-436), he weighs the concept against love and against humility, arguing that the classical virtues each remain tied to creatures in some way, while detachment is free from all creatures ([Treatise on Detachment](https://www.ldysinger.com/@texts2/1320_eckhart/04_tract-abgesch.htm)).

> “And as I pore over all the writings, as far as reason can guide and inform me, I find no virtue superior to complete detachment from all things; for all other virtues are in some way concerned with creatures, but detachment is free from all creatures.” (Meister Eckhart, [On Detachment, trans. Luke Dysinger](https://www.ldysinger.com/@texts2/1320_eckhart/04_tract-abgesch.htm))

The treatise's definition gives the concept its steadiness: true detachment is for the spirit to stand as immovable against joy and sorrow, honor, shame and disgrace, as a mountain of lead stands before a little breath of wind ([On Detachment](https://www.ldysinger.com/@texts2/1320_eckhart/04_tract-abgesch.htm)). In modern German the word has drifted toward seclusion and retirement from the world, a quieter sense than Eckhart's technical use ([Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/german-english/abgeschiedenheit)). His own usage is not outward withdrawal: the treatise holds that the outer man may be active while the inner man remains wholly free and immovable, its hinge image for Christ and for Mary ([On Detachment](https://www.ldysinger.com/@texts2/1320_eckhart/04_tract-abgesch.htm)).

The Stanford Encyclopedia reads Eckhart's paired cultivation of detachment (abgescheidenheit) and releasement (gelȃzenheit) as "a set of more Stoic-sounding negative virtues", formed against a background of Aristotelian and Christian virtues of moral and spiritual formation ([SEP, Meister Eckhart](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/meister-eckhart/)). The treatise's own opening reaches the same ranking from a survey of pagan masters, prophets and scripture: only pure detachment surpasses all things ([Walshe trans., Yale](https://german.yale.edu/sites/default/files/meister_eckhart_on_detachment.pdf)). The parallels citing this page carry the same releasement into Buddhist and Daoist practice.

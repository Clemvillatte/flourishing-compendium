Daily self-examination is the habit Zengzi describes in Analects 1.4: a fixed daily check of one's conduct against three duties, performed by a Confucius disciple and recorded at the head of the Analects. James Legge's translation gives the check in his words.

> "The philosopher Tsang said, 'I daily examine myself on three points:— whether, in transacting business for others, I may have been not faithful;— whether, in intercourse with friends, I may have been not sincere;— whether I may have not mastered and practised the instructions of my teacher.'" (Zengzi, Analects 1.4, trans. James Legge, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3330/pg3330.html))

The three points are relational: faithfulness in work done for others, sincerity with friends, and practice of what one's teacher transmitted. The examination runs daily and in the negative direction, asking where one may have fallen short, rather than cataloguing successes.

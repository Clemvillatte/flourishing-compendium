Logotherapy is Viktor Frankl's form of existential therapy, founded on the premise that a person's primary motivation is to find meaning in life. Frankl called it "the Third Viennese School of Psychotherapy" alongside Freud's psychoanalysis and Alfred Adler's individual psychology ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). The school names a clinical method and a theory of motivation at once. Where Freud placed the will to pleasure and Adler the will to power at the center of the psyche, Frankl placed the will to meaning. Wikipedia summarizes the view as: "Our main motivation for living is our will to find meaning in life" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy, as the article's summary of Frankl's view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). The article gives the stronger formulation as the theory's belief: "striving to find meaning in life is the primary, most powerful motivating and driving force in humans" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)).

The therapy's method of treating anticipatory anxiety is paradoxical intention: the patient is asked to intend the very thing they dread. As the Wikipedia article explains, "To relieve the anticipatory anxiety and treat the resulting neuroses, logotherapy offers paradoxical intention, wherein the patient intends to do the opposite of their hyper-intended goal" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)).

Frankl gives three channels through which meaning is found:

> "We can discover this meaning in life in three different ways: (1) by creating a work or doing a deed; (2) by experiencing something or encountering someone; and (3) by the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering" ([Frankl, quoted in Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy))

The third channel carries the book's most quoted claim:

> "everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms – to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances" ([Frankl, quoted in Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy))

The claim as Frankl states it is narrow: attitude is the one thing that can still be chosen. What follows from that for a life of suffering is a question the essays pursue.

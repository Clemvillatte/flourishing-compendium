Necessitarianism is the claim that things could not have been otherwise, and Spinoza is its clearest systematic defender. Nature and everything in it follows with necessity, and freedom is not exemption from that necessity but adequate understanding of it.

The SEP article on Spinoza puts the core in one line:

> That eternal and infinite being we call God, or Nature, acts from the same necessity from which he exists.

(Spinoza, Ethics, Part IV, Preface, as quoted in the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Baruch Spinoza"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/), §2.1)

The human consequence is double. First, the illusion: "we—thinking ourselves free—are, in our ignorance, unaware of just how we are determined by causes" (§2.3). Second, the repair: reason itself is on the side of necessity — "It is of the nature of Reason to regard things as necessary, not as contingent" (Ethics IIp44, §2.3), and "To perceive by way of adequate ideas is to perceive the necessity inherent in Nature" (§2.3). Freedom, on this account, is "free" only in the sense that whatever happens to us results from our own nature rather than from relations with things outside us (§2.4).

My ethics of acceptance runs on this: understanding the causes of a feeling is the only freedom available to it.

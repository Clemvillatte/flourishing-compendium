Following nature's rhythm is the Daoist instruction to align with the way things already move rather than impose a plan on them. The source image is Daodejing 25, the chain of emulation that ends at what the Chinese calls ziran: men emulate earth, earth emulates heaven, heaven emulates the Dao, and the Dao emulates spontaneity ([Robert Eno, Daodejing ch. 25](https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Daodejing-Eno.pdf)).

> "Men emulate earth; earth emulates heaven (tian); heaven emulates the Dao; the Dao emulates spontaneity."
> — [Robert Eno, Daodejing ch. 25](https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Daodejing-Eno.pdf)

The word the chapter hangs on is ziran (自然), whose two parts mean self and so. Eno glosses it as a thing as it is in itself, without regard for forces that may act upon it, and notes that the term later hardened into the noun Nature. The rhythm to be followed is not a schedule someone hands you; it is what a thing does when nothing forces it off course.

The compendium's modern use ties this to the body's own cycles. Where the Daoist asks you not to override the natural course, circadian and hormonal science asks you not to override light, sleep, menstrual-cycle phase and menopause; the rhythm you follow is the one your biology already keeps.

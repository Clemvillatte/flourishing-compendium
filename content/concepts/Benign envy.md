Benign envy is one of the two forms of envy distinguished by van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters (2009): envy that motivates the envier to move up toward the envied person's position. The parent emotion is defined in the same paper's abstract:

> "Envy is the painful emotion caused by the good fortune of others. This research empirically supports the distinction between two qualitatively different types of envy, namely benign and malicious envy. It reveals that the experience of benign envy leads to a moving-up motivation aimed at improving one's own position, whereas the experience of malicious envy leads to a pulling-down motivation aimed at damaging the position of the superior other." (van de Ven et al. 2009, [Emotion 9(3), 419–429](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19485619/))

### The evidence

The distinction is not armchair taxonomy. Study 1 used guided recall of the two envy types in the Netherlands, a culture with separate words for benign and malicious envy, and found the predicted experiential differences. Studies 2 and 3 used samples from the United States and Spain, where a single word exists for both envy types; a latent class analysis confirmed the existence of separate experiences in both cultures as well. The authors discuss the implications of distinguishing the two envy types for theories of cooperation, group performance, and Schadenfreude ([van de Ven et al. 2009, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19485619/)).

### In everyday life

A colleague's promotion can become either an hour spent updating one's own portfolio or an hour spent retelling the unfairness of it. The feeling at the start is the same pain; the motivation it settles into is the fork. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

Existential meaning, in Michael Steger's framework, is meaning in life built on two pillars: comprehension, the ability to find patterns and significance in one's life, and purpose, long-term goals one is committed to. The chapter opened for this page treats meaning in life as an important variable for human well-being.

> "Comprehension encompasses people's ability to find patterns, consistency, and significance in the many events and experiences in their lives, and their synthesis and distillation of the most salient, important, and motivating factors." (Steger, "Meaning in Life", Oxford Handbook of Positive Psychology, 2nd ed., 2009, [chapter abstract](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28153/chapter-abstract/212977120?redirectedFrom=fulltext))

> "Purpose refers to highly motivating, long-term goals about which people are passionate and highly committed." (Steger, "Meaning in Life", 2009, [chapter abstract](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28153/chapter-abstract/212977120?redirectedFrom=fulltext))

### How the pillars work together

The framework joins the two: people devote significant resources to pursuing their purposes, and the most effective and rewarding purposes arise from and are congruent with people's comprehension of their lives. The chapter also distinguishes sources of meaning and the search for meaning as further dimensions of the construct.

Face negotiation theory is Stella Ting-Toomey's account of conflict as a negotiation over face, the image a person claims in front of others. Developed from 1988 on, it explains the culture-based, individual-based and situational factors that shape how communicators approach conflict. Its working distinction: face is a claimed sense of interactional identity in a situation, while facework is the verbal and nonverbal behavior that protects or saves self, other, or mutual face.

> "People in all cultures try to maintain and negotiate face in all communication situations." (Ting-Toomey, [The Matrix of Face: An Updated Face-Negotiation Theory](https://www.diplomacy.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/The_Matrix_of_Face_An_Updated_Face-Negotiation_Theory.pdf), core assumption 1)

### What the theory claims

Face becomes an issue most in emotionally threatening or identity-vulnerable situations, when the situated identities of the communicators are called into question. The cultural value spectrums of individualism-collectivism and power distance shape facework concerns and styles: individualism and collectivism shape members' preferences for self-oriented face concern versus other-oriented or mutual-oriented concern. In conflict-style terms, self-face concern goes with dominating styles and other-face concern with avoiding and integrating styles.

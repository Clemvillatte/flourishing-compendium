The page title is the wiki's label. The research line it points to is social problem solving, the model Thomas D'Zurilla and Arthur Nezu developed from D'Zurilla & Goldfried (1971) and later turned into problem-solving therapy. Its "flexible strategies" feature is the skill the model calls generation of alternative solutions: solving is done over a set of candidate moves, not the first move that occurs to you.

> "the person focuses on the problem-solving goals and tries to identify as many potential solutions as possible, including both conventional and original solutions" (D'Zurilla, Nezu & Maydeu-Olivares 2004, Social Problem Solving: Theory and Assessment, ch. 1, [author-hosted PDF](https://www.ub.edu/gdne/documents/sps_chapter_in_APA_book.pdf))

The model names the failure mode this replaces. The impulsivity-carelessness style is a dysfunctional pattern in which a person "typically considers only a few solution alternatives, often impulsively going with the first idea that comes to mind" (same chapter). The seed's contrast between flexibility and brute persistence is the wiki's editorial frame; what the opened source supports is the contrast between generating many alternatives and grabbing the first one.

The compendium's principles use the line differently: CO045 cites it as the psychology parallel for switching between thinking modes, and M046 anchors its "cleverness joined to humility" reading in the evidence base for problem-solving therapy, which trains these skills directly.

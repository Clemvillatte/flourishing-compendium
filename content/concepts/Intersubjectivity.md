Intersubjectivity is the sharedness of the world: the fact that objects show up for others as well as for you, and that this sharedness is part of what makes them objective. Husserl's late formulation, quoted in the SEP entry:

> Transcendental intersubjectivity is the absolute and only self-sufficient ontological foundation [Seinsboden], out of which everything objective (the totality of objectively real entities, but also every objective ideal world) draws its sense and its validity.

(Husserl, Hua 9/344, as quoted in the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Edmund Husserl"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

The SEP entry states the argument: it is through an encounter with another subject that you come to realize your own perspective is only one among many, and that when others experience the same objects you do, those objects stop being merely objects-for-you and become objective and real. Husserl called the intentionality that leads into the other's experience "the so-called 'empathy'"; the SEP entry quotes him as describing it as "the intentionality in one's own ego which leads into the alien ego" (Hua 9/321). Edith Stein, working in the same tradition, called empathy "a kind of act of perceiving sui generis."

Merleau-Ponty gives the concept its body-side: the SEP entry on other minds treats his Phenomenology of Perception as arguing that we have direct contact with other subjects rather than inferring them, because perception is already an embodied taking-up of a shared world.

My [[E009 — Practise intersubjectivity]] turns the point into an exercise: notice, in a given moment, that the objects you experience are experienced by others too, and that this is given rather than concluded.

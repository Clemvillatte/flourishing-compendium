Alertness and attention fluctuate in cycles shorter than the day, and the most cited of these is the roughly 90-minute oscillation Kleitman proposed as the basic rest-activity cycle (BRAC). Kleitman suggested the sleep-stage cycle was "only a nocturnal fragment" of an ongoing 24-hour rhythm, and predicted it would appear "in cyclic variations in alertness, the often irresistible drowsiness after a big meal, and the relief that some persons get from brief catnaps" ([Kleitman 1961, p. 361, quoted in Lavie's BRAC chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4471-1969-2_13)).

### What the evidence supports

A review of the sleep-deprivation literature finds the honest state of the field: "Ultradian rhythms that modulate daytime human behavior and cognitive performance exist. However, their subtleness may make them susceptible to masking effects from heightened arousal, attention, and motivation." Some EEG studies demonstrate "an approximate 90-min rhythm in arousal," but "no confirmation has been reached for an underlying common basic rest-activity cycle (BRAC) that ties the phases of the nightly 90-min NREM-REM sleep cyclicity to those of any ensuing similar-period wake time alertness and performance rhythms" ([Lavie, "Ultradian Cognitive Performance Rhythms During Sleep Deprivation," Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4020-8352-5_13)).

The clinical framing is similar: the 90-minute BRAC fluctuation in alertness is "also relevant to cognitive performance" ([Time of day and chronotype in cognitive assessment, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10683050/)). What attention researchers agree on is that alertness is not flat; where they still argue is whether one oscillator produces both the night and day versions of the cycle.

The Monk 2005 reference in my original line corresponds to his work on the post-lunch dip in performance, a related daytime rhythm documented in the sleep-regulation literature ([two-process model review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12912424/)).

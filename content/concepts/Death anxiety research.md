Death anxiety research is the clinical-psychology study of fear of death and its role in mental disorder. A review in Clinical Psychology Review proposed treating death anxiety as a transdiagnostic construct:

> "Death anxiety is considered to be a basic fear underlying the development and maintenance of numerous psychological conditions. Treatment of transdiagnostic constructs, such as death anxiety, may increase treatment efficacy across a range of disorders." (Iverach, Menzies and Menzies 2014, abstract, [Clin Psychol Rev 34(7):580-593](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25306232/))

### What the review covers

The review examines the role of Terror Management Theory and Experimental Existential Psychology in understanding death anxiety, outlines inventories used to evaluate its presence and severity, and reviews assessment and treatment evidence in non-clinical and clinical populations ([Iverach et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25306232/)). It concludes that death anxiety appears to be a basic fear at the core of a range of mental disorders, including hypochondriasis, panic disorder, and anxiety and depressive disorders ([Iverach et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25306232/)). The authors judge that large-scale, controlled studies to determine the efficacy of well-established psychological therapies in treating death anxiety as a transdiagnostic construct are warranted ([Iverach et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25306232/)).

The 2011 review frames mind wandering as the cyclic activity of two core processes: perceptual decoupling, the capacity to disengage attention from perception, and meta-awareness, the ability to take explicit note of the current contents of consciousness. The two can come apart in experience: a mind decoupled from perception can drift for stretches before meta-awareness registers the drift.

> "Findings regarding meta-awareness reveal that the mind is only intermittently aware of engaging in mind wandering." (Schooler et al., 2011, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 15(7), 319–326, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21684189/))

### What meta-awareness is

The review's own definition is narrower than the everyday sense of noticing:

> "Meta-awareness, also known as meta-consciousness or metacognitive awareness, can be defined as one's explicit knowledge of the current contents of thought." (Schooler et al., 2011, p. 321, [full text](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2011/2011-Meta-awareness-perceptual-decoupling-and-the-wandering-mind.pdf))

A central premise of the theory is that this knowledge arrives intermittently: individuals periodically notice the current contents of their mind, rather than monitoring them continuously ([p. 321](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2011/2011-Meta-awareness-perceptual-decoupling-and-the-wandering-mind.pdf)).

### Catching the drift

The review's method for measuring the gap is to compare two ways of catching a wandering mind: participants report catching themselves, and experimenters also interrupt with probes. In a reading study, participants caught themselves mind wandering roughly four times in a 45-minute period, yet probes still caught them mind wandering on about 15 per cent of samples ([p. 322](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2011/2011-Meta-awareness-perceptual-decoupling-and-the-wandering-mind.pdf)). Much drifting goes unnoticed even by the person doing it.

### What noticing does

The review proposes that noticing is functional rather than incidental:

> "From this perspective, meta-awareness directly aids in the identification of mind-wandering lapses, and the subsequent re-engagement of the primary task." (Schooler et al., 2011, p. 323, [full text](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2011/2011-Meta-awareness-perceptual-decoupling-and-the-wandering-mind.pdf))

The intermittent assessment can detect lapses that lower-level implicit monitoring misses, and the review proposes that noticing aids the re-engagement of the primary task.

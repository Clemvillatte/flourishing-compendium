Creative self-efficacy is Patricia Tierney and Steven Farmer's extension of Albert Bandura's self-efficacy construct to creative work: "the belief one has the ability to produce creative outcomes" (Tierney and Farmer 2002, p. 1138, as quoted in [Haase et al. 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1411436)). Their study, using data from two different firms, "tested a new construct, creative self-efficacy, tapping employees' belief that they can be creative in their work roles"; job tenure, job self-efficacy, supervisor behavior and job complexity fed these beliefs, and "creative self-efficacy also predicted creative performance beyond the predictive effects of job self-efficacy" ([OpenAlex record with abstract](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.2307/3069429)).

Later work checked how strong the link is. A meta-analysis of 60 effect sizes from 41 papers found an overall relation of r = .39 between creative self-efficacy and creativity measures, and its authors stress that the results depend on whether creativity is measured by self-report or by objective tests ([Haase et al. 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1411436)). Confidence and creative output travel together, and they are not the same thing.

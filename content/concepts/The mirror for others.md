The compendium's heading "the mirror for others" points here to Mencius' advice to examine oneself when one's actions do not bring the desired response.

### Mencius' ground

The passage the heading rests on, in Legge's translation:

> "If a man love others, and no responsive attachment is shown to him, let him turn inwards and examine his own benevolence. If he is trying to rule others, and his government is unsuccessful, let him turn inwards and examine his wisdom. If he treats others politely, and they do not return his politeness, let him turn inwards and examine his own feeling of respect." (Mencius, Li Lou I, ch. IV, trans. James Legge, [Book of Mencius, book 4](https://www.spiritualpilgrim.net/08_Classics-Library/non-western/China/book-of-Mencius-04.htm))

Its conclusion:

> "When we do not, by what we do, realise what we desire, we must turn inwards, and examine ourselves in every point. When a man's person is correct, the whole kingdom will turn to him with recognition and submission." (same chapter)

The mirror is the self examined; what others see in it is the person so examined. Influence on others is not exerted directly but reflected, from a surface that has first been made true. Legge's own heading for the chapter makes the turn explicit: "With what measure a man metes it will be measured to him again, and consequently before a man deals with others, expecting them to be affected by him, he should first deal with himself" (chapter summary, same page). The next chapter follows the same root to its end:

> "The root of the kingdom is in the State. The root of the State is in the family. The root of the family is in the person of its Head." (Mencius, Li Lou I, ch. V, same translation, same page)

The passage the chapter closes with ties the inward turn to something received rather than achieved: "Be always studious to be in harmony with the ordinances of God, And you will obtain much happiness" (quoting the Book of Poetry, same chapter). The compendium's principles read the mirror two ways at once: the examination is on the person doing the influencing, and its reach runs outward from a corrected person through the family to the kingdom — the same order of roots, not a private self-improvement with no public consequence.

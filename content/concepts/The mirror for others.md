The mirror for others is the idea that a person's effect on other people begins with what they have made of themselves: before dealing with others, expecting them to be affected, deal with yourself. The seed names Mencius, on the great person's influence.

### Mencius' ground

The passage the heading rests on, in Legge's translation:

> "If a man love others, and no responsive attachment is shown to him, let him turn inwards and examine his own benevolence. If he is trying to rule others, and his government is unsuccessful, let him turn inwards and examine his wisdom. If he treats others politely, and they do not return his politeness, let him turn inwards and examine his own feeling of respect." (Mencius, Li Lou I, ch. IV, trans. James Legge, [Book of Mencius, book 4](https://www.spiritualpilgrim.net/08_Classics-Library/non-western/China/book-of-Mencius-04.htm))

Its conclusion:

> "When we do not, by what we do, realise what we desire, we must turn inwards, and examine ourselves in every point. When a man's person is correct, the whole kingdom will turn to him with recognition and submission." (same chapter)

The mirror is the self examined; what others see in it is the person so examined. Influence on others is not exerted directly but reflected, from a surface that has first been made true.

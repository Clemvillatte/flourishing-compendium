**Positioning theory** — Harré — in conversation people position themselves and others with story lines and rights/duties, creating identity moment by moment.

Positioning theory is Rom Harré's account of the local systems of rights and duties that shape small-scale interactions. His own summary statement:

> "Positioning Theory is the study of the nature, formation, influence and ways of change of local systems of rights and duties as shared assumptions about them influence small scale interactions." (Harré, [Positioning Theory and Moral Structure of Close Encounters, Pontifical Academy of Social Sciences Acta 11, 2006, p. 306](https://www.pass.va/content/dam/casinapioiv/pass/pdf-volumi/acta/acta_11/acta11-harre.pdf))

### The triangle

The theory works with three interlocking elements. In Harré's phrasing, "Positioning theory is an analytical scheme that can be used to reveal the inter-relation between speech acts, social meanings of what is said or done; local clusters of rights and duties that influence what people choose to do out of all they know how to do; and story lines, the cultural schemas for living out strips of everyday life" (ibid., p. 297). He adds that "constraints on and opportunities for action arise through local acts of positioning".

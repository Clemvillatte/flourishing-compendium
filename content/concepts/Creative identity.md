“Creative identity” — Beghetto, 2006.

Creative identity, in the compendium, is the idea that seeing oneself as someone who makes things shapes what one goes on to make. The 2006 paper the seed cites is by Ronald Beghetto, and it measures creative self-efficacy, which he defines as "self-judgments of creative ability", not identity as such. The sources below cover that paper and the Four C model of creativity that Beghetto later developed with James Kaufman.

### What the 2006 study found

Beghetto studied 1,322 middle and secondary students. Their mastery- and performance-approach beliefs and teacher feedback on creative ability were positively related to creative self-efficacy. The abstract also reports a less comfortable link:

> "Creative self-efficacy was also linked to student reports of their teachers not listening to them and sometimes feeling that their teachers had given up on them." (Beghetto 2006, [abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326934crj1804_4))

Students with higher creative self-efficacy were more likely to hold positive beliefs about their academic abilities and to plan to attend college, and more likely to report taking part in after-school academic and group activities. These are correlations from one school sample, so they do not show that holding a creative self-image causes better outcomes.

### Where a creative self fits: the Four C model

Kaufman and Beghetto (2009) propose four forms of creativity: everyday "little-c", eminent "Big-C", "mini-c" (creativity inherent in the learning process) and "Pro-c" (the developmental and effortful progression beyond little-c that represents professional-level expertise). ([Review of General Psychology, abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/a0013688)) Beghetto and Kaufman (2007) define mini-c as "the novel and personally meaningful interpretation of experiences, actions, and events" ([Cambridge International, chapter 4](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)). The model gives a way to say that a person can be creative at a small scale without a public record to show for it.

### What the sources do not show

Neither source tests whether adopting a creative identity changes behaviour. They measure self-judgments of ability and classify kinds of creativity. Anything about identity formation goes beyond these papers.

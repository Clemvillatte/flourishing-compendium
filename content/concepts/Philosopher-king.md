"Philosopher-king" — Plato, Republic.

The Republic's Book V sets the condition for a city's rest from its evils in a union: rulers who are philosophers, or philosophers who rule.

> "Until philosophers are kings, or the kings and princes of this world have the spirit and power of philosophy, and political greatness and wisdom meet in one, and those commoner natures who pursue either to the exclusion of the other are compelled to stand aside, cities will never have rest from their evils,—nor the human race, as I believe,—and then only will this our State have a possibility of life and behold the light of day." (Plato, Republic V, [Jowett translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1497/1497-h/1497-h.htm))

The sentence carries the Republic's hardest political claim and its built-in escape hatch at once. The claim: cities will not stop their evils until power and philosophy are held by the same people. The escape hatch: the union is stated as a condition, not a program, and the dialogue never shows a city that meets it. The "commoner natures who pursue either to the exclusion of the other" are the puzzle's core: excellence split in two. The concept's afterlife split along that seam, utopian reading, ironic reading, and the practical question of who should rule when wisdom and power rarely arrive together. In this library it sits beside the portrait of the sage: the same question asked of a person rather than a city.

Addictive patterns are the six components Mark Griffiths proposed as the definition of addiction, applicable to substances and behaviors alike. His own summary of the assertion:

> "[A] behavior should not be classed as an addiction unless there is endorsement and empirical and/or clinical verification of six specific components (i.e., salience, mood modification, tolerance, withdrawal, conflict, and relapse)."

([Griffiths, "The evolution of the 'components model of addiction'," Düşünen Adam 2019, NTU repository PDF](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/37866/1/15023_Griffiths.pdf).)

### What the components are

The model's original definitions, in Griffiths's own wording from the 2005 paper as quoted in his later review:

- Salience: the activity "becomes the most important activity in the person's life and dominates their thinking (preoccupations and cognitive distortions), feelings (cravings) and behaviour (deterioration of socialized behaviour)" ([quoted in the 2019 review](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/37866/1/15023_Griffiths.pdf)).
- Mood modification: the subjective experience of "an arousing 'buzz' or a 'high' or paradoxically a tranquillizing and/or destressing feel of 'escape' or 'numbing'" — many addicts use the behavior "as a coping strategy to 'self-medicate'" ([same](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/37866/1/15023_Griffiths.pdf)).
- Tolerance: "increasing amounts of the particular activity are required to achieve the former effects" ([same](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/37866/1/15023_Griffiths.pdf)).
- Withdrawal: the unpleasant states that occur when the activity is discontinued or reduced ([IGD scale validation, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5370374/)).
- Conflict: conflicts between the person and those around them, with other activities, or "from within the individual themselves (intrapsychic conflict)" ([smartphone applications paper, NTU](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/38058/1/1208001_Griffiths.pdf)).
- Relapse: "repeated reversions to earlier patterns" that can restore "even the most extreme patterns typical of the height of the addiction" after periods of control ([same](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/38058/1/1208001_Griffiths.pdf)).

The framework is now the most cited model for problematic social media use and other behavioral addictions, and it is the theoretical basis for most assessment tools in that field ([PMC social media study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10166459/)). Its reach extends to clinical classification debates: reviewers have noted where Griffiths's components overlap with DSM-5 gambling criteria and where ICD-11 compulsive sexual behavior picks up only salience, conflict, and relapse while leaving mood modification, tolerance, and withdrawal out ([CSBD review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9295215/)). I hold the model as a practical checklist rather than a diagnostic instrument: a passion that shows several of these components is not a passion but a pattern, and the six names give a person the specific things to watch in their own behavior before the pattern consolidates.

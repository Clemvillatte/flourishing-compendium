Ego strength is the compendium's label for a capacity that Roberto Assagioli's psychosynthesis treats as a developed will. The sources consulted here do not use the phrase "ego strength", and they distinguish Assagioli's "I" from the Freudian ego. A review of psychosynthesis in Pastoral Psychology states that "unlike the ego in Freudian thinking, the 'I' is exclusively the observer and director of the individual's actions, not the functions or roles of the personality nor a mediator between demands of the id, superego, and outer world" ([Pastoral Psychology review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5493721/)).

### A will with four aspects

The same review says that in psychosynthesis the will is not "something stern and oppressive". It has "a directive and regulatory function; it balances and ... utilizes all the other activities and energies of the human being without repressing any of them" (quoting Assagioli 2002). Assagioli describes the fully developed will as having four aspects: strong, skillful, good and transpersonal. The review adds that strength "used alone without the other aspects, can actually cause harm to oneself and others". Skillful will is "the ability to obtain desired results with the least possible expenditure of energy" ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5493721/)).

### Stages of a willed act

According to the review, Assagioli holds that any act of will has six stages: "purpose, deliberation, choice, affirmation, planning, and execution". He locates the "principal cause of failure in completing an act of will" in difficulty with one of these stages. The counsellor's role is to help the client see which stage is weaker ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5493721/)).

### Measured, with limits

A paper in PubMed Central developed a Multidimensional Will Scale based on Assagioli's model and reports that "every aspect of the will can be trained and Assagioli proposes a series of practices for this purpose" ([Measuring Strong, Skillful, Good and Transpersonal Will, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11239019/)). Its conclusion is that a quantitative measure of the multidimensional construct was obtained, which is a step in scale development and not evidence that the practices work.

### Scope

These are secondary sources. Assagioli's own books were not read for this page. The compendium's label "ego strength" borrows a term from other schools, and the match to psychosynthesis is loose.

### Definition

Soft startup is the practice of raising a difficult issue gently: complaint stated, feeling owned, accusation withheld. It is the first of Gottman's six conflict-management skills, and the research finding behind it is blunt about stakes:

> How a partner raises an issue in the first three minutes of the conversation is crucial to resolving relationship conflicts. ([The Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-the-six-skills/))

The converse holds too:

> If most of your arguments start softly, your relationship is far more likely to be stable and happy. ([The Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-the-six-skills/))

### Origin

The construct comes out of John Gottman's observational couples research, where the opening minutes of a conflict conversation reliably predicted its trajectory and, over years, the relationship's stability. The skill pairs with mechanics: "When you start sentences with 'I,' you are less likely to seem critical, immediately putting your partner into a defensive position" ([Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-the-six-skills/)). Soft startup is the front end of a chain whose later links are repair and de-escalation.

### A real-life example

The same grievance, two openings. "You never listen to me" produces a defence. "I don't feel heard right now" produces a pause. Nothing else about the evening has changed; the second conversation now has somewhere to go.

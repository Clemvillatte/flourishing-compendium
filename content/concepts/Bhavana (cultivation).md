Bhavana means mental cultivation or development in the published account of Piyadassi Mahathera's talk on Buddhist meditation ([Talk on Bhavana](https://buddhisteducationcentre.org.au/talk-on-bhavana-by-ven-piyadassi-mahathera/)). He describes observing how thoughts appear and disappear during practice, alongside learning and teaching the Dhamma ([Talk on Bhavana](https://buddhisteducationcentre.org.au/talk-on-bhavana-by-ven-piyadassi-mahathera/)).

### Developing particular qualities

MN 62 gives the idea practical content: Rahula is instructed to develop goodwill, compassion and equanimity, as well as mindfulness of breathing ([The Greater Exhortation to Rahula, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.062.than.html)). The translator identifies bhavana as the term rendered “meditation” in Sariputta's initial instruction ([MN 62, note 2](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.062.than.html)).

> "Develop the meditation of compassion." (MN 62, [translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.062.than.html).)

The discourse connects that practice with abandoning cruelty, and then gives detailed training in awareness of breathing ([MN 62](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.062.than.html)). These are the text's instructions and intended results; they should not be read as clinical outcome evidence.

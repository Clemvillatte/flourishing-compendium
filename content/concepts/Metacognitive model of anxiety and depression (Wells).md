positive and negative metacognitive beliefs maintain worry and rumination (Wells, 2009).

Adrian Wells's model assigns anxiety and depression to a thinking style rather than to thought content. The style is the cognitive attentional syndrome, defined in the 2009 book as:

> ...a perseverative thinking style that takes the form of worry and rumination, attentional focusing on threat, and unhelpful coping behaviors (e.g., thought suppression, avoidance, substance use)

What keeps the style running is a layer above it: metacognition, what a person believes about their own thinking ([Faija et al., 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02109/full)). The two belief families do different jobs. Positive metacognitions endorse the strategy: worry feels like preparation, rumination feels like problem solving. Negative metacognitions then appraise the strategy's products as dangerous and out of control ([Hagen and Kennair, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12443011/)).

### The two families, in the model's own examples

> Positive metacognitions are concerned with the benefits of worry and rumination, while negative metacognitions are concerned with the uncontrollability and danger of thoughts.

The depression examples Hagen and Kennair quote from the model run: "Analyzing the causes of my sadness will give me an answer to the problem" on the positive side, and "I can't control my thinking" on the negative side. Rumination is "maintained by the patients' positive and especially negative metacognitions", and it is the negative beliefs that best predict symptoms.

### What the therapy targets

The model's practical edge is the place it aims ([Hagen and Kennair, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12443011/)):

> Whilst CBT focuses on the content of thoughts and invites the patient to reality check this content, MCT addresses perseverative thinking processes and metacognitions and does not address the specific content of this processing.

The question is not whether the sad thought is true. It is whether the analysing machine is doing the person any good.

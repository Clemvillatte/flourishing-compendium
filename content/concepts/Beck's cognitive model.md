**Beck's cognitive model** — automatic thoughts, assumptions and core schemas about self and world maintain depression and anxiety.

### What the model says

Beck's cognitive model of depression was proposed over 40 years ago and has been the predominant theory of depression in the cognitive-behavioural tradition since. A 2011 review by Disner, Beevers, Haigh and Beck summarised its elements:

> "In the 40 years since Aaron Beck first proposed his cognitive model of depression, the elements of this model--biased attention, biased processing, biased thoughts and rumination, biased memory, and dysfunctional attitudes and schemas--have been consistently linked with the onset and maintenance of depression." (Disner, Beevers, Haigh & Beck, 2011, [Nature Reviews Neuroscience, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21731066/))

The same review added the neurobiological reading:

> "Although the mechanisms underlying each element of the model differ, in general the negative cognitive biases in depression are facilitated by increased influence from subcortical emotion processing regions combined with attenuated top-down cognitive control." (Disner, Beevers, Haigh & Beck, 2011, [Nature Reviews Neuroscience, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21731066/))

### The cognitive hierarchy

The model is organised as a hierarchy from deeper to more surface cognition. The depressive schema is a well-organised and interconnected negative internal representation of self, believed to develop through early-life experiences and to remain dormant until triggered by negative life events such as loss or rejection. Automatic thoughts are the more superficial cognitions, more proximal to a given situation but functionally related to one's deeper beliefs and schemas, and are considered the cognitive by-products of activated schemas. Treatment in cognitive-behavioural therapy is described as proceeding in the opposite direction: it typically begins by helping the client discover and test negative automatic thoughts and moves systematically toward modifying deeper beliefs and schemas.

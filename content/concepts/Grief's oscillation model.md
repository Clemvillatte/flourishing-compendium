Grief's oscillation model is Margaret Stroebe and Henk Schut's dual-process model of coping with bereavement (1999). Against earlier models built on working through grief in one steady line, it defines two categories of stressor and a regulatory process that moves between them.

> "This model identifies two types of stressors, loss- and restoration-oriented, and a dynamic, regulatory coping process of oscillation, whereby the grieving individual at times confronts, at other times avoids, the different tasks of grieving." (Stroebe and Schut, [The Dual Process Model of Coping with Bereavement](https://wendyvanmieghem.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/dual-process-model-by-M.-Stroebe-.pdf), Death Studies 23, 1999, abstract)

### The two orientations

Loss-orientation is the concentration on and processing of some aspect of the loss experience itself, most particularly the deceased person. Restoration-orientation is the focus on the secondary stressors of a changed world, the reorganizing of life and the developing of new roles. Oscillation is the alternation between the two, the juxtaposition of confrontation and avoidance: at times the bereaved will be confronted by their loss, at other times avoid memories, be distracted, or seek relief by concentration on other things.

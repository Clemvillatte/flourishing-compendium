Anger management is the clinical field that treats problematic anger as its own target, rather than as a symptom of something else. A central book in the field is DiGiuseppe and Tafrate's Understanding Anger Disorders (2007). The publisher's description sets out its complaint: anger has been "written off as merely an emotional symptom and excluded from most accepted systems of psychiatric diagnosis" ([Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books?vid=9780195170795)).

### What is being managed

The field's working definition of the emotion comes from the book itself: "Anger is a subjectively experienced emotional state with high sympathetic autonomic arousal. It is initially elicited by a perception of a threat (to one's physical well-being, property, present or future resources, self-image, social status or projected image to one's group, maintenance of social rules that regulate daily life, or comfort), although it may persist even after the threat has passed" ([DiGiuseppe & Tafrate 2007, book preview PDF](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780198038160_A23605861/preview-9780198038160_A23605861.pdf)).

### The treatment gap

An Australian evidence summary on young people reports that "the majority of people experiencing problematic anger do not seek support from health services," that even when they do they are "unlikely to receive an effective intervention," and that clinicians "may lack confidence in their ability to assess and provide interventions for this client group" ([Headspace evidence summary](https://headspace.org.au/assets/download-cards/OR17177-EvidenceSummary-Anger-WEB.pdf)).

### What the training works on

The same summary describes the skill side of the field: anger "includes cognitive, affective and behavioural components," it becomes problematic when it occurs "frequently, at high intensity, and leads to secondary problems," and "developing skills to manage anger, such as assertiveness and problem solving, may actually be protective against anxiety and depression" ([Headspace evidence summary](https://headspace.org.au/assets/download-cards/OR17177-EvidenceSummary-Anger-WEB.pdf)). The book's treatment chapters, per its publisher, "review treatment outcome studies and new interventions to improve treatment" ([Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books?vid=9780195170795)).

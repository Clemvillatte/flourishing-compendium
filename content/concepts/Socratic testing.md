Socratic testing is the Meno's account of what makes a belief knowledge. Socrates' distinction, in the Daedalus-statue passage:

> "For true beliefs, as long as they remain, are a fine thing and all they do is good, but they are not willing to remain long, and they escape from a man's mind, so that they are not worth much until one ties them down by (giving) an account of the reason why. [...] After they are tied down, in the first place they become knowledge, and then they remain in place. That is why knowledge is prized higher than correct opinion, and knowledge is different from correct opinion in being tied down."

([Plato, Meno 97e5-98a7, MIT OCW translation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-200-ancient-philosophy-fall-2004/76a62d9a1d03d49cdea8e12f3d9f7ebf_tapmenok_l.pdf).)

### What the tether is

The tether is an account of the reason why — *aitias logismos* — and Plato names it recollection. The Perseus translation of the same passage: "they are of no great value until one makes them fast with causal reasoning. And this process, friend Meno, is recollection, as in our previous talk we have agreed" ([Meno 98a, Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0178:text=Meno:page=98)). The distinction is the basis of the later philosophical definition of knowledge as justified true belief ([Wikipedia, Meno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meno)). Socrates also grants the practical point that makes the tether worth building: "right opinion will be no whit inferior to knowledge in worth or usefulness as regards our actions, nor will the man who has right opinion be inferior to him who has knowledge" ([Meno 98c, Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0178:text=Meno:page=98)). True beliefs guide action well; what they lack is stability under challenge.

### The compendium's use

The principles on behavioral experiments read the same structure clinically: a belief that produces good outcomes is not yet tested, and testing is the giving of the account. The Meno's version is why the compendium treats belief-testing as an intellectual practice rather than a procedure — the tether is the reasoned explanation, and the explanation is what survives the challenge that an untethered opinion does not.

chronic activation without recovery produces cumulative physiological wear (McEwen, 1998).

Allostatic load is Bruce McEwen's term for the cumulative wear the body takes when stress-mediating systems stay chronically activated. The concept was introduced in his 1998 New England Journal of Medicine review, built on allostasis, "the ability to achieve stability through change".

> "The price of this accommodation to stress can be allostatic load, which is the wear and tear that results from chronic overactivity or underactivity of allostatic systems." (McEwen, [Protective and Damaging Effects of Stress Mediators, NEJM 338:171-179, 1998](https://www.psychiatry.wisc.edu/courses/Nitschke/seminar/mcewen_njm_1998.pdf))

### Adaptive then harmful

The paper's structure is the two-sidedness: the autonomic, HPA-axis, cardiovascular, metabolic and immune systems "protect the body by responding to internal and external stress", and the same chronic activation accumulates damage. The review names four situations that produce allostatic load, the first being frequent stress, and notes overexposure to stress hormones "over weeks, months, or years".

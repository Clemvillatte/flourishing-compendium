Colquitt's 2001 validation study in the Journal of Applied Psychology tested how many kinds of fairness employees distinguish. Confirmatory factor analyses "supported a 4-factor structure to the measure, with distributive, procedural, interpersonal, and informational justice as distinct dimensions", a solution that "fit the data significantly better than a 2- or 3-factor solution using larger interactional or procedural dimensions" ([Colquitt, On the dimensionality of organizational justice, 2001, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419799/)). The seed's four labels therefore need one change: Colquitt splits interactional justice into interpersonal and informational parts, so the four are distributive, procedural, interpersonal and informational.

### What each dimension covers

A Wiley review summarises employees' four evaluations as "just pay and outcomes, just decision-making procedures, respectful and dignified treatment, and accurate and honest communication" ([Organizational justice research: A review, synthesis, and research agenda, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/emre.12564)). It credits Greenberg (1993) with defining interpersonal justice as the social side of distributive justice and informational justice as the social side of procedural justice. A Colquitt overview adds that the interactional items assessed "respect, propriety, truthfulness, and justification", and that separating interpersonal and informational justice "acknowledges that the politeness and respectfulness of communication is distinct from its honesty and truthfulness" ([Colquitt, Organizational Justice, 2012, PDF; fragmentary text](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgpsych/readings/6.%20Organizational%20Justice/Colquitt%20(2012)%20Organizational%20Justice.pdf)).

### Outcomes

Colquitt's abstract reports predictive validity "on important outcomes, including leader evaluation, rule compliance, commitment, and helping behavior". Commitment is named; trust is not in that abstract, so the seed's "trust" is not confirmed here (PubMed).

### Limits and disagreement

Some studies still use a three-factor model that merges interpersonal and informational justice into interactional justice (Wiley review). A Brazilian review notes that researchers disagree about merging them, though evidence favours splitting because "they have different effects in the measurement model" ([Psicologia: Teoria e Pesquisa, SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/ptp/a/jyMZVt3t5bGbdPMLBxKTQPq/?format=pdf&lang=en)). A study of the scale in public health found procedural and informational justice "highly correlated" and "a potential omitted factor, procedural-voice justice" ([Enoksen, Examining the Dimensionality of Colquitt's Organizational Justice Scale, SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/01.PR0.116k26w0)).

Benefit finding is "the process of deriving growth from adversity" ([Moore, Rubin and Heaney 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10374125/), PLOS ONE). Its evidence base was summed up in Helgeson, Reynolds and Tomich's meta-analysis of 87 cross-sectional studies.

> "Results from 87 cross-sectional studies reported in 77 articles showed that benefit finding was related to less depression and more positive well-being but also more intrusive and avoidant thoughts about the stressor." (Helgeson, Reynolds and Tomich, [A meta-analytic review of benefit finding and growth](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17032085/), J Consult Clin Psychol 74(5):797-816, 2006, abstract)

### What the evidence shows

The pattern is double-edged. Alongside the associations with less depression and more positive well-being, the same meta-analysis found more intrusive and avoidant thoughts about the stressor, and reported that "benefit finding was unrelated to anxiety, global distress, quality of life, and subjective reports of physical health" ([abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17032085/)). Moderator analyses showed the relations depended on time since the stressor, the measure used, and the sample's racial composition. The evidence is cross-sectional association, not a demonstrated causal engine of growth.

Self-expansion is the motivation to grow the self: to add knowledge, abilities, perspectives and resources to what one is. Arthur and Elaine Aron introduced the self-expansion model in their 1986 book Love as the Expansion of Self, and a 2026 article by Arthur Aron and Jennifer Tomlinson states its base.

> "The self-expansion model (originating in Aron & Aron, 1986; and tested and refined in many studies) proposes that we have evolved with two primary motivations: survival and self-expansion (growth, increasing knowledge and abilities, status, and so forth)." (Aron & Tomlinson, [Self-Expansion Activities with a Partner as One Path to Well-Being, Behavioral Sciences, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13203665/))

### Origin

Arthur and Elaine Aron set out the model in "Love as the Expansion of Self: Understanding Attraction and Satisfaction" (Hemisphere, 1986), a title the 2026 article's reference list carries, and it has since been tested and refined in many studies, as that article records.

### Core claims

One route the model proposes for expansion is close relationships.

> "The model then proposes that a major way people experience self-expansion is through close relationships in which the other is, to some extent, "included in the self," so that one feels they have access to the other's knowledge, resources, and so forth as part of oneself." ([same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13203665/))

The article adds that this inclusion makes forming close relationships exciting, and that over time one gets used to it and the excitement slows. The primary method it proposes for keeping long-term relationships exciting is to "regularly engage in exciting and novel activities with the other, so the relationship becomes associated with that sense of rapid self-expansion".

### In everyday life

The article's own subject is this application: it proposes that couples who regularly take on exciting, novel activities together keep the association between the relationship and growth alive.

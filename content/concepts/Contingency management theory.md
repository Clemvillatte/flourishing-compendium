**Contingency management theory** — delivering reliable rewards for verified behaviour change sustains abstinence and treatment adherence.

Contingency management is the clinical application of operant conditioning: if a behavior is followed by something the person values, it becomes more likely, and the clinic can arrange that on purpose. Nancy Petry, one of its leading developers, defines it in a sentence:

> "Contingency management refers to a type of behavioural therapy in which individuals are 'reinforced', or rewarded, for evidence of positive behavioural change." ([Petry 2011, The Psychiatrist 33:19-24, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3083448/))

The design details matter, because the theory lives in them. The behavior is verified, not promised:

> "In contingency management interventions for substance misuse treatment, urine samples are collected multiple times each week (to detect brief periods of abstinence) and abstinence is reinforced each time negative samples are submitted." (same article)

And the reward escalates with sustained change, which is what turns a series of small choices into a streak worth protecting:

> "Importantly, in effective contingency management interventions, the magnitude of reinforcement provided (voucher amounts or draws for prizes) increases with sustained periods of abstinence." (same article)

The evidence is unusually strong for a psychosocial treatment. In the multisite trial Petry summarizes:

> "Contingency management significantly enhanced retention in treatment, with 49% of the contingency management group completing 12 weeks of treatment v. only 35% the of standard care group. The mean number of weeks of consecutive abstinence from stimulants was 4.4 for those assigned to contingency management v. 2.6 for those assigned to standard care. The percentage of individuals who sustained stimulant abstinence throughout the full 12 weeks was nearly 4 times greater for the contingency management condition (18.7% v. 4.9%)." (same article)

The theory's lesson reaches beyond addiction. It says that when a person cannot yet feel the value of a good choice, the world can lend the value: make the good behavior reliably, immediately worth something, and the behavior will grow while the motive catches up. That is the working logic of [[BE072 — Recognise behavioural symptoms of addiction — loss of control]] and a cousin of [[Habit formation]]'s engineering on the individual side.

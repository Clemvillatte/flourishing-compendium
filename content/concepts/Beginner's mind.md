"Beginner's mind" is the Zen practice of meeting experience as if for the first time. Shunryu Suzuki's Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind is built on one line: "In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities; in the expert's mind there are few" ([Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind, 1970](https://web.stanford.edu/~funn/ZenMindBeginners.pdf)). "In Japan we have the phrase shoshin, which means 'beginner's mind,'" he writes; "The goal of practice is always to keep our beginner's mind" ([Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind](https://web.stanford.edu/~funn/ZenMindBeginners.pdf)).

### Empty, not blank

The emptiness is not ignorance. The book's introduction, by Richard Baker, describes the beginner's mind as "empty, free of the habits of the expert, ready to accept, to doubt, and open to all the possibilities," the kind of mind "which can see things as they are" ([Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind](https://web.stanford.edu/~funn/ZenMindBeginners.pdf)).

A parallel image appears in the first story of the classic Zen anecdote collection: Nan-in pours tea into a professor's cup and keeps pouring. "Like this cup," he says, "you are full of your own opinions and speculations. How can I show you Zen unless you first empty your cup?" ([101 Zen Stories, in Zen Flesh, Zen Bones](https://terebess.hu/zen/mesterek/NaninZengu.html))

### Why it is hard

Suzuki's sober note is that "the most difficult thing is always to keep your beginner's mind" ([Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind](https://web.stanford.edu/~funn/ZenMindBeginners.pdf)). The warning behind it: the habits of the expert accumulate invisibly, and what is already known quietly decides what can be seen next. The compendium's B048 names shoshin directly, and its neighbours are the same family: B178's practice without gaining ideas, D045's empty cup, D191's beginner's pace kept all one's life.

### In the compendium

CO040 names the cognitive cost: the curse of knowledge, the inability to un-know what one knows. SO022 turns the practice outward as cultural humility, meeting another's world without pre-filling it. AT019 is the attentional cousin: the return of attention, not its unbroken presence, is the skill. P097's treating each moment as fresh is the direct translation.

“Corsi e ricorsi” — Vico, The New Science.

Corsi e ricorsi, "courses and recourses," is Giambattista Vico's name for the pattern he claimed to find in the history of nations: every nation runs the same course, and after collapse the course runs again. The New Science (1744) rests that claim on a division of all gentile history into three ages:

> "The age of the gods, in which the gentiles believed they lived under divine governments, and everything was commanded them by auspices and oracles... The age of the heroes, in which they reigned everywhere in aristocratic commonwealths, on account of a certain superiority of nature which they held themselves to have over the plebs. The age of men, in which all men recognized themselves as equal in human nature, and therefore there were established first the popular commonwealths and then the monarchies, both of which are forms of human government." ([Vico, The New Science, Bergin and Fisch translation, §31, open full text](https://archive.org/stream/newscienceofgiam030174mbp/newscienceofgiam030174mbp_djvu.txt))

What makes the pattern a science rather than a list is that it is the same course for all nations, an "ideal eternal history":

> "This then is an instance of an ideal eternal history (which will later be meditated and discovered) whose course is run in time by the histories of all nations." (same book, §114)

The course has a material sequence, from the forests outward:

> "This was the order of human things: first the forests, after that the huts, thence the villages, next the cities and finally the academies." (same book, §239)

And the ricorso is the striking half of the idea: when nations decay into a second barbarism, they do not invent a new pattern; they begin the same course again. The returned medieval barbarism "recurred in the returned barbarian times" in the same fashion as the first (§622), and Book Five is titled "The Recurrence of Human Things in the Resurgence of the Nations" (same book).

Two features keep the idea from fatalism. The course is not mechanical: it is driven by human things, religion above all ("the world of peoples began everywhere with religion", same book), and its stages are legible only in what people made, so Vico's famous axiom holds that "the world of civil society has certainly been made by men, and that its principles are therefore to be found within the modifications of our own human mind." And the cycle is a course through possibilities, not a circle of identical events: the recurrence is "in the same order" (§41), not in the same details. The compendium's principle [[D006 — Move by return (fu) — everything renews in cycles]] is the cross-tradition echo named in the seed, and [[Eternal recurrence]] is the comparison from Nietzsche: a doctrine of the return, but of the pattern rather than the event.

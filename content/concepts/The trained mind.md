The Dhammapada's chapter on the mind praises subduing and guarding it (verses 35-36). It describes the mind as swift and liable to seize whatever it desires, and says that a tamed mind brings happiness. ([Dhammapada 35-36, trans. Buddharakkhita](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.03.budd.html))

> "Wonderful, indeed, it is to subdue the mind, so difficult to subdue, ever swift, and seizing whatever it desires. A tamed mind brings happiness." (Dhammapada 35, trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.03.budd.html))

The same chapter extends the image through its verses: the discerning man straightens the mind as a fletcher straightens an arrow shaft (Dhammapada 33), and one's own well-directed mind does one greater good than any relative can (Dhammapada 43). ([Dhammapada 33, 43, Buddharakkhita translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.03.budd.html))

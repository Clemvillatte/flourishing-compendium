Augustine's Confessions names a movement the contemplative traditions keep rediscovering: what he searched for was within all along, while the search itself carried him outward. He plunged amid the world's fair forms, and found afterwards that he had "sought Thee abroad out of myself."

> "Thou wert with me, but I was not with Thee. Things held me far from Thee, which, unless they were in Thee, were not at all." (Augustine, Confessions X.27, [E. B. Pusey translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3296/3296-h/3296-h.htm))

The return is not a change of address. It is a change in what attention takes as near: the things that held him were not bad in themselves, but he had treated them as if they could hold what they cannot. The compendium's Daoist cousins frame the same turn as returning to the root and to experience before the labels; the empty-boat parable carries the version where the self that returns is itself let go.

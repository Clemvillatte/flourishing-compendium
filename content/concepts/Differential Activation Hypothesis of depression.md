**Differential Activation Hypothesis of depression** — Teasdale — depressive thinking patterns are reactivated by sad mood, deepening episodes over time.

The Differential Activation Hypothesis is John Teasdale's account of why depression returns: an episode does not only pass, it leaves behind patterns of thinking, and those patterns can be switched back on by ordinary low mood.

> "According to the model, for those who have previously experienced relapses, small negative mood shifts may produce recurrence, because they can activate patterns of depressive thinking that are similar to the thoughts experienced in previous depressive episodes." (MacKenzie and Kocovski, [Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy for depression: trends and developments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4876939/), 2016)

### Why the hypothesis matters

The hypothesis explains a vulnerability rather than a symptom, and it drove a treatment. Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy was built "to train recovered recurrently depressed patients to disengage from dysphoria-activated depressogenic thinking that may mediate relapse/recurrence" (Teasdale et al., 2000, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10965637/)). In that trial, 145 recovered recurrently depressed patients were randomized to treatment as usual or MBCT; over 60 weeks, MBCT significantly reduced relapse for patients with three or more previous episodes, and did not for patients with only two.

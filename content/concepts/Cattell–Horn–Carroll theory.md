## In plain words

The Cattell–Horn–Carroll theory (CHC) is the field's working map of mental abilities. Its core: intelligence is a three-stratum hierarchy, with general ability (g) at the top, broad abilities such as fluid and crystallised intelligence in the middle, and narrow skills at the base.

## Where it comes from

The theory integrates two earlier models: "the theory of fluid and crystallized intelligence (Gf-Gc)" of Raymond Cattell and John Horn, and John Carroll's three-stratum theory, "a hierarchical, three-stratum model of intelligence"; "due to substantial similarities between the two theories they were amalgamated" to form CHC ([Wikipedia, CHC theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattell%E2%80%93Horn%E2%80%93Carroll_theory)).

## How it works

Carroll built his model on the psychometric approach, "the objective measurement of individual differences in abilities, and the application of factor analysis, a statistical technique which uncovers relationships between variables and the underlying structure of concepts such as 'intelligence'" ([Wikipedia, CHC theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattell%E2%80%93Horn%E2%80%93Carroll_theory)). The theory "holds that the relationships among them can be derived by classifying them into three different strata: stratum I, 'narrow' abilities; stratum II, 'broad abilities'; and stratum III, consisting of a single 'general ability' (or g)" ([Wikipedia, CHC theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattell%E2%80%93Horn%E2%80%93Carroll_theory)). Fluid intelligence "refers to quantitative reasoning, processing ability, adaptability to new environments and novel problem solving," while crystallised intelligence (Gc) "refers to the accumulation of knowledge (general, procedural and declarative)" ([Wikipedia, CHC theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattell%E2%80%93Horn%E2%80%93Carroll_theory)).

## What it explains

The model explains why abilities come in packages: a strong vocabulary travels with general knowledge, and novel problem-solving travels with processing speed. It also explains their different lifecourses in the ageing literature, since fluid and crystallised abilities peak at different ages. The theory is the standard frame for modern IQ tests, though some researchers, including John Carroll, have questioned both the need for the theory and its empirical basis ([Wikipedia, CHC theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattell%E2%80%93Horn%E2%80%93Carroll_theory)).

## Everyday life

The practical read: minds have a profile, not a single number. Learning that leans on accumulated knowledge (Gc) can keep improving late in life, while novel problem-solving benefits from freshness and rest.

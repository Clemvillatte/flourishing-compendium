Moral development research names the tradition running from Kohlberg's stage theory to the moral-intuition approaches that followed. A summary of where the two meet:

> "In line with most conventional moral theories (Gilligan, 1982; Kohlberg, 1981), MFT posits that morality includes (but is not limited to) the two domains of harm/care and fairness/reciprocity. The harm/care foundation pertains to caring for others and preventing or alleviating harm. Fairness/reciprocity is concerned about issues of justice, fair treatment of individuals and proportionality." (Culiberg, Cho, Kos Koklic and Zabkar 2022, [Journal of Business Ethics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8741588/))

The interesting history is in what changed. Kohlberg scored the reasoning a person gave for a judgement; the intuitionist approaches that followed, Haidt's moral foundations work chief among them, asked instead which moral concerns people actually respond to, and how those concerns differ across people and cultures. The passage above shows the two traditions meeting: the foundations named harm/care and fairness/reciprocity are the concerns Kohlberg and Gilligan had already put at the centre. The same passage calls these two the "individualising" foundations, a label that implies others exist beyond them. For this library the tradition matters because it gives right action an empirical shape: the old commandments, non-harm and justice, turn out to be measurable human concerns, and the wider research tradition keeps asking how they develop.

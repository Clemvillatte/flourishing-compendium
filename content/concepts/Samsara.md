Saṃsāra (Sanskrit, literally "wandering-on") is the beginningless cycle of birth, death, and rebirth driven by karma and ignorance. The seed's summary stands, and the early texts give it a sharper edge: samsara is a process, not a place. Thanissaro Bhikkhu: "Samsara literally means 'wandering-on.' Many people think of it as the Buddhist name for the place where we currently live — the place we leave when we go to nibbana. But in the early Buddhist texts, it's the answer, not to the question, 'Where are we?' but to the question, 'What are we doing?' Instead of a place, it's a process: the tendency to keep creating worlds and then moving into them." ([Thanissaro, "Samsara," 2002](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/samsara.html))

### No discoverable beginning

The Saṁyutta Nikāya's Anamatagga-saṁyutta ("without discoverable beginning") repeats one formula across its images of tears, blood, grass, and mountains: "From an inconstruable beginning comes transmigration. A beginning point is not evident, though beings hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving are transmigrating & wandering on." ([SN 15.11, Duggata Sutta, tr. Thanissaro](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn15/sn15.011.than.html)) The point is not cosmology but urgency: if there is no first mistake, no one is coming to end the wandering for you. "Long have you thus experienced stress, experienced pain, experienced loss, swelling the cemeteries."

### The process reading

Thanissaro's essay presses the definition toward practice: "As one world falls apart, you create another one and go there." The worlds in question are built from craving and identity, and the suffering is not a punishment for wandering but the wearing of the road itself: "The worlds we create keep caving in and killing us." The remedy the Buddha sought is therefore a stop, not a relocation: "This is why the Buddha tried to find the way to stop samsara-ing." In the compendium's terms, samsara names the engine that [[B005 — Intention is karma]] diagnoses and [[B089 — Intervene at intention]] addresses at its lever.

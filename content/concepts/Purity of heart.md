### Definition

Purity of heart is Kierkegaard's name for an undivided will: the heart that wills one thing only, and wills it in truth. His 1847 address opens with the title as thesis and the Apostle as witness:

> Therefore the Apostle says, "Purify your hearts ye double-minded," that is, purify your hearts of double-mindedness; in other words, let your heart in truth will only one thing, for therein is the heart's purity. ([Kierkegaard, Purity of Heart Is to Will One Thing, ch. 3](https://www.religion-online.org/book-chapter/chapter-3-barriers-to-willing-one-thing-variety-and-great-moments-are-not-one-thing/))

The one thing is the Good, and Kierkegaard insists on its shape:

> For the Good without condition and without qualification, without preface and without compromise is, absolutely the only thing that a man may and should will, and is only one thing. ([Kierkegaard, ch. 3](https://www.religion-online.org/book-chapter/chapter-3-barriers-to-willing-one-thing-variety-and-great-moments-are-not-one-thing/))

### Origin

The book is a devotional address written in 1846 and published in Copenhagen in March 1847, a "spiritual preparation for the office of confession" aimed at "that solitary individual" (hiin Enkelte). Its method is diagnostic: chapter after chapter catalogues the barriers that break the will in two, variety, great moments, reward, egocentric service of the Good, and shows each to be a form of double-mindedness. Purity here is not innocence; it is a fought-for singleness that confession keeps honest.

### A real-life example

A volunteer feeds the hungry. One year in, the photos of the feeding are on her profile and the statistics on her CV; on hard days she checks them for warmth. The work has not changed; the will has. Kierkegaard's question is not whether she does good things, but whether the Good is the one thing she wills, and the honest answer requires exactly the solitude his address demands.

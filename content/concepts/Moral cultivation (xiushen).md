Xiushen (修身), usually translated as cultivating the person or self-cultivation, is the Confucian practice of building moral character. One article explains the term: 修 (xiu) means "to repair", "to mend", or "to embellish", while 身 (shen) means "body", so xiushen "literally means 'body-fixing'" and "in its figurative sense, it denotes the cultivation of the self with one's moral character" ([Self-cultivation through art, Taylor and Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00131857.2021.1977624)).

### What the Great Learning says

The Great Learning (Daxue) gives the central line in one translation: "From the Son of Heaven down to the mass of the people, all must consider the cultivation of the person the root of everything besides." The same text runs the sequence backwards: "Wishing to regulate their families, they first cultivated their persons. Wishing to cultivate their persons, they first rectified their hearts. Wishing to rectify their hearts, they first sought to be sincere in their thoughts" ([The Great Learning, UCSD bilingual edition](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~dkjordan/chin/Koong/GreatLearning.html)). It adds that the consensus today is that Confucius did not write it, "but that they closely represent his views and his ways of reasoning".

In the eight-step sequence, cultivating the person is the fifth step, after investigating things, extending knowing, making intentions sincere and rectifying the mind, and before regulating the family, ordering the state and bringing peace to all ([Neo-Confucian Self-Cultivation, Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/620649075/Neo-Confucian-self-cultivation)). A gongfu study calls the fifth step "the turning point between 'inner sageliness' and 'outer kingliness'" ([The Gongfu Theory in The Great Learning, Clausius Press](https://www.clausiuspress.com/assets/default/article/2026/09/20/article_1789898705.pdf)).

### Beyond one text

The Doctrine of the Mean has Confucius say that studiousness, earnest practice and a sense of shame are near to sensibility, benevolence and bravery: "He who knows these three things knows how to cultivate his own character" (Legge, as quoted in the Taylor and Francis article). The Analects frame the path as gradual: "From the age of fifteen on, I have been intent upon learning... from seventy on, I have followed my heart's desire without transgressing what is right" (2.4), and "The Master's Way is nothing but other-regard and self-reflection" (4.15) ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Confucius](https://iep.utm.edu/confucius/)). The Stanford Encyclopedia says Zhu Xi held that anyone "sincerely determined to practice moral self-cultivation" can attain "a degree of ethical self-realization if not sagehood", through reverence (jing) and investigating things ([Zhu Xi, Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2018/entries/zhu-xi/)).

### A caution for essays

The Great Learning is a short text with a later commentary, and Zhu Xi's reading is one influential interpretation. The Scribd copy notes there is "still no conclusive proof" that Zengzi wrote the commentary. Quote the translation you use and name it, because the English for xiu and shen varies.

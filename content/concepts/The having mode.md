The having mode is Erich Fromm's name for one of his two "fundamental modes of experience," the one in which the self relates to the world by possession. In the book's formula, "I am = what I have and what I consume" (p. 27). Fromm describes the mode's inner logic:

> In the having mode, there is no alive relationship between me and what I have. It and I have become things, and I have it, because I have the force to make it mine. But there is also a reverse relationship: it has me, because my sense of identity, i.e., of sanity, rests upon my having it (and as many things as possible).
>
> (Fromm, To Have or To Be?, 1976, pp. 77-78; [quotations page](https://spiritwiki.lightningpath.org/To_Have_or_To_Be))

The contrast is the being mode, the mode of existence "in which one neither has anything nor craves to have something, but is joyous, employs one's faculties productively, is oned to the world" (pp. 18-19). Where the two modes land is plain in Fromm's summary: "In the having mode, one's happiness lies in one's superiority over others, in one's power, and in the last analysis, in one's capacity to conquer, rob, kill. In the being mode it lies in loving, sharing, giving" (p. 81).

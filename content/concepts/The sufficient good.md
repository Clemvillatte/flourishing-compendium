The sufficient good is the good that leaves nothing lacking: not the highest by measure, but the one whose possession makes a life complete. The seed names Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics, where the claim is made about happiness.

### Aristotle's ground

The definition turns on self-sufficiency:

> "for the present we define that to be self-sufficient \"which taken alone makes life choice-worthy, and to be in want of nothing;\" now of such kind we think Happiness to be" (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, book I, ch. VII, trans. D. P. Chase, [Project Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt))

The conclusion drawn from it:

> "So then Happiness is manifestly something final and self-sufficient, being the end of all things which are and may be done." (same work)

And again, later in the same argument: "because Happiness has no lack of anything but is self-sufficient" (book I). The sufficient good is not one good among others to be added to a pile; it is the good that, once present, ends the adding.

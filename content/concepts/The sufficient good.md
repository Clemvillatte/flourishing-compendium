The compendium's heading "the sufficient good" points here to Aristotle's account of happiness as final and self-sufficient. The seed names the Nicomachean Ethics.

### Aristotle's ground

In the opened Chase translation, self-sufficiency makes life "choice-worthy" and leaves it wanting nothing:

> "for the present we define that to be self-sufficient \"which taken alone makes life choice-worthy, and to be in want of nothing;\" now of such kind we think Happiness to be" (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, book I, trans. D. P. Chase, [Project Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt))

The conclusion drawn from it:

> "So then Happiness is manifestly something final and self-sufficient, being the end of all things which are and may be done." (same work)

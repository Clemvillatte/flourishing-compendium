Non-contention is the Daoist refusal to wrangle for position or victory, drawn from two chapters of the Daodejing. Chapter 8 makes it the closing mark of the person of highest excellence, in the water image that runs through the chapter: water benefits all things and occupies, without striving, the low place all men dislike. Chapter 68 gives it a military setting: the skillful fighter assumes no martial port, and victory belongs to the one who keeps from his foes apart. In Legge's translation of chapter 8:

> "And when (one with the highest excellence) does not wrangle (about his low position), no one finds fault with him." (Laozi, [Daodejing 8, Legge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/216))

Chapter 68's summary of the same idea, in Legge's verse rendering:

> "Thus we say, 'He ne'er contends, / And therein is his might.'" (Laozi, [Daodejing 68, Legge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/216))

### What the refusal is not

Neither chapter asks the reader to withdraw from action. Chapter 8 ties the refusal to benefiting all things, and chapter 68 names the best fighter and the effective ruler as the ones who practise it: the one whose commands men most fulfil, Legge notes, "humbly plies his art". Non-contention is a stance within effort, not an exit from it.

I pair the idea with two principles: A017 (Practice gentleness) lists it among its philosophy parallels, and S043 (Be patient with difficult people) sets it beside the practice of patience under provocation.

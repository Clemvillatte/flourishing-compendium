A tipping point, in the network approach process-based therapy uses, is the moment a stable pattern of mutually reinforcing elements shifts abruptly into a new configuration: change resists, then suddenly happens. The manual describes the phenomenon in both directions:

> "A network might be resistant to change until it reaches a tipping point in a specific process. An important sign of an impending tipping point is a critical speeding up or slowing down in the network model." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, 2021, Learning Process-Based Therapy, Chapter 9)

The book adds the dynamic-systems picture: "When the change finally happens, it happens abruptly once the network reaches a tipping point" (Chapter 9), described as a ball rolling out of one valley over a hill into another. For a therapist, the passage's practical reminder is its conditional wording: a network *might* resist change, and *may* shift abruptly once a tipping point is reached — the critical slowing-down sign is one thing to watch, not a rule for what the slowness means.

### The same logic in symptom networks

In the network theory of mental disorders, Denny Borsboom describes why a disorder can outlast its cause: "Strongly connected networks thus feature an asymmetry in their dynamics: although the presence of a given trigger event can activate a strongly connected network, the subsequent absence of that event needs not de-activate it." He calls this hysteresis, "a hallmark of phase transitions", and notes that in weakly connected networks the symptoms are not self-sustaining, so the network "will gradually recover and return to its asymptomatic state" (Borsboom, 2017, [World Psychiatry, via PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5269502/)). On this picture an established pattern can persist after its trigger is gone because the connections themselves keep it going.

### What it is not

A tipping point is not a prediction that change will arrive on schedule, and not a reason to wait for one. It names one possible shape of change, a threshold in a connected system, and does not promise one for any person.

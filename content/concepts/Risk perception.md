Risk perception is the study of the judgments people make when asked to characterize and evaluate hazardous activities and technologies. The field was shaped by Paul Slovic and colleagues' psychometric paradigm, which maps hazards by their perceived characteristics; in that work people's risk judgments track two prominent derived factors, dread risk and unknown risk, and a third factor reflecting the number of people exposed to the risk has been obtained in several studies ([Slovic and Weber](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/chrr/documents/meetings/roundtable/white_papers/slovic_wp.pdf)).

> "Factor 1, labeled "dread risk," is defined at its high (right hand) end of perceived lack of control, dread, catastrophic potential, fatal consequences, and the inequitable distribution of risks and benefits. ... Factor 2, labeled "unknown risk," is defined at its high end by hazards judged to be unobservable, unknown, new, and delayed in their manifestation of harm." (Slovic and Weber, [Perception of Risk Posed by Extreme Events](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/chrr/documents/meetings/roundtable/white_papers/slovic_wp.pdf), white paper, p. 9, factor-analysis passage)

### Lay judgments and control

The two factors separate lay judgment from expert judgment. When experts judge risk, their responses correlate highly with technical estimates of annual fatalities, whereas lay judgments relate more to other hazard characteristics such as catastrophic potential and threat to future generations, so expert-lay conflicts often turn on different definitions of risk rather than on different opinions about acceptable levels ([Slovic and Weber](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/chrr/documents/meetings/roundtable/white_papers/slovic_wp.pdf)). The qualitative characteristics also travel together: hazards rated as voluntary tend also to be rated as controllable and well-known, and the higher a hazard's dread score, the higher its perceived risk and the more people want it reduced and strictly regulated ([Slovic and Weber](https://www.ldeo.columbia.edu/chrr/documents/meetings/roundtable/white_papers/slovic_wp.pdf)).

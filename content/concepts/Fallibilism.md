Fallibilism is the epistemological principle that any belief, however well supported, may turn out to be mistaken — one can know that *p* while it remains possible that *p* is false ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, notes on fallibilism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2023/entries/contextualism-epistemology/notes.html); [SEP, "Certainty"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/certainty/)). The term's philosophical career is largely Charles Sanders Peirce's: his fallibilism held that inquiry never reaches absolute certainty, and that this is a feature of science's self-correcting method rather than a scandal — errors are how investigation makes progress ([on Peirce's fallibilism](https://journals.ysu.am/bulletin-ysu-phil-psych/en/article/view/13509)).

The distinction that organises the compendium's use: fallibilism is not skepticism. The skeptic withholds assent; the fallibilist assents — to the best-tested conclusion available — while holding the assent revisable ([[P047 — Give up the quest for certainty]]; [[P052 — Hold hypotheses, not positions]]). It is the epistemic version of the middle path between dogmatism and relativism ([[P032 — Walk the middle path between objectivism and relativism]]), and it is what makes [[The examined life]] a permanent practice rather than a completed audit ([[P010 — Hold every belief as provisional — fallibilism]] is the compendium's principle form).

The practical reading: treat error as information rather than insult ([[P051 — Take error as information]]), keep the self-concept as revisable as the beliefs ([[P063 — Treat your self-concept as revisable]]), and let intellectual humility do the work that certainty cannot ([[CO068 — Cultivate intellectual humility — the foundation of learning]]; [[CO069 — Learn to think in probabilities — certainty is usually an illusion]]).

An example at its smallest: in any argument, naming in advance the observation that would change your mind — and noticing whether one is available at all.

Its Peircean twin is [[The pragmatic maxim]] — meaning and inquiry where certainty is refused.

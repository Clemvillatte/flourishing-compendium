Right action (samma kammanta) concerns bodily conduct within the Noble Eightfold Path: abstaining from killing, taking what is not given and sexual misconduct ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, Right Action](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)). SN 45.8 lists it alongside right speech and right livelihood as distinct path factors ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)).

> "Right action means refraining from unwholesome deeds that occur with the body as their natural means of expression." (Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Noble Eightfold Path, [Right Action](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html).)

### Restraint and care

Bodhi gives each restraint a positive counterpart: compassion toward living beings, honesty and generosity concerning possessions, and fidelity in sexual relationships ([Right Action, subsections 1-3](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)). Intention matters in his account of killing: he distinguishes deliberate destruction of life from accidental killing ([subsection 1](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

### Lay and monastic practice

Bodhi distinguishes a layperson's restraint from harmful sexual conduct from the celibacy required of monks and nuns ([subsection 3](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)). His discussion explicitly treats coercive sexual conduct as the offender's violation, not the victim's ([subsection 3](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)). Right livelihood extends the ethical training to how a person earns a living ([Right Livelihood](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

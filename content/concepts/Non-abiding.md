Non-abiding is the Diamond Sutra's instruction about how to hold the mind: engaged, but resting on nothing. The sutra's chapter 10 gives the rule in Charles Muller's translation:

> "And so, Subhūti, the bodhisattvas and mahasattvas should give rise to the pure aspiration in this way: they should not give rise to the aspiration while abiding in form. They should not give rise to the aspiration while abiding in sound, odor, taste, touch, or concepts. They should give rise to the aspiration while not abiding in anything." (Diamond Sutra §10, [Muller translation](http://www.acmuller.net/bud-canon/diamond_sutra.html))

The same instruction returns in chapter 14 with the paradox that makes the idea distinctive: "They should give rise to the aspiration that has no abode. If the mind abides, then this is not abiding. Therefore I say that the mind of the bodhisattvas should not abide in the form of charity" ([Diamond Sutra §14, Muller translation](http://www.acmuller.net/bud-canon/diamond_sutra.html)).

### What the rule asks

The passage does not ask for an empty mind. It asks that the awakened mind arise at all, compassion and aspiration included, while refusing to settle into any of the senses' objects or into concepts. The giving is unconditional on what the giver gets back; the aspiration operates without a fixed resting place.

The chapter 14 context makes the refusal a claim about what happens when the mind does abide. The Buddha recalls a lifetime in which his body was cut into pieces, and says that if he had been holding to the notions of self, person, sentient being or life span at that time, he would have felt ill-will; the memory he draws from is of having been free of those notions while practising forbearance ([Diamond Sutra §14](http://www.acmuller.net/bud-canon/diamond_sutra.html)). Abiding in a notion, on this telling, is what makes an injury into a grievance. The instruction to give rise to an aspiration with no abode is therefore not a demand for vagueness but a way of acting that does not put its own continuity at stake in the things it touches.

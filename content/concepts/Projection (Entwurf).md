"Projection (Entwurf)" — Heidegger, Being and Time.

Heidegger's Entwurf is the throwing of Dasein ahead of itself onto its own possibilities. The inventory image carries the point: a life listed as what is present would miss what it is, because what it is includes what it may still become.

> "Dasein is in every case what it can be, and in the way in which it is its possibility." (Heidegger, Being and Time §31, [Macquarrie and Robinson translation, p. 183, beyng.com](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.183.html))
>
> "On the contrary, any Dasein has, as Dasein, already projected itself; and as long as it is, it is projecting. [...] Because of the kind of Being which is constituted by the existentiale of projection, Dasein is constantly 'more' than it factually is, supposing that one might want to make an inventory of it as something-at-hand and list the contents of its Being, and supposing that one were able to do so." (Heidegger, Being and Time §31, [Macquarrie and Robinson translation, p. 185, beyng.com](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.185.html))

### What to practise

The verb in Heidegger's Entwurf is thrown ahead, and the reader's use of it is a habit of description rather than a habit of planning. To describe a life only by what is on hand, by its current facts alone, is to leave out the forward lean that makes those facts matter at all, since a fact only counts against you relative to what you are aiming at. Read the Macquarrie sentence as a practice instruction: whenever you take an inventory of yourself, list what you are still in the middle of becoming, and treat that entry as the most informative one in the list.

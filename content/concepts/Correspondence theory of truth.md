The correspondence theory of truth holds that truth consists in a relation between what is said or believed and reality. The formulation often traced back to [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]] reads:

> "To say of what is that it is not, or of what is not that it is, is false, while to say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true." — Aristotle, Metaphysics, as quoted in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/))

The Encyclopedia notes that "virtually identical formulations can be found in Plato (Cratylus 385b2, Sophist 263b)" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/)).

The theory has a narrow and a broad reading. Narrowly speaking, it is "the view that truth is correspondence to, or with, a fact — a view that was advocated by Russell and Moore early in the 20th century" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/)). Russell's canonical statement of it reads:

> "Thus a belief is true when there is a corresponding fact, and is false when there is no corresponding fact." — Russell, The Problems of Philosophy (1912), as quoted in the SEP ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/))

Broadly, the label covers any view:

> "explicitly embracing the idea that truth consists in a relation to reality, i.e., that truth is a relational property involving a characteristic relation (to be specified) to some portion of reality (to be specified)." — SEP ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/))

The idea has been expressed in so many ways that the SEP speaks of "an extended family of theories and, more often, theory sketches", whose members "employ various concepts for the relevant relation (correspondence, conformity, congruence, agreement, accordance, copying, picturing, signification, representation, reference, satisfaction) and/or various concepts for the relevant portion of reality" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/)). The theory is often associated with metaphysical realism, and Russell and Moore's advocacy of truth as correspondence to a fact was "an integral part of their defense of metaphysical realism"; its traditional competitors, pragmatist, coherentist and verificationist theories, are often associated with idealism, anti-realism or relativism ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/)).

The correspondence theory of truth holds that truth consists in a relation between what is said or believed and reality. Its classical source is Aristotle's definition: "To say of what is that it is not, or of what is not that it is, is false, while to say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth-correspondence/)). The Encyclopedia notes that "virtually identical formulations can be found in Plato (Cratylus 385b2, Sophist 263b)" (SEP).

The theory has a narrow and a broad reading. Narrowly, it is "the view that truth is correspondence to, or with, a fact — a view that was advocated by Russell and Moore early in the 20th century". Broadly, the label covers any view "explicitly embracing the idea that truth consists in a relation to reality, i.e., that truth is a relational property involving a characteristic relation (to be specified) to some portion of reality (to be specified)" (SEP).

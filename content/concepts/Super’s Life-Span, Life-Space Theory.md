Donald Super's life-span, life-space theory treats a career not as a line of jobs but as the shape a whole life takes. Two axes carry the theory: the life-span, the sequence of stages a person moves through from childhood to late adulthood, and the life-space, the set of roles a person occupies at any given time, of which worker is only one.

> "A career is defined as the combination and sequence of roles played by a person during the course of a lifetime." (Super, "A life-span, life-space approach to career development", [Journal of Vocational Behavior 16(3), 1980, article abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016/0001-8791(80)90056-1), as reproduced by OpenAIRE)

### Origin

Super presented the approach in a 1980 article in the Journal of Vocational Behavior. The article introduced the Life-Career Rainbow, a diagram in which the stages of life run along one axis and the roles along the other, so that one person's career can be read as a changing pattern of involvement rather than a single job title.

> "A Life-Career Rainbow is presented as a means of helping conceptualize multidimensional careers, the temporal involvement in, and the emotional commitment to, each role." (Super 1980, [article abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016/0001-8791(80)90056-1), as reproduced by OpenAIRE)

### Core claims

> "These roles include those of child, pupil or student, leisurite, citizen, worker, spouse, homemaker, parent, and pensioner, positions with associated expectations that are occupied at some time by most people, and other less common roles such as those of criminal, reformer, and lover." (Super 1980, [article abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016/0001-8791(80)90056-1), as reproduced by OpenAIRE)

> "The simultaneous combination of life roles constitutes the lifestyle; their sequential combination structures the life space and constitutes the life cycle" (Super 1990, p. 288, as quoted in [Cardello, Parker-Bell and Shao, National Career Development Association](https://www.ncda.org/aws/NCDA/page_template/show_detail/559383?model_name=news_article))

### The two axes

The life-span side of the theory names five stages, each with its own tasks: growth in childhood, exploration in adolescence, establishment in early adulthood, maintenance in middle adulthood, and disengagement in late adulthood. The life-space side holds that the same person is always playing several roles at once, and that the balance among them, not the job alone, is what a career actually is.

### In everyday life

The theory's practical instrument is the Rainbow itself: a person maps how much time and emotional commitment each role receives now, and how they would like that pattern to look. The 1980 article briefly considered this use of the Rainbow in career education and counseling. For someone weighing a change, the theory relocates the question from "which job" to "which pattern of roles", which can show that a work problem is a role-balance problem.

Donald Super's life-span, life-space theory treats a career as the combination and sequence of roles played by a person during the course of a lifetime, with work as one role among many. Two axes carry the theory: the life-span, the stages a person moves through from childhood to late adulthood, and the life-space, the roles a person occupies over a lifetime.

> "A career is defined as the combination and sequence of roles played by a person during the course of a lifetime." (Super, "A life-span, life-space approach to career development", [Journal of Vocational Behavior 16(3), 1980, article abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016/0001-8791(80)90056-1), as reproduced by OpenAIRE)

### Origin

Super presented the approach in a 1980 article in the Journal of Vocational Behavior. The article presents the Life-Career Rainbow, a device for conceptualizing multidimensional careers; an ERIC digest of the theory describes the Rainbow as "the cornerstone of Super's life-span life-space theory".

> "A Life-Career Rainbow is presented as a means of helping conceptualize multidimensional careers, the temporal involvement in, and the emotional commitment to, each role." (Super 1980, [article abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016/0001-8791(80)90056-1), as reproduced by OpenAIRE)

### Core claims

> "These roles include those of child, pupil or student, leisurite, citizen, worker, spouse, homemaker, parent, and pensioner, positions with associated expectations that are occupied at some time by most people, and other less common roles such as those of criminal, reformer, and lover." (Super 1980, [article abstract](https://oamonitor.ireland.openaire.eu/national/search/publication?pid=10.1016/0001-8791(80)90056-1), as reproduced by OpenAIRE)

> "The simultaneous combination of life roles constitutes the lifestyle; their sequential combination structures the life space and constitutes the life cycle" (Super 1990, p. 288, as quoted in [Cardello, Parker-Bell and Shao, National Career Development Association](https://www.ncda.org/aws/NCDA/page_template/show_detail/559383?model_name=news_article))

### The two axes

The life-span side of the theory names five stages: growth in childhood, exploration in adolescence, establishment in early adulthood, maintenance in middle adulthood, and disengagement in late adulthood. The life-space side holds that a person occupies particular roles at particular times, playing them in four theatres: home, school, workplace and community. The ERIC digest lists the nine roles: child, student, leisurite, citizen, parent, spouse, homemaker, worker and pensioner.

### In everyday life

The theory's practical instrument is the Rainbow assessment. An ERIC digest describes the procedure: "clients are asked to develop their current and ideal LCRs", and where there are discrepancies between the two, "clients are then directed to discuss plans to attain ideal LCR" and the barriers in the way. The underlying assumption, as a textbook summary cited by the NCDA article puts it, is "that career choice is a developmental process spanning one's entire life rather than a single decision".

Actively open-minded thinking is Jonathan Baron's standard for good thinking: the willingness to weigh new evidence against a favored belief and to keep searching rather than close early. A Judgment and Decision Making article describes the construct:

> "Going beyond the reflection/impulsivity construct, Baron (1993) developed a reasoning style called actively open-minded thinking (AOT). This style of thinking includes the tendency to weigh new evidence against a favored belief, to spend sufficient time on a problem before giving up, and to consider carefully the opinions of others in forming one's own."
> — [Haran, Ritov & Mellers 2013, "The role of actively open-minded thinking in information acquisition, accuracy, and calibration", Judgment and Decision Making 8(3):188-201 (open journal text)](https://sjdm.org/~baron/journal/13/13124a/jdm13124a.html)

The same study reports what the style predicts: "Actively open-minded thinking (AOT) predicted persistence in information acquisition as well as accuracy and calibration of estimates" ([Haran et al. 2013](https://sjdm.org/~baron/journal/13/13124a/jdm13124a.html)). The seed's forecasting claim is supported at the level of estimates: the study's measures were accuracy and calibration, not agreement.

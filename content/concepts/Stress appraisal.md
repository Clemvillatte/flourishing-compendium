Stress appraisal is the central idea of Richard Lazarus and Susan Folkman's transactional theory of stress (1984). Their definition, as quoted in a PMC article, is: "psychological stress is a particular relationship between the person and the environment that is appraised by the person as taxing or exceeding his or her resources and endangering his or her well-being". Cognitive appraisal is the "process of categorizing an encounter, and its various facets, with respect to its significance for well-being" ([Stress and Coping with Discrimination and Stigmatization, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110961/)).

### Two stages of appraisal

Primary appraisal asks what is at stake: "Am I in trouble or being benefited, now or in the future, and in what ways?" A situation is then categorised as a threat, a challenge or a loss. Secondary appraisal asks "Can I cope with this situation?" and weighs physical, social, psychological and material resources ([PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110961/)). A review of the transactional model summarises the upshot: stress "is not solely caused by external stressors but rather arises from how individuals appraise them" ([Challenge–Hindrance–Threat framework, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11428960/)).

### Challenge and threat together

Lazarus and Folkman held that threat and challenge "are not two ends of a single continuum". Although the two appraisals are negatively correlated, they "can occur simultaneously" ([PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110961/)).

A recent study testing the model reports that "higher appraisal of situational relevance was associated with increased stress, whereas higher appraisal of coping resources was associated with lower stress levels" ([Revisiting Lazarus' transactional model, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13577512/)).

### Coping

Coping is defined as "cognitive and behavioral efforts to master, reduce, or tolerate the internal and/or external demands that are created by the stressful transaction". Lazarus and Folkman proposed two main functions, emotion-focused coping and problem-focused coping ([PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3110961/)).

### Scope

These quotations come from secondary articles that cite the 1984 book, which was not read directly. Appraisal does not remove real danger. The theory says that the person's interpretation and perceived resources shape the stress response.

## What it is NOT



Practical wisdom is not the application of rules. Aristotle is explicit that the circumstances of action vary so much that no set of rules, however complicated, can solve every practical problem.  And it is not separable from character: phronesis and ethical virtue require each other. Modern attempts to formalise phronesis (such as the Aristotelian Phronesis Model) are reconstructions, not Aristotle's own theory.

## Practical implications



Practical wisdom is the skill that generalises: for any domain — health, work, relationships — the expert practitioner is not the one who knows the most rules but the one who reads the situation and chooses the response the situation actually needs. It is acquired by the same route as any expertise: repetition with feedback, across varied cases, over time. This is analogous to modern deliberate practice and to the research on coping flexibility, though the connection is interpretive rather than a demonstrated equivalence. The practical implication for a flourishing practice is to treat rules as training wheels, not as the destination.

expertise built through structured feedback (Ericsson et al., 1993).

Deliberate practice is Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer's term for activities specially designed to improve the current level of performance, with specific goals, immediate feedback and repetition, usually arranged by a teacher or coach ([Ericsson et al. 1993](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/1993-ericsson.pdf), p. 368). The 1993 authors argued that expert performance is the end result of such efforts, and the paper's abstract states the wider claim plainly: in most domains of expertise, individuals begin in childhood a regimen of effortful activities designed to optimize improvement, and many characteristics once believed to reflect innate talent are, on this account, the result of intense practice extended for a minimum of ten years ([Ericsson et al. 1993](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/1993-ericsson.pdf), abstract).

The evidence base was retrospective: the Berlin musicians estimated their weekly practice for each year since starting, accumulated amounts were calculated from those estimates, and the most accomplished players reported the most practice ([Ericsson et al. 1993](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/1993-ericsson.pdf), pp. 373-379). That retrospective method is where later work pressed. A 2019 replication of the original violin design, run double-blind with analyses better suited to the study design, found the effect substantial but considerably smaller than the original, and did not reproduce the correspondence between practice amounts and each skill level ([Macnamara and Maitra 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31598236/), abstract). The contested part is the strength of the accounting claim: what the replication pressed was how much of the difference in skill the practice hours can account for.

### What the practice involves

The 1993 definition:

> “In contrast to play, deliberate practice is a highly structured activity, the explicit goal of which is to improve performance. Specific tasks are invented to overcome weaknesses, and performance is carefully monitored to provide cues for ways to improve it further.” (Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer 1993, [Psychological Review 100(3):363-406](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/1993-ericsson.pdf), p. 368)

Ericsson's later overview compresses the conditions:

> “DP also involves the provision of immediate feedback, time for problem-solving and evaluation, and opportunities for repeated performance to refine behavior.” (Ericsson 2008, [Acad Emerg Med 15(11):988-94](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18778378/), abstract)

### The strong claim and its contested status

The 1993 summary claim:

> “We argue that expertise and expert performance are the result of extensive engagement in relevant practice activities supervised by teachers and coaches and that individual differences in ultimate performance can largely be accounted for by differential amounts of past and current levels of practice.” (Ericsson, Krampe and Tesch-Römer 1993, [Psychological Review 100(3):363-406](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/1993-ericsson.pdf), p. 392)

A 2019 replication of the original violin design did not reproduce the core finding:

> “We did not replicate the core finding that accumulated amounts of deliberate practice corresponded to each skill level. Overall, the size of the effect was substantial, but considerably smaller than the original study's effect size.” (Macnamara and Maitra 2019, [R Soc Open Sci 6(8):190327](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31598236/), abstract)

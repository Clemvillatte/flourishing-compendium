(Horkheimer and Adorno, Dialectic of Enlightenment; Habermas, Theory of Communicative Action).

Instrumental reason is reason reduced to the choice of means. It asks how to get what it has been given, and it has stopped asking whether what it has been given is worth wanting.

> "Reason was supposed to liberate human beings. Instead, in the dominant form it takes as instrumental rationality, it has become the primary instrument of their domination." — [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Critical Theory (Frankfurt School)", §1.5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/critical-theory/)

### How the critique runs

The lineage the entry traces runs through Weber: his "theory of rationalization... stressed the growing dominance of instrumental rationality, or means-end reasoning, through the expanding bureaucratization of society." Horkheimer and Adorno's charge is that this form of reason, having no vocabulary for ends, ends up serving whatever ends survive, and that "humans lose their capacity for critical reflection as their thinking is increasingly oriented solely toward self-preservation within a system in which they are powerless." Habermas's answer, in the seed citation, is to relocate reason in communication: ends become discussable again when the validity of what is said can be questioned by those affected. For flourishing, the concept is a warning about efficiency: a life optimised is not thereby a life examined.

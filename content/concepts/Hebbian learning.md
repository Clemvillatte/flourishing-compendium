Hebbian learning is the principle that connections between neurons strengthen through repeated joint activity. Donald Hebb stated it in The Organization of Behavior (1949, p. 62): "When an axon of cell A is near enough to excite a cell B and repeatedly or persistently takes part in firing it, some growth process or metabolic change takes place in one or both cells such that A's efficiency, as one of the cells firing B, is increased." (as quoted in [Keysers and Gazzola, Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 2014](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4006178/))

### The slogan is not Hebb's

The rhyme "neurons that fire together wire together" is a later summary. Keysers and Gazzola write that "Carla Shatz (but not Hebb himself) has paraphrased his principle in a rhyme", and that "Hebb himself never wrote 'Cells that fire together, wire together'." They add that it was first introduced in a Scientific American article for the lay public. Another paper credits the phrase to Löwel and Singer (1992) ([Synaptic Ensemble Underlying the Selection and Consolidation of Neuronal Circuits, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332426/)). Either way, the line dates from 1992, not 1949.

### What the slogan hides

Keysers and Gazzola read Hebb's wording closely: "He writes not that two neurons need to fire together to increase the efficiency of their connection but that one neuron needs to repeatedly (consistency) take part in firing (causality) the other." So the slogan "bares the risk of obscuring the importance of causation", since "temporal precedence, rather than simultaneity, is the signature of causality". The postulate also names no mechanism, only "some growth process or metabolic change".

### An old idea, newly organised

Hebb did not claim the underlying idea. A history paper notes that he wrote that "the general idea is an old one, that any two cells or systems of cells that are repeatedly active at the same time will tend to become 'associated' so that activity in one facilitates activity in the other" (Hebb, 1949, p. 70, quoted in [The Hebb Synapse Before Hebb, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8566713/)). The same paper describes the "cell assembly" as "a set of neurons and their connecting pathways which act together", and a "phase sequence" as a series of cell assemblies linked over time.

### Later evidence

A paper on cerebellar plasticity says the 1973 discovery of long-term potentiation "demonstrated that synaptic connections can indeed be strengthened in a use-dependent way, thus reflecting a key prediction of the Hebb postulate" (Bliss and Lømo, 1973, in [Non-Hebbian spike-timing-dependent plasticity in cerebellar circuits, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3542521/)). The title of that paper is itself a reminder that some synapses follow rules other than Hebb's. Keysers and Gazzola's abstract adds that "Spike-timing-dependent plasticity is considered the neurophysiological basis of Hebbian learning and has been shown to be sensitive to both contingency and contiguity between pre- and postsynaptic activity".

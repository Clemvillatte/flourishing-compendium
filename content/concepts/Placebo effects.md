A placebo is a treatment intended to appear genuine but which has no pharmaceutical effect; in trials, the placebo effect is the measured difference between the response to such a treatment and no treatment ([Wikipedia, Placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo)). The seed's citation, Kaptchuk et al., 2008, is the BMJ trial that took the effect apart in patients with irritable bowel syndrome. Its stated objective:

> "To investigate whether placebo effects can experimentally be separated into the response to three components—assessment and observation, a therapeutic ritual (placebo treatment), and a supportive patient-practitioner relationship—and then progressively combined to produce incremental clinical improvement in patients with irritable bowel syndrome." ([Kaptchuk et al., 2008, BMJ 336:999, objective](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-e1d2-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content))

The trial's conclusion, verbatim:

> "Factors contributing to the placebo effect can be progressively combined in a manner resembling a graded dose escalation of component parts. Non-specific effects can produce statistically and clinically significant outcomes and the patient-practitioner relationship is the most robust component." ([Kaptchuk et al., 2008, BMJ 336:999](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-e1d2-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content))

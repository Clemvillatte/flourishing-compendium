A placebo is a medicine or treatment intended to appear genuine to its recipient but which has no pharmaceutical effect; the placebo effect is the measurable difference between the response to such a treatment and no treatment:

> "In a clinical trial, a placebo response is the measured response of subjects to a placebo; the placebo effect is the difference between that response and no treatment." ([Wikipedia, Placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo))

The seed's source is Kaptchuk et al., 2008, retained in Sources. The expectation account the article reports is Irving Kirsch's 1985 hypothesis, that placebo effects are produced by the self-fulfilling effects of response expectancies, in which the belief that one will feel different leads a person to actually feel different ([Wikipedia, Placebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo)).

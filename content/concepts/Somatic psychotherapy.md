Somatic psychotherapy (also called body-oriented or body psychotherapy) is the umbrella term for psychotherapies that use bodily techniques as a working part of the treatment. Somatic Experiencing and sensorimotor psychotherapy are named modalities within it ([Myers et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515382/)). A clinical review defines the field this way:

> "Body-oriented or somatic psychotherapies encompass clinical approaches that explicitly focus on body techniques as a means of neurophysiological regulation, therapeutic communication and exploration." ([Myers et al., 2021, § Body-centered psychotherapies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515382/))

### The working premise

The shared premise is that awareness of bodily sensations can reduce psychological distress. In the review's words, "development of somatic awareness through sensory (rather than cognitive) resources (such as interoception, proprioception, kinesthesis), lead to reduction in psychological distress through neurophysiological regulation techniques" ([Myers et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515382/)). The authors describe these as "bottom-up" approaches, complementary to traditional "top-down" modalities; they rely less on verbal cognitive approaches and more on regulatory body systems such as the autonomic, arousal and limbic systems.

### Evidence status

A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis screened 2,180 references and included 18 randomized controlled trials, reporting medium effects of body psychotherapy on psychopathology and psychological distress ([Rosendahl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8458738/)). The same review is candid about the field's standing: "body psychotherapy (BPT) appears not to be a well-established treatment option" ([Rosendahl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8458738/)). One definition cited in that review, from Geuter, is that body psychotherapy is "a treatment employing psychological and bodily means equally" ([Rosendahl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8458738/)).

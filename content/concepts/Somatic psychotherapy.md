Somatic psychotherapy (also called body-oriented or body psychotherapy) is the umbrella term for psychotherapies that treat the body as a working part of the therapy itself, not just the place where symptoms show up. Named modalities under it include Somatic Experiencing and sensorimotor psychotherapy. A clinical review defines the field this way:

> "Body-oriented or somatic psychotherapies encompass clinical approaches that explicitly focus on body techniques as a means of neurophysiological regulation, therapeutic communication and exploration." ([Myers et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515382/))

### The working premise

The shared assumption is that psychological distress can be reached through the body, not only through talking. In the review's words, "development of somatic awareness through sensory (rather than cognitive) resources (such as interoception, proprioception, kinesthesis), lead to reduction of psychological distress through neurophysiological regulation techniques" ([Myers et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515382/)). The authors describe these as "bottom-up" approaches, complementary to the "top-down" modalities that start with thoughts and narratives.

### Evidence status

A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis screened 2,180 references and included 18 randomized controlled trials, reporting medium effects of body psychotherapy on psychopathology and psychological distress ([Rosendahl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8458738/)). The same review is candid about the field's standing: "body psychotherapy (BPT) appears not to be a well-established treatment option" ([Rosendahl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8458738/)). One definition cited there, from Geuter, is that body psychotherapy is "a treatment employing psychological and bodily means equally" ([Rosendahl et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8458738/)).

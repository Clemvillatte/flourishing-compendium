The phrase "instruments for human growth" is the compendium's label for a theme in John Dewey's Democracy and Education: the things around a person, from tools and language to the people they live with, are the means by which growth is directed. The phrase itself does not appear in the book. The nearest wording found in the text is "instrumentalities", used in chapter 3, "Education as Direction". This page therefore reports what Dewey says about growth and about the means of directing it, and does not claim a passage titled with the seed's phrase.

### Growth as the aim

In chapter 4, "Education as Growth", Dewey writes: "Since growth is the characteristic of life, education is all one with growing; it has no end beyond itself." He sets a criterion of value for schooling: "the extent in which it creates a desire for continued growth and supplies means for making the desire effective in fact." Earlier in the chapter he reaches the same conclusion: "life is development, and that developing, growing, is life", and so "the educational process has no end beyond itself; it is its own end" ([Dewey, Democracy and Education, ch. 4, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/852/pg852.txt)).

What makes growth possible is plasticity, which he defines as "essentially the ability to learn from experience; the power to retain from one experience something which is of avail in coping with the difficulties of a later situation" (same source, ch. 4). He separates active habits, which "involve thought, invention, and initiative in applying capacities to new aims", from routine, which "marks an arrest of growth."

### The instruments

In chapter 3, Dewey says the social environment directs the young through "the ways in which persons, with whom the immature being is associated, use things; the instrumentalities with which they accomplish their own ends." He adds that civilisation advances as "a larger number of natural forces and objects have been transformed into instrumentalities of action, into means for securing ends", and that physical things become "social tools", with language playing a large part among them ([Dewey, Democracy and Education, ch. 3](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/852/pg852.txt)). A school, accordingly, needs "tools and physical materials" and not only books.

### What this does not say

Dewey is writing about education and democracy, not about flourishing in the sense of the compendium's essays. Reading his account of growth as an account of personal well-being is the compendium's step. Ryff's six dimensions (ST297) include personal growth, but that comes from Ryff, not Dewey.

### In the compendium

- P018 and P084 are principles on practical wisdom and acting in the situation, which join Dewey's view of principles as methods of inquiry.
- P088 uses Dewey's Human Nature and Conduct on praise and blame as instruments that shape habit. That is a different book from Democracy and Education.
- SP010 (Know yourself as striving) is a Spinoza principle, listed with this concept as a thematic neighbour.
- ST297 is the story on Ryff's six dimensions.

Midlevel terms are the ACT tradition's name for its working concepts: terms like acceptance and defusion that sit between basic behavioral principles and clinical techniques, easy to teach and use, at the cost of some precision. Twohig and Levin state the trade-off directly:

> "In ACT we call these midlevel terms, indicating that the construct is based off a principle, but that users should remember that it is a construct. Midlevel terms are easy to disseminate, but will lack the specificity of the actual principle." (Twohig & Levin, Acceptance and commitment therapy as a treatment for anxiety and depression: A review)

The honesty of the label is its value: it reminds practitioners that "acceptance" or "defusion" is a useful handle, not a basic science entity. The EEMM continues the same discipline at a larger scale, classifying midlevel processes into dimensions and levels so that terms from different traditions can be compared without pretending any of them is bedrock.

Situated learning theory is Jean Lave and Étienne Wenger's account of learning: knowledge is acquired not in the abstract but by taking part in the social situations where it is used. The Wikipedia article on the theory states its scope:

> "Situated learning is a theory that explains an individual's acquisition of professional skills and includes research on apprenticeship into how legitimate peripheral participation leads to membership in a community of practice." ([Wikipedia, Situated learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situated_learning))

### Core claims

The theory's focus, as the article puts it, is that situated learning "takes as its focus the relationship between learning and the social situation in which it occurs" ([same article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situated_learning)). The newcomer begins at the periphery of a community of practice, doing legitimate but simple tasks, and moves toward fuller participation as skill and standing grow.

### Origin

The theory was set out in Lave and Wenger's 1991 book Situated Learning: Legitimate Peripheral Participation (Cambridge University Press), cited in the article's references.

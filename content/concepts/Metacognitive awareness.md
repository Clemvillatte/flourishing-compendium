Metacognitive awareness is the part of metacognition that can be reported and measured: what a person knows about their own thinking, and how they manage it. The best-known measure is Gregory Schraw and Rayne Sperling Dennison's inventory, built "to measure adults' metacognitive awareness", with items "classified into eight subcomponents subsumed under two broader categories, knowledge of cognition and regulation of cognition" ([Assessing Metacognitive Awareness, copy of the 1994 article](https://www.scribd.com/document/346986362/Schraw1994-pdf)). The [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ498462) describes a 52-item inventory and two experiments with 307 undergraduates supporting a two-factor structure.

### Knowledge of cognition

The article defines three kinds. Declarative knowledge is "knowledge about one's skills, intellectual resources, and abilities as a learner". Procedural knowledge is "knowledge about how to implement learning procedures (e.g., strategies)". Conditional knowledge is "knowledge about when and why to use learning procedures".

### Regulation of cognition

Five parts: planning ("planning, goal setting, and allocating resources prior to learning"), information management, monitoring ("assessment of one's learning or strategy use"), debugging ("strategies used to correct comprehension and performance errors"), and evaluation ("analysis of performance and strategy effectiveness after a learning episode").

### What the evidence says

The authors report that the two factors were reliable and intercorrelated (r = .54). They found little evidence that the two make up for each other: in their second experiment the knowledge factor "was related to higher test performance while the regulation of cognition factor was not". A college study found the knowledge factor correlated with GPA and end-of-course grades ([Metacognitive awareness and academic achievement, ERIC PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ854832.pdf)).

Measurement is contested. A validation study with Indian students retained a 40-item model, and its factor analysis produced six factors, not the original eight ([Validation of Metacognitive Awareness Inventory, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8552251/)). The inventory is a self-report, so it records what people report about their thinking. Another paper describes metacognitive awareness as "an off-line measure of metacognitive knowledge" captured before or after the problem-solving activity ([arXiv case study](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06267v1)).

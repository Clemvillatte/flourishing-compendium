The primacy of the ethical is Emmanuel Levinas's reversal of the order of philosophy: before the question of what being is, and before the free subject who might ask it, comes the obligation one person is to another. His own statement of the reversal:

> "Morality is not a branch of philosophy, but first philosophy." (Levinas, Totality and Infinity, 1969 trans., p. 304, quoted in [Jones, 2014](https://www.elgaronline.com/view/journals/lath/2-1/lath.2014.01.03.xml))

### What the reversal rests on

The claim is not an argument from principles but a description of an encounter. What the other person's face does, in Levinas's phenomenology, is command before anything is said: "The naked and defenseless face signifies, with or without words, 'Do not kill me'" (Totality and Infinity, TI 84, in [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/levinas/)). The obligation is asymmetric and prior to choice; Levinas's later work, Otherwise than Being, radicalises it into a subjectivity that is responsibility before it is anything else: "All my inwardness is invested [s'investit] in the form of a despite-me, for-another" (OBBE 11). The compendium principle R093 carries the teaching as a practice: where a project of understanding and a claim of the other person conflict, the claim of the other comes first. A compendium exercise drawn from the concept is to notice, in a difficult exchange, the moment the other's need addresses you before the question of who is right. What the concept does not give: a rule system for cases where two others' claims conflict; Levinas himself pointed to justice and the third party as the required extension, and did not complete it as a procedure.

The pain curve is the meditation tradition's own instrument for working with pain: watching a painful sensation long enough to see it arrive, change, and pass, instead of treating it as a fixed wall. The Mahasi-tradition instruction makes the instrument explicit:

> "Within a set duration of meditation, make up your mind to face and learn from pain. Do not try to change your posture frequently as it would remove an excellent opportunity of learning something from pain. The greatest Pali commentator Venerable Buddhaghosa in his famous work called Visuddhimagga, the Path of Purification, said that pain is covered by a change of postures." (Dhammasami, Mindfulness Meditation Made Easy, ch. 3, [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/article/mindfulness-meditation-made-easy/d/doc1640.html))

### What the curve carries

The method is a bounded, repeated observation: "When pain arises... you should observe that pain, about three or four times and then leave it there. Go back preferably to the primary object, breathing in and out. The pain may get less during this period or it may increase. If it increases, you go back to the pain and observe it again — pain, pain, pain — three or four times, leave it there and come back to the breathing again." The observing is deliberately intermittent so that the pain is seen against the background of the breath, where its curve shows: sensation is not one solid thing but a succession of intensities. The same instruction warns that sharp pain can appear at deeper stages and should then be handled with a teacher, which is the tradition's own safeguard against turning the instrument into endurance for its own sake. My principle X010 carries the general form: treat pain by its duration curve, since a sensation tracked across time is a different object from a sensation endured as permanent. A compendium exercise drawn from the curve is to time-box a discomfort and note its intensity each minute, watching for the change that the fixed view insists is not there. What the curve does not give: a promise that attention removes pain; the instruction only claims it makes pain learnable.

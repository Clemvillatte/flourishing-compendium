The meek is the third Beatitude's word for the person the Sermon on the Mount blesses: "Blessed are the gentle, for they shall inherit the earth" ([Matthew 5:5, World English Bible](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+5%3A5&version=WEB)). The line reworks Psalm 37:11, "the humble shall inherit the land, and shall delight themselves in the abundance of peace" ([WEB](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm+37%3A11&version=WEB)). In English the word carries the older KJV form, "Blessed are the meek: for they shall inherit the earth," which is the phrasing the compendium inherits.

### What the word means

The Greek behind it is praus, the word for a horse trained to the bit or a wind that has died down: strength under control rather than weakness. That sense fits the verse's own logic, since inheriting the earth is a conquest image. The Gospel applies the same quality to Jesus himself: "Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and you will find rest for your souls" ([Matthew 11:29, WEB](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew+11%3A29&version=WEB)). The meekness the Beatitude blesses is therefore not passivity; it is a refusal to seize what you expect to be given.

### Why the compendium keeps it

The concept sits in the wiki because it states a structural claim several traditions reach independently: the yielding posture wins in the long run. The Daoist version is the Daodejing's water model, "there is nothing in the world more soft and weak than water, and yet for attacking things that are firm and strong there is nothing that can take precedence of it" ([DDJ 78, Legge, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)), which Prefer the soft to the hard (D011) develops into a practice. The psychological neighbour is [[Psychological flexibility]], where Kashdan and Rottenberg's review frames adaptability as a fundamental aspect of health ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2998793/)).

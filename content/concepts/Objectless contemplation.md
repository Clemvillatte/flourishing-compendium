In this compendium, objectless contemplation names the prayer described in The Cloud of Unknowing, an anonymous Middle English treatise on contemplative prayer: thoughts about God are set aside, while love remains directed toward God. The treatise's rule is that thought cannot reach God and must be put down, while love can:

> "He may well be loved, but not thought. By love may He be gotten and holden; but by thought never." (The Cloud of Unknowing, chapter 6, [Underhill edition](https://archive.org/details/bookofcontemplat00unde))

### The naked intent

The practice the book prescribes is the stripping of the working mind to one bare direction, and its words for that direction are:

> "it sufficeth enough, a naked intent direct unto God without any other cause than Himself." (The Cloud of Unknowing, [Underhill edition, p. 93](https://archive.org/details/bookofcontemplat00unde))

Every thought, however holy, is to be cast down and covered with a cloud of forgetting, and the contemplative tries to pierce the darkness above with "a sharp dart of longing love" ([chapter 6](https://archive.org/details/bookofcontemplat00unde)). Y078 distinguishes settled concentration with and without an object. K026 prescribes ending a sitting with one minute of open awareness. Both list this entry among their philosophy parallels.

External-locus-of-evaluation research is the work growing out of Carl Rogers' term for where a person's evidence about values comes from. Rogers defined the locus of evaluation as the source of that evidence: internal when the individual is the center of the valuing process, supplied by his own senses; external when it resides in others.

> "When the locus of evaluation resides in others, their judgment as to the value of an object or experience becomes the criterion of value for the individual." (Rogers, [A Theory of Therapy, Personality, and Interpersonal Relationships, 1959](https://dn710806.ca.archive.org/0/items/30-07-24-rapport-sur-la-cybercriminalit-2024-numerique/rogers-1959.pdf), p. 210)

### What the term carries

The external case names a dependency: other people's judgments, not one's own experience, become the measure. Rogers lists among the outcomes of therapy that the client increasingly experiences himself as the locus of evaluation, so the term marks both a problem and a direction of movement. The concept belongs to his person-centred framework, where the valuing process is the object of the theory of therapy.

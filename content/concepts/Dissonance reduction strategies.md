Dissonance reduction strategies are the moves a person makes when two of their own cognitions do not fit: they change one of them, they add new ones, or they change the situation. The account is Festinger's, from A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance (1957). Festinger's starting point is a comparison: "Cognitive dissonance reduction can be seen as an antecedent condition which leads to activity oriented towards dissonance reduction just as hunger leads to activity oriented towards hunger reduction" ([excerpt](https://www.panarchy.org/festinger/dissonance.html)).

> "In general if dissonance exists between two elements, this dissonance can be eliminated by changing one of those elements." (Festinger, [A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance, ch. 1](https://www.panarchy.org/festinger/dissonance.html))

### The three moves

The first move changes the behavior: "the dissonance can, of course, be eliminated by changing the behavioral cognitive element in such a way that it is consonant with the environmental element. The simplest and easiest way in which this may be accomplished is to change the action or feeling which the behavioral element represents" ([Festinger, ch. 1](https://www.panarchy.org/festinger/dissonance.html)). His examples are ordinary: "There are many persons who do stop smoking if and when they discover it is bad for their health." The second move changes the environment instead: "Just as it is possible to change a behavioral cognitive element by changing the behavior which this element mirrors, it is sometimes possible to change an environmental cognitive element by changing the situation to which that element corresponds."

The third move is the one that makes the theory uncomfortable. "But even if it is impossible to eliminate a dissonance, it is possible to reduce the total magnitude of dissonance by adding new cognitive elements" (same text). And the theory predicts avoidance as well as repair: "When dissonance is present, in addition to trying to reduce it, the person will actively avoid situations and information which would likely increase the dissonance" (same text).

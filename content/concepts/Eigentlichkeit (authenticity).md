Eigentlichkeit is Heidegger's word for ownedness in Being and Time — the mode of existence in which one takes up one's own life instead of drifting in the average ways of "the They". The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes the coinage itself carries the idea:

> "The word we translate as 'authenticity' is actually a neologism invented by Heidegger, the word *Eigentlichkeit*, which comes from an ordinary term, *eigentlich*, meaning 'really' or 'truly', but is built on the stem *eigen*, meaning 'own' or 'proper'." ([SEP, Authenticity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/authenticity/))

For Heidegger, "all possibilities of concrete understanding and action are made possible by a background of shared practices opened up by the social context in which we find ourselves, by what he calls the 'They' (*das Man*)". What breaks the drift is the encounter with one's "ownmost" possibility — "the possibility of death as the possible loss of all possibilities". Faced with that, authenticity becomes "standing up for and standing behind what one does—as *owning* and *owning up to* one's deeds as an agent in the world".

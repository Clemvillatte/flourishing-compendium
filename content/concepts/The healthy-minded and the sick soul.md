“The healthy-minded and the sick soul” — James, The Varieties of Religious Experience.

### What it is

My title for the pair of temperaments William James sets against each other in Lectures IV-VII of The Varieties of Religious Experience. The healthy-minded temperament is "the temperament which has a constitutional incapacity for prolonged suffering" (James, Lectures VI-VII, [Gutenberg #621](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt)). Its counterpart, the sick soul, is treated across those two lectures as the other way a human being can be built.

### What it carries

James frames the pair through a quotation from Francis W. Newman: "God has two families of children on this earth" (quoted in James, Lectures IV-V, [Gutenberg #621](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt)), naming "the once-born" and "the twice-born". The happiness motive James starts from is general, not the property of one temperament: "How to gain, how to keep, how to recover happiness, is in fact for most men at all times the secret motive of all they do, and of all they are willing to endure" (James, Lecture IV, [Gutenberg #621](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt)). The lectures that follow work out "the contrast between the two ways of looking at life which are characteristic respectively of what we called the healthy-minded, who need to be born only once, and of the sick souls, who must be twice-born in order to be happy. The result is two different conceptions of the universe of our experience" (James, Lecture VIII, [Gutenberg #621](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt)). In the religion of the once-born, "Happiness and religious peace consist in living on the plus side of the account"; for the twice-born, "Peace cannot be reached by the simple addition of pluses and elimination of minuses from life" (James, Lecture VIII, [Gutenberg #621](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/621/pg621.txt)).

I connect this with P103, which accepts restlessness as the human condition: James's two temperaments give that acceptance a documented shape. A reader of James may notice, optionally, whether their own experience is being offered as evidence about everyone's.

Habit formation is the process by which repeated actions become automatic. In the model of Wendy Wood and David Neal, habits are learned dispositions to repeat past responses, triggered by the features of the context that have covaried with past performance; contexts activate the response directly, without a mediating goal. Formation, on this account, is slow association learning: repetition in an unvarying context.

> "Habits are learned dispositions to repeat past responses. They are triggered by features of the context that have covaried frequently with past performance, including performance locations, preceding actions in a sequence, and particular people. Contexts activate habitual responses directly, without the mediation of goal states." (Wood and Neal 2007, [Psychological Review](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/wp-content/uploads/sites/183/2023/10/wood.neal_.2007psychrev_a_new_look_at_habits_and_the_interface_between_habits_and_goals.pdf))

### The 66-day median

The most-cited timeline comes from Phillippa Lally's 2010 study. Ninety-six volunteers performed a self-chosen daily behaviour in the same context for 12 weeks, and automaticity followed an asymptotic curve: the largest gains came early, with diminishing returns after. The median time to reach 95 percent of the automaticity plateau was 66 days, with a range from 18 to 254 days. Two practical findings travel with it. Performing the behaviour more consistently was associated with a better fit, and missing a single opportunity did not materially affect the process. The popular 21-day figure traces to self-help programmes rather than research; reviewers note that it is generally agreed among researchers that habit formation is a slower process.

### What makes it stick

Because the association is with context rather than goals, habit formation works best where the cue is stable: a fixed time, place or preceding action. The Wood-Neal model's constraints run both ways. Habit associations accrue slowly and do not shift appreciably with current goal states or infrequent counterhabitual responses, so an intention by itself has limited capacity to break or build a habit. The cues do the work.

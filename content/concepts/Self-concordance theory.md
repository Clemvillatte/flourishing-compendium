Self-concordance theory proposes that people do better when their goals fit their developing interests and core values, and that this fit works in two ways: it sustains effort toward the goal, and it makes attaining the goal more rewarding. Kennon Sheldon and Andrew Elliot set out the model in 1999; Sheldon and colleagues tested it across cultures in 2004.

### The model

Sheldon and Elliot define self-concordance as the consistency of goals "with the person's developing interests and core values". Their abstract gives it a dual role.

> "First, those pursuing self-concordant goals put more sustained effort into achieving those goals and thus are more likely to attain them. Second, those who attain self-concordant goals reap greater well-being benefits from their attainment." (Sheldon & Elliot 1999, [PubMed 10101878](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101878/))

The link from attainment to well-being is said to run through need satisfaction, meaning daily experiences of autonomy, competence and relatedness that build up during the period of striving. The model fitted three longitudinal data sets and held independently of self-efficacy, implementation intentions, avoidance framing and life skills.

### Across cultures

The 2004 study asked whether this holds where societies stress duty and group norms. In samples from the United States, China, South Korea and Taiwan, "self-concordance predicted SWB within every culture". The authors suggest that "owning one's actions" may matter for most if not all humans. ([Sheldon et al. 2004](https://scholarship.miami.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Self-Concordance-and-Subjective-Well-Being-in-Four/991031599879802976))

### Limits worth stating

Both sources are abstracts, so the effect sizes and methods are not covered here. The 2004 samples are cultural groups defined by country, not a sweep of all societies, and its authors hedge with "may be important for most if not all humans".

## Other formulations in this library

- goals pursued for autonomous, identity-congruent reasons predict attainment and well-being (Sheldon and Elliot, 1999)
- goals aligned with values are pursued more effectively and predict well-being (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999)
- goals aligned with one's authentic nature predict well-being (Sheldon and Elliot, 1999)
- self-concordant goals predict attainment and well-being (Sheldon & Elliot, 1999; Sheldon et al., 2004)

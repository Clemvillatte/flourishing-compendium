“Reputation as indifferent” — Epictetus, Enchiridion.

The title states the Stoic classification: reputation is an indifferent, a thing outside one's control and outside the sphere of the good. Epictetus opens the Enchiridion with the sorting:

> "Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions." (Epictetus, [Enchiridion, ch. 1, trans. George Long, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html))

### The working instruction

The sorting has a practical edge, stated late in the same manual: "Don't regard what anyone says of you, for this, after all, is no concern of yours" (ch. 50). Since reputation belongs to others' opinions, treating it as a good makes one's peace hostage to what was never in one's power.

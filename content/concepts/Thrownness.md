Thrownness (Geworfenheit) is Heidegger's name for the way human existence always starts in the middle. We wake up already embedded in a family, a language, a historical moment, a body with its moods: "As Dasein, I ineluctably find myself in a world that matters to me in some way or another. This is what Heidegger calls thrownness (Geworfenheit), a having-been-thrown into the world" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Martin Heidegger, fall 2017 edition](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2017/entries/heidegger/)).

### The structure, in Heidegger's own words

Being and Time presents thrownness as a structure of existing, not a biography of bad luck:

> "As an entity which has been delivered over to its Being, it remains also delivered over to the fact that it must always have found itself-but found itself in a way of finding which arises not so much from a direct seeking as rather from a fleeing" (Heidegger, Being and Time, Macquarrie and Robinson trans., p. 174, [beyng.com text](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.174.html))

The two halves matter. First, delivered over: no one audits the terms of their own arrival. Second, the finding is more like fleeing than seeking: we usually discover our situation by way of discomfort, in the mode of turning towards or away, "not one in which we look at thrownness" ([Heidegger, Being and Time, p. 174](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.174.html)). Moods disclose thrownness, which is why Heidegger treats affect as a basic way of knowing where and how one is.

### Determination and freedom

The concept is Heidegger's counterweight to self-creation from zero. The Stanford Encyclopedia puts the constraint plainly: "Besides death, another fundamental source of finitude in human existence is our 'thrownness' into factical determinacy. Heidegger argues that one is 'never in control of one's own being from the ground up' (SZ 284)" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Martin Heidegger](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)). Later existentialism kept the structure and radicalized the freedom: "our essence is not given in advance; we are contingently thrown into existence and are burdened with the task of creating ourselves through our choices and actions" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Existentialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/existentialism/)). Existence, on that reading, is "a reflexive or relational tension between 'facticity' and 'transcendence,' where we are constrained by our facticity but simultaneously endowed with the freedom to exceed or transcend it" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Existentialism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/existentialism/)).

In Heidegger's own scheme the two poles belong together: "Dasein confronts every concrete situation in which it finds itself (into which it has been thrown) as a range of possibilities for acting (onto which it may project itself)" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Martin Heidegger, fall 2017 edition](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2017/entries/heidegger/)). The thrown is exactly what offers possibilities to project onto. Temporally, the match holds: thrownness is Dasein's way of carrying its past, projection its way of holding its future ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Martin Heidegger, fall 2017 edition](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2017/entries/heidegger/)).

### Why it matters for flourishing

The distinction here is between unchosen starting conditions and responsibility for how we inhabit them. Thrownness is not grounds for self-blame about the start, nor an excuse from what follows; Heidegger keeps the existential guilt and asks us to own it, not dissolve it. My principles [[E022 — Practise thrownness (Geworfenheit)]] and [[R106 — Practise situated freedom]] work the two halves. Heidegger's formulation of the payoff is practical: "I become myself by first owning the fact that I can escape neither from my thrownness, nor from the lack of justification for my existence that comes with that thrownness" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Martin Heidegger](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)). Ownership, not escape, is the move.

“Being-values (B-values)” — Maslow, Toward a Psychology of Being.

Maslow's B-values are the values he identified in studying accounts of peak experiences, set against the deficiency-needs that organised his better-known hierarchy. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy does not carry a dedicated entry for them; the fullest secondary summary is in the encyclopaedia article on Maslow:

> "In studying accounts of peak experiences, Maslow identified a manner of thought he called 'being-cognition' (or 'B-cognition'), which is holistic and accepting, as opposed to the evaluative 'deficiency-cognition' (or 'D-cognition'), and values he called 'Being-values'." ([Wikipedia, Abraham Maslow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Maslow))

### The list

The same article gives Maslow's list of B-values with their associated terms, starting with Truth (honesty; reality; simplicity; richness; oughtness; beauty; pure, clean and unadulterated; completeness; essentiality), Goodness, Beauty, Wholeness, Aliveness, Uniqueness, Perfection, Completion, Justice, Simplicity, Richness, Effortlessness, Playfulness and Self-sufficiency.

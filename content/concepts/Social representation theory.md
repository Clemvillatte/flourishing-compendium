Social representation theory, first formulated by Serge Moscovici, is an account of how a community builds the shared common sense it thinks and communicates with ([Höijer, 2011](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/bitstreams/a39614be-a7ca-47fe-b7ac-52c72b55afca/download)). Moscovici coined the term in 1961, in his study of how psychoanalysis was received in different French publics ([Wikipedia, Social representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_representation)). The theory's object:

> "A social representation is a system of values, ideas and practices with a twofold function: first, to establish an order which will enable individuals to orientate themselves in their material and social world and to master it; and secondly to enable communication to take place among members of a community by providing them with a code for social exchange and a code for naming and classifying unambiguously the various aspects of their world and their individual group history." (Moscovici, 1973, p. xiii, quoted in [Höijer, 2011](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/bitstreams/a39614be-a7ca-47fe-b7ac-52c72b55afca/download))

### Anchoring and objectification

The theory posits two mechanisms that generate representations. In Birgitta Höijer's summary:

> "The first mechanism, anchoring, makes the unknown known by bringing it into a well-known sphere of earlier social representations so that we may compare and interpret it. The second mechanism, objectifying, makes the unknown known by transforming it into something concrete we may perceive and touch and thus control." ([Höijer, 2011](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/bitstreams/a39614be-a7ca-47fe-b7ac-52c72b55afca/download))

Anchoring has named subforms: naming, emotional anchoring, thematic anchoring, metaphoric anchoring and anchoring via basic antinomies ([Höijer, 2011](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/bitstreams/a39614be-a7ca-47fe-b7ac-52c72b55afca/download)). Objectification turns an abstract idea into an image. Moscovici's own line: "To objectify is to discover the iconic quality of an imprecise idea or being, to reproduce a concept in an image" (Moscovici, 1984, p. 38, quoted in [Höijer, 2011](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/bitstreams/a39614be-a7ca-47fe-b7ac-52c72b55afca/download)).

### What the theory explains

Moscovici distinguished the reified universe of science, which produces knowledge by rule and procedure, from the consensual universe in which lay publics elaborate and circulate their own versions of what they receive ([Wikipedia, Social representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_representation)). His founding study traced how three segments of 1950s France — the urban-liberal, Catholic and communist milieus — each took up psychoanalytic ideas differently ([Wikipedia, Social representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_representation)).

Consider a new word such as "artificial intelligence" entering ordinary talk. On the theory's account, a community anchors it to what it already knows and objectifies it into fixed images. That is an illustrative extension of the mechanisms, not a documented study.

### Status

The theory remains popular mainly among European social psychologists, and is less known in the United States, partly because much of Moscovici's original work was published in French ([Wikipedia, Social representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_representation)). It is far from a settled doctrine; it attracts ongoing debate ([Wikipedia, Social representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_representation)).

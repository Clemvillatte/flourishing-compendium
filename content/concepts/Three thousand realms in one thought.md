Three thousand realms in one thought (yi nian san qian) is the Tiantai school's contemplation method, expounded by Zhiyi (538-597): the mind's single thought-moment inherently contains the three thousand worlds. The dictionary entry traces the doctrine to the Lotus Sutra and to Zhiyi, founder of the Tiantai school. ([Wisdom Library, "Yi nian san qian"](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/yi-nian-san-qian))

> "Indeed, a single mind possesses the Ten Dharma Realms; one Dharma Realm, in turn, possesses ten Dharma Realms, making one hundred Dharma Realms; each realm possesses thirty types of worlds; thus, one hundred Dharma Realms possess three thousand types of worlds. These three thousand exist within a single thought. If there is no mind, then so be it; but if even a momentary thought arises, it inherently possesses the three thousand." (Mohe Zhiguan, vol. 5, quoted in [A Dictionary of Chinese Buddhist Terms via Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/yi-nian-san-qian))

The entry glosses the arithmetic behind the number: the Ten Dharma Realms each mutually possess the other ten, making one hundred realms; each carries the Ten Suchnesses, making one thousand; each exists in the three worlds (sentient beings, lands, the five skandhas), making three thousand. A follow-up passage in the same volume adds that the containment is not created by action: all natures are inherently so, like a single dust mote containing the ten directions. ([Wisdom Library, "Yi nian san qian"](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/yi-nian-san-qian))

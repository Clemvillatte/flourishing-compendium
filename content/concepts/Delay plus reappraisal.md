Delay plus reappraisal is the compendium's label for a two-step emotion regulation sequence: put time between the provocation and the response, then change the reading of what happened. In James Gross's extended process model, reappraisal belongs to the strategy family of cognitive change, and the model's implementation stage is where general strategies become situation-specific tactics.

> "The task of the implementation stage is to translate this general strategy (e.g., cognitive change) into tactics that are appropriate to the specific situation one is in (e.g., think about this particular rebuff as an accidental oversight rather than a deliberate insult; McRae, Ciesielski, & Gross, 2012)." (Gross, 2015, p. 15, quoted in Isaacowitz and Wolfe, [Emotion Regulation Tactics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423774/), Emotion Review, 2024)

### How the two steps divide the work

The five strategy families in the model are situation selection, situation modification, attention deployment, cognitive change and response modulation. Reappraisal is cognitive change; the delay is the tactic that keeps the response from firing before the new reading is in place. The compendium pairs the sequence with its Stoic material, where the same two steps appear as withholding assent and then re-examining the judgement.

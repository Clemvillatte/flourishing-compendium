Desire (orexis / taṇhā / conatus) — the directed movement of being toward what it lacks or seeks. The traditions divide on whether to purify it (Buddhism), order it (Aristotle, Stoicism), or understand it as essence itself (Spinoza). Contemporary philosophy of mind treats it as a basic state to be analyzed rather than judged.

> "To desire is to be in a particular state of mind." (Tim Schroeder, [Desire, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/desire/))

### What kind of state

The Stanford Encyclopedia maps the main theoretical options: on the most widely held, action-based theory, "for an organism to desire p is for the organism to be disposed to act so as to bring about p"; pleasure-based, good-based and reward-based theories offer alternatives. The traditions named in the seed are not rival theories of this state but rival verdicts on it: the Buddhist analysis of taṇhā as the engine of suffering, the Greek ordering of orexis toward the right objects in the right measure, Spinoza's conatus as the striving by which each thing tries to persist in its being.

Nutritional psychiatry is the field that treats diet and nutrition as targets for the prevention and treatment of mental disorders. The original entry's source is the founding consensus statement of the International Society for Nutritional Psychiatry Research, led by Jerome Sarris:

> "Diet and nutrition offer key modifiable targets for the prevention of mental disorders and have a fundamental role in the promotion of mental health." (Sarris et al., 2015, [World Psychiatry, PMC4592666](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4592666/))

### The evidence base

The statement's case rests on the association it describes as robust: "Robust associations have been established between nutritional quality and mental health, with the bulk of this evidence indicating a protective effect of healthy diets on depressed mood", together with newer research "supporting a detrimental impact of unhealthy diets on the mental health of young people and adults". Its summary sentence draws the field's conclusion: "nutrition and nutraceuticals should now be considered as mainstream elements of psychiatric practice".

The field's premise is stated plainly in its opening: present treatment "is achieving sub-optimal outcomes; in addition little attention is given to preventative efforts", so the search turned to modifiable targets — and diet is one.

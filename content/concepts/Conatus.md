Conatus is Spinoza's term for the innate striving by which "each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being" (*Ethics* E3p6) — a striving so central that he identifies it as the thing's actual essence (E3p7) ([SEP Spinoza's Psychological Theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2022/entries/spinoza-psychological/)). In human beings it appears as desire, and increases or decreases in power of acting are experienced as joy and sadness ([IEP Spinoza](https://iep.utm.edu/spinoz-m/)). Cross-tradition: resonates with Nietzsche's [[Will to power]] and Bergson's élan vital; contrasted with the Buddhist analysis of [[Tanha|craving]] and the Daoist art of not forcing ([[Wu wei]]).

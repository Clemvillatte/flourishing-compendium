The page's title is the compendium's label for a mechanism Dewey describes: a habit that runs smoothly gives no occasion for thought, and only when something blocks it does attention and inquiry begin. His example is everyday: the hand, guided by the eye, reaches for a surface, and an unexpected quality of touch, "a voluptuous smoothness or annoying heat", forces a readjustment ([Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, Part Three, I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/41386/41386-h/41386-h.htm)).

### The hitch that provokes thought

> "Only a hitch in its workings occasions emotion and provokes thought." — Dewey, [Human Nature and Conduct, Part Three, I, p. 178](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/41386/41386-h/41386-h.htm)

In Dewey's account, a novel factor in the surroundings releases an impulse that points somewhere incompatible with the old habit, and at these moments of shifting activity "conscious feeling and thought arise and are accentuated". During the redistribution, old habit supplies the content of the search and impulse its direction: "With conflict of habits and release of impulse there is conscious search" ([Dewey, Part Three, I, pp. 179-180](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/41386/41386-h/41386-h.htm)). The obstruction is not yet a benefit; it is the occasion on which intelligence has something to work on. Dewey also describes disturbance severe enough to break organized activity apart, which cautions against reading every hardship as useful.

### In the compendium

- [[P085 — Let obstruction do its work]] — turns this mechanism into advice: when a usual approach stops working, examine the obstruction before deciding whether to learn around it, change the conditions, or leave.

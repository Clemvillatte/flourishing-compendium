Leadership research is the social-scientific study of leading: what leaders do, what effects they have, and under what conditions. The field's reviews map it by theory families rather than by a single definition. A recent Annual Review of Psychology survey by Bruce Avolio (the seed source's author), Fred Walumbwa and Todd Weber describes the scope:

> "We begin by examining authentic leadership and its development, followed by work that takes a cognitive science approach. We then examine new-genre leadership theories, complexity leadership, and leadership that is shared, collective, or distributed." ([Avolio, Walumbwa and Weber 2009](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.psych.60.110707.163621))

### What the field agrees and argues about

The review also covers "the role of relationships through our review of leader member exchange and the emerging work on followership", plus "substitutes for leadership, servant leadership, spirituality and leadership, cross-cultural leadership, and e-leadership" ([same review](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.psych.60.110707.163621)). Each section of the review ends with open issues, so the field's standing shape is many parallel theory families rather than one settled account.

Beauvoir's title for her existentialist ethics: freedom is ambiguous because it must will both itself and the freedom of others. To will one without the other is bad faith ([Wikipedia Simone de Beauvoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_de_Beauvoir)).

### What it is

The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947) is Simone de Beauvoir's attempt to draw an ethics out of existentialism. Its starting point is the double nature of a human being: "'Rational animal,' 'thinking reed,' he escapes from his natural condition without, however, freeing himself from it" (Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I, trans. Frechtman, [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)). We are both consciousness and thing, freedom and fact. Her programmatic sentence gives the book its stance: "Since we do not succeed in fleeing it, let us therefore try to look the truth in the face. Let us try to assume our fundamental ambiguity. It is in the knowledge of the genuine conditions of our life that we must draw our strength to live and our reason for acting" (Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I, [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)).

### Core claims

The ambiguity is not solved but lived, and the failure to live it takes recognizable forms. Escape is one: "To avoid the anguish of this permanent choice, one may attempt to flee into the object itself, to engulf one's own presence in it. In the servitude of the serious, the original spontaneity strives to deny itself" (Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I, [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)).

The other half is the social one. Freedom is not private property: "freedom always appears as a movement of liberation. It is only by prolonging itself through the freedom of others that it manages to surpass death itself and to realize itself as an indefinite unity" (Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part I, [marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch01.htm)). A freedom that wills only itself, treating other people as scenery, cuts the line it hangs from.

My E070 principle practises this as a check on self-demands: the parent who insists on being either devoted or independent is demanding that the ambiguity resolve, and the suffering spikes with the demand. Naming the both-ness is the ethical act Beauvoir recommends.

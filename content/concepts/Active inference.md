**Active inference** — Friston — organisms act to minimise the discrepancy between predicted and sensed states; perception and action serve the same prediction.

## Field

Biophysiological · Psychological science

### The single imperative

The theory rests on one quantity. Variational free energy is "an upper bound on surprise, where surprise (the negative log probability of sensory samples) corresponds to negative Bayesian log evidence. This means that everything we infer (and do) is in the service of minimizing surprise or maximizing the evidence for our internal or generative models of the world" ([Parr and Friston, active inference review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6191887/)). Friston's own compact statement of the principle: "any self-organizing system that is at equilibrium with its environment must minimize its free energy" ([Friston, "The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?" Nature Reviews Neuroscience 2010, PDF](https://www.uab.edu/medicine/cinl/images/KFriston_FreeEnergy_BrainTheory.pdf)).

### Where action enters

What makes it active inference rather than just predictive coding is that prediction error can be reduced from both ends. Friston's rough-guide formulation: "action can reduce free-energy (i.e. prediction errors) by changing sensory input, whereas perception reduces free-energy by changing predictions. [...] In short, we sample the world to ensure our predictions become a self-fulfilling prophecy and surprises are avoided" ([Friston, "The free-energy principle: a rough guide to the brain?"](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/The%20free-energy%20principle%20-%20a%20rough%20guide%20to%20the%20brain.pdf)). The 2010 paper gives the felt example: "an intuitive example of this process (when it is raised into consciousness) would be feeling our way in darkness: we anticipate what we might touch next and then try to confirm those expectations" ([Friston 2010, PDF](https://www.uab.edu/medicine/cinl/images/KFriston_FreeEnergy_BrainTheory.pdf)).

My principle on predictive cognition holds the practical consequence: the organism is not a passive receiver of sensory data but an active sampler of it, and much of what looks like motivation can be recast as expected-free-energy minimisation ([Parr and Friston, generalised free energy paper, UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10082773/1/Parr-Friston2019_Article_GeneralisedFreeEnergyAndActive.pdf)).

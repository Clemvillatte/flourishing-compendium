Socioanalytic theory is Robert Hogan's account of personality as a social strategy. It explains individual differences by what people are trying to get from the groups they live in. The theory states:

> "people have innate biological needs for (1) acceptance and approval; (2) status, power, and control of resources; and (3) predictability and order. These needs translate into behaviors for getting along with others, getting ahead in terms of status, and making sense of the world." ([Hogan Assessments, Socioanalytic Theory](http://www.hoganassessments.com/sites/default/files/Socioanalytic%20Theory%20(2).pdf))

### Identity and reputation

The theory's signature move is to define personality from two viewpoints at once:

> "Personality from the person's view is his/her identity. It is defined in terms of the strategies one uses to satisfy the needs for acceptance and status. Personality from an observer's point of view is a person's reputation." ([Hogan Assessments, Socioanalytic Theory](http://www.hoganassessments.com/sites/default/files/Socioanalytic%20Theory%20(2).pdf))

Reputation describes a person's behaviour; identity explains it. A review of the theory puts the pair plainly: "Identity is how an actor sees themselves and believes that others will perceive them; while reputation, is essentially how others evaluate a person's behavior, summing up both collective observations" ([Gottlieb, Furnham and Klewe, 2021](https://www.scirp.org/pdf/psych_2021122114111230.pdf)).

### What the theory explains

The publisher's summary reports Hogan's agenda: "the agenda for personality theory is to explain individual differences among people in their ability to get along and get ahead" ([Hogan Assessments, Socioanalytic Theory](http://www.hoganassessments.com/sites/default/files/Socioanalytic%20Theory%20(2).pdf)). The theory's three master motives are summarized in the review as: "Homo Sapiens want to get along (and fear loneliness), get ahead (losing resources, status and control are depressive), and find meaning (chaos and unpredictability are anxiety provoking)" ([Gottlieb, Furnham and Klewe, 2021](https://www.scirp.org/pdf/psych_2021122114111230.pdf)). What connects identity to reputation is social skill: role-taking is "what translates motivation into accomplishment, and identity into successful reputation" ([same review](https://www.scirp.org/pdf/psych_2021122114111230.pdf)).

An everyday-life case (imagined): a manager describes herself as direct and fair-minded. Her team, who experience the directness as sharp, describe her as intimidating. The first description is her identity; the second is her reputation.

### Origin

Hogan first set out the theory in his 1983 Nebraska Symposium chapter, "A Socioanalytic Theory of Personality." The review describes the theory as "an integration of evolutionary psychology, depth psychology and role theory" ([Gottlieb, Furnham and Klewe, 2021](https://www.scirp.org/pdf/psych_2021122114111230.pdf)).

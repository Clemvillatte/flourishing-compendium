The monkey mind is the Buddhist image for the restless, grasping quality of consciousness: what is called mind, intellect, or consciousness arises as one thing and ceases as another, like a monkey swinging through a forest and grabbing one branch after another. The simile appears in the Saṁyutta Nikāya, in a pair of adjacent discourses (SN 12:61 and SN 12:62). ([SN 12:62, dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_62.html))

> "But what’s called ‘mind,’ ‘intellect,’ or ‘consciousness’ by day and by night arises as one thing and ceases as another. Just as a monkey, swinging through a forest wilderness, grabs a branch. Letting go of that, it grabs another branch. Letting go of that, it grabs another one. Letting go of that, it grabs another one. In the same way, what’s called ‘mind,’ ‘intellect,’ or ‘consciousness’ by day and by night arises as one thing and ceases as another." (SN 12:62, [dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_62.html))

In the discourse the simile is given so that the instructed disciple stops relishing consciousness as "this is me, this is my self" and attends instead to dependent co-arising. ([SN 12:62, dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_62.html))

In Gadamer's philosophical hermeneutics, historical consciousness names two different things, and the difference matters for how the essays use the term.

### Historically effected consciousness

Stanford's entry on Gadamer puts the positive sense this way: "Understanding, for Gadamer, is thus always an 'effect' of history, while hermeneutical 'consciousness' is itself that mode of being that is conscious of its own historical 'being effected'—it is 'historically-effected consciousness' (wirkungsgeschichtliches Bewußtsein)." It adds that "awareness of the historically effected character of understanding is, according to Gadamer, identical with an awareness of the hermeneutical situation", which he also calls a "horizon", so that understanding "always occurs from within a particular 'horizon' that is determined by our historically-determined situatedness" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Gadamer](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gadamer/)).

### The historicist sense Gadamer resists

The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy reads Gadamer as arguing against "historicism's objectifying tendencies": "historical situatedness does not result in restrictive limitations. For, limits are precisely what allow one to be open to what is new." On its reading, the "historical consciousness, which Gadamer seeks to undermine" defends a picture of past and present as two separate, "reified" horizons ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Gadamer](https://iep.utm.edu/gadamer/)). Gadamer's own words, as quoted there, are that noting the difference of distant worlds is "only one phase in the process of understanding", and that we must not "solidify" the "self-alienation of past consciousness".

### What follows for understanding

Three consequences are drawn in these two entries:

- Prejudice. Gadamer rehabilitates the old meaning of Vorurteil as "a pre-judgment", arguing that "rather than closing us off, our prejudices are themselves what open us up to what is to be understood". The Internet Encyclopedia notes that his target is the Enlightenment's "prejudice against prejudice", and that "prejudice-free knowledge is neither desirable nor possible".
- Tradition. The Internet Encyclopedia adds that Gadamer "is not espousing a conservative approach to tradition that blindly affirms the whole of a tradition". Tradition "is less a conserving force than a provocative one". Stanford records that Habermas read Gadamer's view of authority and tradition as ideological conservatism, while "Gadamer himself viewed it as merely providing a proper corrective to the over-reaction against these ideas that occurred with the Enlightenment".
- Fusion of horizons. Understanding involves "the formation of a new context of meaning" in which "neither remains unaffected" (Stanford). The Internet Encyclopedia quotes Gadamer: "horizon is. . . something into which we move and that moves with us".

### Use in the essays

One reading for the essays, offered as an interpretation and not a claim from the sources: knowing that your understanding is shaped by history is a step toward understanding better, since it gives you the chance to question inherited assumptions instead of treating them as invisible. On this account the process has no final point. The Internet Encyclopedia says any unity reached "will never be total: understanding refers to a process not a final end."

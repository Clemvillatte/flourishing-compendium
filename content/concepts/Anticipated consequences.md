Anticipated consequences are what Dewey's account of deliberation works on. His definition: "deliberation is a dramatic rehearsal (in imagination) of various competing possible lines of action" ([Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, Part 3, §3, Brock University text](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey_1922_17.html)). The rehearsal is the point:

> "Thought runs ahead and foresees outcomes, and thereby avoids having to await the instruction of actual failure and disaster. An act overtly tried out is irrevocable, its consequences cannot be blotted out. An act tried out in imagination is not final or fatal. It is retrievable."

([Dewey 1922, Part 3, §3, Brock text](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey_1922_17.html).)

### What the foresight is for

Dewey is careful to separate his position from a prediction model: "After all, the object of foresight of consequences is not to predict the future. It is to ascertain the meaning of present activities and to secure, so far as possible, a present activity with a unified meaning" ([Dewey 1922, Part 3, §4, Brock text](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey_1922_18.html)). The imagination does the work: "Each conflicting habit and impulse takes its turn in projecting itself upon the screen of imagination. It unrolls a picture of its future history, of the career it would have if it were given head" ([Part 3, §3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey_1922_17.html)). And the ends are not termini but pivots: "Ends are foreseen consequences which arise in the course of activity and which are employed to give activity added meaning and to direct its further course. They are in no sense ends *of* action. In being ends of *deliberation* they are redirecting pivots *in* action" ([Dewey 1922, Part 3, §6, Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/john-dewey/human-nature-and-conduct/text/chapter-3-6)).

### What makes the deliberation good or bad

Dewey gives the failure condition as well: "Deliberation is irrational in the degree in which an end is so fixed, a passion or interest so absorbing, that the foresight of consequences is warped to include only what furthers execution of its predetermined bias" ([Part 3, §3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey_1922_17.html)). The value of the anticipation is not accuracy of the forecast, which Dewey calls contingent, but what the rehearsal reveals about the present act: "Imaginative forethought of the probable consequences of a proposed act keeps that act from sinking below consciousness into routine habit or whimsical brutality. It preserves the meaning of that act alive" ([Part 3, §4](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Dewey/Dewey_1922/Dewey_1922_18.html)).

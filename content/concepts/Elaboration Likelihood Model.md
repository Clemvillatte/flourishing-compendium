The Elaboration Likelihood Model, developed by Richard Petty and John Cacioppo, splits persuasion into two routes depending on how much thinking the receiver is willing or able to do. A recent neurocognitive study states the split in one sentence:

> "These models posit that source credibility serves as a peripheral cue or heuristic when cognitive elaboration is low, or as a factor that enhances message scrutiny when elaboration is high." (Monahhova et al., [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13630730/))

On the central route, the person works through the argument itself — its quality, meaning, and explanation. On the peripheral route, they lean on surface cues: title style, visual attractiveness, platform labels, creator identity, the speaker's credentials. The same cue can serve either route: credibility works as a shortcut when elaboration is low and as a reason to take the message seriously when elaboration is high.

The model's practical warning is that the two routes are not interchangeable. Persuasion won through the central route, by the receiver's own thinking, is the model's durable kind; persuasion won through cues is borrowed from the cue and fragile.

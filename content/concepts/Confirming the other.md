“Confirming the other” — Buber, I and Thou.

Confirming the other is Buber's term for what the other person does in a dialogic relation. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Buber summarises the claim:

> "For without the form of otherness there can be no confirmation of self insofar as the confirmation of the I is always mediated by the other who confirms me, both at a distance and in relation, or rather in the distance that is relation and the relation that is difference." (Zank & Braiterman, [SEP: Martin Buber](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/))

### Distance and relation

The same entry gives the setting the confirmation depends on:

> "As developed in the essay 'Distance and Relation' (1951), relation cannot take shape apart from or without the prior setting of things, persons, and spiritual beings at a distance." (Zank & Braiterman, [SEP: Martin Buber](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/))

> "For Buber, this setting of things, persons, and beings at a distance is the only way to secure the form of otherness without which there can be no relation." (Zank & Braiterman, [SEP: Martin Buber](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/))

Temporal motivation theory (TMT) is an integrative model proposed by Piers Steel and Cornelius König to explain why people act now on some goals and put off others. It is best known as a model of procrastination. The seed line is accurate as far as it goes, but it leaves out three other inputs: expectancy, value and impulsiveness. The sources below are abstracts and search excerpts; the 2006 paper itself was not read in full.

### What the 2006 paper proposes

> "Focusing on the fundamental features of picoeconomics, expectancy theory, cumulative prospect theory, and need theory, we construct a temporal motivational theory (TMT)." (Steel & König 2006, Academy of Management Review 31(4):889-913, [abstract](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amr.2006.22527462))

A public-service-motivation preprint quoting the paper ([EasyChair](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/1fLm/open)) gives the core claim: "TMT indicates that motivation can be understood by the effects of expectancy and value, weakened by delay, with differences for rewards and losses" (p. 897). The theory combines expectancy, value, delay and impulsiveness (sensitivity to delay). In the formula as written in recent papers, the denominator is one plus impulsiveness times delay, not impulsiveness times delay alone: Motivation = Expectancy × Value / (1 + Impulsiveness × Delay). The constant avoids a division by zero when the delay is zero. ([ADHD and procrastination study, PMC 13033046](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13033046/)) The compendium's "other formulation" line omits the constant, and a reader should use the form with the 1.

### Evidence from the procrastination meta-analysis

Steel (2007) reviewed procrastination in a meta-analysis based on 691 correlations. Strong and consistent predictors were task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy and impulsiveness, along with conscientiousness and facets such as self-control, distractibility, organisation and achievement motivation. In contrast, neuroticism, rebelliousness and sensation seeking showed only a weak connection. The author states that these effects "prove consistent with temporal motivation theory, an integrative hybrid of expectancy theory and hyperbolic discounting". ([Steel 2007, Psychological Bulletin 133:65-94, PubMed 17201571](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201571/)) Consistency of results with a theory does not isolate its separate parts, and the meta-analysis is correlational.

### Limits

TMT treats procrastination as a motivation problem. The compendium's principle [[MO045 — Recognise procrastination as emotion regulation, not laziness]] draws on a different account, and no source opened here tests the two against each other. Later work simplified the model into three sources of motivation, namely value, time horizon and expectancy, according to one excerpt (Steel et al., 2018, as cited in the ADHD and procrastination study above).

Neugier is Heidegger's term in Being and Time for a restless way of seeing that goes after the new for its own sake. In §36 he introduces it as "the tendency towards 'seeing'", adding that it "is not confined to seeing" but is a way of letting the world be encountered in perception (Being and Time §36, trans. Macquarrie and Robinson, [complete text](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf)).

### What he says it does

When curiosity "has become free", it "concerns itself with seeing, not in order to understand what is seen (that is, to come into a Being towards it) but just in order to see. It seeks novelty only in order to leap from it anew to another novelty." He then lists its marks. It is "characterized by a specific way of not tarrying alongside what is closest", it is "concerned with the constant possibility of distraction", and it has the character of "never dwelling anywhere": "Curiosity is everywhere and nowhere." It "has nothing to do with observing entities and marvelling at them", and being "amazed to the point of not understanding is something in which it has no interest." It concerns itself instead "with a kind of knowing, but just in order to have known" ([§36](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf)).

He ties it to the previous section: "Idle talk controls even the ways in which one may be curious. It says what one 'must' have read and seen" ([§36](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf)).

### Not a moral verdict

Heidegger adds a caution in §34 that applies equally here. His interpretation, he writes, "is purely ontological in its aims, and is far removed from any moralizing critique of everyday Dasein" ([Being and Time, end of §34](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e265eb50aee2d7e8a81ae69/t/5ff90abe346f087732825582/1610156759396/Heidegger-Martin-Being-and-Time+-+Complete+Text.pdf)). So the passage describes a feature of everyday life and does not tell anyone to stop being curious.

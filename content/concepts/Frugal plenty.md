Frugal plenty is the Epicurean thought that having little, when the little is enough, loses nothing to luxury. In the Letter to Menoeceus, Epicurus grounds it in independence from outward things: contentment with little if we have not much, on the ground that those who stand least in need of luxury have the sweetest enjoyment of it.

> "Plain fare gives as much pleasure as a costly diet, when once the pain of want has been removed, while bread and water confer the highest possible pleasure when they are brought to hungry lips." (Epicurus, [Letter to Menoeceus](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Letter_to_Menoeceus), Hicks translation)

### What the practice is

The training side is habituation: accustoming oneself to simple and inexpensive diet supplies all that is needful for health, enables a man to meet the necessary requirements of life without shrinking, and renders us fearless of fortune. The point is not asceticism but the removal of the pain of want, after which plain and costly fare please equally. Whatever is natural is easily procured; only the vain and worthless are hard to win.

“Voluntary discomfort” — Seneca, Letters.

Voluntary discomfort is Seneca's Stoic practice of choosing hardship in calm times so that real hardship, when it arrives, is already familiar. The instruction appears in Letter 18 of the Moral Letters to Lucilius, framed as a lesson drawn "from the teachings of great men".

> "Set aside a certain number of days, during which you shall be content with the scantiest and cheapest fare, with coarse and rough dress, saying to yourself the while: 'Is this the condition that I feared?'" (Seneca, [Moral Letters to Lucilius 18.5](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_18))

### Why before the fact

The letter's reasoning is timing: "It is precisely in times of immunity from care that the soul should toughen itself beforehand for occasions of greater stress". The soldier comparison follows: peacetime drills so that violence, when it comes, meets a prepared defense.

### Definition

A repair attempt is any move, words, a joke, a hand on the shoulder, a request to start over, meant to stop a conflict from spiralling. In the literature summarising Gottman's framework:

> Repair attempts are bids to de-escalate conflict via the use of humor, taking breaks, or minimization of issues. ([Rueda et al. 2018, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6538476/))

The word "bid" matters: an attempt can be offered and still fail, because the other half of the skill is reception. Gottman's apartment-lab studies found the fate of a couple's repairs told more about the marriage's future than the presence of conflict itself.

### Origin

The construct comes out of John Gottman's observational research on couples, where video-coded conflict conversations revealed that stable couples fight too, and what separates them from distressed ones is de-escalation. Later work refined the picture: newlywed studies found the most effective repairs were "pre-emptive", arriving in the first minutes and addressing the emotional climate (shared humor, affection, taking responsibility for part of the issue) rather than the logic of the dispute.

### A real-life example

Halfway through a sharp argument about money, one partner says, "That came out harsher than I meant. Can I try again?" The words are small; the effect is a dropped shoulder and a slower sentence. The disagreement is still there, and now it can be had without casualties.

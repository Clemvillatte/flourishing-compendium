Unconscious thought theory (UTT) is Ap Dijksterhuis and Loran Nordgren's 2006 account of what the mind does with a problem when attention is elsewhere: unconscious thought is not a weaker cousin of deliberation but a mode with its own strengths, and for complex choices it can do better work than conscious deliberation. Their abstract states the theory and its most quotable claim:

> "We present a theory about human thought named the unconscious-thought theory (UTT). The theory is applicable to decision making, impression formation, attitude formation and change, problem solving, and creativity. It distinguishes between two modes of thought: unconscious and conscious. Unconscious thought and conscious thought have different characteristics, and these different characteristics make each mode preferable under different circumstances." ([Dijksterhuis & Nordgren, 2006, abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/j.1745-6916.2006.00007.x/))

> "For instance, contrary to popular belief, decisions about simple issues can be better tackled by conscious thought, whereas decisions about complex matters can be better approached with unconscious thought." ([Dijksterhuis & Nordgren, 2006, abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/j.1745-6916.2006.00007.x/))

### Origin

The theory formalized a line of experiments beginning with Dijksterhuis's 2004 studies on unconscious processing of information about choice options, and its most famous exhibit was the 2006 Science paper on the "deliberation-without-attention effect": the hypothesis that people facing a difficult choice make a better decision after a period of distraction than after an equally long period of conscious deliberation, an effect the replication literature calls the unconscious thought advantage ([Nieuwenstein et al., 2015](https://www.ejwagenmakers.com/2015/Nieuwenstein%20et%20al.%20JDM%202015.pdf)). UTT, in Perspectives on Psychological Science, gave the effect a vocabulary: two modes of thought, each with its own capacity and its own best circumstances.

### The evidence status

The theory's flagship effect has not survived replication well, and the page's job includes saying so plainly. A 2015 meta-analysis and large-scale replication attempt reported: "we conducted a meta-analysis and a large-scale replication study (N = 399) that met the conditions deemed optimal for replicating the UTA. Consistent with the reliability account, the large-scale replication study yielded no evidence for the UTA, and the meta-analysis showed that previous reports of the UTA were confined to underpowered studies that used relatively small sample sizes" ([Nieuwenstein et al., 2015](https://www.ejwagenmakers.com/2015/Nieuwenstein%20et%20al.%20JDM%202015.pdf)). The paper's conclusion sentence: "Accordingly, we conclude that there exists no reliable support for the claim that a momentary diversion of thought leads to better decision making than a period of deliberation" ([Nieuwenstein et al., 2015](https://www.ejwagenmakers.com/2015/Nieuwenstein%20et%20al.%20JDM%202015.pdf)). The everyday wisdom the theory rode in on, "sleep on it", survives as a comfort rather than a finding.

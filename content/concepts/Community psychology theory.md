Community psychology theory studies well-being at the level of settings and systems. Community psychologists "seek to understand people within the social contexts of their lives in order to promote a better quality of life for all people" ([Kloos et al., ch. 1, p. 3](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/community-psychology-fourth-edition-sample-chapter.pdf)). Sense of community and empowerment are two of the textbook's eight core values for the field.

> "Community psychology concerns the relationships of individuals with communities and societies. By integrating research with action, it seeks to understand and enhance quality of life for individuals, communities, and societies." (Kloos, Hill, Thomas, Case, Scott and Wandersman, [Community Psychology: Linking Individuals and Communities, 4th ed., APA, ch. 1, p. 3](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/community-psychology-fourth-edition-sample-chapter.pdf))

### Two of the eight core values

Sense of community is "a feeling of belongingness, interdependence, and mutual commitment that links individuals as a collective", a perception the textbook traces to McMillan and Chavis and to Sarason ([ch. 1, p. 24](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/community-psychology-fourth-edition-sample-chapter.pdf)). Empowerment is "the process of enhancing the possibilities for people to control their own lives (Rappaport, 1987)" ([ch. 1, p. 27](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/community-psychology-fourth-edition-sample-chapter.pdf)). Both appear among the field's eight core values, where sense of community and "empowerment and citizen participation" sit alongside social justice, respect for human diversity, collective wellness, collaboration, empirical grounding, and a multilevel strengths-based perspective ([Exhibit 1.1, p. 24](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/community-psychology-fourth-edition-sample-chapter.pdf)).

Historicity (German Geschichtlichkeit) is the idea that human existence is historical in its very make-up, not just a thing that happens to have a past. Britannica says Heidegger's treatment in Division II of Being and Time "is one of the most poorly understood sections of the work", and that with the concept he indicates that "historical questions and themes are legitimate topics of ontological inquiry" ([Britannica, Being and Time](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Being-and-Time)).

### Heidegger: inheriting possibilities

Stanford's entry on Heidegger summarises: "Because Dasein is temporal, Heidegger argues, it can also 'historize'—that is, we always encounter situations on the basis of a set of dispositions, skills, practices, and projects that we have inherited from our community." The past "opens up a set of possibilities which determine the significance of, and delimit the availability of, the options for action that are open to us at any given moment", so "we exist as beings who always have a future in terms of a past that has been 'handed down' to us" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Heidegger](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)).

Britannica reads the point as active: Dasein "possesses a heritage on which it must act", and historicity "thus means making a decision about how to actualize (or act upon) salient elements of a collective past". It adds that the treatment answers an earlier view of human life as "historical" in a sense that was "passive and devoid of intentionality".

In the translated text, the vocabulary of heritage, fate and destiny is spelled out: "Resoluteness implies handing oneself down by anticipation to the 'there' of the moment of vision; and this handing down we call 'fate'. This is also the ground for destiny, by which we understand Dasein's historizing in Being-with Others" ([Being and Time, translated excerpt, Macquarrie and Robinson text as hosted by the Dominican House of Studies](https://www.dhspriory.org/kenny/PhilTexts/Heidegger/BeingTime11.doc)). I opened this only as a search excerpt, so the wording is checked against that excerpt.

### Gadamer: situatedness and horizon

Stanford's Gadamer entry carries the idea into interpretation. understanding "always occurs from within a particular 'horizon' that is determined by our historically-determined situatedness", and "understanding, for Gadamer, is thus always an 'effect' of history" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Gadamer](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gadamer/)). See [[Historical consciousness]] for the further steps.

### Cautions

Readers of Heidegger should keep two points in view. A philosophy journal essay argues that his historicity makes authenticity communal: "the personal choices of the authentic individual are never original", and the possibilities for decision "are always already socially constituted" ([Florida Philosophical Review, UCF](https://cah.ucf.edu/fpr/article/why-heidegger-is-not-an-existentialist-interpreting-authenticity-and-historicity-in-being-and-time/)). The same essay flags the risk that the idea slides into "relativistic provincialism", where each community has "its own relative heritage". And Britannica notes Heidegger ties the account to a people or Volk, a link whose political reading is contested, as the title of a University of California Press chapter shows: [Historical Destiny and National Socialism in Heidegger's Being and Time](https://publishing.cdlib.org/ucpressebooks/view?docId=ft5k4006n2;chunk.id=d0e743;doc.view=print). Use the concept with those readings in mind.

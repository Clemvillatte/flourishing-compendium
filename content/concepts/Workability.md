In Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), [[Workability]] is a criterion for assessing actions in relation to a person's chosen values: those values provide the standard for judging whether an action is useful. ACT is rooted in functional contextualism, a pragmatic philosophy whose truth criterion is applied against stated goals. Steven C. Hayes states the relationship directly:

> "ACT emphasizes workability as a truth criterion, and chosen values as the necessary precursor to the assessment of workability because values specify the criteria for the application of workability." — Steven C. Hayes, ["Philosophical roots" (2006), contextualscience.org](https://contextualscience.org/book/export/html/49325)

### What the test does

The compendium's practice kernel [[K135 — Workability check]] applies the criterion to holding a view: does doing so help a person act in line with their values?

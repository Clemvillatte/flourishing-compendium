"Workability" is the test Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) uses in place of literal truth: a thought, memory, urge or action is assessed by what it helps a person do, not by whether it is correct. The term belongs to ACT's philosophical base, functional contextualism, which takes successful working toward a stated goal as its truth criterion. Steven C. Hayes, who developed ACT, states the clinical consequence directly.

> "ACT emphasizes workability as a truth criterion, and chosen values as the necessary precursor to the assessment of workability because values specify the criteria for the application of workability." — Steven C. Hayes, ["Philosophical roots" (2006), contextualscience.org](https://contextualscience.org/book/export/html/49325)

### What the test replaces

The question shifts from whether a belief is reasonable to whether holding it helps the person live as they intend. Hayes's own outline of ACT analysis puts it concretely: "The value of any action is its workability measured against the client's true values (those he/she would have if it were a free choice)." In session, the examination of a client's control strategies is "based entirely on the clients experience, with workability as the unit of analysis", and the therapist is warned to be "extremely careful not to dictate or demand that the client evaluate their control strategies as unworkable". The verdict comes from the client's life, not from the clinician's logic.

- [[P155 — Make workability your everyday criterion]] — grounds the criterion in ACT's own sources: chosen values supply the direction a response is assessed against.

- [[SP008 — Treat good and evil as relations, not properties]] — good and evil assessed by what hinders or helps the power of living: the same test with an older vocabulary (Hayes et al., 2011).
- [[P171 — Watch your explanatory style]] — interpretations of setbacks carry a workability question: what does explaining it this way let the person do next (James, Pragmatism).
- [[S051 — Recognise that your judgements are the source of your distress]] — the judgement, not the event, is the thing checked for workability (Peirce, "How to Make Our Ideas Clear").
- [[K135 — Workability check]] — the practice kernel: "Is holding this view working for me? Does it help me act in ways that align with my values?"

“Hermeneutics of the self” — Ricoeur, Oneself as Another.

A hermeneutics of the self reads a self the way a reader reads a text: as something interpreted, not inspected. Paul Ricoeur's version, worked out in Oneself as Another (1990), replaces the immediate self-knowledge of the Cartesian tradition with detours — through action, through narrative, through others — on which whatever selfhood gets established is always attested rather than proven.

> "The philosopher explains that 'sameness' (idem-identity) presumes a permanence in time, as opposed to the different, the changing, and the variable; in contrast, 'selfhood' (ipse-identity) does not imply anything of that kind, as it allows for other modalities of non-identical identity." — [Truc, Études Ricœuriennes / Ricœur Studies 2(2) (2011), full text](https://ricoeur.pitt.edu/ojs/ricoeur/article/download/51/28), expounding Ricœur's Oneself as Another

### Why narrative is the detour that works

The distinction matters because a life changes and still remains one's own. Sameness alone cannot hold a life together, and selfhood alone cannot explain the recognisable person; the two are mediated, in Ricoeur's own words as quoted in the same article, in that "it is within the framework of narrative theory that the concrete dialectic of selfhood and sameness [...] attains its fullest development." A life story is where change and continuity negotiate. The payoff for flourishing is a form of self-understanding that does not demand certainty: the self is like a text one keeps re-reading, and its identity lies in the reading, with others, over time.

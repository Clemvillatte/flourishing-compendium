The empathy-altruism hypothesis is C. Daniel Batson's claim that feeling empathy for a person in need produces a motivation aimed at that person's welfare, and not at some hidden benefit for the helper. In the words of one of his papers, the hypothesis "claims that prosocial motivation associated with feeling empathy for a person in need is directed toward the ultimate goal of benefiting that person, not toward some subtle form of self-benefit" ([Batson et al., 1988, PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3418490/)).

### How it was tested

The method was to propose self-interested alternatives and test them against it. Three examples from the abstracts:

- Empathic joy: three experiments tested whether people help to share in the victim's joy at improvement. "Results of none of the experiments patterned as predicted by the empathic-joy hypothesis; instead, results of each were consistent with the empathy-altruism hypothesis" ([Batson et al., 1991](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1941512/)).
- Reward and punishment: five studies tested whether empathic people help to gain praise and pride or to avoid guilt and shame. The results "did not support either the empathy-specific reward or the empathy-specific punishment hypothesis" ([Batson et al., 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3418490/)).
- Social evaluation: in one study participants "were led to believe that no one--including the person in need--would ever know if they declined to help," and a positive relationship between empathic emotion and offering help remained ([Fultz et al., 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3712222/)).

### Limits

These are the abstracts of laboratory studies from one research programme, and each tests a specific egoistic rival. They show that those particular rivals fit the data poorly. They do not prove that no self-interested explanation could ever account for helping, and the 1988 abstract itself speaks only of evidence that "continues to mount". I have not read Batson's 2011 book, the seed source, so this page does not summarise it.

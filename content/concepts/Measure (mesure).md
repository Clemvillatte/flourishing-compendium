In The Rebel, Camus connects measure with the limit that rebellion affirms ([Camus, The Rebel, 1951](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/albert-camus-the-rebel)). The rebel's refusal, he writes, means among other things that "there is a limit beyond which you shall not go", and this "no" affirms the existence of a borderline ([Camus, The Rebel, Part One](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/albert-camus-the-rebel)). That limit, once discovered, becomes a standard:

> "If the limit discovered by rebellion transfigures everything, if every thought, every action that goes beyond a certain point negates itself, there is, in fact, a measure by which to judge events and men." ([Camus, The Rebel, Part Five, "Moderation and Excess"](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/albert-camus-the-rebel))

### What rebellion has to do with moderation

Camus sets measure against nihilism rather than against rebellion:

> "Moderation is not the opposite of rebellion. Rebellion in itself is moderation, and it demands, defends, and re-creates it throughout history and its eternal disturbances." ([Camus, The Rebel, Part Five, "Moderation and Excess"](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/albert-camus-the-rebel))

The link runs through the limit. Nihilist thought, "because it neglects this frontier", reaches the point of justifying total destruction or unlimited conquest, and Camus argues that revolutionary thought must return to "thought that recognizes limits" ([Camus, The Rebel, Part Five](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/albert-camus-the-rebel)). He gives the idea a Greek ancestry:

> "Heraclitus, the discoverer of the constant change of things, nevertheless set a limit to this perpetual process. This limit was symbolized by Nemesis, the goddess of moderation and the implacable enemy of the immoderate." ([Camus, The Rebel, Part Five](https://www.thetedkarchive.com/library/albert-camus-the-rebel))

### What to practise

The limit Camus describes is not a temperament, a refusal to get carried away. It is a discovery that a certain kind of action negates itself, and that the negation is where the standard comes from. To practise measure is to look for the frontier inside your own project — the point past which the good you wanted becomes the harm you are doing — and to hold the line there. The discipline, in Camus's wording, is that rebellion demands and re-creates moderation; it is not the opposite of it.

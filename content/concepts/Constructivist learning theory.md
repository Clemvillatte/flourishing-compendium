understanding is built through active construction, not passive reception (Piaget; Bruner).

### The core claim

The encyclopaedia article on constructivism states the theory's opening definition:

> "Constructivism is a theory that suggests that learners do not passively acquire knowledge through direct instruction." ([Wikipedia, Constructivism (philosophy of education)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_(philosophy_of_education)))

The same article credits Piaget with the formalisation and names the mechanisms:

> "He identified processes of assimilation and accommodation as crucial in this interaction, as individuals construct new knowledge from their experiences." ([Wikipedia, Constructivism (philosophy of education)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_(philosophy_of_education)))

### Assimilation and accommodation

The article gives both processes:

> "When individuals assimilate new information, they integrate it into their existing framework without altering that framework." ([Wikipedia, Constructivism (philosophy of education)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_(philosophy_of_education)))

> "Accommodation is the process of adjusting one's mental representation of the external world to fit new experiences." ([Wikipedia, Constructivism (philosophy of education)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_(philosophy_of_education)))

Fricker is Miranda Fricker, the philosopher who gave epistemic injustice its name and its two-part analysis in Epistemic Injustice: Power and the Ethics of Knowing (2007). The book's project is to home in on forms of injustice that are distinctively epistemic: a wrong done to someone specifically in their capacity as a knower.

> "Testimonial injustice occurs when prejudice causes a hearer to give a deflated level of credibility to a speaker's word; hermeneutical injustice occurs at a prior stage, when a gap in collective interpretive resources puts someone at an unfair disadvantage when it comes to making sense of their social experiences." (Fricker, [Epistemic Injustice, Introduction](https://academic.oup.com/book/32817/chapter/274999524), OUP 2007)

### The two forms

Fricker's own summary: testimonial injustice is caused by prejudice in the economy of credibility, hermeneutical injustice by structural prejudice in the economy of collective hermeneutical resources. Her examples: the police not believing you because you are black; suffering sexual harassment in a culture that still lacks that critical concept. The book also constructs two hybrid ethical-intellectual virtues of epistemic justice, testimonial and hermeneutical, aimed at forestalling the harms.

Early childhood education research, in James Heckman's economic treatment, is the study of how early environments shape child, adolescent and adult achievement. His 2006 Science summary states the field's core finding: life cycle skill formation is "a dynamic process in which early inputs strongly affect the productivity of later inputs".

> "Investing in disadvantaged young children is a rare public policy initiative that promotes fairness and social justice and at the same time promotes productivity in the economy and in society at large. Early interventions targeted toward disadvantaged children have much higher returns than later interventions such as reduced pupil-teacher ratios, public job training, convict rehabilitation programs, tuition subsidies, or expenditure on police." (Heckman, [Skill Formation and the Economics of Investing in Disadvantaged Children](https://jenni.uchicago.edu/papers/Heckman_Science_v312_2006.pdf), Science 312(5782): 1900-1902, 2006, the article's concluding section)

### The equity-efficiency finding

The same article's argument for the timing: "A serious trade-off exists between equity and efficiency for adolescent and young adult skill policies. There is no such trade-off for policies targeted toward disadvantaged young children." Its summary of the current allocation: "society overinvests in remedial skill investments at later ages and underinvests in the early years."

Emotion awareness is the ability to notice what one is feeling and label it in specific terms, rather than carrying a vague sense of feeling good or bad. In the research literature it is measured as emotion differentiation or emotional granularity, an individual difference introduced by Lisa Feldman Barrett, James Gross and colleagues in 2001.

> "Individuals differ considerably in their emotion experience. Some experience emotions in a highly differentiated manner, clearly distinguishing among a variety of negative and positive discrete emotions. Others experience emotions in a relatively undifferentiated manner, treating a range of like-valence terms as interchangeable." (Barrett, Gross, Christensen & Benvenuto, Cognition and Emotion 15(6), p. 713, 2001, [full PDF](https://www.affective-science.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/01MaprelationDiffReg.pdf))

In their study, participants kept a 14-day diary of their most intense emotion experiences, and differentiation scores were computed from how distinctly they rated like-valenced emotion terms against each other. More differentiated negative emotion experience went with more frequent regulation of negative emotion, particularly at higher emotional intensity; positive differentiation was unrelated to regulation. The paper's title carries the working idea: knowing what you're feeling is a precondition for knowing what to do about it.

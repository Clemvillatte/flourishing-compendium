Ego psychology is the psychoanalytic school that puts the ego's own functions at the centre of the theory. The American Psychological Association defines it this way:

> "in psychoanalysis, an approach that emphasizes the functions of the ego in controlling impulses, planning, and dealing with the external environment." ([APA Dictionary of Psychology](https://dictionary.apa.org/ego-psychology))

### What changed from classical theory

The APA entry says ego psychology "differs from classical psychoanalysis in proposing that the ego contains a conflict-free sphere of functioning". Heinz Hartmann's Ego Psychology and the Problem of Adaptation (1939; English translation 1958) is the founding statement. In the account on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ego_psychology), Hartmann held that the ego has innate capacities that help a person adapt, among them "perception, attention, memory, concentration, motor coordination, and language". Under what he called "an average expectable environment", these grow into ego functions that are autonomous from the drives, not products of frustration and conflict.

### Where Erikson fits

The same article lists Erik Erikson among later psychoanalysts whose studies of infants and children were integrated into ego psychology. It says Erikson "provided a bold reformulation of Freud's biologic, epigenetic psychosexual theory" through his study of socio-cultural influences on ego development.

### Conflict does not disappear

The Wikipedia account adds that Hartmann "recognized, however, that conflicts were part of the human condition". On that account, certain ego functions may become conflicted by aggressive and libidinal impulses.

This page reads collective responsibility through Iris Marion Young's social connection model of responsibility for structural injustice.

> "The social connection model of responsibility says that all agents who contribute by their actions to the structural processes that produce injustice have responsibilities to work to remedy these injustices." (Young, [Responsibility and Global Justice: A Social Connection Model](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/social-philosophy-and-policy/article/abs/responsibility-and-global-justice-a-social-connection-model/9308EE478561C7CE31E1F5A8F26CBE04), Social Philosophy and Policy, 2006, abstract)

The model contrasts with the liability model. Young specifies five distinguishing features:

> "it does not isolate perpetrators; it judges background conditions of action; it is more forward looking than backward looking; its responsibility is essentially shared; and it can be discharged only through collective action." (same source, abstract)

The responsibility it names is shared rather than vested in a collective agent. Marin, discussing Young's model: "Unlike collective responsibility, which belongs to collective agents, responsibility for structural injustice is shared: Each individual bears it personally, but together with others (109–10). Only by joining with others in collective action can one discharge it (111–2)." ([Marin, Structural Responsibility](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0BD6D560DB5A68105A5B47438F03FE7F/S0003055424001072a.pdf/structural_responsibility.pdf), 2024, quoting Young's Responsibility for Justice with page numbers)

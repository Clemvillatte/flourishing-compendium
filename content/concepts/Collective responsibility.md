Collective responsibility, in Iris Marion Young's treatment, is responsibility for structural injustice: the harms that emerge from large-scale social processes without any single perpetrator. Her social connection model replaces the courtroom picture of blame.

> "The social connection model of responsibility says that all agents who contribute by their actions to the structural processes that produce injustice have responsibilities to work to remedy these injustices." (Young, [Responsibility and Global Justice: A Social Connection Model](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/social-philosophy-and-policy/article/abs/responsibility-and-global-justice-a-social-connection-model/9308EE478561C7CE31E1F5A8F26CBE04), Social Philosophy and Policy, 2006)

She specifies five features that distinguish the model from the liability model:

> "it does not isolate perpetrators; it judges background conditions of action; it is more forward looking than backward looking; its responsibility is essentially shared; and it can be discharged only through collective action." (same source)

The compendium uses "Identity development" for the Eriksonian research tradition on how a coherent sense of self forms. The field's framing, from a decade review:

> "One of the key developmental tasks in adolescence and young adulthood is to develop a coherent sense of self and identity (Erikson, 1968)." (Branje, de Moor, Spitzer and Becht 2021, opening sentence of the main text, [Journal of Research on Adolescence 31(4):908-927](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9298910/))

### Maturation and stability

"Synthesizing research on the development of identity, we show that identity development during adolescence and early adulthood is characterized by both systematic maturation and substantial stability" (Branje et al. 2021, [PMC9298910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9298910/)). The review "discusses the role of life events and transitions for identity and the role of micro‐processes and narrative processes as a potential mechanisms of personal identity development change" (Branje et al. 2021, [PMC9298910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9298910/)).

### Across the lifespan

A more recent study carries the tradition's lifelong reading: "Also, the commonly held belief that identity formation stops by the end of adolescence, or “emerging adulthood”, misrepresents Erikson's (1963) work and subsequent research. Instead, identity formation consists of a dynamic process that allows individuals to continuously evaluate and update their commitments across the adult lifespan." (Weller, de Moor and Klimstra 2025, [Journal of Personality](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12988339/))

Vipassana, literally "clear-seeing" and often translated insight meditation, is treated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu as a quality of mind rather than a technique: "Vipassana is not a meditation technique. It's a quality of mind — the ability to see events clearly in the present moment" ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu, One Tool Among Many](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/onetool.html)). The Satipatthana Sutta presents the four frames of reference as the direct path:

> "This is the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of Unbinding — in other words, the four frames of reference." (MN 10, Satipatthana Sutta, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [AccessToInsight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html))

The four frames are body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities, each observed "ardent, alert, & mindful" ([MN 10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)).

### What insight looks at

Thanissaro adds a caution against treating mindfulness as the whole path: "Although mindfulness is helpful in fostering vipassana, it's not enough for developing vipassana to the point of total release." Insight, he says, "needs to be teamed with samatha", which he glosses as "the ability to settle the mind comfortably in the present". The questioning that follows is directed at all experience and pursued "until it leads to a fivefold understanding of all events: in terms of their arising, their passing away, their drawbacks, their allure, and the escape from them" ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu, One Tool Among Many](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/onetool.html)).

The sutta's own instruction has the same shape. In the section on the body, the monk "remains focused on the phenomenon of origination with regard to the body, on the phenomenon of passing away with regard to the body", and "remains independent, unsustained by (not clinging to) anything in the world" (MN 10, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)).

### What it is not

On Thanissaro's account vipassana is not a technique that can be picked up on its own, nor a synonym for mindfulness. The sources here give one translator's reading of the tradition.

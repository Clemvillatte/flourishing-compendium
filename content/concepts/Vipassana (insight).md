Vipassana is the Buddhist term for insight: the direct seeing of how experience is put together, classically developed through the four frames of reference described in the [Satipatthana Sutta](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html):

> "This is the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of Unbinding — in other words, the four frames of reference." (MN 10, Satipatthana Sutta, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [AccessToInsight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html))

The four frames are body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities, each observed "ardent, alert, & mindful" with greed and distress put aside ([MN 10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)). The compendium's seed names the practice Buddhist meditation.

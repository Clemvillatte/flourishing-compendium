Vipassana, literally "clear-seeing" and often translated insight meditation, is described by Thanissaro Bhikkhu as "a method using a modicum of tranquillity to foster moment-to-moment mindfulness of the inconstancy of events as they are directly experienced in the present" ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu, One Tool Among Many](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/onetool.html)). The Satipatthana Sutta presents the four frames of reference as the direct path:

> "This is the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of Unbinding — in other words, the four frames of reference." (MN 10, Satipatthana Sutta, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu, [AccessToInsight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html))

The four frames are body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities, each observed "ardent, alert, & mindful" ([MN 10](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)).

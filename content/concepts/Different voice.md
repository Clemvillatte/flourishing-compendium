The different voice is Carol Gilligan's name for a way of reasoning about moral problems that psychology had been scoring as immaturity: reasoning in terms of relationships, context, care and responsibility rather than in terms of rules, rights and universals. The 1982 book In a Different Voice argued that the fault lay with the instruments, not the reasoners. Gilligan's later retrospective states the claim in its careful form: the voice is not a female voice.

> "The different voice, then, is identified not by gender but by theme. Its difference arises from joining reason with emotion, self with relationships." (Carol Gilligan, [Looking Back to Look Forward: Revisiting In a Different Voice](https://classics-at.chs.harvard.edu/classics9-carol-gilligan-looking-back-to-look-forward-revisiting-in-a-different-voice/))

### Care as a human ethic

In the retrospective's framing, what sounded feminine under one arrangement of values sounds human under another: "Within a patriarchal framework, care is a feminine ethic. Within a democratic framework, care is a human ethic." The ethic itself she describes plainly: "Care is a relational ethic, grounded in a premise of interdependence." Its two moral injunctions, do not oppress and do not abandon, "live side by side".

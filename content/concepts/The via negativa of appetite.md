"The via negativa of appetite" is the companion's heading, and its carrier is the subtraction method the tradition articulates in Pseudo-Dionysius. In *The Mystic Theology*, the way up is by taking away. The seeker prays "to enter within the super-bright gloom, and through not seeing and not knowing, to see and to know that the not to see nor to know is itself the above sight and knowledge," and the method is given a sculptor's image: "just as those who make a lifelike statue, by extracting all the encumbrances which have been placed upon the clear view of the concealed, and by bringing to light, by the mere cutting away, the genuine beauty concealed in it" (Pseudo-Dionysius, *The Mystic Theology*, [Caput II](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_Dionysius_the_Areopagite/Mystic_Theology/Caput_II), trans. Parker; [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_Dionysius_the_Areopagite/Mystic_Theology)). The chapters then perform the removal in litany form, Caput V ascending through denials: the Cause "is neither soul, nor mind, nor has imagination, or opinion, or reason, or conception... neither is It darkness, nor light; nor error, nor truth; neither is there any definition at all of It, nor any abstraction" ([Caput V](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_Dionysius_the_Areopagite/Mystic_Theology/Caput_V)). What Dionysius describes is a theological practice, not a regimen of desire; the companion's application to appetite is an analogy with the sculptor image, not a claim that Dionysius prescribed it. The analogy reads: the pursuit of appetite is rarely starved of addition — more options, more intensity, more occasions — and what it lacks is subtraction. On the analogy, the flesh's limits, satiety, the point past which more becomes less, function like the encumbrances on the statue: the cutting away is what lets the figure stand out. The practice the analogy suggests is to let each limit end the pursuit where the limit ends it, and to treat the remainder, what still stands when nothing more is added, as the figure.

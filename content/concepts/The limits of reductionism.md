The limits of reductionism is Whitehead's warning that a successful abstraction is not yet the thing itself: models, categories and simplified schemes are indispensable and cannot be mistaken for the concrete reality they were abstracted from. His name for the mistake is the fallacy of misplaced concreteness, "the 'error of mistaking the abstract for the concrete'" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/), citing 1925 [1967: 51]). In Process and Reality he makes the same point about philosophy's own categories:

> "This fallacy consists in neglecting the degree of abstraction involved when an actual entity is considered merely so far as it exemplifies certain categories of thought. There are aspects of actualities which are simply ignored so long as we restrict thought to these categories." (Whitehead, Process and Reality, pp. 7–8, in [Open Horizons](https://www.openhorizons.org/the-spectral-luminosity-of-ordinary-things-whiteheads-fallacy-of-misplaced-concreteness.html))

### What the warning carries

The target is not abstraction itself, which Whitehead held unavoidable, but forgetting the degree of it. His sharpest example is scientific materialism: "The enormous success of the scientific abstractions", Whitehead wrote, "has foisted onto philosophy the task of accepting them as the most concrete rendering of fact," with the result that "modern philosophy has been ruined... this juggling with abstractions can never overcome the inherent confusion introduced by the ascription of misplaced concreteness to the scientific scheme" (1925 [1967: 55], in [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/)). The compendium principle BI006 carries the applied form: neurotransmitters are not simple cause-and-effect, and complexity matters. A compendium exercise drawn from the fallacy is to take any confident single-cause explanation and name what the scheme abstracted away. What the warning does not give: an argument that reduction is useless; the claim is about mistaking the model for reality, not against using the model.

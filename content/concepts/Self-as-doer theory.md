Houser-Marko and Sheldon (2006) introduced the self-as-doer as "a new motivational construct" to answer: "Why do some people persist in goal pursuit, even in the face of boredom or setbacks, whereas others quickly give up their goals?" Studies 1 and 2 "found longitudinal evidence that those who more strongly endorse doer statements regarding their goals (i.e., exerciser, dieter, runner) show greater behavioral persistence and attainment regarding such goals, even controlling for other relevant constructs such as expectancy, self-concordance, commitment, and neuroticism" ([Houser-Marko and Sheldon, Motivating Behavioral Persistence: The Self-As-Doer Construct, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, abstract at SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167206287974)). The seed's "I am a runner" example matches the abstract's runner and exerciser statements.

### Doers versus outcomes

Sheldon's later review describes the construct as people's "propensity to define themselves as a verb (as the doer of a goal) rather than as a noun (as the haver of a goal)". Participants turned goals into doer phrases ("boost my GPA" became "good grade-getter") and rated their identification with each. The review reports that self-as-doer predicted long-term effort and mediated the effect of self-concordance on sustained effort ([Sheldon, Becoming oneself: the central role of self-concordant goal selection, 2014, PDF](http://static1.1.sqspcdn.com/static/f/82279/28040277/1544226692223/Sheldon+-+Becoming+Oneself+2014.pdf?token=wpRmasmfKhEhLehu4p9468yR1Ig=); [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24981515/)). So the seed's contrast of action framing with outcome framing is an interpretation: the measured contrast is doer identification, not an outcome-focused alternative.

### Priming evidence

Study 3 primed the self-as-doer with The Little Engine That Could (compared with Curious George): those who also wrote an essay applying the story's message to themselves, not to others, "showed the greatest persistence in physically demanding tasks" (SAGE abstract).

### Limits

The abstract names three studies and the review summarises them; I did not open the full article, effect sizes or any replication. The wiki's wider claim that action-based identity framing "sustains goal pursuit" should be limited to goal persistence in these studies, not to wellbeing in general.

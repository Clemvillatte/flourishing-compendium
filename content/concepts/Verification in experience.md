Verification in experience is the pragmatist test of belief: a belief is tried by living it, and what the trying yields is the belief's meaning and, in the long run, its truth or falsehood. William James's formulation in Pragmatism:

> "TRUE IDEAS ARE THOSE THAT WE CAN ASSIMILATE, VALIDATE, CORROBORATE AND VERIFY. FALSE IDEAS ARE THOSE THAT WE CANNOT."
> — [James, Pragmatism, Lecture VI, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5116/5116-h/5116-h.htm)

The consequence James draws is that truth is not a label an idea carries but something that happens to it:

> "Truth HAPPENS to an idea. It BECOMES true, is MADE true by events. Its verity is in fact an event, a process: the process namely of its verifying itself, its veri-FICATION."
> — [James, Pragmatism, Lecture VI, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5116/5116-h/5116-h.htm)

The claim is not that whatever works is true, whatever feels good goes. It is that the only access anyone has to an idea's relation with reality runs through experience: an idea is verified when acting on it leads satisfactorily where it pointed — through the series of conjunctions the idea promised. Where no experiential difference could follow from holding the belief one way or the other, the pragmatist asks what content the dispute has left.

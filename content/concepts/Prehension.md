Prehension is Alfred North Whitehead's term for the grasping by which each unit of reality takes in its world; the word is derived from the Latin prehendere, meaning to grasp ([Wikipedia, Prehension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehension)). The term comes from his Process and Reality, the major work of his process philosophy. The Stanford Encyclopedia's summary of the system the term belongs to:

> "The basic unit of reality in Whitehead's system is an event-like entity called 'actual occasion,' which is the procedural integration or 'concrescence' of processes of data transfer ('prehensions') into unities that become new data." ([Seibt, "Process Philosophy", SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/process-philosophy/))

On this account an occasion is not a thing that then has relations; it is a taking-in of what came before it. That is the use the compendium makes of the term: P003 (Relation is primary) cites Whitehead for the claim that relations are constitutive rather than added, and P072 lists the concept among its philosophy parallels.

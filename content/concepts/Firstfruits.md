Firstfruits is the offering practice of the Hebrew Bible: the first portion of the harvest, given before the rest is used. Deuteronomy 26 gives it a ritual: on entering the land, the farmer takes of the first of all the fruit of the earth, puts it in a basket and brings it to the place God chooses; there it is set before the Lord, and the household rejoices together with the Levite and the stranger.

> "Honour the Lord with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase: So shall thy barns be filled with plenty, and thy presses shall burst out with new wine." (Proverbs 3:9-10, [KJV](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs+3%3A9-10&version=KJV))

### What the practice trains

The generalization in Proverbs moves the rule from harvest to all increase, and the ceremony in Deuteronomy keeps the social frame: the basket is brought, the declaration is made, and the rejoicing includes those outside the household. The compendium reads the structure, across traditions, as a training in stance: arriving in any system as a debtor to it rather than as an extractor.

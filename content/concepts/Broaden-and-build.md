Broaden-and-build is Barbara Fredrickson's theory of what positive emotions are for. In her 2001 American Psychologist paper she proposed that experiences of positive emotions "broaden people's momentary thought-action repertoires, which in turn serves to build their enduring personal resources," physical, intellectual, social and psychological ([Fredrickson 2001](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Frederickson_Broaden-and-build-2001.pdf)). Negative emotions narrow: fear says flee, anger says attack. Positive emotions widen the range of what comes to mind, and what is gained outlasts the feeling.

### What each emotion urges

The theory assigns each emotion a broadening urge. Joy creates "the urge to play, push the limits, and be creative"; interest, "the urge to explore, take in new information and experiences"; contentment, "the urge to savor current life circumstances and integrate these circumstances into new views of self and of the world"; love, a recurring cycle of these urges within safe, close relationships ([Fredrickson 2001](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Frederickson_Broaden-and-build-2001.pdf)).

The 2004 restatement compresses it: joy sparks the urge to play, interest to explore, contentment to savour and integrate, and the broadened mindsets "promote discovery of novel and creative actions, ideas and social bonds," resources that "function as reserves that can be drawn on later" ([Fredrickson 2004](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1693418/)).

### What the evidence showed

The evidence Fredrickson assembled was candidly preliminary. Alice Isen's two decades of experiments showed positive-affect participants thinking in more unusual, flexible, integrative ways, and Fredrickson and Branigan's film study found joy and contentment led participants to list more things they wanted to do than a neutral film did. The undo hypothesis added physiology: after a timed, evaluated speech task raised cardiovascular activity, participants shown joy or contentment films returned to baseline faster, across three independent samples.

With Thomas Joiner she found the upward spiral: positive emotions and broad-minded coping, assessed five weeks apart, each predicted increases in the other, so that positive affect and broadened coping "serially enhance one another."

### Limits

Two limits belong on the page. Fredrickson herself called the mechanisms of undoing "unknown" and the evidence preliminary. And a later offshoot was formally withdrawn: the 2005 Fredrickson-Losada paper's claim of a critical positivity ratio of 2.9013, built on fluid-dynamics equations, was retracted in part by American Psychologist after Brown, Sokal and Friedman concluded the claim "is entirely unfounded"; Fredrickson agreed the model was inaccurate ([Brown et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.7006); [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2013/12/model-emotions)). The core broaden-and-build claims were not the retracted part.

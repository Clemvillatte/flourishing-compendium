“Purity of the mirror of the heart” — al-Ghazali, Ihya Ulum al-Din.

Al-Ghazali's image in the Ihya' — Book XXI, the Marvels of the Heart — is that the heart is a mirror made to receive reality:

> "Man's heart, as a mirror, is potentially capable of having reflected in it the real essence of all things, and thus of coming to know them." (al-Ghazali, [The Marvels of the Heart, trans. Skellie, Ihya' Ulum al-Din Book XXI](https://archive.org/stream/imamalghazalienglish/Al-ghazalisMarvelsOfTheHeart_djvu.txt))

### Rust and polish

The mirror is not static. Disobedience and appetite cloud it: "The blameworthy influences are like a darkening smoke that rises up over the mirror of the heart and is heaped up upon it time after time until it becomes dark and murky and entirely veiled from God the Exalted", while "obedience to God, praise be to Him, by striving against the appetites polishes the heart, but disobedience to Him blackens it". The polish, in the tradition Ghazali transmits, is remembrance of God (dhikr). The concept names a discipline rather than a mood: purity is recovered by work on the self, not wished for.

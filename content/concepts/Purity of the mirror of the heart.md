“Purity of the mirror of the heart” — al-Ghazali, Ihya Ulum al-Din.

Al-Ghazali's image in the Ihya' — Book XXI, the Marvels of the Heart — is that the heart is a mirror, and that its condition changes with conduct:

> "The blameworthy influences are like a darkening smoke that rises up over the mirror of the heart and is heaped up upon it time after time until it becomes dark and murky and entirely veiled from God the Exalted." (al-Ghazali, [The Marvels of the Heart, trans. Skellie, Ihya' Ulum al-Din Book XXI](https://archive.org/stream/imamalghazalienglish/Al-ghazalisMarvelsOfTheHeart_djvu.txt))

### Rust and polish

The direction of the change runs both ways: "Obedience to God, praise be to Him, by striving against the appetites polishes the heart, but disobedience to Him blackens it." The passage also ties the clean heart to "the remembrance (dhikr) [of God]", quoting the Qur'an's "Verily in remembrance of God do hearts find rest! (13:28)". The concept names a discipline rather than a mood: purity is recovered by work on the self.

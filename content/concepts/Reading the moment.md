"Reading the moment" names the practice at the centre of the Zhouyi, or Book of Changes: consulting the sixty-four hexagrams to read the shape of the present situation before acting. The text began as a divination manual of the Zhou dynasty and grew into a Chinese classic whose philosophy is a discussion of time, location and judgment derived from those divinatory practices ([Hon, SEP, Chinese Philosophy of Change (Yijing), §1.4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-change/)).

> "It is from divination that we develop an acute sensitivity to the combined effects of temporal and spatial factors that determine our judgment." (Hon, [Chinese Philosophy of Change (Yijing), §1.4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-change/))

### What the reading gives

In early China the inquirer brought a real decision to a divination, a war, a marriage or a response to disaster, and the coded result had to be interpreted, with expert help, into an action plan; the tradition treated that interpretation as the heart of the practice ([Hon, §1.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-change/)). A hexagram reading offers the inquirer the situation as a moment with a direction: where the pressure sits and which conditions are ripe, so that judgment can meet the situation rather than fight it.

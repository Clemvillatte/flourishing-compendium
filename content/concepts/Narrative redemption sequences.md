Narrative redemption sequences are the story-shape in which a bad scene turns good. Dan McAdams and colleagues introduced the coding scheme in 2001: in a redemption sequence, "the bad is redeemed, salvaged, mitigated, or made better in light of the ensuing good," while a contamination sequence runs the other way, "the good is spoiled, ruined, contaminated, or undermined by what follows it" ([McAdams, Reynolds, Lewis, Patten and Bowman, PSPB 27 (2001), p. 474, quoted in the Sedikides and Wildschut handbook chapter](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~crsi/Sedikides%20and%20Wildschut%202016.pdf)). The finding the seed names, predict well-being, is the programme's most robust result.

### The evidence

In the original study, 269 midlife adults and 125 college students told open-ended stories of meaningful life episodes; blind raters tallied the two sequences. People who told stories with many redemption sequences scored higher on the Generativity Scale and tended to be happier, and participants who told contamination sequences showed more signs of depression ([APA Monitor on Psychology, Our stories, ourselves](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2011/01/stories)). The chapter definition gives the coder's rule: a redemption sequence "marks a transition in a life narrative account from an emotionally negative scene to a positive outcome or attribution about the self," while contamination is coded when "the negative affect overwhelms, destroys, or erases the effects of the preceding positivity" ([McAdams and McLean, Narrative Identity chapter, edX PDF](https://prod-edxapp.edx-cdn.org/assets/courseware/v1/b359406bfd0a296f41392387a0807ef9/asset-v1:SmithX+PSY374x+1T2017+type@asset+block/Wk2__Narrative_Identity__McAdams___McLean__2013__.pdf)). Later work ties redemption to psychological maturity, identity maturity and sobriety, and to prosocial behavior, with modest rank-order stability across time ([narrative identity and midlife transition study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15283488.2023.2258152); [APA Monitor](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2011/01/stories)).

Two cautions belong on the page. The associations are correlational, and the redemption arc is culturally freighted: McAdams himself notes that stories of agency and redemption "tend to be highly favored, at least in American society" ([McAdams 2019, First We Invented Stories](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~drkelly/McAdamsFirstWeStoriesThenTheyChangedUs2019.pdf)).

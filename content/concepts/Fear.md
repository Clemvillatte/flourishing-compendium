Fear is the emotion that mobilizes defense against threat. In the account of Öhman and Mineka, it is the central motive state of an evolved module for fear elicitation and fear learning: a behavioral, mental and neural system tailored to the potentially life-threatening situations of human evolutionary history.

> "An evolved module for fear elicitation and fear learning with 4 characteristics is proposed, (a) The fear module is preferentially activated in aversive contexts by stimuli that are fear relevant in an evolutionary perspective, (b) Its activation to such stimuli is automatic, (c) It is relatively impenetrable to cognitive control, (d) It originates in a dedicated neural circuitry, centered on the amygdala." (Öhman and Mineka 2001, [Fears, phobias, and preparedness, Psychological Review 108(3): 483-522](http://people.whitman.edu/~herbrawt/classes/390/Ohman.pdf), abstract)

### A proposed account

Öhman and Mineka describe the proposed module as activating defensive behavior, such as immobility or fight-flight, together with bodily responses and feelings. They identify avoidance and escape as characteristic motivations of fear.

Stimulus control concerns arranging environmental cues so that a target behavior occurs in their presence, while competing behaviors occur elsewhere ([Bootzin, 1972, p. 395](https://www.med.upenn.edu/cbti/assets/user-content/documents/Bootzin%201972.pdf)). Bootzin applied this approach to insomnia by trying to establish the bed and bedroom as cues for sleep ([p. 395](https://www.med.upenn.edu/cbti/assets/user-content/documents/Bootzin%201972.pdf)).

> To achieve stimulus control, the environment is structured so that the target response occurs contingent upon the presence of particular stimuli; and competing responses are not allowed to occur in their presence.
>
> Richard R. Bootzin, “Stimulus Control Treatment for Insomnia,” [p. 395](https://www.med.upenn.edu/cbti/assets/user-content/documents/Bootzin%201972.pdf).

### Treatment and mechanism

Bootzin's original report was a case study, and he cautioned that improvement might have resulted from factors other than stimulus control ([pp. 395–396](https://www.med.upenn.edu/cbti/assets/user-content/documents/Bootzin%201972.pdf)). A later systematic review found evidence of benefit for insomnia compared with control conditions, while judging the included studies generally poor in quality ([Verreault et al., 2024, Summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jsr.14008)). That review also challenged the proposed conditioning mechanism and urged caution because of bias and inconsistencies in its network meta-analysis ([Summary](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jsr.14008)).

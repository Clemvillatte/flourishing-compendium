Granovetter is the compendium's shorthand for Mark Granovetter's 1973 paper "The Strength of Weak Ties", the argument that acquaintances matter more than friendships for information, mobility and cohesion. Its tool is the analysis of social networks, linking micro and macro levels of sociological theory.

> "It is argued that the degree of overlap of two individuals' friendship networks varies directly with the strength of their tie to one another. The impact of this principle on diffusion of influence and information, mobility opportunity, and community organization is explored. Stress is laid on the cohesive power of weak ties." (Granovetter, [The Strength of Weak Ties](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~golbeck/INST633o/granovetterTies.pdf), American Journal of Sociology 78(6), 1973, abstract)

### The argument

Strong ties run inside circles that already know each other, so what a close friend knows, their friends tend to know too. A weak tie reaches into a different circle, and removing it does more damage to how far information can travel. From the individual's point of view, weak ties are an important resource in making possible mobility opportunity; from the macroscopic vantage, they play a role in effecting social cohesion.

Multiple Code Theory (MCT), developed by the psychoanalyst Wilma Bucci, holds that emotional and other information is processed in three systems. A paper in the Journal of Psycholinguistic Research describes them: "(a) the subsymbolic system that processes the continuous flow of emotional and sensory stimuli in global and analogical modes... (b) the nonverbal symbolic system that creates images and representations from the ongoing flow of subsymbolic experience; and (c) the verbal symbolic system that connects and translates these images and representations into words" ([Epistemological Foundation for the Use of the Linguistic Measures of the Referential Process, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10936-025-10149-1?fromPaywallRec=false)). An introduction to a journal issue says Bucci developed MCT in light of advances in cognitive science, incorporating a distinction between nonsymbolic and symbolic representations, "including both imagery and language (Bucci, 1997)" ([Introduction to the Issue, Journal of Psycholinguistic Research](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10936-025-10190-0.pdf)).

### The referential process

The systems are linked by what Bucci calls the referential process, defined as "the function of connecting the continuous and complex flow of emotional and sensory experience to mental images and linguistic expressions, thus making it communicable to others". It runs through three phases: arousal, symbolizing (narrative), and reflecting or reorganizing. In the arousal phase "the emotional activation is strong but not well defined or verbalized"; in the last, once the affect is "sufficiently contained", there is "an opportunity for a reorganizing phase" in which new connections and schemas can form ([Dreaming in quarantine, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8451209/)).

### Emotion schemas

Emotion schemas are "clusters of representations of events incorporating similar bodily, sensory and motoric processes activated in relation to different people in different contexts" ([Connecting emotions and words, Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11097-015-9417-z?error=cookies_not_supported&code=4023bdc9-c92f-4c33-8d11-5075964b23a8)). Painful schemas can be "consistently activated at the subsymbolic level, which may remain disconnected from the symbolic levels" (Springer paper above).

### Clinical use and measurement

Referential Activity, first introduced by Bucci and Freedman (1978), measures "the degree to which a speaker or writer is connected to non-verbal experience, including affect" ([Research in Psychotherapy PDF](https://www.researchinpsychotherapy.org/index.php/rpsy/article/download/122/106)). Wikipedia says MCT's model of psychopathology is "based primarily on dissociation", with traumatic experience leaving subsymbolic parts of schemas disconnected from symbolic codes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_Code_Theory)).

### Limits

The sources I opened are mostly written by Bucci or by researchers using her measures; I found no independent critique. The seed phrase "that therapy must connect" is the theory's clinical emphasis: a good session "would entail the activation of the RP" (Springer paper above), not a proven requirement.

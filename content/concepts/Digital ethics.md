Digital ethics asks how we should live and act in a world shaped by information and communication technologies. The most developed philosophical treatment in my sources is Luciano Floridi's, who calls the field information ethics. The publisher describes his book as "the first philosophical monograph entirely and exclusively dedicated to Information Ethics", whose subject is "the ethical impact of ICTs on human life and society" ([Oxford Academic, book abstract](https://academic.oup.com/book/35378)).

### A three-part model

Floridi starts with a simple picture of a moral agent he calls Alice: "Alice can avail herself of some information (information as a resource) to generate some other information (information as a product), and in so doing affect her informational environment (information as target)." Each part has its own questions. Resource concerns include the digital divide and the reliability of sources, product concerns include accountability, plagiarism and deception, and environment concerns include privacy, security and censorship ([Floridi, chapter 2](https://academic.oup.com/book/35378/chapter/301301333)).

### Beyond single technologies

He argues that treating the subject one technology at a time, as in computer, internet or robot ethics, misses "the more fundamental phenomenon of information in all its varieties". His proposal is to see information ethics as "primarily an environmental ethics, the main concern of which is (or should be) the ecological management and well-being of the whole infosphere" ([chapter 2](https://academic.oup.com/book/35378/chapter/301301333)). In his terms this is a macroethics, "a theoretical, field-independent, applicable ethics", as against a microethics, "a practical, field-dependent, applied, and professional ethics".

The chapter ends with a hope that bears on flourishing: "a pressing task is to formulate an information ethics that can treat the world of data, information, and knowledge, with their relevant life-cycles, as a new environment, the infosphere, in which human beings, as informational organisms, may be flourishing" ([chapter 2](https://academic.oup.com/book/35378/chapter/301301333)).

### Shared responsibility

Later in the book, Floridi discusses "ontic trust, the hypothetical pact among all agents and patients presupposed by IE" ([chapter 15 abstract](https://academic.oup.com/book/35378/chapter-abstract/301304711)). The idea spreads responsibility across all agents, which suggests that online relationships are a shared concern and not only a matter for individual users. That reading is the page's own and is not drawn from the abstract.

### Scope

Floridi's own term is information ethics, and my label "digital ethics" is a looser one. The page draws on one full chapter and several chapter abstracts. The chapter on privacy and the one on distributed morality, which bear most on relationships, were not read in full.

Authenticity is the congruent relationship between an entity's external characteristics and its internal values — being, outwardly, what one inwardly is ([Frontiers in Psychology, "The Essence of Authenticity"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.629654/full)). The modern concept has two parents who agree more than their literatures admit: Rogers' congruence — the match between the experience felt and its symbolization, such that the person no longer maintains a front ([Rogers and Heidegger on the self](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/3953/395354217005/html/)) — and Heidegger's *Eigentlichkeit*, authenticity as owning one's ownmost possibilities rather than drifting in the anonymous "they" ([[E034 — Practise authenticity (Eigentlichkeit)]]).

The mechanism is the removal of the double life: maintaining a presented self costs attention and produces the anxiety of exposure ([[D101 — Drop pretense — stop standing on tiptoe]]; [[D185 — Prefer being to seeming]] is the Daoist form — the uncarved block again). The compendium's caution cuts the other way too: authenticity is not spontaneity-ism (saying whatever one feels) — the authentic person still chooses what to express, from the real self rather than from the audience ([[D086 — Don't sell your nature for glory]]; [[R035 — Practise pono]] is the Hawaiian cognate — rightness and balance).

The distinction to hold: authenticity is a relation between inner and outer, not a fixed "true self" to be discovered once — the inner side is also revised by living ([[P063 — Treat your self-concept as revisable]] is its fallibilist partner).

An example at its smallest: one commitment made this week in one's own words rather than the inherited ones — then noticed: heavier or lighter?

The traditions that question its premise are also its kin: [[Anatta]] and [[Self-as-context]] are rival accounts of the self whose existence authenticity assumes.

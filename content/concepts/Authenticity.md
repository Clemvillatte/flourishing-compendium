Authenticity is the congruent relationship between what a person presents outwardly and what they hold inwardly. A recent review puts the definitional core in one line:

> "In brief, authenticity as consistency is the congruous relationship between an entity's external characteristics and its internal values." (Dammann et al. 2020, [Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.629654/full))

The modern concept has two parents who agree more than their literatures admit: Rogers' congruence, the match between the experience felt and its symbolization, such that the person no longer maintains a front ([Rogers and Heidegger on the self](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/3953/395354217005/html/)), and Heidegger's *Eigentlichkeit*, authenticity as owning one's ownmost possibilities rather than drifting in the anonymous "they".

### What congruence removes

On my reading, the mechanism is the removal of the double life: maintaining a presented self costs attention and produces the anxiety of exposure. The Daoist form of the same move is preferring being to seeming. The caution cuts the other way too: authenticity is not spontaneity-ism, saying whatever one feels. The authentic person still chooses what to express, from the real self rather than from the audience.

### Not a fixed "true self"

The distinction to hold: authenticity is a relation between inner and outer, not a fixed "true self" to be discovered once. The inner side is also revised by living; treating the self-concept as revisable is the fallibilist partner of this concept.

### In everyday life

One commitment made this week in one's own words rather than the inherited ones, then noticed: heavier or lighter? (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

The traditions that question its premise are also its kin: [[Anatta]] and [[Self-as-context]] are rival accounts of the self whose existence authenticity assumes.

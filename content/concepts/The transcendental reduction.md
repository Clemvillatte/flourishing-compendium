“The transcendental reduction” — Husserl, Cartesian Meditations.

The transcendental reduction is Husserl's name for the systematic analysis of the intentional correlation between subjectivity and world: the move from simply living among things to studying how those things come to appear to consciousness. It begins with the epoché, the bracketing of the natural attitude, the everyday stance that takes the world as simply there. The Stanford Encyclopedia's account of the turn:

> "But for this investigation to be possible, we cannot simply continue to live in the natural attitude. We keep it, in order to be able to investigate it, but we bracket its validity. It is this procedure that is known by the name of *epoché*." (Dan Zahavi, [SEP: Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

### What the bracketing does

The reduction does not deny the world. Its aim, in the same account, is to suspend a dogmatic attitude, not reality itself:

> "The purpose of the epoché is not to doubt, neglect, abandon, or exclude reality from our research; rather its aim is to suspend or neutralize a certain dogmatic *attitude* towards reality. We must take a step back from our naive and unexamined immersion in the world and thematize the fact that reality is always revealed and examined from some perspective or another." ([SEP: Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

What is left standing after the bracket is the field of intentional acts and their contents, which the reduction then examines for its structures. The relation of the two steps:

> "Strictly speaking, the epoché can be seen as the first step towards what Husserl terms the *transcendental reduction*, which is his name for the systematic analysis of the intentional correlation between subjectivity and world." ([SEP: Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

The seed names the Cartesian Meditations, where Husserl ties the reduction to Descartes' method explicitly:

> "Phenomenological reduction, as exclusion of all positing of existence, is nothing other than the radical carrying out of the Cartesian epoché." (Husserl, Cartesian Meditations §16, as quoted in [Philopedia: Edmund Husserl](https://philopedia.org/philosophers/edmund-gustav-albrecht-husserl/))

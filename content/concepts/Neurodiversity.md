Neurodiversity is a concept that treats neurological differences such as autism, ADHD and dyslexia as human variation. Singer's stated aim was to balance the medical paradigm, which emphasised the need to 'cure' such conditions, with "sociological, even adaptive models of difference in human functioning" ([Birkbeck, Judy Singer](https://www.bbk.ac.uk/about-us/fellows/judy-singer)). The compendium's seed citation is Judy Singer, 1998. Singer, an Australian sociologist, wrote an honours thesis at the University of Technology, Sydney, titled "Odd People In: The Birth of Community Amongst People on the Autistic Spectrum: A Personal Exploration of a New Social Movement Based on Neurological Diversity" ([Singer, 1998, as described by Edinburgh Library](https://discovered.ed.ac.uk/discovery/fulldisplay/alma9924298553202466/44UOE_INST:44UOE_VU2)). In her own later account she quotes the line from her related book chapter that is credited with introducing the word:

> "For me, the key significance of the 'Autistic Spectrum' lies in its call for and anticipation of a politics of Neurological Diversity, or 'Neurodiversity.'" (Singer, quoted in her essay [NeuroDiversity: The Birth of an Idea](http://dickyricky.com/books/psych/NeuroDiversity%20-%20The%20Birth%20of%20an%20Idea%20-%20Judy%20Singer.pdf), a copy of the text hosted on a third-party site)

### Who coined it is disputed

Attribution to Singer alone is contested. Botha and colleagues write that "the terms 'neurological diversity' and 'neurodiversity' were first printed in 1997 and 1998, respectively, in the work of the journalist Harvey Blume, who himself attributed them not to Singer but rather to the online community of autistic people," and that the concept was "collectively theorised and developed by autistic activists, primarily in the autistic community email list Independent Living in the mid-1990s" ([Botha et al., 2024, Autism](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/13623613241237871)). The same paper says Singer's thesis was revised into "a briefer book chapter, published in 1999," so the year attached to the chapter varies between sources. The National Autistic Society reaches a middle position: "It is probably more accurate to say that Singer was the first use the word and describe the concept in an academic context" ([National Autistic Society](https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/identity/the-neurodiversity-movement)).

### In the compendium

The linked principles, MO067 on ADHD and motivation and BE073 on behavioural symptoms of ADHD, apply the word to ADHD. Singer's thesis centred on the autistic spectrum, and none of the sources opened here discuss ADHD as such, so the extension to those principles is the compendium's own.

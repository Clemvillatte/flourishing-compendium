The witness, in Śaṅkara's Advaita Vedānta, is consciousness in its role as the presence by which experience is known. Śaṅkara distinguishes witnessing consciousness from the mind, mental qualities and cognitive modes, and says consciousness is not composed of physical materials whereas the mind is composed of inert subtle matter ([Neil Dalal, SEP, Śaṅkara §3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/)). Witnessing consciousness always accompanies cognition (BṛUBh 1.4.10; KeUBh 1.2, 2.4) ([SEP, Śaṅkara §3.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/)).

> "From the standpoint of individual experience, witnessing consciousness is the locus of one’s subjective being, the basic sense of self-existence, and the foundation of self-identity. It is the glue that unifies disparate forms of experience like waking, dreaming, and different modes of perception, and it accounts for memory and diachronic personal identity (BrSūBh 2.3.31; BṛUBh 4.3.7)."
> — [Neil Dalal, SEP, Śaṅkara §3.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/)

Śaṅkara is careful that the witness is not a second mind watching the first. It is not itself a meta-function such as inner sense, higher-order cognition or deliberate introspective awareness, and it is not a separate substantial entity with agency; it is intransitive and receptive, the passive witnessing itself (BṛUBh 1.4.10; TaiUBh 2.1.1; KeUBh 2.4) ([SEP, Śaṅkara §3.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/)).

That account is provisional. From the absolute order, Śaṅkara strips consciousness of its relational properties, including its status as witness ([SEP, Śaṅkara §3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/)).

The ecological self, in Arne Naess's deep ecology, is the self understood through its constitutive relations with the living world, not only through its relations with other humans. Naess's own statement, from his 1986 lecture "Self-realization", as quoted in Bill Devall's essay:

> "We may be said to be in, of, and for nature from our very beginning. Society and human relations are important, but our self is richer in its constitutive relations." (Naess, "Self-realization", 1986, quoted in Devall, [The Ecological Self](https://social-ecology.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Devall-Ecological-Self.pdf))

The same passage continues: these relations include relations with "our home bioregion, or with plants and animals which co-inhabit our living space".

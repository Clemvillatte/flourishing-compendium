“Distinguishing appearance from reality” — Plato, Republic.

Plato's Republic gives the classic version of the distinction in two images. In Book VI Socrates cuts a line into visible and intelligible sections: "the first section in the sphere of the visible consists of images. And by images I mean, in the first place, shadows, and in the second place, reflections in water and in solid, smooth and polished bodies and the like" (Plato, [Republic VI, Jowett translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1497/pg1497.txt)). The sections are ranked by "clearness and want of clearness", and "the copy is to the original as the sphere of opinion is to the sphere of knowledge".

Book VII tells the same story as a cave. Prisoners who "see only their own shadows, or the shadows of one another, which the fire throws on the opposite wall of the cave" take themselves to be "naming what was actually before them":

> To them, I said, the truth would be literally nothing but the shadows of the images. (Plato, [Republic VII, Jowett translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1497/pg1497.txt))

Turning round is painful: the released prisoner "will suffer sharp pains; the glare will distress him" (Plato, [Republic VII, Jowett translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1497/pg1497.txt)).

The compendium pairs the Republic's images with the Stoic discipline of seeing impressions as appearances, and with cognitive findings that vividness gets mistaken for accuracy, frequency or probability. The distinction becomes a check to run on one's own experience, including the moods and biases that recolor it.

“Moral courage research” — Hannah, Avolio and Walumbwa, 2011.

Sean Hannah, Bruce Avolio and Fred Walumbwa gave the research field a working definition, building on earlier accounts of courage ([Hannah, Avolio and Walumbwa, 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6EE546A20E6A106968CDF45F317676B1/S1052150X00004899a.pdf/relationships-between-authentic-leadership-moral-courage-and-ethical-and-pro-social-behaviors.pdf)):

> ...we define moral courage as follows: 1) a malleable character strength, that 2) provides the requisite conation needed to commit to personal moral principles, 3) under conditions where the actor is aware of the objective danger involved in supporting those principles, 4) that enables the willing endurance of that danger, 5) in order to act ethically or resist pressure to act unethically as required to maintain those principles.

The construct earns its keep where judgment and action come apart. The authors put the problem plainly ([Hannah, Avolio and Walumbwa, 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6EE546A20E6A106968CDF45F317676B1/S1052150X00004899a.pdf/relationships-between-authentic-leadership-moral-courage-and-ethical-and-pro-social-behaviors.pdf)):

> We suggest that the conation to act ethically, as represented by moral courage, will help explain why one actor will step up and act while another who arrives at the same ethical judgment in the same situation will stand by and fail to act.

### What the field study found

The paper's evidence came from a four-month field study in a military context ([Hannah, Avolio and Walumbwa, 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6EE546A20E6A106968CDF45F317676B1/S1052150X00004899a.pdf/relationships-between-authentic-leadership-moral-courage-and-ethical-and-pro-social-behaviors.pdf)):

> Results from this four-month field study demonstrated that authentic leadership was positively related to followers' displays of moral courage. Further, followers' moral courage fully mediated the effects of authentic leadership on followers' ethical and pro-social behaviors.

So courage sat between leadership and behaviour: it carried the effect. The study sits inside a larger theory the authors credit to a companion paper, "moral conation, the impetus to act with moral purpose", which they propose stems from moral courage, moral efficacy, and moral psychological ownership ([Hannah, Avolio and Walumbwa, 2011](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6EE546A20E6A106968CDF45F317676B1/S1052150X00004899a.pdf/relationships-between-authentic-leadership-moral-courage-and-ethical-and-pro-social-behaviors.pdf)). Two people can share a judgment; what separates them is whether the ownership, the confidence, and the endurance line up behind it. The definition's first clause, "a malleable character strength", is the one practitioners should notice: on this account courage is trainable, not a fixed endowment.

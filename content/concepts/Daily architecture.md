Daily architecture is this compendium's label for structuring the day around practice: a morning intention, cue-based micro-practices through the day, an evening review. The label names the point of the structure: a day with no architecture makes each practice a fresh decision, while a day with one makes the practices the default.

### The monastic anchor

The Buddhist monastic day is the schedule: the training is the day's structure itself. The step-by-step training in the Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta builds the day from moderation in the base activities:

> "Be one who is moderate in eating. Considering it appropriately, take your food not playfully, nor for intoxication, nor for putting on bulk, nor for beautification, but simply for the survival & continuance of this body, for ending its afflictions, for the support of the holy life." ([MN 107](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN107.html))

And the night is divided into watches of wakefulness:

> "Be one who is devoted to wakefulness. During the day, sitting & pacing back & forth, cleanse your mind of any qualities that would hold the mind in check. During the first watch of the night [dusk to 10 p.m.], sitting & pacing back & forth, cleanse your mind of any qualities that would hold the mind in check. During the second watch of the night [10 p.m. to 2 a.m.], reclining on your right side, take up the lion's posture... During the last watch of the night [2 a.m. to dawn], sitting & pacing back & forth, cleanse your mind of any qualities that would hold the mind in check." ([MN 107](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN107.html))

### The Stoic anchor

The Stoic evening audit is the other pole. Seneca's practice of pleading his cause before himself once the lamp is taken out:

> "I pass the whole day in review before myself, and repeat all that I have said and done: I conceal nothing from myself, and omit nothing." (Seneca, *On Anger* 3.36, [Aubrey Stewart translation](https://www.stoicsource.com/seneca/on-anger/3.36/))

The evening review closes the loop: the day's evidence examined and used to adjust the next day's structure, which is why the tradition pairs the morning preparation with the evening account.

### The distinction to hold

Architecture is not rigidity. The structure serves the practice and is redesigned when it stops serving it; the point is a day that supports the person, not a person who serves the schedule. Its contemplative cognates are [[Ritual]] (structure as practice) and the [[The middle path]]-paced schedule of [[The returning practice]]'s micro-resets through the day.

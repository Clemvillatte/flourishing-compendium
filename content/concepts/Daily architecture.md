Daily architecture is this compendium's label for structuring the day around practice: a morning intention, cue-based micro-practices through the day, an evening review ([[K242 — Daily architecture]]). Its two classical anchors: the Buddhist monastic day, in which the training is the schedule — moderation in eating, the watches of the night devoted to wakefulness ([MN 107, Gaṇakamoggallāna Sutta](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN107.html)) — and the Stoic evening audit, Seneca's practice of summoning the mind to account: "I retrace my entire day and take myself to account for my misdeeds and shortcomings. Nothing is hidden; nothing is omitted" (Seneca, *On Anger* 3.36.1-3, [translation](https://www.stoicsource.com/seneca/on-anger/3.36/)).

The mechanism: structure externalizes intention. A day with no architecture makes each practice a fresh decision; a day with one makes the practices the default. The evening review closes the loop — the day's evidence examined and used to adjust the next day's structure, which is why the tradition pairs the morning preparation with the evening account ([[S065 — Practise self-examination daily]]; [[S120 — End the evening with review]]).

The distinction to hold: architecture is not rigidity. The structure serves the practice and is redesigned when it stops serving it ([[K241 — Seasonal redesign]]) — the point is a day that supports the person, not a person who serves the schedule.

An example at its smallest: the two-minute evening audit — one line each: what went well, what was avoided, tomorrow's first act.

Its contemplative cognates are [[Ritual]] (structure as practice) and the [[The middle path]]-paced schedule of [[The returning practice]]'s micro-resets through the day.

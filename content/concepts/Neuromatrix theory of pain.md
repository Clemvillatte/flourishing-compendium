Ronald Melzack's 2001 paper states the theory: "pain is a multidimensional experience produced by characteristic 'neurosignature' patterns of nerve impulses generated by a widely distributed neural network - the 'body-self neuromatrix' - in the brain. These neurosignature patterns may be triggered by sensory inputs, but they may also be generated independently of them" ([Melzack, Pain and the neuromatrix in the brain, J Dent Educ 2001, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11780656/)). The abstract adds: "Pain, then, is produced by the output of a widely distributed neural network in the brain rather than directly by sensory input evoked by injury, inflammation, or other pathology".

### What feeds the neuromatrix

In an earlier paper, Melzack lists the inputs: sensory inputs from skin, viscera and other somatic receptors; visual and other inputs that shape the cognitive interpretation of the situation; "phasic and tonic cognitive and emotional inputs from other areas of the brain"; intrinsic neural inhibitory modulation; and the body's stress-regulation systems, "including cytokines as well as the endocrine, autonomic, immune and opioid systems" ([Melzack, From the gate to the neuromatrix, Pain 1999, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10491980/)). He says the theory moves away from "the psychophysical concept that seeks a simple one-to-one relationship between injury and pain".

### Why it was proposed

The 2001 abstract says chronic pain syndromes are "often characterized by severe pain associated with little or no discernible injury or pathology" and that the relationship with chronic stress "is poorly understood", so the theory offers "a new conceptual framework". A handbook chapter summary notes it was proposed "largely on the basis of phantom limb phenomena" ([Wiley, The Neuromatrix in Behavioral Medicine](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118453940.ch35)).

### Reading the seed note

The seed says pain is "not a simple sensory input". That is accurate to the abstract, which says somatic sensory input "is only a part" of the influences on the output pattern. The neuromatrix is described as "genetically determined and modified by sensory experience", so the theory does not treat pain as imaginary or independent of the body. It is a theory; I did not open an independent test of its claims.

The restless world is the idea that reality is not a collection of finished things but an ongoing activity. Nothing holds still long enough to be pinned down as merely what it is; what a thing is, is what it is doing next. The oldest and most compressed statement of it is Heraclitus:

> "You cannot step twice into the same rivers; for fresh waters are ever flowing in upon you."
> — [Heraclitus, fragment 12, trans. John Burnet, Early Greek Philosophy, Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_(annotated))

A companion fragment goes further: "We step and do not step into the same rivers; we are and are not." The river is the same and is not the same, and so is the person standing in it. Identity persists through change rather than despite it — the banks hold while the waters pass.

### Process before substance

The tradition that took this seriously is process philosophy, whose most systematic figure is Alfred North Whitehead. Where classical metaphysics begins with enduring substances that have properties, Whitehead begins with events:

> "'Actual entities' — also termed 'actual occasions' — are the final real things of which the world is made up. There is no going behind actual entities to find anything more real."
> — [Whitehead, Process and Reality (1929), quoted in Larval Subjects](https://larvalsubjects.wordpress.com/2009/03/07/objectiles-and-actual-occasions/)

Each actual occasion is a moment of becoming that perishes as it completes itself and becomes a fact for what follows. Whitehead's principle of process states the consequence plainly: how an entity becomes constitutes what it is; its being is constituted by its becoming. On this view restlessness is not a defect of the world but its basic constitution.

The practical corollary for a person: expecting stillness from a restless world is a category error. What can be aimed at is not an end to change but a workable relation to it.

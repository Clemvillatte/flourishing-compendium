**Processing Fluency Theory** — Reber — ease of processing is experienced as pleasant and judged as true; beauty follows fluency.

Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman's 2004 review makes the perceiver's processing, and not the object's features, the engine of aesthetic pleasure. Fluency is itself hedonically marked, and the same ease feeds truth judgments, which is why the theory reaches past aesthetics into epistemology.

> "The more fluently perceivers can process an object, the more positive their aesthetic response." (Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman 2004, [PDF](https://tiffaning.com/files/Reber%20et%20al_2004_Processing%20fluency%20and%20aesthetic%20pleasure.pdf))
>
> "Second, processing fluency is itself hedonically marked and high fluency is subjectively experienced as positive, as indicated by psychophysiological findings." (Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman 2004)
>
> "In fact, there is growing empirical evidence that people use a common source for evaluations of both beauty and truth—processing fluency." (Reber, Schwarz and Winkielman 2004)

The heading names the background frequencies of the world: what has usually happened, which any new claim has to be weighed against before it is believed. The seed line points to Hume's Enquiry, where the section on probability gives the classical statement:

> "All probability, then, supposes an opposition of experiments and observations, where the one side is found to overbalance the other, and to produce a degree of evidence, proportioned to the superiority. A hundred instances or experiments on one side, and fifty on another, afford a doubtful expectation of any event; though a hundred uniform experiments, with only one that is contradictory, reasonably beget a pretty strong degree of assurance." (Hume, Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, VI)

On Hume's account the tally is kept not by reason but by habit. "Custom, then, is the great guide of human life", the principle that "makes us expect, for the future, a similar train of events with those which have appeared in the past".

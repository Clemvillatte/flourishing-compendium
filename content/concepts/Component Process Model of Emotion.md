The Component Process Model of Emotion is Klaus Scherer's account of emotion as an unfolding of appraisal checks. Emotion, in this framework, is an episode of synchronized change across the organism's subsystems, produced by a sequence of evaluations of an event.

> "In the framework of the component process model, emotion is defined as an episode of interrelated, synchronized changes in the states of all or most of the five organismic subsystems in response to the evaluation of an external or internal stimulus event as relevant to major concerns of the organism." (Scherer, [Appraisal Considered as a Process of Multilevel Sequential Checking](https://acousticslab.org/RECA293/293Files/293Downloads/Scherer2001_AppraisalEmotion.pdf), in Appraisal Processes in Emotion: Theory, Methods, Research, Oxford University Press, pp. 92-120, 2001, p. 93, section "The Component Process Model")

### The sequential checks

The appraisal objectives are four questions the organism puts to the event: "1. How relevant is this event for me? Does it directly affect me or my social reference group? (relevance) 2. What are the implications or consequences of this event and how do these affect my well-being and my immediate or long-term goals? (implications) 3. How well can I cope with or adjust to these consequences? (coping potential) 4. What is the significance of this event with respect to my self-concept and to social norms and values? (normative significance)" ([Scherer 2001, p. 94, section "The Nature of the Stimulus Evaluation Checks"](https://acousticslab.org/RECA293/293Files/293Downloads/Scherer2001_AppraisalEmotion.pdf)). The chapter presents this as "the sequential check theory of emotion differentiation as part of a dynamic model of emotion (the component process model of emotion)" ([Scherer 2001, p. 93](https://acousticslab.org/RECA293/293Files/293Downloads/Scherer2001_AppraisalEmotion.pdf)).

(Sonnentag and Fritz, 2007), during off-time predicts next-day engagement and long-term health.

The construct comes from the Recovery Experience Questionnaire, developed to assess how individuals unwind and recuperate from work during leisure time. Psychological detachment from work is the first of four recovery experiences the measure differentiates, alongside relaxation, mastery and control — the sense of being mentally away from work rather than still turning it over. In validation studies with a total sample of 930, the four experiences were distinct factors, and relations with job stressors and psychological well-being were moderate.

> "Confirmatory factor analyses with a calibration and a cross-validation sample (total N=930) showed that four recovery experiences can be differentiated: psychological detachment from work, relaxation, mastery, and control." (Sonnentag & Fritz, 2007, Journal of Occupational Health Psychology 12(3), 204–221, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17638488/))

The short 4-item measures were built for longitudinal and diary research, which is where the claims about off-time detachment predicting next-day states are tested.

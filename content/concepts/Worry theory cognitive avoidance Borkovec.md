The avoidance theory of worry is Thomas Borkovec's account of what pathological worry is doing: predominantly verbal thought that can function as cognitive avoidance in response to threatening images, blocking the emotional processing those images would drive. The account is developed in Borkovec, Ray and Stöber's 1998 article ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf), Cognitive Therapy and Research 22(6):561-576).

> "Descriptively, worry involves a predominance of negatively valenced verbal thought activity. When we worry, we are talking to ourselves a lot about negative things, most often about negative events that we are afraid might happen in the future." (Borkovec, Ray and Stöber, [Worry: A cognitive phenomenon intimately linked to affective, physiological, and interpersonal behavioral processes](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf), Cognitive Therapy and Research 22(6):561-576, 1998, section "The Nature and Functions of Worry")

### The avoidance mechanism

The theory rests on a functional difference between thought and imagery that the article reports from other laboratories: "Verbal thoughts about emotional material elicit very little cardiovascular response, whereas images of the same material evoke significantly greater response" ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf)). On the article's account, verbal worry is remote from the affective and physiological systems that imagery reaches. The article also reports a treatment observation from the studies it reviews: "successful therapy changed the clients' thought/imagery ratios; they were no longer different from those of nonanxious people" ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf)). The cost follows from the same isolation: "It means that emotional processing will be inhibited, and thus negative emotional meaning and emotional disturbance will be maintained." ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf))

### Why the avoidance persists

If worry suppresses the felt part of anxiety, it can be self-rewarding: "If worry as an avoidance response does immediately suppress somatic/affective aspects of anxious experience, it may thereby be negatively reinforced." The article is candid that the how remains open: "At this point, how worry provides this suppression is speculative. It may involve the actual suppression of aversive images, but this seems unlikely... It is more likely that worrisome thinking is the immediate cognitive avoidance response to such images when they do occur."

### The honest limit

The avoidance account was offered with its suppression mechanism explicitly speculative, and the arousal evidence it cites was short-term laboratory work.

The avoidance theory of worry is Thomas Borkovec's account of what pathological worry is doing: a stream of negatively charged verbal thought that keeps feared material out of imagery, and by doing so keeps the fear from being processed. The theory's fullest statement is Borkovec, Ray and Stöber's 1998 chapter ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf), in Worry: New Perspectives on an Old Problem).

> "Descriptively, worry involves a predominance of negatively valenced verbal thought activity. When we worry, we are talking to ourselves a lot about negative things, most often about negative events that we are afraid might happen in the future." (Borkovec, Ray and Stöber, [The nature and functions of worry](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf), 1998)

### The avoidance mechanism

The theory rests on a functional difference between thought and imagery: "verbal thoughts about emotional material elicit very little cardiovascular response, whereas images of the same material evoke significantly greater response" ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf)). Verbal worry is therefore remote from the affective and physiological systems that imagery reaches, which is why "successful therapy changed the clients' thought/imagery ratios". The cost follows from the same isolation: "It means that emotional processing will be inhibited, and thus negative emotional meaning and emotional disturbance will be maintained." ([Borkovec, Ray and Stöber 1998](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/19832/1/Borkovec%20Ray%20St%C3%B6ber%20(1998)%20COTR.pdf))

### Why the avoidance persists

If worry suppresses the felt part of anxiety, it can be self-rewarding: "If worry as an avoidance response does immediately suppress somatic/affective aspects of anxious experience, it may thereby be negatively reinforced." The chapter is candid that the how remains open: "At this point, how worry provides this suppression is speculative. It may involve the actual suppression of aversive images, but this seems unlikely... It is more likely that worrisome thinking is the immediate cognitive avoidance response to such images when they do occur."

### The honest limit

The avoidance account was offered as a theory in progress, with its suppression mechanism explicitly speculative, and the arousal evidence was short-term laboratory work. The compendium pairs this account with the later research lines it links here, intolerance of uncertainty and metacognitive beliefs about worry, which ask different questions about what worry is for.

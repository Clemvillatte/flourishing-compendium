"Enriched experience" is my label for John Dewey's idea, in Art as Experience (1934), of "an experience": a stretch of life that comes to a fulfilled close. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Dewey's aesthetics says that for Dewey "aesthetic experience is experience in its integrity", something that can occur "in all quarters of life, not just in art" ([Leddy and Puolakka, SEP: Dewey's Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)).

### What makes it "an experience"

In "an experience", "the material of experience is fulfilled, consummated, and forms an integral whole", as "when a game is played through, a conversation is rounded out or when we finish 'that meal in a Paris restaurant'". The entry lists recurring features. The experience has a temporal structure, "dynamic because it takes time to complete", and it builds up, so that "the later phases build on earlier ones and carry them further rather than just mechanically following them." It is also intensive, condensing emotional and formal features into one experience ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)).

### The opposite

The entry contrasts this with the anesthetic. In Dewey's words, quoted there, "Things happen, but they are neither definitely included nor decisively excluded; we drift." Such stretches lack "genuine initiations and concludings", and there is "experience, but so slack and discursive that it is not an experience" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)).

### Scope

Dewey's term is "an experience", and "enriched experience" is my gloss. The entry says Dewey "never puts forth an explicit definition of aesthetic experience in terms of necessary and sufficient conditions", so the features above are recurring marks rather than a checklist. Only the encyclopedia entry was consulted for this page, and practical advice about enriching daily life is an extension for the reader to weigh.

Creative destruction is Joseph Schumpeter's term, from Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy (1942), for the process by which capitalism renews itself: new goods, methods of production, markets and forms of organization displace the old ones from within ([Schumpeter, ch. VII, The Process of Creative Destruction](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf)). His claim is not that markets occasionally break things, but that this churning is what the system is:

> "Capitalism, then, is by nature a form or method of economic change and not only never is but never can be stationary." (Schumpeter, [The Process of Creative Destruction, ch. VII](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf))

> "The opening up of new markets, foreign or domestic, and the organizational development from the craft shop and factory to such concerns as U.S. Steel illustrate the same process of industrial mutation—if I may use that biological term—that incessantly revolutionizes the economic structure from within, incessantly destroying the old one, incessantly creating a new one. This process of Creative Destruction is the essential fact about capitalism." (Schumpeter, [The Process of Creative Destruction, ch. VII](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf))

### Competition that strikes at foundations

Schumpeter's target is the textbook picture of competition as price-cutting among existing firms, a picture he says persists in economic analysis even though it is not that kind of competition which counts ([ibid.](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf)). In capitalist reality, he argues, "it is not that kind of competition which counts but the competition from the new commodity, the new technology, the new source of supply, the new type of organization", a competition that "strikes not at the margins of the profits and the outputs of the existing firms but at their foundations and their very lives" ([ibid.](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf)). Later in the chapter he calls this the "perennial gale of creative destruction" ([ibid.](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf)).

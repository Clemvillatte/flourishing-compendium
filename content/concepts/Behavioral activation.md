Behavioral activation (BA) is a structured psychotherapy for depression built on a single behavioural insight: action precedes and produces motivation, not the reverse. The term was first used around 1990 for the behavioural components of cognitive therapy; Jacobson and colleagues' 1996 component analysis then showed that the behavioural activation component alone matched the efficacy of the full cognitive-behavioural package, which launched BA as a stand-alone treatment ([Ekers et al., Cochrane review of behavioural activation therapies for depression](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6461437/); [Jacobson et al., 1996](https://www.personal.kent.edu/~dfresco/CBT_Readings/CPSP_Jacobson_et_al_BA.pdf)). The current canonical formulation is Martell, Dimidjian & Herman-Dunn's clinician's guide ([Cambridge, “Behavioural activation for depression”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/behavioural-activation-for-depression/EBC5FC0D9D550FFBE6F982B33BF91EE9)).

The mechanism it works on is the depression loop: low mood triggers withdrawal and avoidance; withdrawal removes the activities — people, places, mastery, pleasure — that once rewarded effort; life grows emptier, mood sinks further. BA interrupts the loop from the behaviour side: activity scheduling (plan specific activities at specific times — [[BE014 — Learn to use activity scheduling — plan specific activities at specific times]]), graded tasks and the five-minute rule to lower the barrier to starting ([[BE016 — Learn to use the just 5 minutes technique — lower the barrier to starting]]), and self-monitoring of the link between mood and what you actually did, so the pattern becomes data ([[AF036 — Learn to regulate through action — affect follows behaviour]]). Mastery and pleasure are scheduled deliberately, because competence-driven behaviour rebuilds motivation ([[BE018 — Learn to schedule mastery activities — competence-driven behaviour]]). Rumination is treated as avoidance to be replaced, not a problem to be analysed ([[CO048 — Distinguish worry from problem-solving — worry is not planning]]).

The distinction to hold: BA is not “fake it till you make it” positivity. Nothing is pretended — the bet is smaller and harder-headed: that engagement with life predicts well-being, and that the feeling follows the doing often enough to be worth acting on ([[S033 — Do not retreat from the world]]; [[D054 — Practice daily — small consistent effort compounds]]). It is the clinical sibling of Aristotle's habituation — become just by doing just acts ([[A004 — Become virtuous by doing virtuous acts]]) — and of the Stoic rule that the truths are tasks, not beliefs ([[B008 — Treat the truths as tasks, not beliefs]]).

An example at its smallest: the depressed afternoon planned as two items only — a twenty-minute walk and one message to a friend — written down the night before, done badly rather than skipped, and logged with the mood that followed.

The Buddhist ancestor is [[Right effort]] — the four efforts are action-first treatment of the mind in clinical clothes.

Behavioral activation (BA) is a structured psychotherapy for depression built on a single behavioural insight: action precedes and produces motivation, not the reverse. The term was first used around 1990 for the behavioural components of cognitive therapy; Jacobson and colleagues' 1996 component analysis then showed that the behavioural activation component alone matched the efficacy of the full cognitive-behavioural package, which launched BA as a stand-alone treatment. The Cochrane protocol states both points:

> "The term behavioural activation appears to have been used for the first time in 1990, as a description of the behavioural components in cognitive therapy (Hollon 1990)." ([Ekers et al., Cochrane protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6461437/))

> "Jacobson showed that the behavioural component of cognitive‐behavioural therapy (CBT) was as effective as the full package of CBT, and investigators developed a new and more comprehensive model of behavioural activation that would be amenable to dissemination (Jacobson 1996; Jacobson 2001)." ([Ekers et al., Cochrane protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6461437/))

The current canonical formulation is Martell, Dimidjian & Herman-Dunn's clinician's guide ([Cambridge, "Behavioural activation for depression"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/behavioural-activation-for-depression/EBC5FC0D9D550FFBE6F982B33BF91EE9)).

### The depression loop

The mechanism it works on is the depression loop: low mood triggers withdrawal and avoidance; withdrawal removes the activities (people, places, mastery, pleasure) that once rewarded effort; life grows emptier, mood sinks further. BA interrupts the loop from the behaviour side. Activity scheduling plans specific activities at specific times; graded tasks and the five-minute rule lower the barrier to starting; self-monitoring of the link between mood and what you actually did turns the pattern into data. Mastery and pleasure are scheduled deliberately, because competence-driven behaviour rebuilds motivation. Rumination is treated as avoidance to be replaced, not a problem to be analysed.

### Not "fake it till you make it"

The distinction to hold: BA is not "fake it till you make it" positivity. Nothing is pretended. The bet is smaller and harder-headed: that engagement with life predicts well-being, and that the feeling follows the doing often enough to be worth acting on. On my reading it is the clinical sibling of [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]]'s habituation, becoming just by doing just acts, and of the Stoic rule that the truths are tasks, not beliefs.

### In everyday life

The depressed afternoon planned as two items only, a twenty-minute walk and one message to a friend, written down the night before, done badly rather than skipped, and logged with the mood that followed. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

The Buddhist ancestor is [[Right effort]], the four efforts as action-first treatment of the mind in clinical clothes.

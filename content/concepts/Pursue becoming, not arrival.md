"Pursue becoming, not arrival" comes from Maslow's Toward a Psychology of Being. In the chapter on deficiency and growth motivation, he flags a problem with his own earlier framing:

> One major difficulty with this conception as so far presented is its somewhat static character. Self-actualization, since I have studied it mostly in older people, tends to be seen as an ultimate or final state of affairs, a far goal, rather than a dynamic process, active throughout life, Being, rather than Becoming. If we define growth as the various processes which bring the person toward ultimate self-actualization, then this conforms better with the observed fact that it is going on all the time in the life history. (Maslow, 1968, ch. 3)

The correction matters for how motivation itself is described. A coming-to-rest picture, in which desire quiets once satisfied, fits hunger better than it fits growth:

> Thus the coming-to-rest theory isn't adequate even for deficiency motivation. However, when we examine people who are predominantly growth-motivated, the coming-to-rest conception of motivation becomes completely useless. In such people gratification breeds increased rather than decreased motivation, heightened rather than lessened excitement. (Maslow, 1968, ch. 3)

His alternative is a direction rather than a finish line:

> Growth is instead a continued, more or less steady upward or forward development. (Maslow, 1968, ch. 3)

For the compendium's principles, the phrase marks this shift: the goal-shaped picture of flourishing, in which one arrives and then maintains, gives way to a process-shaped one in which the pursuit itself is the thing being described. Maslow's own caution supplies the check on the static reading: because he studied self-actualization mostly in older people, the arrival picture was partly a sampling artifact, and the dynamic reading fits what he observed across whole life histories. A person working from this concept is not asked where they have gotten to, but which way they are moving and whether gratification is breeding further motivation.

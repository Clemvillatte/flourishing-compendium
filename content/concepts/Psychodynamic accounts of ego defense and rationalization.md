the clinical tradition of mapping the self's disguises (Vaillant's defense-hierarchy research, as the modern cousin).

George Vaillant's decades with the Harvard Grant Study turned the psychodynamic list of defenses into a rated hierarchy, from psychotic through immature and neurotic to mature. His framing of the class itself:

> "The third coping category, like fever and leukocytosis, is involuntary. It entails deploying unconscious homeostatic mechanisms that reduce the disorganizing effects of sudden stress, DSM-5 offers a tentative hierarchy of defenses, from psychotic to immature to mature. The 70-year prospective Study of Development at Harvard provides a clinical validation of this hierarchy" (Vaillant 2011, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22034454/))

In his system, immature defenses include projection, schizoid fantasy, hypochondriasis, passive aggression, and acting out; the mature ones include altruism, humor, suppression, anticipation, and sublimation.

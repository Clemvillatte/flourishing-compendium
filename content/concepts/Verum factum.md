Verum factum is Giambattista Vico's principle that knowledge and making are one. As the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy states it:

> "His statement of the principle that verum et factum convertuntur, that 'the true and the made are...convertible,' or that 'the true is precisely what is made' (verum esse ipsum factum)." (Timothy Costelloe, "Giambattista Vico", Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

The principle grounds Vico's break with Descartes: given that "the norm of the truth is to have made it" (DA, 52), Vico reasons that clear and distinct ideas cannot be the source of truth, since "the mind does not make itself as it gets to know itself" ([Costelloe, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vico/)).

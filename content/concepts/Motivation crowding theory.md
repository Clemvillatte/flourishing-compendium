Frey and Jegen's 2001 survey states the effect: "external intervention via monetary incentives or punishments may undermine, and under different identifiable conditions strengthen, intrinsic motivation" ([Frey and Jegen, Motivation Crowding Theory, Journal of Economic Surveys 15(5), 589-611, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-6419.00150)). Weakening is crowding-out and strengthening is crowding-in. They conclude that crowding effects are "an empirically relevant phenomenon, which can, in specific cases, even dominate the traditional relative price effect".

### Evidence from psychology

A Frey summary for the Australian Productivity Commission says social psychologists "have empirically identified that external intervention, in the form of a reward, reduces individuals' intrinsic incentives". It leans on the meta-analysis of 128 studies from 1971 to 1997 by Deci, Koestner and Ryan (1999): tangible rewards "undermine intrinsic motivation for interesting tasks" in a "highly significant and very reliable way", with a "moderately large" effect. The effect is "stronger with monetary than with symbolic rewards" and "larger with expected than with unexpected rewards" ([Frey, Productivity Commission PDF](https://www.pc.gov.au/research/supporting/behavioural-economics/frey.pdf)). The same text says the effect holds when rewards are perceived as controlling.

### Economics and the debate

Festré and Garrouste (2015) note that "the empirical relevance of motivation crowding out is a controversial issue in economics and psychology", partly because two literatures grew from "different theoretical traditions" ([Theory and evidence in psychology and economics, EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/blajecsur/v_3a29_3ay_3a2015_3ai_3a2_3ap_3a339-356.htm)). A later review of altruistic crowding-out presents 13 behavioural economics models and concludes that the effect can be explained "in many (at least 13) different ways", so competing models remain open ([Review of behavioral economics models, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/joes.12606)).

### Reading the seed note

"Dependent origination" (paṭiccasamuppāda) is the early Buddhist teaching that things arise in dependence on conditions. No link in the chain of suffering stands on its own, so each can be interrupted by removing what it depends on. The canonical statement is the twelve-link chain of SN 12.1:

> "Ignorance is a requirement for choices. Choices are a requirement for consciousness. Consciousness is a requirement for name and form. Name and form are requirements for the six sense fields. The six sense fields are requirements for contact. Contact is a requirement for feeling. Feeling is a requirement for craving. Craving is a requirement for grasping. Grasping is a requirement for continued existence. Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth. Rebirth is a requirement for old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress to come to be."
>
> (SN 12.1, Paṭiccasamuppādasutta, tr. Bhikkhu Sujato, [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net/sn12.1/en/sujato))

The same sutta runs the chain in reverse, link by link, and closes each reversal with the formula "That is how this entire mass of suffering ceases." What the chain gives the compendium's principles is conditional structure: suffering is assembled step by step from conditions, which makes it something to be examined and altered rather than endured as a fact. Nagarjuna's Mulamadhyamakakarika later made this conditionality the basis of emptiness, the connection the related line below carries.

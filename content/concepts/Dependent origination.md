"Dependent origination" (paṭiccasamuppāda) is the early Buddhist teaching that things arise in dependence on conditions. The canonical statement is the twelve-link chain of SN 12.1:

> "Ignorance is a requirement for choices. Choices are a requirement for consciousness. Consciousness is a requirement for name and form. Name and form are requirements for the six sense fields. The six sense fields are requirements for contact. Contact is a requirement for feeling. Feeling is a requirement for craving. Craving is a requirement for grasping. Grasping is a requirement for continued existence. Continued existence is a requirement for rebirth. Rebirth is a requirement for old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress to come to be."
>
> (SN 12.1, Paṭiccasamuppādasutta, tr. Bhikkhu Sujato, [SuttaCentral](https://suttacentral.net/sn12.1/en/sujato))

The same sutta also describes the cessation of suffering: "That is how this entire mass of suffering ceases." SN 12.1 thus sets out conditions for the arising of suffering and conditions for its cessation.

Read forward, the chain says suffering has a structure: it is produced, link by link, by conditions that can be named. Read backward, it says the structure can be unwound: when ignorance fades and ceases with no residue left behind, choices cease, and with them the rest of the sequence down to old age and death. The practical edge is that craving and grasping, the links most open to interruption, sit in the middle of the chain, which is where the meditation traditions put their work.

### Nagarjuna's reading

Centuries later Nagarjuna made dependent origination the hinge of his Middle Way, opening his treatise by closing off the escape routes:

> "Neither from itself nor from another, / Nor from both, / Nor without a cause, / Does anything whatever, anywhere arise." (Mulamadhyamakakarika 1.1, [Garfield translation, Archive.org full text](https://archive.org/stream/NagarjunaTheFundamentalWisdomOfTheMiddleWay/Nagarjuna%20-%20The%20Fundamental%20Wisdom%20Of%20The%20Middle%20Way_djvu.txt))

Things arise, but only dependently, and that, he argues, is just what emptiness means:

> "Whatever is dependently co-arisen / That is explained to be emptiness. / That, being a dependent designation, / Is itself the middle way." (Mulamadhyamakakarika 24.18)

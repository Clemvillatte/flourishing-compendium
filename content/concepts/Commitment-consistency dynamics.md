“Commitment-consistency dynamics” — Cialdini, 2001.

Commitment and consistency is Cialdini's principle that people tend to behave in line with their previous actions and obligations. The classic demonstration is the foot-in-the-door tactic. Guadagno, Asher, Demaine and Cialdini summarise the tactic and its conditions:

> "A requester using the foot-in-the-door (FITD) tactic begins by gaining compliance with a small request and then advances to a related, larger request. Previous work has demonstrated that a strong preference for consistency among targets of the tactic can enhance the FITD effect." (Guadagno, Asher, Demaine & Cialdini, 2001, [Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167201277008))

The same paper's conclusion gives the individual-difference reading:

> "The authors conclude that high- and low-PFC individuals are likely to become more or less consistent with an action (respectively) when focused on the personal implications of that action." (Guadagno, Asher, Demaine & Cialdini, 2001, [Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167201277008))

The body-for-others is the body as it exists for another person's gaze, in Sartre's account in Being and Nothingness. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy does not give a passage on the body-for-others as such. It does give the account of the Look, from which the idea follows ([SEP, Sartre, section 4.3](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)).

### The Look

The entry asks the reader to imagine peeping through a keyhole, "pre-reflectively immersed and absorbed in the scene". Then footsteps sound, and "we have an involuntary apprehension of ourselves as an object in the eyes of another", a "pre-moral" experience of shame, "a shudder of recognition that we are the object that the other sees" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)). In the entry's reading, Sartre cares less about shame as a moral emotion than about the brief impression of shame as an event that discloses the for-itself's being-for-others ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)).

### What is constituted outside me

Sartre "describes the discovery that part of what I am is constituted outside me, in and through the look of another freedom". The entry adds that this holds whether or not one is mistaken on a given occasion, as when the floor creaks and no one is there ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)). Through this account Sartre treats the other person as a condition of one's own being, and not as a conclusion reached by argument.

### Conflict

Sartre's early view is bleak: "The essence of the relations between consciousness is not the Mitsein (being-with); it is conflict" (Being and Nothingness, 1943 [1956: 429], as quoted in the [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)). Social life is, on this view, an attempt to control the impact of the Look.

### Scope

The entry covers the Look and being-for-others, and it points readers to other sources on Sartre's treatment of embodiment. It does not supply Sartre's own wording on the body-for-others, and Being and Nothingness is cited at second hand. The entry reports Sartre's phenomenology, not evidence about wellbeing, and its bleak view of relations with others is described by the entry as Sartre's early position.

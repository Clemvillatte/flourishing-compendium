The insatiability of craving is Schopenhauer's structural claim about desire: wanting does not end with getting, because wanting keeps regenerating itself. In the Haldane-Kemp translation of The World as Will and Idea (the online text preserves some typographic quirks of the scanned edition):

> "All willing arises from want, therefore from deficiency, and therefore from suffering, The satisfaction of a wish ends it; yet for one wish that is satisfied there remain at least ten which are denied." (Schopenhauer, The World as Will and Idea, trans. Haldane and Kemp, [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_World_as_Will_and_Idea/Volume_1/Third_Book))

### No object closes the loop

The passage continues: the desire lasts long, the demands are infinite, the satisfaction short. "But even the final satisfaction is itself only apparent; every satisfied wish at once makes room for a new one; both are illusions; the one is known to be so, the other not yet." The image that follows is Schopenhauer's own: "No attained object of desire can give lasting satisfaction, but merely a fleeting gratification; it is like the alms thrown to the beggar, that keeps him alive to-day that his misery may be prolonged till the morrow."

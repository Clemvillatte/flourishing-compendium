Interpersonal psychoanalysis is a school of psychoanalytic theory and treatment based on the work of the American psychiatrist Harry Stack Sullivan (1892-1949). Sullivan's definition of personality, from The Interpersonal Theory of Psychiatry (1953), is the tradition's anchor:

> "the relatively enduring pattern of recurrent interpersonal situations which characterize a human life" (Sullivan, 1953, pp. 110-111, quoted in [Hopwood, Wright, Ansell & Pincus, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3675800/))

The researchers quoting this definition add that it makes the interpersonal situation the fundamental unit of analysis in interpersonal theory: events involving a self and other, associated with an affective experience ([Hopwood et al., 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3675800/)).

### What the analyst listens for

Sullivan believed that the details of a patient's interpersonal interactions with others can provide insight into the causes and cures of mental disorder. The work attends to personifications, a person's internalised views of self and others, and to selective inattention, which the overview describes as keeping threatening aspects of interpersonal relationships outside awareness ([Wikipedia, Interpersonal psychoanalysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interpersonal_psychoanalysis)).

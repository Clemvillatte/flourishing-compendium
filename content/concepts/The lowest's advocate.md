The lowest's advocate is the stance of the Matthew 25 tradition: the claim that the ultimate judge is met, identically, in the least regarded person present. The identification is stated twice, once in blessing and once in judgment:

> "And the King shall answer and say unto them, Verily I say unto you, Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me." (Matthew 25:40, KJV, [Sacred Texts Archive](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bib/kjv/mat025.htm))

### What the advocacy carries

The verse's mirror runs the other way too: "Inasmuch as ye did it not to one of the least of these, ye did it not to me" (Matthew 25:45). The teaching makes the socially invisible person the site of the whole account — feeding, clothing, visiting — so that no treatment of anyone is private. As a cultural parallel the tradition has functioned as a standing argument for the priority of the vulnerable in any moral scheme: the test of a community is what happens to its least powerful member, because that is where the excuses run out. My principle M033 carries the practical edge: do not mock the vulnerable, since in this tradition the mocking has a specific addressee. A compendium exercise drawn from the advocacy is to ask, of any group one belongs to, who its "least" currently is, and what is being done to them. What the advocacy does not give: romantic suffering or the claim that the poor are morally better; the identification is about the judge's location, not the person's virtue.

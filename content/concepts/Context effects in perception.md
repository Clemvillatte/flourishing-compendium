“Context effects in perception” — Bar, 2004.

Context effects in perception are the ways surroundings shape what is seen. Bar's 2004 review of visual objects in context states its subject plainly:

> We see the world in scenes, where visual objects occur in rich surroundings, often embedded in a typical context with other related objects. — Bar, 2004, *Nat Rev Neurosci* 5:617–29, [abstract (opened)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn1476)

The review's Key Points give the working claim in both directions:

> Objects can be used to make predictions about probable contexts and about other objects that might be found in the same scene, and contexts can be used to inform the identification of individual objects. — Bar, 2004, [Key Points (opened)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn1476)

The mechanism Bar proposes runs on expectations built from experience:

> These context frames can be viewed as sets of expectations that are derived from exposure to real-world scenes. — Bar, 2004, [Key Points (opened)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrn1476)

Seeing, on this account, is not a neutral registration. The scene primes the object, and the object primes the scene. The compendium builds its horizon and expectation principles on this ground.

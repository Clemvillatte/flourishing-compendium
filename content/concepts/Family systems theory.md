Family systems theory treats the family as one emotional unit rather than a collection of individuals. The psychiatrist Murray Bowen originated it with eight interlocking concepts, and Salvador Minuchin's structural variant, in Families and Family Therapy (1974), added the vocabulary of subsystems, boundaries and hierarchies. The compendium's seed names both: Bowen, 1978; Minuchin, 1974 ([Bowen Center](https://www.thebowencenter.org/introduction-eight-concepts); [Minuchin 1974 excerpts](https://digitalcommons.wayne.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1103&context=csr)).

### Bowen's view

The theory's premise is emotional interdependence: "it is the nature of a family that its members are intensely connected emotionally," and "a change in one person's functioning is predictably followed by reciprocal changes in the functioning of others" ([Bowen Center](https://www.thebowencenter.org/introduction-eight-concepts)). Bowen's eight concepts are triangles, differentiation of self, nuclear family emotional process, family projection process, multigenerational transmission process, emotional cutoff, sibling position and societal emotional process. The clinical centre is differentiation of self: the capacity to hold a considered position under emotional pressure while staying connected to the people pressuring you.

### Minuchin's structure

Minuchin maps the family into subsystems, spousal, parental, sibling, each circumscribed by boundaries that are "rules defining who participates and how" ([Minuchin, 1974](https://digitalcommons.wayne.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1103&context=csr)). Boundaries run on a continuum from diffuse (enmeshment) to rigid (disengagement): in enmeshed families "the heightened sense of belonging requires a major yielding of autonomy," while disengaged families struggle to come to a member's assistance under stress. The theory's caution is worth keeping: a normal family displays characteristics of each style at various times, and trouble shows when a family continually operates at an extreme.

### Evidence and limits

The theory's research base is real but uneven. A 2022 scoping review of the differentiation-of-self literature found ample support for it as a predictor of psychological health and marital quality, and a paucity of high-quality research, particularly longitudinal and randomized studies ([Müller et al., 2022, J Clin Med](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36429464/)).

The compendium uses the theory to give family principles a mechanism.

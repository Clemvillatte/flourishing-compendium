Expressive writing is James Pennebaker's paradigm: writing continuously, for a few sessions, about one's deepest thoughts and feelings regarding an emotional upheaval — not about the events as report but about what they meant and felt ([Pennebaker's expressive writing paradigm](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/expressive-writing-one-type-of-confrontation/); the paradigm originates in Pennebaker & Beall, 1986). The protocol is deliberately simple — 15–20 minutes, three or four days, no editing for style, kept private — and the accumulated research finds reliable effects on well-being and health markers relative to control writing ([growing literature since](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0718-48082017000300213)).

The mechanism is structure, not venting: raw venting amplifies ([[P113 — Express by shaping, not venting]]), rumination circles ([[Rumination]] is its failure mode), but writing forces the chaotic material into narrative — sequence, cause, word-choice — which is what converts re-experiencing into comprehension ([[SP053 — Connect affects to ordered thought]]; [[P048 — Think reflectively — turn experience over]]). The Stoic journal and the evening review are the same technology in older clothes ([[S122 — Keep a journal]]; [[K157 — Evening review]]).

The distinction to hold: the writing helps when it moves toward meaning, not merely discharge — disclosure *capacity* matters ([[AF077 — Learn emotional disclosure — the capacity to share feelings in a way that helps]]), and for some people some subjects, suppression is not maladaptive ([[AF032]]). The compendium's kernel is [[K245 — Expressive writing]].

An example at its smallest: four evenings, twenty minutes, one unwritten-about event — then the pages destroyed unread, the standard protocol's privacy being what makes honesty cheap.

It is [[The examined life]] made procedural — the self audited in written words.

Discernment of spirits is the practice of reading one's inner movements to tell which lead toward good and which away from it. Its classic statement is in Ignatius of Loyola's Spiritual Exercises, which gives two sets of rules, one "proper for the first week" and one with "greater discernment of spirits" for the second ([Spiritual Exercises, Christian Classics Ethereal Library](https://ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises.all.html)). The language is explicitly religious, with a good spirit and an "evil spirit", and this page reports it in those terms.

### Consolation and desolation

Ignatius defines his terms. "I call it consolation when some interior movement in the soul is caused, through which the soul comes to be inflamed with love of its Creator and Lord." Desolation is "all the contrary", including "darkness of soul, disturbance in it, movement to things low and earthly, the unquiet of different agitations and temptations", and finding oneself "all lazy, tepid, sad" ([Spiritual Exercises](https://ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises.all.html)).

### Rules for acting

In desolation, "never to make a change", but to remain "firm and constant" in the resolutions made before. It also helps "intensely to change ourselves against the same desolation", through more prayer, meditation and examination. A person in desolation should "labor to be in patience", and a person in consolation should consider "how he will be in the desolation which will come after" ([Spiritual Exercises](https://ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises.all.html)).

### Reading the course of a thought

In the second set, Ignatius advises: "We ought to note well the course of the thoughts, and if the beginning, middle and end is all good, inclined to all good, it is a sign of the good Angel." If a train of thought ends in something "less good than what the soul had previously proposed to do", or if it "disquiets or disturbs the soul", it is a sign of the evil spirit ([Spiritual Exercises, second-week rules](https://ccel.org/ccel/ignatius/exercises/exercises.xix.ii.html)). He adds that a person who has perceived the pattern should look back at the thoughts' course, so as "to guard for the future against his usual deceits."

### Scope

The Exercises are a devotional text and make theological claims about God and angels that this page does not assess. Treating the rules as a general method for testing experience is my use, and the source does not say that. A translation of the text, not a scholarly commentary, was consulted.

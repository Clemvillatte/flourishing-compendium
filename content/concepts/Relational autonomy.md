The feminist critique that autonomy is not independence but the capacity to act within constitutive relationships — care, recognition, and social conditions are its media, not its enemies ([SEP Feminist Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminist-ethics/)).

Relational autonomy names a family of feminist accounts that revise the ideal of the self-governing individual. The Stanford Encyclopedia's entry on feminist perspectives on autonomy marks the metaphysical point at issue:

> "'Relational' may also deny the metaphysical notion of atomistic personhood, emphasizing instead that persons are socially and historically embedded, not metaphysically isolated, and shaped by factors such as race and class." ([Stoljar, Feminist Perspectives on Autonomy, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-autonomy/))

### What follows

If persons are socially embedded, then the capacity to act on one's own reasons is something supported or undermined by one's relations, and the autonomous life is not the unencumbered one. The seed line above carries the practical reading: care, recognition and social conditions are the media of autonomy, not its enemies.

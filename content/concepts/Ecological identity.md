Ecological identity is the part of a person's self-understanding that is built in relation to the natural world. The term is traced to Mitchell Thomashow's 1995 book Ecological Identity: Becoming a Reflective Environmentalist, which defines it on page 3 (as quoted in a 2026 systematic review of environment-related identity terms):

> "all the different ways people construe themselves in relationship to the earth as manifested in personality, values, actions, and sense of self. Nature becomes an object of identification." (Thomashow, 1995, p. 3, quoted in [Zhuravleva and Harré, Sustainability, 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/7/3270))

### Why the term is slippery

The same review finds that environment-related identity terms "are often inconsistently defined, with substantial overlap" ([review abstract](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/7/3270)). Within its three-part framework, the authors place "ecological identity" with the dimension of connection to nature, which they describe as "the psychological or emotional sense of belonging in the natural world".

### A measurable neighbour

Mayer and Frantz (2004) built the Connectedness to Nature Scale, a measure of "individuals' trait levels of feeling emotionally connected to the natural world". Their abstract reports that it correlates with "identity as an environmentalist" ([Oberlin repository record](https://digitalcommons.oberlin.edu/faculty_schol/2433/)). The scale is a related measure of connection to nature, not Thomashow's definition of ecological identity.

The label "restlessness as weather" is this wiki's phrase; the sources I opened do not use it. The Gita verses it cites speak of wind. In Swami Mukundananda's translation, Arjuna says: "The mind is very restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate, O Krishna. It appears to me that it is more difficult to control than the wind." Krishna answers: "what you say is correct; the mind is indeed very difficult to restrain. But by practice and detachment, it can be controlled" ([Bhagavad Gita 6.34, Swami Mukundananda](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/34); [Bhagavad Gita 6.35](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/6/verse/35)).

### Where the Gita and the wiki part ways

The Gita says the mind can be restrained by practice (abhyasa) and detachment (vairagya). That is a language of control, whereas the wiki principle "Treat restlessness as weather, not as enemy" suggests letting it pass. The Gita does not say that the mind should be left alone, so cite the verses for the weather-like quality of the mind, not for the non-resistance stance.

### A Buddhist parallel

The Pali tradition treats restlessness and worry (uddhacca-kukkucca) as the fourth of the five hindrances. A Buddhist-studies translator writes that "restlessness-and-worry are like turbulent winds that lash and stir up the mind into ripples and waves", and that restlessness shows as "the inability to stay with one mental object for a long time" ([Dharmafarer, A 1.2 On the Abandoning of Hindrances, PDF; file title "Microsoft Word - 16.3 Nivarana Pahana Vagga a1.2 piya"](https://www.themindingcentre.org/dharmafarer/wp-content/uploads/2009/12/16.3-Nivarana-Pahana-Vagga-a1.2-piya.pdf)). The simile in the discourses compares it to "water blown into ripples by the wind", so that a person cannot see a reflection of their face ([Jhanas in Theravada Buddhist Meditation, Buddhist Publication Society text on BuddhaSasana](https://www.budsas.org/ebud/jhanas/jhanas03.htm)).

The same source says the Buddha also compared restlessness and worry to slavery, and that release from the hindrances is like emancipation from slavery. For practice, the translator's text gives calm (samatha) as the remedy for restlessness, and mindfulness of breathing for a scattered mind (A 6.115, 6.116). A meditation-centre newsletter observes that "simply being mindful is a big step": mindfulness does not eliminate a hindrance but gives "better perspective of what is happening" ([Insight Meditation Center, The Hindrance of Restlessness and Worry, PDF](https://www.insightmeditationcenter.org/wp-content/uploads/newsletters/pdf/07_2008.pdf)). That is the nearest the sources come to the weather idea.

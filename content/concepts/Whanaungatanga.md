Whanaungatanga is relationship, kinship, a sense of family connection: in Te Aka Māori Dictionary's gloss, a relationship built through shared experiences and working together, one that provides people with a sense of belonging ([Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/search/?keywords=whanaungatanga)). The entry traces how it develops: out of kinship rights and obligations that serve to strengthen each member of the kin group, and it extends beyond blood to others with whom one builds a close familial, friendship or reciprocal relationship ([Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/search/?keywords=whanaungatanga)). Read as a psychological concept, what it names is belonging made out of mutual obligation rather than sentiment alone: the connection is produced by what people do together and owe each other, and the sense of belonging is the felt result. That is why it pairs naturally with Ubuntu's relational identity and with care ethics: in each, a person is constituted through relationship rather than prior to it. What the dictionary entry does not claim — and what this page therefore does not assert — is any developmental priority over other Māori values; earlier wording implying that rested on a dead citation and has been removed.

Whanaungatanga is relationship, kinship, a sense of family connection: in Te Aka Māori Dictionary's gloss, a relationship built through shared experiences and working together, one that provides people with a sense of belonging ([Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/search/?keywords=whanaungatanga)). The entry traces how it develops: out of kinship rights and obligations that serve to strengthen each member of the kin group, and it extends beyond blood to others with whom one builds a close familial, friendship or reciprocal relationship ([Te Aka Māori Dictionary](https://maoridictionary.co.nz/search/?keywords=whanaungatanga)). Read as a psychological concept, the definition rewards attention in two places. First, the connection is constituted by what people do together and owe each other — shared work, mutual obligation — so belonging here is produced and maintained rather than merely felt; the dictionary's phrase "provides people with a sense of belonging" makes the sense a result of the relationship, not its precondition. Second, the extension past blood kinship makes the concept portable: a working group, a team or a community of practice can be a site of whanaungatanga wherever reciprocal relationship and shared experience are actually present. On the dictionary's own terms, the concept names belonging as an ongoing accomplishment of relationship.

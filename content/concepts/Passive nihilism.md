Passive nihilism is Nietzsche's name for the weakened form of the loss of values: the collapse happens, but the person collapses with it. The original entry's source is The Will to Power, the posthumous notebook collection where Nietzsche sorted the phenomenon into active and passive forms. The Wikipedia summary of the distinction:

> "According to Nietzsche, nihilism often manifests in a distorted form as passive nihilism, masking its life-denying nature behind religious dogmas, conventional morality, and societal norms. Against this tendency, Nietzsche recommended active nihilism, which openly acknowledges the lack of meaning and uses its negative force to dismantle established values." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilism))

### The difference

Both forms face the same event — the guiding values and ideals are gone. The article frames Nietzsche's understanding of the event itself: he "understood it as a broad cultural phenomenon in which people lose the values and ideals guiding their lives", exploring "the causes and consequences of this shift in evaluative outlook, examining reactions to it and ways of overcoming it". Passive nihilism is one reaction: instead of using the loss of meaning to dismantle worn-out values, it hides the loss behind the old dogmas and norms, denying life while pretending to keep it.

“Subitism” — Huineng, Platform Sutra.

Subitism is the Chan (Zen) teaching that awakening arrives all at once rather than being assembled step by step. The word comes from the Latin subitus (sudden) and marks the Southern school of Huineng, the Sixth Patriarch, against the gradualist school of Shenxiu: where gradualism polishes the mind like a mirror, dusting it daily, Huineng's answering verse holds that buddha-nature is always already pure, leaving no place for dust to land. The Platform Sutra grounds the doctrine in the story of the verse contest and the midnight transmission of the robe.

> "The dharma itself is the same, but in seeing it there is a slow way and a fast way. Seen slowly, it is the gradual; seen fast it is the sudden [teaching]. Dharma is without sudden or gradual, but some people are keen and others dull; hence the names 'sudden' and 'gradual.'" (Platform Sutra, section 39, Yampolsky translation, quoted in [IEP, Huineng](https://iep.utm.edu/huineng/))

### What the teaching does and does not claim

Read closely, the doctrine is less a claim about speed than about readiness. The same reality is seen slowly or fast; the difference lies in the seer. The Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes that the term dun, usually rendered "sudden," might better be rendered "poised" or "ready": those who awaken suddenly are those already keen and fast, "ready to awaken in action, poised to break through to fuller, wise and compassionate living." Subitism therefore does not deny practice; it denies that awakening is a distant wage paid out after enough polishing.

The compendium's principle B130 carries the practical consequence: expect awakening to shift in a moment, and treat practice as the making of readiness rather than the installment plan for enlightenment.

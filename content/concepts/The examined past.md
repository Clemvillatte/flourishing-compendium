In [On Anger 3.36](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Of_Anger/Book_III), Seneca describes a daily review of conduct.

> "The spirit ought to be brought up for examination daily." (Seneca, De Ira 3.36, Aubrey Stewart translation, 1900, [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Of_Anger/Book_III))

He reports following the custom himself: when the lamp is taken out of his sight and his wife, who knows his habit, has stopped talking, he goes over the whole day, repeating what he said and did, and hides nothing from himself. Seneca pardons each fault on the condition of not repeating it, and the corrections he records concern manner as much as content: arguing too contentiously, reprimanding someone with more freedom than he ought.

The heading names a Stoic practice: putting the past under deliberate review instead of letting it settle into memory unexamined. The fullest ancient description is Seneca's, in On Anger:

> "The spirit ought to be brought up for examination daily. It was the custom of Sextius when the day was over, and he had betaken himself to rest, to inquire of his spirit: 'What bad habit of yours have you cured to-day? what vice have you checked? in what respect are you better?'" (Seneca, De Ira 3.36, Basore translation)

Seneca follows the custom himself. When the lamp is taken away and his wife has stopped talking, he passes "the whole day in review" and repeats "all that I have said and done", concealing nothing and omitting nothing.

### What the review is for

The questions are corrective rather than judicial. Seneca pardons each fault on the condition of not repeating it, and the corrections he records concern manner as much as content: arguing too contentiously, reprimanding someone with more freedom than he could bear. The past is examined to change what happens next.

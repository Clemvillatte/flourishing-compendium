The heading names a Stoic practice: putting the past under deliberate review instead of letting it settle into memory unexamined. Seneca describes the discipline in On Anger:

> "The spirit ought to be brought up for examination daily." (Seneca, De Ira 3.36, Aubrey Stewart translation, 1900)

He reports following the custom himself, pleading his cause before himself when the lamp is taken out and his wife has stopped talking:

> "I pass the whole day in review before myself, and repeat all that I have said and done: I conceal nothing from myself, and omit nothing." (Seneca, De Ira 3.36, Stewart translation)

Seneca pardons each fault on the condition of not repeating it, and the corrections he records concern manner as much as content: arguing too contentiously, reprimanding someone with more freedom than he ought.

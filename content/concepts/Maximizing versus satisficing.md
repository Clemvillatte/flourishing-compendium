“Maximizing versus satisficing” — Schwartz et al., 2002.

Maximizing versus satisficing names two decision-making styles that Schwartz and colleagues set against each other in their 2002 studies of choice ([Cheek and Schwartz, On the meaning and measurement of maximization](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-the-meaning-and-measurement-of-maximization-Cheek-Schwartz/230869c792600acb01064df0f3d1620d14df3b12)):

> "Building on Herbert Simon's critique of rational choice theory, Schwartz et al. (2002) proposed that when making choices, some individuals — maximizers — search extensively through many alternatives with the goal of making the best choice, whereas others — satisficers — search only until they identify an option that meets their standards, which they then choose." (Cheek and Schwartz, [On the meaning and measurement of maximization](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-the-meaning-and-measurement-of-maximization-Cheek-Schwartz/230869c792600acb01064df0f3d1620d14df3b12))

The 2002 paper's own abstract states the finding ([Schwartz et al., Maximizing versus satisficing, Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Maximizing-versus-satisficing:-happiness-is-a-of-Schwartz-Ward/aec5cbb241ecb4375694fab83af436040374f2e4)):

> "Can people feel worse off as the options they face increase? The present studies suggest that some people—maximizers—can. Study 1 reported a Maximization Scale, which measures individual differences in desire to maximize. Seven samples revealed negative correlations between maximization and happiness, optimism, self-esteem, and life satisfaction, and positive correlations between maximization and depression, perfectionism, and regret. Study 2 found maximizers less satisfied than nonmaximizers (satisficers) with consumer decisions, and more likely to engage in social comparison. Study 3 found maximizers more adversely affected by upward social comparison. Study 4 found maximizers more sensitive to regret and less satisfied in an ultimatum bargaining game. The interaction between maximizing and choice is discussed in terms of regret, adaptation, and self-blame." (Schwartz et al., 2002, [abstract, Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Maximizing-versus-satisficing:-happiness-is-a-of-Schwartz-Ward/aec5cbb241ecb4375694fab83af436040374f2e4))

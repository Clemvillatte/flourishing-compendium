The seed holds for risky choice, the type studied by Tversky and Kahneman, where the effect is reliable. Levin, Schneider and Gaeth (1998) argue that earlier results were mixed because studies used different operational definitions of framing. They distinguish three kinds of valence framing: risky-choice framing (Tversky and Kahneman's), attribute framing, which affects the evaluation of object or event characteristics, and goal framing, which affects the persuasiveness of a communication. ([Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 76:149-188, PubMed 9831520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9831520/)) The quantitative claims below concern risky-choice framing only.

### The original demonstration

> "The psychological principles that govern the perception of decision problems and the evaluation of probabilities and outcomes produce predictable shifts of preference when the same problem is framed in different ways." (Tversky & Kahneman 1981, Science 211:453-458, [PubMed 7455683](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7455683/))

They found reversals of preference in monetary choices, hypothetical and real, and in questions about the loss of human lives. They note that "the dependence of preferences on the formulation of decision problems is a significant concern for the theory of rational choice".

### How strong and how general

A 2023 systematic review calls the risky-choice framing effect "reliable" and reports that it has "proven robust in hundreds of empirical examinations". ([Kühberger 2023, EXCLI Journal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10620856/)) The review estimates the effect at about d = 0.60 for the classic Asian disease problem format, which translates to r = 0.28. It stresses that "there is no single true effect size", with meta-analytic effect sizes for variants ranging from d = 0.16 to d = 0.91.

The same review reports that stronger effects are often assumed under fast, intuitive processing, and that deliberation tends to shrink the effect. It also says that no consistent relationship with cognitive style or risk style has emerged, and that age differences, if present, are small. Its central argument is that framing effects point to "the adaptiveness of the processes underlying human judgment and choice rather than simply showing human irrationality".

### What this page does not claim

The studies concern one-off choices in surveys and experiments. They do not show how much framing shapes major life decisions, and the review treats the mechanism as still debated.

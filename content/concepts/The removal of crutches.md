The removal of crutches names the refusal of supports that make weakness permanent. Nietzsche puts it in a single image in Thus Spake Zarathustra, at the close of "The Pale Criminal": "I am a railing alongside the torrent; whoever is able to grasp me may grasp me! Your crutch, however, I am not." ([Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, tr. Common](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/friedrich-nietzsche/thus-spake-zarathustra/thomas-common/text/chapter-6))

### What the removal carries

The railing and the crutch do different jobs: a railing stays where the torrent is, something to grasp in crossing, while a crutch replaces the leg entirely and keeps it from bearing weight. Nietzsche refuses to be the second thing — help that becomes load-bearing removes the very capacity it was asked to restore. My principle BE030 carries the modern, experimental form of the same point: safety behaviours and subtle avoidance undermine exposure, because the anxious person never learns the feared outcome fails to arrive; the crutch is what prevents the disconfirmation. The teaching and the clinical finding agree that the timing matters: grasp the railing while crossing, but set it down on the far bank. A compendium exercise drawn from the removal is to list one's current supports and ask of each whether it is being crossed with or leaned on entirely. What the removal does not give: a ban on all support; the refusal is aimed at the crutch that replaces the limb, not the hand that steadies the crossing.

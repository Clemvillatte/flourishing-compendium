### Definition

Person-activity fit is the degree to which a well-being practice suits the person doing it. The same gratitude journal that transforms one reader bores another into quitting. In Lyubomirsky and Layous's positive-activity model, fit is a moderator of what an activity does for you:

> An optimal person-activity fit (i.e., the overlap between activity and person features) further predicts increases in well-being. ([Lyubomirsky & Layous 2013](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Current_Directions_in_Psychological_Science-2013-Lyubomirsky-57-62.pdf))

### Origin

The construct grew out of Lyubomirsky's sustainable-happiness research, which found that intentional activities can raise well-being, but only under good conditions. The 2005 model (Lyubomirsky, Sheldon & Schkade) already made activity choice person-relative; Schueller (2010) showed that people stick with activities they enjoy and benefit from. The 2013 model fixed fit as one of the levers: a creative person may gain more from flow activities, an introvert more from a private gratitude journal. Fit is not the only criterion, the authors caution, since an activity must also be genuinely efficacious. The person still needs both a will and a proper way.

### A real-life example

Two friends take up running. One loves it and it takes. The other drags through every session and gives up by March, concluding that exercise "doesn't work for her." Switch her to swimming or long walks and the story reverses. What looked like a character flaw was a fit problem.

Skillful responsiveness is the Zhuangzi's picture of mastery: a reading of the situation so exact that the response meets no resistance, rather than force applied to the situation. The book's emblem is Cook Ting, the butcher whose knife stays sharp for nineteen years because it travels the openings the ox already has. Lord Wen-hui praises his skill, and Cook Ting's answer separates the two things the concept joins:

> "What I care about is the Way, which goes beyond skill. When I first began cutting up oxen, all I could see was the ox itself. After three years I no longer saw the whole ox. And now - now I go at it by spirit and don't look with my eyes." (Zhuangzi, Inner Chapters, "The Secret of Caring for Life," translated by Burton Watson, [The Complete Works of Chuang Tzu](https://terebess.hu/english/chuangtzu.html))

### What the mastery is made of

The stages matter, and the change is trained rather than gifted. As he puts it: "I go along with the natural makeup, strike in the big hollows, guide the knife through the big openings, and follow things as they are." Even at the top of his skill he does not coast. "Whenever I come to a complicated place, I size up the difficulties, tell myself to watch out and be careful, keep my eyes on what I'm doing, work very slowly, and move the knife with the greatest subtlety, until - flop! the whole thing comes apart like a clod of earth crumbling to the ground." The skill is responsiveness all the way down: reading the situation and answering with exactly the movement it permits.

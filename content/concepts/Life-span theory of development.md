Paul Baltes's framework for development as a process that runs from birth to death rather than stopping at adulthood.

> "In 1987 Baltes identified and later adapted a number of key concepts that compose lifespan theory." Studied empirically since the 1960s and '70s, the lifespan development approach proposes an "integrative perspective on development as a multidimensional, multidirectional, context-specific and malleable phenomenon that goes beyond classic conceptions of a linear, unidirectional growth or differentiation." (Gautreau, Gould & Forsythe, [Aging and orthopedics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/), Can J Surg, 2016, summarizing Baltes 1987)

### Core commitments

Development, on this view, comprises gains and losses throughout the lifespan rather than increasing gains until mid-adulthood followed by slow declines into old age ([Gautreau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/)). At any point there can be gains and losses in functioning and in adaptation that may co-occur ([Gautreau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/)). Baltes's later work develops this into the model of selection, optimization, and compensation, in which managing the trade-off between gains and losses is the central task of successful development ([Baltes 1997, cited in Gautreau et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/)).

Life-span development theory treats development as multidimensional and multidirectional, with gains and losses occurring throughout life ([Gautreau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/)).

> "Development also comprises gains and losses throughout the lifespan rather than increasing gains until mid-adulthood followed by slow declines into old age." ([Gautreau, Gould & Forsythe, 2016, "Change is multidimensional and multidirectional"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/))

The theory holds that at any point there can be gains and losses in functioning and in adaptation that may co-occur, so that a person's development is not captured by a single trajectory of growth followed by decline ([Gautreau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/)). In 1987 Baltes identified and later adapted a number of key concepts that compose lifespan theory ([Gautreau et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4961492/)).

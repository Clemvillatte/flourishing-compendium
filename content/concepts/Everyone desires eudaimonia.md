Everyone desires [[Eudaimonia|eudaimonia]] is Aristotle's claim in Nicomachean Ethics I: every other good is chosen at least partly for the sake of happiness, while happiness is chosen only for itself.

> "Now such a thing happiness, above all else, is held to be; for this we choose always for self and never for the sake of something else, but honour, pleasure, reason, and every virtue we choose indeed for themselves (for if nothing resulted from them we should still choose each of them), but we choose them also for the sake of happiness, judging that by means of them we shall be happy. Happiness, on the other hand, no one chooses for the sake of these, nor, in general, for anything other than itself." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.7, 1097a-b, tr. W. D. Ross, [MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html))

### What counts as agreement

The universality claim itself is in chapter 4. Aristotle writes: "Verbally there is very general agreement; for both the general run of men and people of superior refinement say that it is happiness, and identify living well and doing well with being happy; but with regard to what happiness is they differ, and the many do not give the same account as the wise" ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.4, tr. W. D. Ross, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html)). So the agreement is about the word and the aim. It leaves open what happiness is, which is the question the rest of the book works on.

### Why the claim holds

The argument runs through finality and self-sufficiency. Honour, pleasure and virtue are each chosen for themselves, yet also for the sake of happiness; nothing is chosen for the sake of happiness, so it alone is final. It is also self-sufficient: what makes life desirable and lacking in nothing is not one good among others, since it would not be made more desirable by adding any further good to it. Aristotle's conclusion, in Ross's rendering: "Happiness, then, is something final and self-sufficient, and is the end of action."

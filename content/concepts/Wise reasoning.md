Wise reasoning is Igor Grossmann's research program's answer to what wisdom is, when studied empirically: not a saintly personality type but a set of reasoning strategies people deploy, or fail to deploy, situation by situation. The framework was synthesized from earlier models by Grossmann and colleagues in 2013 and given its fullest statement in Grossmann's 2017 review "Wisdom in Context" ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf), Perspectives on Psychological Science).

> "They include (a) intellectual humility or recognition of limits of own knowledge, (b) appreciation of perspectives broader than the issue at hand, (c) sensitivity to the possibility of change in social relations, and (d) compromise or integration of different opinions (see Figure 1)." (Grossmann, [Wisdom in Context](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf), Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(2):233-257, 2017)

### What varies: the situation

The review's central empirical claim is that wise thinking is state-like, not trait-like. In its words, "wise thinking varies from one situation to another, with self-focused contexts inhibiting wise thinking", and "people vary dramatically in their likelihood of utilizing wise thought from one situation to the next" ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). Even nominated wisdom exemplars in an Austrian study showed high reliability of wise thought within an interview day and only modest consistency across days.

### The trainable part

The situational picture carries a practical corollary: if contexts inhibit or enable wise thinking, instructions can too. The review reports that "an ego-decentering cognitive mind-set enables wise thinking about personally meaningful issues". In one experiment, graduating seniors reflecting on recession-era job prospects imagined their career unfolding "as if you were a distant observer" and scored higher on wisdom-related reasoning than those imagining events "before your own eyes as if you were right there". In another, students who adopted the perspective of "an Icelandic citizen living in Iceland" before the 2008 U.S. election reasoned more wisely about polarized political issues. Follow-up experiments on relationship conflicts showed the self-other asymmetry known as Solomon's paradox can be eliminated by perspective instructions ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf), summarizing Grossmann and Kross 2014).

### The honest limit

The framework's measures are content analyses of people's spoken or written reflections on conflicts, scored for the four facets, not a test with right answers. The ego-decentering effects are short-term laboratory boosts to scored reasoning, and the claim that wisdom is not a fixed trait rests partly on exactly this variability of measurement.

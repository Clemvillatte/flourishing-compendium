Wise reasoning is Igor Grossmann's research program's answer to what wisdom is, when studied empirically: not a saintly personality type but a set of reasoning strategies people deploy, or fail to deploy, situation by situation ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). The source for this entry is Grossmann's 2017 review "Wisdom in Context" ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf), Perspectives on Psychological Science, university-hosted PDF).

> "They include (a) intellectual humility or recognition of limits of own knowledge, (b) appreciation of perspectives broader than the issue at hand, (c) sensitivity to the possibility of change in social relations, and (d) compromise or integration of different opinions (see Figure 1)." (Grossmann, [Wisdom in Context](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf), Perspectives on Psychological Science 12(2):233-257, 2017)

### What varies: the situation

The review emphasizes variation in wise reasoning across situations ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). In its words, "wise thinking varies from one situation to another, with self-focused contexts inhibiting wise thinking", and "People vary dramatically in their likelihood of utilizing wise thought from one situation to the next" ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). Even nominated wisdom exemplars in an Austrian study showed high reliability of wise thought within an interview day and only modest consistency across days ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)).

### Perspective-taking experiments

The review reports experiments in which instructions changed how people reasoned ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). It reports that "an ego-decentering cognitive mind-set enables wise thinking about personally meaningful issues" ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). In one experiment, graduating seniors reflecting on recession-era job prospects imagined their career unfolding "as if you were a distant observer" and scored higher on wisdom-related reasoning than those imagining events "before your own eyes as if you were right there" ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). In another, students who adopted the perspective of "an Icelandic citizen living in Iceland" before the 2008 U.S. election reasoned more wisely about polarized political issues ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). Follow-up experiments on relationship conflicts showed the self-other asymmetry known as Solomon's paradox can be eliminated by perspective instructions ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf), summarizing Grossmann and Kross 2014).

### The honest limit

The experiments measure the immediate effect of perspective instructions on scored reasoning ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)). The review itself frames the open question as evaluating "how contextual factors influence the malleability of wise thought" and developing "empirically grounded interventions aiming to promote wisdom-related processes in schools, work settings, and daily life", which is a research program's agenda rather than a demonstration that wisdom is durably trainable ([Grossmann 2017](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf)).

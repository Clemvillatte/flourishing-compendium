Goal adjustment capacity is the ability to let go of a goal that has become unattainable and to take up another. The construct comes from Wrosch, Scheier, Miller, Schulz and Carver's 2003 paper "Adaptive self-regulation of unattainable goals," and its two halves are usually taken apart: disengagement, the withdrawal of "behavioral efforts and psychological commitment" from a goal that cannot be reached, and reengagement, the tendency to "identify new goals, commit to new goals, and start pursuing new goals" ([Wrosch et al., PSPB 29 (2003), PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15018681/); [Wrosch review, PMC4145404](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4145404/)).

### What the studies found

The 2003 paper ran three studies: 115 undergraduates, 120 young and older adults, and 45 parents, half of them parents of children with cancer ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15018681/)). The findings confirmed that both capacities can be associated with high subjective well-being, that they have interactive effects, and that among older adults disengagement needs to be accompanied by reengagement to influence well-being positively ([Concordia, Wrosch Lab, full text](https://www.concordia.ca/content/dam/artsci/wrosch-lab/documents/PSPB_UG_2003.pdf)). A later review of the programme drew the asymmetry sharply: disengagement capacities ameliorate psychological distress and protect physical health, while reengagement capacities predict positive aspects of well-being, purpose and positive affect, but rarely distress or health outcomes ([Wrosch review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4145404/)).

The model's own caution is that the two processes are relatively independent: disengaging from an unattainable goal does not automatically lead to reengagement ([PMC, goal motives paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4104823/)).

### In the compendium

The compendium carries the construct as the psychological core of letting go. MO058 states it directly: disengage from unattainable goals because letting go is adaptive. P041's Deweyan ends-in-view and MO034's review-and-adjust practice give the reengagement side a discipline; MO074 and MO075 build the monitoring and maintenance around it, MO076 and SE079 keep motivation serving life rather than the reverse, and P176 prefers growth goals to validation goals so the letting-go costs less. D098's li ming, accepting what destiny brings, is the Confucian form of the same move, and AT078 keeps attention's purpose fixed while its objects change.

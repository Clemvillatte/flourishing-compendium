The bundle theory is Hume's account of the self in A Treatise of Human Nature: what introspection finds is not a simple, persisting subject but a succession of perceptions. The introspective test, in the Treatise's own words:

> "For my part, when I enter most intimately into what I call myself, I always stumble on some particular perception or other, of heat or cold, light or shade, love or hatred, pain or pleasure. I never can catch myself at any time without a perception, and never can observe any thing but the perception." (T 1.4.6.3)
> — [Hume, A Treatise of Human Nature, 1.4.6 "Of personal identity" (Hume Texts Online)](https://davidhume.org/texts/t/full)

The positive claim follows: the rest of mankind, Hume ventures, "are nothing but a bundle or collection of different perceptions, which succeed each other with an inconceivable rapidity, and are in a perpetual flux and movement" (T 1.4.6.4). The bundle is not unstructured: "the true idea of the human mind, is to consider it as a system of different perceptions or different existences, which are link'd together by the relation of cause and effect" (T 1.4.6.19). And the identity we think we find in it is produced rather than discovered: "The identity, which we ascribe to the mind of man, is only a fictitious one, and of a like kind with that which we ascribe to vegetables and animal bodies" (T 1.4.6.15).

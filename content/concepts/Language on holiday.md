Wittgenstein's diagnosis, in the Philosophical Investigations, of where philosophical confusion comes from: words used outside the language-games that give them their everyday use. The trouble comes from language cut loose from its work.

> The confusions which occupy us arise when language is like an engine idling, not when it is doing work.

(Wittgenstein, Philosophical Investigations §132, [selection](https://topologicalmedialab.net/xinwei/classes/readings/Wittgenstein/PhiloInvestigations_sel.pdf))

The same diagnosis appears at §38 in the famous phrase "philosophical problems arise when language goes on holiday" ([Mitchell, DBU overview](https://www.dbu.edu/mitchell/modern-resources/wittgensteinoverview.html)). On holiday, the engine idles: the words still run, but nothing they do is answerable to the practices that normally keep them honest. The compendium lists it as a philosophy parallel to the Buddhist papanca, or mental proliferation (see B070).

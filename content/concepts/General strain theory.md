General strain theory (GST) is Robert Agnew's account of why strain pushes some people toward crime and delinquency. His 1992 abstract describes it as a theory "capable of overcoming the criticisms of previous strain theories" and sets out "the three major types of strain": "(1) strain as the actual or anticipated failure to achieve positively valued goals, (2) strain as the actual or anticipated removal of positively valued stimuli, and (3) strain as the actual or anticipated presentation of negatively valued stimuli" ([Agnew, 1992, Criminology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1745-9125.1992.tb01093.x)).

### Emotion is the link

In GST, strain does not lead to crime directly. A study hosted by Baylor's ISR on strain, negative emotions and deviant coping summarises it: "GST posits that strain generates negative emotions that provide motivation for deviance as a coping strategy because such emotional forces create pressure for corrective action". Agnew called anger the "most critical emotional reaction" to strain, but "strain increases the likelihood that individuals will experience one or more of a range of negative emotions" ([Baylor ISR PDF](http://www.baylorisr.org/wp-content/uploads/johnson_strain_negative.pdf)).

The Wiley Encyclopedia of Criminology explains why anger matters: it "increases a person's feeling of entitlement and desire for revenge". Anger, frustration, depression and fear are listed as examples of the negative states linking strain and criminal coping ([Wiley Encyclopedia of Criminology and Criminal Justice, "General Strain Theory"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781118519639.wbecpx042)).

### Coping decides the outcome

Not everyone under strain offends. The same encyclopedia entry says that "delinquent or criminal coping may not result if the individual has different forms of emotional, physical, or spiritual coping resources". A University of Illinois Springfield paper on the theory describes three kinds of coping: cognitive (minimising or denying the negative feeling), behavioural (acting to solve its perceived cause) and emotional (reducing the feeling without denying or solving it) ([Blake and Carey, University of Illinois Springfield](https://www.uis.edu/sites/default/files/2026-04/Blake-Carey-Agnew.pdf)).

### Which strains are most dangerous

According to a published test of the theory, Agnew (2001) holds that the strains most likely to lead to delinquency are those that "(1) are perceived as unjust, (2) are high in magnitude, (3) are related to low social control, and (4) create pressure for delinquent coping" ([Oliphant, 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0044118X211036919)).

### Appraisal changes the path

Agnew wrote that "Anger results when individuals blame their adversity on others" (Agnew, 1992, p. 59, quoted in a [BYU-hosted study](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=4873&context=facpub)). The study's authors argue that when the cause of strain is not attributed to others, adolescents do not tend to become angry and delinquent.

### A limit

A 2001 test found that "strain, negative emotions, and legitimate coping are all related, although not always in the expected direction" ([Broidy, 2001, Criminology](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1745-9125.2001.tb00915.x)).

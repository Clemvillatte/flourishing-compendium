“Life affirmation” — Wong, 2016.

In The Gay Science §276, Nietzsche describes amor fati through a wish to learn to see what is necessary as beautiful ([The Gay Science §276](https://vishvasa.github.io/english/prose/nietzsche/gay-science/4_Book_Four/276/)). A scholarly discussion of the doctrine calls this section "the chief source for this aspect of his thought" ([Ridley, "Nietzsche, Nature, Nurture"](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/390200/1/_filestore.soton.ac.uk_users_amr3_mydocuments_WINWORD_Nietzsche_20Nature_20Nurture_20_28Final_29.doc)):

> I want to learn more and more how to see what is necessary in things as what is beautiful in them [...] Amor fati: let that be my love from now on! I do not want to wage war against ugliness. I do not want to accuse; I do not even want to accuse the accusers. Let looking away be my only negation! And, all in all and on the whole: some day I want only to be a Yes-sayer! (Nietzsche, The Gay Science §276, [vishvasa.github.io text](https://vishvasa.github.io/english/prose/nietzsche/gay-science/4_Book_Four/276/))

Wong's 2016 discussion of existential positive psychology offers a related perspective: "Properly understood, existential psychology is about the human drama of courage, creativity, and the celebration of life in the midst of suffering and death" ([Wong, 2016](https://www.meaning.ca/web/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/179-13-370-2-10-20171211.pdf)).

Wong also argues that people can grow through confronting "negative existential givens and the dark side of human existence" ([Wong, 2016](https://www.meaning.ca/web/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/179-13-370-2-10-20171211.pdf)).

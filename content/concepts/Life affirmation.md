“Life affirmation” — Wong, 2016.

Life affirmation is the stance of saying yes to one's life as it is. Its classical statement is the New Year's aphorism that opens Book Four of Nietzsche's The Gay Science:

> I want to learn more and more how to see what is necessary in things as what is beautiful in them [...] Amor fati: let that be my love from now on! I do not want to wage war against ugliness. I do not want to accuse; I do not even want to accuse the accusers. Let looking away be my only negation! And, all in all and on the whole: some day I want only to be a Yes-sayer! (Nietzsche, The Gay Science §276, [vishvasa.github.io text](https://vishvasa.github.io/english/prose/nietzsche/gay-science/4_Book_Four/276/))

The contemporary psychological home of the construct is Paul T. P. Wong's existential positive psychology, defined in his 2016 paper as an approach in which "Existential positive psychology (EPP) or positive existential psychology represents a natural amalgamation between PP and existential psychology". Its core claim runs against one-sided positivity: "Existential philosophers and psychologists have long discovered that authentic happiness arises from embracing suffering as the essence of the human condition", and Wong's duality hypothesis "states that positives cannot exist apart from negatives and that authentic happiness grows from pain and suffering". The paper closes the loop with two borrowed paradoxes, from Camus and Rollo May: "there is no love of life without despair" and "the ultimate paradox is that negation becomes affirmation".

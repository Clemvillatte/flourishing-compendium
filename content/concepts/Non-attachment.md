Non-attachment is the Buddhist quality of meeting experience without clinging to it. The Dhammapada states the claim in its own terms: grief and fear grow out of attachment, and a person without attachment has no grief left for fear to grow from.

> "From attachment springs grief, from attachment springs fear. For one who is wholly free from attachment there is no grief, whence then fear?" (Dhammapada 214, [Buddharakkhita translation, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.16.budd.html))

### A distinct construct

The term does not refer to the attachment of attachment theory. Sahdra, Shaver and Brown describe the Buddhist notion as release from mental fixations, and distinguish it from the Western construct: secure or insecure attachment rests on internal working models about security, while nonattachment rests on insight into the constructed and impermanent nature of mental representations ([Sahdra et al. 2010, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20155561/)). The companion page [[Nonattachment]] carries that research tradition and its scale.

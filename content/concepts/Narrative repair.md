Narrative repair is Hilde Lindemann Nelson's account, in Damaged Identities, Narrative Repair (2001), of how identities injured by oppressive group relations can be mended. The book's premise, as the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy's summary has it, is that identities and one's moral standing in society are narratively constituted, and so can be narratively damaged by oppression and oppressive practices (Gotlib, [Feminist Ethics and Narrative Ethics, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/fem-e-n/)).

> "One can also resist harmful master narratives through a counterstory, whose purpose it is to 'root out the master narratives in the tissue of stories that constitute an oppressive identity and replace them with stories that depict the person as morally worthy' (Lindemann 2001, 150)." ([Gotlib, IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/fem-e-n/), quoting Lindemann)

### Two kinds of moral damage

The IEP summary distinguishes two kinds of moral damage that can affect the cohesion of an identity: one deprives a person of important social goods, the other of self-respect. In this account, both can be repaired through counterstories, which resist, challenge, and overcome the damaging master narratives inflicted by the powerful on the vulnerable (Gotlib, IEP).

In his 1958 psychoanalytic account, Winnicott treats the capacity to be alone as "one of the most important signs of maturity in emotional development" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)). Its basis, he proposes, is a paradox: "it is the experience of being alone while someone else is present" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)).

### The paradox

The basic experience is "being alone, as an infant and small child, in the presence of mother" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)). Winnicott names the relationship at work ego-relatedness: "the relationship between two people, one of whom at any rate is alone; perhaps both are alone, yet the presence of each is important to the other" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)). He suggests that id-relationships strengthen the ego when they occur in a framework of ego-relatedness ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)). In time "the individual introjects the ego-supportive mother and in this way becomes able to be alone without frequent reference to the mother or mother symbol" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)).

### What the capacity rests on

The capacity "depends on the existence of a good object in the psychic reality of the individual," an internal object well enough established that the person can "rest contented even in the absence of external objects and stimuli" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)). "I am alone" is a development from "I am," dependent on the infant's awareness of the continued existence of a reliable mother ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)). The payoff is discovery: "It is only when alone (that is to say, in the presence of someone) that the infant can discover his own personal life" ([Winnicott 1958](https://web.english.upenn.edu/~cavitch/pdf-library/Winnicott_Capacity_To_Be_Alone.pdf)).

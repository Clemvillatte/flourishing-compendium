The speech gate is this compendium's name for the Buddhist check run before speaking, given canonical form in the Abhaya Sutta. The sutta sorts words by three tests — factual or not, beneficial or not, endearing or disagreeable to the listener — and adds timing: words the Tathagata knows to be factual, true, beneficial but unendearing and disagreeable to others, he says, having a sense of the proper time; words that are unfactual, untrue, or unbeneficial he does not say, whether endearing or not ([MN 58, Abhaya Sutta](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN58.html)). The gate is conjunctive: truth is necessary but not sufficient, and the right truth said at the wrong time fails it.

The mechanism: the tests are about reception as well as content. Speech does its work on the listener's mind, so what the words are to the listener matters as much as whether they are correct — and the motive the sutta names is "sympathy for living beings," which is what the timing serves. The gate is the practical core of [[Right speech]], and its compendium form is the kernel [[K208 — Speech gate]].

The distinction to hold: the gate is not silence, and withheld speech is not agreement. The sutta explicitly allows disagreeable speech — correction, challenge — through the gate when it is factual, true, and beneficial, said with a sense of the proper time; what the gate refuses is the untrue, the unbeneficial, and the right thing said at the wrong moment.

An example at its smallest: the kernel's pause — true? timely? beneficial? kind? — one breath before speaking.

Its modern structural cousin is [[Nonviolent communication]] (the same checks as a four-part protocol); its compendium kin are the speech disciplines — [[B023 — Practice right speech]] and [[B148 — Speak lovingly]].

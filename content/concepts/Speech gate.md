The speech gate is this compendium's name for the Buddhist check run before speaking. Its canonical form is the Abhaya Sutta (MN 58), where a prince sent by a rival teacher asks whether the Buddha ever says words that are unwelcome to others. The Buddha answers with a baby on the prince's lap:

> "What do you think, prince: If this young boy, through your own negligence or that of the nurse, were to take a stick or a piece of gravel into its mouth, what would you do?" "I would take it out, lord. If I couldn't get it out right away, then holding its head in my left hand and crooking a finger of my right, I would take it out, even if it meant drawing blood. Why is that? Because I have sympathy for the young boy." (MN 58, Thanissaro translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.058.than.html))

### Three tests and a clock

He then sorts words by whether they are factual and true, whether they are beneficial, and whether they are endearing and agreeable to the listener. Words that are untrue, or true but unbeneficial, he does not say, whether or not they would please. Of the two cases that pass the first two tests he says:

> "In the case of words that the Tathagata knows to be factual, true, beneficial, but unendearing & disagreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them." (MN 58, Thanissaro translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.058.than.html))

The same timing clause is attached to words that are factual, true, beneficial and agreeable. The reason given for all six cases is "Because the Tathagata has sympathy for living beings."

The gate is therefore conjunctive: truth is necessary but not sufficient, and the right words at the wrong moment fail it. It is the practical core of [[Right speech]], and its compendium form is the kernel [[K208 — Speech gate]].

### What it is not

It is not silence. Disagreeable speech passes the gate when it is factual, true and beneficial and the timing is right, as the baby story shows: sometimes the kind act is to reach into the mouth. And withheld speech is not agreement.

### A small case

One breath before speaking, asking: is it true, is it timely, is it beneficial, is it kind. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported case.)

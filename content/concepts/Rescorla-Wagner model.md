**Rescorla-Wagner model** — associative learning is driven by surprise: prediction error, not mere pairing, updates expectations.

The Rescorla-Wagner model is Robert Rescorla and Allan Wagner's 1972 theory of Pavlovian conditioning. A later modeling paper states its core in three sentences:

> "One of the most influential models of associative learning, the Rescorla-Wagner model of classical conditioning [1], states that for learning to occur the US must be surprising or, more precisely, unpredicted. Accordingly, the increase in associative strength (V), where V represents the weight of the CS-US link on a particular CS-US pairing, is proportional to the degree to which the US is unexpected (the delta rule). With each CS-US pairing (trial) the prediction error –the discrepancy between the predicted outcome and the actual outcome– is reduced, increasing the associative strength between the elements until the CS fully predicts the US, at which point no further learning occurs." (Mondragón et al., [SSCC TD, PLoS ONE 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4108321/))

### What it replaces

Under the model, contiguity alone does nothing: once the outcome is fully predicted, further pairings produce no learning. The model thereby explains blocking and the degraded contingency effects that pure contiguity accounts could not.

The technology acceptance model (TAM) was developed by Fred Davis to explain why people adopt information technology. Davis defined perceived usefulness as "the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would enhance his or her job performance" and perceived ease of use as "the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would be free from effort" (Davis 1989, quoted in [Australasian Journal of Information Systems](https://ajis.aaisnet.org/index.php/ajis/article/download/34/431/616)).

### Davis 1989

Davis's MIS Quarterly paper developed and validated scales for the two constructs, "hypothesized to be fundamental determinants of user acceptance". In two studies of 152 users and four application programs, perceived usefulness correlated with self-reported current usage (r = .63) and self-predicted future usage (r = .85). Perceived ease of use correlated with current usage (r = .45) and future usage (r = .59). "In both studies, usefulness had a significantly greater correlation with usage behavior than did ease of use", and regression suggested "perceived ease of use may actually be a causal antecedent to perceived usefulness" ([Davis 1989 abstract, UCL construct database](https://verdi.cs.ucl.ac.uk/constructDB/publications/davis-perceived-1989.html)).

### A longitudinal test

Davis, Bagozzi and Warshaw (1989) followed 107 users. Intentions measured after a one-hour introduction correlated 0.35 with system use 14 weeks later. Perceived usefulness "strongly influenced peoples' intentions", while ease of use "had a small but significant effect on intentions as well, although this effect subsided over time" ([Davis, Bagozzi & Warshaw 1989, EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/inmormnsc/v_3a35_3ay_3a1989_3ai_3a8_3ap_3a982-1003.htm)).

### Mixed evidence

A meta-analysis of 26 empirical studies found that the correlation between usefulness and acceptance was somewhat strong. However, "the relationship between ease of use and acceptance is weak, and its significance does not pass the fail-safe test" ([Ma & Liu, meta-analysis](https://www.irma-international.org/viewtitle/3781/?isxn=9781615200979)).

### Scope

TAM predicts adoption of workplace systems from users' beliefs. It does not measure whether a technology serves wellbeing or flourishing, and the compendium's link to its principle is thematic only.

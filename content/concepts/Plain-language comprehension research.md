Research showing that plain, concrete language improves comprehension, trust, and recall compared with jargon and abstraction.

The flagship finding is Oppenheimer's 2006 series on needlessly complex vocabulary. Undergraduates believed big words would make them look smart; the experiments found the reverse, and traced the effect to how hard the reader has to work.

> "However, a majority of undergraduates admit to deliberately increasing the complexity of their vocabulary so as to give the impression of intelligence." (Oppenheimer 2006, [Applied Cognitive Psychology](https://cahill.people.unm.edu/480-21/Oppenheimer-2006-Applied_Cognitive_Psychology.pdf))
>
> "Experiments 1–3 manipulate complexity of texts and find a negative relationship between complexity and judged intelligence. This relationship held regardless of the quality of the original essay, and irrespective of the participants' prior expectations of essay quality. The negative impact of complexity was mediated by processing fluency." (Oppenheimer 2006, abstract)

The paper's own summary line: "All in all, the effect is extremely robust: needless complexity leads to negative evaluations."

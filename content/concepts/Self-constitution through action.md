Self-constitution through action is the view, argued in the letters of Judge Vilhelm in Kierkegaard's Either/Or (1843), that a person does not start with a finished personality. The personality forms through choosing, and a life that keeps choice at arm's length loses definition.

> "choice itself is decisive for a personality's content; in choice personality immerses itself in what is chosen, and when it does not choose it wastes consumptively away" (Either/Or II, "The Balance Between the Aesthetic and the Ethical in the Development of Personality," Hong trans., [excerpts PDF](https://www.stephenhicks.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/KierkegaardS-Either-Or-excerpts.pdf)).

### The either/or

The letters put a blunt alternative to their reader: "You have to choose either/or! If you go just for the aesthetic life you choose despair. If you go for the ethical, you do your duty" (Either/Or II). The aesthete treats choice as a picking among options, one taste against another, always postponable. On Vilhelm's account that postponement is itself the loss: "Your choice is an aesthetic choice, but an aesthetic choice is no choice. The act of choosing is a literal and strict expression of the ethical" (Either/Or II).

### Choosing oneself

The decisive choice is not primarily a choice between goods. "I choose absolutely, and I choose absolutely precisely through having chosen not to choose this thing or that" (Either/Or II). What gets chosen is the chooser: "One chooses oneself, not in one's immediacy, not as this contingent individual, one chooses oneself in one's eternal validity" (Either/Or II). The aesthetic life is not abolished by this choice — "the whole of the aesthetic returns in its relativity" (Either/Or II) — but it no longer sets the terms.

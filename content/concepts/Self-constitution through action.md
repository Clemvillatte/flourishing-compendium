Self-constitution through action is the view argued in the letters of Judge Vilhelm in Kierkegaard's Either/Or (1843): choosing shapes personality, and a life that keeps choice at arm's length loses definition.

> "choice itself is decisive for a personality's content; in choice personality immerses itself in what is chosen, and when it does not choose it wastes consumptively away" (Either/Or II, "The Balance Between the Aesthetic and the Ethical in the Development of Personality," [excerpts PDF](https://www.stephenhicks.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/KierkegaardS-Either-Or-excerpts.pdf)).

### The either/or

Vilhelm puts the alternative plainly: "What I have said to you so often I say once more, or rather I shout it to you: either/or!" (Either/Or II). He warns against treating choice as indefinitely postponable: "Now, if a man could constantly balance on the tip of the moment of choice it would be foolish to say it might be too late for a man to choose." And he names the postponement itself as the loss: "Your choice is an aesthetic choice, but an aesthetic choice is no choice. The act of choosing is a literal and strict expression of the ethical" (Either/Or II).

### Choosing oneself

The decisive choice is not primarily a choice between goods. "I choose absolutely, and I choose absolutely precisely through having chosen not to choose this thing or that" (Either/Or II). What gets chosen is the chooser: "One chooses oneself, not in one's immediacy, not as this contingent individual, one chooses oneself in one's eternal validity" (Either/Or II). The aesthetic life is not abolished by this choice — "the whole of the aesthetic returns in its relativity" (Either/Or II) — but it no longer sets the terms.

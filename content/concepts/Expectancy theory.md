**Expectancy theory** — Vroom — motivation equals the product of expectancy that effort works, and the valence of the outcome.

Expectancy theory is Victor Vroom's account of work motivation, set out in Work and Motivation (1964). Motivational force is a product of three beliefs: that effort will lead to performance (expectancy), that performance will lead to outcomes (instrumentality), and that the outcomes are valued (valence). Because the model multiplies the three terms, a zero in any one of them zeroes the product: someone certain the effort is futile, or indifferent to the reward, has no motivational force for the act.

> "The VIE model, usually referred to as the Expectancy Theory, postulates that motivation is a product of expectancy, instrumentality, and valence (Vroom, 1964)." (Osafo, Paros & Yawson, [SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440211021896))

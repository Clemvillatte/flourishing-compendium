The self as construction is the view that a person's sense of who they are is assembled from experience, memory and social interaction, not discovered as a fixed essence. The seed cites Markus and Wurf (1987) and Baumeister (1998). Markus and Wurf state the position most directly:

> "There is not a fixed or static self, but only a current self-concept constructed from one's social experiences." (Markus and Wurf, 1987, Annual Review of Psychology 38:299-337, p. 306, [PDF](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev.ps.38.020187.001503))

### The working self-concept

Markus and Wurf argue that the self-concept "can no longer be explored as if it were a unitary, monolithic entity" (p. 300). They treat it as a multifaceted set of "images, schemas, conceptions, prototypes, theories, goals, or tasks" (p. 301). At any moment only part of this is in play: "the working self-concept, or the self-concept of the moment, is best viewed as a continually active, shifting array of accessible self-knowledge" (p. 306), and which conceptions are accessible varies "depending on the individual's motivational state or on the prevailing social conditions" (p. 306). The review also reports the symbolic interactionist suggestion "that all self-knowledge derives from social interaction" (p. 305) ([Markus and Wurf, 1987](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev.ps.38.020187.001503)). The authors are describing a suggestion from that tradition there, not asserting it as settled.

### Baumeister's account

I could not open Baumeister's 1998 chapter in the Handbook of Social Psychology. A later text that describes it says Baumeister grouped selfhood into three dimensions. Reflexive awareness yields "a body of knowledge and belief about the self, often called the self-concept." The second is relating to others: people "come to know themselves by interacting with others." The third is the executive function, the self as "a doer" that makes choices and exerts control ([Self-Regulation and the Executive Function, University of Minnesota PDF](https://assets.csom.umn.edu/assets/71708.pdf)). That text presents the three-part scheme as based on "presumably" basic phenomena, and it does not use the word construction, so describing Baumeister as holding that the self is constructed rests on this secondary account and the compendium's reading.

### In the compendium

SE001 (Understand that the self is constructed, not discovered), SE010 and SO003 draw on this. The research above shows that self-knowledge is organised, variable by situation, and shaped by social experience. It does not show that the self is an illusion or that there is nothing stable in a person, and the passages opened here do not address that question. The philosophical claims in K002, K011 and ST083 go beyond what the psychology opened here supports.

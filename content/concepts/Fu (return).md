Fu (復, "to return") is the name this wiki gives the pattern that chapter 16 of the Daodejing describes: things go through activity and growth, then each returns to its root. James Legge's translation renders that return as a return to stillness.

> "All things alike go through their processes of activity, and (then) we see them return (to their original state). When things (in the vegetable world) have displayed their luxuriant growth, we see each of them return to its root." (Laozi, Daodejing 16, trans. James Legge, [Sacred Texts Archive](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm))

The chapter opens by asking for vacancy and stillness guarded with unwearying vigour. It then ties knowledge of the "regular, unchanging rule" to intelligence, and says that not knowing it "leads to wild movements and evil issues". Near the end, someone possessed of the Tao "endures long". Fu (return) is this wiki's name for the pattern; the evidence opened here is Legge's English rendering of the chapter, plus a dictionary gloss of the character.

“The empirical temper” — Mill, A System of Logic.

### What it is

I use this title for the disposition that runs through Mill's A System of Logic: trust the observed uniformity, and state exactly how far it has been observed. Mill's model case is the empirical law, defined with deliberate modesty: "Scientific inquirers give the name of Empirical Laws to those uniformities which observation or experiment has shown to exist, but on which they hesitate to rely in cases varying much from those which have been actually observed, for want of seeing any reason why such a law should exist" (Mill, A System of Logic, book III, ch. XVI, [Gutenberg #27942](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/27942/pg27942.txt)). Such a law "is an observed uniformity, presumed to be resolvable into simpler laws, but not yet resolved into them" (Mill, A System of Logic, book III, ch. XVI, [Gutenberg #27942](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/27942/pg27942.txt)).

### What it carries

The temper has two sides. On one side, standing firm on what experience has actually shown, including in human affairs: "It is only in the simplest branches of science that empirical laws are ever exactly true; and not always in those" (Mill, A System of Logic, book VI, ch. V, §2, [Gutenberg #27942](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/27942/pg27942.txt)). On the other, hesitating to rely on an observed uniformity in cases that differ much from those observed, because the simpler laws into which it might be resolved are unknown: "if we knew these, we should also know what are its limits; under what conditions it would cease to be fulfilled" (Mill, A System of Logic, book III, ch. XVI, [Gutenberg #27942](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/27942/pg27942.txt)), where "these" are those underlying laws.

I connect this temper to CO028: the outside view begins from base rates, the record of how cases like this one have gone, rather than from the felt uniqueness of the present case. The connection adds the caution that accompanies it: a base rate is an approximate generalization, held within its observed range, and the more the case departs from that range the weaker the warrant.

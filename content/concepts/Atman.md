The ātman is the true Self, the witnessing consciousness that Śaṅkara's Advaita Vedānta describes as invariable despite the fluctuations of mental modes, and as numerically identical with [[Brahman]] ([Śaṅkara, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/shankara/)). What appears as an individual person (jīva) is the ātman seen through [[Maya (illusion)|māyā]]'s superimposition; realization ([[Moksha]]) is the knowledge "I am Brahman" (ahaṃ brahmāsmi, BṛU 1.4.10), which negates the error of superimposition ([Śaṅkara, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/shankara/); [IEP Advaita Vedanta](https://iep.utm.edu/advaita-vedanta/)). Cross-tradition: the Buddhist counter is [[Anatta]] (no-self); the Daoist resonance is the uncarved simplicity of [[Ziran (naturalness)]].

> "The fundamental thrust of Advaita Vedānta is that the ātman is pure non-intentional consciousness. It is one without a second, nondual, infinite existence, and numerically identical with brahman." (Dalal, [Śaṅkara](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/shankara/), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy)

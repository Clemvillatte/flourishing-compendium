“Presence” — Buber, I and Thou.

Presence is Martin Buber's term (Gegenwart) for what shows itself in encounter, in contrast to the relation of a subject to an object. The Stanford Encyclopedia's account of Buber states it:

> "As understood by Buber, revelation is the revelation of 'presence' (Gegenwart). In contrast to 'object' (Gegenstand), the presence revealed by revelation as encounter occupies the space 'in between' the subject and an other (a tree, a person, a work of art, God)." ([Zank & Braiterman, Martin Buber, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/))

### The space between

The same article stresses that the I-Thou "cannot be reduced to a concept" but points to the experience of encounter, and that the "in between" is either mutual or it "remains pre-dialogic". Presence belongs to the encounter itself, not to either party alone.

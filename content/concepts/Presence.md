“Presence” — Buber, I and Thou.

Presence is Martin Buber's term (Gegenwart) for what shows itself in an I-Thou encounter: a mode of being-with that is not the relation of a subject to an object. The Stanford Encyclopedia's account of Buber ties it to encounter:

> "As understood by Buber, revelation is the revelation of 'presence' (Gegenwart). In contrast to 'object' (Gegenstand), the presence revealed by revelation as encounter occupies the space 'in between' the subject and an other (a tree, a person, a work of art, God)." ([Zank & Braiterman, Martin Buber, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/))

### What the space between means

The same article stresses that the I-Thou "cannot be reduced to a concept" but points to the experience of encounter, and that the "in between" is either mutual or it "remains pre-dialogic". Presence is the quality of the mutual case: the other is met, not used.

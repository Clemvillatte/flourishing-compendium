Perspectivism, in Nietzsche's formulation, is the claim that things are not seen from nowhere. The Gay Science gives the practical version in the aphorism on what to learn from artists: "We should learn all this from artists, and moreover be wiser than they" ([Nietzsche, The Joyful Wisdom, trans. Thomas Common, Gutenberg #52124](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52124)).

> "To withdraw from things until one no longer sees much of them, until one has even to see things into them, _in order to see them at all_—or to view them from the side, and as in a frame—or to place them so that they partly disguise themselves and only permit of perspective views—or to look at them through coloured glasses, or in the light of the sunset—or to furnish them with a surface or skin which is not fully transparent: we should learn all this from artists, and moreover be wiser than they."
> — [Nietzsche, The Joyful Wisdom (GS 299), trans. Thomas Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52124)

The lesson Nietzsche draws is that this is how seeing works everywhere; the artist just makes the mechanism obvious. He then argues we should be the poets of our lives, and first of all in the smallest and most commonplace matters ([Nietzsche, GS 299](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52124)).

The same book gives the counter-move in the aphorism on the perspective that flatters: without the art of seeing ourselves from a distance, we live under the spell of the perspective that makes the closest and the commonest seem immensely large and like reality in itself ([Nietzsche, The Joyful Wisdom, GS 78](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/52124)).

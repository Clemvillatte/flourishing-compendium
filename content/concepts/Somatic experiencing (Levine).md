Somatic experiencing is Peter Levine's body-based approach to trauma, set out in Waking the Tiger (1997). Its core mechanism, as later summarized in the 2015 Frontiers paper with Levine as co-author, is the alternating in small doses between activation and a settled resource state — pendulation — held to complete defensive responses interrupted at the time of the threat ([epocheclinical.com summary of Payne, Levine, and Crane-Godreau 2015](https://epocheclinical.com/modalities/somatic-experiencing/)). Levine's own description of the movement:

> "We then move slowly and rhythmically, back and forth, from one to the other in a figure-eight pattern. By beginning with the healing vortex, we pick up the support and resources needed to successfully negotiate the trauma vortex. By moving between these vortices, we release the tightly bound energies at their cores — as if they were being unwound."

([Levine, Waking the Tiger, Part 3, ch. 14, p. 199, quoted at SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/waking-the-tiger/important-quotes/).)

### The two core moves

**Titration** is the dosing: approaching difficult material in the smallest workable increment rather than all at once, borrowed from chemistry, where a substance is added drop by drop so the reaction happens in controlled steps ([EmEase glossary, titration](https://emease.com/glossary/titration)). **Pendulation** is the shape of the movement: shifting attention between that small dose of activation and something calmer, so distress rises and settles instead of building without a break ([EmEase glossary, pendulation](https://emease.com/glossary/pendulation)). The two are paired by design: "pendulation describes the shape of the movement; titration describes the dosing" ([metaphorex.org, pendulation entry](https://www.metaphorex.org/entries/pendulation/)).

### The ground claim

The theory rests on Levine's animal observation: wild animals threatened routinely rarely develop lasting traumatic symptoms, because the nervous system's survival response completes and discharges, where the human version is often interrupted by freezing or helplessness ([Simply Psychology summary](https://www.simplypsychology.com/articles/somatic-experiencing-trauma)). The 2015 Frontiers paper frames the method's attentional core as interoception, tuning to internal body sensation rather than the narrative content of the memory ([EmEase, somatic experiencing](https://emease.com/glossary/somatic-experiencing)). Levine's own one-line epistemic ground: "Healing trauma requires a direct experience of the living, feeling, knowing organism" ([Levine, Waking the Tiger, quoted at Relational Frontier](https://garden.relationalfrontier.com/practices/somatic-experiencing)).

"Pragmatic solidarity" — Bernstein, The Pragmatic Turn.

The pragmatist line on solidarity runs through Rorty's provocation and Bernstein's correction. Rorty framed the choice as solidarity or objectivity: solidarity is something a community builds, not something inquiry discovers. Bernstein's fallibilism keeps the building but refuses the forced choice, treating openness to critique as a virtue of the practice rather than a concession to relativism.

> "In my utopia, human solidarity would be seen not as a fact to be recognised by clearing away 'prejudice' or burrowing down to previously hidden depths but, rather, as a goal to be achieved. It is to be achieved not by inquiry but by imagination, the imaginative ability to see strange people as fellow sufferers. Solidarity is not discovered by reflection but created." (Richard Rorty, Contingency, Irony and Solidarity, p. xvi, [as reprinted in Solidarity in Contingency](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0487.pdf))
>
> "In Bernstein's words: 'fallibilism, in its robust sense, is not a rarified epistemological doctrine. It consists of a set of virtues'." (Bernstein, The Abuse of Evil, p. 29, [as reprinted in Solidarity in Contingency](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0487.pdf))

Rorty's companion line names what the solidarity is made of: "human solidarity is based on a sense of a common danger, not on a common possession or a shared power" (Contingency, Irony and Solidarity, p. 91).

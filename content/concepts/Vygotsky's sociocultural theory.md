Vygotsky's sociocultural theory holds that higher cognition develops first as social interaction and is then internalised, with the zone of proximal development guiding support. The zone is defined in Mind in Society:

> "It is the distance between the actual developmental level as determined by independent problem solving and the level of potential development as determined through problem solving under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers." (p. 86)
> — [Vygotsky 1978, Mind in Society: The Development of Higher Psychological Processes, Harvard University Press, ch. 6 (FAU full PDF)](https://home.fau.edu/musgrove/web/vygotsky1978.pdf)

The zone names what is still forming rather than what has formed: it "defines those functions that have not yet matured but are in the process of maturation, functions that will mature tomorrow but are currently in an embryonic state. These functions could be termed the 'buds' or 'flowers' of development rather than the 'fruits' of development" (p. 86). The chapter's summary line gives the theory its direction: "The actual developmental level characterizes mental development retrospectively, while the zone of proximal development characterizes mental development prospectively" (p. 86). Teaching aimed at the zone works on functions in formation, which is why assessment that only tests independent performance misses them.

## Sources

- [Vygotsky 1978, Mind in Society: The Development of Higher Psychological Processes, Harvard University Press, ch. 6 (FAU full PDF)](https://home.fau.edu/musgrove/web/vygotsky1978.pdf) — the zone-of-proximal-development definition, the buds/flowers passage and the retrospectively/prospectively sentence, quoted verbatim (p. 86)

The anchoring effect is the pulling of a judgment toward a reference point, even when that reference point has no business near the judgment: "a psychological phenomenon in which an individual's judgments or decisions are influenced by a reference point or 'anchor' which can be completely irrelevant" ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). The canonical demonstration belongs to [[CO018]]: Tversky and Kahneman's roulette wheel, stopping on 10 or 65, moved estimates of African countries in the United Nations from an average of 25 percent to 45 percent ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)).

### Why the first number wins

The oldest explanation is adjustment that runs out of steam: people set an anchor, then "use this anchor as a point of reference to adjust their answers," and the theory "explains inaccuracy in guessing by suggesting that people adjust insufficiently, rendering their final guess closer to the anchors" ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). A rival account locates the pull in memory rather than arithmetic: "anchoring is a special case of semantic priming, where the anchor acts as the prime," making anchor-consistent information easier to reach while alternatives are compared against it ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). The two stories conflict on paper; some researchers treat them as complementary ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)).

### Not just trivia questions

The effect survives contact with professionals. In one judicial study, "even experienced legal professionals were affected by anchoring," and "this remained true even when the anchors provided were arbitrary and unrelated to the case in question" ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). Real-estate agents shown a house with different listing prices denied being influenced by the initial price, "but the results showed that both groups were equally influenced by that anchor" ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). Ariely's social security study shows the same indifference to relevance: audience members with higher last-two-digits "would submit bids that were between 60 percent and 120 percent higher than those with the lower social security numbers" ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). Menus exploit it openly: "the high prices at the top of the menu act as anchor values," making the mid-list dishes feel reasonable ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)).

### What helps

Debiasing research is mostly discouraging, with one exception: "the consider-the-opposite (COS strategy) has been the most reliable in mitigating the anchoring bias," asking a person "to consider the possibilities the opposite of their perceptions and beliefs" ([Wikipedia, Anchoring effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anchoring_effect)). For the reader deciding under a first offer or a list price, the practical moves follow: name the anchor for what it is, generate your own estimate before seeing theirs, and reason from the opposite anchor deliberately.

### Why it matters for flourishing

Anchoring matters here because first impressions of value, of people, and of one's own possibilities all work this way. Principle [[CO018]] carries the primary experiment; this page carries the spread of the effect into prices, courts and menus. The flourishing lesson is symmetry: knowing the mechanism helps you discount anchors others set, and helps you notice when the anchor is your own first thought in disguise.

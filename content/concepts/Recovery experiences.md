four ways people recuperate from work during leisure time (Sonnentag and Fritz, 2007).

The Recovery Experience Questionnaire differentiates four recovery experiences: psychological detachment from work, relaxation, mastery, and control. The four emerged as distinct factors in calibration and cross-validation samples totaling 930, and in a subsample of 271 they showed moderate relations with job stressors and psychological well-being. The authors discuss potential applications of the short four-item measures in longitudinal and diary research.

> "Confirmatory factor analyses with a calibration and a cross-validation sample (total N=930) showed that four recovery experiences can be differentiated: psychological detachment from work, relaxation, mastery, and control." (Sonnentag & Fritz, 2007, Journal of Occupational Health Psychology 12(3), 204–221, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17638488/))

The framework's central claim is that recovery is carried by experiences, not by activities. A review of the literature states it this way:

> "Recovery experiences are strategies through which individuals restore their energy resources and maintain well-being despite stressful situations. Sonnentag and Fritz have argued that it is not only the activities themselves, but also the psychological experiences associated with the activities that are relevant for recovery."
> — [Tuisku et al., Industrial Health (2016), PMC4939860](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4939860/)

The same review gives each of the four its one-line definition:

> "Psychological detachment implies disengaging mentally from work during off-job time. Relaxation is a state characterized by low sympathetic activation and increased positive affect. Mastery experiences refer to off-job processes associated with facing new challenges and learning. Control applied to leisure time refers to self-determination in deciding how to spend one's free time."
> — [same source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4939860/)

A later review notes which of the four carries the most consistent evidence:

> "Of these four experiences, detachment seems to be most consistently associated with positive changes in well-being (for reviews, see Sonnentag and Fritz 2015; Wendsche and Lohmann-Haislah 2017)."
> — [Virtanen et al., International Archives of Occupational and Environmental Health (2019), PMC7007884](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7007884/)

What the sources do not test: the four-experience framework is measured with the REQ and its translations; the compendium's everyday use (trying different breaks and comparing how fatigue changes) applies the taxonomy without testing it.

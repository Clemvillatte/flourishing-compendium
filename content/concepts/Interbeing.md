Interbeing is Thich Nhat Hanh's word for the dependence of things on one another. In an excerpt from The Art of Living he recalls looking for an English word to describe "our deep interconnection with everything else", liking the word "togetherness", and finally coming up with "interbeing": "To be" is always to "inter-be." A book of his titled Interbeing appears in the front-matter list of his other books in The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching.

> "We are empty of a separate, independent self. We cannot be by ourselves alone. We can only inter-be with everything else in the cosmos." (Thich Nhat Hanh, [The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WSB8yRfJtDfY1JmfXzHLHdVr4LiDzAcV), ch. 19, "The Three Doors of Liberation", originally published by Parallax Press in 1998)

### Emptiness and practice

In chapter 19, Nhat Hanh explains emptiness through the absence of a separate self and connects it with the classical doctrine: "Emptiness does not mean nonexistence. It means Interdependent Co-Arising, impermanence, and nonself." Chapter 8 says the same of the teachings as a set: "Interbeing is an important characteristic of all the Buddha's teachings. When you touch one, you touch all."

Chapter 16 applies the word to practice. There he writes that it is "not possible to practice one element of the Noble Eightfold Path without practicing all seven other elements", which he calls the nature of interbeing.

“Katastematic pleasure” — Epicurus, Key Doctrines.

Katastematic pleasure is the pleasure of a settled state rather than of a process. In the Letter to Menoeceus, Epicurus defines the pleasure he takes as the goal in just this way:

> By pleasure we mean the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul. (Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus, [epicurus.net text](http://www.epicurus.net/en/menoeceus.html))

The letter is explicit that this is not indulgence. Pleasure as the end and aim does not mean "the pleasures of the prodigal or the pleasures of sensuality", and a pleasant life is produced not by "an unbroken succession of drinking-bouts and of revelry" but by "sober reasoning, searching out the grounds of every choice and avoidance". The person with a clear understanding of these things directs every preference and aversion toward "health of body and tranquillity of mind, seeing that this is the sum and end of a blessed life". Wisdom is the beginning and the greatest good, and "we cannot live pleasantly without living wisely, honorably, and justly; nor live wisely, honorably, and justly without living pleasantly".

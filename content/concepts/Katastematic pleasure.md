“Katastematic pleasure” — Epicurus, Key Doctrines.

Katastematic pleasure, in Epicurus's ethics, is the pleasure of a settled state rather than of a process. The Stanford Encyclopedia's account of the ethics explains that happiness (eudaimonia), according to Epicurus, is "a form of pleasure in its own right — what Epicurus called catastematic or (following Cicero’s Latin translation) ‘static’ as opposed to ‘kinetic’ pleasure" ([Epicurus, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/)):

> Catastematic pleasure, on the contrary, is (or is taken in) a state rather than a process: it is the pleasure that accompanies well-being as such. ([Epicurus, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/))

The complementary class, kinetic pleasures, "seem to be of the non-necessary kind", "such as those resulting from agreeable odors or sounds, rather than deriving from replenishment, as in the case of hunger or thirst", and the encyclopedia notes that "the precise nature of this distinction is debated" ([Epicurus, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/)). Epicurus's own definition in the Letter to Menoeceus gives the state-pleasure its body-and-soul form:

> By pleasure we mean the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul. (Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus, [epicurus.net text](http://www.epicurus.net/en/menoeceus.html))

The letter is explicit that this is not indulgence. Pleasure as the end and aim does not mean "the pleasures of the prodigal or the pleasures of sensuality", and a pleasant life is produced not by "an unbroken succession of drinking-bouts and of revelry" but by "sober reasoning, searching out the grounds of every choice and avoidance". The person with a clear understanding of these things directs every preference and aversion toward "health of body and tranquillity of mind, seeing that this is the sum and end of a blessed life". Wisdom is the beginning and the greatest good, and "we cannot live pleasantly without living wisely, honorably, and justly; nor live wisely, honorably, and justly without living pleasantly" ([Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus](http://www.epicurus.net/en/menoeceus.html)).

“Vitality” — Ryan and Frederick, 1997.

Vitality, in the sense the compendium uses, is subjective vitality: the felt sense of being alive and having energy available for what you care about. Richard Ryan and Christina Frederick gave the construct its modern psychological form in 1997, inside self-determination theory, and built the scale researchers still use. Its interest for a writer on flourishing is that vitality behaves like an indicator light for the whole system: it responds to body (illness, sleep, pain) and to motivation (energy rises with autonomous pursuits and drains under controlled ones), so a drop can point at either.

> "Ryan and Frederick (1997, p. 530) defined subjective vitality as the 'experience of possessing energy and aliveness,' or the feeling of having energy available to the self." (Frederick and Ryan, [The Energy behind Human Flourishing](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/b155eafb-778a-47b5-92cb-1c1a7ef715cb/content), Oxford Handbook of Self-Determination Theory)

### What it is not

Vitality is not arousal and not the absence of fatigue alone. Anger and anxiety activate, and both sit apart from vitality, which is energy you can harness for purposive action. The compendium pairs it with hau, the Māori concept of vital essence carried in breath and land, in principle R039: both traditions treat aliveness as something received, tended and spent rather than simply owned.

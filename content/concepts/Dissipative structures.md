Dissipative structures are ordered patterns that exist only while energy or matter flows through them. The term is Ilya Prigogine's, developed with Isabelle Stengers for a general readership in Order Out of Chaos. The 1977 Nobel Prize in Chemistry cited Prigogine "for his contributions to non-equilibrium thermodynamics, particularly the theory of dissipative structures" ([Nobel Prize summary](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/1977/summary/)). His one-sentence version of the discovery:

> "It is shown that non-equilibrium may become a source of order and that irreversible processes may lead to a new type of dynamic states of matter called 'dissipative structures'." (Prigogine, [Nobel Lecture, 1977](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/prigogine-lecture.pdf), abstract)

### Order fed by flow

The classical picture had equilibrium as the only home of order: crystals, quiet systems at rest. Prigogine's examples live otherwise. A fluid heated from below stays at rest until a critical temperature gradient is crossed; then convection begins and a pattern of rolling cells appears, in which "a macroscopic number of molecules have to move in a coherent fashion over macroscopic times to realize the flow pattern." In his words, "above some critical value certain fluctuations are amplified and give rise to a macroscopic current. A new supermolecular order appears which corresponds basically to a giant fluctuation stabilized by exchanges of energy with the outside world. This is the order characterized by the occurrence of 'dissipative structures'" ([Nobel Lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2018/06/prigogine-lecture.pdf)). Cut the flow and the structure goes with it, which is what separates it from a crystal.

His compact statement of the machinery: "There are three aspects which are always linked in dissipative structures: the function as expressed by the chemical equations, the space-time structure, which results from the instabilities, and the fluctuations, which trigger the instabilities" (same text). Order Out of Chaos, written with Stengers, carries this thermodynamics into a wider argument about nature and time.

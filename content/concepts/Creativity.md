Creativity is the making of what is new and valuable. The compendium's traditions read it as life affirming itself: Bergson's élan, Dewey's growth, Nietzsche's value-creation. Psychology's anchor review, Beth Hennessey and Teresa Amabile's 2010 synthesis, treats the study of it as essential to human progress.

> "The psychological study of creativity is essential to human progress. If strides are to be made in the sciences, humanities, and arts, we must arrive at a far more detailed understanding of the creative process, its antecedents, and its inhibitors." (Hennessey & Amabile 2010, [Annual Review of Psychology](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.psych.093008.100416))

A recent review in Frontiers in Psychology puts the working definition in plain terms: "It is the ability to generate novel ideas and frameworks and to embed them in forms that can be shared with others", a capacity it credits across science, technology, the arts, education, industry and everyday collaboration, citing Hennessey and Amabile. The 2010 review's own verdict on its field is double-edged: research has "grown theoretically and methodologically sophisticated", yet the field shows "a growing fragmentation", and deeper understanding needs "more interdisciplinary research, based on a systems view of creativity that recognizes a variety of interrelated forces operating at multiple levels". The compendium keeps both notes in view: creativity is a real, studied capacity, and its study is, in the reviewers' word, a fragmented one.

**Integrative Theory of Communication Work** — Kramer — communication about work builds shared meaning and identity among members.

The seed's attribution and description do not match the literature found. The Integrative Theory of Communication Work (ITCW) was introduced by Erin Donovan and Abigail Hazlett in Human Communication Research in 2024, not by Kramer, and it concerns communication as effortful labour during a health stressor, not shared meaning and identity in organisations. No source opened for this page connects the name to Michael Kramer. Kramer's organisational-communication work, which does deal with shared meaning, is a separate body of scholarship and is not covered here.

### What the theory says

> "There is extensive evidence that when people are experiencing health stressors, they are also coping with communication stressors." (Donovan & Hazlett, The labor of communicatively coping: toward an Integrative Theory of Communication Work, Human Communication Research 50(2):230-239, [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article/50/2/230/7468099))

The authors propose "a more defined way of theorizing how people encounter and manage communicative demands". The theory draws on the transactional model of stress and coping and on illness trajectory theory. Among its listed assumptions are:

- Communication is effortful and stressful when the stakes are high and the demands of the situation exceed the resources to cope with them.
- Communication work is divisible labour and can be shared.
- Communication work is carried out because of perceived benefits that outweigh perceived costs.

The authors describe the theory as "an interpretive theory" concerned with "describing socially constructed realities in localized settings".

### Early empirical work

A 2024 follow-up paper developed a communication work scale from a post-positivist standpoint, using a sample of American adults with chronic health conditions. Factor analyses gave stable structures, and the project produced "preliminary quantitative evidence" for the theorised relationships among effort, preparation, duty, division of labour and costs and benefits. ([Advancing the Integrative Theory of Communication Work, Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10410236.2024.2428934)) The theory is new and the evidence is early.

### What this page does not claim

Nothing here shows that the theory describes how work-related communication builds shared meaning or identity. A supervisor may wish to rename the seed gloss, or to move the compendium's principles to a concept that matches them, such as an organisational-identification page.

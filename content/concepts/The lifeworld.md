The lifeworld (Lebenswelt) is Husserl's name for the world as lived before any theory touches it: the world of home, work, tools and other people that we take for granted and act within. The Stanford Encyclopedia gives the plainest rendering:

> "The lifeworld is, not surprisingly, the world we live and feel at home in. It is the world of experience, which we are all acquainted with, which we take for granted and which forms the basis for our daily actions" (Zahavi, [Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

Wikipedia's formulation stresses what makes it philosophical bedrock: it is "a universe of what is self-evident or given, a world that subjects may experience together" ([Wikipedia, Lifeworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifeworld)). Husserl introduced the concept in The Crisis of European Sciences and Transcendental Phenomenology (1936), though it names something every person already knows firsthand.

### The home of the natural attitude

The lifeworld is where consciousness starts, not where it ends up. It is "a subject-relative world that contains objects with affordances—cups to drink from, spoons to eat with, chairs to sit on, trees to climb in or seek shelter under," and at the same time "a historically and culturally shaped world, a world of cumulative traditions and sedimented meaning that we as bodily subjects are anchored and socialized into" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/)). Ordinarily we do not even notice this background: "In ordinary life we are so absorbed in the world that we simply take its reality for granted," a stance Husserl calls the natural attitude ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/)). The phenomenological reduction does not leave this world; it returns to it, now seen for what it is. That is why [[The natural attitude]] is the home the suspension returns to.

### The soil of science

The Crisis argument is Husserl's answer to scientism: the very sciences that claim to replace experience stand on it. As Husserl argues, "we should never forget that scientifically valid knowledge has first-person plural intentionality as its precondition" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/)). The same world, seen two ways:

> "the difference between the world of perceptual experience and the world as determined by the natural sciences is not a difference between the world for us and the world in itself, but rather a difference between two ways in which one and the same world can appear to us" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

Science's models have their roots here:

> "Its abstract idealized models have their roots in the lifeworld, which serves as the soil from which scientific concepts grow, and depend not only on the world of immediate, perceptual, experience, but also on a long tradition of cultural and linguistic acquisitions and accomplishments" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

Late Husserl adds the cross-cultural edge: everyone lives from a homeworld, and only the encounter with an alienworld reveals it as one perspective among others ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Edmund Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/)).

### Descendants

Merleau-Ponty made the lived body the center of the same program: the "fundamental philosophical act" is "to 'return to the lived world beneath the objective world'" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Merleau-Ponty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/merleau-ponty/)). Habermas gave the term a sociological career: the lifeworld is "a background stock of cultural knowledge that is 'always already' familiar to agents" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Habermas](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/habermas/)), and his warning is now a standard tool of social critique: "the whole program of subjecting the lifeworld to the imperatives of the market must be subject to scrutiny" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Habermas](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/habermas/)).

### Why it matters for flourishing

My principle [[E004 — Practise the lifeworld (Lebenswelt)]] treats attention to this level as a discipline, not a nostalgia trip. Suffering that medicine misses (meaning, home, rhythm, recognition) often lives here, and wisdom has no other material to work with. The page pairs naturally with [[D081 — Recognize the relativity of perspectives]]: the homeworld feels absolute until another one shows up.

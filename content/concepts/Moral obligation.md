Moral obligation is the sense that one must act, whatever one feels. The seed cites Batson (2011) on obligation and empathy. In the sources I opened, Batson does not use the phrase as a heading; his nearest category is principlism, "motivation with the ultimate goal of upholding some moral principle—for example, a principle of fairness or justice, or the utilitarian principle of greatest good for the greatest number" (Batson 2011, p. 220, quoted in the [Stanford Encyclopedia, Empirical Approaches to Altruism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2021/entries/altruism-empirical/index.html)).

### Batson on empathy versus principle

Batson's book tests whether people can "care for others for their sakes (altruism) rather than simply for our own (egoism)". Its theory "centers on the empathy-altruism hypothesis, which claims that other-oriented feelings of sympathy and compassion for a person in need (empathic concern) produce motivation with the ultimate goal of having that need removed" ([Altruism in Humans, Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/1802)). A blog summary lists his four prosocial motives: egoism, empathy-altruism, collectivism and principlism, and says principlism "helps others out of duty to an impersonal principal" ([Global Dashboard summary](https://globaldashboard.org/2011/02/17/the-uses-and-abuses-of-altruism/)).

The Stanford Encyclopedia quotes Batson: "To the best of my knowledge, there is no clear empirical evidence that upholding justice (or any other moral principle) functions as an ultimate goal. Nor is there clear empirical evidence that rules this possibility out" (Batson 2011, p. 224). So the empirical case for duty as a motive is open in his own account.

A related chapter finds that empathy-induced altruism "is not always a friend of the common good" and, in some social dilemmas, "can pose a more powerful threat than does self-interested egoism" ([Batson, Friend or Foe of the Common Good?, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1057/9780230116269_3?error=cookies_not_supported&code=b19c0c2f-f06a-4e34-88df-5cf8bd97e70d)). That is one reason a sense of obligation can matter beside feeling.

### The philosophical sense

For Kant, "the fundamental principle at the basis of all of our moral duties is a categorical imperative", categorical "in virtue of applying to us unconditionally", and "morality is 'duty' for human beings because it is possible... for our desires and interests to run counter to its demands" ([Kant's Moral Philosophy, Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-moral/)). Supererogation is the contrasting class of actions that go "beyond the call of duty": "morally good although not (strictly) required" ([Supererogation, Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/supererogation/)).

### Caution

Do not present Batson as defining moral obligation. Cite him for the empirical study of empathic and principled motives, and cite the philosophical entries for the concept of duty.

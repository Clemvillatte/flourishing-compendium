**Anxiety buffer disruption theory** — Pyszczynski, Kesebir — reminders of mortality normally defend via culture; when that buffer is broken, anxiety and defensiveness intensify.

## Field

Sociocultural · Psychological science

The theory is Pyszczynski and Kesebir's 2011 application of terror management theory to trauma. The abstract states the claim: "It posits that PTSD results from a disruption in one's anxiety-buffering mechanisms, which normally provide protection against anxiety in general and death anxiety in particular. The disruption of these mechanisms leaves the individual defenseless in the face of overwhelming anxiety, which leads to the major symptom clusters of PTSD: re-experiencing, hyper-arousal, and avoidance" ([Pyszczynski and Kesebir 2011, PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20924831/)).

### What the buffer is

Terror management theory supplies the protective system the disruption hits. The potential for terror is managed by "an anxiety-buffering system consisting of cultural worldviews, self-esteem, and close interpersonal relationships," which "mitigate existential terror by imparting a sense that one is a person of value living in a meaningful world" ([Landau and Pyszczynski, TMT and COVID-19, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7498956/)). The 2016 summary in Current Directions: "Traumatic life events undermine the capacity of one's cultural worldview, self-esteem, and close relationships to manage anxiety, rendering people unable to cope with the distress produced by these events" ([Pyszczynski and Taylor, "When the Buffer Breaks," SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0963721416645537)).

### The climate connection

My principle on treating climate change as a sociocultural stressor is grounded in the same literature. The COVID-19 analysis gives the pattern: "compromised anxiety buffers leave people vulnerable to experiencing higher levels of death anxiety than usual," when the threat simultaneously undermines access to the parts of the buffer — social connection, institutional trust, routine — that would normally absorb it ([Landau and Pyszczynski, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7498956/)). Climate threat operates the same way: the awareness it raises is difficult to manage because the threat itself disrupts the buffers.

The polis is Aristotle's word for the self-governing community in which a full human life becomes possible. His Politics opens by arguing that the polis is not a contract but a natural end, the thing households and villages grow toward.

> "Thus also the city-state is prior in nature to the household and to each of us individually."
> — Aristotle, Politics I.2, 1253a18-19, Rackham translation, via [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0058:book=1:section=1253a)

The word for this priority is that the whole comes before the part. Aristotle's reasoning is teleological rather than mechanical: the polis is what the earlier partnerships are for, so it is more fully what they are than they are themselves. The claim is not that people do not exist before the city, but that they do not complete themselves outside it.

### The political animal argument

Aristotle's ground for the polis's naturalness is a claim about speech rather than about sociability alone. Animals have voice, enough to signal pleasure and pain. Humans alone have logos, and logos is what picks out the advantageous and the harmful, the just and the unjust. Shared judgement of that kind is what makes a household and a city, not just a herd. The passage's most famous line, that a person outside the polis by nature rather than misfortune is "either a lower animal or a god", is where the argument lands: someone who cannot or will not enter the partnership is either below the human form or has outgrown it.

> "It is clear therefore that the state is also prior by nature to the individual; for if each individual when separate is not self-sufficient, he must be related to the whole state as other parts are to their whole, while a man who is incapable of entering into partnership, or who is so self-sufficing that he has no need to do so, is no part of a state, so that he must be either a lower animal or a god."
> — Aristotle, Politics I.2, 1253a25-30, via [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.01.0058:book=1:section=1253a)

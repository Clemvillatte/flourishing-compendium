James Marcia built on Erikson's account of the adolescent identity crisis and gave it a measurable structure. In his 1966 study, 86 college men were interviewed, and each was placed in one of four statuses along two dimensions, crisis and commitment:

> "Criteria for inclusion in 1 of 4 identity statuses were the presence of crisis and commitment in the areas of occupation and ideology." (Marcia, [Development and Validation of Ego-Identity Status](https://archive.org/download/marcia1966/marcia1966.pdf), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 3(5), 1966, abstract)

The four statuses are identity achievement, moratorium, foreclosure, and identity diffusion. For diffusion, Marcia specifies:

> "The identity-diffusion subject may or may not have experienced a crisis period; his hallmark is a lack of commitment." (Marcia, [1966](https://archive.org/download/marcia1966/marcia1966.pdf), the identity-diffusion paragraph)

Marcia describes foreclosure as follows:

> "A foreclosure subject is distinguished by not having experienced a crisis, yet expressing commitment. It is difficult to tell where his parents' goals for him leave off and where his begin. He is becoming what others have prepared or intended him to become as a child." (Marcia, [1966](https://archive.org/download/marcia1966/marcia1966.pdf), the foreclosure paragraph)

On how the statuses behaved in the study, the results section reports ([Marcia, 1966](https://archive.org/download/marcia1966/marcia1966.pdf)): "It appears that foreclosure subjects tend to maintain high goals in spite of failure." The abstract adds that those in the status characterized by adherence to parental wishes "subscribed significantly more to authoritarian values" ([Marcia, 1966](https://archive.org/download/marcia1966/marcia1966.pdf)).

Self-distancing, in Kross's formulation, is a process that allows people to transcend their egocentric viewpoint when reasoning about an experience, viewing themselves from the perspective of a distanced observer, akin to a fly on the wall ([Kross, Gard, Deldin, Clifton & Ayduk 2012, Journal of Applied Psychology 97(3), 771-784 (RASCL Berkeley PDF)](https://rascl.studentorg.berkeley.edu/assets/files/kross_etal_2012_jap.pdf)). The Kross et al. 2014 paper tests one language-based way of increasing it. The abstract states: "Studies 1a and 1b were proof-of-principle studies. They demonstrated that using non-first-person pronouns and one's own name (rather than first-person pronouns) during introspection enhances self-distancing" ([Kross et al. 2014, JPSP 106(2), 304-324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/)).

The same abstract concludes: "Together, these findings demonstrate that small shifts in the language people use to refer to the self during introspection consequentially influence their ability to regulate their thoughts, feelings, and behavior under social stress, even for vulnerable individuals" ([Kross et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/)).

The finding is that the shift in pronoun does the regulatory work.

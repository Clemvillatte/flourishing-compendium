**Regulatory mode theory** — Higgins, Kruglanski — behaviour is steered by two modes, locomotion (doing) and assessment (evaluating); balance predicts wellbeing.

The theory's founding paper splits self-regulation into two functions, each with its own scale. One spouse hunts for the perfect parking spot; the other wants to get moving. Both are doing regulation.

> "Assessment constitutes the comparative aspect of self-regulation that critically evaluates alternative goals or means to decide which are best to pursue and appraises performance." (Kruglanski et al. 2000, [paper PDF, Columbia](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/higgins/papers/kruglanski%202000%20rmode.pdf))
>
> "Locomotion constitutes the aspect of self-regulation concerned with movement from state to state, including commitment of psychological resources to initiate and maintain such movement." (Kruglanski et al. 2000, [paper PDF, Columbia](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/higgins/papers/kruglanski%202000%20rmode.pdf))

Later work tied the modes to outcomes: assessment predicts procrastination and inaction inertia, while locomotion predicts steady progress.

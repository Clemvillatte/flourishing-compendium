Spinoza's Ethics, Part IV, builds a portrait of the free person (homo liber): the one who lives by reason rather than fear. The title claim — that such a person loves all — is the part's teaching that the virtuous desire the good for everyone.

> "The good which every man, who follows after virtue, desires for himself he will also desire for other men, and so much the more, in proportion as he has a greater knowledge of God." (Spinoza, Ethics IV, Prop. XXXVII, Elwes translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/971/971-0.txt))

### Who the free person is

The part defines the free person through reason: one who "lives under the guidance of reason", "is not led by fear", and "directly desires that which is good" (Prop. LXVII proof). The propositions that follow give the portrait its edge: the free person "thinks of death least of all things; and his wisdom is a meditation not of death but of life" (Prop. LXVII), and "never acts fraudulently, but always in good faith" (Prop. LXXII).

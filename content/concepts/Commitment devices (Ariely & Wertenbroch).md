## In plain words

A commitment device is a way of binding your future self: you arrange, in advance, for the wrong choice to become costly or impossible, so the intention you hold now survives the weakness you will have later. Odysseus tied to the mast is the type scene.

## Where it comes from

The general definition: a commitment device "is a mechanism or arrangement that implements precommitment by deliberately restricting one's own future options in order to make a commitment credible" ([Wikipedia, Commitment device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_device)). The concept "emerged independently in the mid-1950s, when Thomas Schelling analyzed commitment as a bargaining tactic and Robert Strotz modeled how a rational agent might benefit from constraining future choices" ([Wikipedia, Commitment device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_device)). The compendium's original entry cites Ariely and Wertenbroch's 2002 study of deadlines as a framework reference; no specific findings from that paper are claimed below.

## How it works

The device is "a specific implementation of precommitment, in which a person or group arranges, in advance, for certain future options to be restricted, costly, or unavailable, in order to change current or future behavior" ([Wikipedia, Commitment device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_device)). The logic runs in two contexts: "game theory, where the device is directed at influencing another party... and behavioral economics, where it is directed at one's own future self to overcome weakness of will" — the general who destroys a bridge, and "a person who deposits money into an account with withdrawal penalties forces their future self to bear a cost for breaking the commitment" ([Wikipedia, Commitment device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_device)).

## What it explains

The device explains how people counter time-inconsistent preferences in the behavioral-economics version: today's planner sets the terms that tomorrow's tempted self must live with. The evidence base is young and uneven: commitment contracts "have produced meaningful increases in savings in developing-country field trials, but evidence for sustained effects on health behaviors such as exercise and smoking cessation is more limited," and a Philippine smoking-cessation contract "increased quit rates, but take-up was modest" ([Wikipedia, Commitment device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_device)).

## Everyday life

The article's mechanism in plain terms: a person "accepts a penalty for failing to meet a self-chosen goal," so the failure becomes costly in advance ([Wikipedia, Commitment device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_device)).

Amour de soi is Rousseau's name for the natural love of one's own being: the drive of self-preservation before society reshapes it. In the SEP's summary of the Discourse on Inequality, "Rousseau attributes to all creatures an instinctual drive towards self-preservation. Human beings therefore have such a drive, which he terms amour de soi (self love)," a drive that "directs us first to attend to our most basic biological needs for things like food, shelter and warmth" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rousseau/)).

### Versus amour-propre

The concept earns its keep in the contrast with amour-propre, the comparative self-love that appears with social life. Amour-propre is the "new type of self-interested drive, concerned with comparative success or failure as a social being," which "make[s] the need to be recognized by others as having value and to be treated with respect central to the felt interests of each human being" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rousseau/)). In unequal societies this need turns toxic: subordinates misrepresent themselves to superiors, and even the flattered find no satisfaction in the adulation. Rousseau held that amour-propre tends toward "toxic and self-defeating ('inflamed') forms," while education and social arrangements could in principle let it take "a benign character" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rousseau/)).

### The moderator: pity

Amour de soi is not selfishness run loose; in the state of nature it is tempered by pitié. In Cole's translation of the Discourse: "It is therefore certain that pity is a natural sentiment, which, by moderating in every individual the activity of self-love, contributes to the mutual preservation of the whole species" ([Rousseau, Discourse on Inequality, Cole translation, Project Gutenberg #11136](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11136/pg11136.txt)).

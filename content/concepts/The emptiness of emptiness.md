“The emptiness of emptiness” — Nagarjuna, MMK 13.

### What it is

In chapter thirteen of the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, Nagarjuna turns his analysis on his own central term. Emptiness is the claim that things lack an essential nature, and chapter thirteen asks what follows if someone starts treating emptiness itself as a thing with an essence. The argument is conditional: "If a bit of the non-empty existed, a bit of the empty would also exist. If there did not exist a bit of the non-empty, how could the empty exist?" (Nagarjuna, MMK 13.7, trans. Batchelor, [Verses from the Centre](https://www.leighb.com/VfromC.htm)). The empty is defined only against the non-empty, so emptiness cannot stand as an independent metaphysical item.

### What it carries

The chapter closes with the warning that gives the concept its name: "The Conquerors taught emptiness as the forsaking of all views. Those who view emptiness are taught to be without realisation" (Nagarjuna, MMK 13.8, trans. Batchelor, [Verses from the Centre](https://www.leighb.com/VfromC.htm)). My reading of the warning is medicinal: emptiness was prescribed as the relinquishing of fixed positions, and a patient who takes the medicine as a new position to hold has made the medicine into the disease. The emptiness of emptiness is therefore not a deeper emptiness behind emptiness; it is the instruction to apply the analysis to the analysis itself, so that the teaching does not become one more essence to defend.

My B141 principle uses the same warning as a check on argument: write down what a claim using the word "emptiness" means, then apply the same scrutiny to that written claim. The everyday form is the person who answers every question with "everything is empty" and has thereby stopped asking.

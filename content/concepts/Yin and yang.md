Yin and yang (陰陽) are the paired complementary principles that run through Chinese philosophy and medicine: yin names the shady, receptive, cooling, contracting side of a polarity; yang the sunny, active, warming, expanding side. The earliest Chinese characters for yin and yang appear on oracle bones (skeletal remains used in ancient Chinese divination at least as early as the 14th century BCE), where they simply described natural phenomena such as weather, especially the movement of the sun; in their earliest usages, yin and yang existed independently and were not connected ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)). The first written record of the two characters together appears in a verse from the Shijing (Book of Songs): "Viewing the scenery at a hill, looking for yinyang", indicating that yang is the sunny side and yin is the shady side of a hill ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)). The concept later became one of the dominant concepts shared by different schools throughout Chinese philosophy ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)).

Three basic themes underlie nearly all deployments of the concept:

- yinyang as the coherent fabric of nature and mind, exhibited in all existence
- yinyang as jiao (interaction) between the waxing and waning of the cosmic and human realms
- yinyang as a process of harmonization ensuring a constant, dynamic balance of all things

None of these conceptions ranks the two sides: yin and yang carry valuational equality rooted in the unified, dynamic, harmonized structure of the cosmos ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)). Its cosmogony:

> "The Tao produced One; One produced Two; Two produced Three; Three produced All things. All things leave behind them the Obscurity (out of which they have come), and go forward to embrace the Brightness (into which they have emerged), while they are harmonised by the Breath of Vacancy." (Daodejing 42, [Legge translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

Yin and yang differ from Western moral dualisms such as good-versus-evil or spirit-versus-matter: those rank their two sides, while yin and yang hold that each requires the other, yang generated from yin and yin from yang, mutually alternating ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)). They are processes rather than fixed essences: the most enduring interpretation relates them to qi as a dynamic, natural form of flowing energy, and the medical texts subdivide each organ into yin and yang aspects — the activity or function of an organ is its yang aspect, its substance the yin aspect ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)).

### Practical implications

The practical value of yin-yang thinking is diagnostic: it asks whether a problem is one of excess or deficiency, of over-activation or stagnation, and seeks dynamic balance rather than elimination of one side. The medical texts are explicit that yin should flow smoothly and yang vivify steadily, regulating themselves to maintain equilibrium ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/yinyang/)). For flourishing, this suggests that rest and activity, solitude and connection, effort and ease are complements, each meaningless without the other. Health, whether of a body, a relationship, or a working life, is a balance continually re-tuned; it never simply stays achieved.

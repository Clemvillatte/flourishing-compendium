In the Rinzai line of Zen, a student receives a single assigned kōan, most often Joshū's reply "Mu" to a monk's question whether a dog has Buddha-nature, and works that one case as the entry into practice. The Gateless Gate, the collection named in the seed, lists "Joshu's Dog" as its first case ([Wikisource index](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Gateless_Gate)).

### The assigned word

> "A kōan is formulated like a riddle or puzzle and is designed in such a way that intellectual reasoning alone cannot solve it without breaking through the barrier of ego-consciousness by driving it to its limit." — Nagatomo, [Japanese Zen Buddhist Philosophy, §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/japanese-zen/)

Rinzai training begins by assigning a hosshi, a breakthrough kōan, usually the mu-kōan or "the sound of one hand", and students are told to concentrate on the "word-head", the phrase itself ([Wikipedia, "Koan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C5%8Dan)). The Stanford Encyclopedia adds that the practitioner must tackle the Mu "by mobilizing the whole of his or her person" ([Nagatomo, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/japanese-zen/)).

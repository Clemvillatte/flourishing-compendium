Right view (Pali sammā diṭṭhi) is the first factor of the Buddhist Eightfold Path: seeing things as they are rather than as craving and habit present them. Britannica summarises the factor as "an accurate understanding of the nature of things, specifically the Four Noble Truths" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Eightfold-Path)). The Pali discourses define the view by what a person who holds it discerns:

> "When a disciple of the noble ones discerns stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, and the way of practice leading to the cessation of stress, then he is a person of right view, one whose view is made straight, who is endowed with verified confidence in the Dhamma, and who has arrived at this true Dhamma." (MN 9, Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta, Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.009.than.html))

### Two grades of view

The Great Forty discourse splits the factor in two. The mundane grade is a working conviction about how the world runs: there is what is given and sacrificed, actions have fruits, this world and the next exist. The noble grade is a path factor, the discernment of a person developing the noble path, and the same discourse makes the factor the hinge of the whole training:

> "Of those, right view is the forerunner. And how is right view the forerunner? In one of right view, wrong view is abolished. The many evil, unskillful qualities that come into play with wrong view as their condition are also abolished, while the many skillful qualities that have right view as their condition go to the culmination of their development." (MN 117, Mahācattārīsaka Sutta, [Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.117.than.html))

Because view comes first, a wrong view does not merely produce wrong conclusions; it aims the rest of the path, and effort spent under it lands somewhere other than intended.

Right view (Pali sammā diṭṭhi) is the first factor of the Buddhist Eightfold Path. Britannica summarises the factor as "an accurate understanding of the nature of things, specifically the Four Noble Truths" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Eightfold-Path)). The Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta defines the view by what a person who holds it discerns, in the sutta's own abbreviated formula:

> "When a disciple of the noble ones discerns stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, and the way of practice leading to the cessation of stress, then he is a person of right view... who has arrived at this true Dhamma." (MN 9, Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta, Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.009.than.html))

The Kaccānagotta Sutta gives the view a middle-way test: avoiding the extremes of "everything exists" and "everything doesn't exist", it knows just stress arising and passing.

### Two forms of right view

The Mahācattārīsaka Sutta distinguishes a form of right view with effluents, siding with merit, from a form that is noble, without effluents, transcendent, a factor of the path. The everyday form is spelled out as a set of convictions: there is what is given, offered and sacrificed; good and bad actions have fruits and results; this world and the next exist. The transcendent form is the discernment of a person developing the noble path. The same discourse calls right view the forerunner of the skillful qualities that depend on it:

> "Of those, right view is the forerunner. And how is right view the forerunner? In one of right view, wrong view is abolished. The many evil, unskillful qualities that come into play with wrong view as their condition are also abolished, while the many skillful qualities that have right view as their condition go to the culmination of their development." (MN 117, Mahācattārīsaka Sutta, [Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.117.than.html))

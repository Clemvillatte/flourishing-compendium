### Definition

Personal engagement is the presence of a person in a role: doing the work while also being in it, physically, cognitively, and emotionally. William Kahn gave the construct its classic form after studying camp counsellors and architects, defining it as:

> Personal engagement is the simultaneous employment and expression of a person's "preferred self" in task behaviors that promote connections to work and to others, personal presence (physical, cognitive, and emotional), and active, full role performances. ([Kahn 1990](https://ia600108.us.archive.org/view_archive.php?archive=/24/items/wikipedia-scholarly-sources-corpus/10.2307%252F25027810.zip&file=10.2307%252F256287.pdf))

### Origin

Kahn's 1990 Academy of Management Journal paper asked when people bring their selves into a role and when they hold back. His answer ran through three conditions people tacitly test: how meaningful it is to bring themselves in, how safe it is to do so, and how available they are. Disengagement is the mirror image, "the uncoupling" of selves from roles: people withdraw and defend the self instead of employing and expressing it. The idea seeded the later, broader literature on work engagement.

### A real-life example

Two teachers deliver the same lesson. One performs it, eyes on the clock, self parked at the door. The other argues the examples with real delight, remembers a student's question from Tuesday, and leaves tired but full. Same role, same duties; different selves present. Students know the difference within minutes.

### Related concepts

- [[Flow]]: full absorption in a task, the experiential core Kahn's engagement overlaps.
- [[Psychological safety]]: the felt permission to show one's self without cost.
- [[Burnout]]: the long defeat of the self that engagement guards against.

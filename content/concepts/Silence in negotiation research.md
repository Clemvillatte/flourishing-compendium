Silence in negotiation research is the experimental study of what silent pauses do in bargaining. The central paper is Curhan, Overbeck, Cho, Zhang and Yang's "Silence is golden" (Journal of Applied Psychology, 2022), which defines an "extended silence" as any period of silence lasting for at least 3 s and treats it as a dyad-level variable: "an extended silence can only occur when both parties are silent, regardless of which party initiates it".

> "Study 1 reveals a direct correlation between naturally occurring silent pauses lasting at least 3 s (extended silence) and value creation behaviors and outcomes. Study 2 shows that instructing one or both parties to use extended silence leads to value creation." (Curhan et al., [Silence is golden, Journal of Applied Psychology 107(1), 2022, author-hosted PDF](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Publications/Curhan_Silence_is_Golden.pdf), abstract)

### Why silence works here

The authors test two accounts. On the internal reflection perspective, silence interrupts default, fixed-pie thinking and produces a more deliberative mindset, which creates value (finding trades both sides want). On the social perception perspective, silence intimidates and helps claim value from the counterpart. The data supported the first: Studies 3 and 4 found greater deliberative mindset and reduced fixed-pie perceptions among silence users, and the authors "find no evidence for any associations between extended silence and the proportion of value claimed or subjective value of the counterpart". One boundary condition appeared in Study 3: "when status differences are salient, the use of silence by higher status parties leads to value creation, whereas the use of silence by lower status parties does not". In Study 4, instructing negotiators to use silence worked better for value creation than instructing them to problem-solve.

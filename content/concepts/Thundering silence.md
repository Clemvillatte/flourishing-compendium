“Thundering silence” — Vimalakirti Sutra.

The phrase names the most famous moment of the Vimalakirti Sutra. In the chapter on nonduality, the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī observes that every explanation of nonduality is itself dualistic, since explaining anything uses words against what they point at. Asked for his own account, Vimalakirti answers with nothing:

> "Thereupon, the Licchavi Vimalakīrti kept his silence, saying nothing at all." (The Teaching of Vimalakīrti, Toh 176, 8.34, trans. Robert A. F. Thurman)

Mañjuśrī applauds the silence as the true entrance: "Here there is no use for syllables, sounds, and ideas." The phrase "thunderous silence" is the commentarial tradition's label for the moment, not the sutra's own wording. What the label carries is the paradox at the centre of the concept: this silence says more than speech could, not because nothing is communicated but because what is communicated exceeds the dualism of speaker, listener and word that any statement would set up. The silence is "thunderous" in effect, not volume.

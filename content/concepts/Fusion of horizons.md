Fusion of horizons (German Horizontverschmelzung) is Hans-Georg Gadamer's term for how understanding actually happens, set out in Truth and Method (1960). A horizon is "the range of vision that includes everything that can be seen from a particular vantage point" ([Gadamer, Truth and Method, trans. Weinsheimer and Marshall](https://www.pierre-legrand.com/excerpts_from_gadamer.pdf)). Understanding, for Gadamer, is never the neutral transplant of an interpreter into the other's world; it is "always the fusion of these horizons supposedly existing by themselves" ([Truth and Method](https://www.pierre-legrand.com/excerpts_from_gadamer.pdf)).

### What the fusion is

The fusion is not a compromise midpoint. In Gadamer's account, understanding "involves the formation of a new context of meaning that enables integration of what is otherwise unfamiliar, strange or anomalous," a mediation between what is familiar and what is alien "in which neither remains unaffected" ([SEP, Gadamer](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gadamer/)). The IEP stresses the term's active side: "fusion refers to the active and the on-going nature of understanding, not a static, hegemonic unity" ([IEP, Gadamer](https://iep.utm.edu/gadamer/)). To acquire a horizon at all, Gadamer writes, one "learns to look beyond what is close at hand, not in order to look away from it but to see it better, within a larger whole and in truer proportion" ([Truth and Method](https://www.pierre-legrand.com/excerpts_from_gadamer.pdf)).

The background condition is what Gadamer calls historically effected consciousness: interpreters are shaped by the history and tradition they interpret, so the projection of the historical horizon is "simultaneously superseded" as the fusion occurs ([Truth and Method](https://www.pierre-legrand.com/excerpts_from_gadamer.pdf); [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gadamer/)). The model of the whole process is dialogue or conversation.

### In the compendium

The compendium carries the concept socially rather than textually. E080 practises the fusion directly: one's own horizon is challenged and expanded by the other's, and openness is the discipline. SO013 treats cultural meaning as constructed rather than given, SO063 and SO064 treat migration, acculturation and cultural adaptation as processes one can design, and SO072 brings the fusion into clinical work as cultural formulation. The stories show the fusion and its failures: ST288's Joel and Clementine erasing each other, ST285's Ricoeurian mimesis and ST312's redemptive sequences building the narrative bridge, ST287's Will finishing the story, and D124 looking for the right in the other side.

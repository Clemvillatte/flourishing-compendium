Respiratory sinus arrhythmia (RSA) is the heart-rhythm modulation tied to breathing: heart rate rises during inhalation and falls during exhalation. The research line matters here because RSA amplitude can be trained, and the training travels through a measurable reflex.

The Lehrer and Gevirtz review of heart rate variability biofeedback states the definition plainly:

> RSA is the heart pattern that occurs when heart rate increases during inhalation and decreases during exhalation.

(Lehrer & Gevirtz, "Heart rate variability biofeedback: how and why does it work?," Frontiers in Psychology 5:756, 2014, [full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00756/full))

The same review gives the mechanism my breathing principles lean on. During biofeedback the participant breathes slowly and tries to maximize RSA, producing a sine-wave-like curve; the mechanism is a confluence of phase relationships between heart rate and breathing at specific frequencies, phase relationships between heart rate and blood pressure, the activity of the baroreflex, and resonance characteristics of the cardiovascular system. The review reports that when biofeedback is practiced twice daily at home over about a three-month period, resting baroreflex gain increases.

My principle [[D144 — Breathe from the heels]] cites this line of work for slow diaphragmatic breathing raising vagal tone. The construct also underwrites the broader vagal-tone story I tell on [[Parasympathetic activation]].

The intention-behavior gap is the finding that what people intend to do predicts what they then do only partly. The term comes out of Paschal Sheeran's review of the evidence, which set out to measure the gap and then find the variables that moderate it.

> "This chapter addresses two questions; how big is the 'gap' between intentions and behavior, and what psychological variables might be able to 'bridge' the intention–behavior gap?" (Sheeran, 2002, "Intention–Behavior Relations: A Conceptual and Empirical Review", European Review of Social Psychology 12, abstract, [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14792772143000003))

### How big the gap is

The review drew on a meta-analysis of ten previous meta-analyses. Sheeran and Webb later summarised its result: "Sheeran (2002) meta-analyzed 10 previous meta-analysis (422 studies in total) and found a 'large' sample-weighted average correlation between intentions measured at one time-point and measures of behavior taken at a subsequent time-point (r+ = 0.53)." A correlation of that size means most of the variance in behavior is left unexplained by intention alone; behavior type, properties of the intention itself, habits and automaticity all moderate the relation.

### The best-validated bridge

The remedy with the strongest evidence is planning. In Sheeran and Webb's review: "One of the most widely researched and best-validated tools for improving the translation of intentions into action is forming if-then plans or implementation intentions." An if-then plan names the opportunity to act and the obstacle that must be dealt with, so the intention carries its own trigger.

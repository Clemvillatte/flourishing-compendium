The Stoic discipline of attention — the continuous monitoring of impressions (phantasiai) as they arise, withholding assent until they are tested. It is the practical core of [[The discipline of assent]] and the Stoic counterpart to mindfulness.

The IEP article on Stoicism gives the short form: "Prosochê = applying key ethical precepts to the present moment, mindfulness" ([IEP, Stoicism](https://iep.utm.edu/stoicism/)). The longer form is in Epictetus's own instructions to his students. In the Discourses he names the work of philosophy as it bears on judgment: "And where is your work? In desire and in aversion, that you may not be disappointed in your desire, and that you may not fall into that which you would avoid; in your pursuit and avoiding, that you commit no error; in assent and suspension of assent, that you be not deceived" ([Discourses 1.4, Long translation, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/discourses.1.one.html)).

The attention is continuous because the danger never sleeps. Epictetus says God has set a guardian in each person, "a guardian who never sleeps, is never deceived" (Discourses 1.14), and the Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Epictetus summarizes the training's trajectory: "As the habit of screening impressions becomes established, correct responses will begin to come automatically. Yet constant vigilance is still required, to guard against backsliding" ([SEP, Epictetus](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epictetus/)). A person practising prosochē watches impressions the way a gatekeeper watches arrivals, giving none entry until it has shown its credentials.

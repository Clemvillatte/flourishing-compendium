The problem of induction is Hume's discovery, in A Treatise of Human Nature (1739) and again in the Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding (1748), that no argument can establish that the future will resemble the past. The finding is not that induction is useless, since it is the only tool we have, but that its reliability cannot be proven without circularity. The future is never guaranteed by the past.

### The argument in his own words

The clearest statement is the Enquiry's bread example, Section IV:

> "The bread, which I formerly eat, nourished me; that is, a body of such sensible qualities was, at that time, endued with such secret powers: but does it follow, that other bread must also nourish me at another time, and that like sensible qualities must always be attended with like secret powers? The consequence seems nowise necessary."

([Hume, Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, Section IV Part II, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm).)

Hume then names why no proof is available:

> "That there are no demonstrative arguments in the case seems evident; since it implies no contradiction that the course of nature may change, and that an object, seemingly like those which we have experienced, may be attended with different or contrary effects."

> "It is impossible, therefore, that any arguments from experience can prove this resemblance of the past to the future; since all these arguments are founded on the supposition of that resemblance."

([Enquiry, Section IV Part II, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm).)

His answer to what does drive expectation is habit: "Custom, then, is the great guide of human life" ([Enquiry, Section V Part I, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/9662/9662-h/9662-h.htm)).

### Why I keep it

The concept matters for flourishing in two ways. First, it disciplines expectation: plans that treat a regularity as a guarantee are built on something no argument can supply, and Hold events lightly (D110) works this into a practice. Second, it explains why prediction errors feel like betrayal when they are structurally inevitable, and why the base-rate literature (Know the base rate neglect, CO020) keeps finding that people update on vivid single cases instead of frequencies.

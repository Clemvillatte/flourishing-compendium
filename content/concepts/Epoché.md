Epoché is suspension: the deliberate withholding of an automatic mental act. The word carries two traditions that agree more than their literatures admit.

### The Pyrrhonian suspension

For the Pyrrhonian skeptics, epochē is the suspension of judgment induced by equipollence:

> "Scepticism is an ability to set out oppositions among things which appear and are thought of in any way at all, an ability by which, because of the equipollence in the opposed objects and accounts, we come first to suspension of judgment and afterwards to tranquillity." (*Outlines of Pyrrhonism* I.8, quoted in [SEP, "Sextus Empiricus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sextus-empiricus/))

And when judgment suspends, tranquility follows:

> "tranquillity follows suspension of judgment 'as a shadow follows a body'" (*Outlines of Pyrrhonism* I.29, quoted in [SEP, "Sextus Empiricus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sextus-empiricus/))

The suspension is the means, the peace is the fruit. The method page is [[Equipollence]], the assembling of equal-weight reasons that does the suspending.

### The Husserlian bracketing

For Husserl, the epoché is the phenomenological bracketing: suspending the naive acceptance that the world is "just there" (the natural attitude), not to deny it but to turn attention from what the world is to how it is given:

> "We keep it, in order to be able to investigate it, but we bracket its validity. It is this procedure that is known by the name of *epoché*." (Husserl, quoted in [SEP, "Edmund Husserl"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/))

The aim is positive: "The purpose of the epoché is not to doubt, neglect, abandon, or exclude reality from our research; rather its aim is to suspend or neutralize a certain dogmatic *attitude* towards reality" ([SEP, "Edmund Husserl"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/)). Freed from the general thesis of the natural attitude, consciousness can be described as it lives, which is why the epoché became the founding move of phenomenology and, downstream, of this compendium's phenomenological practices. Pyrrho's practice of waiting before assent is the shared root: in both, something that normally happens to us (belief, world-taking) is placed on hold and examined.

### The distinction to hold

Suspension is not doubt. The Pyrrhonians explicitly distinguished their epochē from the Academic skeptics' doubt, which is itself a belief (that one cannot know); suspension refuses even that. And bracketing is not denial: Husserl brackets the natural attitude the way a geometer brackets a figure's exactness to study its structure; the world is put in parentheses, not crossed out.

### In everyday life

A first impression arrives (an insult, a headline, a flash of fear), and instead of assenting ("this is what is happening"), it is held for one breath as how things appear: an insult appearing, fear appearing. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.) That interval is the daily-life epoché ([[E006 — Practise the epoché in daily life]]).

Its contemplative cognates are [[Sati]] (bare attention that withholds the gloss) and the Daoist fasting of the mind; its [[The discipline of assent|Stoic cousin is the discipline of assent]], the pause of [[Clarity of mind]].

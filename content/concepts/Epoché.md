Epoché is suspension — the deliberate withholding of an automatic mental act. The word carries two traditions that agree more than their literatures admit. For the Pyrrhonian skeptics, epochē is the suspension of judgment induced by *equipollence* (isostheneia): for any claim, assemble opposing arguments of equal persuasive force, so that belief "withholds itself" ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Sextus Empiricus"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sextus-empiricus/), citing *Outlines of Pyrrhonism* I.8, I.26, I.29). For Husserl, the epoché is the phenomenological bracketing: suspending the naive acceptance that the world is "just there" — the natural attitude — not to deny it but to turn attention from what the world is to *how* it is given ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Edmund Husserl"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/), citing *Ideas I* §32). Pyrrho's practice of waiting before assent is the shared root: in both, something that normally happens to us (belief, world-taking) is placed on hold and examined.

The mechanism is the same in both: automatic assent is replaced by an interval. The Pyrrhonians found that when judgment suspends, tranquility (ataraxia) follows "like a shadow following a body" (*Outlines of Pyrrhonism* I.29) — the suspension is the means, the peace is the fruit. Husserl's bracketing likewise serves a positive aim: freed from the general thesis of the natural attitude, consciousness can be described as it lives — which is why the epoché became the founding move of phenomenology and, downstream, of this compendium's phenomenological practices ([[E001 — Practise the phenomenological reduction]]; [[E006 — Practise the epoché in daily life]]). The method page is [[Equipollence]] — the assembling of equal-weight reasons that does the suspending.

The distinction to hold: suspension is not doubt. The Pyrrhonians explicitly distinguished their epochē from the Academic skeptics' doubt, which is itself a belief (that one cannot know); suspension refuses even that. And bracketing is not denial: Husserl brackets the natural attitude the way a geometer brackets a figure's exactness to study its structure — the world is put in parentheses, not crossed out.

An example at its smallest: a first impression arrives — an insult, a headline, a flash of fear — and instead of assenting ("this is what is happening"), it is held for one breath as *how things appear*: an insult appearing, fear appearing. That interval is the daily-life epoché ([[E006 — Practise the epoché in daily life]]).

Its contemplative cognates are [[Sati]] (bare attention that withholds the gloss) and the Daoist fasting of the mind ([[D046 — Practice fasting of the mind (xinzhai)]]); its [[The discipline of assent|Stoic cousin is the discipline of assent]], the pause of [[Clarity of mind]].

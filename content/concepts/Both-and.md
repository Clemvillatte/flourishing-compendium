“Both-and” — James, A Pluralistic Universe.

Both-and is the reading of James's pluralism I name with the hyphen. It is not a claim that contradictions are true, but that the one-and-the-many problem need not be settled into a single side. In A Pluralistic Universe James classifies philosophers by temperament, and the classification is the entry into the question:

> "The pragmatic temperament appears in the book's opening chapter, where (following a method he first set out in 'Remarks on Spencer's Definition of Mind as Correspondence') James classifies philosophers according to their temperaments: in this case 'tough-minded' or 'tender-minded'." (Goodman, [SEP: William James](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/))

The passage James borrows from Bergson gives the both-and reading its shape:

> "James employs Henri Bergson's critique of 'intellectualism' to argue that the 'concrete pulses of experience appear pent in by no such definite limits as our conceptual substitutes are confined by. They run into one another continuously and seem to interpenetrate' (PU 127)." (Goodman, [SEP: William James](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/james/))

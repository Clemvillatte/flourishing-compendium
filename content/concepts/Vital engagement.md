## Facets

- **studies** — Nakamura and Csikszentmihalyi, 2003

Vital engagement is Jeanne Nakamura and Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi's name for the compound a good life keeps producing: absorption plus significance. As a later paper in the flow literature states it:

> "In the flow literature, Nakamura (2001); Nakamura and Csikszentmihalyi (2002, 2003) introduced the construct of vital engagement as a relationship to an activity or object that is characterized by a strong felt connection between the self and the activity/object and at the state level is 'characterized both by experiences of flow (enjoyed absorption) and by meaning (subjective significance)' (2003, p. 87)." ([Tse, Cahill, Nakamura & Fung, 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10191-z))

### Why both halves are needed

The construct exists because flow alone is not enough: the state of enjoyed absorption can attach to gambling or marathon gaming as easily as to a craft or a calling. The same paper's recent studies set vital engagement against what it calls low-meaning flow, high engagement with low meaningfulness, and found that "vital engagement activities (vs. low-meaning flow activities) were predominantly associated with more positive experiences, but not necessarily less negative experiences, at the activity level", with the personal-level well-being relationships "less consistent ... across studies" ([Tse et al., 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10191-z)). The chapter that introduced the idea traced how a series of flow experiences, each satisfying in itself, can over time compound into a relationship with a pursuit that matters, which is where the construct gets its name: engagement that is vital, in both senses.

### Here

- [[P175 — Seek vital engagement — meaning plus absorption]] is the practice built on this concept: choose and shape activities for both halves, since absorption without meaning is a slot machine, and meaning without absorption is a duty.

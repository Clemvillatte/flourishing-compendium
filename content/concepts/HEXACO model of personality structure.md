The HEXACO model is a six-factor taxonomy of personality traits proposed by Michael Ashton and Kibeom Lee. It grew out of lexical studies of personality-descriptive words in many languages, which kept recovering six broad dimensions where the Big Five tradition has five. The model's case, as the authors put it:

> "The authors argue that a new six-dimensional framework for personality structure—the HEXACO model—constitutes a viable alternative to the well-known Big Five or five-factor model." (Ashton and Lee, [Empirical, Theoretical, and Practical Advantages of the HEXACO Model of Personality Structure](https://differentialclub.wdfiles.com/local--files/5fm/Ashton%202007%20HEXACO%20Model%20JPSP.pdf), Personality and Social Psychology Review 11(2), 2007, abstract)

### The honesty-humility factor

The six factors are Honesty-Humility (H), Emotionality (E), eXtraversion (X), Agreeableness (A), Conscientiousness (C) and Openness to Experience (O). The addition that does the most work is the first:

> "Honesty-Humility represents the tendency to be fair and genuine in dealing with others, in the sense of cooperating with others even when one might exploit them without suffering retaliation." (Ashton and Lee 2007, "Theoretical Interpretation" section)

The authors read the H and A factors as two complementary aspects of reciprocal altruism: H covers fairness when exploitation would go unpunished, A covers forgivingness versus anger in ongoing relations. That reading gives the trait model a place for honesty and modesty that five-factor questionnaires tend to scatter or miss.

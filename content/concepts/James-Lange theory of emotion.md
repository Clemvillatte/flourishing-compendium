The James-Lange theory is one of the earliest theories of emotion within modern psychology ([Wikipedia, James-Lange theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James%E2%80%93Lange_theory)). William James set out its core in "What is an Emotion?" (Mind, 1884): the perception of an event triggers bodily changes directly, and the feeling of those changes as they occur is the emotion ([James, 1884](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm)). The theory is named for James and Carl Lange ([Wikipedia, James-Lange theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James%E2%80%93Lange_theory)).

> "The hypothesis here to be defended says that this order of sequence is incorrect, that the one mental state is not immediately induced by the other, that the bodily manifestations must first be interposed between, and that the more rational statement is that we feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble, and not that we cry, strike, or tremble, because we are sorry, angry, or fearful, as the case may be." (James, [What is an Emotion?](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm), Mind 9, 1884, pp. 188-205, opening section)

### What the theory inverts

James places bodily changes between perceiving the event and experiencing the emotion, reversing the common-sense sequence he illustrates with the bear example ([James, 1884](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm)). His thesis in his own words is "that the bodily changes follow directly the PERCEPTION of the exciting fact, and that our feeling of the same changes as they occur IS the emotion" ([James, 1884](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm)).

The Wikipedia overview reports that the theory has been criticized and modified over its history, with modern theorists proposing that the experience of emotion is modulated by both physiological feedback and other information ([Wikipedia, James-Lange theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James%E2%80%93Lange_theory)).

Reversal theory, associated with Michael Apter, holds that experience is organised in pairs of opposite "metamotivational" states. A sport-psychology paper explains that there are four domains, each with a bistable pair: telic and paratelic for means and ends, conformist and negativistic for rules, mastery and sympathy for transactions, and autic and alloic for relationships. "For each domain, only one metamotivational state from the pair can be experienced at a given point in time" ([Comparison of metamotivational dominance and cultural identity between Japanese National Team and Māori All Blacks rugby players, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727547/)). The seed's "paracentric" is a misspelling of paratelic, and the telic and paratelic pair is only one of four.

### Telic and paratelic

Apter described the telic state as "a state of mind in which one conceives oneself to be pursuing an important goal, the behavior being subsidiary and chosen to achieve the goal", and the paratelic as one "in which the orientation is toward the behavior itself, together with its concomitant sensations, in this case any goal being conceived as subsidiary" ([Reversal theory: a new approach to psychotherapy, Anuario de Psicologia, PDF](https://www.raco.cat/index.php/anuariopsicologia/article/viewFile/64605/88631)). The telic state is "a serious one"; the paratelic is "a more lighthearted or playful one", oriented to the present moment.

### Re-colouring arousal

The seed's phrase "re-colouring the same arousal" matches the theory. A therapy paper says the telic state is linked to a "relaxation-anxiety" dimension, and after reversal to the paratelic state this dimension "is inverted" so that it becomes a "boredom-excitement" dimension ([Anuario de Psicologia, reversal theory and the eclectic therapist, PDF](https://revistes.ub.edu/index.php/Anuario-psicologia/article/download/9361/12046/15094)). The theory replaces homeostasis with bistability: two preferred arousal levels, only one operating at a time (raco.cat paper).

### What causes a flip

Reversals come from frustration, satiation, or a contingent event (Hudson et al., Reversal theory in lectures, [LJMU PDF](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/17720/1/2022-v11-02-Hudson-et-al.pdf)). Desselles's review says "reversals are involuntary; they are not under direct voluntary control of the individual but just happen under particular circumstances", though there are techniques to induce a state, such as using a comedy film to induce the paratelic state ([Manipulating Motivational States: A Review, Reversal Theory Portal PDF](https://reversaltheory.net/wp-content/uploads/2013-v1-Desselles.pdf)).

### Limits

Apter writes that research "has so far focused mainly on the telic-paratelic pair, and research on the other three pairs has lagged behind" ([Some Suggestions for Future Research, Reversal Theory Portal](https://reversaltheory.net/wp-content/uploads/2013-v1-Apter.pdf)). In a study of 3 lectures by Hudson and colleagues, surprisingly few students reversed, suggesting more stability than expected (LJMU PDF). Several sources here are written within the theory's own research community.

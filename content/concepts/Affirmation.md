Affirmation is Deleuze's word for what Nietzsche's yes does. In Nietzsche and Philosophy, Deleuze reads the whole of Nietzsche as the replacement of the dialectic's negation with an affirmative difference:

> "Nietzsche's 'yes' is opposed to the dialectical 'no'; affirmation to dialectical negation; difference to dialectical contradiction; joy, enjoyment, to dialectical labour; lightness, dance, to dialectical responsibilities."

([Deleuze, Nietzsche and Philosophy, quoted in the book's analysis at Posthegemony](https://posthegemony.wordpress.com/2013/02/07/nietzsche-and-philosophy/).)

### What the yes does

Affirmation is not acquiescence. Deleuze is explicit that "this affirmation [...] has nothing to do with acceptance of or acquiescence to how things are, what is, but rather purely an act of creation" ([slideshare summary of the book](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/download-full-ebook-of-nietzsche-and-philosophy-gilles-deleuze-instant-download-pdf/279593391)). The negative is not denied but transmuted: "The negative becomes a power of affirming: it is subordinated to affirmation and passes into the service of an excess of life" ([Deleuze, quoted in the GitHub full text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hdarlim/deleuze/main/Works/1.%20Nietzsche%20and%20Philosophy.pdf)). Negation is therefore "secondary" and becomes "joyful destruction" when it is put in the service of creation ([same](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hdarlim/deleuze/main/Works/1.%20Nietzsche%20and%20Philosophy.pdf)).

The ethical center of Deleuze's reading is the eternal return as selection. What returns is only what affirms: "Nietzsche's practical teaching is that difference is happy; that multiplicity, becoming and chance are adequate objects of joy by themselves and that only joy returns" ([Deleuze, Nietzsche and Philosophy, p. 190, quoted at Posthegemony](https://posthegemony.wordpress.com/2013/02/07/nietzsche-and-philosophy/)). And the summary line Deleuze gives the operation: "To affirm is to unburden: not to load life with the weight of higher values, but to create new values which are those of life, which make life light and active" ([quoted in Hardt, Gilles Deleuze: An Apprenticeship in Philosophy](https://www.scribd.com/doc/132036203/Michael-Hardt-Gilles-Deleuze-an-Apprenticeship-in-Philosophy-1993)).

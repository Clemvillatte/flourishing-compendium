Opposite action is a skill from Marsha Linehan's dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) in which a person acts against the action urge that comes with an emotion. Sauer-Zavala and colleagues describe where it sits: techniques that address emotionally avoidant responding have been built into multicomponent treatments for borderline personality disorder, "including DBT (the opposite action skill)," and they trace this to emotion science "suggesting a fundamental way to change emotional experiences is to alter the action-tendencies associated with them" (Sauer-Zavala et al., "Countering Emotional Behaviors in the Treatment of Borderline Personality Disorder," Personality Disorders, 2020, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7483202/)).

> "However, this skill has generally not been presented in isolation, making it difficult to draw conclusions regarding its effect on the frequency of emotionally-avoidant behaviors, negative affectivity, and BPD symptoms." (Sauer-Zavala et al., 2020, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7483202/))

### What the evidence is

Rizvi and Linehan (2005) is the treatment study the Sauer-Zavala paper cites. Its abstract says the study "sought to pilot test a short-term intervention for maladaptive shame in borderline personality disorder (BPD) based on the skill of 'opposite action' from dialectical behavior therapy." Five women were treated "using a single-subject, multiple-baseline design," and the results "indicate that, although state ratings of shame are highly variable, it is possible to reduce shame about a specific event over a short period of time" ([Rizvi & Linehan, 2005, Cognitive and Behavioral Practice 12(4), 437-447, abstract via Rutgers](https://www.researchwithrutgers.com/en/publications/the-treatment-of-maladaptive-shame-in-borderline-personality-diso)). That is a five-person pilot, and the reduction it reports concerns shame about one event. The compendium's principles should be read as describing a clinical skill with preliminary support, not a proven law of emotion.

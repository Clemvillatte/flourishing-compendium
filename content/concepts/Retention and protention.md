Retention and protention are Husserl's names for the two half-invisible margins that make a moment of experience more than a point. Every ongoing experience has a now-phase narrowly held, a retention that keeps the just-elapsed phase present as just-past, and a protention that reaches indefinitely toward the phase about to occur.

The structure as the SEP entry on Husserl states it:

> There is (i) a 'primal impression' narrowly directed toward the strictly circumscribed now-phase of the object. The primal impression is accompanied by (ii) a 'retention', or retentional aspect, which provides us with a consciousness of the just-elapsed phase of the object thereby furnishing the primal impression with a past-directed temporal context, and by (iii) a 'protention', or protentional aspect, which in a more-or-less indefinite way intends the phase of the object about to occur thereby providing a future-oriented temporal context for the primal impression.

(Dan Zahavi, "Edmund Husserl," [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/), citing Hua 9/201)

The entry's key caution is that these are not memory and anticipation. Retention and protention are "structural components of any ongoing intentional act" that "occur without our active or deliberate contribution," while episodic memory and episodic future thinking are their own acts and presuppose this work. The melody is the standard example: retaining the note that just sounded is not the same act as remembering having heard that melody last summer.

Dewey describes experience as a connected movement of doing and undergoing: we act, receive what follows, and carry that consequence into what happens next ([Art as Experience, chapter III](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/middle/having_an_experience.pdf)). A pause can consolidate an earlier phase and prepare a later one, rather than simply interrupting movement ([same chapter](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/middle/having_an_experience.pdf)).

> "Experiencing like breathing is a rhythm of intakings and outgivings." (Dewey, Art as Experience, [chapter III, Having an Experience](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/middle/having_an_experience.pdf)).

The relation matters more than alternation alone: earlier acts contribute to a developing whole, while undergoing prevents the sequence from becoming a disconnected succession of excitations ([chapter III](https://people.wou.edu/~girodm/middle/having_an_experience.pdf)). This is an account of the organisation of experience, not a clinical finding about a particular breathing exercise.

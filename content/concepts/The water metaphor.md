The water metaphor is the Daoist instruction to be useful the way water is useful: by not contending. Daodejing 8 puts it in two lines:

> "Highest good is like water: water benefits the things of the world and does not contend. Dwell in places that the masses of men despise."
> — [Robert Eno, Daodejing ch. 8](https://terebess.hu/english/tao/Daodejing-Eno.pdf)

Water's advantage is structural, not moral. It goes down when other things go up, it takes the shape of whatever holds it, and so it is always in the position that nothing else is competing for. The facet listed for this concept — adapt to the vessel — is the same point in the language of containers: the water does not fight the jar, it fills it.

The chapter the metaphor points toward comes back in Daodejing 78, where the soft overcomes the hard; D011 quotes that passage in its own case. The still water of Inner Chapter 7 of the Zhuangzi and the still mirror of the same tradition give the companion image from the other side: water that does not contend is also water that nothing can distract ([The still mirror]]).

The body scan is the first formal practice taught in mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR). Jon Kabat-Zinn developed MBSR in 1979 at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center; his retrospective describes the clinic as an eight-week outpatient course ([Kabat-Zinn 2003](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1093/clipsy.bpg016)). The practitioner lies on the back and moves attention through the body region by region, starting with the toes of the left foot, feeling whatever is present or noticing its absence ([Full Catastrophe Living, ch. 5](https://osher.ucsf.edu/sites/osher.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/Full-Catastrophe-Living.pdf)).

### The practice

The instructions ask for about 45 minutes a day, six days a week, in the first two weeks ([Full Catastrophe Living, ch. 5](https://osher.ucsf.edu/sites/osher.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/Full-Catastrophe-Living.pdf)). Attention rests in each region while the breath is imagined moving into and out of it; where nothing is felt, the absence is the experience ([Full Catastrophe Living, ch. 5](https://osher.ucsf.edu/sites/osher.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/Full-Catastrophe-Living.pdf)). The stated aim is to develop concentration and flexibility of attention simultaneously and to reestablish contact with the body, not to force relaxation ([Full Catastrophe Living, ch. 5](https://osher.ucsf.edu/sites/osher.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/Full-Catastrophe-Living.pdf)). Falling asleep is a common initial difficulty; the intention is to "fall awake" ([Full Catastrophe Living, ch. 5](https://osher.ucsf.edu/sites/osher.ucsf.edu/files/inline-files/Full-Catastrophe-Living.pdf)).

### The chronic pain program

Kabat-Zinn's 1982 report described a 10-week Stress Reduction and Relaxation Program for 51 chronic pain patients who had not improved with traditional care ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)). The meditation trained an attentional stance the paper calls detached observation of proprioception, which the paper says appeared to uncouple pain's sensory dimension from its affective and evaluative alarm reaction ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)). At 10 weeks, 65 percent of patients showed a reduction of at least 33 percent on the Pain Rating Index, 50 percent a reduction of at least 50 percent ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)). The results concern the entire 10-week program, not the scan alone, and the study was uncontrolled ([Kabat-Zinn 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7042457/)).

### Evidence and limits

A 2025 meta-analysis of 29 randomized trials with 2,191 participants found mindfulness training improved self-reported interoception with a small-to-medium effect (Hedges' g = 0.31) ([Treves et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-22661-4)). On objective measures the picture is weaker: earlier meta-analytic work found small, heterogeneous effects with no differences on proximal measures like heartbeat perception ([Treves et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-22661-4)). The pooled findings concern mindfulness interventions collectively, not the body scan's independent effect. Gibson reports practitioners finding the scan relaxing or uncomfortable, arguing that a non-evaluative attentional style matters; that is his argument, not a demonstrated mechanism ([Gibson 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02012/full)).

### In the compendium

BI054 "Learn body scan meditation: systematic body awareness" carries the practice and adds the note that boredom is part of the training. AT050 "Learn body scan: systematic attention through the body" places it among attention practices, and AF005 and BI053 connect it to interoception. B011 "Make mindfulness the master tool (sati)" names the underlying faculty and B043 "Develop insight (vipassana)" lists the scan among its practice kernels. D053 "Balance alertness and ease" names the balance the falling-asleep difficulty tests: relaxed alertness, neither drowsy nor tense. P139 "Know that cognition is embodied sense-making" grounds practice in posture, movement and breath, the territory the scan covers.

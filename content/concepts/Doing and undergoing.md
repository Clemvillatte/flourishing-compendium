"Doing and undergoing" is John Dewey's name for the two connected aspects of experience: the part where a person acts on the world, and the part where the world answers back and is felt ([Dewey, Having an Experience](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm)). Dewey sets the pair out in Art as Experience (1934), in the chapter "Having an Experience" ([ibid.](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm)).

> "An experience has pattern and structure, because it is not just doing and undergoing in alternation, but consists of them in relationship." (Dewey, [Having an Experience, Art as Experience (1934)](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm))

### The connection is the point

Dewey's explanation centres on perceiving the relationship between an action and its consequences ([Dewey, Having an Experience](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm)). The relation is not automatic; acting and suffering the result are two different things until the doer grasps the tie between them:

> "The action and its consequence must be joined in perception. This relationship is what gives meaning; to grasp it is the objective of all intelligence." (Dewey, [Having an Experience](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm))

Dewey uses the burning-hand example to stress perceiving the relation between action and consequence: "To put one's hand in the fire that consumes it is not necessarily to have an experience" ([ibid.](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm)). He adds that a child's experience may be intense while the relations between undergoing and doing are only slightly grasped, and that such experience does not have great depth or breadth ([ibid.](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm)).

### Where art enters

Dewey's interest in the pair is aesthetic. The same relation of outgoing and incoming energy, he argues, is what a work of art organises ([Dewey, Having an Experience](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm)):

> "In short, art, in its form, unites the very same relation of doing and undergoing, outgoing and incoming energy, that makes an experience to be an experience." (Dewey, [Having an Experience](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/us/an-experience.htm))

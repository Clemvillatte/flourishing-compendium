Engaged Buddhism brings contemplative practice into the work of relieving suffering in society ([Thich Nhat Hanh, interview with Velcrow Ripper](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/interview-for-shambhala-lions-roar-september-2007)). Explaining his response to the Vietnam War, Thich Nhat Hanh said that awareness of victims' suffering called for practical help, alongside continued meditation ([“Ho Chi Minh City”](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/interview-for-shambhala-lions-roar-september-2007)).

### Practice during relief work

> That is why you have to find ways to maintain your practice, maintain your solidity, your freedom, your peace while you are doing the work.
>
> Thich Nhat Hanh, quoted by Velcrow Ripper, “I am Home,” [Shambhala Sun, September 2007, republished by Plum Village](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/interview-for-shambhala-lions-roar-september-2007).

In the same passage, he warns that relief work can exhaust people when they lose their practice ([interview](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/interview-for-shambhala-lions-roar-september-2007)). He describes nonviolent action as an expression of love, naming violence and family breakdown among the social problems to which it can respond ([interview](https://plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/interviews-with-thich-nhat-hanh/interview-for-shambhala-lions-roar-september-2007)).

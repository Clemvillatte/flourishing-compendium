Equity theory is J. Stacy Adams's account of fairness in social exchange, developed in the early 1960s for workplace settings. People judge fairness by comparing their ratio of outcomes to inputs against the ratio of a referent other. Inputs are what a person perceives as their contributions to the exchange; outcomes are perceived receipts such as pay, fringe benefits and status. Both sides of the ratio live in perception: "The values of outcomes and inputs are, of course, as perceived by Person."

> "Inequity exists for Person whenever he perceives that the ratio of his outcomes to inputs and the ratio of Other's outcomes to Other's inputs are unequal." (Adams 1965, p. 280, [Inequity in Social Exchange](https://ia600601.us.archive.org/21/items/15341_Readings/15341_Readings/Justice/InequityInSocialExchange_Adams_text.pdf))

Inequity in either direction, under-reward or over-reward, creates a tension the theory says people reduce by six means: altering inputs, altering outcomes, distorting inputs and outcomes cognitively, leaving the field, acting on the Other, or changing the object of comparison.

Here purpose means an aim or reason for carrying on. The original entry line says Frankl made purpose the thing that lets a person bear almost any how, and connects it to Aristotle's final cause in a life. Frankl's own account is more concrete than that gloss. In the camp sections of Man's Search for Meaning he describes what a psychologist could actually do with prisoners:

> As we said before, any attempt to restore a man's inner strength in the camp had first to succeed in showing him some future goal.

(Viktor E. Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning, "Experiences in a Concentration Camp," [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/stream/frankl-viktor-mans-search-for-meaning-1963/FRANKL_Viktor_Man's_Search_For_Meaning-1963_djvu.txt))

The passage continues: a prisoner who could be given a why, an aim for his life, was strengthened to bear the terrible how of his existence. That is the claim the compendium's principles work with. [[MO061]] treats purpose as the thing that sustains motivation, drawing on Frankl's account of meaning as motivation. [[PP025 — Clarify purpose as a direction]] treats purpose as a direction to be pursued rather than a goal to be achieved, and adds a research finding (Hill and Turiano) linking purposeful lives to lower mortality over fourteen years of follow-up. The original entry's Aristotle comparison is a framing the compendium gives; the Frankl passage above is what the source supports.

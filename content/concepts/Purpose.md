Purpose is the having of a why: an aim that organizes a life and gives suffering and effort a point. The seed line names its two carriers: Frankl's term for what enables bearing almost any how, and Aristotle's final cause, the for-the-sake-of-which that a flourishing life is organized around. The compendium's principles work the concept from both ends: [[MO061]] treats purpose as the thing that keeps motivation alive, and [[PP025 — Clarify purpose as a direction]] treats purpose as a direction rather than a goal to be achieved.

Frankl's version is the sharpest because it was written from the camps. His line, quoting Nietzsche, is that a why to live lets a person bear almost any how. The full passage in Man's Search for Meaning reads:

> Whenever there was an opportunity for it, one had to give them a why—an aim—for their lives, in order to strengthen them to bear the terrible how of their existence.

(Viktor E. Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning, "Experiences in a Concentration Camp," [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/stream/frankl-viktor-mans-search-for-meaning-1963/FRANKL_Viktor_Man's_Search_For_Meaning-1963_djvu.txt))

The psychological research takes purpose as a measurable construct, distinct from goals and mood. Purpose in this literature is a stable, self-organizing aim that directs daily behavior. On the compendium's own principle [[PP025 — Clarify purpose as a direction]], purpose is a direction rather than a destination, and the empirical anchor cited there is the Hill and Turiano finding that purposeful individuals lived longer over fourteen years of follow-up, even when controlling for other markers of psychological and affective well-being.

The distinction that separates purpose from related ideas matters for flourishing. Purpose differs from meaning in that meaning is the broader sense that life is about something (coherence, purpose, and mattering); purpose is the aim-giving edge of that. Purpose differs from goals in that goals are achieved and checked off, while purpose is a direction that keeps generating goals. The research literature behind the compendium's principles treats purpose as sustaining motivation under adversity rather than making work easier, and as measurable separately from well-being.

### How the compendium uses it

- [[MO061 — Learn to cultivate meaning — purpose sustains motivation]] uses purpose as the motivational engine behind Frankl's will to meaning
- [[PP025 — Clarify purpose as a direction]] treats purpose as a chosen direction large enough to organize daily action
- [[AF062]] names purpose as one of the domains of well-being alongside enjoyment, relationships, and growth
- [[B155]] connects purpose to goal commitment and life purpose as psychological parallels (Damon, The Path to Purpose)

### Related concepts

- [[Meaning]] — the broader concept of which purpose is the aim-giving edge
- [[The will to meaning]] — Frankl's term for the drive that purpose answers
- [[Mattering]] — the sense that one's life counts, distinct from having an aim
- [[Goal adjustment capacity]] — purpose is a direction that generates goals rather than a goal to be achieved
- [[Eudaimonia]] — Aristotle's account of the end that organizes a life
- [[Noogenic neurosis]] — Frankl's term for the emptiness that follows when purpose is absent

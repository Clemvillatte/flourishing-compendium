Moral situation research is the experimental literature behind the seed's blunt claim that "context dominates in real moral choice". Its philosophical spearhead is John Doris's Lack of Character, which denies that virtues are what the ancients assumed: "robust traits: if a person has a robust trait, they can be confidently expected to display trait-relevant behavior across a wide variety of trait-relevant situations, even where some or all of these situations are not optimally conducive to such behavior" (2002, 18, quoted in [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character/)). Situationists "argue that traits are not robust in this sense": much of human behavior "is attributable to seemingly trivial features of the situations in which persons find themselves".

### The experiments

The evidence is famous. Seminary students who agreed to give a talk on helping those in need encountered, on the way, a confederate slumped over and groaning; "those who were told they were already late were much less likely to help than those who were told they had time to spare" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character/)). Wikipedia's numbers: "nearly 63% of those in a low-hurry condition stopped to help, compared with only 10% in the high-hurry condition", and helping "was not predicted by religious personality measures" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situationism_(psychology))). Persons who found a dime in a phone booth were far more likely to help a stranger who dropped papers. And in Milgram's obedience experiments "the great majority of subjects, when politely though firmly requested by an experimenter, were willing to administer what they thought were increasingly severe electric shocks to a screaming 'victim'" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character/)); 65% proceeded to the maximum 450 volts ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situationism_(psychology))).

### The debate

The inference is contested. Trait psychologists reply that behavior comes from trait-situation interactions, and "interactionism is currently an accepted personality theory" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situationism_(psychology))). The encyclopedia frames the residue: the studies are taken to show that people have "only narrow, 'local' traits", and the challenge is to "develop an account of character that is consistent with empirical results".

### In the compendium

The compendium's response is to move from traits to trained situational attention. A046, "See the particular," and A038's practical judgment (gnome) are phronesis, the trained capacity the ancient view actually needs; P084, "Act in the situation, not from the rulebook," is situationism read as instruction; A051 and A104's equity (epieikeia) corrects the rule exactly where situations outrun it; P039's rehearsal trains the judgment under the time pressure the seminary students lacked. The comparison with the virtue tradition is structural, not a claim that Aristotle knew the studies.

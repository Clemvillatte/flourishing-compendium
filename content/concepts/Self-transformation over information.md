Self-transformation over information is Pierre Hadot's name for the priority ancient philosophy gave to changing the person over stocking the mind. On his reading, the schools taught doctrines, but the doctrines served a practice whose object was the practitioner.

> "For the Stoics, the parts of philosophy—physics, ethics, and logic—were not, in fact, parts of philosophy itself, but rather parts of philosophical discourse. But philosophy itself—that is, the philosophical way of life—is no longer a theory divided into parts, but a unitary act, which consists in living logic, physics, and ethics" (Hadot, Philosophy as a Way of Life, [full text](https://vdoc.pub/documents/philosophy-as-a-way-of-life-spiritual-exercises-from-socrates-to-foucault-5e96amgvfim0)).

### What the exercises are for

The instrument of that act is what Hadot calls spiritual exercises: "The object of spiritual exercises is precisely to bring about this transformation" (Philosophy as a Way of Life). The model is athletic, not academic: "just as, by dint of repeated physical exercises, athletes give new form and strength to their bodies, so the philosopher develops his strength of soul, modifies his inner climate, transforms his vision of the world, and, finally, his entire being" (Philosophy as a Way of Life). Reading the theory is not the work; the theory is equipment for a training whose result is a different way of seeing and being.

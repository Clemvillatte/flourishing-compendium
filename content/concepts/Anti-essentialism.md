Anti-essentialism is the refusal of fixed inner natures. Nietzsche's clearest published statements come early and late. In Human, All Too Human: "But everything has become: there are no eternal facts, just as there are no absolute truths. Consequently what is needed from now on is historical philosophizing, and with it the virtue of modesty" ([Nietzsche, HAH, quoted in Knoll's perspectivism paper](https://philarchive.org/archive/KNONPA)). In Beyond Good and Evil: "There are no moral phenomena at all, but only a moral interpretation of phenomena" ([BGE 108, quoted in the same paper](https://philarchive.org/archive/KNONPA)).

### The Gay Science passage

The 1882 book contains the fullest formulation. In the aphorism on the consciousness of appearance: "What is 'appearance' for me now? Certainly not the opposite of some essence: what could I say about any essence except to name the attributes of its appearance! Certainly not a dead mask that one could place on an unknown x or remove from it!" ([Nietzsche, The Gay Science, Kaufmann translation, Stanford PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~jsabol/existentialism/materials/nietzsche-gay-science.pdf)). The denial is double: there is no essence behind the appearance, and there is no thing-in-itself for the appearance to conceal. In the notebook passages of 1885-1886 this becomes the line often quoted: "There are no facts, only interpretations" ([NL 7[60], as noted by Philoscopia](https://www.philoscopia.com/en/quotes/nietzsche/)).

### The concept's limit

The position is often misread as relativism, and the distinction matters here. Nietzsche's own words in the same period: "There is only a perspectival seeing, only a perspectival knowing" ([On the Genealogy of Morals, III §12, Philoscopia](https://www.philoscopia.com/en/quotes/nietzsche/)). What is denied is not the possibility of better accounts, but the possibility of a view from nowhere. The Gay Science aphorism on skeptics gives the operative attitude: "I favor any skepsis to which I may reply: 'Let us try it!'" ([Nietzsche, The Gay Science, #51, quoted in the HMC reading guide](https://pages.hmc.edu/beckman/philosophy/nietzsche/Gay.htm)). Anti-essentialism is not the claim that all accounts are equal; it is the claim that the only thing that settles an account is what happens when you try it.

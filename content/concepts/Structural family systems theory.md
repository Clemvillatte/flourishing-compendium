Structural family systems theory is Salvador Minuchin's model of family functioning, developed with colleagues during the 1960s in therapeutic work with families and young people. Its core claim is that a family's distress lies less in individual members than in the organization of their relationships: the subsystems inside the family, the boundaries around those subsystems, and the hierarchy that distributes power among them. Therapy aims to restructure those patterns directly.

> "The boundaries of a subsystem are said to be the rules defining who participates and how. The function of boundaries is to protect the differentiation of the subsystem. Every family subsystem is said to have specific tasks and make specific demands on its members; and the development of interpersonal skills achieved in these subsystems is predicated on the subsystem's freedom from interference by other subsystems, as might be seen with a diffuse subsystem boundary. According to this approach, proper functioning within subsystems implies clear boundaries." (Vetere, [Structural family therapy](https://opencourses.emu.edu.tr/pluginfile.php/60191/mod_resource/content/0/Minuchin.pdf), Child Psychology & Psychiatry Review 6(3), 2001)

### What the theory claims

Structure, in this model, refers to "the organisational characteristics of the family at any point in time, the family subsystems, and the overt and covert rules that are said to influence interpersonal choices and behaviours in the family". Families contain subsystems, such as the couple, parental and sibling subsystems, and "subsystems are organised hierarchically in a way that regulates power within and between subsystems". The therapeutic wager follows from the model: when the structure of the relational group changes, the positions of its members change, and through that repositioning each member's experience can change, which is where the approach looks for relief of symptomatic distress.

### What it has been criticized for

The model has a documented history of critique. Vetere's overview records that the approach "was extensively critiqued during the 1980s by feminist writers and during the 1990s by those interested in the implications of a social constructionist position", and that it "continues to evolve in response to challenges mounted from within and outwith the systemic field".

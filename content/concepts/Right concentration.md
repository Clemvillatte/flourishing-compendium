Right concentration (sammā samādhi) is the eighth factor of the Buddhist Noble Eightfold Path ([The Fourth Noble Truth, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/ptf/dhamma/sacca/sacca4/index.html)). DN 2 describes four jhānas, or absorptions: "Quite withdrawn from sensuality, withdrawn from unskillful mental qualities, he enters and remains in the first jhana: rapture and pleasure born from withdrawal, accompanied by directed thought and evaluation," each jhāna quieter than the last, ending in "purity of equanimity and mindfulness, neither-pleasure nor stress" ([Samaññaphala Sutta, DN 2](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.02.0.than.html)).

### Tuning, not forcing

The practice is tuned rather than forced. In the Sona Sutta the Buddha teaches a former musician with his own instrument: "when the strings of your vina were neither too taut nor too loose, but tuned to be right on pitch, was your vina in tune & playable?" ([Sona Sutta, AN 6.55](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)) Over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness, overly slack persistence to laziness, so the practitioner determines the right pitch and picks up the theme. Concentration is an achievement of calibration, the opposite of white-knuckled effort.

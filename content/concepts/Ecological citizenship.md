Ecological citizenship is Andrew Dobson's account of the obligations people have because of the environmental effects of how they live. The Oxford Academic description of his book Citizenship and the Environment says it "focuses on duties as well as rights, and its conception of political space is not the state or the municipality, or the ideal speech community of cosmopolitanism, but the 'ecological footprint'" ([Dobson, Citizenship and the Environment](https://academic.oup.com/book/36215)).

### Obligations follow impacts

In chapter 3, Dobson writes that ecological citizenship "deals in the currency of non-contractual responsibility", reaches into private as well as public life, and is "explicitly non-territorial". The effects that create it are best described as "action at a distance". The main duty is "to ensure that ecological footprints make a sustainable, rather than an unsustainable, impact", meaning that a person's footprint should not "compromise or foreclose the ability of others in present and future generations to pursue options important to them" ([Dobson, Citizenship and the Environment, ch. 3](https://academic.oup.com/book/36215/chapter/315523039)).

### Asymmetry and justice

The chapter says the obligations are owed asymmetrically: only those who occupy ecological space in a way that limits others owe them, and they end when the imbalance has been addressed. It names justice as the first virtue of ecological citizenship, aimed at "a just distribution of ecological space" ([ch. 3](https://academic.oup.com/book/36215/chapter/315523039)). Dobson also rejects an ecocentric version, because he regards justice as something that can only arguably be predicated of non-human beings.

### Scope

My one-line entry cites Dobson's book only. This page relies on the publisher's description and the open chapter text. Other scholars have defended, criticised or adapted the idea, and those debates are not covered here. Dobson's account is a theory of political obligation, not a claim about inner life, so connections to wellbeing are for the reader to draw.

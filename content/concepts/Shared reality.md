Shared reality, in Higgins's account, is the state where people experience that what they feel, believe and care about is held in common with others. The claim Higgins 2019 makes is that the shared thing becomes the real thing. His book's introduction puts it this way: "Humans want to experience that their feelings, beliefs, and concerns are shared by others" ([E. Tory Higgins, Shared Reality: What Makes Us Strong and Tears Us Apart, Oxford 2019](https://academic.oup.com/book/35191/chapter-abstract/299566938)).

> "And what humans *share* with others is what they experience to be *real*. It is a *shared reality*."
> — [E. Tory Higgins, Shared Reality: What Makes Us Strong and Tears Us Apart, Oxford University Press 2019](https://academic.oup.com/book/35191/chapter-abstract/299566938)

The theory has a double edge that the book's subtitle names. On one side, shared realities connect us to close others, create common interests and make life meaningful; they become the truth about what to feel and what to believe. On the other side, because of this, we distrust and even fear the members of any other group whose shared realities differ ([E. Tory Higgins 2019](https://academic.oup.com/book/35191/chapter-abstract/299566938)).

The epistemology matters as much as the motivation. What one person takes to be an opinion becomes objective when several others take it up too; that is the move Hardin and Higgins's 1996 paper is remembered for, and the one the compendium's intersubjectivity principles pick up.

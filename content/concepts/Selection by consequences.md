Selection by consequences is B. F. Skinner's 1981 Science paper's causal principle: consequences select the behavior that follows them, just as they select the traits that reproduce in a species. The paper's opening:

> Selection by consequences is a causal mode found only in living things, or in machines made by living things. It was first recognized in natural selection, but it also accounts for the shaping and maintenance of the behavior of the individual and the evolution of cultures. In all three of these fields, it replaces explanations based on the causal modes of classical mechanics.

(B. F. Skinner, "Selection by Consequences," Science 213(4507), 1981, [abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4331065/), p. 501)

The paper runs the principle at three levels. Natural selection is the first: reproduction as a first consequence led to cells, organs and organisms reproducing themselves under increasingly diverse conditions. Operant conditioning is the second: new responses strengthened by the events that follow them. The third is cultural: "The effect on the group, and not the reinforcing consequences for individual members, is responsible for the evolution of culture" (p. 502).

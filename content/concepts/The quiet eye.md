The quiet eye is Joan Vickers' term for the steady final gaze before a skilled action: the last fixation on the target, held before and into the critical movement, which in her studies of expert athletes predicts accuracy. The formal definition:

> "The quiet eye has five measureable characteristics. It is formally defined as the final fixation or tracking gaze that is 1) located on a specific location or object in the task space within 3° of visual angle (or less) for a minimum of 100 ms. The 2) onset of the QE occurs prior to a 3) critical movement in the task, and the 4) offset occurs when the gaze deviates of the object or location by more than 3° degrees of visual angle (or less) for a minimum of 100 ms." (Vickers, 2007, [quieteyesolutions.com](http://www.quieteyesolutions.com/content/Vickers_2007/Introduction.htm))

### What the gaze shows

The fifth characteristic is the empirical core: "The duration of the quiet eye is longer in experts than non-experts, and also longer during successful versus unsuccessful trials (Vickers, 1996, 2007)." In the original free-throw study, experts held their final fixation for around 970 ms on made shots against about 810 ms on missed shots, while near-experts stayed under 400 ms either way (Vickers, 1996, as tabulated in the Sirnik et al. 2022 review of visual attention in basketball shooting). The finding generalises across aimed skills, and quiet-eye training studies report performance gains when the gaze is trained rather than the movement. My kernel K109 carries the practice side; the concept holds the research. What the literature does not test: the quiet eye as a general contemplative attention outside aimed motor tasks; Vickers' proposed mechanism is visuomotor programming, and the appealing reading of it as quieted self-referential thought is an interpretation, not a finding.

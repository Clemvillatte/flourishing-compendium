Das Nichts, "the nothing", is the centre of Heidegger's 1929 inaugural lecture What Is Metaphysics?. The lecture's claim is that the nothing is met in a particular mood, anxiety, and that this meeting is what makes beings stand out as beings. The passage below is from the Pathmarks translation (GA 9, p. 90).

### Met in anxiety

"In anxiety beings as a whole become superfluous." Heidegger denies that anxiety wipes anything out: "Beings are not annihilated by anxiety, so that nothing is left." He also denies that the nothing is produced by a mental act of negation: "No kind of annihilation of the whole of beings in themselves takes place in anxiety; just as little do we produce a negation of beings as a whole in order to attain the nothing for the first time." Instead, "the nothing makes itself known with beings and in beings expressly as a slipping away of the whole" ([Heidegger, What Is Metaphysics?, Pathmarks p. 90](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/Pathmarks/Pathmarks.090.html)).

### Nihilation

He gives the activity of the nothing its own name: "This wholly repelling gesture toward beings that are slipping away as a whole, which is the action of the nothing that closes in on Dasein in anxiety, is the essence of the nothing: nihilation. It is neither an annihilation of beings nor does it spring from a negation." The sentence that became famous follows: "The nothing itself nihilates." ([Pathmarks p. 90](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/Pathmarks/Pathmarks.090.html)).

### Why it matters to him

Nihilation, he writes, "manifests these beings in their full but heretofore concealed strangeness as what is radically other - with respect to the nothing. In the clear night of the nothing of anxiety the original openness of beings as such arises: that they are beings — and not nothing." The nothing "brings Da-sein for the first time before beings as such" ([Pathmarks p. 90](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/Pathmarks/Pathmarks.090.html)).

### A contested passage

Sorensen's Stanford Encyclopedia entry reports that Carnap quoted lines from the lecture, ending with "The Nothing itself nothings", and that the paragraph "became notorious as a specimen of metaphysical nonsense." Sorensen replies that "there is a difference between a failure to understand and an understanding of failure" and suggests Carnap should consider that Heidegger's sentences might be "illuminating nonsense" ([SEP, "Nothingness"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nothingness/)). Other critics, he adds, think the passages carry too many meanings.

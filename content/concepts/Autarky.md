Autarky (autarkeia) is self-sufficiency: the ideal of needing nothing external for the good life. The word comes "from the Ancient Greek word αὐτάρκεια, which means 'self-sufficiency' (derived from αὐτο-, 'self', and ἀρκέω, 'to suffice')" ([Wikipedia Autarkeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarkeia)). Historically the ideal shaped Greek political culture, "emphasizing economic self-sufficiency and local self-rule" ([Wikipedia Autarkeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarkeia)).

The schools gave it different weights. "In Stoicism the concept of autarky represents independence from anything external, including independence from personal relationships, so as to immune one from vagaries of fortune. The Stoic sage is autarkic by being dependent only on his own virtue" ([Wikipedia Autarkeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarkeia)). "In Epicureanism the concept of autarky represents having the fewest possible requirements for living a life of pleasure, free of pain (aponia)" ([Wikipedia Autarkeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarkeia)). Aristotle's version is the contemplative's: the most self-sufficient activity, bounded by bodily needs, moderate means, and a self-sufficiency that includes family and fellow citizens.

Autarky is not isolation. In all its versions it is independence of flourishing from fortune — a scale of needing less, not a wall against others.

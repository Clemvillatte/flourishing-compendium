Being-toward-death (Sein-zum-Tode) is Heidegger's name for the stance in which Dasein takes ownership of its own finitude instead of fleeing it. Heidegger defines death as "the possibility of the absolute impossibility of Dasein" (SZ 250, as quoted in the [SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)) — the one possibility that is mine alone, which no one can take over for me, and which individualizes: "The anticipation of death, Heidegger argues, 'individualizes Dasein down to itself' (SZ 263)" ([SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)).

The everyday alternative is flight. Inauthentic Dasein "flees from this experience of nothingness, doubling down on its conformist adherence to everyday norms and identities," seeking "reassurance that it is living as one is supposed to (SZ 267–8)" ([SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)) — the retreat into [[The they (das Man)|the they]]. Authentic Dasein, by contrast, "will *anticipate* or *run forward toward* death (SZ 266)" ([SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)). Anticipation is not morbid preoccupation: it "does not involve 'brooding over' death, or 'thinking about death' and 'pondering over when and how this possibility may perhaps be actualized' (SZ 261)" ([SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)). It consists in taking up one's finite existence as finite.

The payoff is freedom. In anticipating death, "one can authentically understand and choose among the factical possibilities lying ahead of that possibility which is not to be outstripped. (i.e., death; SZ 263)" ([SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)). By accepting that every possibility is "unavoidably prone to nullity," I "liberate myself to choose my own projects without the illusion that, existentially speaking, one choice is any better or worse than another" ([SEP Heidegger article](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/)).

For the flourishing project the concept reframes mortality from threat to clarifier: living toward death, rather than in denial of it, is what lets a finite agent own its choices. It resonates with [[Memento mori]] and with Buddhist contemplations of mortality, though Heidegger's own frame is ontological rather than therapeutic.

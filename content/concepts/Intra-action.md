Barad's coinage, from Meeting the Universe Halfway, is designed to replace one assumption in the word "interaction":

> "The notion of intra-action is a key element of my agential realist framework. The neologism 'intra-action' signifies the mutual constitution of entangled agencies. That is, in contrast to the usual 'interaction,' which assumes that there are separate individual agencies that precede their interaction, the notion of intra-action recognizes that distinct agencies do not precede, but rather emerge through, their intra-action. It is important to note that the 'distinct' agencies are only distinct in a relational, not an absolute, sense, that is, agencies are only distinct in relation to their mutual entanglement; they don't exist as individual elements." (Barad 2007, Meeting the Universe Halfway, p. 33, [as quoted in a journal article hosted at tidsskrift.dk](https://tidsskrift.dk/KKF/article/download/28152/24779/65404); the quoting article's author is not named in the material I opened)

The quotation does its metaphysical work inside a single sentence: distinct agencies are allowed to be "distinct" only "in a relational, not an absolute, sense," and what precedes them is not a stage with two actors already on it but a mutual entanglement out of which the actors come. For the compendium the payoff is grammatical before it is metaphysical, and it lands on practice: if the parties are made by the relation, then asking what a person brings to a relationship already assumes the wrong order of events. The question has to run the other way — what relations is this person part of, and what do those relations keep producing? — and P147's notice, that you and the world co-constitute each other, is the observation intra-action names from the physics side.

**Plain words** "Interaction" pictures two finished things bumping. "Intra-action" says there were no two finished things: the bump is what finishes them. You and what you are dealing with make each other in the dealing, which is why Barad insists the separate agencies are only separate "in a relational, not an absolute, sense."

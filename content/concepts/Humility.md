Humility, in the psychological literature, is not self-deprecation. June Price Tangney's review, the field's touchstone, contrasts the folk reading (unworthiness, low self-regard) with the theoreticians' construct: an accurate assessment of one's characteristics, an ability to acknowledge limitations, and a forgetting of the self. The accurate self-assessment matters twice over. It means the humble person neither inflates strengths nor performatively deflates them, and it pairs with an outward orientation, an appreciation of what other people and things contribute. Later work grounds the same idea in relationships: humility as judged by others, combining accurate self-assessment with an other-oriented stance, predicts relationship and leadership outcomes (Owens et al., 2013).

### The key elements

> Specifically, the key elements of humility seem to include the: accurate assessment of one's abilities and achievements (not low self-esteem, self-deprecation), ability to acknowledge one's mistakes, imperfections, gaps in knowledge and limitations (often vis-à-vis a "higher power"), openness to new ideas, contradictory information, and advice, keeping of one's abilities and accomplishments, one's place in the world, in perspective (e.g., seeing oneself as just one person in the larger scheme of things), relatively low self-focus, a "forgetting of the self," while recognizing that one is but one part of the larger universe, appreciation of the value of all things, as well as the many different ways that people and things can contribute to our world. (Tangney 2000, pp. 73-74, as quoted verbatim in Michalec et al. 2025, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11745895/))

The tradition behind the construct is older and wider. Aquinas treats humility as a virtue moderating the appetite for one's own excellence (Summa Theologiae II-II, q.161), Augustine and the kenosis distinction mark the Christian line, and Peirce's fallibilism carries the epistemic version: holding every belief as provisional. What ties the theological and the research literatures together is the same double move. See yourself plainly, strengths included, and hold that self lightly, as one part of a larger whole.

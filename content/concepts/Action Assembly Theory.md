## In plain words

Action assembly theory is John Greene's account of how thought becomes speech and gesture. Its stated aim: "Action assembly theory (AAT) seeks to explain message behavior (both verbal and nonverbal) by describing the system of mental structures and processes that give rise to those behaviors" ([Greene's encyclopedia entry](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781405186407.wbieca008.pub2)). In plain words, the things you say are assembled on the spot from stored building blocks, which is how you can say things you have never said before.

## Where it comes from

Greene published the theory in 1984 as "A cognitive approach to human communication: An action assembly theory," Communication Monographs 51, 289-306. The abstract states the puzzle it answers: "An essential characteristic of human communicative behavior is that it is at once novel and creative yet patterned and repetitive" ([Taylor & Francis, article record](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03637758409390203)).

## How it works

The core unit is the procedural record. "Procedural records are mental structures that incorporate knowledge about behavioral features (what to do and say) along with information about interactional features (how it affects others) and contextual features (when it applies)" ([Bylund, Peterson & Cameron 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3297682/)). A record joins the assembly when its activation crosses a threshold, and activation depends on how often and how recently it has been used. The assembled records form an output representation that guides the behaviour; the reviewing authors give a lego analogy: enough properly connected records produce a recognizable structure.

## What it explains

The theory covers the production side of communication, how behaviour can be new each time yet still patterned. As Greene's encyclopedia summary states, the theory "does not yield specific, testable predictions about behavior in the abstract"; its assumptions must be specified case by case ([Greene's encyclopedia entry](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781405186407.wbieca008.pub2)).

## Everyday life

Imagine finding words for a grieving friend. AAT would describe this as drawing on elements from earlier conversations and combining them for the new situation. Rehearsal and repeated use strengthen the records, so skill grows through practice rather than intention alone.

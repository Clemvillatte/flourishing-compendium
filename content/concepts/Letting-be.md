Letting-be is the English rendering of Heidegger's later term Gelassenheit, a comportment toward things and beings that lets them be instead of ordering them. On the translation itself: "Gelassenheit has thus often been translated as 'serenity', 'letting be', 'abandonment', 'detachment', but by far the most widespread (and deemed most appropriate by scholars; Schürmann, 2001) is the term 'releasement'" ([Dasein Foundation, Gelassenheit](https://dasein.foundation/gelassenheit)). The comportment shows up across his later texts. In "The Question Concerning Technology" the bringing-forth he describes works by letting: "The four ways of being responsible bring something into appearance. They let it come forth into presencing [An-wesen]" ([Heidegger, The Question Concerning Technology, PDF p. 4](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/a/3282/files/2018/01/Heidegger_TheQuestionConcerningTechnology.pdf)). Its opposite is enframing (Gestell), the revealing that challenges the actual to show up as standing-reserve.

### Releasement toward things

The practical form Heidegger gives letting-be in the Discourse on Thinking is Gelassenheit zu den Dingen, releasement toward things:

> "We let technical devices enter our daily life, and at the same time leave them outside, that is, let them alone, as things which are nothing absolute but remain dependent upon something higher. I would call this comportment toward technology which expresses \"yes\" and at the same time \"no,\" by an old word, releasement toward things." ([Heidegger, Discourse on Thinking, p. 54, full text](https://archive.org/stream/heidegger-discourse-on-thinking/Heidegger%20-%20Discourse%20on%20Thinking_djvu.txt))

Heidegger adds that this is not passivity: "Yet releasement toward things and openness to the mystery never happen of themselves. They do not befall us accidentally. Both flourish only through persistent, courageous thinking" ([same text, p. 56](https://archive.org/stream/heidegger-discourse-on-thinking/Heidegger%20-%20Discourse%20on%20Thinking_djvu.txt)).

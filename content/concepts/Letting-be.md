Letting-be is Martin Heidegger's name for the comportment that lets things come forth into their own presence instead of being ordered as raw material. In "The Question Concerning Technology" the bringing-forth he describes works this way: "The four ways of being responsible bring something into appearance. They let it come forth into presencing [An-wesen]" ([Heidegger, The Question Concerning Technology, p. 4](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/a/3282/files/2018/01/Heidegger_TheQuestionConcerningTechnology.pdf)). Its opposite is enframing (Gestell), the revealing that challenges the actual to show up as standing-reserve.

### Releasement toward things

The practical form Heidegger gives it is Gelassenheit zu den Dingen, releasement toward things. A commentary quoting the Discourse on Thinking: with awareness of what the devices do, man "becomes free of them", free "to 'let go of them at any time'", and able "to deny them the right to dominate us, and so to wrap, confuse, and lay waste our nature"; this disposition Heidegger calls "releasement toward things" ([Heidegger 1966, Discourse on Thinking, pp. 53-54, quoted in Minerva vol. 10](https://minerva.mic.ul.ie/vol10/Heidegger.pdf)). Releasement "does not belong to the realm of willing"; it is a non-willing that waits ([same commentary](https://minerva.mic.ul.ie/vol10/Heidegger.pdf)).

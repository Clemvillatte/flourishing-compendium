Husserl's distinction, worked out in the Second Meditation of the Cartesian Meditations, is that what a mental act explicitly presents is never all that it means. Every act carries an implicit surplus, its "horizon":

> "Every subjective process has a process 'horizon', which changes with the alteration of the nexus of consciousness to which the process belongs and with the alteration of the process itself from phase to phase of its flow — an intentional horizon of reference to potentialities of consciousness that belong to the process itself." (Husserl, Cartesian Meditations §19, Cairns trans., p. 44, [full-text PDF](https://ia800101.us.archive.org/15/items/CartesiamMeditations/12813080-husserl-cartesian-meditations.pdf))

> "Intentional analysis is guided by the fundamental cognition that, as a consciousness, every cogito is indeed (in the broadest sense) a meaning of its meant [Meinung seines Gemeinten], but that, at any moment, this something meant [dieses Vermeinte] is more something meant with something more than what is meant at that moment 'explicitly.'" (Husserl, Cartesian Meditations §20, Cairns trans., p. 46, [full-text PDF](https://ia800101.us.archive.org/15/items/CartesiamMeditations/12813080-husserl-cartesian-meditations.pdf))

The horizons are not static, and the passage says so twice: "The horizons are 'predelineated' potentialities" (§19, p. 44), and the move from implicit to explicit is given as an operation on them — "We can ask any horizon what 'lies in it'; we can explicate or unfold it, and 'uncover' the potentialities of conscious life at a particular time" (§19, pp. 44-45). Husserl adds that the explication never finishes: "Precisely thereby we uncover the objective sense meant implicitly in the actual cogito, though never with more than a certain degree of foreshadowing. This sense, the cogitatum qua cogitatum, is never present to actual consciousness as a finished datum…" (§19, p. 45; the sentence continues with the new horizons continuously awakened).

**Plain words** Whatever you are noticing now, you are also meaning more than you are noticing: the back of the cup, the rest of the sentence, the next note. Explicit is what is in view; implicit is the rest of what you mean, waiting to be unfolded.

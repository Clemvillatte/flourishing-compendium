Husserl's distinction, worked out in the Second Meditation of the Cartesian Meditations, is that what a mental act explicitly presents is never all that it means. Every act carries an implicit surplus, its "horizon":

> "Every subjective process has a process 'horizon', which changes with the alteration of the nexus of consciousness to which the process belongs and with the alteration of the process itself from phase to phase of its flow — an intentional horizon of reference to potentialities of consciousness that belong to the process itself." (Husserl, Cartesian Meditations §19, Cairns trans., p. 44, [full-text PDF](https://ia800101.us.archive.org/15/items/CartesiamMeditations/12813080-husserl-cartesian-meditations.pdf))

> "Intentional analysis is guided by the fundamental cognition that, as a consciousness, every cogito is indeed (in the broadest sense) a meaning of its meant, but that, at any moment, this something meant is more something meant with something more than what is meant at that moment 'explicitly'." (Husserl, Cartesian Meditations §20, Cairns trans., p. 46, [full-text PDF](https://ia800101.us.archive.org/15/items/CartesiamMeditations/12813080-husserl-cartesian-meditations.pdf))

Husserl's example territory is perception: the front of the house is given, the back is meant along with it, and the implicit meant can be made explicit by walking round. But the structure is general for him; the horizon of a present remembering, a judgment or a name is built the same way, out of predelineated possibilities. The implicit content is not a hidden second object; it is the same meaning, anticipated: "The horizons are 'predelineated' potentialities" (§19, p. 44). And the move from implicit to explicit is a defined operation, not a different kind of act: "We can ask any horizon what 'lies in it'; we can explicate or unfold it, and 'uncover' the potentialities of conscious life at a particular time" (§19, pp. 44-45).

**Plain words** Whatever you are noticing now, you are also meaning more than you are noticing: the back of the cup, the rest of the sentence, the next note. Explicit is what is in view; implicit is the rest of what you mean, waiting to be unfolded.

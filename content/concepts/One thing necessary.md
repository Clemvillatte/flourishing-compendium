"One thing necessary" is the phrase in Luke 10:42, at the end of the story in which Martha, busy with hospitality, asks Jesus to tell her sister Mary to help, and Jesus answers her. The compendium uses the phrase for the idea of a single priority beneath many demands.

> "But the Lord answered her, 'Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her.'" (Luke 10:41-42, English Standard Version, [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+10%3A41-42&version=ESV))

### The wording varies

English versions differ because the manuscripts do. The New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition reads "you are worried and distracted by many things, but few things are needed—indeed only one. Mary has chosen the better part," and its note on verse 42 says: "Other ancient authorities read but only one thing is needed" ([NRSVUE, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+10%3A38-42&version=NRSVUE)). The ESV note on the same verse reads: "Some manuscripts few things are necessary, or only one" ([ESV, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+10%3A41-42&version=ESV)). So the exact phrase "one thing is necessary" is one attested reading among several, which matters for any essay that leans on it as a quotation.

### What the text does not say

The passage names the contrast between being "anxious and troubled about many things" and one thing needed. It does not say in these verses what the one thing is. Mary is described as one who "sat at Jesus's feet and listened to what he was saying" (NRSVUE v. 39), and her portion is called "good" or "better." Readings that turn this into a doctrine of contemplation over action are later interpretation, and this page does not draw on any commentary.

### In the compendium

The principles below are linked by the compendium's seed citation, which calls Luke 10:42 a cultural parallel. The links include Y063 (Hold one practice to steady the mind) and SP090 (Center your life on the highest good). Whether those principles state the same idea as Luke is a question for the reader, and the passage itself does not decide it.

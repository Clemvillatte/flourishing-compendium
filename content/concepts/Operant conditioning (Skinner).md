B. F. Skinner's account of learning by consequences: behaviour that operates on the environment becomes more or less probable depending on what follows it. The system was laid out in Science and Human Behavior (1953), with the schedules of reinforcement worked out in Ferster and Skinner (1957).

### The operant, in Skinner's words

> "The unit of a predictive science is, therefore, not a response but a class of responses. The word 'operant' will be used to describe this class. The term emphasizes the fact that the behavior operates upon the environment to generate consequences." — [Skinner, Science and Human Behavior (1953), pp. 65](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)

The strengthening process itself is defined by its effect on frequency: "In operant conditioning we 'strengthen' an operant in the sense of making a response more probable or, in actual fact, more frequent" ([Skinner, 1953, p. 65](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)). What is strengthened is the class defined by its consequence, not the single act.

### How it explains, and where it reaches

The mechanism the quotes carry is frequency: consequences change how often a class of responses occurs. Skinner's own summary of its reach: "Through operant conditioning the environment builds the basic repertoire with which we keep our balance, walk, play games, handle instruments and tools, talk, write, sail a boat, drive a car, or fly a plane" ([Skinner, 1953, p. 66](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)). Imagine that checking a phone is followed by receiving a message, and checking becomes more frequent under those conditions; in this hypothetical, the message functions as reinforcement.

**Ego depletion theory Baumeister** — self-control draws on a limited resource that is consumed by use and restored by rest and glucose.

The research definition, in the multilab preregistered replication that tested it: self-control is conceptualized as a limited resource that becomes depleted after a period of exertion resulting in self-control failure, a state known as ego depletion, typically tested with a sequential-task paradigm in which an initial self-control task reduces performance on a subsequent one ([Hagger et al. 2016, Perspect Psychol Sci 11(4):546-573, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/)).

The empirical status is part of the concept's content: a meta-analysis of ego-depletion experiments found a medium-sized effect, but subsequent meta-analyses questioned the size and existence of the effect and identified instances of possible bias, which prompted the registered replication report ([Hagger et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/)).

> "Multiple laboratories (k = 23, total N = 2,141) conducted replications of a standardized ego-depletion protocol based on a sequential-task paradigm by Sripada et al. Meta-analysis of the studies revealed that the size of the ego-depletion effect was small with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) that encompassed zero (d = 0.04, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.15]." — [Hagger et al. 2016, abstract, PubMed 27474142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/)

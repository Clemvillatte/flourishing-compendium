The original entry line for this page pointed at Bhagavad Gita 2.67, where the mind carried away by the senses is compared to a ship in a storm. The verse itself, in Edwin Arnold's translation:

> The mind that gives itself to follow shows of sense / Seeth its helm of wisdom rent away, / And, like a ship in waves of whirlwind, drives / To wreck and death.

(Bhagavad Gita, Book II, [Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial, Project Gutenberg #2388](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2388))

The sense in the verse is a helm-of-wisdom image: give the mind to sense-objects and the wisdom that steers is torn away. My title, "the strong claim of the loud voice," is its own gloss on that image, applied to a different register: the loud internal voice that insists on its story. The concept names the pull a vivid, insistent, self-directed thought exerts over quieter judgment, and the practice is to notice the pull rather than obey it.

The title is a compendium coinage, not a source phrase; the Gita verse is the anchor the original entry gives it, and the gloss is my own bridge between the ancient image and the modern voice.

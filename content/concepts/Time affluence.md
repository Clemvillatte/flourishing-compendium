## The concept

Time affluence is the subjective sense of having enough time, together with autonomy over its use. Tim Kasser and Kennon Sheldon, who gave the concept its name, defined it as a perception rather than an objective amount of free time, and set it against material affluence as an alternative route to well-being. Across four studies they found that the experience of time affluence contributed to life satisfaction and positive affect independently of income level.

## Facets

- **and well-being** — Whillans et al., 2016

Later work shifted the emphasis from having time to valuing it. Whillans, Weidman and Dunn (2016), across six studies with 4,690 participants, found that people who prioritize time over money report greater subjective well-being, an effect that held controlling for materialism and for current feelings of time or material affluence.

> “Around the world, increases in wealth have produced an unintended consequence: a rising sense of time scarcity. We provide evidence that using money to buy time can provide a buffer against this time famine, thereby promoting happiness. Using large, diverse samples from the United States, Canada, Denmark, and The Netherlands (n = 6,271), we show that individuals who spend money on time-saving services report greater life satisfaction. A field experiment provides causal evidence that working adults report greater happiness after spending money on a time-saving purchase than on a material purchase.”
>
> — Whillans, Dunn, Smeets, Bekkers and Norton, [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1706541114), 2017

The 2017 buying-time studies add the causal half: money spent on time-saving services buys back the very resource whose scarcity undermines well-being.

## The concept

Time affluence is the subjective sense of having enough time, together with autonomy over its use. Tim Kasser and Kennon Sheldon, who conceptualized the construct, defined it as a perception rather than an objective amount of free time, and set it against material affluence as an alternative route to well-being. Across four studies, the experience of time affluence was associated with life satisfaction and positive affect independently of income level.

## Facets

- **and well-being** — Whillans et al., 2016

Later work moved from feeling time-affluent to prioritizing time. Whillans, Weidman and Dunn (2016) studied the stable preference to prioritize time over money: across six studies (N = 4,690), people with that preference reported greater subjective well-being, and the association held after controlling for materialism, material striving, and current feelings of time or material affluence. A third construct concerns using money to buy time: in the 2017 buying-time studies, individuals who spent money on time-saving services reported greater life satisfaction, and a field experiment found that working adults reported greater happiness after a time-saving purchase than after a material purchase.

> “Around the world, increases in wealth have produced an unintended consequence: a rising sense of time scarcity. We provide evidence that using money to buy time can provide a buffer against this time famine, thereby promoting happiness. Using large, diverse samples from the United States, Canada, Denmark, and The Netherlands (n = 6,271), we show that individuals who spend money on time-saving services report greater life satisfaction. A field experiment provides causal evidence that working adults report greater happiness after spending money on a time-saving purchase than on a material purchase.”
>
> — Whillans, Dunn, Smeets, Bekkers and Norton, Buying time promotes happiness, [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1706541114), 2017 (excerpt from the Abstract)

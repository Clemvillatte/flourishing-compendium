**Self psychology** — Kohut — development and therapy hinge on selfobject experiences: mirroring, idealisation and twinship.

Self psychology is the psychoanalytic theory Heinz Kohut introduced in the mid-1970s as a further development of psychoanalysis, with a model of development and a model of therapy. It treats the self as the central part of the personality, built and kept stable through relationships with significant others. The sources below are secondary descriptions and one empirical study; Kohut's own books were not opened.

### The self and its selfobjects

A 2017 nursing-research paper summarises Kohut's view: selfobjects are "significant others or objects we experience as parts of our self". Kohut described the self as a dynamic system with three axes (the tripolar self): a grandiose axis that sustains a stable sense of self-esteem, an idealizing axis that supports a solid system of goals and ideals, and a twinship axis that supports affectionate relationships and a sense of fellowship. ([Rønning & Bjørkly 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5340182/))

### Three selfobject needs

Each axis develops through a matching need that other people meet.

> "The selfobject needs for mirroring are the need to be valued by others and to be admired as well as to be confirmed by others. The selfobject needs for idealization are the urge to create idealized images of significant others and to merge with idealized selfobjects. The selfobject needs for twinship/alter-ego are the need to feel likeness to others and to be included in their fellowship." (Rønning & Bjørkly 2017, [PMC5340182](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5340182/))

The same paper adds that selfobject relations nourish the self's capacity to remain stable, so that a person can more easily achieve or keep self-cohesion.

### Does it hold up empirically?

Banai, Mikulincer and Shaver (2005) ran seven studies on the three needs and on avoiding acknowledging them, using a new self-report measure. Their abstract says the findings "supported and refined Kohut's ideas about the independence of the 3 selfobject needs" and the contribution of these needs to "problems in interpersonal functioning, mental health, self-cohesion, and affect regulation", and that they reveal links between Kohut's theory and attachment theory. ([Hebrew University record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/selfobject-needs-in-kohuts-self-psychology-links-with-attachment-/)) The abstract describes support for specific constructs measured by questionnaire, not validation of the whole theory or its therapeutic claims.

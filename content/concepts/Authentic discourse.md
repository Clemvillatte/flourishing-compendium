The term is grounded here in Heidegger, Being and Time, §34. Discourse (Rede) is for Heidegger the articulation of intelligibility: it picks out the significant features of our active engagement with the world, the features we understand, interpret, and to which we affectively respond. Discourse underlies language rather than resulting from it, and communication in his sense means letting another see with us what we have pointed out, sharing an orientation toward something. The concept matters for my account of idle talk because idle talk is not a different activity but a degradation of discourse. As the Stanford Encyclopedia's article on Heidegger puts it:

> "But primordial discourse can degrade into idle talk (Gerede). We then no longer possess the skills to pick out the rich, fine-grained features of situations that guide a genuine understanding. Instead, we make use of an articulation that is levelled down in such a way that anyone can understand it and talk about it: 'Idle talk is the possibility of understanding everything without previously making the thing one's own' (SZ 169)." (Mark Wrathall, [Martin Heidegger, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/), first published January 31, 2025)

Authentic discourse, then, is discourse that keeps hold of the things themselves: the speaker has made the matter their own, and the words still point to the fine-grained features of the situation rather than to a flattened articulation that circulates on its own. The inner quotation is Heidegger's own formula from Being and Time, and the practice it names is the opposite of the idle talk my exposure exercises target: understanding a thing by going through it, not understanding everything without previously making the thing one's own.

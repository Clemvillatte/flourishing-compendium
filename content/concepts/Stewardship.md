"Stewardship" — Whitehead, Process and Reality.

Stewardship is the holding of something that is not one's own: resources, a place, a tradition, a life, with the duty to keep and grow it rather than use it up. The compendium's R028 link carries the Māori term kaitiakitanga, guardianship of the natural world. The Bible's parable of the talents gives the idea its sharpest scene: servants are entrusted with sums, and the question at the return of the master is what each did with what was handed over.

> "His lord said unto him, Well done, thou good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful over a few things, I will make thee ruler over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy lord." (Matthew 25:21, King James Version, [BibleHub](https://biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/25.htm))

### What the holding is for

The parable's praise goes to the servant who put what was entrusted to work, and its charge falls on the one who buried it. Stewardship, read this way, is neither ownership nor renunciation: it is answerable care. The compendium's principle R028 makes it a practice — guardianship of people and places one did not make and will not keep. Whitehead's Process and Reality is the book this heading was first filed under; the parable in Matthew is where the idea is stated.

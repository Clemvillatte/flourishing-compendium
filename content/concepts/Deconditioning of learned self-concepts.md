"Deconditioning of learned self-concepts" is a general label, not the name of a single research programme. The closest experimental work in the sources consulted comes from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), which tests techniques for loosening the hold of negative self-referential thoughts. This page covers that work and stays within what the studies show.

### Laboratory evidence for defusion

A meta-analysis of laboratory studies of cognitive defusion pooled twelve studies (22 samples, n = 659). It found that "defusion produced large reductions in believability (Hedges' g = 1.04) and discomfort (g = 1.12)", with word repetition showing larger effects on believability than other techniques ([Oppo et al. 2026, Journal of Contextual Behavioral Science](https://contextualscience.org/publications/defusion_techniques_selfreferential_thought_laboratorybased_experimental_research)). In an earlier experiment, non-clinical undergraduates in a defusion condition reported less emotional discomfort and believability of a negative self-referential thought than those in distraction or control conditions ([Masuda et al. 2010, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19716550/)).

### Self-as-context

ACT distinguishes three aspects of self. Self-as-context is described in a trial paper as "experiential contact with a persistent and an unchanging perspective from which all experiences are observed", and patients learn to build it "whilst simultaneously liberating themselves from any over-attachment to a conceptualised self" ([Springer, dismantling trial](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41811-021-00118-6)). A study of ACT for chronic pain found that "increases in SAC were associated with improved functioning" ([Change in Self-as-Context, chronic pain study](https://www.em-consulte.com/es/article/1150703/resume/change-in-self-as-context-perspective-taking-occur)).

### Mixed results

The findings are not uniform. In one experimental test, defused statements lowered discomfort, but believability "actually increased" ([Maynooth experimental test](https://mural.maynoothuniversity.ie/id/eprint/4954/1/YBH_experimental%20test.pdf)). A chatbot-delivered defusion study found no significant differences among groups on believability, discomfort or willingness ([Springer, chatbot study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40732-021-00478-7)). The pilot dismantling trial of self-as-context had 16 participants and found a small between-arm effect on functioning (0.20) and depression (0.39) ([Springer, dismantling trial](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41811-021-00118-6)).

### Scope

These studies address negative thoughts about the self in short experiments and small trials. They do not test whether a lifelong, conditioned self-concept is undone. The link to the uncarved block comes from me, not from these sources.

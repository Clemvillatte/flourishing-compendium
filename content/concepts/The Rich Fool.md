The Rich Fool is a parable told in the Gospel of Luke, chapter 12, verses 16 to 21. A farmer's land produces so much that he has nowhere to store it, so he plans larger barns and promises his soul a life of ease. God calls him a fool and tells him his soul is required that night.

### The setting

Jesus tells the parable in answer to a request. A man in the crowd asks him to make his brother divide an inheritance. Jesus replies, "Man, who made me a judge or a divider over you?" and then says: "Take heed, and beware of covetousness: for a man's life consisteth not in the abundance of the things which he possesseth" (Luke 12:13-15, King James Version, [BibleHub](https://biblehub.com/kjv/luke/12.htm)). The parable follows directly as an illustration of that warning.

### The farmer's reasoning

The farmer "thought within himself, saying, What shall I do, because I have no room where to bestow my fruits?" His plan is to "pull down my barns, and build greater". He then says, "I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years; take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry" (Luke 12:17-19, KJV, [BibleHub](https://biblehub.com/kjv/luke/12.htm)). In the text he speaks only to himself and never mentions anyone else, including the people who might need the surplus.

### The verdict

> "But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided? So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God." (Luke 12:20-21, KJV)

The World English Bible renders the same lines as "You foolish one, tonight your soul is required of you. The things which you have prepared—whose will they be?" ([Luke 12, World English Bible](https://bible-api.com/luke+12:13-21?translation=web)). The parable's final line contrasts treasure laid up for oneself with being "rich toward God".

### Scope

This page uses the biblical text only. It gives no commentary from biblical scholars, so how the parable has been interpreted historically is not covered here.

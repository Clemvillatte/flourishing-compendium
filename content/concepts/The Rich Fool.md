"The Rich Fool" — Gospel of Luke 12.16-21, as cultural parallel.

The Rich Fool is Jesus' parable of a farmer whose harvest outgrows his barns. He tears them down, builds bigger ones, and tells his soul to take its ease — and God calls him a fool: "this night thy soul shall be required of thee." The parable's charge is not wealth itself but the project of securing a self against the future with things.

> "But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee: then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided? So is he that layeth up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God." (Luke 12:20-21, King James Version, [BibleHub](https://biblehub.com/kjv/luke/12.htm))

### What the parable is for

The fool's error is the monologue: he reasons only within himself, counts only his fruits and goods, and addresses his own soul as a storehouse to be filled. The compendium's principle SP025 reads the parable's lesson into its own territory: greed, luxury and drunkenness are treated there as species of madness, and the rich man's night-time audit is the madness made visible. The parable asks the reader where security is being sought.

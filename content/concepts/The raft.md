“The raft” — Buddha, MN 22 (Bhikkhu Bodhi, trans. “The Exposition of the Simile of the Snake and the Raft”).

The raft is the Buddha's simile for the Dhamma, his teaching, as a vehicle used for crossing and then released. In MN 22 a traveler comes to a great expanse of water, the near shore dubious and risky, the further shore secure and free from risk, with neither ferryboat nor bridge. He binds grass, twigs, branches and leaves into a raft and crosses to safety with the effort of his hands and feet ([MN 22, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)).

> "Monks, I will teach you the Dhamma compared to a raft, for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of holding onto." (MN 22, [Thanissaro translation, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html))

### What should be done with the raft

Having crossed, the man might hoist the raft on his head or carry it on his back and go wherever he likes; the Buddha asks the monks whether that would be doing what should be done with the raft, and they answer no. What should be done is to drag it on dry land or sink it in the water. The discourse closes the simile: "In the same way, monks, I have taught the Dhamma compared to a raft, for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of holding onto. Understanding the Dhamma as taught compared to a raft, you should let go even of Dhammas, to say nothing of non-Dhammas." ([MN 22](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html))

The simile sits in a discourse about clinging to views, alongside the simile of the water-snake, in which the Dhamma grasped wrongly harms its holder as a snake seized by the tail. Thanissaro Bhikkhu's introduction to the discourse notes that the two similes work together: the Dhamma has to be grasped, and grasped properly, before it can be let go ([MN 22 introduction](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html)).

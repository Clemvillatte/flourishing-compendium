“Immediate and reflective value” — Dewey, Experience and Nature.

Dewey's distinction separates the value that simply occurs from the value that survives inquiry. His formulation of the first:

> "Values are values, things immediately having certain intrinsic qualities. Of them as values there is accordingly nothing to be said, they are what they are. All that can be said of them concerns their generative conditions and the consequences to which they give rise." ([Dewey, Experience and Nature, ch. IX](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.188224/2015.188224.Experience-And-Nature-John-Dewey_djvu.txt))

Immediate values are had, not judged. "Of immediate values as such, values which occur and are possessed and enjoyed, there is no theory at all; they just occur, are enjoyed, possessed; and that is all." A liking, a loveliness, a charm: each simply is what it is, and the moment discourse begins, something else has begun too.

### What reflection adds

The second term of the pair is the work of criticism. The passage turns on belief: "If a man believes in ghosts, devils, miracles, fortune-tellers, the immutable certainty of the existing economic regime, and the supreme merits of his political party and its leaders, he does so believe; these are immediate goods to him." When the question of real value is raised, "the appeal is to criticism, intelligence. And the court of appeal decides by the law of conditions and consequences." The reflective verdict remains an immediate good when it arrives, but one "whose character now depends upon the reflective operations whose conclusion it is."

### In the compendium

The distinction underwrites the compendium's principle that immediate values should be honored and enriched rather than overridden, and it runs through the practice entries: [[ST308 — Deliberate Practice]] is reflection organized around skill, and [[K264 — Deliberation timeout]] marks the point at which deliberation itself stops paying. Reflection, for Dewey, is for the sake of "instituting and perpetuating more enduring and extensive values," not for replacing them.

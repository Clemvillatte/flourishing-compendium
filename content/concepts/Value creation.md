For Nietzsche, value creation is the human act of assigning worth. Zarathustra states it in the chapter on the thousand and one goals:

> "Values did man only assign to things in order to maintain himself—he created only the significance of things, a human significance! Therefore, calleth he himself 'man,' that is, the valuator. Valuing is creating: hear it, ye creating ones! Valuation itself is the treasure and jewel of the valued things. Through valuation only is there value; and without valuation the nut of existence would be hollow." (Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, "The Thousand and One Goals", Common translation, [Project Gutenberg #1998](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1998))

On this passage, worth is not found ready-made in things: it is assigned, and the assigning is the human creative act.

### Creating as relief from suffering

Zarathustra links the creative act to the alleviation of suffering. In the chapter "In the Happy Isles" he says: "Creating, that is the great salvation from suffering, and life's alleviation. But for the creator to appear, suffering itself is needed, and much transformation" (Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, Common translation, [Project Gutenberg #1998](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1998)). The Common translation prints a dash after "Creating"; it is shown here as a comma so as to keep the sentence readable.

In the chapter on the thousand and one goals, the same logic is applied to whole peoples: "Peoples once hung over them tables of the good", and "Change of values" is described as the "change of the creating ones". This is a philosophical claim about how worth comes to exist, and neither passage is offered as psychological evidence.

### What it is not

Value creation is not arbitrary invention. In the passage the creators are those who "endure the pangs of the child-bearer", so the work is costly. It is also not a claim that suffering is good in itself, and the text says only that the creator needs it in order to appear.

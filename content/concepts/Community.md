Community, in the After Virtue sense, is the local form of life in which a person's identity, roles and goods are constituted together with others. Alasdair MacIntyre's defense of it is Aristotelian in shape: the community is intimate and reciprocating, bound by shared ends.

> "In After Virtue, Alasdair MacIntyre defended the Aristotelian ideal of the intimate, reciprocating local community bound by shared ends, where people simply assume and fulfill socially given roles (MacIntyre 1984)." (Daniel Bell, [Communitarianism, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/communitarianism/), section 1 "Universalism Versus Particularism", substantive revision 7 June 2024)

### An objection

Daniel Bell records an objection to this ideal: "But this pre-modern Gemeinschaft conception of an all-encompassing community that members unreflectively endorse seemed distinctly ill-suited for complex and conflict-ridden large-scale industrialized societies" ([Bell, SEP, section 1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/communitarianism/)).

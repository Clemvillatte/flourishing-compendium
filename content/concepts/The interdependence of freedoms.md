The interdependence of freedoms is Simone de Beauvoir's claim, argued across The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947), that a person's freedom cannot be willed or fulfilled alone: it needs the freedom of other people, and willing one's own freedom commits one to willing theirs. The claim is not a slogan of universal benevolence. It follows from her account of what freedom is — the movement of a human being past a given situation toward an open future — and from the fact that the future is kept open by other people's projects, not by one's own.

### Why freedom needs other freedoms

Beauvoir's argument runs through the structure of projects. A project only has meaning in a world that other people have taken up: "One can reveal the world only on a basis revealed by other men. No project can be defined except by its interference with other projects" ([Part II, ch. 1](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch02.htm)). The ends a free person sets can outlast her life only if other freedoms carry them forward, so the wish to be free already contains the wish that others be free.

> "To will oneself free is also to will others free. This will is not an abstract formula. It points out to each person concrete action to be achieved."

(Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part II, ch. 1; [opened text](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch02.htm).)

She had put the same point in her earlier essay Pyrrhus and Cineas: "every man needs the freedom of other men and, in a sense, always wants it, even though he may be a tyrant" ([Part II, ch. 1](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch02.htm)). The tyrant is the person who wants others' freedom as a tool and refuses to assume what that wish demands.

### Freedom is not permission

The claim does not mean that every limit on another person is oppression. In Part III she writes: "I am oppressed if I am thrown into prison, but not if I am kept from throwing my neighbor into prison" ([Part III, ch. 2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm)). Freedom, on her definition, is "not to have the power to do anything you like" but to surpass the given toward an open future, and "the existence of others as a freedom defines my situation and is even the condition of my own freedom" ([Part III, ch. 2](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch03.htm)). Stopping an act of oppression is therefore not a rival freedom but the defense of the shared future both parties need.

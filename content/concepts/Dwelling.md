Heidegger's name, in the lecture "Building Dwelling Thinking", for the human way of being on the earth. His starting point is his own reading of the word for building: on his account, the German bauen belongs with ich bin, so that "I am" already says "I dwell" ([Heidegger, Building Dwelling Thinking, trans. Albert Hofstadter](https://www.contentarchive.wwf.gr/images/pdfs/pe/katoikein/Filosofia_Building%20Dwelling%20Thinking.pdf)):

> "The way in which you are and I am, the manner in which we humans are on the earth, is Buan, dwelling. To be a human being means to be on the earth as a mortal. it means to dwell." (Heidegger, [Building Dwelling Thinking, trans. Albert Hofstadter](https://www.contentarchive.wwf.gr/images/pdfs/pe/katoikein/Filosofia_Building%20Dwelling%20Thinking.pdf))

The lecture draws three things from what language says in the word bauen:

> "1. Building is really dwelling. 2. Dwelling is the manner in which mortals are on the earth. 3. Building as dwelling unfolds into the building that cultivates growing things and the building that erects buildings." (Heidegger, [ibid.](https://www.contentarchive.wwf.gr/images/pdfs/pe/katoikein/Filosofia_Building%20Dwelling%20Thinking.pdf))

### What dwelling does

Dwelling is a manner of existence, and its fundamental character Heidegger states directly:

> "The fundamental character of dwelling is this sparing and preserving. It pervades dwelling in its whole range." (Heidegger, [ibid.](https://www.contentarchive.wwf.gr/images/pdfs/pe/katoikein/Filosofia_Building%20Dwelling%20Thinking.pdf))

The order of building and dwelling is the reverse of the usual one: "The nature of building is letting dwell... Only if we are capable of dwelling, only then can we build" ([Heidegger, ibid.](https://www.contentarchive.wwf.gr/images/pdfs/pe/katoikein/Filosofia_Building%20Dwelling%20Thinking.pdf)).

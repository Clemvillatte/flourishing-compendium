**Protection motivation theory** — Rogers — protective behaviour follows from perceived threat severity and vulnerability combined with response efficacy.

Protection motivation theory (PMT) is Ronald Rogers's account of how fear appeals and threat information translate, or fail to translate, into protective behaviour. A food-safety application states the structure:

> "PMT posits that two underlying processes, threat appraisal and coping appraisal, underlie peoples' adoption of protective behaviors when faced with a threat or hazard." (Zhu et al., [Consumers' Food Safety Risk Communication on Social Media, IJERPH 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8345550/))

### The two appraisals

The same article describes each process: "Threat appraisal is a process of estimating the severity and vulnerability of a threat, while coping appraisal refers to the process of evaluating the response efficacy and self-efficacy of the individual who is exposed to the threat". Coping appraisal further includes response barriers, which "emphasize the potential costs of coping mechanisms such as time, effort, money, etc.". The seed line above compresses the point into severity plus vulnerability plus efficacy.

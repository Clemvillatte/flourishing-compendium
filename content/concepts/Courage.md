Courage (andreia) is one of the four cardinal virtues: acting well in the face of fear. [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]] defines it precisely: "courage is a mean with respect to things that inspire confidence or fear, in the circumstances that have been stated; and it chooses or endures things because it is noble to do so, or because it is base not to do so" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.6-9, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)). The brave person, in his summary, "feels and acts according to the merits of the case and in whatever way the rule directs" ([Nicomachean Ethics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### What courage is not

Aristotle's sharpest teaching is negative: he distinguishes courage from several states that resemble it. The citizen-soldier who fears disgrace more than death comes closest to the real thing, "for it is due to shame and to desire of a noble object"; those who fight from passion are "pugnacious but not brave; for they do not act for honour's sake nor as the rule directs" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.6-9](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)). The mark of the real thing is that it is chosen "because it is noble to do so" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.6-9](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### The clinical pairing

The clinical layer pairs with the virtue as an editorial comparison: BE026 on approaching what one fears, AF035 on avoidance's negative reinforcement, BE025 on avoidance as anxiety's core maintaining factor, and the companion concept Graded exposure (Wolpe) for approaching feared situations step by step. Aristotle's own training account, as the IEP summarizes it, is habituation: "by acting in the face of frightening things and being habituated to be fearful or confident, some become brave and others cowardly" ([IEP, Aristotle's Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/aris-eth/)).

### Here

SP065's acting from strength rather than fear is a modern echo of Aristotle's noble motive. S031's doing what is fitting, not what is easy, and S041's duty even when it is unpleasant, are my Stoic pairings. E503 pairs courage's two faces: face simultaneously your greatest suffering and your highest hope. S063 adds the Stoic limit: do not fear what you cannot control.

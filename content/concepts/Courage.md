Courage (andreia) is one of the four cardinal virtues: acting well in the face of fear. Aristotle defines it precisely: "courage is a mean with respect to things that inspire confidence or fear, in the circumstances that have been stated; and it chooses or endures things because it is noble to do so, or because it is base not to do so" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.6-9, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)). The brave person "faces and who fears the right things and from the right motive, in the right way and from the right time," feeling confidence under the corresponding conditions ([Nicomachean Ethics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### What courage is not

Aristotle's sharpest teaching is negative: five counterfeits look like courage and are not. The citizen-soldier who fears disgrace more than death comes closest, "because it is due to shame and to desire of a noble object"; the professional soldier's experience, passion, sanguine optimism and plain ignorance of the danger come lower. None of these is brave "for honour's sake... as the rule directs" — the mark of the real thing is that it is chosen "because it is noble to do so" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.6-9](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### The clinical cousin

The bridge between the ethics and the clinic is habituation. As the IEP's account of Aristotle puts it, "by acting in the face of frightening things and being habituated to be fearful or confident, some become brave and others cowardly" ([IEP, Aristotle's Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/aris-eth/)). The compendium's clinical layer gives the same structure in modern terms: BE026 on approaching what one fears, AF035 on avoidance's negative reinforcement, BE025 on avoidance as anxiety's core maintaining factor. Graded exposure is the training table; the virtue is the disposition it can build.

### In the compendium

SP065's acting from strength rather than fear is the modern echo of Aristotle's noble motive. S031's doing what is fitting, not what is easy, and S041's duty even when it is unpleasant, carry the same structure. E503 pairs courage's two faces: face simultaneously your greatest suffering and your highest hope. S063 adds the Stoic limit: do not fear what you cannot control.

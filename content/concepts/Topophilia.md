“Topophilia” — Yi-Fu Tuan, as modern analysis.

Topophilia is the love of place: the affective tie that binds a person to a landscape, a house, a city block or a stretch of coast. The geographer Yi-Fu Tuan introduced the term into geography in a 1961 essay and made it the spine of his 1974 book, where it covers the whole range of human feeling for the material environment, from a fleeting pleasure in a view to the deep attachment to home as the locus of memories and livelihood.

> "Tuan extended his original 1961 essay in the book Topophilia (1974) to incorporate a much broader range of emotions and experiences between people and place, defining topophilia as ' ... the affective bond between people and place or setting.'" (Dean Sinclair, ["Tuan's Topophilia: The Making of a Citation Classic"](https://digitalcommons.kennesaw.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1550&context=thegeographicalbulletin), The Geographical Bulletin, 1995, quoting Tuan 1974)

### Why a writer on flourishing would care

Attachment to place supplies continuity, memory and a daily sensory friendship with one's surroundings. The compendium's principle M022 turns the concept into practice: root yourself in a place, and treat the rooting as an achievement worth protecting when life allows it.

“Harmony” — Confucius, The Analects; Plato, Republic.

Three classical traditions converge on this word, and each makes a different instrument of it. Heraclitus draws on the lyre: attunement as the product of opposing pulls. Confucius draws on social relations: ease toward others without flattering them. Plato draws on music theory: harmony as the correct ordering of parts within a whole.

### Heraclitus: attunement out of opposition

Fragment B8: "Opposition brings together, and from differing tones comes the most beautiful attunement, [and all things come about according to strife]." And B51: "They do not understand how one thing differing from itself agrees with itself; it is a conjunction turning back on itself, like that of the bow and the lyre" ([Sassi, "How Musical was Heraclitus' Harmony? A reassessment of 22 B 8, 10, 51 DK", Rhizomata 2015](https://arpi.unipi.it/bitstream/11568/751024/2/Heraclitus'Harmony.%20rhiz-2015-0002.pdf); B51 after Hippolytus, Refutation IX 9). The bow works because the string pulls against the wood; the lyre sings because opposing tensions hold.

### Confucius: affability without flattery

Analects XIII.23 gives the term its social sense:

> "The superior man is affable, but not adulatory; the mean man is adulatory, but not affable." (Confucius, Analects XIII.23, Legge translation, [Standard Ebooks](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/confucius/analects/james-legge/text/chapter-13))

The passage contrasts two ways of being with others: ease without flattery on one side, flattery without ease on the other. The compendium's principle on practising harmony (he) carries the Chinese term behind the contrast.

### Plato: the soul as attunement

In the Republic, justice turns out to be an internal arrangement. Socrates concludes that the just man has "harmonized these three principles, the notes or intervals of three terms quite literally the lowest, the highest, and the mean" ([Plato, Republic IV.443d, Shorey translation, Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0168%3Abook%3D4%3Asection%3D443d)).

### Why the three travel together

All three uses share a core: harmony requires parts that differ. There is no attunement without differing tones, no affability without distinct positions, no ordered soul without separate faculties. A life in harmony, on this entry's reading, is a life in which the tensions sound together.

Reflective practice is "the ability to reflect on one's thoughts, emotions, actions, decisions, assumptions, and professional experiences so as to take a critical stance or attitude towards improving one's own practice, understanding, judgment, and future actions, while challenging automatic reactions and assumptions and engaging in a process of continuous adaptation and learning" ([Wikipedia, Reflective practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_practice)). Donald Schön's 1983 book The Reflective Practitioner introduced the pair of terms the field still uses. A clinical-education review states the distinction compactly: "These two distinct types of reflection have been referred to as reflection-on-action, in which individuals think back on past events, and reflection-in-action, in which individuals are aware of and process events as they happen" ([Larsen, London and Emke, Perspectives on Medical Education 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5035279/)).

### Models of reflection

Several of the standard models predate Schön's book. Borton's 1970 cycle compressed reflection into three questions: What, So what, and Now what. Kolb and Fry's experiential learning cycle (1975) gave the same movement a four-stage form. Argyris and Schön's 1978 theory of action added the rungs that matter for practice: single-loop learning corrects the error and keeps the strategy, while double-loop learning "involves the modification of objectives, strategies or policies so that when a similar situation arises a new framing system is employed" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_practice)). Gibbs's 1988 structured debriefing turns the cycle into questions a professional can run after any episode: description, feelings, evaluation, analysis, conclusion, action plan.

### What reflection is worth

The Wikipedia article carries the limit that matters for anyone tempted to treat journaling as a cure-all: "Although reflection alone is not fully reliable, because insight, introspection, and self-appraisal can be distorted by cognitive biases, reflective practice can still be a major source of professional development when combined with critical analysis, feedback, and evidence-based knowledge" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_practice)).

> "Reflection is an important human activity in which people recapture their experience, think about it, mull it over and evaluate it. It is this working with experience that is important in learning." (Boud and colleagues, quoted in [Wikipedia, Reflective practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_practice))

The same page notes that some scholars have claimed precursors of reflective practice in ancient texts such as Buddhist teachings and the Meditations of Marcus Aurelius ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflective_practice)).

In Heidegger's reading of Hölderlin's elegy, homecoming is not arriving at an address. It is a return into nearness to an origin, and the homeland is defined by that nearness rather than by geography:

> "The homeland itself dwells near. It is the point of proximity to the hearth and source." (Heidegger, "Remembrance of the Poet," in Existence and Being, 1949, p. 277, [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/download/existenceandbeingheidegger/Existence-And-Being%20Heidegger_djvu.txt))

> "Homecoming is the return into the proximity of the source." (Heidegger, "Remembrance of the Poet," in Existence and Being, 1949, p. 278, [Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/download/existenceandbeingheidegger/Existence-And-Being%20Heidegger_djvu.txt))

Heidegger adds that the return is only possible for one who has first wandered and carried the burden of the voyage, because only such a wanderer could go over into the source and experience there what the nature of the sought-for might be, then come back more experienced. Proximity to the source, he writes, "is a mystery," and the return home may consist chiefly, and perhaps for a long time, in getting to know it; we never get to know a mystery by unveiling or analysing it, only by carefully guarding it as mystery. For the sake of that knowledge there must always be another who comes home for the first time and tells of it.

**Plain words** Homecoming, for Heidegger, is not the trip back but the standing near what you came from. The wandering beforehand is not wasted; it is what makes the return a return.

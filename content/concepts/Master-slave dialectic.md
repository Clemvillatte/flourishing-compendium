The master-slave dialectic (lordship and bondage) is Hegel's analysis of recognition in the Phenomenology of Spirit: self-consciousness exists only by being acknowledged by another self-consciousness, and the relation that develops between two who meet this requirement is the master-slave structure. The original entry's claim, that the master's position secretly depends on the slave, is the argument's payoff: the master can consume what the slave produces only because the slave has worked on it, and the master's certainty about his own independence rests on a recognition he cannot supply himself.

The paper's statement of the key contrast, verbatim from the Baillie translation:

> Labour, on the other hand, is desire restrained and checked, evanescence delayed and postponed; in other words, labour shapes and fashions the thing.

(Hegel, Phenomenology of Spirit, "Lordship and Bondage," §195, Baillie translation, [Marxists.org](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/ph/phba.htm))

The same section's argument for why work, not consumption, is where self-consciousness comes to itself: through his work the bondsman cancels his dependence on natural existence, and "through this re-discovery of himself by himself" becomes aware of "having and being a 'mind of his own'". The master, who merely consumes, gets back only the evanescence desire always gets; the bondsman, who shapes, sees his own form standing there.

The compendium's [[D068 — Yield in conflict to remain whole]] reads the dialectic against the grain: the one who will not win the struggle is the one who ends up free, and yielding is not capitulation but the smarter term of the recognition bargain.

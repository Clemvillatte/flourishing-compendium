“Lokasangraha, the welfare of the world” — BG 3.20.

Lokasangraha is the Bhagavad Gita's term for the reason a free person still acts: for the upholding of the world. The concept's verse, in Arnold's rendering:

> By works alone
> Janak and ancient saints reached blessedness!
> Moreover, for the upholding of thy kind,
> Action thou should'st embrace.
> ([Bhagavad Gita, ch. III, tr. Edwin Arnold, The Song Celestial](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2388/2388-h/2388-h.htm))

### Why the emancipated act

The chapter's logic is that even one who needs nothing remains bound to the example. Krishna's own stated reason for acting, a few verses later: "And I should break earth's order and commit / Her offspring unto ruin, Bharata!" And the closing statement of the motive:

> Even as the unknowing toil, wedded to sense,
> So let the enlightened toil, sense-freed, but set
> To bring the world deliverance, and its bliss
> Not sowing in those simple, busy hearts
> Seed of despair.
> ([Bhagavad Gita, ch. III, Arnold](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/2388/2388-h/2388-h.htm))

The enlightened act as the unenlightened do, with the same work and a different aim. Welfare of the world is thus not charity added to duty; it is what makes duty worth doing for someone who has nothing left to gain.

My principle on acting for the world's welfare and the principle on taking the vast motivation as the frame of daily life both grow from this root.

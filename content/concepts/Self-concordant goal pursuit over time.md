Self-concordant goal pursuit over time is the sequence described in Sheldon and Elliot's self-concordance model: goals that fit a person's developing interests and core values are pursued with more sustained effort, are more likely to be reached, and bring more well-being when they are reached. The abstract of the 1999 paper puts it this way:

> "The self-concordance of goals (i.e., their consistency with the person's developing interests and core values) plays a dual role in the model. First, those pursuing self-concordant goals put more sustained effort into achieving those goals and thus are more likely to attain them. Second, those who attain self-concordant goals reap greater well-being benefits from their attainment." (Sheldon and Elliot, 1999, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 76(3):482-497, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101878/))

### The mechanism

The abstract says the link from attainment to well-being is "mediated by need satisfaction, i.e., daily activity-based experiences of autonomy, competence, and relatedness that accumulate during the period of striving." The model fitted 3 longitudinal data sets and was "independent of the effects of self-efficacy, implementation intentions, avoidance framing, and life skills" ([Sheldon and Elliot, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101878/)). The time element is therefore twofold: effort over the striving period, and need-satisfying daily experience that accumulates before and after the goal is reached.

### Later work

Sheldon's 2014 review in Personality and Social Psychology Review notes that people "are typically unaware of their own implicit motivations and potentials, they may pick goals that do not serve them well," so that "self-concordant" goal selection "is a difficult self-perceptual skill." It reports that goal self-concordance "predicts more persistent goal effort and, thus, better goal attainment over time," and that selection is helped by personality variables and interpersonal contexts that promote "accurate self-insight and personal autonomy" ([Sheldon, 2014, Pers Soc Psychol Rev](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24981515/)). A cross-cultural study by Sheldon and colleagues (2004) opens by describing self-concordant individuals as those who "pursue life goals with a sense that they express their authentic choices rather than with a sense that they are controlled by external forces" ([Sheldon et al., 2004](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2004_SheldonElliotRyanChirkovetal.pdf)). I read only the opening of that paper.

### What is and is not shown

The sources opened here support the effort, attainment and need-satisfaction path. They do not show that self-concordant goals are good in themselves: the 2014 abstract says people may misjudge their own motives, so feeling that a goal is one's own is not proof that it fits. Only the 1999 and 2014 abstracts were read, not the full papers.

"Transaction" as the compendium uses it comes from John Dewey and Arthur F. Bentley's Knowing and the Known (1949), where it names the third and most developed of three ways of presenting inquiry. The book's preface defines the transactional viewpoint as:

> "willing under hypothesis to treat all [human] behavings, including [their] most advanced knowings, as activities not of [them]self alone, nor even as primarily [theirs], but as processes of the full situation of organism-environment." (Dewey & Bentley, Knowing and the Known, 1949, p. vi, quoted in [Wikipedia, "Transactionalism"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactionalism))

The earlier levels are self-action, "where things [and thereby people] are viewed as acting on their own powers", and interaction, where thing is balanced against thing as in Newton's third law (Dewey & Bentley 1949, pp. vi, 120, quoted in the same article). What interaction still assumes, and transaction drops, is that the terms entering the relation are already fixed and separately known before the inquiry begins. The same preface puts the replacement directly:

> "Instead, observer and observed are held in close organization. Nor is there any radical separation between that which is named and the naming." (Dewey & Bentley, Knowing and the Known, 1949, p. vi, quoted in [Wikipedia, "Transactionalism"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactionalism))

The word carries its everyday sense of a deal between two parties, but the hyphenated form "trans-action" was chosen to stress that the action runs across the whole situation rather than between two ready-made units. A recent curriculum-theory article states the contrast: whereas interaction presupposes relatively independent entities that act upon one another, in transaction "the terms involved are defined and redefined within the ongoing course of inquiry" (Park, "From powerful knowledge to learners' knowledge", Journal of Curriculum Studies, 2026, on Dewey & Bentley 1949, pp. 101-108). The book's own summary of what the transactional view claims for itself:

> "the right to see together, extensionally and durationally, much that is talked about conventionally as if it were composed of irreconcilable separates." (Dewey & Bentley, Knowing and the Known, 1949, p. 120, quoted in [Wikipedia, "Transactionalism"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transactionalism))

For the compendium's principles, the concept does one job: it removes the seams. A person is not a sealed unit that meets an outside world at the skin; living is a single process of organism and environment, and the environment is only this environment for this organism. The linked principles use it that way — the organism's doings and the setting's responses are one situation, feelings are information about that situation, and cognition runs out into the tools and arrangements it uses. The seed's original attribution to Dewey's Experience and Nature was a carrier mismatch; the term belongs to the late Dewey of Knowing and the Known.

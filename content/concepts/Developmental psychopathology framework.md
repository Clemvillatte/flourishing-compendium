The developmental psychopathology framework, built by Dante Cicchetti and colleagues, studies normal and abnormal development together, treating disorder and health as outcomes of a developmental process rather than conditions a person simply has. Sheree Toth and Cicchetti's article on child maltreatment states what marks the field out:

> "The essence of developmental psychopathology, and what distinguishes it from either clinical or developmental psychology, involves its focus on both normal and abnormal developmental processes." (Toth & Cicchetti 2013, [PMC4520222](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4520222/))

Two of its working principles are equifinality and multifinality: "similar adverse experiences can result in diverse outcomes (multifinality) and... different types of adversity may eventuate in similar outcomes (equifinality)". Risk, on this view, is probabilistic, and the same framework covers resilience: its investigators "are as invested in understanding pathways to competent outcomes despite adversity as they are in addressing maladaptive trajectories", and equally in why some people who have "diverged onto a deviant pathway are able to resume positive functioning".

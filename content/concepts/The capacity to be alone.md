The capacity to be alone is Donald Winnicott's 1958 name for an achievement most people would never think to call one: being alone, contentedly, as a self. His paper opens with the programmatic sentence: "I wish to make an examination of the capacity of the individual to be alone, acting on the assumption that this capacity is one of the most important signs of maturity in emotional development" ([Winnicott, The Capacity to be Alone, International Journal of Psycho-Analysis 1958](https://ia800407.us.archive.org/10/items/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone_text.pdf)). The clinical observation behind it: in treatment there come silent phases, and "this silence, far from being evidence of resistance, turns out to be an achievement on the part of the patient" ([Winnicott 1958](https://ia800407.us.archive.org/10/items/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone_text.pdf)).

### The paradox at the base

The theory's strangest and most durable claim is that this capacity is built from company. The basis of the capacity is a paradox: the experience of being alone while someone else is present. The infant who plays contentedly while the mother is reliably there is doing the developmental work; the internalized reliable other is what lets the adult later rest alone. "It is only when alone (that is to say, in the presence of someone) that the infant can discover his own personal life" ([Winnicott 1958](https://ia800407.us.archive.org/10/items/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone_text.pdf)). The developmental ladder runs through language: "'I am alone' is a development from 'I am', dependent on the infant's awareness of the continued existence of a reliable mother whose reliability makes it possible for the infant to be alone and to enjoy being alone, for a limited period" ([Winnicott 1958](https://ia800407.us.archive.org/10/items/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone_text.pdf)).

The mature version of the capacity rests on the internal object: it "depends on the existence of a good object in the psychic reality of the individual," with internal relationships "well enough set up and defended for the individual (at any rate for the time being) to feel confident about the present and the future" ([Winnicott 1958](https://ia800407.us.archive.org/10/items/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone/winnicott-capacity-to-be-alone_text.pdf)). What looks like needing no one is, on this account, the residue of having had someone.

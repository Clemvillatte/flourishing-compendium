“The Between” — Buber, I and Thou.

"The between" (das Zwischen) is Martin Buber's name for the space of real encounter between one person and another. The seed cites I and Thou (1923); Between Man and Man is the later dialogical parallel. Neither book was opened for this page, so Buber's own wording is not quoted. The account rests on the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Buber (Zank and Braiterman).

### The idea

The entry describes I and Thou as expressing "a dual structure of human relations that permeates our daily lives and confronts us with a fundamental choice in every situation: to reduce what is before us to a mere 'it' reflecting back on ourselves as equally reduced, or being open to an encounter of the other as a Thou". ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Buber](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/))

The weight falls on neither term.

> "The emphasis is not on the I or on the Thou as such – to think those as isolated concepts or ideas immediately incarcerates them in the I-It relation – but on the 'between.'" (Stanford Encyclopedia, section 3)

The entry adds that the presence revealed in an encounter "occupies the space 'in between' the subject and an other", and that this in-between "is either 'mutual' (gegenseitig), or it remains pre-dialogic". Buber's relations with nature are called pre-dialogic, because they are not reciprocal. Between people the relation involves "reciprocity but not identity".

### Distance as a condition

The entry notes a point from Buber's later work: setting persons "at a distance is the only way to secure the form of otherness without which there can be no relation". The between is therefore not a merging of selves. It depends on difference.

### Criticism and limits

The entry reports that Buber insisted the dialogic principle was "not an abstract conception but an ontological reality that he pointed to but that could not be properly represented in discursive prose". It also records objections to his reduction of relations to two kinds, I-Thou and I-It: Rosenzweig complained, "In your setting up the I-IT, you give the I-Thou a cripple for an opponent." The compendium's description of the between as "ontological" follows Buber's own claim, which is a philosophical position, not an empirical finding.

### What this page does not claim

The entry does not discuss the Pacific notions of vā that the compendium's principles link to this concept, and no source here supports an identity between Buber's between and those ideas. Treat the link as an analogy made by the compendium.

Pu (朴) is Laozi's image for original simplicity: the uncarved block of wood before it is shaped into anything specific. It is not innocence or ignorance — the block is not empty — but the state of a person before roles, credentials, opinions and performances are carved onto them. The Daodejing uses pu repeatedly (chapters 15, 19, 28, 32, 37): chapter 32 says of it that "the uncarved block, though small, none under heaven can make its subject" (樸雖小，天下莫能臣也); chapter 37 calls it the "nameless uncarved block" by which desires are stilled; chapter 19 pairs it with the plain — "see the plain, embrace the uncarved block" (見素抱樸). The prescription: the sage holds the whole rather than being carved by a part.

The psychological reading: beneath conditioning there is an original, uncut simplicity that is not damaged by experience, only buried. The practice is to strip back the performance and act from that unpolished core — which is sturdier than any persona because it is not maintaining an image. This is the point where pu connects to ziran (naturalness) and wu wei (non-forcing): the uncarved block is what acts when nothing is forced.

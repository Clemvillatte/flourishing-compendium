Burnout is the WHO's name for a syndrome resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. The ICD-11 definition: "Burn-out is a syndrome conceptualized as resulting from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. It is characterized by three dimensions: 1) feelings of energy depletion or exhaustion; 2) increased mental distance from one's job, or feelings of negativism or cynicism related to one's job; and 3) reduced professional efficacy," and it "refers specifically to phenomena in the occupational context" ([Wikipedia, Occupational burnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_(psychology))). A much older and looser analogue, sometimes compared to burnout, is acedia, the noonday demon of the desert monastics.

### The three dimensions

The syndrome's shape comes from Christina Maslach's research: emotional exhaustion, "depersonalization (feeling low-empathy towards other people in an occupational setting)," and "reduced feelings of work-related accomplishment" ([Wikipedia, Occupational burnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_(psychology))). The Maslach Burnout Inventory (1981) operationalises burnout as exactly this "three-dimensional syndrome consisting of emotional exhaustion, depersonalization (an unfeeling and impersonal response toward recipients of one's service, care, treatment, or instruction), and reduced personal accomplishment" ([Wikipedia, Occupational burnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_(psychology))).

### Where it comes from

Maslach and Leiter locate the causes in the person-job mismatch rather than the person alone: burnout connects to six areas of work life, "workload, control, reward, community, fairness, and values," so prevention "requires a combination of organizational change and worker education" ([Wikipedia, Occupational burnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_(psychology))). The demands-resources model tells the same story from the workload side: demands drive exhaustion, missing resources drive disengagement ([Wikipedia, Occupational burnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_(psychology))).

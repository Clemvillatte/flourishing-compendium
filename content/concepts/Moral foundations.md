## Facets

- **and authority** — Haidt, 2012

Moral Foundations Theory started from a puzzle: why are moral judgments so similar across cultures, yet sometimes so variable? Jesse Graham, Jonathan Haidt and colleagues built a pluralist answer, and settled on a list ([Graham et al., 2013](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf)):

> Haidt and Joseph identified five best candidates: Care/harm, Fairness/cheating, Loyalty/betrayal, Authority/subversion, and Sanctity/degradation.

The list was never meant to be closed. The chapter adds: "We believe that there are more than five; for example, Haidt (2012) has suggested that Liberty/oppression should be considered a sixth foundation" ([Graham et al., 2013](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf)). The names also shifted over the theory's life — "Prior to 2012, we used slightly different terms: Harm/care, Fairness/reciprocity, Ingroup/loyalty, Authority/respect, and Purity/sanctity" ([Graham et al., 2013](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf)). So a seed line saying "harm and fairness as primary intuitions (Haidt, 2012)" is reading the newest naming, where the older literature used Harm/care and Fairness/reciprocity.

### Intuitions first

The theory sits on the social intuitionist insight that "Intuitionism: Intuitions come first, strategic reasoning second" ([Graham et al., 2013](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf)). A moral intuition, on their definition, is:

> "the sudden appearance in consciousness, or at the fringe of consciousness, of an evaluative feeling (like–dislike, good–bad) about the character or actions of a person, without any conscious awareness of having gone through steps of search, weighing evidence, or inferring a conclusion" ([Graham et al., 2013](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf)). Reasoning still happens, but mostly to explain and defend reactions already formed.

### The political pattern

The five foundations were partly built to explain moral disagreement across the political spectrum. The chapter's summary of the evidence: "In fact, care and fairness are valued highly across the political spectrum, with liberals on average endorsing them slightly more than conservatives. Loyalty, Authority, and Sanctity, in contrast, show a clear linear increase in importance moving from extreme liberals to extreme conservatives" ([Graham et al., 2013](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.uci.edu/dist/1/863/files/2020/06/Graham-et-al-2013.AESP_.pdf)). The seed's framing — harm and fairness as the primary intuitions — describes the two foundations that both sides lean on; the rest of the list is where the divergence lives.

**Co-rumination theory Rose** — excessively discussing problems within close friendships deepens both intimacy and depressive symptoms.

Co-rumination is Amanda Rose's name for a way friends talk that feels like support and works like a trap. Two people go over a problem again and again, speculate about it, and dwell on how bad it feels, and the talking itself draws them closer while it deepens the bad mood. Rose's research group, summarizing the construct she introduced in 2002, defines it like this:

> "Co-rumination is a recently developed construct and is defined as 'excessively discussing personal problems within a dyadic relationship' (Rose, 2002)."

> "Co-rumination, or excessively discussing problems, is characterized by mutual encouragement of problem talk, rehashing problems, speculating about problems, and dwelling on negative affect (Rose, 2002)." ([Rose, Carlson and Waller 2007, Developmental Psychology 43(4):1019-1031, APA](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/dev-4341019.pdf))

The theory's interest lies in its double character. The authors place it deliberately between two things that each look innocent on its own:

> "We think of co-rumination as lying at the intersection of self-disclosure and rumination."

> "However, whereas self-disclosure can involve any personal topic and can be brief, co-rumination involves an excessive focus on problems and concerns."

> "However, whereas rumination is a cognitive, solitary process, co-rumination is a social, conversational process." (same paper)

The longitudinal evidence bears out both sides of the trade. For girls, co-rumination predicted more depressive and anxiety symptoms and at the same time more positive friendship quality, each feeding the other in a loop:

> "For girls, a reciprocal relationship was found in which co-rumination predicted increased depressive and anxiety symptoms and increased positive friendship quality over time, which, in turn, contributed to greater co-rumination." (same paper)

My principle [[AF074 — Recognise co-rumination — sharing distress can amplify it]] draws the practical warning: when a conversation about a problem circles without producing a next step, the closeness it creates is real, and so is the cost.

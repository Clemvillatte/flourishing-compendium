“Self-overcoming” — Nietzsche, Thus Spoke Zarathustra.

## What it is

The chapter of Zarathustra that gives this concept its text is titled "Self-Surpassing" in Thomas Common's translation ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). In it, Zarathustra reports following living things and listening to them ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). All living things are obeying things, he reports ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). Whatever cannot obey itself is commanded ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). And when a living thing commands even itself, it must atone for its commanding, and it becomes the judge and avenger and victim of its own law ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)).

Then Life herself speaks the secret:

> And this secret spake Life herself unto me. “Behold,” said she, “I am that WHICH MUST EVER SURPASS ITSELF. ... Whatever I create, and however much I love it,—soon must I be adverse to it, and to my love: so willeth my will.
>
> — Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, "Self-Surpassing," trans. Thomas Common (capitals in the translation) ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm))

Zarathustra reports that wherever he found a living thing, there he found Will to Power ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). He teaches that only where there is life is there will, and he calls that will Will to Power ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). Life, in her speech, says that whatever she creates, and however much she loves it, she must soon be adverse to it, and to her love ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)).

Zarathustra says that good and evil which would be everlasting do not exist, and must of their own accord surpass themselves anew ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)). He says that whoever has to be a creator in good and evil must first be a destroyer, and break values in pieces ([Zarathustra, trans. Common](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)).

## In the compendium

The principle [E103](../principles/E103%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20self-overcoming%20%28Selbst%C3%BCberwindung%29.md) takes this chapter as its text and quotes the same passages. Its caution is worth carrying over. In the chapter, surpassing is described as something life does, with cost. The self that commands itself must atone for it. The principle reads the doctrine descriptively before prescriptively, and resists softening it into a program of self-improvement.

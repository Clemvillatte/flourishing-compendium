**Reinforcement-Affect Theory** — Byrne, Clore — we come to like stimuli and people associated with positive affect; evaluative conditioning forms attitudes.

Reinforcement-affect theory is Donn Byrne and Gerald Clore's learning-theory account of liking and attraction. A later retrospective by Clore and Schwarz states its key claim:

> "However, the reinforcement–affect model maintained that the key ingredient is the subjective experience of reward and that merely associating positive feelings with others is sufficient to generate liking for them, regardless of whether they are actually the source of the affect." (Schwarz & Clore, [Mood as Information: 20 Years Later](https://dornsife.usc.edu/norbert-schwarz/wp-content/uploads/sites/231/2023/11/03_pi_schwarz___clore_mood.pdf))

### Transfer of feeling

The model's origin is described in the same retrospective: Clore's "work with Donn Byrne had documented profound emotional influences on interpersonal attraction, which were couched in learning theory terms in their reinforcement–affect model". On the model, a person associated with rewarding stimuli becomes liked through that association, and a person associated with punishing stimuli becomes disliked.

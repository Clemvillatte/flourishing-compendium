“Constructivist approach to meaning” — Kelly, 1955; Neimeyer and Raskin, 2001.

The constructivist approach to meaning is George Kelly's idea, elaborated by later constructivist psychotherapists such as Robert Neimeyer, that meaning is not found lying in events but built out of them, and that the building can be done differently. Kelly's psychology of personal constructs rests on one sentence, his fundamental postulate:

> "A person's processes are psychologically channelized by the ways in which he anticipates events." ([Kelly, The Psychology of Personal Constructs (1955), open excerpt](https://www.infoamerica.org/documentos_pdf/kelly02.pdf))

The building blocks are constructs, patterns of likeness and difference a person uses to anticipate what events mean and what will come next:

> "A person anticipates events by construing their replications." (same text)

What makes the approach constructivist rather than merely cognitive is the standing possibility of rebuilding. Kelly's term for this is constructive alternativism, and the text states its consequence plainly:

> "But it does remind us that all our present perceptions are open to question and reconsideration, and it does broadly suggest that even the most obvious occurrences of everyday life might appear utterly transformed if we were inventive enough to construe them differently." (same text)

The system keeps contact with the world through prediction, and it stays honest through revision:

> "In all of this we look to events to confirm our predictions and to encourage our venturesome constructions."

> "Disconfirmation, even in those cases where it is disconcerting, provides grounds for reconstruction—or of repentance, in the proper sense of that term—and it may be used to improve the accuracy and significance of further anticipations." (same text)

Meaning, on this account, is a working structure: it guides anticipation, it gets tested, and it can be rebuilt. The view matters for flourishing because it makes despair interpretable as a stuck construction rather than a final fact. The compendium's principle [[E010 — Practise the constitution of meaning]] draws on exactly this, and the neighbors [[Fusion of horizons]] and [[Learning by doing]] carry constructivist insight on the philosophical and the practical side.

Contingencies of self-worth are the domains on which a person has staked their self-esteem. Jennifer Crocker and Connie Wolfe's model holds that global self-esteem is informed only by self-evaluations in those contingent domains, so successes and failures there generalize to one's felt worth as a person.

> "A contingency of self-worth is a domain or category of outcomes on which a person has staked his or her self-esteem, so that person's view of his or her value or worth depends on perceived successes or failures or adherence to self-standards in that domain." (Crocker and Wolfe, [Contingencies of Self-Worth](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6a760291307e26027c8c5a39/t/6a7a6dfef31cab43f5369c87/1786408447478/Crocker&Wolfe_2001_PsychRev.pdf), Psychological Review 108(3):593-623, 2001, p. 594)

### The domains and the fragility

The Contingencies of Self-Worth Scale measures seven of them: "Factor analysis on the items using varimax rotation yields the predicted seven interpretable and theoretically meaningful factors: approval, appearance, God's love, family support, school competency, competition, and virtue, all of which had high internal consistency" ([p. 599](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6a760291307e26027c8c5a39/t/6a7a6dfef31cab43f5369c87/1786408447478/Crocker&Wolfe_2001_PsychRev.pdf)). The paper's claim about them is qualified: "Recent evidence suggests that contingencies of self-worth, and the resultant fragility or instability of self-esteem, may be more important aspects for understanding the link between self-esteem and behavior" ([p. 594](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6a760291307e26027c8c5a39/t/6a7a6dfef31cab43f5369c87/1786408447478/Crocker&Wolfe_2001_PsychRev.pdf)). On outcomes, the paper reports that "Contingent self-esteem is also associated with anxiety, hostility, defensiveness, and the risk of depression" ([p. 614](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6a760291307e26027c8c5a39/t/6a7a6dfef31cab43f5369c87/1786408447478/Crocker&Wolfe_2001_PsychRev.pdf)) and that "contingencies of self-worth in concert with negative life events may be a significant risk factor for depression" ([p. 611](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6a760291307e26027c8c5a39/t/6a7a6dfef31cab43f5369c87/1786408447478/Crocker&Wolfe_2001_PsychRev.pdf)).

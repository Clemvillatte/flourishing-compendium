Conservation of Resources (COR) theory is Stevan E. Hobfoll's 1989 model of stress, presented as an alternative to the phenomenological stress models the paper criticises. The founding paper's abstract states the model:

> "This resource-oriented model is based on the supposition that people strive to retain, project, and build resources and that what is threatening to them is the potential or actual loss of these valued resources." (Hobfoll, 1989, abstract, [PubMed record, PMID 2648906](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2648906/))

### Origin

Hobfoll published the model in American Psychologist in 1989 as "a new attempt at conceptualizing stress". The paper's stated target was the state of stress research: the abstract charges that existing conceptualizations were "too phenomenological and ambiguous, and consequently, not given to direct empirical testing" (same record).

### Core claims

The theory's central claim, as the abstract puts it, is that threat is about resources:

> "what is threatening to them is the potential or actual loss of these valued resources" (Hobfoll, 1989, abstract, [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2648906/))

A handbook summary of the theory states its best-known asymmetries: "loss is more salient than gain, and loss begets loss" (Holmgreen, Conservation of Resources Theory, in The Handbook of Stress and Health, [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/9781118993811.ch27), chapter abstract).

### How the theory explains

A resource theory explains by accounting: on the model, a stressor is anything that threatens or takes away what a person values, and the abstract lists the threat, the actual loss, and the investment as the occasions of stress. The same handbook summary adds that the theory treats "the desire to defend, conserve, and acquire these valued resources" as what motivates behaviour in the face of stress (same chapter, abstract).

### In everyday life

A person juggling a new baby, a sick parent and a work deadline illustrates the kind of situation the model addresses: several things the person values (sleep, support from others, time) are threatened at once. The model's reading of such a situation would be in terms of threatened resources rather than a single stressor. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

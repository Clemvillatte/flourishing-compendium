Goal conflict research — what happens when a person's own goals work against each other (Emmons and King, 1988).

The classic study is Emmons and King's 1988 research in the personal strivings framework. Eighty-eight undergraduates listed 15 of their personal strivings and rated how much conflict existed between them and how ambivalent they felt about each. Diary and experience-sampling methods measured affect and physical symptoms.

> "Conflict and ambivalence were associated with high levels of negative affect, depression, neuroticism, and psychosomatic complaints. Conflict was also associated with health center visits and illnesses over the past year." (Emmons & King 1988, [abstract, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3397863/))

A one-year follow-up found the conflict and ambivalence ratings stable, and that they predicted psychosomatic complaints over time. A third study sampled undergraduates' thoughts and activities over three weeks: participants were less likely to act on conflictful and ambivalent strivings, but spent more time thinking about them.

## Sources

- [Emmons & King, 1988, "Conflict among personal strivings", Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 54(6), 1040-1048, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3397863/) — abstract findings quoted verbatim

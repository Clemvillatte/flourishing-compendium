Pratyahara (Sanskrit: प्रत्याहार, Pratyāhāra), the fifth of the eight limbs of Patanjali's yoga, is the withdrawal of the senses ([definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratyahara)). Patanjali lists it among the eight means of yoga in Book II of the Yoga Sutras, between breath control and attention ([Johnston, Yoga Sutras II.29](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2526)). The sutra that defines it, in Johnston's translation:

> "The right Withdrawal is the disengaging of the powers from entanglement in outer things, as the psychic nature has been withdrawn and stilled." (Patanjali, [Yoga Sutras II.54, trans. Johnston](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2526))

The next sutra gives the fruit of the practice:

> "Thereupon follows perfect mastery over the powers." (Patanjali, [Yoga Sutras II.55, trans. Johnston](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2526))

### Where the work happens

Johnston's definition puts the leverage on the mind, not the sense organs: the powers disengage "as the psychic nature has been withdrawn and stilled", so the withdrawal is a consequence of stillness rather than a feat of suppression. The compendium's two principles build on this: Y054 (Withdraw the senses on schedule) makes the withdrawal a chosen daily act before collapse enforces it, and Y055 (Retract like the tortoise when needed) reads the same withdrawal through the tortoise simile, which its own page cites from Bhagavad Gita 2.58.

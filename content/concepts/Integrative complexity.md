the finding that the capacity to hold opposing elements without premature closure predicts good judgment under stress (Tetlock's integrative-complexity research, as framework).

Integrative complexity is a construct for the structure of thought rather than its content. It scores verbal material, usually on a 1-7 scale, for two components: differentiation, the perception of different dimensions within a stimulus domain and the taking of different perspectives; and integration, the development of conceptual connections among differentiated dimensions or perspectives.

> Briefly, the successive versions of the theory focus on the complexity of information processing and decision making, complexity being defined and measured (usually on a 1-7 scale) in terms of degrees of differentiation and integration... Differentiation refers to the perception of different dimensions within a stimulus domain, and to the taking of different perspectives when considering the domain. It is a necessary but not sufficient prerequisite for integration, which is the development of conceptual connections among differentiated dimensions or perspectives. (Suedfeld, Tetlock & Streufert, "Conceptual/integrative complexity", in Smith (ed.), Motivation and personality: Handbook of thematic content analysis, [UBC author copy](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~psuedfeld/Chapter.pdf))

### State, not only trait

The integrative complexity viewpoint studies state complexity: the level of differentiation and integration shown in thought and behavior in a particular situation and context. The chapter records that complexity changes in response to fatigue, stress, intrapsychic conflict and social factors such as accountability and self-presentation. The scoring extends to essentially any connected verbal discourse, which is the basis of the archival work on historical events, individual lives and international relations.

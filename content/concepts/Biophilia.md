“Biophilia” — Wilson, Biophilia.

- Capaldi et al., 2014
- Kahn, 1999

Edward O. Wilson's hypothesis names the human draw toward the living: the pull of a garden, a forest, a pet, even a window with a tree in it. His claim, as the reviewing authors present it, is that the affinity is part of evolved human nature, not a cultural taste.

> "is the innately emotional affiliation of human beings to other living organisms. Innate means hereditary and hence part of ultimate human nature" — Wilson, as quoted in [Barbiero & Marconato, Visions for Sustainability 6 (2016), full text](https://ojs.unito.it/index.php/visions/article/download/1755/1642/0)

### What the definition commits to

The later formulation the review quotes is broader: biophilia as "the innate tendency to focus upon life and lifelike forms, and in certain circumstances to affiliate with them emotionally." The review's own gloss: "It is innate, but not instinctive, and it is based on a set of learning rules that appear to be genetically determined." Innate, on this account, does not mean fixed: the tendency is prepared by evolution, in Wilson's view, and filled in by each life.

The orienting response was named by Pavlov, who called it the "what is it" reflex. A Springer entry defines it as "the reflexive response to an environmental stimulus such that there is a transient orientation of the individual to the stimulus", first described by Sechenov in the 1850s, and notes that it declines with repeated exposure ([Orienting Response, Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-0-387-79948-3_1317?error=cookies_not_supported&code=0e0f7933-48e7-4f7b-9ebf-34658732e26a)). Sokolov's contribution was a mechanism for why it fades and returns, which makes the theory more specific than the seed's description of novel stimuli capturing attention.

### The comparator model

A PMC review of infant habituation says the response is "a cluster of responses elicited by the detection of a nonthreatening, moderately intense novel or unexpected stimulus: suppressed heart rate, respiration, skin resistance, pupil dilation, and reduced motor activity, including the fixing of sensory receptors at or toward the source or location of the stimulus". In Sokolov's account, repeated exposure forms an internal representation, and the magnitude of the response is "inversely proportional to the match between that external stimulus and the organism's internal representation of it" ([Infant Visual Habituation, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2758574/)). Sokolov called the representation a "neuronal model". In Thompson's history of habituation, the model develops in the cortex and inhibits an amplifying system tied to the ascending reticular activating system; "if a new or altered stimulus occurs which does not match the model, then inhibition is released and response strength recovers" ([Habituation: A History, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2714193/)).

### Novelty and significance

A review of concealed-information research states that the response is elicited by novelty and also by significance: "when a stimulus carries a special significance (e.g., one's own name) an enhanced orienting response occurs" ([A new theoretical perspective on concealed information detection, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10078248/)). It distinguishes the involuntary orienting reflex, a response to unexpected events as formulated by Sokolov (1963, 1966), from the voluntary orienting reaction evoked by stimuli primed as significant.

### Limits

One article on orienting and habituation notes that Sokolov proposed a match-mismatch comparator but "no attempt was made by him and his followers to specify the nature of this mechanism" ([Article on orienting and habituation, PDF hosted on Gershon Ben-Shakhar's site; file title "UNCORRECTED PROOF"](https://www.openu.ac.il/personal_sites/gershon-ben-shakhar/BGP2002.pdf)). Wikipedia's habituation page lists rival accounts, among them the Groves and Thompson dual-process theory, so the comparator model is one explanation among several ([Wikipedia, Habituation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habituation)). The seed's "exploration of the source" fits the "fixing of sensory receptors" at the stimulus, but it is not stated as exploration in these sources.

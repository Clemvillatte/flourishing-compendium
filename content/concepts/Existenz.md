Existenz is Karl Jaspers's term for human existence as it is lived and reflected on from within, in contrast with how it can be described from outside. That gloss is an interpretation based on the contrast in the Stanford Encyclopedia entry between scientific proof-forms and "subjective/existential self-reflection". Thornhill and Miron's Stanford Encyclopedia entry places it among three "essential existential modalities of human life": "orientation, existence and metaphysical transcendence". Each is tied to a way of knowing: "orientation is cognitively determined by objectively verifiable knowledge or by positive or scientific proof-forms, existence is determined by subjective/existential self-reflection, and transcendence is determined by the symbolic interpretation of metaphysical contents" ([SEP, "Karl Jaspers", §4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaspers/)).

### Limit situations

The entry describes Jaspers's early emphasis on "limit situations" (Grenzsituationen): "moments, usually accompanied by experiences of dread, guilt or acute anxiety, in which the human mind confronts the restrictions and pathological narrowness of its existing forms, and allows itself to abandon the securities of its limitedness, and so to enter new realm of self-consciousness" ([SEP, §4](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaspers/)). The entry presents these as the place where thought is pushed toward the unconditioned. The entry describes limit situations and Existenz in separate passages and does not tie them together in a single sentence, so the link between them is a reading and not a quotation.

### A term that faded

The entry notes that "even the term Existenz became increasingly scarce in his post-1945 publications", replaced "to a large extent, by ideas of shared humanity" grounded in "culture, tradition and ethically modulated political life" ([SEP, §6](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/jaspers/)).

Kahneman and Klein's 2009 paper examined the conditions separating skilled intuition from overconfident or biased impressions, comparing the heuristics-and-biases and naturalistic decision-making approaches ([Kahneman & Klein, 2009, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)). The concept names the paper's subject: expertise whose rapid judgments are genuine skill.

> "Two conditions must be satisfied for skilled intuition to develop: an environment of sufficiently high validity and adequate opportunity to practice the skill." (Kahneman & Klein, [Conditions for intuitive expertise: a failure to disagree](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/kahneman-klein-2009.pdf), American Psychologist 64(6), 2009, p. 520)

### Conditions for skilled intuition

Both authors endorse Herbert Simon's definition of skilled intuition: "The situation has provided a cue: This cue has given the expert access to information stored in memory, and the information provides the answer. Intuition is nothing more and nothing less than recognition" (Simon, 1992, p. 155, quoted in [Kahneman & Klein, 2009](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/kahneman-klein-2009.pdf)). The paper adds that without opportunities to learn, "a valid intuition can only be due to a lucky accident or to magic—and we do not believe in magic" ([Kahneman & Klein, 2009](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/kahneman-klein-2009.pdf)). The authors' conclusion, in the opened abstract, is that evaluating the likely quality of an intuitive judgment requires assessing the predictability of the environment and the individual's opportunity to learn its regularities, and that subjective experience is not a reliable indicator of judgment accuracy ([Kahneman & Klein, 2009, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19739881/)).

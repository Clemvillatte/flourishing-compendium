Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) is Marsha Linehan's treatment, developed from her 1993 manuals, for people whose emotions fire easily, hit hard and take long to subside. Its emotion modulation side is the skills-training module that teaches people to work with those emotions: identifying and naming them, checking whether they fit the facts, and changing unwanted ones by acting against their action urges.

> "Our aim in this chapter is to describe a set of emotion regulation skills developed within the context of dialectical behavior therapy (DBT; Linehan, 1993a, 1993b) and their potential to serve as a transdiagnostic intervention." (Neacsiu, Bohus and Linehan, [DBT skills: an intervention for emotion dysregulation](https://www.psychopap.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/NecsiuBohusLinehan2013DialecticalBehaviorTherapyskills_Aninterventionforemotiondysregulation..pdf), 2015)

### Why skills, and which

DBT treats the problem as trainable rather than as weakness of will: "Emotion dysregulation is the inability, even when one's best efforts are applied, to change in a desired way emotional cues, experiences, actions, verbal responses, and/or nonverbal expressions under normative conditions." The best-known of the change skills is opposite action, which rests on the idea "that deliberate actions opposite to those associated with unwanted emotions can effectively change emotions as well as action tendencies". The module pairs such change skills with acceptance skills, a dialectic the therapy is named for.

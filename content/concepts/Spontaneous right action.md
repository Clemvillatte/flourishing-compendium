Spontaneous right action is Wang Yangming's teaching that genuinely knowing the good already issues in acting. In his doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action, knowledge is the activation of innate moral knowledge (liangzhi) in contact with a situation, and action is responding to that situation; action is not an operation subsequent to knowledge but, in the account of the encyclopedia article, "a fundamental mode of human life." ([IEP, "Wang Yangming"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/))

> "There have never been people who know but do not act. Those who are supposed to know but do not act simply do not yet know." (Wang Yangming, Chuanxi Lu, quoted in [IEP, "Wang Yangming (1472—1529)"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/))

Wang's example for how knowing is achieved in acting: "If you want to know bitterness, you have to eat a bitter melon yourself." When knowledge and action appear separate, he attributes the split to selfish desire or false learning blocking the activation of true knowledge. ([IEP, "Wang Yangming"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/))

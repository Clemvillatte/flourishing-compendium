The page follows the sense the citing principle uses, trust in one's own nature consulted in the choice of goals, and connects that sense to a research line in the self-determination tradition: Sheldon and Elliot's self-concordance model (1999), an application of self-determination theory to personal goals.

### Goals that fit the person

> "The self-concordance of goals (i.e., their consistency with the person's developing interests and core values) plays a dual role in the model. First, those pursuing self-concordant goals put more sustained effort into achieving those goals and thus are more likely to attain them. Second, those who attain self-concordant goals reap greater well-being benefits from their attainment." — [Sheldon & Elliot, 1999, abstract (PubMed, PMID 10101878)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101878/)

The dual role matters for the trust the label names: the abstract reports more sustained effort among people pursuing self-concordant goals, and greater well-being benefits when those goals are attained. The model links those benefits to satisfaction of autonomy, competence and relatedness, "daily activity-based experiences" that "accumulate during the period of striving" ([Sheldon & Elliot, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101878/)).

The dual role is what separates the trust from a preference for goals one happens to like. A goal can feel appealing and still be discordant, and the model's two-step structure gives a reason: the effort a person sustains and the benefit a person reaps both track whether the goal expresses the person's developing interests and core values, which is not something an observer can read off the goal's content. The trust the label names is therefore a trust in a specific kind of information, the fit between a goal and a self that is still developing, and not a trust that any felt pull is worth following.

The mediation claim also gives the trust a timescale. The needs are satisfied in daily activity during the striving, which means the model does not require a person to wait until a goal is attained to find out whether it was worth pursuing; the fit shows up in what the days of working on it are like. That is a reflection the model motivates rather than a result it establishes: a person choosing among goals can watch what the daily activity of striving on each is like, since the abstract locates need satisfaction there, and let that inform the choice.

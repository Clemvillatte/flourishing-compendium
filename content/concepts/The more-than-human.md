The more-than-human is the phrase David Abram coined in The Spell of the Sensuous (1996) for the broad commonwealth of earthly life that includes human culture and also exceeds it. His own account of the coinage:

> "After stumbling around for a while in the tangled thickets of English, I finally concocted a new phrase—the more-than-human world—by which to articulate the broad commonwealth of earthly life as a realm that manifestly includes human culture, with all our creativity, our arts, and our technology, but which also (necessarily) exceeds human culture." (Abram, "On the Origin of the Phrase 'More-Than-Human'", in [More Than Human Rights](https://mothrights.org/wp-content/themes/moth/assets/images/book/pdfs/ripped/15-More-Than-Human-Rights_Book-On-the-Origin-of-the-Phrase-More-Than-Human.pdf))

The intention behind it, he continues, was "to indicate that the realm of humankind (with our culture and technology) is a subset within a larger set," the human world embedded within and dependent upon the more-than-human world that exceeds it.

### What the phrase carries

The "more" is deliberately ambiguous: by it Abram also meant "to encourage a new humility on the part of humankind," the more being qualitative as well as quantitative. The phrase replaces the flat word "environment" with something that names the enveloping life we are part of. Abram's later formulation of the payoff: "Whenever we become intensely engaged by other styles and shapes of life, when we drop away our concern for ourselves and begin to celebrate and praise other beings and elements that exceed our exclusively human concerns, then—paradoxically—we most realize and epitomize our humanity" ([Abram, "On Being Human in a More-than-Human World"](https://www.davidabram.org/essays/magic-and-machine-65hd4-fj357-pblhw)). The compendium principle R130 carries the applied sense: human flourishing is nested inside, not separate from, the flourishing of the living world. A compendium exercise drawn from the phrase is to walk a familiar place and name five non-human participants in it, noticing how the "environment" becomes a community of others. What the phrase does not give: an argument that humans are dispensable, only that we are not the whole.

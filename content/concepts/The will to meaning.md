The will to meaning is Viktor Frankl's term for the primary motivational force he held to be at work in human life ([Man's Search for Meaning, full text PDF](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)). He built the term against the two psychologies he trained in: the striving is for meaning, where Freud's theory centred on pleasure and Adler's on superiority ([full text PDF](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)). His own statement of the contrast:

> "According to logotherapy, this striving to find a meaning in one's life is the primary motivational force in man. That is why I speak of a will to meaning in contrast to the pleasure principle (or, as we could also term it, the will to pleasure) on which Freudian psychoanalysis is cantered, as well as in contrast to the will to power on which Adlerian psychology, using the term "striving for superiority," is focused." (Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning, [full text PDF](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf); the source copy renders "centered" as "cantered", quoted as it appears)

The claim was tested where Frankl landed: the camps. The book's most quoted passage gives the freedom the construct rests on: "everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms—to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances, to choose one's own way" ([full text PDF](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)). Meaning is then discovered three ways: "by creating a work or doing a deed; by experiencing something or encountering someone; and by the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering" ([full text PDF](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)). And the concern it names is not comfort: "man's main concern is not to gain pleasure or to avoid pain but rather to see a meaning in his life" ([full text PDF](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)).

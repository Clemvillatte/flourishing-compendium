threats to freedom produce boomerang effects (Brehm, 1966).

The theory posits that people hold specific behavioral freedoms, and when a freedom is threatened or eliminated, a motivational state is aroused that seeks to restore it — often by doing the very thing that was forbidden. The magnitude of the reaction tracks the importance of the threatened freedom and the size of the threat. The compendium's linked principles work both directions of the effect: notice the push-back hand in yourself, and don't press others to the point of rebellion.

> "Psychological reactance" is "the motivational state that is hypothesized to occur when a freedom is eliminated or threatened with elimination" (Brehm and Brehm, 1981, p. 37, quoted in [Reynolds-Tylus & Tobias, 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00056/full)).

> "Reactance is a motivational state that provokes individuals to seek to restore their threatened or lost freedom (Brehm, 1966)." (Reynolds-Tylus & Tobias, 2019, [Frontiers in Communication](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00056/full))

The zone of proximal development is Vygotsky's term for the space between what a learner can do alone and what the same learner can do with help. Learning happens there — not where a task is already easy, and not where it is out of reach even with support.

> "The distance between the actual developmental level as determined by independent problem solving and the level of potential development as determined through problem solving under adult guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers."
> — [Vygotsky, Mind in Society (1978), p. 86, quoted in Chaiklin, "The zone of proximal development in Vygotsky's analysis"](https://lchc.ucsd.edu/mca/Mail/xmcamail.2002_11.dir/att-0211/02-Chaiklin_The_zone_of_proximal_development_in_Vygotsky.doc)

The definition was built for assessment. Two children who solve the same problems independently can look identical, yet differ sharply in what they can do with a teacher's guidance — and Vygotsky held that this second measure, the zone, is the better indicator of the course their learning will take.

The idea has a consequence for anyone teaching, parenting or training: the right task is one the person cannot yet do alone but can do with you. Too easy wastes the zone; too hard has no zone to work in. What is done in collaboration today, Vygotsky's accompanying formulation runs, can be done independently tomorrow.

Schadenfreude is pleasure felt at someone else's misfortune. The term is German, borrowed whole into English because no native word covers it; the dictionary gloss quoted in the review literature is "satisfaction or pleasure felt at someone else's misfortune" (Random House Webster's Unabridged Dictionary, 1998, quoted in Smith et al. 2009, [PDF](http://www.gruberpeplab.com/teaching/psych3131_summer2015/documents/6.1_Smith2009_ExploringWhenAndWhyOfSchadenfreude.pdf)).

### When it arises

The summary review by Smith, Powell, Combs and Schurtz lists the circumstances in which the emotion commonly appears: "Although schadenfreude is a socially undesirable emotion, research reveals at least three conditions in which it commonly arises. One condition is when observers gain from the misfortune. ... A second condition is when another's misfortune is deserved. ... A third condition is when a misfortune befalls an envied person." Of these, the deservingness route is the best mapped: "Notwithstanding this important definitional question, deservingness may be the royal road to schadenfreude, as a persuasive series of theoretical and empirical papers spearheaded by Norman Feather shows clearly (e.g., Feather, 1999, 2006; Feather & Sherman, 2002)."

The envy route matters for practice, because it turns schadenfreude into a diagnostic: the flash of pleasure at a rival's fall measures the envy that was already there.

### What it costs

The pleasure is real and the price is social. A study of how schadenfreude expressers are perceived found: "Study 1 (N = 90) showed that actors expressing schadenfreude elicit a more negative impression and more avoidance intentions than those who display a general negative behavior." The effect was not situational: "Study 2 (N = 90) showed that such an effect did not depend upon participants' competition with the target of the observed schadenfreude." (Pagliaro, Brambilla, Di Prinzio & Teresi, Psicologia sociale, 2019, [PDF](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)) Gloating damages trust and invites reprisal.

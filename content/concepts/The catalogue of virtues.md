The catalogue of virtues is [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]]'s table in Nicomachean Ethics II.7 of the character virtues as means between vices of excess and deficiency. The general definition:

> "Virtue, then, is a state of character concerned with choice, lying in a mean, i.e. the mean relative to us, this being determined by a rational principle, and by that principle by which the man of practical wisdom would determine it. Now it is a mean between two vices, that which depends on excess and that which depends on defect."

([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.6, trans. W.D. Ross, MIT Internet Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html).)

### The table

The II.7 catalogue, in Ross's translation, pairs each sphere with its mean, excess, and deficiency. The fear and confidence row: "With regard to feelings of fear and confidence courage is the mean; of the people who exceed, he who exceeds in fearlessness has no name [...], while the man who exceeds in confidence is rash, and he who exceeds in fear and falls short in confidence is a coward" ([II.7, MIT text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)). The money rows: "With regard to giving and taking of money the mean is liberality, the excess and the defect prodigality and meanness," and "a mean, magnificence (for the magnificent man differs from the liberal man; the former deals with large sums, the latter with small ones), an excess, tastelessness and vulgarity, and a deficiency, niggardliness" ([II.7, MIT text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)). The honor row: "With regard to honour and dishonour the mean is proper pride, the excess is known as a sort of 'empty vanity', and the deficiency is undue humility" ([II.7, MIT text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)). The full table as usually printed gives fear/courage, pleasure/temperance, money-minor/liberality, money-major/magnificence, honor-major/magnanimity, honor-minor/proper ambition, anger/good temper, self-expression/truthfulness, conversation/wit, social conduct/friendliness, shame/modesty, indignation/righteous indignation ([Rochester table](https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~tim/study/ARISTOTLE'S%20NICOMACHEAN%20ETHICS%20%20Table%20of%20Virtues%20and%20Vices.pdf)).

### The practical instruction

The catalogue's own use is in the II.9 closing: "the intermediate state is in all things to be praised, but that we must incline sometimes towards the excess, sometimes towards the deficiency; for so shall we most easily hit the mean and what is right" ([II.9, MIT text](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)). The NDPR review gives the scholarly gloss: "one should sometimes incline towards excess and sometimes towards deficiency; that way we shall most easily hit the mean and do well" ([Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/nicomachean-ethics-books-ii-iv-3/)).

Controlled motivation is the self-determination theory term for motivation whose source is pressure: external demands and rewards, or internally felt guilt, anxiety and pride. Ryan and Deci's classic-definitions review:

> Introjection describes a type of internal regulation that is still quite controlling because people perform such actions with the feeling of pressure in order to avoid guilt or anxiety or to attain ego-enhancements or pride.
>
> (Ryan and Deci, "Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations: Classic Definitions and New Directions", Contemporary Educational Psychology 25(1), 2000, section "The Different Types of Extrinsic Motivation", [open PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf))

The external form is the other half: "Such behaviors are performed to satisfy an external demand or obtain an externally imposed reward contingency," and people "typically experience externally regulated behavior as controlled or alienated, and their actions have an external perceived locus of causality" (Ryan and Deci 2000). The distinction matters because the two sources behave differently: the theory's evidence review links the autonomous forms to "greater engagement", "better performance" and "greater psychological well-being", leaving controlled motivation as what those outcomes are compared against (Ryan and Deci 2000).

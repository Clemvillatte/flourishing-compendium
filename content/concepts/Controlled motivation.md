Controlled motivation is the self-determination theory term for motivation sourced in pressure: external demands and rewards, or internally felt guilt, anxiety and pride. Ryan and Deci's classic-definitions review:

> Introjection describes a type of internal regulation that is still quite controlling because people perform such actions with the feeling of pressure in order to avoid guilt or anxiety or to attain ego-enhancements or pride.
>
> (Ryan and Deci, "Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations: Classic Definitions and New Directions", Contemporary Educational Psychology 25(1), 2000, [open PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf))

The external form is the other half: "Such behaviors are performed to satisfy an external demand or obtain an externally imposed reward contingency," and people "typically experience externally regulated behavior as controlled or alienated, and their actions have an external perceived locus of causality" (Ryan and Deci 2000). Introjection illustrates why an internally felt motive can still be controlling: the person acts under pressure to avoid guilt or anxiety, or to obtain pride or ego-enhancement.

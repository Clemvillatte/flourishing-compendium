Repetition (Danish: Gjentagelsen) is Kierkegaard's 1843 category for a return that is not mere recall: life lived forward recovers what has been. The book's opening formula:

> "Repetition and recollection are the same movement, only in opposite directions; for what is recollected has been, is repeated backwards, whereas repetition properly so called is recollected forwards." (Søren Kierkegaard, Repetition, [full text, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.128273/2015.128273.Repetition_djvu.txt))

### The dialectic

The category is built against the Greek doctrine of recollection:

> "The dialectic of repetition is easy; for what is repeated has been, otherwise it could not be repeated, but precisely the fact that it has been gives to repetition the character of novelty. When the Greeks said that all knowledge is recollection they affirmed that all that is has been; when one says that life is a repetition one affirms that existence which has been now becomes." (Søren Kierkegaard, Repetition, [full text, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/in.ernet.dli.2015.128273/2015.128273.Repetition_djvu.txt))

The old is not retrieved; it becomes again, and in becoming it is new.

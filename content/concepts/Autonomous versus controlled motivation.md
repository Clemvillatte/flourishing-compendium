This page holds the comparison between the two halves of the self-determination theory distinction, now split into its granular concepts: [[Autonomous motivation]] and [[Controlled motivation]]. The comparison's source is Ryan and Deci's review of the classic definitions, which sorts motivation by its reasons rather than its strength:

> we distinguish between different types of motivation based on the different reasons or goals that give rise to an action. The most basic distinction is between intrinsic motivation, which refers to doing something because it is inherently interesting or enjoyable, and extrinsic motivation, which refers to doing something because it leads to a separable outcome.
>
> (Ryan and Deci, "Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivations: Classic Definitions and New Directions", Contemporary Educational Psychology 25(1), 2000, [open PDF](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_IntExtDefs.pdf))

Within extrinsic motivation the theory grades autonomy: "extrinsic motivation can vary greatly in the degree to which it is autonomous," with integrated regulation most autonomous and introjected regulation "still quite controlling" (Ryan and Deci 2000). The seed's summary line, "integrated regulation versus introjected regulation (Deci and Ryan, 2000)", names the two poles of that grading. For the compendium's use of each half, see the granular pages; the citing principles treat the comparison as the evidence that the source of a motivation, not only its intensity, predicts its outcomes.

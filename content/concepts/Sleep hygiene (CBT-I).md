Sleep hygiene is education about everyday habits that support sleep. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine's clinical guideline for chronic insomnia defines it: sleep hygiene therapy "involves teaching patients about healthy lifestyle practices that improve sleep," and the practices it lists are "keeping a regular schedule, having a healthy diet and regular daytime exercise, having a quiet sleep environment, and avoiding napping, caffeine, other stimulants, nicotine, alcohol, excessive fluids, or stimulating activities before bedtime" ([Schutte-Rodin et al., 2008, Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine 4(5), 487-504](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2576317/)).

> "Although all patients with chronic insomnia should adhere to rules of good sleep hygiene, there is insufficient evidence to indicate that sleep hygiene alone is effective in the treatment of chronic insomnia. It should be used in combination with other therapies." (Schutte-Rodin et al., 2008, [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2576317/))

### Hygiene is not CBT-I

The compendium pairs sleep hygiene with cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), but the two differ. The Morin task-force review lists the treatments that met criteria as empirically supported: "Stimulus control therapy, relaxation, paradoxical intention, sleep restriction, and cognitive-behavior therapy" ([Morin et al., 2006, Sleep 29(11), 1398-1414, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17162986/)). Sleep hygiene is not on that list. A 2025 systematic review of 42 randomised trials (4,245 adults) found significant improvement on the Insomnia Severity Index with sleep hygiene education (mean difference 3.4 points before to after treatment), but it was "inferior" to CBT-I (mean difference 3.8 points on the same scale), and "the majority of included trials (85.71 %) had a high overall risk of bias" ([Ruan et al., 2025, Sleep Medicine Reviews 82, 102109, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40449065/)). So hygiene advice is reasonable general guidance and a component of a fuller treatment, with weak evidence as a stand-alone cure for insomnia.

Tathatā, "suchness", is the Mahayana name for things as they are once the mind's projections are set aside: reality before it has been sorted into my labels and my wants. The Awakening of Faith, a foundational Mahayana treatise, defines the term:

> "What is meant by the soul as suchness (*bhûtatathatâ*), is the oneness of the totality of things (*dharmadhâtu*), the great all-including whole, the quintessence of the Doctrine. For the essential nature of the soul is uncreate and eternal." (The Awakening of Faith, trans. D. T. Suzuki, [Sacred Texts Archive](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/taf/taf21.htm))

### A word that admits its own limits

The treatise is explicit that language strains under the job. "But the term suchness is all that can be expressed in language, and through this term all other terms may be disposed of." The definition is deliberately negative — not a description of what suchness contains, but an instruction to stop adding and excluding: "In the essence of suchness, there is neither anything which has to be excluded, nor anything which has to be added."

### Neither existence nor non-existence

The treatise refuses every slot the ordinary mind offers it:

> "Thus we understand that suchness (*bhûtatathatâ*) is neither that which is existence, nor that which is non-existence, nor that which is at once existence and non-existence, nor that which is not at once existence and non-existence; that it is neither that which is unity, nor that which is plurality, nor that which is at once unity and plurality, nor that which is not at once unity and plurality." (The Awakening of Faith, trans. D. T. Suzuki, [Sacred Texts Archive](https://sacred-texts.com/bud/taf/taf21.htm))

The refusals are the teaching: each category offered is another projection to be set down. The Zen tradition later gives this same point its own form — the awakened seeing is "not two", and suchness is the name for what is seen (see the [Stanford Encyclopedia's Zen entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2022/entries/japanese-zen/)).

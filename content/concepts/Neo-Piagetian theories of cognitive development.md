Neo-Piagetian theories keep Piaget's stages but explain them through information processing. An Oxford chapter says a group of researchers, among them Pascual-Leone, Case and Fischer, "relied on the perspective of information processing to offer novel extensions of Piaget's theory from the 1970s onwards", and that they appeal to "mental attention, working memory capacity, executive control, or attentional inhibition" to explain the dynamics and variability of development ([Post-Piagetian Perspectives of Cognitive Development, Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61768/chapter/553520912?guestAccessKey=)). Wikipedia states the shared logic: higher stages "place increasingly higher demands" on processing efficiency and working memory capacity, "so that their order of appearance reflects the information processing possibilities at successive ages" ([Wikipedia, Neo-Piagetian theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Piagetian_theories_of_cognitive_development)). Some of those sentences carry a citation-needed flag there, so treat that summary as provisional.

### The main theorists

Demetriou's chapter summary lists five theories: Pascual-Leone, Case, Halford, Fischer, and his own. "The first three of these theories emphasize the role of working memory as a factor of transition across the main developmental stages of thought". Fischer's "emphasizes the social and the learning dimensions", while Demetriou's "integrates considerations about special processes in different domains of thought and also the role of self-awareness and self-regulation" ([Demetriou, Piaget and the Neo-Piagetians, Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/9462515/Demetriou_A_in_press_Piaget_and_the_Neo_Piagetians_In_H_Miller_Ed_The_SAGE_Encyclopedia_of_Theory_in_Psychology)).

On Wikipedia's account, Pascual-Leone proposed that mental power rises from one unit at 2 to 3 years to a maximum of seven units at 15, while Case held that capacity "recycles over a succession of four main stages" and Halford focused on relational complexity (Wikipedia). A Case-based study from ERIC reports the idea that development within each Piagetian stage reflects the growth of working memory for that stage's operations, from about one "chunk" to about four ([A Neo-Piagettian Approach to Development, ERIC PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED199089.pdf)).

### Evidence and disagreements

Cowan summarises the shared claim: neo-Piagetians held that cognitive changes "indirectly resulted from improvements in the foundations of cognitive processing, including processing speed... processing resources including energy and working memory capacity... and the ability to bind items together to form more complex dependencies" ([Cowan, Why and how to study working memory development, PDF](https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2020/Cowan-2020-Lannee-psychologique-why-and-how-WM.pdf)). The theories differ on what grows. A Wikipedia passage says Halford objected to Case's definition of capacity, arguing that mental capacity "cannot be specified in reference to executive functions".

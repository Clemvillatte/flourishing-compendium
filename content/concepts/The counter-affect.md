The counter-affect is the Buddhist practice the texts call cultivating the opposite (pratipakṣā-bhāvanā): when an unwholesome state of mind takes hold, the move is to give the mind a different, wholesome object that carries the opposite feeling, and let the old state starve. The practice does not argue with the affect; it replaces it.

The early texts give the method directly. In the discourse on the relaxation of thoughts:

> "There is the case where evil, unskillful thoughts — imbued with desire, aversion, or delusion — arise in a monk while he is referring to and attending to a particular theme. He should attend to another theme, apart from that one, connected with what is skillful. When he is attending to this other theme, apart from that one, connected with what is skillful, then those evil, unskillful thoughts — imbued with desire, aversion, or delusion — are abandoned and subside." (MN 20, Vitakkasaṇṭhāna Sutta, Thanissaro translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

### Each hindrance has its own counter

The discourse on nutriment makes the pairing explicit. Each hindrance grows on a food and is starved by a specific opposite: "There is the theme of unattractiveness. To foster appropriate attention to it: This is lack of food for the arising of unarisen sensual desire." Ill will's lack of food is "awareness-release", sloth and drowsiness are starved by "the potential for effort, the potential for exertion, the potential for striving", and restlessness and anxiety by "the stilling of awareness" (SN 46.51, Thanissaro translation, [Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn46/sn46.051.than.html)). The counters are affects, not syllogisms: goodwill answers ill will as a feeling, and effort answers sloth as energy.

Valuation as growth-direction is the Deweyan position that values are not destinations to be reached but directions of change. In Dewey's own words:

> "Honesty, industry, temperance, justice, like health, wealth and learning, are not goods to be possessed as they would be if they expressed fixed ends to be attained. They are directions of change in the quality of experience. Growth itself is the only moral 'end'."

([Dewey, Reconstruction in Philosophy, MW12:181, quoted in SEP notes](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2025/entries/dewey/notes.html).)

The position does two things at once. It makes valuation practical rather than subjective: the situation that calls for a value judgment "is existential, but it exists as something whose good or value resides [...] in something to be attained in action" ([Stark, quoting Dewey's Theory of Valuation, p. 5](https://davidcstark.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/paper_whats_valuable-2.pdf)). And it makes the criterion of a good value its effect on the course of activity, not its correspondence to a fixed end.

### What the theory claims

Dewey's Theory of Valuation (1939) distinguishes prizing from appraising. In common speech, "the words 'valuing' and 'valuation' are verbally employed to designate both prizing, in the sense of holding precious, dear [...] and appraising in the sense of putting a value upon, assigning value to" ([Dewey 1939: 5, quoted in Stark](https://davidcstark.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/paper_whats_valuable-2.pdf)). The Internet Archive full text of the monograph puts the practical test plainly: ends-in-view are "appraised or valued as good or bad on the ground of their serviceability in the direction of behavior dealing with states of affairs found to be objectionable because of some lack or conflict in them" ([Dewey, Theory of Valuation, full text](https://archive.org/stream/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp_djvu.txt)).

### The flourishing turn

The consequence for a flourishing practice is the one the compendium holds: since "growth itself is the only moral 'end'" ([Dewey, RIP, MW12:181](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2025/entries/dewey/notes.html)), the question to ask of a value is not whether it names the right terminus but whether it keeps the quality of experience improving along the path. The same idea recurs in Dewey's educational writing: "Not perfection as a final goal, but the ever-enduring process of perfecting, maturing, refining is the aim in living" ([same source](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2025/entries/dewey/notes.html)).

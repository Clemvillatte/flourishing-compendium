Self-actualization, in Abraham Maslow's original statement, is the need to become what one has it in one to become. In his 1943 paper "A Theory of Human Motivation" he writes: "A musician must make music, an artist must paint, a poet must write, if he is to be ultimately happy. What a man can be, he must be. This need we may call self-actualization" ([Maslow, 1943, Classics in the History of Psychology](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm)).

> "It refers to the desire for self-fulfillment, namely, to the tendency for him to become actualized in what he is potentially. This tendency might be phrased as the desire to become more and more what one is, to become everything that one is capable of becoming." (Maslow, 1943, p. 382-383, [York University text](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm))

### What Maslow says about its place

Maslow credits the term to someone else: "This term, first coined by Kurt Goldstein, is being used in this paper in a much more specific and limited fashion." He treats it as the top of a hierarchy of "relative prepotency," where higher needs emerge once lower ones are gratified. He also warns against reading the order as fixed: "We have spoken so far as if this hierarchy were a fixed order but actually it is not nearly as rigid as we may have implied." And he describes partial satisfaction, not stages that are completed in turn: "most members of our society who are normal, are partially satisfied in all their basic needs and partially unsatisfied in all their basic needs at the same time" (all from the same paper). His own illustrative figures, labelled arbitrary, give the average citizen only about 10 per cent satisfaction of self-actualization needs.

### Scope of this page

The 1943 paper is the earliest source opened for this page. The page's seed citations are Maslow's later books (1968, 1970), which were not opened here, so the seed wording about "full use and exploitation of talents" is not quoted. The link between the concept and growth or "becoming" comes from Maslow's Toward a Psychology of Being, also unopened. The compendium's principle SE065 (Learn self-actualization) adds a practical framing that Maslow's paper does not give.

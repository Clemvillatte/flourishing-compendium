The job demands-resources (JD-R) model, introduced by Demerouti, Bakker, Nachreiner and Schaufeli in 2001, sorts working conditions into two broad categories and traces each to its own outcome: demands toward exhaustion and burnout, resources toward engagement. In the original study's abstract, the model "proposes that working conditions can be categorized into 2 broad categories, job demands and job resources", and the analyses found that "Job demands are primarily related to the exhaustion component of burnout, whereas (lack of) job resources are primarily related to disengagement" ([Demerouti et al. 2001, Journal of Applied Psychology 86(3), PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419809/)).

The two processes are the model's point. Burnout follows the health-impairment path, where sustained demands exceed what the person can sustain; engagement follows the motivational path, where resources such as autonomy, feedback and support do their work. The model was built on three occupational groups — human services, industry and transport, total N = 374 — and the same pattern held in each, which is why its authors proposed it as an overarching model for occupations generally rather than a single-sector finding.

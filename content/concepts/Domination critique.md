The domination critique, in Erich Fromm's Escape from Freedom (1941), is an analysis of why people seek to control others or submit to someone who does. Britannica summarises the book as charting "the growth of freedom and self-awareness from the Middle Ages to modern times" and analysing, "using psychoanalytic techniques, ... the tendency, brought on by modernization, to take refuge from contemporary insecurities by turning to totalitarian movements such as Nazism" ([Britannica: Escape from Freedom](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Escape-from-Freedom)).

### Freedom as a source of anxiety

A review in Social Forces gives the argument in outline. Fromm held that rapid social change, particularly the rise of capitalism, freed people from authority and from the ties of community. This "freedom from" left them "anxious, homeless, powerless", and "the embrace of authoritarianism alleviated this discomfort, especially submission to a powerful leader, along with sadomasochistic aggression, and/or conformity" ([Social Forces review](https://academic.oup.com/sf/article/101/1/e1/6544313)). Control of others and submission are, in this reading, two answers to the same fear.

### What Fromm proposed instead

An Oxford chapter on Fromm and psychoanalysis quotes him: the alternative is for a person to "unite himself with the world in the spontaneity of love and productive work", rather than seeking "security by such ties with the world as destroy his freedom and the integrity of his individual self" ([Oxford chapter on Freud and the Frankfurt School](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28270/chapter/213421926)). The same chapter says Fromm made the key psychological problem "the specific kind of relatedness of the individual towards the world".

### Scope

The sources describe domination and submission as responses to insecurity in a historical setting, and they do not give a test of Fromm's claims. Britannica and the other summaries are secondary, and Escape from Freedom itself was not read for this page. The compendium's wording calls domination the assertive pole of Fromm's authoritarian mechanism. Those summaries describe sadomasochism and submission to a leader, and they do not use the term "pole".

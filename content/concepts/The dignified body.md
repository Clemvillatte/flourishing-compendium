The dignified body is the bodily half of Aristotle's portrait of the great-souled person (*megalopsychia*) in Nicomachean Ethics IV.3. The passage on bearing, in Bartlett's translation:

> "Also, slowness of movement seems to be the mark of a great-souled man, as well as a deep voice and steady speech; for he who is serious about few things is not given to hastiness, nor is anyone ever vehement who supposes that nothing is great, whereas a shrill voice and quickness result from these things."

([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.3, 1125a12-17, trans. Robert Bartlett, Internet Archive full text](https://archive.org/stream/AristotlesNicomacheanEthics/Aristotles%20-%20Nicomachean%20Ethics_djvu.txt).)

### What the body expresses

The connection between the character and the carriage is stated in the passage's logic: what one takes seriously shows in pace and tone. Aristotle even describes the magnanimous person's physical bearing as an expression of his state: "his movements are slow, his voice deep, and his speech measured" (NE 1125a11-12; [Hankins paper](https://www.keithhankins.com/uploads/2/1/7/9/21794922/hankins_-_aristotelian_magnanimity_-_1-26_draft.pdf)). The Irwin translation of the same line: "The proud man seems to have slow movements, a deep voice and calm speech. For since he takes few things seriously, he is in no hurry, and since he counts nothing great, he is not strident" ([quoted at philosopher.eu](https://www.philosopher.eu/others-writings/aristotle-on-pride-megalopsuchia/)). The passage's content is my principle on posture in ancient dress: the body is not a neutral container the character uses, but one of the places the character is.

### The honest limit

The portrait is of a specific Greek virtue with a specific social setting — height, "beautiful and useless things," the refusal to ask favors (NE 1124b; [Internet Archive text](https://archive.org/stream/AristotlesNicomacheanEthics/Aristotles%20-%20Nicomachean%20Ethics_djvu.txt); [WIST quotations](https://wist.info/aristotle/37666/)) — and modern scholarship notes the limit: the physical attributes "include masculinity, stature (NE 1123b 6), and a deep, measured voice (NE 1125a 14)" ([BYU aporia paper](https://aporia.byu.edu/pdfs/wilcken-magnanimity.pdf)). The transferable part is the structure, not the stature: a settled estimate of what is great produces a body that is not in a hurry.

Aporia is the productive dead end: the state of being unable to move forward in an inquiry, and the thing an inquiry is sometimes meant to reach. The Greek word means "difficulty, perplexity", built from aporos, "impassable", literally "without a passage" ([Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/aporia)). My source text is Plato's Meno, where the impasse arrives on purpose. Socrates questions Meno about virtue until Meno can no longer answer, and Meno compares him to a fish that numbs.

> "And if I may venture to make a jest upon you, you seem to me both in your appearance and in your power over others to be very like the flat torpedo fish, who torpifies those who come near him and touch him, as you have now torpified me, I think. For my soul and my tongue are really torpid, and I do not know how to answer you" (Plato, Meno, trans. Benjamin Jowett, [Internet Classics Archive](http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/meno.html)).

### The paradox of inquiry

From the numbing, Meno draws a skeptical conclusion: inquiry is impossible. "And how will you enquire, Socrates, into that which you do not know? What will you put forth as the subject of enquiry? And if you find what you want, how will you ever know that this is the thing which you did not know?" (Meno, trans. Jowett, same page). Socrates answers with the theory of recollection, and the dialogue treats the impasse as the opening of the search rather than its refutation.

### Aristotle's repair

Aristotle turned the same experience into a method. Before inquiring into first philosophy, he says, one must lay out the puzzles, because a knot cannot be untied by someone who does not know it is there.

> "For those who wish to get clear of difficulties it is advantageous to discuss the difficulties well; for the subsequent free play of thought implies the solution of the previous difficulties, and it is not possible to untie a knot of which one does not know." (Aristotle, Metaphysics III.1, trans. W.D. Ross, [Internet Classics Archive](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.3.iii.html))

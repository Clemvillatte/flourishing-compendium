In Spinoza's Ethics, an affect is a change in the body's power of acting together with the idea of that change ([Ethics Part III, D3](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)). The definition covers both active and passive states ([D3](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)).

> "By 'affect' I understand states of a body by which its power of acting is increased or lessened, helped or hindered, and also the ideas of these states. Thus, if we can be the adequate cause of any of these states, the affect in question is what I call an 'action'; otherwise it is a 'passion' [= 'passive state']." — Spinoza, Ethics Part III, Definition 3, trans. Bennett ([Early Modern Texts](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf))

Bennett's translation defines pleasure as "a man's passing from a lesser perfection to a greater" and unpleasure as "a man's passing from a greater perfection to a lesser." Love is "a pleasure accompanied by the idea of an external cause," and hate "unpleasure accompanied by the idea of an external cause" ([Definitions of the affects, 2, 3, 6, 7](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)).

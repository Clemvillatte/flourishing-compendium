In Spinoza's Ethics, an affect is a state of a body by which its power of acting is increased or lessened, together with the idea of that state ([Ethics Part III, D3](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)). The definition covers both active and passive states: an affect of which we can be the adequate cause is an action, otherwise a passion ([D3](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)).

> "By 'affect' I understand states of a body by which its power of acting is increased or lessened, helped or hindered, and also the ideas of these states. Thus, if we can be the adequate cause of any of these states, the affect in question is what I call an 'action'; otherwise it is a 'passion' [= 'passive state']." — Spinoza, Ethics Part III, Definition 3, trans. Bennett ([Early Modern Texts](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf))

The affects are defined in turn: pleasure is "a man's passing from a lesser perfection to a greater," unpleasure "a man's passing from a greater perfection to a lesser" ([Definitions of the affects, 2, 3](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)). Love is "a pleasure accompanied by the idea of an external cause," and hate "unpleasure accompanied by the idea of an external cause" ([Definitions of the affects, 6, 7](https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665part3.pdf)).

The definition is doing two things at once, and the pairing is what makes the account distinctive. An affect is a bodily state and the idea of that state, which means a person does not have a feeling and separately a thought about the feeling; the feeling arrives already thought about, and the thinking is part of what it is. The definition also puts the direction of the power at the centre, since an affect is a change in what a body can do rather than a change that merely happens to it.

The action/passion line is the part a person can act on. An affect of which a person is the adequate cause is an action, and one whose cause is elsewhere is a passion, which means the same bodily change can be either depending on where the causing is happening. A person applying the concept can read their own affects as information about the power of their situation rather than as instructions, and the reading is what the definitions are for.

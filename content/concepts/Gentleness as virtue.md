The compendium uses "Gentleness as virtue" for Aristotle's treatment of the mean concerned with anger (Greek praotes, rendered "Meekness" in this translation). In the Nicomachean Ethics:

> "Now he who is angry at what and with whom he ought, and further, in right manner and time, and for proper length of time, is praised, so this Man will be Meek since Meekness is praised." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book IV, chapter V in the standard Bekker numbering, printed as Chapter VII in this edition, Chase translation, [Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt))

### The mean and its neighbours

The chapter defines the territory: "Meekness is a mean state, having for its object-matter Anger" (Aristotle, NE IV, Chase translation, [Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)). The meek person is "imperturbable, and not being led away by passion, but being angry in that manner, and at those things, and for that length of time, which Reason may direct" (Aristotle, NE IV, [Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)). The mean "is thought to err rather on the side of defect, inasmuch as he is not apt to take revenge but rather to make allowances and forgive" (Aristotle, NE IV, [Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)). The defect, angerlessness, "is blamed: I mean, they who are not angry at things at which they ought to be angry are thought to be foolish" (Aristotle, NE IV, [Gutenberg #8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

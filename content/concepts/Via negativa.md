The via negativa is the negative way: what cannot be said positively is approached by negation. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy describes the apophatic approach it names:

> "Apophatic mysticism (from the Greek 'apophasis,' meaning negation or 'saying away') is contrasted with kataphatic mysticism (from the Greek 'kataphasis,' meaning affirmation or 'saying with'). Apophatic mystics claim that nothing positive can be said about objects or states of affairs that they experience." (SEP, "Mysticism")

The SEP credits the introduction of the via negativa to Christianity to Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite, who "also wrote works in the kataphatic approach on religious symbolism and the names of God, while still stressing that God cannot be fully captured by any name" ([SEP, "Mysticism"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mysticism/)).

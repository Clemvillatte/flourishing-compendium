The SOC model "was introduced by Paul and Margret Baltes (1990)" as "a meta-model of general developmental processes based in the life-span developmental psychology tradition" ([The Model of Selection, Optimization, and Compensation, Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-69892-2_109-1)). A Groningen chapter says it was "originally conceptualized as a meta-theory of successful aging" that can apply across the lifespan, with successful development defined as "the maximization of gains" and "the minimization of losses" ([The Model of Selection, Optimization, Compensation, University of Groningen PDF](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/568509804/3_s2.0_B9780128127568000049_main.pdf)).

### Three processes, four strategies

Selection means delineating goals and committing to a subset. It has two forms: elective selection, "developing a goal hierarchy, based on importance, urgency, or preference", and loss-based selection, which occurs "when engaging in compensatory behavior is too costly and setting new goals would be the most adaptive response" (Groningen chapter). Optimization is "the acquisition, refinement, and use of relevant means to achieve selected goals". Compensation "refers to processes that are geared toward the maintenance of functioning when facing or anticipating losses", for example "switching to other means", which can include technological aids or instrumental social support. An Oxford chapter adds that compensation and loss-based selection are alternatives: compensation keeps the goal, whereas loss-based selection reorganises around the loss ([The Theory of Selection, Optimization, and Compensation, Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/36329/chapter/318702706); the contrast also appears in [Psychological Principles of Successful Aging Technologies, Max Planck PDF](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2100144_6/component/file_2100143/content)).

### A worked example

The Cambridge paper The Process of Successful Ageing gives a case: an elderly person whose spouse is terminally ill "may give up the domain of sexuality altogether, or may restrict some goals and involvements in the social network at large, but increase efforts in the domain of leisure activities and family" ([Cambridge, Ageing and Society](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C1E6F81ADC2C4D91484A8819C7BED061/S0144686X00003603a.pdf/div-class-title-the-process-of-successful-ageing-div.pdf)).

### Scope and cautions

The seed says "successful ageing", which is the original focus, but the model is "a life-span model of successful development" that is also applied to work ([Exploring the Use of Selection, Optimization, and Compensation, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8866242/)). The Groningen chapter adds that "specific criteria of successful aging are subject to cultural and personal values", so what counts as a gain or a loss is not fixed by the model. I did not open an outcome meta-analysis, so I make no claim about how strongly SOC use predicts wellbeing.

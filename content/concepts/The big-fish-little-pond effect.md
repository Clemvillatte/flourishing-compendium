The big-fish-little-pond effect (BFLPE) is the finding that equally able students have lower academic self-concepts in schools where classmates' average ability is high. A summary from Western Sydney University puts it this way: the effect "predicts that equally able students have lower academic self-concepts when attending schools where the average ability levels of classmates is high, and higher academic self-concepts when attending schools where the school-average ability is low" ([Western Sydney University research record](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/big-fish-little-pond-effect-on-academic-self-concept-cross-cultur/)).

### Marsh 1987

Herbert Marsh's 1987 paper in the Journal of Educational Psychology "reanalyzed the Youth in Transition data, supported the generality of the earlier Marsh and Parker (EJ) findings, and demonstrated new theoretical implications of the big-fish-little-pond effect" ([ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ364447)). The theoretical model holds that individual ability relates positively to academic self-concept ("I do well academically, so I feel good about my abilities"), whereas the average ability of the class or school relates negatively to it ("There are students in my class (or school) who are really bright, and I don't feel as bright as them") ([PMC study of medical students](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156796/)).

### Mechanism and evidence

A meta-analysis states that "Marsh (1987) argued that this social comparison mechanism lies at the heart of the BFLPE" and reports the effect to be stronger among high school students, in Asia, and for verbal self-concept ([Frontiers meta-analysis](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01569/full)). A longitudinal analysis over eight years found that school-average ability had negative effects on academic self-concept, school grades and aspirations, while its total effect on educational attainment was non-significant ([Marsh & O'Mara 2010, UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1504303/3/Marsh_O_Mara_2010._pre_galley_proofs_Long_Term_Total_Negative_Effects_of_School_Average_Ability_on_Diverse_Educational_Outcomes.pdf)).

### Scope

The effect is documented for academic self-concept in school settings. It does not by itself show that a person's wider sense of worth depends on the peer group. Whether the pattern extends beyond schools is not tested in these education sources. The 1987 paper itself was not read, only its abstract and later reviews.

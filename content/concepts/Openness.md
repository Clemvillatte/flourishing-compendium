Openness, in the Big Five tradition, is a personality trait. Kaufman and colleagues describe Openness/Intellect as "the trait most closely associated with creativity and creative achievement," and split it into two aspects: Openness to Experience, "reflecting cognitive engagement with perception, fantasy, aesthetics, and emotions," and Intellect, "reflecting cognitive engagement with abstract and semantic information, primarily through reasoning" ([Kaufman et al., 2016, Journal of Personality 84(2), 248-258, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jopy.12156)).

> "We confirmed the hypothesis that whereas Openness predicts creative achievement in the arts, Intellect predicts creative achievement in the sciences." (Kaufman et al., 2016, [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jopy.12156))

### What the study covered

The authors report four demographically diverse samples totaling 1,035 participants. They also found that "Extraversion additionally predicted creative achievement in the arts, independently of Openness," and that the link between Intellect and scientific creativity "may be due at least in part to" cognitive ability and divergent thinking (same abstract). The finding concerns a trait measured by questionnaire and its statistical link to creative achievement, which is narrower than the everyday sense of being open to people or ideas.

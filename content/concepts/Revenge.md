(Carlsmith et al., 2008), predicts worse mood and more rumination than forgiveness.

The paradoxical finding from Carlsmith, Wilson and Gilbert: people expect hedonic rewards from punishing an offender, but revenge can have consequences precisely the opposite of what people expect. In three studies, those who punished kept ruminating about the offender, while those who did not punish "move on" and think less about the offender. People also failed to appreciate how different it feels to witness punishment rather than instigate it.

> "People expect to reap hedonic rewards when they punish an offender, but in at least some instances, revenge has hedonic consequences that are precisely the opposite of what people expect." (Carlsmith, Wilson & Gilbert, 2008, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 95(6), 1316–1324, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19025285/))

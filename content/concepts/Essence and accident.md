In [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]], essence is what it is to be a thing of a given kind, and accidents are the features it can have or lack without ceasing to be that kind. Cohen and Reeve's Stanford Encyclopedia entry explains that "'Essence' is the standard English translation of Aristotle's curious phrase to ti ên einai, literally 'the what it was to be' for a thing," and that he sometimes uses the shorter to ti esti, "the what it is" ([SEP, "Aristotle's Metaphysics", §7](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)).

### Definition states the essence

The entry quotes the Topics: "a definition is an account (logos) that signifies an essence." What belongs to a thing "in respect of itself" belongs to it in its essence, and Aristotle's point, as the entry puts it, is about things and not words: "The definition of tiger does not tell us the meaning of the word 'tiger'; it tells us what it is to be a tiger, what a tiger is said to be intrinsically" ([SEP, §7](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)).

### The pale man

An accidental unity such as a pale man is not an essence: the entry says it "does not specify the 'what it is' of any primary being." Essence in the primary sense "will belong to things that are species of a genus and to nothing else" (Metaphysics 1030a11-12, as quoted in the entry). So "[m]an is a species, and so there is an essence of man; but pale man is not a species" ([SEP, §7](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)). In the Categories, the idea shows up as a distinction between what is "said of" a subject and what is "in" it, and the entry says it "seems to recur in other works as the distinction between accidental vs. essential predication" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-metaphysics/)).

### Scope

The entry itself flags that the exact meaning of Aristotle's claim that a thing is the same as its essence, and the arguments for it, "are matters of scholarly controversy." The account here is limited to what the entry lays out.

### Here

- [[E007 — Practise eidetic seeing]] works from Husserl's idea of an essence as an object of its own kind of intuition. The Aristotelian distinction is background for the question that principle asks: what can change while a thing stays what it is.

Mastery learning is an instructional strategy Benjamin Bloom outlined in 1968 as "learning for mastery" and later shortened to "mastery learning" (Guskey, [Mastery Learning (2015), pp. 752-759](https://tguskey.com/wp-content/uploads/Mastery-Learning-1-Mastery-Learning.pdf)). Its root idea comes from John B. Carroll's 1963 model of school learning: aptitude is better read as an indicator of how fast a child can learn than of a ceiling on what the child can learn, so degree of learning depends on the time actually spent relative to the time needed (Guskey, 2015, pp. 752-753).

The strategy runs as a cycle that the chapter describes unit by unit: teachers organize the concepts and skills into learning units of about a week or two of instructional time, teach the unit, then administer a brief formative assessment paired with individualized corrective activities for what each student has not yet mastered, followed by a parallel second assessment to determine the extent of mastery before the class moves on (Guskey, 2015, pp. 753-754).

> "instead of using assessments only as evaluation devices that mark the end of each unit, Bloom recommended using them as part of the instructional process to diagnose individual learning difficulties (feedback) and to prescribe remediation procedures (correctives)." (Guskey, [Mastery Learning, International Encyclopedia of the Social & Behavioral Sciences, 2nd ed. (2015), p. 753](https://tguskey.com/wp-content/uploads/Mastery-Learning-1-Mastery-Learning.pdf))

### The 2 sigma benchmark

In the comparisons reported by Bloom and reproduced by Guskey, the average mastery learning student was about one standard deviation above the average of the conventional control class, above 84% of its students, and the average tutored student about two standard deviations above it, above 98% (Bloom, 1984, p. 4, as quoted in Guskey, 2015, p. 758). Bloom's research question was whether practical group instruction could achieve the tutoring results: one-to-one tutoring is "too costly for most societies to bear on a large scale" (Bloom, 1984, pp. 4-5, as quoted in Guskey, 2015, p. 758). These are the results of the Anania and Burke dissertation studies, not a general guarantee of effectiveness.

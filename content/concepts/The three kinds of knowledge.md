The three kinds of knowledge are Spinoza's ladder in the Ethics: knowledge from random experience (opinion or imagination), reason, and intuitive knowledge. In the Stanford Encyclopedia's reading, the first kind gives us no more than this:

> Such ideas do not convey adequate and true knowledge of the world, but only a relative, partial and subjective picture of how things presently seem to be to the perceiver.
>
> (Steven Nadler, "Baruch Spinoza", [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/))

The second kind, reason, "is the apprehension of the essence of a thing through a discursive, inferential procedure" (SEP Spinoza), and the third takes over where reason leaves off: "The third kind of knowledge, intuition, takes what is known by Reason and grasps it in a single, perspicacious act of the mind" (SEP Spinoza). This is the knowledge of things "under the aspect of eternity" (SEP Spinoza), and it carries the highest joy:

> The third kind of knowledge generates a love for its object, and in this love consists not a passionate joy but an active one, even blessedness itself.
>
> (SEP Spinoza)

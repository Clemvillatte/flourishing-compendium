Behavioral epigenetics is the study of epigenetic modifications of genomic regions in response to variations in environmental conditions, as a source of variation in biological and behavioral phenotypes ([Weaver 2007, review abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17965624/)). In a 2004 study in rats, Weaver and colleagues reported differences in DNA methylation at a glucocorticoid receptor gene promoter in the offspring's hippocampus.

> "Thus we show that an epigenomic state of a gene can be established through behavioral programming, and it is potentially reversible." (Weaver et al., [Epigenetic programming by maternal behavior](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1276), Nature Neuroscience 7:847-854, 2004, abstract)

### What the study found

Increased pup licking and grooming and arched-back nursing by rat mothers altered the offspring epigenome at a glucocorticoid receptor gene promoter in the hippocampus. The methylation differences emerged over the first week of life, were reversed with cross-fostering, and persisted into adulthood; a histone deacetylase inhibitor removed the group differences in methylation, gene expression and stress responses, "suggesting a causal relation among epigenomic state, GR expression and the maternal effect on stress responses in the offspring" ([abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn1276)).

The findings are bounded to rats, the measured promoter and the reported interventions; the paper marks the change as potentially reversible.

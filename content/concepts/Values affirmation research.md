Values affirmation research is the experimental literature on what happens when people write briefly about values they hold dear before facing a threat. The theory behind it, laid out in Geoffrey Cohen and David Sherman's 2014 review, starts from a motive: "The cardinal motive of the self-system, according to self-affirmation theory, is global self-integrity" ([Sherman et al. 2021, Self-Affirmation Interventions, UCSB lab PDF](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/sherman/david/sites/labs.psych.ucsb.edu.sherman.david/files/pubs/sherman.lokande.muller.cohen_.2021.sa_.pdf)).

### The mechanism

The review's own summary of how the intervention works:

> "Self-affirmations do not need to engender superiority or mastery; they simply need to satisfy personal worth. [...] This psychological buffer attenuates physiological stress responses (Creswell et al., 2005; Sherman, Bunyan, Creswell, & Jaremka, 2009) and defensive responses (Sherman & Cohen, 2006), facilitating message acceptance and behavior change."

([Ehret and Sherman, "Public Policy and Health: A Self-Affirmation Perspective," 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2372732214549472).)

The procedure is deliberately small: "The most common procedure allows individuals to select a personal value (e.g., relationships with family and friends, religion, or sense of humor) and write a brief essay about why that value is meaningful" ([same](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2372732214549472)). The point is not persuasion but threat reduction — the affirmation gives the self-system an alternative resource, so the threatening information no longer requires a defensive reply.

### What it delivers

The Cohen and Sherman review connects the mechanism to change in real settings: "affirmations help people to maintain a narrative of personal adequacy in threatening circumstances. They thus buffer individuals against threat and reduce defensive responses to it" ([Cohen and Sherman 2014, The Psychology of Change, Annual Review of Psychology 65, UCSB lab PDF](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/sherman/david/sites/labs.psych.ucsb.edu.sherman.david/files/pubs/cohen_sherman_2014.pdf)). The most-studied application is in education, where brief values-affirmation writing exercises improved performance for students under identity threat ([Annual Reviews lecture page](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/multimedia/10.1146/do.multimedia.2013.12.17.238)). Health-behavior meta-analyses find "small but reliable effects" on message acceptance, intentions, and behavior ([Ehret and Sherman 2014](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2372732214549472)).

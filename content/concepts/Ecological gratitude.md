“Ecological gratitude” — Mayer and Frantz, "The Connectedness to Nature Index".

Ecological gratitude, as the compendium uses it, is gratitude directed at the natural world. The seed's source needs a correction: Mayer and Frantz's 2004 paper is titled "The connectedness to nature scale" (not "Index"), and it measures nature connectedness, not gratitude. It does not test or define ecological gratitude. The evidence below combines that paper with one study that links gratitude and connection to nature directly.

### What Mayer and Frantz measured

> "Five studies assessed the validity and reliability of the connectedness to nature scale (CNS), a new measure of individuals' trait levels of feeling emotionally connected to the natural world." (Mayer & Frantz 2004, Journal of Environmental Psychology 24(4):503-515, [abstract at Oberlin](https://digitalcommons.oberlin.edu/faculty_schol/2433/))

The authors say the paper "supports ecopsychologists' contention that connection to nature is an important predictor of ecological behavior and subjective wellbeing". It supplies a way to measure the sense of belonging to nature that gratitude might build on or express. It says nothing about the emotion of thanks.

### A study that does connect gratitude and nature

Chen and colleagues (2022) divided 890 university students into gratitude, recreation and control conditions. They found that positive emotions of self-transcendence partially mediated the effect of the gratitude condition on connection to nature. They also found that these emotions and connection to nature fully mediated an indirect effect of gratitude on willingness to take part in, and sacrifice for, environmental protection. ([Frontiers in Psychology 13:908138](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9244548/))

The study used the 14-item Connectedness to Nature Scale among its measures. Its authors give limits: the emotion-elicitation method was not the most effective, data were collected by online questionnaire, and the sample was university students. They also found no group differences on the cognitive measures of self-nature overlap, and suggest emotions may promote emotional connection to nature but not cognitive connection.

### What this page does not claim

The Chen study is a single online experiment with university students. It does not show that gratitude to nature produces lasting behaviour change.

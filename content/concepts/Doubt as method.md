Doubt as method is Descartes' procedure in the Meditations: doubt not because one is a sceptic, but to find what survives doubt. The work opens with a resolution: "I thought it requisite (if I had a designe to establish any thing that should prove firme and permanent in sciences) that once in my life I should clearly cast aside all my former opinions, and begin a new from some First principles" ([Descartes, Meditations, Molyneux translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/70091/70091-h/70091-h.htm)). The rule of the inquiry is to treat the doubtful as false: "by removing from me whatever is in the least doubtful, as if I had certainly discover'd it to be altogether false, and will proceed till I find out some certainty, or if nothing else, yet at least this certainty, That there is nothing sure" (ibid.).

### The firm point

The doubt is driven to its limit by a hypothetical deceiver: "I suppose a certain powerful and (if it be lawful to call him so) evil deluder, who useth all his endeavours to deceive me in all things" (ibid.). What survives is the sentence the whole structure rests on:

> "without Doubt therefore I am, if he can deceive me; And let him Deceive me as much as he can, yet he can never make me not to Be, whilst I think that I am. Wherefore I may lay this down as a Principle, that whenever this sentence I am, I exist, is spoken or thought of by Me, 'tis necessarily True." — Descartes, Meditation II ([Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/70091/70091-h/70091-h.htm))

The plan is stated with Archimedes' lever as its image: "Archimedes required but a point which was firm, and immoveable that he might move the whole Earth, so in the perfect undertaking Great things may be expected, if I can discover but the least thing that is true and indisputable" (ibid.). Doubt here is a tool with a use and an end, not a resting place.

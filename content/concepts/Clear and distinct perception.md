## In plain words

Clear and distinct perception is Descartes' mark of what the intellect can know with certainty. A perception is clear when it is present and manifest to an attentive mind, distinct when, beyond clarity, it is cleanly separated from everything else. It is the hinge between his method of doubt and his rebuilding of knowledge.

## Where it comes from

The concept runs through the Meditations, but the definitions come from the later Principles: "I call a perception 'clear' when it is present and accessible to the attentive mind... I call a perception 'distinct' if, as well as being clear, it is so sharply separated from all other perceptions that it contains within itself only what is clear" ([SEP, Descartes' Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/)). The glosses: "clarity contrasts with obscurity, and distinctness with confusedness" ([SEP, Descartes' Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/)).

## How it works

The method of doubt clears the ground: the meditator treats all candidates for belief "as if false," "pretending for a time that these former opinions are utterly false and imaginary" ([SEP, Descartes' Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/)). What survives is what is perceived clearly and distinctly, and the Fourth Meditation argues to a divine guarantee of the rule: "Whatever I perceive clearly and distinctly is guaranteed true, because I am the creature of an all-perfect God" ([SEP, Descartes' Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/)). The rule's justification has limits the scholarship flags: clear and distinct apprehension "marks a merely necessary condition of perfect knowledge, not a sufficient condition" ([SEP, Descartes' Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-epistemology/)), and the appeal to God to certify the rule raises the famous circularity worry, the Cartesian Circle.

## What it explains

The concept explains the shape of certainty Descartes sought: not vividness of feeling but separation from confusions. It also explains why the senses rarely qualify — their deliverances are typically clear enough but confused with judgments mixed in. Spinoza transformed the notion into his own criterion of true ideas.

## Everyday life

The practical residue: before trusting a judgment, ask two questions — is it fully present to attention, and is it separated from what I merely fear or hope? Most errors are not unclear perceptions but indistinct ones, where fear or habit is mixed into the thing itself.

Autistic cognition: detail-focused processing is the cognitive-style account of autism associated with Uta Frith and Francesca Happé's weak coherence theory. The theory holds that autistic people often process information in terms of parts and details, showing strength in detail-oriented processing and pattern recognition, where the typical style pulls for gist, global form and meaning.

> A review of over 50 empirical studies of coherence suggests robust findings of local bias in ASD, with mixed findings regarding weak global processing. — Happé and Frith, ["The weak coherence account: detail-focused cognitive style in autism spectrum disorders" (2006), Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders 36(1), 5-25](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16450045/)

### Bias, not deficit

The account's history matters to its use. Frith's 1989 original framed weak coherence as a core deficit in central processing, a failure to extract global form and meaning. The 2006 review recasts it: the detail focus may represent an outcome of superiority in local processing, may be a processing bias rather than a deficit, and may occur alongside rather than explain the difficulties in social cognition. On the review's evidence, local bias appears not to be a mere side-effect of executive dysfunction and may be independent of theory of mind deficits. The style reading is what the compendium carries: detail focus as a characteristic way of processing, with strengths attached to it, not a broken version of the typical style.

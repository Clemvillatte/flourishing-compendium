Rogers's proposal that unconditional positive regard is one of the conditions that enable therapeutic change ([Rogers, 1957](https://www.simplypsychology.org/unconditional-positive-regard.html); [Elliott and Freire](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/27736/1/Rogers1957_Commentary.pdf)).

## The six conditions

Rogers's 1957 paper set out six conditions he held to be necessary and sufficient for constructive personality change: two people in psychological contact; the client incongruent, vulnerable or anxious; the therapist congruent or genuine in the relationship; the therapist's unconditional positive regard for the client; the therapist's empathic understanding of the client's frame of reference; and the client's perception of that empathy and regard to at least a minimal degree ([McLeod, rendering of Rogers 1957](https://www.simplypsychology.org/unconditional-positive-regard.html)).

## What the regard is

Unconditional positive regard is the therapist's warm acceptance of the client, whatever the client says or does in session ([McLeod](https://www.simplypsychology.org/unconditional-positive-regard.html)). Rogers described it as “caring for the [client], but not in a possessive way or in such a way as simply to satisfy the therapist's own needs” (Rogers, 1959, p. 208, quoted in [McLeod](https://www.simplypsychology.org/unconditional-positive-regard.html)). Rogers believed that experiencing unconditional acceptance helps clients overcome childhood conditions of worth, love and regard made contingent on meeting certain standards ([McLeod](https://www.simplypsychology.org/unconditional-positive-regard.html)).

## Rogers's proposal

Rogers proposed the six conditions as jointly necessary and sufficient for constructive personality change, with the client's perception of empathy and regard to at least a minimal degree ([McLeod, rendering of Rogers 1957](https://www.simplypsychology.org/unconditional-positive-regard.html)). Elliott and Freire note the apparent paradox in the proposal: acceptance brings about change, since Rogers's hypothesis states that if the therapist, instead of attempting to change the client, strives to accept the client unconditionally, change will follow naturally ([Elliott and Freire](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/27736/1/Rogers1957_Commentary.pdf)).

> “No other conditions are necessary. If these six conditions exist, and continue over a period of time, this is sufficient. The process of constructive personality change will follow”
>
> — Carl Rogers, closing statement of the hypothesis in The necessary and sufficient conditions of therapeutic personality change (1957), as reproduced in [Elliott and Freire](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/27736/1/Rogers1957_Commentary.pdf)'s commentary, which cites a 1996 reproduction of the paper

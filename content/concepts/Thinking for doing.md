The phrase is William James's, from the chapter on reasoning in The Principles of Psychology. ([James, Principles of Psychology, 1890](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57634/pg57634.txt))

> "My thinking is first and last and always for the sake of my doing, and I can only do one thing at a time." (James, The Principles of Psychology, vol. 2, ch. XXII, [Project Gutenberg #57634](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57634/pg57634.txt))

The sentence comes with a practical excuse. James writes that his excuse is necessity, the necessity his finite and practical nature lays upon him, and that a human attention dispersed over everything at once would simply stare vacantly. ([James, Principles of Psychology, 1890](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57634/pg57634.txt))

expressed humility improves team performance and psychological safety (Owens and Hekman, 2016).

Bradley Owens and David Hekman's team-performance study made the mechanism a social one: humility travels. Their abstract states the design, "Using data from 607 subjects organized into 161 teams (84 laboratory teams, 77 organizational field teams)," and the model:

> "We develop and test a theoretical model, positing that, when leaders behave humbly, followers emulate their humble behaviors, creating a shared interpersonal team process (collective humility)." ([Owens and Hekman, 2016, Academy of Management Journal 59(3): 1088-1111](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amj.2013.0660))

### Contagion, then performance

The chain runs from behavior to shared state to result: the collective humility "creates a team emergent state focused on progressively striving toward achieving the team's highest potential (collective promotion focus), which ultimately enhances team performance." Three studies support it, including a field study in a health services context, and the findings, in the authors' summary, "demonstrat[e] that leader behavior can spread via social contagion to followers, producing an emergent state that ultimately affects team performance." The leadership contribution they draw is behavioral: leaders lead by example, and a specific set of leader behaviors moves a whole team.

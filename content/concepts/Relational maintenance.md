“Relational maintenance” — Stafford and Canary, 1991.

Relational maintenance names the behaviors that keep a valued relationship at its level of intimacy. The canonical typology comes from Stafford and Canary's factor analyses. A couples-research article states it:

> "Canary and Stafford (1992) identified five such strategies: 1) positivity, or interacting with one's partner in a cheerful and optimistic manner; 2) openness, which refers to discussing and disclosing information about the relationship with one's partner; 3) assurances, which are messages that stress one's desires to continue the relationship; 4) social networks, which entails relying on, or interacting with common relatives/friends; and, 5) shared tasks, which involves maintaining the relationship by partaking in everyday activities such as housework." (Badr & Carmack Taylor, [Effects of Relationship Maintenance..., 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9976549/))

### The everyday register

The interest of the typology is its smallness: much of the maintenance is ordinary work, chores and cheerfulness, rather than crisis repair. The same study measured maintenance with the 29-item Relationship Maintenance Strategies Measure from the same research program.

Awe is the emotion of perceived vastness that exceeds one's existing mental structures and forces accommodation. Dacher Keltner and Jonathan Haidt, who gave the concept its modern analysis, state the two appraisals directly:

> "We suggest that two appraisals are central and are present in all clear cases of awe: perceived vastness, and a need for accommodation, defined as an inability to assimilate an experience into current mental structures." (Keltner & Haidt 2003, [Cognition and Emotion 17(2), 297–314](https://docslib.org/doc/10448325/keltner-haidt-awe-2003-pdf))

Something is perceived as vast (physically, conceptually, socially), and that vastness creates the need to adjust one's schemas to take it in. The paradigm triggers are nature at scale, the moral sublime (witnessing exceptional goodness), and art or music that overflows the categories.

### What the accommodation does

On the compendium's reading, the mechanism is the schema-adjustment: awe is the feeling of mental structure being revised, which is why it is studied alongside the "small self" effect, the vanishing of self-salience, and increased generosity and decreased entitlement in the emerging research. The Stoic statement of the same experience is seeing the smallness of human affairs. The contemplative traditions treat the same territory as a door rather than a rush: welcome peak experiences without chasing them.

### The distinction to hold

Awe is not delight or excitement. Delight fits its object into existing categories; awe breaks and rebuilds them. Delight in the ordinary is the everyday practice; [[Awe]] is the earthquake. And chasing awe for its own self-expansion inverts it: the chase re-centres the self the experience dissolves.

### In everyday life

One unhurried look at the night sky, taken monthly, with no photograph. The accommodation is the point, and the phone's camera relieves it. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

Its contemplative cognates are [[Shunyata]] (vastness beyond categories) and the [[Dao]] it gestures at.

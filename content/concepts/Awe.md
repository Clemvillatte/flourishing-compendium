Awe is the emotion of perceived vastness that exceeds one's existing mental structures and forces accommodation — the two appraisals Dacher Keltner and Jonathan Haidt identified as central to all clear cases of awe: something is perceived as vast (physically, conceptually, socially), and that vastness creates a need for accommodation, an adjustment of one's mental schemas to take it in ([Keltner & Haidt, "Approaching awe" (2003)](https://docslib.org/doc/10448325/keltner-haidt-awe-2003-pdf); [evolutionary account](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/17540739221136893)). The paradigm triggers are nature at scale, the moral sublime (witnessing exceptional goodness), and art or music that overflows the categories.

The mechanism is the schema-adjustment: awe is the feeling of mental structure being revised, which is why it reliably produces the "small self" effect — the vanishing of self-salience — and, in the emerging research, increased generosity and decreased entitlement ([[S100 — See the smallness of human affairs]] is its Stoic statement). The contemplative traditions treat the same territory as a door rather than a rush: welcome peak experiences without chasing them ([[P115 — Welcome peak experiences — don't chase them]]; [[B176 — Treat the present moment as the miracle]]; [[P185 — The sovereign cure for worry is a hold on larger realities]]).

The distinction to hold: awe is not delight or excitement — delight fits its object into existing categories; awe breaks and rebuilds them ([[D180 — Delight in the ordinary]] is the everyday practice; [[Awe]] is the earthquake). Chasing awe for its own self-expansion inverts it — the chase re-centres the self the experience dissolves.

An example at its smallest: one unhurried look at the night sky, taken monthly, with no photograph — the accommodation is the point, and the phone's camera relieves it.

Its contemplative cognates are [[Shunyata]] (vastness beyond categories) and the [[Dao]] it gestures at.

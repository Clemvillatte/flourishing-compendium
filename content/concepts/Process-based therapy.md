Process-based therapy (PBT) is the framework presented in Stefan G. Hofmann and Steven C. Hayes's clinical manual: instead of asking which named treatment fits which named disorder, the clinician asks which processes of change are active in this particular person, and targets them directly. The book that carries the model announces it as a redefinition, not a new brand:

> "Process-based therapy (PBT) is a new definition of what evidence-based therapy even means. It’s not a new treatment method—it’s a new way of thinking about treatment methods." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, Learning Process-Based Therapy (2021), Prologue)

### Origin

The book's argument is that evidence-based therapy had drifted into "protocols for syndromes": treatment packages keyed to DSM diagnoses that, on its account, fit individuals poorly and multiplied faster than the evidence could sort them. The model was set out in two works the book cites as its foundation, Hayes and Hofmann's 2018 Process-based CBT volume and their 2019 paper "The future of intervention science: Process-based therapy", and given its full clinical manual in Learning Process-Based Therapy.

> "PBT is not a new therapy—it is a new model of evidence-based therapy. The goal of PBT is to move toward understanding and targeting the processes of change in a given individual and to move away from nomothetic, group-based analyses that tend to miss important individual processes that may be vital for effective and efficient treatment." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, Learning Process-Based Therapy (2021), Chapter 1)

### Core claims

The shift the model asks for runs from content to function and from groups to individuals:

> "PBT emphasizes the importance of function over content" (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, Learning Process-Based Therapy (2021), Chapter 1)

The aim, in the book's own summary:

> "The aim of PBT is to best understand which core biopsychological processes to target in an individual given their specific goals and stage of intervention, and how to best do so, using functional analysis, complex network approaches, and identification of core change processes developed from evidence-based treatments (Hayes & Hofmann, 2018)." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, Learning Process-Based Therapy (2021), Chapter 1)

Three working parts carry the model. A network model maps the person's problem as interacting elements rather than a symptom of an underlying disease: "A network is made up of single parts that link together and influence one another." Functional analysis, done as a new ten-step form of diagnosis, identifies which processes maintain the problem. And treatment kernels, specific change methods, are fitted to the identified processes:

> "The basic elements of these strategies are treatment kernels—specific change methods designed to modify processes of change." (Hofmann, Hayes & Lorscheid, Learning Process-Based Therapy (2021), Chapter 10)

### How the model explains

PBT is a meta-framework: it organises rather than replaces other approaches. The book's hope is that it can accommodate any empirically based treatment kernel properly linked to evidence-based processes of change. The model's taxonomy of processes is the Extended Evolutionary Meta-Model (EEMM), which classifies change processes into six psychological dimensions (affect, cognition, attention, self, motivation, overt behavior) nested into two further levels (biophysiological and sociocultural), with variation, selection, retention and context operating in each. The diagnostic question becomes: which of these processes, in this person's network, is doing the maladaptive work, and what would make it adaptive?

### In everyday life

The model's stance generalises beyond the clinic. Someone whose low mood, isolation, doom-scrolling and poor sleep form a mutually reinforcing cluster could be formulated as a network: the cluster is mapped, hypotheses formed about which process carries the most weight (say, attention rigidly fixed on threatening news), and a kernel targeting attentional flexibility tried, then re-measured. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported client or study result.)

### In the compendium

The compendium's psychological flexibility principles carry the ACT lineage PBT grew from; the EEMM's variation-selection-retention scaffold is the standing method the compendium's operations apply to new practice-kernel writing and rewrites (not a blanket retrofit of the existing corpus).

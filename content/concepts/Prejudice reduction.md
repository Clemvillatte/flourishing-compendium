Prejudice reduction, in the research sense, is what actually happens when groups meet under the right conditions. The benchmark is Pettigrew and Tropp's 2006 meta-analysis of intergroup contact theory:

> With 713 independent samples from 515 studies, the meta-analysis finds that intergroup contact typically reduces intergroup prejudice. Multiple tests indicate that this finding appears not to result from either participant selection or publication biases, and the more rigorous studies yield larger mean effects. These contact effects typically generalize to the entire outgroup, and they emerge across a broad range of outgroup targets and contact settings.

(Pettigrew & Tropp, "A meta-analytic test of intergroup contact theory," JPSP 90(5), 2006, [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737372/))

The dataset behind this was enormous: "slightly more than 250,000 participants from 38 different countries." The later mediation meta-analysis (Pettigrew & Tropp 2008) found three routes: contact works "by (1) enhancing knowledge about the outgroup, (2) reducing anxiety about intergroup contact, and (3) increasing empathy and perspective taking," with anxiety reduction and empathy stronger than knowledge.

The compendium's [[P135 — Practice tolerance without indifference]] and [[SO046 — Guard against in-group bias]] are the personal disciplines this research supports: tolerance is not enough, and the contact has to be real.

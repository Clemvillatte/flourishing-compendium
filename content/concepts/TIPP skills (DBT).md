TIPP is a set of four body-based crisis skills from the distress tolerance module of Dialectical Behavior Therapy, developed by Marsha Linehan. The letters stand for Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing and Paired muscle relaxation. A US Department of Veterans Affairs teaching transcript calls them "the first series of distress tolerance skills" and lists the last skill as Progressive Muscle Relaxation, so the final letter is named differently across teaching materials ([VA MIRECC DBT Visual Review transcript](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/visn16/docs/DBT_Visual_Review_Part_2_Transcript.pdf)).

### The four skills

The VA flash cards give the instructions for Temperature as: "Holding your breath, put your face in a bowl of cold water, or hold a cold pack on your eyes and cheeks. Hold for 30 seconds." For intense exercise, they say to "expend your body's stored up physical energy by running, walking fast, jumping, jumping jacks, playing basketball, etc." For paired muscle relaxation, "while breathing out, say the word 'Relax' in your mind" and release the tension ([VA MIRECC DBT flash cards](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/visn16/docs/DBT_Visual_Review_Flash_Cards.pdf)).

The transcript describes paced breathing this way: "finding a rhythm where the out breath is a second or two longer than the in-breath can initiate a calming feeling in the body". It teaches paired relaxation by tensing a muscle group, relaxing it, and noticing the sensation; each group is done twice before moving on ([VA transcript](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/visn16/docs/DBT_Visual_Review_Part_2_Transcript.pdf)).

### Cautions

The same flash cards carry warnings: "Very cold water can decrease your heart rate rapidly" and "Intense exercise can raise your heartrate rapidly", each with advice to consult a health care provider before using the skill if you have a medical condition ([VA flash cards](https://www.mirecc.va.gov/visn16/docs/DBT_Visual_Review_Flash_Cards.pdf)).

### Scope

The sources are clinical teaching materials, not trials of the skills. The explanations that cold water works through the dive reflex appear in popular guides and were not tested in the materials above. The compendium's earlier note cites Linehan 1993, whose manual was not consulted for this page.

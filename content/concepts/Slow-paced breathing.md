Slow-paced breathing is the deliberate slowing of breath below the everyday rate, typically to under ten breaths per minute. A systematic review of the psycho-physiology defines its subject as "slow breathing techniques (<10 breaths/minute)" and finds that during these practices "a common trend emerges" with changes "(at near 6 b/min)" ([Zaccaro, Piarulli, Laurino et al., Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/)). The review's headline mechanism: "Slow breathing techniques promote autonomic changes increasing Heart Rate Variability and Respiratory Sinus Arrhythmia paralleled by Central Nervous System (CNS) activity modifications" ([Zaccaro et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/)).

### What slows with the breath

The vagal story is the reason the practice travels with stress management. The review concludes that slow breathing techniques "seem to promote a predominance of the parasympathetic autonomic system with respect to the sympathetic one, mediated by the vagal activity," and that "[t]he enhancement of vagal tone within the cardiovascular system is reflected by the increase of both HRV power and RSA" ([Zaccaro et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/)). Respiratory sinus arrhythmia is "consistently considered a robust index of parasympathetic activity," and the reviewed studies link "the slowing of breath rhythm to increases in RSA" ([Zaccaro et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/)). On the subjective side, the same review reports "increased comfort, relaxation, pleasantness, vigor and alertness, and reduced symptoms of arousal, anxiety, depression, anger, and confusion" ([Zaccaro et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137615/)).

### The controlled trial

The 2023 Stanford trial put the practice against a standard. In a remote randomized study, three different daily 5-min breathwork exercises were compared with an equivalent period of mindfulness meditation over 1 month; the conditions were "(1) cyclic sighing, which emphasizes prolonged exhalations; (2) box breathing, which is equal duration of inhalations, breath retentions, and exhalations; and (3) cyclic hyperventilation with retention, with longer inhalations and shorter exhalations" ([Balban, Neri, Kogon et al., Cell Reports Medicine 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/)). The result: "breathwork, especially the exhale-focused cyclic sighing, produces greater improvement in mood (p < 0.05) and reduction in respiratory rate (p < 0.05) compared with mindfulness meditation" ([Balban et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/)). Five minutes a day of the exhale-weighted form was enough to separate from mindfulness on both mood and respiratory rate.

The somatic marker hypothesis is Antonio Damasio's account of how emotion enters reasoning: decision-making is steered by marker signals that arise in the body's bioregulatory processes, some consciously felt, some not.

> "The key idea of this hypothesis is that decision-making is a process that is influenced by marker signals that arise in bioregulatory processes, including those that express themselves in emotions and feelings. This influence can occur at multiple levels of operation, some of which occur consciously, and some of which occur non-consciously." (Bechara & Damasio, [The somatic marker hypothesis: A neural theory of economic decision, 2005](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Readings/The%20somatic%20marker%20hypothesis.pdf), abstract)

### Origin

Damasio proposed the hypothesis to explain the patients with ventromedial prefrontal damage: normal intellect, abnormal emotion and feeling, disastrous decisions. The 2005 review by Bechara and Damasio restates and defends it.

### What the patients showed

The founding observation was a dissociation: the patients had "normal intellect" alongside "abnormalities in emotion and feeling, along with the abnormalities in decision-making", and "the latter observations led to the somatic marker hypothesis (Damasio, 1994; Damasio et al., 1991)" ([2005, p. 339](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Readings/The%20somatic%20marker%20hypothesis.pdf)). The hypothesis "attributes these patients' inability to make advantageous decisions in real-life to a defect in an emotional mechanism that rapidly signals the prospective consequences of an action, and accordingly assists in the selection of an advantageous response option" ([2005, p. 339](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Readings/The%20somatic%20marker%20hypothesis.pdf)). The marker signals are called somatic because "they relate to body-state structure and regulation even when they do not arise in the body proper but rather in the brain's representation of the body" (Damasio, [1996, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8941953/)). The seed's two references, Damasio 1994 and Bechara et al. 1994, are the founding works the 2005 review cites back to; the account above rests on the review's restatement.

### In everyday life

A gut sense of "don't take that deal" that arrives before any conscious accounting of its terms is what the hypothesis says a marker does: a body-based signal that frames the option before reasoning finishes. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

Research on schadenfreude — pleasure at others' misfortune — showing it erodes trust and invites reciprocal hostility. That is the seed's legacy formulation. The opened study measures global impression and avoidance intentions, not trust erosion or reciprocal hostility ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)).

## What the research shows

Schadenfreude is the malicious satisfaction and pleasure felt at someone else's misfortune ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). Stefano Pagliaro's Italian team tested what happens when this pleasure is expressed publicly and witnessed ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)).

In two studies, each with 90 undergraduates, participants read a fictional scenario about a job interview ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). Study 1 compared a rival who gloated over a third candidate's car accident with one who broke a rule and one who showed no reaction ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). Study 2 used three conditions: control, schadenfreude without competition, and schadenfreude with competition ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). Participants rated their global impression of the rival and their intentions toward him ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)).

The finding, in the authors' summary:

> Two studies consistently showed that observing someone who expresses schadenfreude is detrimental for both the impression that we form about this person and our willingness to approach him.
> — Pagliaro, Brambilla, Di Prinzio & Teresi, "Observing schadenfreude," Psicologia Sociale, 2019, §4, General discussion ([author copy](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf))

In Study 1, observers rated the gloating candidate more negatively than the neutral candidate, and the rule-breaking candidate also rated worse than neutral ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). Between the gloater and the rule-breaker, the impression difference went in the predicted direction but did not reach statistical significance ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). Participants showed a stronger intention to avoid the gloater than both the rule-breaker and the neutral candidate ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). In both studies, the authors' mediation analysis found the effect on avoidance intentions ran through the impression measure ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)).

Avoidance intentions exceeded the control condition in both competition conditions, and no statistically significant difference was detected between those two conditions ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)). The impression did shift somewhat, with less negative judgments when observers were in competition with the victim, though the authors note all were applying for the same job ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)).

Even just observing schadenfreude produces important consequences for social interactions, and those consequences are measured as impressions and intentions ([Pagliaro et al., 2019](https://www.mbrambilla.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Pagliaro-et-al-2019-Schadenfreude.pdf)).

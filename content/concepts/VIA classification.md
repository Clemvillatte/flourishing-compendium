The VIA classification is Peterson and Seligman's 2004 systematic catalogue of the traits that make up a good life. Built by Christopher Peterson and Martin Seligman with a team of 55 scientists reviewing psychological, philosophical and theological literature, it identifies 24 character strengths ([editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)) organized under six virtues — wisdom and knowledge, courage, humanity, justice, temperance and transcendence ([editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)). The classification distinguishes broad virtues from the strengths that define them ([editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)):

> "the psychological ingredients—processes or mechanisms—that define the virtues" (Peterson & Seligman 2004, p. 13, quoted in the [Frontiers in Psychology editorial on the classification, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/))

The classification groups love, kindness and social intelligence under humanity; forgiveness, humility, prudence and self-regulation under temperance; gratitude, hope, humor, appreciation of beauty and spirituality under transcendence ([editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)). The measure built on it, the VIA Inventory of Strengths, is in the [2021 editorial's](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/) words "the most popular" of the character-strengths assessments and "has been administered to 13,000,000 people worldwide." For a writer, its value is diagnostic in the old sense: a vocabulary for which of the 24 a life already expresses and which it starves.

### Where it came from

The name has a history worth knowing. VIA originally stood for "Values in Action," but the team later dropped the expansion to keep the focus on character rather than values per se; VIA now stands on its own, and in Latin it means "the path" ([Frontiers in Psychology editorial, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)). The classification was built to give the young positive-psychology research program a foundation for studying what enables and promotes good character, and Peterson and Seligman expected it to evolve: strengths could be added or deleted, redundant ones combined, and the organization under core virtues reformulated ([editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)). It has become, in the [editorial's](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/) judgment, one of the main building blocks of positive psychology.

### What the research says, and where it stops

Studies have linked character-strength profiles to health and well-being, and strengths interventions to "general and domain-specific well-being, personal resources, personal growth, performance, and optimal functioning" ([editorial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/)). The [editorial's](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8060440/) own verdict on the field is measured: it calls for further robust experimental and longitudinal research to support causal explanations and provide professionals with intervention protocols.

“VIA classification” — Peterson and Seligman, 2004.

The VIA classification is a systematic catalogue of the traits that make up a good life. Built by Christopher Peterson and Martin Seligman with a team reviewing psychological, philosophical and theological literature, it identifies 24 character strengths organized under six virtues — wisdom and knowledge, courage, humanity, justice, temperance and transcendence. The strengths serve as the route to the virtues:

> "the psychological ingredients—processes or mechanisms—that define the virtues" (Peterson & Seligman 2004, p. 13, quoted in the Frontiers in Psychology editorial on the classification, 2021)

So the virtue of humanity is reached through kindness, love and social intelligence; temperance through forgiveness, humility, prudence and self-regulation; transcendence through gratitude, hope, humor, appreciation of beauty and spirituality. The measure built on it, the VIA Inventory of Strengths, has been administered to millions of people. For a writer, its value is diagnostic in the old sense: a vocabulary for which of the 24 a life already expresses and which it starves.

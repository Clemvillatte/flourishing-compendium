The wider self is the compendium's umbrella term for the proposal that the boundary of a person is wider than the skin, so that things outside the body — a place, a river, other living beings — can be part of the self rather than objects for it. The inherited citation names Whitehead's Process and Reality, but the clearest modern statement of the idea is Arne Naess's ecological self, and the compendium's practice work is built on Naess.

### Naess's version

In his 1986 lecture "Self-realization: an ecological approach to being in the world," Naess writes that "we underestimate ourself" and "tend to confuse our 'self' with the narrow ego." With "sufficient comprehensive (all-sided) maturity, we cannot help but 'identify' our self with all living beings," and his one-sentence definition is "The ecological self of a person is that with which this person identifies" ([Naess 1995, quoted in Practise ecological self](https://scispace.com/pdf/self-realization-an-ecological-approach-to-being-in-the-jarqxwpglv.pdf)). The claim has teeth: "The requisite care flows naturally if the 'self' is widened and deepened so that protection of free nature is felt and conceived as protection of ourselves" ([Naess 1995](https://scispace.com/pdf/self-realization-an-ecological-approach-to-being-in-the-jarqxwpglv.pdf)).

His illustrations are plain. Watching a flea die in acid under his microscope, he says his empathy "was a process of identification: that 'I saw myself in the flea'." In court, a Sami man called a river "part of himself" ([Naess 1995](https://scispace.com/pdf/self-realization-an-ecological-approach-to-being-in-the-jarqxwpglv.pdf)).

### Why it matters

The concept is the structural claim behind a family of practices: identification, in Naess's sense, is what makes care for the wider self feel like self-interest rather than sacrifice. It connects forward to the compendium's ecological principle (see [[R119 — Practise ecological self]]) and sideways to Whitehead's process metaphysics, where an organism's identity is not a substance persisting through change but a pattern of relation. See also [[The quiet ego]] for the research-side account of identity that includes others without losing the self.

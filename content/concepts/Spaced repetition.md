Spaced repetition is the finding that distributing practice over time improves retention relative to massed practice — studying the same material in separated sessions rather than back to back. The evidence base is Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted and Rohrer's meta-analysis of distributed practice:

> "This review found 839 assessments of distributed practice in 317 experiments located in 184 articles." (Cepeda et al., [Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks: A review and quantitative synthesis](https://www.evullab.org/pdf/CepedaPashlerVulWixtedRohrer-PB-2006.pdf), Psychological Bulletin 132(3), 2006)

### What the review found

The meta-analysis quantified the basic effect: "The average observed benefit from distributed practice (over massed practice) in these studies was 15%". It also found that the timing interacts with the goal: "the ISI producing maximal retention increased as retention interval increased" — the optimal gap between study sessions grows with how long you want to remember.

### In everyday life

The review's own framing is practical: to remember something for a year, space the sessions further apart than to remember it for a day. A student reviewing vocabulary on Monday, Wednesday and Sunday is running the massed-versus-spaced comparison the meta-analysis pooled.

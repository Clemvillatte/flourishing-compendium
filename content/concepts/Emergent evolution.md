"Emergent evolution" — Morgan, Emergent Evolution.

Emergent evolution is the title and thesis of the comparative psychologist Conwy Lloyd Morgan's 1923 book, developed from his Gifford lectures at St Andrews. The claim: evolution does not merely reshuffle what already exists. At critical points something genuinely new appears, and the new arrivals are the interesting part of the story.

Morgan's own statement of the emphasis is compact:

> "Under what I here call emergent evolution stress is laid on this incoming of the new." (Morgan, [Emergent Evolution, ch. 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_01.html))

The concept turns on a distinction Morgan borrowed from George Henry Lewes: a resultant is an effect you could compute from the parts in advance; an emergent is not. When hydrogen and oxygen combine into water, the liquidity of the water is, in Morgan's phrase from a later lecture, "emergently new and unpredictable before the event" ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html)). What emerges, Morgan insisted, is never a new substance out of nothing; it is a new pattern of relation among things that already exist:

> "If it be asked: What is it that you claim to be emergent? — the brief reply is: Some new kind of relation." ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html))

The course of events, on this reading, is continuous in its persistence and novelty in its direction: "emergent evolution seeks to interpret, on the one hand, the persistence and continuity of natural events, and, on the other hand, progressive advance with novelty" ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html)). And the steps, however dramatic they look in hindsight, are not separate miracles: "the emergent step, though it may seem more or less saltatory, is best regarded as a qualitative change of direction, or critical turningpoint, in the course of events" ([ch. 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_01.html)). Life from matter, mind from life: each involves what came before, and none was deducible from it alone.

In the compendium, the idea backs [[P077 — Understand choice as emergent, not commanded]]: a decision, like Morgan's water, is a new relatedness among existing materials rather than a command issued from outside them.

"Emergent evolution" — Morgan, Emergent Evolution.

Emergent evolution is the title and thesis of the comparative psychologist Conwy Lloyd Morgan's 1923 book, developed from his Gifford lectures at St Andrews. The claim: evolution does not merely reshuffle what already exists. At critical points something genuinely new appears, and the new arrivals are the interesting part of the story.

Morgan's own statement of the emphasis is compact:

> "Under what I here call emergent evolution stress is laid on this incoming of the new." (Morgan, [Emergent Evolution, ch. 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_01.html))

The concept turns on a distinction Morgan takes from George Henry Lewes, whom the first chapter cites: "Lewes says that the nature of emergent characters can only be learnt by experience of their occurrence; hence they are unpredictable before the event" ([ch. 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_01.html)). A resultant is an effect you could compute from the parts in advance; an emergent is not. When hydrogen and oxygen combine into water, the liquidity of the water is, in Morgan's phrase from a later lecture, "emergently new and unpredictable before the event" ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html)). Morgan describes what emerges as a new kind of relatedness among pre-existent events:

> "IF it be asked : What is it that you claim to be emergent ?-the brief reply is : Some new kind of relation." ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html))

The course of events, on this reading, is continuous in its persistence and novelty in its direction: "emergent evolution seeks to interpret, on the one hand, the persistence and continuity of natural events, and, on the other hand, progressive advance with novelty" ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html)). About the steps themselves, however dramatic they look in hindsight, Morgan writes: "the emergent step, though it may seem more or less saltatory, is best regarded as a qualitative change of direction, or critical turningpoint, in the course of events" ([ch. 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_01.html)). Morgan's own examples run from chemistry to mind, which he calls "an emergent quality which expresses new relatedness of the conscious order" ([lecture 3](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Morgan/Morgan_1923/Morgan02_03.html)).

The compendium applies Morgan's language of new relatedness to [[P077 — Understand choice as emergent, not commanded]]. The application is an interpretive analogy: a decision, like Morgan's water, is presented as a new relatedness among existing materials rather than a command issued from outside them.

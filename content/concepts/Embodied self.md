The embodied self is the self as grounded in the body's own signals. A review in Psychological Research puts the thesis:

> "Here we review recent evidence suggesting that the birth, maintenance, and loss of the abstract concept of 'self' is deeply tied to interoception, the sense of internal physiological signals." (Monti, Porciello, Panasiti and Aglioti, [The inside of me: interoceptive constraints on the concept of self](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9674731/), Psychological Research, 2022)

Interoception is "the sense of the physiological condition of the body (Craig, 2002)" (same source). The review's conclusion runs stronger than ordinary embodiment: the self-concept is "deeply embodied", and "whenever we think of ourselves, we think with our body and not just with our mind." (same source)

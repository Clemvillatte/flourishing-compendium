The courage to act against impulse is the strength to do what reason or duty calls for while inclination pushes the other way. The phrase is the compendium's own heading; the seed names Kant's Groundwork, and the passage it rests on is Kant's account of acting from duty against inclination.

### Kant's ground

Kant names courage among the qualities of temperament, and immediately limits it:

> "courage, resolution, perseverance, as qualities of temperament, are undoubtedly good and desirable in many respects; but these gifts of nature may also become extremely bad and mischievous if the will which is to make use of them, and which, therefore, constitutes what is called character, is not good." (Kant, Fundamental Principles of the Metaphysic of Morals, trans. T. K. Abbott, [Project Gutenberg #5682](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5682/5682-h/5682-h.htm))

What makes the will good is its capacity to act on the law rather than on inclination:

> "Now an action done from duty must wholly exclude the influence of inclination and with it every object of the will, so that nothing remains which can determine the will except objectively the law, and subjectively pure respect for this practical law, and consequently the maxim that I should follow this law even to the thwarting of all my inclinations." (same work)

His example is a person with every reason to want death, who keeps living anyway:

> "if adversity and hopeless sorrow have completely taken away the relish for life; if the unfortunate one, strong in mind, indignant at his fate rather than desponding or dejected, wishes for death, and yet preserves his life without loving it- not from inclination or fear, but from duty- then his maxim has a moral worth." (same work)

### In the compendium

The compendium applies the heading to the emotional impulses of ordinary life, not only to questions of duty.

- [[K038 — If there is a remedy, why be angry; if there is none, what use is anger]] — the verse that asks what anger is for, whether or not trouble can be remedied
- [[R005 — Practise forgiveness]] — Tutu's argument that forgiveness is "the best form of self-interest", made against the pull of resentment
- [[SP058 — Practice tenacity]] — Spinoza's courage (animositas), the desire to preserve one's being by the dictates of reason alone
- [[BE022 — Learn to use opposite action — do the opposite of what the emotion urges]] — Linehan's definition of opposite action, acting against the emotional urge

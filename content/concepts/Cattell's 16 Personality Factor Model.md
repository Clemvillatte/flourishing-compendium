Cattell's 16 Personality Factor Model is Raymond Cattell's factor-analytic answer to what personality is made of: sixteen primary source traits, measurable by questionnaire, that combine into the surface behaviours everyone can see. The factor list, as the standard textbook summary has it:

> "Cattell (1957) identified 16 factors or dimensions of personality: warmth, reasoning, emotional stability, dominance, liveliness, rule-consciousness, social boldness, sensitivity, vigilance, abstractedness, privateness, apprehension, openness to change, self-reliance, perfectionism, and tension. He developed a personality assessment based on these 16 factors, called the 16PF." ([OpenStax, Psychology 2e, 11.7](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/11-7-trait-theorists))

### What the model carries

The method is the model's real idea. Cattell took the trait vocabulary, had thousands of people rated, and factor-analysed the correlations; what clustered on the surface he called surface traits, and the underlying variables recovered by the analysis he called source traits, the stable structure generating the visible clusters. The scoring is dimensional by design: "Instead of a trait being present or absent, each dimension is scored over a continuum, from high to low." No one is a warm person or a cold one; each person occupies a point on each of sixteen lines. The model's descendants, above all the five-factor model, kept the method and disagreed about the count, which is itself the factor-analytic lesson: the number of basic dimensions is something the data argues about, not something intuition settles. My principle AT042 carries a distant applied echo, in that the factors include ones (tension, apprehension, emotional stability) that shift measurably with states like sleep loss. A compendium exercise drawn from the model is to describe a familiar person twice, once by surface behaviours and once by guessing which underlying dimensions produce them. What the model does not give: causal explanation of the traits; it locates a person in a structure, and says little about how the structure got there.

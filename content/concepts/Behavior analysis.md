Behavior analysis is the tradition founded on B. F. Skinner's experimental analysis of behavior. Its explanatory core is the three-term contingency: "a stimulus (the light) is the occasion upon which a response (stretching the neck) is followed by reinforcement (with food)"; Skinner adds, "We must specify all three terms" ([Skinner, Science and Human Behavior, ch. VII, p. 108](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)). This account emphasises environmental circumstances, reinforcement history and the future probability of responses ([Skinner, Science and Human Behavior, ch. V, p. 62](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf); [Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)).

### The operant

The unit of analysis is not a single act but the operant, a class of responses defined by its consequences: "The word 'operant' will be used to describe this class. The term emphasizes the fact that the behavior operates upon the environment to generate consequences" ([Skinner 1953, ch. V, p. 65](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)). Reinforcement cannot change a response that has already occurred; what changes is "the future probability of responses in the same class" ([Skinner 1953, ch. V, p. 87](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)). Skinner compares operant conditioning with evolutionary selection: "Operants grow strong because they are followed by important consequences in the life of the individual" ([Skinner 1953, ch. V, p. 90](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/ScienceHumanBehavior.pdf)).

### Readings and criticisms

Psychological behaviorism aims to explain human and animal behavior in terms of external physical stimuli, responses, learning histories and reinforcements, and the encyclopedia entry names Skinner's work on schedules of reinforcement as its fullest expression ([Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)). Chomsky's 1959 review of Verbal Behavior argued that children's rapid acquisition of language is underdetermined by reinforcement history ([Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)). Other critics hold that behavior depends on how the individual represents or interprets the environment, and U. T. Place argued that qualia resist behavioral analysis ([Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)). Graham describes behaviorism as no longer dominant in psychology, while noting continuing behaviorist contributions to behavior therapy and animal-learning research ([Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/behaviorism/)).

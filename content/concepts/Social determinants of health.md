Social determinants of health are the social conditions that shape how long and how well people live. The World Health Organization defines them this way:

> "Social determinants of health – broadly defined as the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age, and people's access to power, money and resources – have a powerful influence on health inequities." ([WHO](https://www.who.int/health-topics/social-determinants-of-health))

The same WHO page lists as examples access to quality housing, education, social protection and job opportunities, along with macroeconomic and urbanization policies and governance. It adds that "research shows that these social determinants can outweigh genetic influences or healthcare access in terms of influencing health" ([WHO](https://www.who.int/health-topics/social-determinants-of-health)).

### Marmot's argument

Michael Marmot's 2005 Lancet paper begins from a gap in numbers: a spread of life expectancy of 48 years among countries and 20 years or more within countries, which he says "is not inevitable." The abstract states that "a burgeoning volume of research identifies social factors at the root of much of these inequalities in health," and that these determinants matter for communicable and non-communicable disease alike ([Marmot, 2005, Lancet 365(9464):1099-1104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15781105/)). In the paper's text he calls for "a third major thrust" alongside health systems and relief of poverty: "to take action on the social determinants of health," with the broader aim of "improving the circumstances in which people live and work" ([Marmot, 2005, UCL eprint](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/676/1/Microsoft_Word_-_MM_Lancet_365_1099_UCL_eprints.pdf)).

### The Commission's report

The Commission on Social Determinants of Health, set up by WHO in 2005 and chaired by Marmot, delivered its final report in July 2008 ([WHO Commission page](https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/equity-and-health/world-report-on-social-determinants-of-health-equity/commission-on-social-determinants-of-health); [WHO EB124/9](https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/eb124/b124_9-en.pdf)). The report's overview says health inequities "arise because of the circumstances in which people grow, live, work, and age, and the systems put in place to deal with illness," and that these conditions "are, in turn, shaped by political, social, and economic forces" ([WHO final report overview](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-IER-CSDH-08.1)). The Commission's three overarching recommendations were to improve daily living conditions, to tackle the inequitable distribution of power, money and resources, and to measure and understand the problem and assess the impact of action ([WHO Commission page](https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/equity-and-health/world-report-on-social-determinants-of-health-equity/commission-on-social-determinants-of-health)). The Executive Board paper summarising the report says such health inequities "mostly point to policy failure" ([WHO EB124/9](https://apps.who.int/gb/ebwha/pdf_files/eb124/b124_9-en.pdf)).

The principles listed apply this literature to flourishing: A006 (Expect flourishing to require external goods), P138 (Take responsibility for the social conditions of growth) and SO075 (Use systems-level intervention for social suffering).

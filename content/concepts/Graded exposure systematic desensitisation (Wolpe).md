Graded exposure is the practice of approaching a feared situation in small, ordered steps, from the least disturbing to the most. Its clinical form, systematic desensitisation, was developed by Joseph Wolpe between 1948 and 1956 and published in Psychotherapy by Reciprocal Inhibition (1958); its modern descendants, like Foa's prolonged exposure for post-traumatic stress disorder, keep the graded approach while dropping some of the scaffolding ([Cambridge, BJPsych Advances](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-advances/article/psychotherapy-by-reciprocal-inhibition-wolpes-unique-legacy-to-the-evolution-of-cognitivebehavioural-therapy/A34DE39AC4C20EA598209C8150F9117F); [Foa and McLean, Annu Rev Clin Psychol 12 (2016)](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-021815-093533)).

### Wolpe's mechanism

Wolpe's principle of reciprocal inhibition is stated in his own words: "if a response antagonistic to anxiety can be made to occur in the presence of anxiety-evoking stimuli so that it is accompanied by a complete or partial suppression of the anxiety responses, the bond between these stimuli and the anxiety responses will be weakened" (Wolpe 1958, p. 71, quoted in [BJPsych Advances](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-advances/article/psychotherapy-by-reciprocal-inhibition-wolpes-unique-legacy-to-the-evolution-of-cognitivebehavioural-therapy/A34DE39AC4C20EA598209C8150F9117F)). The antagonistic response was usually deep muscle relaxation, often Jacobson's technique, and the patient worked up an anxiety hierarchy, "the most disturbing item is placed at the top, and the least disturbing at the bottom," mostly in imagination ([BJPsych Advances](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-advances/article/psychotherapy-by-reciprocal-inhibition-wolpes-unique-legacy-to-the-evolution-of-cognitivebehavioural-therapy/A34DE39AC4C20EA598209C8150F9117F); [Singapore Medical Journal](http://smj.sma.org.sg/1302/1302smj6.pdf)). Wolpe credited Mary Cover Jones, whom he reportedly dubbed "the mother of behaviour therapy," as the inspiration ([BJPsych Advances](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-advances/article/psychotherapy-by-reciprocal-inhibition-wolpes-unique-legacy-to-the-evolution-of-cognitivebehavioural-therapy/A34DE39AC4C20EA598209C8150F9117F)).

### The modern evidence, and its edge

Foa and McLean's 2016 review describes prolonged exposure's two components, in vivo exposure to trauma reminders and imaginal exposure, and reports that prolonged exposure "generally leads to rapid improvement and maintenance of gains over time, up to five years posttreatment" ([Annual Review of Clinical Psychology](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-021815-093533)). A 2008 Institute of Medicine report concluded that exposure therapy was the only PTSD treatment with sufficient evidence for its efficacy ([Annual Review of Clinical Psychology](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-021815-093533)). The edge is worth knowing: a meta-analysis of exposure therapies' effect on quality of life found exposure superior to controls at posttreatment but not at follow-up, where "the longer-term impact of exposure on QOL is unclear" ([VA meta-analysis](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/articles/article-pdf/id1635142.pdf)).

### In the compendium

The compendium scales the technique to self-help size. BE027 teaches graded exposure directly, start small and build up; BE025 names avoidance as the maintaining factor the approach undoes; BE026 gives the exposure principles. The cross-tradition parallels are marked as such: A011's courage (andreia) is the Aristotelian virtue version, B029's gradual training the Buddhist one, S063's refusal to fear the uncontrollable the Stoic one, and B066's watching states arise and pass the contemplative mechanism the hierarchy exploits. P163's creative hopelessness supplies the letting-go, and K117 is the compendium's kernel form.

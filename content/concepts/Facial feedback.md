The facial feedback hypothesis holds that the face acts as an input to emotion as well as an output of it: "people’s affective responses can be influenced by their own facial expression (e.g., smiling, pouting), even when their expression did not result from their emotional experiences" ([Wagenmakers et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1745691616674458)).

### The classic study, and the replication

The famous test was the pen study. Strack, Martin and Stepper had participants hold a pen in the mouth in ways that "either inhibited or facilitated the muscles typically associated with smiling without requiring subjects to pose in a smiling face", and "Subjects reported more intense humor responses when cartoons were presented under facilitating conditions than under inhibiting conditions" ([Strack, Martin and Stepper 1988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3379579/)). A 17-lab direct replication, meta-analytically combined, found a much smaller effect on the funniness ratings than the original: "The original Strack et al. (1988) study reported a rating difference of 0.82 units on a 10-point Likert scale", but "Our meta-analysis revealed a rating difference of 0.03 units with a 95% confidence interval ranging from -0.11 to 0.16" ([Wagenmakers et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1745691616674458)). The pen-measured effect in that replication was near zero.

### Where that leaves the hypothesis

The later Many Smiles Collaboration study, a "preregistered, multicentre study" with "Data from n = 3,878 participants spanning 19 countries", found that "a facial mimicry and voluntary facial action task could both amplify and initiate feelings of happiness", while "evidence of facial feedback effects was less conclusive when facial feedback was manipulated unobtrusively via a pen-in-mouth task" ([Coles et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36266452/)). The task split matters: mimicry and voluntary facial action supported effects on feelings of happiness, whereas the pen-in-mouth findings were less conclusive. So the honest formulation is the seed's: evidence mixed, phrase with care.

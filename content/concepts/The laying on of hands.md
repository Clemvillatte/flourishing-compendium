The laying on of hands is a gesture from the Christian tradition: one person places hands on another as part of healing, blessing, commissioning or receiving the Spirit. In the Gospel texts it is not a symbol of something absent but an ordinary means through which healing and commissioning are said to happen. The gesture has kept its place in ordination rites to the present, which is why it is a durable case of embodied practice — meaning carried by touch rather than words.

### The scriptural pattern

The gesture runs through both healings and blessings in the Gospels and Acts. Jairus asks Jesus directly for it: "My little daughter is at the point of death. Please come and lay your hands on her, that she may be made healthy, and live" ([Mark 5:23, World English Bible, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+5%3A23&version=WEB)). Paul does it in Malta: "The father of Publius lay sick of fever and dysentery. Paul entered in to him, prayed, and laying his hands on him, healed him" ([Acts 28:8, WEB, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Acts+28%3A8&version=WEB)). The risen Jesus' instruction extends the practice to the community: "they will lay hands on the sick, and they will recover" ([Mark 16:18, WEB, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+16%3A18&version=WEB)).

It is also the rite of commissioning. The elders' hands pass on a gift: "Don't neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you by prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the elders" ([1 Timothy 4:14, WEB, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Timothy+4%3A14&version=WEB)). Hebrews lists it among the elementary teachings: "of the teaching of baptisms, of laying on of hands, of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment" ([Hebrews 6:2, WEB, Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Hebrews+6%3A2&version=WEB)).

### Why it matters to flourishing

I pair the gesture with two lines of evidence on touch. The behavioral line: in a diary study of 404 adults interviewed nightly for 14 days, receiving a hug on conflict days was associated with smaller drops in positive mood and smaller rises in negative mood ([Murphy et al. 2018, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0203522)). The physiological line: women who had ten minutes of partner physical contact before a standard stress test showed lower cortisol and heart-rate responses, and the authors conclude the findings "imply a direct protective effect of touch on stress-related neurobiological systems" ([Ditzen et al. 2007, Psychoneuroendocrinology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17499441/)).

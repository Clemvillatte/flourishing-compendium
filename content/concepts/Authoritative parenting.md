“Authoritative parenting” — Baumrind, 1991.

Baumrind's longitudinal programme of research sorted parenting into types along two dimensions: demandingness and responsiveness. Authoritative parenting is the combination of high demandingness with high responsiveness.

### What Baumrind found

The 1991 paper reports the Family Socialization and Developmental Competence programme, which followed families across ratings taken at ages 4, 9 and 15. The abstract states the finding:

> "Parenting types were identified that differ on the bases of commitment and balance of demandingness and responsiveness. Authoritative parents who are highly demanding and highly responsive were remarkably successful in protecting their adolescents from problem drug use, and in generating competence. Authoritative upbringing, although sufficient, is not a necessary condition to produce competent children." (Baumrind, 1991, [The Journal of Early Adolescence](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0272431691111004))

The sample is described in the same abstract as 139 adolescents and their parents from a predominantly affluent, well-educated, Caucasian population. That scope limits what the study can be read as showing.

### The two-dimensional shape

Baumrind's typology is organised around two axes. Demandingness is the parent's demand for mature behaviour, and responsiveness is the parent's warmth, acceptance and involvement. Authoritative parenting sits in the high-demanding, high-responsive quadrant, against authoritarian (high demandingness, low responsiveness) and permissive (low demandingness, high responsiveness). Maccoby and Martin's extension added the neglectful quadrant.

The result Baumrind reports is not that authoritative parenting is the only route to competence. The abstract says authoritative upbringing is sufficient but not necessary.

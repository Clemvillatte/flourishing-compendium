"Conversation as method" is this compendium's framing for a precaution Jane Addams describes in Twenty Years at Hull-House: when addressing an audience on the Settlement and its vicinity, she took a neighbor with her, so that any hasty generalization would be curbed by the presence of an auditor who knew the conditions more intimately than she could hope to do ([Addams, Twenty Years at Hull-House, 1910, Gutenberg #1325](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1325)). The book's illustration: visitors to the new Settlement questioned a young woman guest minutely concerning "these people" without once suspecting that they were talking to one who had been identified with the neighborhood from childhood ([Addams 1910](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1325)).

> "I never addressed a Chicago audience on the subject of the Settlement and its vicinity without inviting a neighbor to go with me, that I might curb any hasty generalization by the consciousness that I had an auditor who knew the conditions more intimately than I could hope to do." — [Addams, Twenty Years at Hull-House, 1910](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1325)

### The discipline

The method is a discipline against hasty generalization: the speaker's claim is tested in the room by the person who knows the conditions more intimately ([Addams 1910](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1325)).

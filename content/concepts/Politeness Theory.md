Brown and Levinson's model explains linguistic politeness through attention to face: positive face concerns approval and closeness, while negative face concerns freedom from imposition ([Penelope Brown, 2015, p. 327](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1719314_10/component/file_2152013/content)).

> "Politeness is ascribed to a speech act, or to an interactional move" (Brown, [p. 328](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1719314_10/component/file_2152013/content); excerpt).

The model distinguishes avoiding an act, speaking indirectly off record, speaking baldly on record, and using positive or negative redress; its assessment considers power, social distance and the imposition involved ([p. 327](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1719314_10/component/file_2152013/content)).

### Limits

Brown reviews challenges to the model's universality and its ranking of strategies ([p. 328](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1719314_10/component/file_2152013/content)). Indirectness is not always judged most polite, and the meaning of an utterance depends on the interaction rather than on a permanently polite form of words ([p. 328](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_1719314_10/component/file_2152013/content)).

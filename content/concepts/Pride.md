"Pride"

Pride is the emotion the traditions cannot agree about. The wisdom tradition treats it as the fall itself, the saying "pride goes before destruction" carrying the warning; Aristotle's ethics makes it the crown of the virtues, a self-estimate that is both high and earned.

> "Pride, then, seems to be a sort of crown of the virtues; for it makes them greater, and it is not found without them." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.3, [Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html))
>
> "Now the proud man, since he deserves most, must be good in the highest degree; for the better man always deserves more, and the best man most." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.3, [Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html))

The condition Aristotle puts on the crown is the whole argument: "Now the man is thought to be proud who thinks himself worthy of great things, being worthy of them." The estimate has to fit the worth, and the two halves of the modern research program, authentic and hubristic pride, split along exactly that seam.

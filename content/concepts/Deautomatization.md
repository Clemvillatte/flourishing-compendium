Arthur Deikman's 1966 paper "De-automatization and the Mystic Experience" opens by announcing "a psychological model of the mystic experience", one "conceptualized as one of de-automatization" ([Deikman 1966, author's site](https://www.deikman.com/deautomat.html)). The model starts from automatization: practice makes perception and action drop out of awareness.

> "Automatization is assumed to be a basic process in which the repeated exercise of an action or of a perception results in the disappearance from consciousness of its intermediate steps." — Deikman, "Experimental Meditation" ([author's site](https://www.deikman.com/experimental.html))

The APA Dictionary gives the everyday version: a skill becomes "routine" and requires "little if any conscious effort or direction" ([APA Dictionary](https://dictionary.apa.org/automatization)). Deikman proposes that meditation runs this process in reverse:

> "De-automatization is the undoing of automatization, presumably by reinvestment of actions and percepts with attention." — Deikman, "Experimental Meditation" ([author's site](https://www.deikman.com/experimental.html))

In meditation the percept receives intense attention while abstract categorization is set aside:

> "the active intellectual style is replaced by a receptive perceptual mode." — Deikman 1966, "De-automatization and the Mystic Experience" ([author's site](https://www.deikman.com/deautomat.html))

The result Deikman describes is a temporary freshness:

> "colors may appear to have gained (temporarily) a new richness and vividness so that the natural world is seen in a 'fresh' state." — Deikman, "Bimodal Consciousness" / Implications page ([author's site](https://www.deikman.com/implications.html))

He is careful about what this is:

> "de-automatization is not a regression but rather an undoing of a pattern in order to permit a new and perhaps more advanced experience." — Deikman, "Experimental Meditation" ([author's site](https://www.deikman.com/experimental.html))

It is a hypothesis built from mystic literature and his own experiments, not a proof of unmediated access to reality. The 1966 paper's conclusion states the model in full: a mystic experience "is the production of an unusual state of consciousness", brought about "by a de-automatization of hierarchically ordered structures of perception and cognition, structures that ordinarily conserve attentional energy for maximum efficiency in achieving the basic goals of the individual: biological survival as an organism and psychological survival as a personality" ([Deikman 1966, author's site](https://www.deikman.com/deautomat.html)).

"Klesha transmuted into wisdom" is a Tibetan Buddhist teaching about the afflictive emotions (Skt. kleśa), listed in Tibetan sources as the five poisons: desire, anger, delusion or ignorance, pride and jealousy ([Rigpa Wiki](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Five_poisons)). Longchenpa's *Precious Garland of the Four Themes of Gampopa* calls the teaching "purifying confusion into deep awareness": when the face of each emotion's own ground is recognized, the emotion releases itself as one of five aspects of deep awareness ([Beru Khyentse Rinpoche, commentary](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/vajrayana/dzogchen-advanced/commentary-on-chapter-4-of-longchenpa-s-dzogchen-text-beru-khyentse-rinpoche)).

### The five poisons and the five wisdoms

Beru Khyentse Rinpoche gives the mapping in his commentary on chapter 4, verse 80:

> "When the deceptive confusion of the five poisonous emotions purifies itself, they become five great deep awarenesses: 1. Longing desire purifies itself into individualizing deep awareness 2. Hostility purifies itself into mirror-like deep awareness 3. Naivety purifies itself into sphere of reality (dharmadhatu) deep awareness 4. Arrogance purifies itself into equalizing deep awareness 5. Envy purifies itself into accomplishing deep awareness" ([Beru Khyentse Rinpoche, commentary on chapter 4, verse 80](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/vajrayana/dzogchen-advanced/commentary-on-chapter-4-of-longchenpa-s-dzogchen-text-beru-khyentse-rinpoche))

Each emotion and its wisdom are one mental activity seen two ways: "This deep awareness is deep awareness from its facet of when it is purified, but from its facet of when it is unpurified, it is a disturbing emotion" ([same commentary](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/vajrayana/dzogchen-advanced/commentary-on-chapter-4-of-longchenpa-s-dzogchen-text-beru-khyentse-rinpoche)). The commentary also grades the treatments by vehicle: the sutra vehicle cleanses the emotions away with antidotes (meditation on unattractiveness for longing desire, love for hostility, dependent arising for naivety), the three outer tantras transform them with methods, and dzogchen lets them automatically release themselves into their own grounds, not abandoning them ([same commentary](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/vajrayana/dzogchen-advanced/commentary-on-chapter-4-of-longchenpa-s-dzogchen-text-beru-khyentse-rinpoche)).

"Klesha transmuted into wisdom" is a Tibetan Buddhist teaching about the afflictive emotions (Skt. kleśa; the "five poisons" of longing desire, hostility, naivety, arrogance and envy). The teaching says they are not permanent defects to be destroyed: when their own ground is recognized, they release themselves as the five aspects of deep awareness, the wisdoms of an awakened mind.

### The five poisons and the five wisdoms

The fullest mapping comes from the Dzogchen tradition. Longchenpa's Precious Garland of the Four Themes of Gampopa, in Beru Khyentse Rinpoche's commentary, presents it as a numbered correspondence:

> "When the deceptive confusion of the five poisonous emotions purifies itself, they become five great deep awarenesses: 1. Longing desire purifies itself into individualizing deep awareness 2. Hostility purifies itself into mirror-like deep awareness 3. Naivety purifies itself into sphere of reality (dharmadhatu) deep awareness 4. Arrogance purifies itself into equalizing deep awareness 5. Envy purifies itself into accomplishing deep awareness" ([Beru Khyentse Rinpoche, Study Buddhism](https://studybuddhism.com/en/advanced-studies/vajrayana/dzogchen-advanced/commentary-on-chapter-4-of-longchenpa-s-dzogchen-text-beru-khyentse-rinpoche))

The commentary treats each emotion and its wisdom as one mental activity seen two ways: "This deep awareness is deep awareness from its facet of when it is purified, but from its facet of when it is unpurified, it is a disturbing emotion." The graded approaches also matter. Sutra vehicles cleanse the emotions away with antidotes (meditation on unattractiveness for desire, love for hostility, dependent arising for naivety); outer tantras transform them with methods; the unsurpassable vehicle of dzogchen lets them automatically release themselves into their own grounds, unabandoned.

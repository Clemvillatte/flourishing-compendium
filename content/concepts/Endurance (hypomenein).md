"Endurance (hypomenein)" — Stoics, [[T014 — Marcus Aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]], Meditations.

Endurance (hypomenein) is the capacity to stay present under what is hard rather than flee it or escalate it. The Stoic template is Marcus Aurelius, whose Meditations keep returning to the question of what a person has already passed through and can still bear. His self-audit in Book 5 turns endurance into a source of courage: the count of things already survived.

> "Remember withal through how many things thou hast already passed, and how many thou hast been able to endure; so that now the legend of thy life is full, and thy charge is accomplished." (Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 5.25, George Long translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2680/pg2680.txt))

### What the endurance is for

The passage does not glorify suffering; it reminds the reader of accumulated evidence. My principle AF041 carries the modern training edge: distress tolerance, the capacity to bear intense affect without making it worse. What Marcus adds is the retrospective angle — a person in hardship can look back at their own record and act from it.

The taxonomy of desires is Epicurus's classification, in his Letter to Menoeceus, of what we want. The passage is a sorting tool: it separates what a person needs from what they merely want.

> "We must consider that of desires some are natural, others vain, and of the natural some are necessary and others merely natural; and of the necessary some are necessary for happiness, others for the repose of the body, and others for very life."
> — Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus §127, Bailey 1926 translation, via [EpicureanFriends](https://www.epicureanfriends.com/wcf/menoeceus/)

The classification proceeds in stages. First, a desire is either natural or vain. Second, a natural desire is either necessary or merely natural. Necessary desires split again into three: some are needed for happiness, some for the body's ease, and some for life itself.

### How the classification is used

The Letter gives one further instruction about the natural. Epicurus writes that "all that is natural is easy to be obtained, but that which is superfluous is hard", and that "bread and water produce the highest pleasure, when one who needs them puts them to his lips".

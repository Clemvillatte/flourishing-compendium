The polyphonic self is the compendium's name for a claim Bakhtin makes about novels and the compendium transfers to persons. In Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics (1963), Bakhtin argues that Dostoevsky invented a new kind of novel in which the characters are not instruments of the author's argument but independent voices the author cannot silence or reduce. He calls the form polyphonic:

> "A plurality of independent and unmerged voices and consciousnesses, a genuine polyphony of fully valid voices is in fact the chief characteristic of Dostoevsky's novels."

([Bakhtin, Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics, trans. Caryl Emerson, University of Minnesota Press 1984, p. 6](https://books.google.com/books/about/Problems_of_Dostoevsky_s_Poetics.html?id=MkXAzSbkU8QC); corroborated by [Wikipedia's chapter summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problems_of_Dostoevsky%27s_Poetics).)

### From the novel to the person

The transfer the compendium makes is Bakhtin's own method read in reverse. A Dostoevsky character is, in Bakhtin's formulation, an object of authorial discourse and at the same time a subject of its own directly signifying discourse ([Wikipedia, Problems of Dostoevsky's Poetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Problems_of_Dostoevsky%27s_Poetics)). Applied to a person, that means the parts of a self are not characters in a story one part is telling. They are voices with their own worlds, and the mistake the concept names is trying to reduce them to one.

The concept is one of several the compendium uses for the non-unitary self. William James had already noted that "a man has as many social selves as there are individuals who recognize him and carry an image of him in his mind" ([The Principles of Psychology, Vol. I, ch. X, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57628/57628-h/57628-h.htm)), which is the empirical side of the same point. The polyphonic version adds what the Jamesian version lacks: a way of holding the parts together without merging them, since a plurality "with equal rights and each with its own world, combine but are not merged in the unity of the event" ([Bakhtin, quoted in scielo](https://www.scielo.br/j/bak/a/GK9yJVbF67TqNLbRDdwVJGw/?format=pdf&lang=en)).

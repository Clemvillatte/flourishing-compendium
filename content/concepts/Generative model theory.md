In Andy Clark's account of predictive processing, a generative model represents how interacting causes could produce observed sensory inputs. The account proposes that perception and action involve matching incoming signals with top-down predictions.

> "A generative model, in this quite specific sense, aims to capture the statistical structure of some set of observed inputs by tracking (one might say, by schematically recapitulating) the causal matrix responsible for that very structure. A good generative model for vision would thus seek to capture the ways in which observed lower-level visual responses are generated by an interacting web of causes – for example, the various aspects of a visually presented scene." (Andy Clark, [Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of cognitive science](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Whatever%20next.pdf), Behavioral and Brain Sciences 36(3), 2013, section 1.1, printed p. 182)

### What the framework claims

Clark proposes that mismatches between predictions and sensory input guide changes to the model. His framework connects perception, learning and action through attempts to reduce those mismatches.

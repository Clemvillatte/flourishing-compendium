In the Daodejing's eighth chapter, the highest excellence is compared to water: water benefits all things and occupies, without striving, the low place that all men dislike, and so its way is near to the Tao. ([Daodejing 8, Legge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

> "The highest excellence is like (that of) water. The excellence of water appears in its benefiting all things, and in its occupying, without striving (to the contrary), the low place which all men dislike. Hence (its way) is near to (that of) the Tao." (Daodejing 8, Legge translation, [Project Gutenberg ebook #216](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

The chapter continues through the excellences of residence, mind (abysmal stillness), associations, government, conduct of affairs, and timeliness of movement, and ends: when the one with the highest excellence does not wrangle about his low position, no one finds fault with him. ([Daodejing 8, Legge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt))

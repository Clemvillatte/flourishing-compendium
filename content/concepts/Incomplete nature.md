“Incomplete nature” — Deacon, Incomplete Nature.

Terrence Deacon's title names a wager: that the things we most want to explain — purpose, value, meaning, mind — are intrinsically incomplete, constituted by what is not there, and that this absence is not a defect in our theories but a causal feature of the world.

> "What is absent is responsible for the causal power of organization (p. 195)." — [Deacon, Incomplete Nature, quoted in Logan, Information 3(3) (2012), 290–306, full text](https://openresearch.ocadu.ca/id/eprint/843/1/Logan_DeaconPrecisReview_2012.pdf)

### How absence does work

Deacon's route is constraint: "Constraints are related to absences in the sense that they prevent certain things from happening." A constraint is a missing possibility, and it is exactly what lets heat become work, noise become message, and process become organism. His hierarchy runs from thermodynamics through self-organising morphodynamics to teleodynamics, which "can be understood as characterizing the distinguishing dynamics of life" — a system that exists because of the consequences of its own continuance. The payoff for the essays on flourishing is a physical argument for taking lack seriously: desire, need, and aspiration are not noise added onto a complete machine but the way certain kinds of organisation are built.

"Participate in the world's becoming" is a reading of Whitehead's Process and Reality. On this account the world is an ongoing production rather than a finished collection of things, and every actual thing is one of the acts by which that production continues. Whitehead states the scheme in Part I, where the "Category of the Ultimate" replaces Aristotle's category of primary substance with a formula of advance:

> The ultimate metaphysical principle is the advance from disjunction to conjunction, creating a novel entity other than the entities given in disjunction. The novel entity is at once the togetherness of the 'many' which it finds, and also it is one among the disjunctive 'many' which it leaves; it is a novel entity, disjunctively among the many entities which it synthesizes. The many become one, and are increased by one. (Whitehead, Process and Reality, corrected ed., Part I, ch. II "The Categoreal Scheme", p. 21)

The basic unit of reality is an act of synthesis: something gathers the many things it inherits, becomes one new thing, and is then handed on to whatever comes next. Two of the Categories of Explanation make the participatory side of this explicit. The first states that the world itself is a process of such becomings; the fourth states that whatever exists is available to every subsequent becoming:

> That the actual world is a process, and that the process is the becoming of actual entities. Thus actual entities are creatures; they are also termed 'actual occasions.' (Whitehead, Process and Reality, corrected ed., Part I, ch. II, p. 22)

> In other words, it belongs to the nature of a 'being' that it is a potential for every 'becoming.' This is the 'principle of relativity.' (Whitehead, Process and Reality, corrected ed., Part I, ch. II, p. 22)

The fourth category speaks of potential, so the claim is one of availability rather than felt involvement: to exist at all is already to be implicated in the advance, whether or not one notices. Whitehead describes an actual entity's formation as an "adventure of self-formation": the actual entity is the product of the interplay of physical pole with mental pole, "and extensive relations mould qualitative content and objectifications of other particulars into a coherent finite experience" (Process and Reality, corrected ed., Part IV, ch. III "Flat Loci", p. 308). He is describing the formation of an actual occasion here, and consciousness is, in his words, generally negligible within it; any application to a human life is an extension of the scheme rather than something the passages themselves assert.

The passage the compendium's principle rests on concerns what the process never does:

> The notion of nature as an organic extensive community omits the equally essential point of view that nature is never complete. It is always passing beyond itself. This is the creative advance of nature. (Whitehead, Process and Reality, corrected ed., Part IV, ch. I "Coordinate Division", p. 289)

The compendium draws an ethical invitation from this cosmology, and the invitation is marked as an application. Since the world keeps passing beyond itself, a person is one strand of the advance rather than a finished product placed into a finished world: a place where an inherited world is gathered into something that did not exist before, and that then becomes part of what others inherit. On this reading, the live questions are less "where have I gotten to?" than "what am I synthesizing from what I was given, and what am I passing on?"

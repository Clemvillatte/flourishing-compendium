Attention management is the practice of governing where attention goes: choosing what receives it, protecting it from capture, and handling what switching between tasks costs. The compendium's seed evidence for the switching cost is Sophie Leroy's 2009 finding that attention does not transfer cleanly from one task to the next; cognitions about the first task persist into the second, and the second task's performance suffers while they do.

> Time pressure is needed on a completed task to reduce attention residue when people switch to a next task. — Leroy, ["Why is it so hard to do my work? The challenge of attention residue when switching between work tasks" (2009), Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 109(2), 168-181](https://www.scribd.com/document/856551539/c-Coek-Info-Why-is-It-So-Hard-to-Do-My-Work-the-Challenge-of-a-1)

### What the experiments found

Leroy ran two experiments on switching between work tasks. Finishing the first task before switching was not, by itself, enough to disengage from it; what helped was finishing under time pressure, which facilitated the ending of the cognitions associated with the completed task and contributed to higher performance on the next task. The management lesson the paper supports is about transitions: a task ends for attention when it is closed, and closure has conditions. The compendium's fuller treatment of the mechanism is on the Attention residue page.

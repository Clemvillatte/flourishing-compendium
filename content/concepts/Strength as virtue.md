In the fifth book of The Gay Science, Nietzsche looks at the moral vocabulary of his age, its humane and gentle words, and sees weakness dressed up as virtue. ([Nietzsche, The Gay Science V.377, Common translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52881/pg52881.txt))

> "He may parade it as his virtue; there is no doubt whatever that weakness makes people gentle, alas, so gentle, so just, so inoffensive, so 'humane'!" (Nietzsche, The Gay Science, Book V, aphorism 377, Common translation, [Project Gutenberg #52881](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52881/pg52881.txt))

The same passage counts the other side: those who love danger, war and adventure, who do not make compromises, and who count themselves among the conquerors. The virtue on offer here is strength rather than gentleness. ([Nietzsche, The Gay Science V.377, Common translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52881/pg52881.txt))

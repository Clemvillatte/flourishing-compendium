Breakdown as breakthrough is the shared insight of Heidegger and Jaspers that failure can be the moment the hidden structure of a life first becomes visible. Heidegger's version concerns equipment: a tool in use is invisible, and only when it fails does the whole it belonged to light up. "When an assignment has been disturbed—when something is unusable for some purpose—then the assignment becomes explicit," and "the context of equipment is lit up, not as something never seen before, but as a totality constantly sighted beforehand in circumspection. With this totality, however, the world announces itself." ([Heidegger, Being and Time](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.105.html))

### What the breakthrough carries

The pattern generalises beyond tools. Jaspers gives it the existential form: the boundary situations (death, suffering, struggle, guilt) are the walls we founder against, and the foundering (Scheitern) is where a self that was coasting on the everyday either breaks or wakes. The convergence is the point: what functions is invisible, and only its failure makes the structure of one's world an object of attention at all. This is why my principle E092 carries the applied reading: practise shipwreck, meaning deliberate small-scale encounters with failure rather than a lifetime of arrangements that postpone it. A compendium exercise drawn from the concept is to take one current breakdown (a plan, a tool, a relationship that stopped working) and write down what its smooth functioning had been hiding. What the concept does not give: a promise that breakdown always improves anything; the world announcing itself is a moment of visibility, and what is done with it is a separate question.

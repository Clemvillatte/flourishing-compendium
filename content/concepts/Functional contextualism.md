Functional contextualism is the philosophy of science behind Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT). Hayes and colleagues describe it as "a type of psychological pragmatism that extends Skinner's radical behaviorism" and link it to "the prediction and influence, with precision, scope, and depth, of whole organisms interacting in and with a context considered historically and situationally" ([Hayes et al., Behavior Therapy, via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3635495/)).

### Truth as successful working

The core move is a pragmatic truth criterion. A paper in The Counseling Psychologist says that in this view "'truth' is a matter of 'successful working' or accomplishing an analytic purpose, not correspondence between models and ontological reality". Because a truth criterion "requires a goal to be applied (one has to answer 'working toward what?')", the goal in functional contextualism is "the prediction-and-influence of psychological events" ([Hayes, Pistorello and Levin, 2012, p. 979](https://www.apa.org/education-career/ce/acceptance-commitment.pdf)).

The Association for Contextual Behavioral Science puts the same idea plainly: "the truth and meaning of an idea lies in its function or utility, not in how well it is said to mirror reality" ([ACBS, About Contextualism](https://contextualscience.org/book/export/html/202)).

### Why the goal matters for values

The Counseling Psychologist paper draws a clinical consequence. There is "minimal interest in what is 'true' in any ontological sense" when clients struggle to decide whether their thoughts are correct, and "a great interest in workability". It adds that "when workability is the ultimate criterion, it also becomes clearer that values and goals matter." The ACBS page says ACT treats chosen values as the necessary precursor to assessing workability "because values specify the criteria for the application of workability" ([ACBS, Philosophical roots](https://contextualscience.org/book/export/html/839)).

### Two kinds of contextualism

A Behavior Therapy article distinguishes descriptive contextualism (it names hermeneutics, narrative psychology and social constructionism), "which seeks an appreciation of the participants in a whole event", from functional contextualism (it names behavior analysis), "which seeks the prediction and influence of ongoing interactions between whole organisms and historically and situationally defined contexts" ([Hayes, Behavior Therapy, 2004, ACBS-hosted PDF](https://contextualscience.org/sites/default/files/318%20ACT%20RFT%20and%20the%20Third%20Wave%20BT%202004.pdf)).

### A limit worth keeping

Workability is relative to a stated goal. The same article says "there must be the clear a priori statement of an analytic goal", and that in contextualism "ultimate goals enable analysis" and "are not themselves the results of analysis". So the framework cannot, by itself, say which goals are worth having.

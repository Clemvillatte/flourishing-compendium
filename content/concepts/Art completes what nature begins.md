The formula is Aristotle's account of the relation between technē and physis: art neither replaces nature nor merely copies it. In the Physics the point carries the argument that natural things have ends. If a house were made by nature, it would be made the way it is now made by art; and then comes the sentence the concept is named for:

> "generally art in some cases completes (epiteleitai) what nature cannot bring to a finish, and in others imitates her (mimeitai). If, therefore, artificial products are for the sake of an end, so clearly also are natural products." (Aristotle, Physics 199a15-17, Barnes translation, as quoted in [Techne and Physis](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/76d1/7b1a27ea0165a26ec023fd6fd40ee2794acf.pdf))

The other home of the idea is the biological works, where generation is the model. There Aristotle gives art the role of external principle:

> "Art is the principle and form of the thing that comes to be; but it is located elsewhere than in that thing, whereas the movement of nature is located in the thing itself that comes to be." (Aristotle, Generation of Animals 735a2-4, as quoted in [the same paper](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/76d1/7b1a27ea0165a26ec023fd6fd40ee2794acf.pdf))

What art supplies from outside, nature usually supplies from within, and the cases where it cannot are where art finishes the job. The formula gives the compendium a middle road between fatalism and self-making: the raw material is given, the finish is not. Character work, practice and expression all take something begun by nature and carry it to a form it would not reach alone.

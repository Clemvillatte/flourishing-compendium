The search for meaning is Viktor Frankl's name for the primary human drive. Against Freud's will to pleasure and Adler's will to power, logotherapy holds that "striving to find meaning in life is the primary, most powerful motivating and driving force in humans" ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). Frankl arrived at the claim before he could test it, and then tested it in the worst available laboratory: the system was developed as theory and confirmed, he says, in the Nazi camps, where the theory is what he survived by ([Wikipedia, Logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)).

### Three ways to find it

The book's most practical passage gives the search three channels. According to Frankl, "We can discover this meaning in life in three different ways: (1) by creating a work or doing a deed; (2) by experiencing something or encountering someone; and (3) by the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering" ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning, quoted in Wikipedia's Logotherapy article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). The first is creative work; the second is experience and love; the third is the one that made the book famous, because it claims a meaning available even when nothing else is.

### The last freedom

That third channel rests on the book's most quoted claim: "everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms – to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances" ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning, quoted in Wikipedia's Logotherapy article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). Frankl held that no one escapes every biological, sociological, or psychological determinant, yet a person remains "capable of resisting and braving even the worst conditions" ([Frankl, The Will to Meaning, quoted in Wikipedia's Logotherapy article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy)). The stance is not optimism; it is the observation that between stimulus and response there is a space, and that the search for meaning happens there.

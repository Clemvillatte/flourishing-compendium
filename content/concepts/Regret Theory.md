**Regret Theory** — Loomes, Sugden — anticipated regret enters decisions; people pay to avoid it and act to minimise it.

Regret theory, formulated by Graham Loomes and Robert Sugden (with a parallel version by David Bell), is a theory of rational choice under uncertainty in which the comparison with forgone options is built into the value of a choice. A regret-aversion experiment paper states the two assumptions:

> "People experience regret when the foregone outcome would have been better, and rejoicing when the foregone outcome would have been worse. The second assumption of regret theory is that the emotional consequences of decisions are anticipated and taken into account when making decisions." (Zeelenberg & Beattie, [Consequences of Regret Aversion, OBHDP 1996](https://pure.uvt.nl/ws/files/641736/zeelenberg-1996_OBHDP.pdf))

### What it predicts

Because regret is anticipated, people do not merely weigh payoffs and probabilities: they also shy away from options whose bad outcomes they would have to compare with better forgone ones. On the evidence reviewed in the same paper, most individuals weigh anticipated regret more heavily than anticipated rejoicing.

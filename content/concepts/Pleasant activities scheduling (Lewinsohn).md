Pleasant activities scheduling is the depression treatment Peter Lewinsohn built from his reinforcement model of depression. Behavioral activation, the paper summarizing the lineage notes, was "originally developed to treat depression" ([Mazzucchelli, Kane & Rees 2010, PMC2882847](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2882847/)); the Lewinsohn version works through the schedule:

> "Based on this theory, Lewinsohn and colleagues developed a behavioral treatment of depression in which an activity schedule is constructed for each patient based on activities and events that have been enjoyable, pleasant, meaningful, or interesting for them in the past."
> — [Mazzucchelli, Kane & Rees 2010, "Behavioral activation interventions for well-being: a meta-analysis", PMC2882847](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2882847/)

The model behind the schedule: "Individuals with depression find fewer activities pleasant and engage in pleasant activities less frequently, and therefore obtain less positive reinforcement than other individuals (MacPhillamy & Lewinsohn, 1974)" ([Mazzucchelli et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2882847/)). The treatment then has the patient monitor daily mood and participation in these activities "in order to see the connection between them", before being "encouraged and taught how to increase the frequency and quality of pleasant events in his or her life, and to decrease unpleasant ones" ([Mazzucchelli et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2882847/)).

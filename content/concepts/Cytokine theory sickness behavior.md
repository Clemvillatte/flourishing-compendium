Cytokine-induced sickness behavior is the pattern of reduced appetite, sleepiness, social withdrawal and fatigue that follows infection. Dantzer and Kelley's review describes how proinflammatory cytokines act in the brain to produce this pattern.

> "It was subsequently shown that physiological concentrations of proinflammatory cytokines that occur after infection act in the brain to induce common symptoms of sickness, such as loss of appetite, sleepiness, withdrawal from normal social activities, fever, aching joints and fatigue." (Dantzer & Kelley 2007, [PMC1850954](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1850954/))

The review describes the syndrome as "part of a motivational system that reorganizes the organism's priorities to facilitate recovery from the infection". It also describes how the brain learns of the infection: "Cytokines convey to the brain that an infection has occurred in the periphery", either "via the traditional endocrine route via the blood or by direct neural transmission via the afferent vagus nerve". On this account, the fatigue of illness is part of an organised response, alongside fever and the rest of the symptom list.

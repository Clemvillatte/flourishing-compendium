The still mirror is Zhuangzi's image for how an untroubled mind meets the world. In Inner Chapter 7 the instruction is to stop being an embodier of fame, a storehouse of schemes, an undertaker of projects, a proprietor of wisdom; what replaces all that busyness is emptiness, and the mind that is empty works like a mirror ([Burton Watson, Zhuangzi ch. 7](https://terebess.hu/english/chuangtzu.html)).

> "The Perfect Man uses his mind like a mirror - going after nothing, welcoming nothing, responding but not storing. Therefore he can win out over things and not hurt himself."
> — [Burton Watson, Zhuangzi, Inner Chapter 7 "Fit for Emperors and Kings"](https://terebess.hu/english/chuangtzu.html)

The mirror's three negations name what a still mind does not do: it does not chase, it does not grasp, it does not hoard. What is left is response without residue. The image is picked up again in the Way of Heaven, where the sage's stillness is not something he chooses because stillness is good; nothing among the ten thousand things is enough to disturb him ([Burton Watson, Zhuangzi ch. 13](https://terebess.hu/english/chuangtzu1.html)).

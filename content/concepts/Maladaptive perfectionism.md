Maladaptive perfectionism is the unhealthy side of perfectionism: striving that runs on fear of failure and negative evaluation instead of the wish to do well. The construct comes from Hewitt and Flett's 1991 Multidimensional Perfectionism Scale, which "suggested that perfectionism is a three-dimensional construct, comprising self-oriented perfectionism, socially prescribed perfectionism, and other-oriented perfectionism" ([Kozłowska and Kuty-Pachecka, Current Issues in Personality Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10699297/)).

### Why the prescribed kind hurts

The dimension the research singles out: "Socially prescribed perfectionism characterizes the beliefs that others are demanding perfectionistic standards and shows strong associations with depression" ([same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10699297/)). The unhealthy pattern overall is marked by very high standards, a feeling that what is done is never good enough, an intense fear of failure, and a lack of satisfaction with one's actions ([same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10699297/)). The distress claim the seed line makes is documented in the same review: "maladaptive perfectionism is associated with higher levels of psychological distress" ([same article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10699297/)).

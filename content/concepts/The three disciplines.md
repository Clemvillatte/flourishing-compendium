The three disciplines are Epictetus's curriculum, stated in Discourses 3.2: three areas of study in which a person must train. Pierre Hadot named them the Discipline of Desire, the Discipline of Action and the Discipline of Assent, and argued they are the lived versions of Stoic physics, ethics and logic respectively ([Hadot, The Inner Citadel; summarized by Donald Robertson](https://donaldrobertson.name/2012/10/18/the-system-of-stoic-philosophy/)).

### The passage

Epictetus gives the three in order, and marks the first as most urgent:

> "There are three areas of study, in which a person who is going to be good and noble must be trained. That concerning desires and aversions, so that he may never fail to get what he desires nor fall into what he would avoid. That concerning the impulse to act and not to act, and, in general, appropriate behaviour; so that he may act in an orderly manner and after due consideration, and not carelessly. The third is concerned with freedom from deception and hasty judgement, and, in general, whatever is connected with assent."

([Epictetus, Discourses 3.2.1-2, trans. Robin Hard, quoted in the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/epictetu/).)

The same passage in Oldfather's translation: "The first has to do with desires and aversions, that he may never fail to get what he desires, nor fall into what he avoids; the second with cases of choice and of refusal, and, in general, with duty, that he may act in an orderly fashion, upon good reasons, and not carelessly; the third with the avoidance of error and rashness in judgement, and, in general, about cases of assent" ([Discourses 3.2.1-2, trans. Oldfather, Aitia journal](https://journals.openedition.org/aitia/10498?lang=en)).

### Why the map matters

The disciplines are a map of the whole person, and each has its own failure mode. Desire fails by wanting what is not up to us. Action fails by acting carelessly or out of role. Assent fails by agreeing to impressions before testing them. The third discipline is the refinement of the other two: every desire and every action runs through a judgment, so training the judgment is upstream of both. Hadot's reading, that the three correspond to physics, ethics and logic as lived practices, is what makes the map a curriculum rather than a list ([Hadot, quoted in Modern Stoicism](https://modernstoicism.com/hadots-active-stoic-exercises-by-anitra-russell/)).

Stress inoculation training is Donald Meichenbaum's cognitive-behavioral method for building coping capacity before stress arrives, on the vaccine metaphor: expose a person to manageable doses of a stressor while teaching coping skills, so the real encounter meets a prepared system. In Meichenbaum's own summary, "SIT consists of three interlocking and overlapping phases: A conceptual educational phase, A skills acquisition and skills consolidation phase, An application and follow-through phase" ([Meichenbaum, Stress Inoculation Training chapter](http://brown.uk.com/anxiety/stress-inoculation.pdf)).

### The three phases

The first phase reframes the client's own story. "In a collaborative fashion, a more facilitative reconceptualization of the clients' stressful experiences and reactions is formulated. Rather than conceiving their stressors as being overwhelming, uncontrollable, unpredictable, debilitating, and hopeless, the SIT trainer helps clients develop a sense of 'learned resourcefulness'" ([Meichenbaum chapter](http://brown.uk.com/anxiety/stress-inoculation.pdf)). The second phase teaches and rehearses the coping skills: relaxation, cognitive restructuring, problem solving, self-instruction, social supports, each tailored to the client. The third is where the vaccine metaphor earns its name: "The final application and follow-through phase of SIT includes opportunities for clients to apply the variety of coping skills on a graduated basis across increasingly demanding levels of stressors (that is, following the 'inoculation' concept)" ([Meichenbaum chapter](http://brown.uk.com/anxiety/stress-inoculation.pdf)).

The package is explicitly tailored, not off the shelf: "In each instance, the clinical application of SIT has been individually tailored to the specific target population and circumstances" ([Meichenbaum chapter](http://brown.uk.com/anxiety/stress-inoculation.pdf)), varying with the nature of the stressors, acute or chronic, and the client's resources.

### Evidence status

SIT is well established as a flexible clinical framework, but the trial base for specific diagnoses is thinner than the method's reputation. A US Defense Health Agency evidence brief on SIT for PTSD reported that a March 2019 literature search "identified no randomized controlled trials on the efficacy of SIT for the treatment of PTSD published in the time period since the search conducted for the" previous review ([PHCoE Evidence Brief, 2021](https://www.health.mil/Reference-Center/Publications/2021/04/26/PHCoE-Evidence-Brief-Stress-Inoculation-Training-for-Posttraumatic-Stress-Disorder-508)).

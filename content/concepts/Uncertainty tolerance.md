## The construct

The literature usually measures tolerance through its opposite, intolerance of uncertainty (IU). IU represents a person's tendency to negatively interpret and react to uncertain events, and is assumed to be a trait-like dispositional characteristic resulting from negative beliefs about uncertainty and its implications ([Vukosavljević Gvozden et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631188/)).

## The Dugas model

The construct entered the modern literature with Dugas and colleagues' model of generalized anxiety disorder, the most comprehensive conceptual model of the relationship between IU and anxiety psychopathology ([Bomyea et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480197/)). In the original study, 24 GAD patients and 20 non-clinical controls were compared on four process variables: intolerance of uncertainty, beliefs about worry, poor problem orientation, and cognitive avoidance ([Dugas et al., 1998, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/)). Intolerance of uncertainty was pivotal in distinguishing GAD patients from non-clinical subjects, and the discriminant function correctly classified 82% of participants ([Dugas et al., 1998, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9613027/)). Later work describes the model as holding that IU plays a key role in the acquisition and maintenance of worries ([Vukosavljević Gvozden et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8631188/)).

## Treatment findings

If intolerance of uncertainty maintains worry, growing tolerance should reduce it. In a CBT program for GAD, changes in IU and worry were tracked bi-weekly across treatment, and change in IU mediated change in worry, with reductions in IU accounting for 59% of the reductions in worry observed over the course of treatment ([Bomyea et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480197/)).

> “Results revealed that change in IU mediated change in worry (ab = -0.20; 95% CI [-.35, -.09]), but change in worry did not mediate change in IU (ab = -0.16; 95% CI [-.06, .12]). Findings indicated that reductions in IU accounted for 59% of the reductions in worry observed over the course of treatment, suggesting that changes in IU are not simply concomitants of changes in worry.”
>
> — Bomyea, Ramsawh, Ball, Taylor, Paulus, Lang and Stein, [Intolerance of uncertainty as a mediator of reductions in worry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4480197/), Journal of Anxiety Disorders, 2015 (Abstract)

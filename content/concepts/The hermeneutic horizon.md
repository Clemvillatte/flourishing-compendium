The hermeneutic horizon is Hans-Georg Gadamer's figure for the situatedness of understanding: we see everything from a particular vantage point, and that vantage point sets what can be seen. His definition, from Truth and Method:

> "Every finite present has its limitations. We define the concept of 'situation' by saying that it represents a standpoint that limits the possibility of vision. Hence essential to the concept of situation is the concept of 'horizon'. The horizon is the range of vision that includes everything that can be seen from a particular vantage point." (Gadamer, Truth and Method, 2003 Continuum ed., p. 302, in [Journal of Dialogue Studies](https://www.dialoguestudies.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Hans-Georg-Gadamers-Truth-and-Method-Revisited-On-the-Very-Idea-of-a-Fusion-of-Horizons-in-Intense-Asymmetric-and-Intractable-Conflicts.pdf))

### What the concept carries

The horizon is not a prison but a starting position that can widen. Gadamer's move, against the romantic ideal of escaping one's standpoint, is that understanding happens between horizons rather than from nowhere: "In the process of understanding, a real fusing of horizons occurs – which means that as the historical horizon is projected, it is simultaneously superseded" (2003, p. 307). And again: "Rather, understanding is always the fusion of these horizons supposedly existing by themselves" (2003, p. 306). I connect this with M008's use of the middle as a vantage point: the between-position, belonging fully to neither world, is a horizon honest about itself, and its yield comes from the fusing rather than from neutrality. A compendium exercise drawn from the concept is to name one's horizon before interpreting, the tradition and assumptions one reads from, and to let the encounter revise it. What the concept does not give: a method for measuring horizons or their overlap; it is a philosophical account of understanding, not a psychometric one.

Shunyata (Sanskrit śūnyatā, "emptiness") is the Mahāyāna Buddhist doctrine that all phenomena lack svabhāva (inherent, independent, self-sufficient existence). It is the central concept of Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka school (c. 150 – 250 CE), whose foundational text, the Mūlamadhyamakakārikā (MMK), is the systematic defence of the claim ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarjuna)). Emptiness is always the emptiness of something: in Nāgārjuna's case, the emptiness of inherent nature (svabhāva). The SEP entry states that "The central concept around which all of Nāgārjuna's philosophy is built is the notion of emptiness", and that emptiness is "not some kind of primordial reality ante rem but a corrective to a mistaken view of how the world exists" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nagarjuna/)).

The argument proceeds from dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda): because everything arises in dependence on causes and conditions, nothing exists from its own side. Nāgārjuna "equated the emptiness of dharmas with their dependent origination, and thus with their being devoid any permanent substance or primary, substantial existence (svabhava)" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9A%C5%ABnyat%C4%81)). The MMK's most-cited formulation (24.18) states: "We state that conditioned origination is emptiness. It is mere designation depending on something, and it is [[The middle path|the middle path]]."

The doctrine of the two truths (MMK 24.8–10) separates conventional truth, the everyday world of functioning objects and persons, from ultimate truth, the realisation that all such things are empty. Nāgārjuna is explicit that emptiness itself is empty: it is not a primordial reality underlying phenomena, and it must not be reified into a new metaphysical foundation. The Madhyamaka distinguishes intellectual understanding of emptiness from realisation, and notes that phenomena continue to appear inherently existent even after the argument is understood.

### The nihilism charge

The charge of nihilism was levelled at Madhyamaka from the beginning, and Nāgārjuna addresses it directly: emptiness is a claim about how things exist, and the MMK stakes the whole system on it:

> "All is possible when emptiness is possible. Nothing is possible when emptiness is impossible." (MMK 24.14, [Wikipedia carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarjuna))

The SEP entry notes that the Mādhyamikas themselves are "very clear that their position avoids both of the extreme views", the belief in svabhāva as well as its nihilistic opposite. Shunyata is a philosophical conclusion about the mode of existence of ordinary things: a corrective to a mistaken view of how the world exists, in the SEP's phrasing, rather than a mystical state of blankness or void.

### Practical implications

For flourishing, shunyata functions as a [[Cognitive defusion|cognitive defusion]] tool of unusual generality: because no state, role, or possession has fixed inherent existence, no state is final, and no identity has to be defended. It converges with anatta on the person and extends the same logic to objects, goals, and institutions. In modern terms it is closest to the research on cognitive fusion and on de-reification of thoughts. The practical instruction targets grasping: care continues, and one engages fully while holding lightly.

In focused attention (FA) meditation, the practitioner selects an object — commonly the sensations of breathing — and keeps attention on it, returning each time the mind wanders. That chosen object is the anchor: the stable point attention is brought back to. The account below follows the review that introduced the FA/open-monitoring distinction into the meditation-science literature.

> "A widespread style of Buddhist practice involves sustaining selective attention moment by moment on a chosen object, such as a subset of localized sensations caused by respiration. To sustain this focus, the meditator must also constantly monitor the quality of attention. At first, the attention wanders away from the chosen object, and the typical instruction is to recognize the wandering and then restore attention to the chosen object. For example, while intending to focus on localized sensations around the nostrils caused by breathing, one might notice that the focus has shifted to the pain in one's knee. One then 'releases' this distraction, and returns to the intended object." ([Lutz, Slagter, Dunne and Davidson, 2008, Box 1](https://chm-web.files.svdcdn.com/production/files-publications/LutzAttentionTrendsInCognitiveSciences.pdf))

### What counts as an anchor

The review defines the practice by its object: FA meditation "entails voluntary focusing attention on a chosen object in a sustained fashion." The example given is breath sensation, localized sensations around the nostrils, but the definition leaves the object open. The practice is the returning, not the object: noticing that focus has shifted, releasing the distraction, and going back.

### What the returning trains

The same passage says the practice cultivates "the acuity and stability of sustained attention on a chosen object" and develops three regulative skills: a monitoring faculty that stays vigilant to distractions without destabilizing the focus, the ability to disengage from a distracting object without further involvement, and the ability to redirect focus promptly to the chosen object.

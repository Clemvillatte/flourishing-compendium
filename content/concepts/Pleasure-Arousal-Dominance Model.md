**Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance Model** — Mehrabian, Russell — emotional states and environments can be located along pleasure, arousal and dominance dimensions.

The Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance (PAD) model, developed by Albert Mehrabian and James A. Russell, locates any emotional state in a three-dimensional space. A peer-reviewed application states its structure:

> "The PAD model proposes a categorization of emotions on three independent dimensions: pleasure, arousal and dominance. Pleasure refers to the general positive and negative feelings experienced, arousal refers to the level of alertness and physical activity, and dominance refers to the feelings of control, non-restriction and autonomy." (Bran & Vaidis, [On the Characteristics of the Cognitive Dissonance State, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7101003/))

### What the dimensions distinguish

The model's point is that the same two dimensions do not separate states the third one separates: "anger is reported as an unpleasant, aroused and moderately dominant emotion, while boredom is reported as a slightly unpleasant, unaroused and mostly non-dominant emotion (Mehrabian, 1980)".

Unconscious processes are mental operations that shape thought and behavior without being available to introspection. Wikipedia's working definition: the unconscious is "the part of the psyche that is not available to introspection," and its processes "exist beneath the surface of conscious awareness" while exerting "an effect on conscious thought processes and behavior" ([Wikipedia, Unconscious mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind)). The idea predates Freud: Leibniz allowed "for some thoughts that were unconscious, the so called 'petites perceptions'" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness/)), and Helmholtz described perception itself as "unconscious conclusions" ([Wikipedia, Unconscious inference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_inference)).

### Two unconsciouses

The Freudian unconscious is a dynamic place: "ideas and drives that have been subject to the mechanism of repression: anxiety-producing impulses in childhood are barred from consciousness, but do not cease to exist, and exert a constant pressure in the direction of consciousness" ([Wikipedia, Unconscious mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind)). The cognitive tradition replaced the drama with plumbing, and named its object differently: researchers speak of a "'cognitive unconscious' (John Kihlstrom), an 'adaptive unconscious' (Timothy Wilson), or a 'dumb unconscious' (Loftus and Klinger), which executes automatic processes but lacks the complex mechanisms of repression" ([Wikipedia, Unconscious mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind)). Its findings are cumulative: "individuals automatically register and acquire more information than they are consciously aware of or can consciously remember and report," including automatic tallies of how often events occur, made "regardless of the instructions they receive" ([Wikipedia, Unconscious mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind)).

### Speed against flexibility

The division of labor between conscious and unconscious processing is a trade: "Though unconscious automatic processes can be extremely efficient and rapid, they typically operate in ways that are more fixed and predetermined than those which involve conscious self-awareness," so conscious control "is of most importance when one is dealing with novel situations and previously unencountered problems" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness/)). Skill acquisition rides the gradient down: conscious awareness dominates "the initial learning phase, which gradually gives way to more automatic processes of the sort that require little attention or conscious oversight" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness/)). The clinical evidence is striking. Blindsight patients "may achieve a higher accuracy than would be expected from chance alone" on visual guesses "without any conscious awareness of any stimuli" ([Wikipedia, Blindsight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindsight)). Procedural memory "lets us perform some actions (such as writing or riding a bike) even if we are not consciously thinking about it" ([Wikipedia, Implicit memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implicit_memory)).

### Why it matters for flourishing

The humility here is not optional equipment. "No major contemporary philosopher believes that all of mentality is available to be discovered by introspection," and the first-person advantage in assessing the factors behind our judgments "is more modest than non-psychologists generally tend to assume" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, Introspection](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/introspection/)). That is the footing of the compendium's [[CO077 — Recognise the limits of introspection — you do not have privileged access to your own mind]] and the reason [[E089 — Practise the hermeneutics of suspicion]] treats self-report as evidence, not verdict. The upside is equally real: most of skilled living runs on processes that work precisely because they are not supervised.

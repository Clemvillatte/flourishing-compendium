Congruence is Carl Rogers' term for the alignment between what a person actually experiences and what their awareness represents of it. In his 1957 statement of the conditions of therapeutic personality change, congruence is the third condition, and it sits on the therapist's side of the relationship.

> "The third condition is that the therapist should be, within the confines of this relationship, a congruent, genuine, integrated person. It means that within the relationship he is freely and deeply himself, with his actual experience accurately represented by his awareness of himself. It is the opposite of presenting a facade, either knowingly or unknowingly." (Rogers, [The necessary and sufficient conditions of therapeutic personality change](https://counselling-tutor.s3.eu-west-2.amazonaws.com/CSR+Documents/The+Necessary+and+Sufficient+Conditions+of+Therapeutic+Personality+Change.pdf), Journal of Consulting Psychology 21(2), 1957)

### The counterpart construct

The same paper defines the client-side condition as incongruence: a "discrepancy between the actual experience of the organism and the self picture of the individual insofar as it represents that experience". Rogers allows that the therapist need not be "a paragon" in the rest of life; it is enough to be "accurately himself in this hour of this relationship".

The cunning of reason (List der Vernunft) is Hegel's name for the indirect way the historical process gets its results: people pursue their own passions and aims, and the outcome exceeds and redirects those aims. The phrase comes from the introduction to his Lectures on the Philosophy of History.

> "This may be called the cunning of reason — that it sets the passions to work for itself, while that which develops its existence through such impulsion pays the penalty, and suffers loss." (Hegel, Lectures on the Philosophy of History, introduction, Sibree translation, [hegel.net PDF](https://hegel.net/assets/pdf/en/history.pdf))

### What the passage is saying

The phrase sits inside a claim about passion rather than about scheming. The special interest of passion is "inseparable from the active development of a general principle"; the universal results from the particular and from its negation. The general idea never enters the fight itself; "It remains in the background, untouched and uninjured", while particular passions contend, wear out and pay. The particular is mostly too trifling against the general: "individuals are sacrificed and abandoned", and "The Idea pays the penalty of determinate existence and of corruptibility, not from itself, but from the passions of individuals" (p. 48 in this edition). What an era's actors intend is not a guide to what their acts will have meant.

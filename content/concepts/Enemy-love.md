"Enemy-love" — Gospel of Matthew 5.

Enemy-love is the demand set at the center of the Sermon on the Mount. In the King James rendering, the passage first quotes the received rule and then overturns it:

> "Ye have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy. But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you." (Matthew 5:43-44, [KJV, Biblehub](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/5.htm))

The stated ground is not the enemy's deserts but the pattern of a providence that does not sort recipients: "for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good, and sendeth rain on the just and on the unjust" (Matthew 5:45, [KJV](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/5.htm)). The command is deliberately unconditioned. It does not ask whether the enemy will change, and it offers no exception clause for enemies who stay hostile.

The passage closes the loophole that would make the command easy: "For if ye love them which love you, what reward have ye? do not even the publicans the same?" (Matthew 5:46, [KJV](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/5.htm)). Reciprocated love is the baseline case; enemy-love is the case that shows whether love is anything more than exchange.

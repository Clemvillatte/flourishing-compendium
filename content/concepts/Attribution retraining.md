Attribution retraining is a cognitive intervention that teaches someone to read an ambiguous social event as accidental or situational rather than hostile. It grew out of social information processing research on childhood aggression, which found that children who presumed hostile intent in peers retaliated more. The best known programs were built by Cynthia Hudley and Sandra Graham for aggressive elementary school boys, and later packaged as the BrainPower curriculum, a twelve session school-based program. Dodge's review of this line of work summarises what the training actually targets:

> "Hudley and Graham (1993) focused specifically on changing hostile attributional biases in their sample of African American elementary school-aged aggressive boys. They developed and implemented a 12-session cognitive intervention designed to help boys more accurately identify others' intentions, to presume benign intent when the portrayed intent is ambiguous, and to link nonaggressive behavioral responses to benign intent attributions." (Kenneth A. Dodge, [Translational science in action: Hostile attributional style and the development of aggressive behavior problems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2745254/), Development and Psychopathology 18(3), 2006, "Interventions to Alter Attributional Style" section)

The intervention trains three linked skills: reading the social cues accurately, choosing a benign reading when intent is genuinely unclear, and pairing that benign reading with a nonaggressive response. In the random-assignment experiments Dodge reviews, trained boys showed lower hostile attribution bias on both hypothetical and engineered provocations, and teachers rated them as less reactively aggressive afterwards, though office discipline referrals did not always move. The concept belongs to the wider CBT family of procedures that shift habitual interpretations; its distinctive move is targeting the interpretation of someone else's intent rather than the interpretation of one's own performance, which is where the academic-motivation variant of attribution retraining puts its weight.

De (德) is the Daoist term for the power or virtue a thing has when it is [[Dao|aligned with the Dao]]: not moral goodness in the rule-following sense, but the characteristic vitality and effectiveness of a thing living out its own nature. Chapter 51 of the Daodejing gives the canonical chain:

> "All things are produced by the Tao, and nourished by its outflowing operation. They receive their forms according to the nature of each, and are completed according to the circumstances of their condition." (Daodejing 51, Legge translation, [sacred-texts.com](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm))

Where the Dao is the way the world works, de is what that way looks like instantiated in a particular being: the way water's de is flowing and yielding, or a tree's de is growing. The same tradition names the highest form "mysterious virtue" (玄德), the formula Legge renders:

> "(The Tao) produces (all things) and nourishes them; it produces them and does not claim them as its own; it does all, and yet does not boast of it; it presides over all, and yet does not control them. This is what is called 'The mysterious Quality' (of the Tao)." (Daodejing 10, Legge translation, [sacred-texts.com](https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm))

### The Zhuangzi gallery

In the Zhuangzi, de becomes more psychological. Inner Chapter 5, "The Seal of Virtue Complete" (德充符), is the sustained meditation on it: a gallery of the mutilated, the deformed and the ugly, men who have lost their feet, men whose appearance frightens the world. The chapter's own formula:

> "So it is that when one's virtue is extraordinary, (any deficiency in) his bodily form may be forgotten." (Zhuangzi, Inner Chapter 5, [Legge translation, Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/seal-of-virtue-complete/ens))

In every case the wholeness of their inner de draws people to them more powerfully than beauty, rank or eloquence. The chapter's title announces the paradox: the completeness of the spirit may be most perfectly sealed in the body that is broken. De, in other words, is not dependent on conventional standing, appearance, or physical completeness.

### The practical reading

The compendium's principle D004 ("Cultivate de: your embodied character-power") carries the practical reading: de is what your character looks like when it stops being a performance and becomes an embodied capacity, what you do well and naturally, without the interference of self-conscious effort.

### In everyday life

The craftsperson whose skill has sunk into the hands: the line that comes out straight without watching, the apology that lands because it is not rehearsed. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

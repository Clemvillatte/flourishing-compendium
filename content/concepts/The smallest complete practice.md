The smallest complete practice is the compendium's name for the idea that a practice can be scaled down until it fits inside a single act, without losing what makes it a practice. Its formulation names two: one conscious breath and one kind act. Both halves have classical ground. The breath is the oldest unit of Buddhist meditation: the Ānāpānasati Sutta instructs a monk to sit "holding his body erect, and setting mindfulness to the fore. Always mindful, he breathes in; mindful he breathes out," and to discern simply, "Breathing in long, he discerns, 'I am breathing in long'" ([Ānāpānasati Sutta MN 118, Thanissaro translation, AccessToInsight](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.118.than.html)).

### The Daoist arithmetic

The same logic of small complete units runs through the Dao De Jing, in Legge's translation: "The tree which fills the arms grew from the tiniest sprout; the tower of nine storeys rose from a (small) heap of earth," and a trip of a thousand li, as Legge renders chapter 64, "commenced with a single step" ([Laozi, Dao De Jing 64, Legge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)). The step is not less than the trip for being small; it is the trip in its only available form. Chapter 64 continues with the rule the compendium's D059, do the difficult while it is easy, carries forward: "He who acts (with an ulterior purpose) does harm... The sage does not act (so), and therefore does no harm" ([Dao De Jing 64.3, Legge](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)).

### Why complete matters

The sutta's claim for the practice is stated in its benefit sentence: "Mindfulness of in-&-out breathing, when developed & pursued, is of great fruit, of great benefit" ([MN 118, AccessToInsight](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.118.than.html)). The unit is small but not partial: attention to the body, mindfulness, and the calming of what the breath touches are all inside one breath. The kind act half of the formulation is the compendium's own pairing; the rationale for the smallest unit is that a practice needs a form that survives the worst day, so that the practice is available exactly when it is hardest to begin.

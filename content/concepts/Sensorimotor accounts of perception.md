Sensorimotor accounts of perception hold that perceiving is something a creature does rather than something that happens inside it. The founding statement is J. Kevin O'Regan and Alva Noë's 2001 paper in Behavioral and Brain Sciences, which attacks the assumption that vision works by building an internal picture of the world and activating it. Their counter-proposal, in the paper's abstract:

> "We propose that seeing is a way of acting. It is a particular way of exploring the environment. Activity in internal representations does not generate the experience of seeing. The outside world serves as its own, external, representation." (O'Regan and Noë, [A sensorimotor account of vision and visual consciousness](https://www.rctn.org/vs265/noe-oregan.pdf), Behavioral and Brain Sciences 24(5), 2001)

### The machinery of the account

What the perceiver brings is not a picture but practical knowledge of sensorimotor contingencies, the ways sensory input changes with movement. Their formulation: "The central idea of our new approach is that vision is a mode of exploration of the world that is mediated by knowledge of what we call sensorimotor contingencies." The experience of seeing, on this view, is skill: "visual experience is a mode of activity involving practical knowledge about currently possible behaviors and associated sensory consequences. Visual experience rests on know-how, the possession of skills." The paper gathers support from sensorimotor adaptation, visual filling-in, visual stability despite eye movements, change blindness, sensory substitution and color perception.

Attribution research studies how ordinary people explain behavior: what they credit, what they miss, and what that costs. The field's founding insight is Heider's, that people behave like intuitive psychologists, and its sharpest finding is Ross's, that this intuitive psychologist leans too hard on character.

> "The first identified (Heider, 1958) and most frequently cited bias or error, one which we shall term the fundamental attribution error, is the tendency for attributers to underestimate the impact of situational factors and to overestimate the role of dispositional factors in controlling behavior." (Lee Ross, "The Intuitive Psychologist and His Shortcomings", Advances in Experimental Social Psychology 10, 1977, [chapter PDF, Internet Archive](https://ia800601.us.archive.org/21/items/15341_Readings/15341_Readings/Social_Cognition/Ross_Intuitive_Psychologist_in_Adv_Experiment_Soc_Psych_vol10_p173_text.pdf))

### Why it matters for flourishing

The bias is not a logic slip; it is a habit of seeing. When another person's struggle reads as their character while the situation that shaped it goes unnoticed, the error feeds blame and misdesigns the situations people actually live in. The compendium uses attribution research as a corrective instrument: noticing what a situation is doing to someone is part of seeing them accurately, and seeing accurately is upstream of both compassion and repair.

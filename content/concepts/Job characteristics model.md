The job characteristics model is a theory of work design ([Wikipedia, Job characteristics model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_characteristics_model)). Its original version, constructed by Greg R. Oldham and J. Richard Hackman in 1975, proposed five core job characteristics (skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy and feedback) affecting work-related outcomes through three psychological states: experienced meaningfulness, experienced responsibility, and knowledge of results ([Wikipedia, Job characteristics model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_characteristics_model)). Hackman and Oldham presented the final form of the theory in their 1980 book Work Redesign ([Wikipedia, Job characteristics model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_characteristics_model)).

> "Results from a summary of 259 studies and 219,625 participants showed that 14 work characteristics explained, on average, 43% of the variance in the 19 worker attitudes and behaviors examined." (Humphrey, Nahrgang & Morgeson, [Integrating motivational, social, and contextual work design features](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17845089/), Journal of Applied Psychology 92(5), 2007, abstract)

### Evidence and later revision

The theory grew out of 1960s work redesign, which reacted against the simplification of jobs into repetitive tasks after routinized work was found to breed dissatisfaction ([Wikipedia, Job characteristics model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_characteristics_model)). In the 1980 revision, Hackman and Oldham added two moderators (knowledge and skill, and context satisfaction), removed absenteeism and turnover as outcomes, and concentrated attention on internal work motivation and other affective outcomes, following weak empirical support for some of the original outcome variables ([Wikipedia, Job characteristics model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_characteristics_model)).

The meta-analytic result above comes from a review of broader work-design characteristics, not a direct test solely of the original five-characteristic model; within that review, motivational characteristics explained 34% of the variance in job satisfaction ([Humphrey, Nahrgang & Morgeson, 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17845089/)).

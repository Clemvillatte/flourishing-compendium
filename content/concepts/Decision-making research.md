Decision-making research is the study of how people actually choose: what they search through, when they stop, and how they feel about what they picked. The seed line for this page anchors it in Barry Schwartz's work on satisficing versus maximizing (Schwartz, 2004), and the strand's empirical core is a 2002 article in the Journal of Personality and Social Psychology that measured who wants the best and what wanting the best costs.

> "Seven samples revealed negative correlations between maximization and happiness, optimism, self-esteem, and life satisfaction, and positive correlations between maximization and depression, perfectionism, and regret." (Schwartz et al., [JPSP 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12416921/), abstract)

### The best versus the good enough

The line the study runs on comes from Herbert Simon. As the Iyengar, Wells and Schwartz paper recounts it, "Half a century ago, Simon (1955, 1956, 1957) introduced an important distinction between maximizing and satisficing as choice-making strategies," and that investigation frames itself as "expanding upon Simon's (1955) seminal theory" ([Psychological Science](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/874/874.pdf)). The Maximization Scale sorted people along this line, and the findings cut against the culture of more: "compared with satisficers, maximizers were more likely to engage in an exhaustive search of all available options and to compare their decisions with those of other people," and "maximizing tendencies were positively correlated with regret, depression, and decision difficulty, and negatively correlated with happiness, life satisfaction, optimism, and satisfaction with decision outcomes" (same text).

The wrinkle that keeps the strand honest, in the job-search study's own words: maximizing was "positively correlated with objectively better decision outcomes" and at the same time "associated with more negative subjective evaluations of these decision outcomes" ([Iyengar et al.](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/874/874.pdf)).

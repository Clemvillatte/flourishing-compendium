Dukkha (Pali) is the first Noble Truth: the fact that experience, when grasped, is unsatisfactory. The Buddha's first sermon enumerates it:

> "Now this, monks, is the noble truth of stress: Birth is stressful, aging is stressful, death is stressful; sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair are stressful; association with the unbeloved is stressful, separation from the loved is stressful, not getting what is wanted is stressful. In short, the five clinging-aggregates are stressful." (SN 56.11, [Thanissaro translation, dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN56_11.html))

The word is often translated "suffering", but its range is wider: a background unsatisfactoriness, a friction built into conditioned experience itself.

### Pain and the second arrow

My reading separates pain from suffering: the first arrow (pain) is unavoidable; the second arrow, the suffering added by resisting, proliferating, or clinging, is optional. The Sallatha Sutta gives the image:

> "Just as if they were to shoot a man with an arrow and, right afterward, were to shoot him with another one, so that he would feel the pains of two arrows, in the same way, when touched with a feeling of pain, the uninstructed run-of-the-mill person sorrows, grieves, & laments, beats his breast, becomes distraught. So he feels two pains, physical & mental." (SN 36.6, [Thanissaro translation, dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN36_6.html))

The second arrow is one form dukkha takes; the term itself names the whole background of unsatisfactoriness. This is what makes the first truth a task rather than a verdict: study the friction, find what feeds it.

### The cross-tradition family

Dukkha's cross-tradition family is the problem-statements of the wisdom traditions: the Stoic passio (suffering from judgments), the existential angst, and [[Hedonic adaptation|the hedonic treadmill of psychology]]. What distinguishes dukkha is its precision: it is not life itself but grasped experience, and its cure is named in the same breath, the cessation of craving.

### In everyday life

The frictions of an ordinary day, each one a small experiment: the phone's glow gone flat on the tenth check, the promotion that satisfied for a week. (Illustrative scenario, not a reported participant or study result.)

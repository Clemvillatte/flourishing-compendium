Implicit theories of intelligence are people's lay beliefs about what intelligence is: a fixed quantity one is born with, or a quality that can be grown. Carol Dweck's model, developed with Ellen Leggett in 1988, calls the two poles entity theory and incremental theory, and traces how the belief steers goals and responses to failure. The beliefs are "implicit" because their holders may not be aware of them ([Dweck and Leggett 1988, summarised in Sociology](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0261429419864272)).

### The model

Dweck and Leggett's social-cognitive model "specifies how individuals' implicit theories orient them toward goals, and how these goals then influence cognitive, affective, and behavioral responses" ([Dweck and Leggett 1988, full text](https://www.scribd.com/document/260120551/Dweck-Leggett-1988-a-Social-cognitive-Approach)). The core findings: children who believe intelligence is "a malleable, increasable, controllable quality" pursue learning goals, seek challenge and persist; children who believe it is "a fixed or uncontrollable trait" pursue performance goals, avoid challenge and show the helpless pattern. Dweck's later chapter clarifies a point often missed: "These are really beliefs about control, not stability," and incremental theorists "do not necessarily believe that everyone starts out with the same talent" ([Dweck, Implicit Theories, 2011 chapter](http://www.rpforschools.net/Faith/Mindset/Dweck%20Implicit%20Theories%202011.pdf)).

The scale items show the measure's directness: an entity item asserts "You have a certain amount of intelligence, and you can't really do much to change it," an incremental item "No matter how much intelligence you have, you can always change it quite a bit" ([Dweck 2011 chapter](http://www.rpforschools.net/Faith/Mindset/Dweck%20Implicit%20Theories%202011.pdf)).

### Evidence and its limits

A meta-analytic review of the achievement link found incremental theorists more likely to have higher grades, with entity beliefs also positively associated but at lower magnitude, and concluded that entity theories can cut either way depending on the outcome ([PMC, meta-analytic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5996155/)). The compendium's sibling page, Fixed views of self, carries the failed-premises test of the mindset programme itself ([Burgoyne et al. 2020](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2020/Burgoyne_Hambrick_Macnamara_2020_Mindset.pdf)).

### In the compendium

The principles translate the theory into practice on both sides of the belief. SE062 cultivates the growth orientation directly, and MO008 extends it to motivation itself, trainable rather than fixed. S117 handles failure without despair, the incremental theorist's signature response; P015 makes growth the criterion of value. The contemplative parallels hold the same openness in other vocabularies: B048's beginner's mind and D045's empty cup, S139's Stoicism as practice rather than destination, A055's responsibility for character, CO045's cognitive flexibility and D156's warning that rigidity is death's companion.

Biddle's 1986 review abstract says role theory "explains roles by presuming that persons are members of social positions and hold expectations for their own behaviors and those of other persons", and notes that "at least five perspectives may be discriminated": functional, symbolic interactionist, structural, organizational, and cognitive role theory ([Biddle, Recent Developments in Role Theory, Annual Review of Sociology 12, 67-92, Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/soc/12/1)). The seed's description matches the abstract, but the abstract also stresses that role theory is a family of perspectives, not one theory.

### Core terms

A Kent review quotes Banton's definition of a role as "the expected behaviour associated with a social position" and Merton's definition of a social position as "a position in a social system involving designated rights and obligations". Merton's 1957 role-set paper shows that a single status carries multiple roles, with different people holding different expectations, "a potential source of conflict" ([Role Theory, Kent Academic Repository PDF](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/55160/1/Role%20Theory.pdf)). The Springer entry on social roles defines them as "a socially defined pattern of behavior that is expected of a person who occupies a certain social position or belongs to a particular social category", and notes that individually, "roles function as schemas" ([Social Roles, Springer Nature Link](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-19650-3_2469)).

### Conflict and strain

Biddle's article says that where norms come from several sources, individuals "are often subjected to role conflicts in which they must contend with antithetical norms for their behavior", and that such conflicts "produce strain". Role conflict is "normally defined as the concurrent appearance of two or more incompatible expectations for the behavior of a person" ([Biddle 1986, full text copy hosted on sci-hub; the abstract is at Annual Reviews](https://moscow.sci-hub.se/1144/d19d3119afe0b8ee49b3a276a5dc1d89/biddle1986.pdf)). Goode (1960) emphasised strain from participating in multiple roles (Kent PDF). The seed also names ambiguity, which none of these excerpts discuss, so that part is unverified here.

### Debates

The Kent review describes two schools: a structuralist view, where roles influence behaviour (Linton, Parsons and Shils, Stryker), and an interactionist view, where roles are acted out through interaction (Goffman, Ralph Turner), with calls since the 1980s to combine them. A sociology article says role conflict was attractive to functionalists because it bore on system stability and to interactionists because it was a focus of everyday negotiation, while observing that by the mid-1980s role theory's "star had started to wane" ([Field theory, role theory and role conflict, Journal of Classical Sociology](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1468795X231208456)).

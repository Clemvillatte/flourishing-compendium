“Consummation” — Dewey, Art as Experience.

Consummation is Dewey's term for the fulfilled close of an experience, the moment at which the material of experience forms an integral whole. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's entry on Dewey's aesthetics states it:

> "In 'an experience' the material of experience is fulfilled, consummated, and forms an integral whole, as for example, when a game is played through, a conversation is rounded out or when we finish 'that meal in a Paris restaurant' (AE: 43)." (Leddy & Puolakka, [SEP: Dewey's Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/))

### The close

The same entry gives the close's shape:

> "Aesthetic experience also does not terminate at some random point. Rather, its close is a summation of its earlier phases, either in the sense that the energy that has gathered up during the experience is released or the experience reaches a fulfillment in some other sense." (Leddy & Puolakka, [SEP: Dewey's Aesthetics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/))

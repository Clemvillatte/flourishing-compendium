Clinical thresholds are the points at which ordinary suffering becomes something a professional should evaluate. The DSM-5 criterion that turns a symptom list into a disorder is distress and impairment: classification systems require that symptoms "cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning" (DSM-5, quoted in Maj et al., [The clinical characterization of the adult patient with depression](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7491646/), World Psychiatry, 2020). NIMH's public guidance adds a duration marker for seeking help.

> "Seek professional help if you are experiencing severe or distressing symptoms that have lasted 2 weeks or more." (NIMH, [Caring for Your Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health))

### What the guidance lists

The symptoms NIMH lists with that sentence include difficulty sleeping, appetite or weight changes, difficulty getting out of bed because of mood, difficulty concentrating, loss of interest in usually enjoyable things, inability to complete usual tasks, and feelings of irritability, frustration or restlessness. The same page directs people with concerns to a primary care provider, who can refer to a psychologist, psychiatrist or clinical social worker. The disorders named in the title each carry their own diagnostic criteria in the DSM-5 and ICD-11; what they share is the general threshold quoted above.

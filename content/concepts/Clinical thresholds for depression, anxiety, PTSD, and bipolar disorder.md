This page brings together public help-seeking guidance and diagnostic passages; it does not establish one clinical threshold for all four conditions. NIMH's public guidance gives a duration marker for seeking help:

> "Seek professional help if you are experiencing severe or distressing symptoms that have lasted 2 weeks or more, such as:"
>
> - "Difficulty sleeping"
> - "Changes in appetite or unplanned weight changes"
> - "Difficulty getting out of bed in the morning because of mood"
> - "Difficulty concentrating"
> - "Loss of interest in things you usually find enjoyable"
> - "Inability to complete usual tasks and activities"
> - "Feelings of irritability, frustration, or restlessness"
>
> (NIMH, [Caring for Your Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health))

The same page directs people with concerns to a primary care provider, who can refer to a psychologist, psychiatrist or clinical social worker.

### Depression and PTSD examples

In a discussion of depression, Maj and colleagues quote the DSM-5 requirement that symptoms "cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning" (DSM-5, quoted in Maj et al., [The clinical characterization of the adult patient with depression](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7491646/), World Psychiatry, 2020). For PTSD, NIMH states that "People may be diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) if their symptoms last for an extended period after a traumatic event and begin to interfere with aspects of daily life, such as relationships or work" ([NIMH, PTSD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/post-traumatic-stress-disorder-ptsd)). The specific duration criteria for depression, anxiety and bipolar disorder are not covered by the sources opened for this page; that scope gap is flagged to the supervisor.

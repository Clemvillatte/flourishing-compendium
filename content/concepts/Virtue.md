Virtue (Greek aretē) is excellence of character: a stable disposition to feel, choose and act well, acquired through [[Habituation|habituation]] rather than by nature or reasoning in a single moment. Aristotle's formula is that virtue comes about "neither by nature nor apart from nature" (Nicomachean Ethics 1103a, 24-25), and that habituation from childhood "makes an enormous difference" to how a life turns out (1103b, 23-25). Virtue is a hexis, an active holding of the soul in a "stable equilibrium" from which one chooses knowingly and for the sake of the action itself ([IEP, Aristotle's Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/aris-eth/)).

### The mean and practical wisdom

The doctrine of the mean is not a numerical midpoint. Virtue "is a mean" between excess and deficiency "by its thinghood and by the account that unfolds what it is for it to be" (1107a, 7-8): courage sits between rashness and cowardice, temperance between indulgence and insensibility, and where the mean falls is judged by perception in the particular case, which is why practical wisdom depends on moral virtue for its aim (1144a, 7-9) and why the merely continent person, holding impulses in check through anxious control, is not yet the virtuous one, whose parts of the soul are blended in choice ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/aris-eth/)).

### The empirical fight

Modern psychology put the idea on trial. Situationists such as John Doris and Gilbert Harman read the classic experiments (Good Samaritan studies, Milgram-style obedience) as showing that people "typically lack character" (Doris 2002, p. 2) and that morally trivial features of situations move behaviour more than global traits do ([SEP, Empirical Approaches to Moral Character](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character-empirical/)). Defenders answer that the experiments test one-off behaviour while character reveals itself over time and varied circumstances, and that the situationists debunk a caricature of the virtues ([Kamtekar 2004](https://www.mit.edu/~shaslang/mprg/kamtekarSVE.pdf)). Positive psychology translated the concept for research: the VIA classification organises 24 character strengths under six virtues (wisdom, courage, humanity, justice, temperance, transcendence), and using signature strengths in new ways yields modest, reliable gains in well-being (Peterson and Seligman 2004; the wiki's PP036).

### The modern revival and the compendium

Contemporary virtue ethics has two instigating documents: Anscombe's "Modern Moral Philosophy" (1958), which argued that obligation language survives from a law conception of ethics that has lost its lawgiver and proposed a return to virtue and moral psychology, and MacIntyre's After Virtue (1981), which rebuilt the virtues out of practices, narrative unity and tradition ([IEP, Virtue Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/virtue/)). The compendium carries the idea across tracks: AF036 (affect follows behaviour) is habituation's psychology; PP036 applies the VIA strengths; Y040 warns from the Gita against virtue performed for an audience. Confucian [[Ren]], the Buddhist pāramitās and Stoic "virtue as the only good" are the same idea under other skies.

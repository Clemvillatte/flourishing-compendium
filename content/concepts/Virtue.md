Virtue (Greek aretē) is excellence of character: a stable disposition to feel, choose and act well. In Aristotle's account the moral virtues are acquired through [[Habituation|habituation]]: they come about "neither by nature nor apart from nature" (Nicomachean Ethics 1103a, 24-25), and habituation from childhood "makes an enormous difference" to how a life turns out (1103b, 23-25). Virtue is a hexis, an active holding of the soul in a "stable equilibrium" from which one chooses knowingly and for the sake of the action itself (as the [IEP account of Aristotle's ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/aris-eth/) puts it).

### The mean and practical wisdom

The doctrine of the mean is not a numerical midpoint. Aristotle holds that each virtue "is a mean" between excess and deficiency "by its thinghood and by the account that unfolds what it is for it to be" (1107a, 7-8): courage sits between rashness and cowardice, temperance between indulgence and insensibility. Where the mean falls is judged by perception in the particular case, which is why practical wisdom depends on moral virtue for its aim (1144a, 7-9). It is also why the merely continent person, holding impulses in check through anxious control, is not yet the virtuous one: the virtuous person's parts of the soul are blended in choice, and the life of virtue is pleasant in its own right (1099a, 6-17). Aristotle ties the whole to flourishing: virtue is the practical road to effective action, though happiness remains vulnerable to misfortune ([IEP](https://iep.utm.edu/aris-eth/)).

### Character and situation

Modern psychology put the idea on trial. Situationists such as John Doris and Gilbert Harman argue from the classic experiments (Good Samaritan studies, Milgram-style obedience) that people "typically lack character" (Doris 2002, p. 2) and that morally trivial features of situations move behaviour more than global traits do ([SEP, Empirical Approaches to Moral Character](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character-empirical/)). Rachana Kamtekar replies that the situationists debunk a caricature: the experiments test one-off behaviour, while character reveals itself over time and varied circumstances, and the virtues are best modelled as skills ([Kamtekar 2004](https://www.mit.edu/~shaslang/mprg/kamtekarSVE.pdf)). Positive psychology translated the concept for research as measurement: the VIA classification organises 24 character strengths under six virtues (wisdom, courage, humanity, justice, temperance, transcendence; Peterson and Seligman 2004; the wiki's PP036 trains one exercise from it).

### The modern revival and the compendium

Contemporary virtue ethics has two instigating documents: Anscombe's "Modern Moral Philosophy" (1958), which argued that obligation language survives from a law conception of ethics that has lost its lawgiver, and proposed a return to virtue and moral psychology, and MacIntyre's After Virtue (1981), which rebuilt the virtues out of practices, narrative unity and tradition ([IEP, Virtue Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/virtue/)). The compendium carries the idea across tracks, as comparisons rather than identities: AF036 (affect follows behaviour) is habituation's psychological cousin; PP036 applies the VIA strengths; Y040 warns from the Gita against virtue performed for an audience. Confucian [[Ren]], the Buddhist pāramitās and the Stoic "virtue as the only good" are the same family of ideas under other skies.

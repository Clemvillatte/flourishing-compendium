“Durée” — Bergson, Time and Free Will.

Durée is Henri Bergson's name for time as it is actually lived: a continuous flow in which each moment carries the ones before it ([SEP, "Henri Bergson"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)). He introduced it in his first book, the Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience (1889), which appeared in English as Time and Free Will ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)), in F. L. Pogson's authorized translation ([Gutenberg ebook 56852, title page](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm)). Pogson translates the term as "pure duration" ([ibid.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm)).

> "Pure duration is the form which the succession of our conscious states assumes when our ego lets itself live, when it refrains from separating its present state from its former states. For this purpose it need not be entirely absorbed in the passing sensation or idea; for then, on the contrary, it would no longer endure. Nor need it forget its former states: it is enough that, in recalling these states, it does not set them alongside its actual state as one point alongside another, but forms both the past and the present states into an organic whole, as happens when we recall the notes of a tune, melting, so to speak, into one another." (Bergson, [Time and Free Will, Pogson translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm), Chapter II, pp. 100-101)

### Lived and measured time

Bergson's target is clock time: a clock face, a calendar and a timeline all set moments side by side, each one external to the next, and that layout, he argues, is borrowed from space rather than being the time a person lives ([Bergson, Time and Free Will, Pogson translation, ch. II](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm)).

His chapter states the contrast directly: within the ego there is succession without mutual externality, while outside the ego there is mutual externality without succession (Chapter II, p. 108, [ibid.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm)). The [Stanford Encyclopedia's entry on Bergson](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/) reads this as a response to Kant, who had treated time as a homogeneous medium. Bergson unmixes space and time, and the entry describes duration as a qualitative multiplicity whose states interpenetrate rather than sit side by side ([SEP, ibid.](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)).

The book ends by tying the whole account to freedom, in its Conclusion ([p. 232](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm)):

> "To act freely is to recover possession of oneself, and to get back into pure duration." (Bergson, [Time and Free Will, Pogson translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/56852/56852-h/56852-h.htm), Conclusion, p. 232)

In Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), openness to unwanted inner experience and action guided by chosen values are trained as a pair, not as alternatives. The page title is the compendium's phrasing of that pairing; ACT's own terms are acceptance and committed action. In the model's first presentation, Hayes and colleagues defined six core components, with acceptance and committed action among them.

> "The approach combines processes of acceptance and mindfulness with those of commitment and behavioural change to produce increased psychological flexibility and an expanded behavioural repertoire." (Webster, [Introduction to acceptance and commitment therapy](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/introduction-to-acceptance-and-commitment-therapy/D67B44FDED4147CA35C1AA8FA9D3DDA5), Advances in Psychiatric Treatment 17(4), 2011)

### What each half contributes

Acceptance in ACT is a position of non-judgemental awareness towards thoughts, feelings and bodily sensations as they arise. The client learns to notice that attempts to control unwanted private experience create a struggle, and to let go of that struggle while still working on external issues that can be changed. Committed action is the other half: learning to move in valued directions while in the presence of unwanted or painful thoughts and feelings. The two are trained together because each names what the other leaves out: acceptance without valued action becomes mere tolerance, and action that waits until every difficult feeling is defeated never starts.

The compendium uses "Functional accounts of emotion" for the family of theories that explain emotions by what they do. The framing paper by Dacher Keltner and James Gross:

> "Summarising diverse theories and observations, we propose that functional accounts of emotions: (1) address why humans have emotions; (2) define emotions as solutions to problems and opportunities related to physical and social survival; (3) treat emotions as systems of interrelated components; and (4) focus on the beneficial consequences of emotions." (Keltner and Gross 1999, abstract, [Cognition and Emotion 13(5):467-480](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/026999399379140))

### What the approach adds

The abstract presents the account's scope: the article outlines "the history, elements, and variations of functional accounts of emotions" and considers "how functional accounts of emotion vary, including in terms of their level of analysis, specificity, manner of organisation, and range of focus" (Keltner and Gross 1999, [abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/026999399379140)). The empirical strategies it lists include "the study of emotion dysfunction, the effects of emotions on others, and the relations between emotions and personal and social outcomes" (Keltner and Gross 1999, [abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/026999399379140)).

### The dyad finding

Richardson, Dale and Kirkham recorded the gaze of pairs of subjects in live, unscripted conversation about things they could see in front of them. Cross-recurrence analysis found the eye movements of the two speakers coupled across several seconds, with the coupling increasing when both had heard the same background information beforehand.

> "These results provide a direct quantification of joint attention during unscripted conversation and show that it is influenced by knowledge in the common ground." (Richardson, Dale & Kirkham, 2007, [Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01914.x))

### The group finding

Dikker and colleagues extended the measurement to whole groups with portable EEG. As summarised in Davidesco's review:

> "Dikker et al. (2017) recorded EEG activity from a group of 12 students in a biology high school classroom." "The extent to which brain activity was synchronized across students was found to predict self-reported student engagement: Students who reported being more engaged exhibited higher brain synchrony with their peers." (Davidesco, 2020, [CBE Life Sciences Education](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8711813/))

The same review reports the classroom activities as lectures, instructional videos and group discussions, and notes that pairs of students who demonstrated higher brain synchrony also reported higher social closeness.

### What the studies show

Richardson, Dale and Kirkham recorded the gaze of pairs of subjects in live, unscripted conversation about things they could see in front of them. Cross-recurrence analysis found the eye movements of the two speakers coupled across several seconds, with the coupling increasing when both had heard the same background information beforehand.

> "These results provide a direct quantification of joint attention during unscripted conversation and show that it is influenced by knowledge in the common ground." (Richardson, Dale & Kirkham, 2007, [Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01914.x))

The finding is a behavioural one: it tracks where eyes go, and the study reports the common-ground manipulation rather than an inference about inner states.

Dikker and colleagues extended the measurement to whole groups with portable EEG, recording twelve high school students across a semester of regular classes. The study's claim is about synchrony across a class rather than within a pair.

> "A novel analysis technique to assess group-based neural coherence demonstrates that the extent to which brain activity is synchronized across students predicts both student class engagement and social dynamics." (Dikker et al., 2017, [Current Biology, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28457867/))

The same record adds that brain-to-brain synchrony is described by the authors as a possible neural marker for dynamic social interactions, likely driven by shared attention mechanisms.

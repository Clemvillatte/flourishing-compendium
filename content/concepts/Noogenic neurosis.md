Noogenic neurosis is Viktor Frankl's term for a neurosis that begins in the dimension of meaning rather than in the psyche. In The Will to Meaning he introduces the coinage and its contrast:

> "For this type of neuroses, logotherapy has coined the term 'noögenic neuroses' in contrast to neuroses in the usual sense of the word, i.e., psychogenic neuroses. Noögenic neuroses have their origin not in the psychological but rather in the 'noölogical' (from the Greek 'noos' meaning mind) dimension of the human existence." (Frankl, The Will to Meaning, [excerpt](https://www.panarchy.org/frankl/meaning.html))

### What distinguishes it

The chapter's definition paragraph runs:

> "Noögenic neuroses do not emerge from conflicts between drives and instincts but rather from conflicts between various values; in other words, from moral conflicts, or, to speak in a more general way, from spiritual problems. Among such problems, existential frustration often plays a large role."

The passage also notes that within logotherapy "spiritual" does not have a primarily religious connotation but refers to the specifically human dimension. The consequence Frankl draws is that the adequate therapy for such cases is logotherapy rather than psychotherapy in general.

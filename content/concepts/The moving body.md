Merleau-Ponty devotes a chapter of Part One of Phenomenology of Perception to the body as it moves. There he argues that movement is not commanded by a spectator mind; it is the body itself that understands.

> "it is the body which 'catches' (kapiert) and 'comprehends' movement. The cultivation of habit is indeed the grasping of a significance, but it is the motor grasping of a motor significance." (Merleau-Ponty, Phenomenology of Perception, "The Spatiality of One's Own Body and Motility", Colin Smith translation, [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/merleaupontyphenomenologyofperception))

### What the moving body does

The chapter's examples are the feather in a hat kept at a safe distance without calculation, and the car driven through a narrow opening by someone who simply sees that they can "get through". Movement here is a form of understanding: "The body is our general medium for having a world". It posits a biological world around the acts of conservation, elaborates new significance through motor habits such as dancing, and builds instruments when its natural means fall short, projecting a cultural world around itself.

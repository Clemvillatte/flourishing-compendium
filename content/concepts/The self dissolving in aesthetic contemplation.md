Schopenhauer's third book describes what remains when a person stops using the world for their purposes. In aesthetic contemplation the perceiver gives the whole power of the mind to perception and loses himself in the object: for that time he exists only as the pure subject, and the individual, with its wants, dissolves.

> "he who is sunk in this perception is no longer individual, for in such perception the individual has lost himself; but he is pure, will-less, painless, timeless subject of knowledge." (Schopenhauer, The World as Will and Idea III, §34, [Haldane and Kemp translation, Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/38427/38427-h/38427-h.html))

The loss is a relief. In section 38 he adds that the person tormented by passion, want or care is suddenly revived and restored by a single free glance into nature: happiness and unhappiness disappear along with the individual, and it is all the same whether the perceiving eye belongs to a mighty king or to a wretched beggar.

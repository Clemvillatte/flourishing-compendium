“Internal relations” — Whitehead, Process and Reality.

An internal relation is a relation that enters into what its terms are. On the usual view a thing could have been just what it is with different relations, as a brick is the same brick wherever it is laid. On the doctrine Whitehead names, at least some relations are not like that: remove them and the thing is not the same thing any more.

> "This internal relatedness is the reason why an event can be found only just where it is and how it is,—that is to say, in just one definite set of relationships. For each relationship enters into the essence of the event; so that, apart from that relationship, the event would not be itself. This is what is meant by the very notion of internal relations." — [Whitehead, Science and the Modern World, ch. VII (1926), full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/68611/68611-h/68611-h.htm)

### What turns on it

The chapter presents the doctrine carefully rather than absolutely: the relatednesses of an event are internal "so far as concerns that event, though not necessarily so far as concerns the other relata." The original entry citation to Process and Reality marks the later, fuller development of the metaphysics. The everyday question the doctrine raises for a life is illustrative rather than demonstrated: if a person's relations were internal to them in this way, a life would not be a self that happens to have relationships but a self partly made of them.

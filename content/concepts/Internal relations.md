“Internal relations” — Whitehead, Process and Reality.

An internal relation is a relation that enters into what its terms are. On the usual view a thing could have been just what it is with different relations, as a brick is the same brick wherever it is laid. On the doctrine Whitehead names, at least some relations are not like that: remove them and the thing is not the same thing any more.

> "The theory of the relationship between events at which we have now arrived is based first upon the doctrine that the relatednesses of an event are all internal relations, so far as concerns that event, though not necessarily so far as concerns the other relata. This internal relatedness is the reason why an event can be found only just where it is and how it is,—that is to say, in just one definite set of relationships. For each relationship enters into the essence of the event; so that, apart from that relationship, the event would not be itself. This is what is meant by the very notion of internal relations." — [Whitehead, Science and the Modern World, ch. VII (1926), full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/68611/68611-h/68611-h.htm)

### What turns on it

Whitehead draws a boundary inside the doctrine: the relation is internal for the event, "though not necessarily so far as concerns the other relata." The seed citation to Process and Reality marks where the metaphysics is completed; the doctrine is already at work here, where it does the everyday duty the compendium cares about. If a person's relations are internal to them, then a life is not a self that happens to have relationships but a self that is made of them, and changing the relationships changes the being. The Buddhist parallels in the seed links, on nothing existing alone and emptiness as relational fullness, carry the same conviction from the other side of the world.

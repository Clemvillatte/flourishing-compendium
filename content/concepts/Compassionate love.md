“Compassionate love” — Underwood, 2008.

Compassionate love is a form of love centred on the good of another person. Lynn Underwood's conceptual work (the seed cites 2008 and 2009) frames it as "attitudes and actions related to giving of self for the good of the other", and Susan Sprecher and Beverley Fehr's 2005 scale made it measurable. Berscheid's 2010 review counts it among four types of love in adult relationships.

### Definitions

> "Compassionate love is an attitude toward other(s), either close others or strangers or all of humanity; containing feelings, cognitions, and behaviors that are focused on caring, concern, tenderness, and an orientation toward supporting, helping, and understanding the other(s), particularly when the other(s) is (are) perceived to be suffering or in need." (Sprecher & Fehr 2005, p. 630, [PDF](https://ggsc.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Sprecher_Fehr_2005_Compassionate_love_for_close_others_and_humanity.pdf))

Sprecher and Fehr say they chose the name partly because of Underwood's work (2002), noting her point that compassion alone leaves out "some of the emotional and transcendent components which the word love brings in". Underwood's own wording (2009, p. 4) is known here only from a later paper that quotes it. ([PMC5763452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5763452/))

### One of four loves

Berscheid's "Love in the Fourth Dimension" (Annual Review of Psychology 61, 2010) argues that it is important to consider the presence or absence of four types of love, each tied to different causal conditions and a different course over time. A later paper names them as compassionate, passionate, companionate and attachment love. ([Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.psych.093008.100318); [PMC5763452](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5763452/))

### What the scale studies found

Sprecher and Fehr's three validation studies (total N = 700) used a 21-item scale. Compassionate love was moderately, not highly, correlated with empathy, which they take as evidence the two are distinct, and it was associated with self-reported helping. Compassionate love for strangers and humanity was more strongly associated with volunteering, while compassionate love for close others was more strongly related to giving social support. People scored higher for close others than for strangers, and women scored higher than men. ([Sprecher & Fehr 2005](https://ggsc.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Sprecher_Fehr_2005_Compassionate_love_for_close_others_and_humanity.pdf))

### Limits to keep in view

The participants in the first study were undergraduate students at a midwestern U.S. university (N = 354, 65% women), and the measures were self-reports. The findings show how a questionnaire relates to other self-reports in that population, not how people behave across cultures or life stages.

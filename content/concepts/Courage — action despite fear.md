“Courage: action despite fear” — Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III.

Courage as action despite fear is Aristotle's definition in the Nicomachean Ethics. The brave person does not lack fear; they fear the right things:

> The man, then, who faces and who fears the right things and from the right motive, in the right way and from the right time, and who feels confidence under the corresponding conditions, is brave; for the brave man feels and acts according to the merits of the case and in whatever way the rule directs. — Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III, Ross translation, [Internet Classics Archive (opened)](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)

The despite is explicit. The chapter says the brave person feels the pain and faces it anyway:

> death and wounds will be painful to the brave man and against his will, but he will face them because it is noble to do so or because it is base not to do so. — Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics III, Ross translation, [Internet Classics Archive (opened)](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)

The chapter adds that it is the mark of a brave man to face things that are, and seem, terrible for a man, because it is noble to do so and disgraceful not to do so. The compendium builds its approach-despite-discomfort practices on this ground.

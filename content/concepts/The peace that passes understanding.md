The peace that passes understanding is Paul's phrase for a peace that guards the mind without being produced by it: not the calm that follows worked-out reasoning, but one that "passeth" it. The seed names Philippians 4.7, and the phrase is the King James rendering of the verse.

### The verse

> "And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus." (Philippians 4:7, KJV, [Biblehub](https://www.biblehub.com/philippians/4-7.htm))

The image of the verse is of a garrison: the peace "keeps" hearts and minds the way a guard keeps a city. What it guards them against is the anxiety of the preceding verse's world, and it does so without those minds first having to think their way to safety.

### In the compendium

The compendium holds this phrase beside its near opposite, the Spinozist peace that comes through understanding, and treats the two as describing the same shore from two sides.

- [[SP089 — Rest in the peace that understanding brings]] — the Spinozist counterpart, where the clear and distinct idea is the garrison
- [[SP051 — Understand the affect to transform it]] — the remedy through understanding that the phrase contrasts with
- [[SP048 — Aim at the third kind, which is joy]] — the third kind of knowledge and its joy, Spinoza's nearest neighbor to the verse
- [[Y132 — End in rest, not striving]] — the Gita's "call that state 'peace'", which ends in rest rather than argument

Anti-representationalism is Rorty's target-marking in Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature: the rejection of the picture of mind as a mirror of reality, and with it the idea of knowledge as accuracy of representation. His own statement of the picture:

> "The picture which holds traditional philosophy captive is that of the mind as a great mirror, containing various representations – some accurate, some not – and capable of being studied by pure, nonempirical methods."

([Rorty, Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature, quoted in the FIU lecture notes](https://faculty.fiu.edu/~harrisk/Notes/Epistemology%20and%20Metaphysics/American%20Pragmtism%20-%20Rorty%20Edited.htm).)

### What replaces the mirror

The book's constructive claim, from Sellars and Quine, is that justification is social rather than representational: "[...] we understand knowledge when we understand the social justification of belief, and thus have no need to view it as accuracy of representation" (PMN, 170; [SEP, Richard Rorty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rorty/)). The upshot: "we see knowledge as a matter of conversation and of social practice, rather than as an attempt to mirror nature" (PMN, 171; [same](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rorty/)). Rorty's label for the stance is "epistemological behaviorism," an attitude he attributes to Dewey and Wittgenstein (PMN, 174; [same](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rorty/)).

### The vocabulary choice

The position connects directly to the compendium's principle on choosing vocabularies by what they let you do. Rorty's later formulation is the anti-representationalist's answer to the question of what language is for: there is "no fixed task for language to perform," and the choice between vocabularies is made by their consequences rather than by their correspondence to an independent structure ([Koop, "Rorty's Linguistic Turn," PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/KOORLT)). The mirror picture and the tool picture give the same sentences different jobs; the anti-representationalist says the tool picture is the one worth having.

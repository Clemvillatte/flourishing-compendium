**Kohlberg's theory of moral development** — moral reasoning ascends preconventional, conventional and postconventional levels through six stages.

Lawrence Kohlberg built the theory on people's reasoned responses to moral dilemmas. A later paper using the theory states its levels compactly:

> "Kohlberg's theory of moral development asserts that people progress through different stages of moral reasoning as their cognitive abilities and social interactions mature. Individuals at the lowest stage of moral reasoning (preconventional stage) judge moral issues based on self-interest, those with a medium stage (conventional stage) judge them based on compliance with rules and norms, and those at the highest stage (postconventional stage) judge moral issues based on universal principles and shared ideals." (Jean-Tron et al., [How the COVID-19 pandemic affects the moral reasoning of pediatric residents and the general population, BMC Medical Education 23 (2023), 380, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37226125/))

### The sequence and its critics

The theory claims a fixed order: the sequence does not vary, though the rate of progression and the end stage reached vary by individual, and the justification's logic, not its content, assigns the stage ([Haidet et al., 2003, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12668541/)). The compendium carries the theory's best-known critique in a linked story and concept: Carol Gilligan's different voice, on which moral maturity is also voiced as care and relationship, not only as justice ([ST246 — Gilligan's Different Voice]; see [[Gilligan's ethics-of-care theory]]).

“Conflict spirals” — Gottman.

Conflict spirals name the pattern in which one partner's negative affect is met with the other's negative affect, escalating over time. The original demonstration is the Levenson and Gottman longitudinal study:

> "Several affective variables also predicted decline in marital satisfaction, including a pronounced sex difference in negative affect reciprocity: Marital satisfaction declined most when husbands did not reciprocate their wives' negative affect, and when wives did reciprocate their husbands' negative affect." (Levenson & Gottman, 1983, [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4020618/))

### The replication problem

The Gottman affective-process models have not always replicated. A 2006 study by Heyman and colleagues reported:

> "The major findings of Gottman et al. failed to replicate. In particular, men's rejection of their partners' influence, the lack of men's deescalation of partners' negative affect, and women's negative start up were not predictive of relationship status." (Heyman et al., 2006, [Journal of Family Psychology, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1828692/))

The same study found that only 2 of the 22 affective processes examined were significantly associated with remaining intact versus separation.

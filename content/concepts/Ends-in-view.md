“Ends-in-view” — Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct.

Dewey uses "end-in-view" for the foreseen consequence that guides present action. He argues that an end belongs inside activity, as a tool for directing it, not outside it as a fixed destination. The passages below are from Part Three, section VI, of Human Nature and Conduct (1922), read in the Project Gutenberg text.

### Ends arise inside action

> "They are not strictly speaking ends or termini of action at all. They are terminals of deliberation, and so turning points in activity." (Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, Part Three, VI, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt))

He sets this against theories that place the end beyond action, whether pleasure (the utilitarian view) or perfection and self-realisation, and says the popular notion of "ideals" is infected by the idea of a fixed end beyond activity.

> "Ends are foreseen consequences which arise in the course of activity and which are employed to give activity added meaning and to direct its further course." (Dewey, same section)

### The end as a means in present action

> "In a strict sense an end-in-view is a means in present action; present action is not a means to a remote end." (Dewey, same section)

His image is the mariner: "A mariner does not sail towards the stars, but by noting the stars he is aided in conducting his present activity of sailing." Reaching the harbour changes the direction of activity and does not end it. As he puts it, "We know without thinking that our "ends" are perforce beginnings."

### Why they are revisable

Earlier in the same Part, Dewey contrasts two uses of reason. One holds an end fixed and deliberates only about means. The other treats the end as open.

> "The former regards the end-in-view in deliberation as tentative and permits, nay encourages the coming into view of consequences which will transform it and create a new purpose and plan." (Dewey, Part Three, section on deliberation and calculation, same text)

This is the textual basis for the compendium's "revise them" in the principle [[P041 — Set ends-in-view, and revise them]]. The wording "revisable goals" is the compendium's gloss. Dewey's own word is "tentative".

### What this does not settle

Dewey is describing how purposeful action works. He does not offer a goal-setting method, and the link to modern goal-adjustment or behavioural-activation research is the compendium's own bridge, not one Dewey draws.

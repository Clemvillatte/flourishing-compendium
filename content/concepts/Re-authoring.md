“Re-authoring” — White & Epston, Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends.

Re-authoring is the narrative therapy practice introduced to the field by David Epston and Michael White. The Dulwich Centre's practice description states it:

> "Re-authoring conversations take place between a therapist and the person(s) who have come to see them and involve the identification and co-creation of alternative story-lines of identity." ([Re-Authoring: Some answers to commonly asked questions, Dulwich Centre](https://dulwichcentre.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Re-Authoring_some_answers_to_commonly_asked_questions.pdf))

### Contradictions as material

The practice rests on an assumption about stories: "no one story can possibly encapsulate the totality of a person's experience, there will always be inconsistencies and contradictions". The work begins from events that contradict the dominant problem story (unique outcomes), and builds outward: "no matter how significant a single event might become, no matter how strongly it may contradict the dominant problematic story-line, one event on its own will always be vulnerable. It is vitally important to link the unique outcome to other events, to link these into an alternative story-line."

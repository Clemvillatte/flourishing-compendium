Social exchange theory is a family of models concerned with how social relationships begin, continue and end ([Bosak, 2019, “Definition” and “Introduction”](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-16999-6_1999-1)). In these models, people or institutions exchange material and nonmaterial resources through successive interactions ([“Introduction”](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-319-16999-6_1999-1)).

### Exchange and theoretical limits

Cropanzano and Mitchell's review examines the rules of exchange, the resources exchanged, and the relationships that develop ([2005, abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0149206305279602)). They caution that applications and tests often rely on ideas that have not been fully specified ([abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0149206305279602)).

> Despite its usefulness, theoretical ambiguities within SET remain.
>
> Russell Cropanzano and Marie S. Mitchell, “Social Exchange Theory: An Interdisciplinary Review,” [abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0149206305279602). SET abbreviates social exchange theory.

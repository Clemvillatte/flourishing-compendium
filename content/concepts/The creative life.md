The creative life, as Nietzsche presents it in Thus Spake Zarathustra, is a mode of existence rather than a career: the person becomes a source of new values instead of inheriting old ones. The book teaches it through images of transformation.

> "I tell you: one must still have chaos in one, to give birth to a dancing star. I tell you: ye have still chaos in you." (Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, Prologue §5, Thomas Common translation, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-0.txt))

### What creation requires

In the first chapter's metamorphoses, the spirit that loves tradition's "Thou shalt" must become a lion that wins freedom for new creating, and the lion must still become a child: "Innocence is the child, and forgetfulness, a new beginning, a game, a self-rolling wheel, a first movement, a holy Yea". Nietzsche adds that "for the game of creating, my brethren, there is needed a holy Yea unto life". The creative stance is the child's, and it draws on what has not yet been formed.

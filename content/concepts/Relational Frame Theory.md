**Relational Frame Theory** — Hayes — human cognition is learned, arbitrarily applicable relating; human language creates the potential for a distinct form of suffering.

Steven Hayes and colleagues built RFT on one behavioral capacity: relating one event to another based on social convention rather than the events' physical properties. Teach a child that a name is an object, and the object is the name for free. Language and cognition grow from that capacity, and the theory has often been argued to provide the basic science foundation for Acceptance and Commitment Therapy.

> "Despite the conservatism of an RFT approach, therefore, a new type of behavioral process is suggested and a new technical term is offered. The new process is arbitrarily applicable relational responding (or framing events relationally)" (Hayes, Fox, et al. 2001, p. 46, quoted in [Hayes & Barnes-Holmes 2004, p. 215](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1285006/pdf/15540506.pdf))

The three defining properties of a frame are mutual entailment, combinatorial entailment, and transformation of stimulus function: what a thing means, and the effect it can have, travels through the relation. A 2024 overview by Harte and Barnes-Holmes gives the properties in plain language, each with its own example:

> "According to RFT, there are three fundamental properties that define a relational frame: mutual entailment, combinatorial entailment, and the transformation of stimulus functions. Mutual entailment refers to a bidirectional relation between two stimuli. For example, if A is taller than B, this mutually entails that B is shorter than A. Combinatorial entailment refers to novel relations that emerge between and among stimuli when three or more stimuli are related. For example, if A is taller than B and B is taller than C, then additional relations will emerge such that A is taller than C and C is shorter than A. The transformation of stimulus functions refers to a change in the functions of one or more stimuli in a frame resulting from a change in the functions of other stimuli in that frame. For example, if X, Y, and Z are coordinate, and reinforcing functions are established for X (through direct pairing), Y and Z may also acquire reinforcing properties. It is critical to note that this transformation of stimulus functions occurs in the absence of direct reinforcement." (Harte & Barnes-Holmes 2024, ["The Traditional RFT Account", Perspectives on Behavior Science](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40614-024-00407-3.pdf))

Two words in the passage can use unpacking. Coordinate means standing in a same-as relation: things in a coordinate frame may then share functions. Reinforcing functions are the pulls that make something worth pursuing, as the pull of a favored food. The third property carries the theory's reach into human life: a change in the pull or the push of one member of a frame can reach the other members without direct reinforcement of those members.

A concrete picture, offered as an illustration rather than a study: someone reading apartment listings learns that flat A is closer to the station than flat B, and derives without being told that B is farther from the station than A. Told next that flat B is closer to the station than flat C, the person derives that A is closer than C as well. If the person values short commutes, the station-side flat gains appeal through the relation alone, not through any visit to it.

### What defines a frame

Hayes and Barnes-Holmes distinguish the features that define a relational frame from the learning history proposed to explain it. They describe that history as reinforcement across multiple examples.

> "The contextually controlled qualities of mutual entailment, combinatorial mutual entailment, and transformation of stimulus functions are outcomes, not processes. They do not explain relational frames; they define them." (Hayes & Barnes-Holmes 2004, [p. 213](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1285006/pdf/15540506.pdf))

That history is operant learning, in their words:

> "The principles and procedures that are employed to explain relational operants in RFT are identical to those used to explain any operant: contacted consistencies in contingencies across multiple exemplars." (Hayes & Barnes-Holmes 2004, [p. 216](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1285006/pdf/15540506.pdf))

### Why the theory exists

The theory was laid out in the 2001 collection Relational Frame Theory: A Post-Skinnerian Account of Human Language and Cognition, edited by Hayes, Barnes-Holmes and Roche. The 2004 article is the authors' reply to Palmer's review of that book. In it, they argue that Skinner's definition of verbal behavior constrained experimental research on language and cognition, pulling researchers back to the animal laboratory rather than toward human language.

> "In the RFT book we suggested that the failure to mount a robust experimental program on language and cognition is an inevitable result of Skinner's definition of verbal behavior." (Hayes & Barnes-Holmes 2004, [p. 218](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1285006/pdf/15540506.pdf))

### The clinical reach

The link to Acceptance and Commitment Therapy is stated by later summaries in careful terms:

> "Both relational frame theory (RFT) and acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) are based on the assumption that the evolution of human language (conceptualized as derived relational responding) creates the potential for a form of psychological suffering unique to the human species. Furthermore, it has often been argued that RFT provides the basic science foundation for ACT." (Harte & Barnes-Holmes, [A Primer on Relational Frame Theory, 2021](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/38851/chapter-abstract/337815591))

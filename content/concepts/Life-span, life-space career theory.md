Donald Super's account of careers as a lifelong development of the self, in which a person moves through stages while enacting multiple social roles.

> "The life-span, life-space theory comprises five stages (i.e., life-span) of growth, exploration, establishment, maintenance, and disengagement/decline, with each stage addressing career development tasks and roles (i.e., life-space)." (Luke, McIlveen & Perera, [A Thematic Analysis of Career Adaptability in Retirees Who Return to Work](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4756173/), Frontiers in Psychology, 2016, Introduction, summarizing Super)

The two halves of the name divide the theory's two axes. The life-span axis supplies the five stages; the life-space axis supplies the roles and their theatres: in the Life-Career Rainbow, each individual takes on multiple roles or life spaces, often simultaneously and to varying degrees, such as citizen, worker, and parent, and each of these roles is enacted in different theatres or life-spans, for example home and workplace ([Lytle, Foley & Cotter, 2015, §Super's Career Development Theory](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4474155/)). Adjustment to career and working life is seen as central to and continuous with self-concept ([Luke et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4756173/)).

Attentional breadth is the spatial extent of the area over which spatial attention is applied ([Goodhew, The Breadth of Visual Attention, ch. 1 excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811087/96217/excerpt/9781108796217_excerpt.pdf)):

> "For now, the working definition will be that attentional breadth refers to the spatial extent of the area over which spatial attention is applied, with the assumption that the area is contiguous and approximately elliptical."
> — [Goodhew, The Breadth of Visual Attention, Cambridge University Press (open excerpt)](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811087/96217/excerpt/9781108796217_excerpt.pdf)

Global/local processing concerns the level of a hierarchical stimulus, illustrated by Navon's compound figures ([Chrétien et al. 2026, "Global-local visual processing as a candidate mechanism"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12992678/)). The article describes the two levels:

> "These correspond to distinct attentional levels: on the one hand, global processing involves perceiving the overall structure or configuration of a stimulus, whereas on the other hand local processing focuses on its finer details or individual elements."
> — [Chrétien, Poirel, Knops & Viarouge 2026, "Local/global visual processing impacts numerosity-magnitude conflict resolution", Scientific Reports (open access)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12992678/)

The founding paradigm is Navon's hierarchical letters: "In his seminal work, Navon introduced hierarchical figures in which a large letter (the global level) is made up of smaller letters (the local level)" ([Chrétien et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12992678/)). The standard adult finding is the global precedence effect: "faster and more accurate responses to global-level targets, and interference from the unattended level in incongruent trials, particularly when attention needs to be directed to local information" ([Chrétien et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12992678/)). In Goodhew's summary of Navon's results, global-level information "was processed and affected performance even when participants were instructed to attend to the local level, whereas the reverse did not occur" — metaphorically, "participants are inclined to see the forest before the trees" ([Goodhew](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811087/96217/excerpt/9781108796217_excerpt.pdf)).

## The intention half

The planning half of the practice is Gollwitzer's implementation intention: a plan that links an anticipated critical situation to a goal-directed response, "Whenever situation x arises, I will initiate the goal-directed response y!" ([Gollwitzer, 1999](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf)). Implementation intentions are subordinate to goal intentions and specify the when, where, and how of responses leading to goal attainment, so that the person commits to respond to a certain situation in a specific manner rather than linking the self to a desired end state ([Gollwitzer, 1999](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf)). They delegate control of goal-directed responses to anticipated situational cues, which elicit the responses automatically when encountered ([Gollwitzer, 1999](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf)).

> “Of the participants who had reported strong goal intentions to perform a BSE during the next month, 100% did so if they had been induced to form additional implementation intentions. If no additional implementation intentions were formed, however, the strong goal intention alone produced only 53% goal completion.”
>
> — Gollwitzer, reporting Orbell, Hodgkins and Sheeran (1997), in [Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf), American Psychologist 54 (1999)

The same paper reports that for difficult-to-implement projects written down over Christmas vacation, two thirds of participants who had formed implementation intentions carried them out, against one fourth of those without ([Gollwitzer, 1999](https://www.prospectivepsych.org/sites/default/files/pictures/Gollwitzer_Implementation-intentions-1999.pdf)).

## The reflection half

The reflection half is tested in the Di Stefano, Gino, Pisano and Staats experiments. Once a person has accumulated a certain amount of experience with a task, the benefit of accumulating additional experience is inferior to the benefit of deliberately articulating and codifying the experience already accumulated ([Di Stefano et al., abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ebg/heccah/1181.html)). The framework was studied with a field experiment combined with two laboratory experiments, and the superior performance outcomes were explained by both a cognitive mechanism, improved task understanding, and an emotional one, increased self-efficacy ([Di Stefano et al., abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/p/ebg/heccah/1181.html)).

## The practice

A weekly review schedules both in one sitting: written reflection on the week's lessons, and if-then plans for the week ahead.

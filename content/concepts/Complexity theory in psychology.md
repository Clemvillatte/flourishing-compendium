Complexity theory in psychology is the application of the complex-systems approach to psychological processes. In this approach, the dynamics of mind and behavior are studied as arising from interactions among components or agents, with patterns that are emergent, self-organising and nonlinear.

> "Within the field of psychology, the complex systems approach focuses on explaining the dynamics resulting from the interactions of individuals or groups in the context of mental and behavioral processes (Holland, 2014; Tononi, 2004)." (Edanur Dayıoğlu, [review of Han L. J. van der Maas, Complex-Systems Research in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805186/), Psychometrika, 2025)

### The feature set

The same review describes how the approach explains its systems: "the characteristics of complex systems, such as self-organization, unpredictable behavior, and patterns that emerge as a result of the interactions of subsystems, are explained", and the book's first chapter addresses "the nonlinear nature of psychological processes" ([Dayıoğlu 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805186/)). For the account of complex adaptive systems generally, separate from the psychology-specific review, Sturmberg's overview supplies the interaction features: in it, interactions within a complex adaptive system are "Sensitive to initial condition, i.e. bound by their historical and contextual conditions", and "Complex dynamic systems have two key characteristics, they self-organise without external control and exhibit feedback resulting in newly created, i.e. emergent (at times unforeseen), behaviours" ([Sturmberg 2017, sections "A Detailed Description of 'Complex Adaptive Systems'" and "Complex Systems Theory: An Overview"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187952/)).

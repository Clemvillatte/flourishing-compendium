Complexity theory in psychology treats psychological phenomena as complex adaptive systems. In this view, the dynamics of mind and behavior arise from interactions among components or agents, and the resulting patterns are emergent, self-organising and nonlinear.

> "Within the field of psychology, the complex systems approach focuses on explaining the dynamics resulting from the interactions of individuals or groups in the context of mental and behavioral processes (Holland, 2014; Tononi, 2004)." (Edanur Dayıoğlu, [review of Han L. J. van der Maas, Complex-Systems Research in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805186/), Psychometrika, 2025)

### The feature set

The same review describes how the approach explains its systems: "the characteristics of complex systems, such as self-organization, unpredictable behavior, and patterns that emerge as a result of the interactions of subsystems, are explained", and the book's first chapter addresses "the nonlinear nature of psychological processes" ([Dayıoğlu 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805186/)). The fourth feature in the seed's list comes from the general complexity literature: in Sturmberg's overview, interactions within a complex adaptive system are "Sensitive to initial condition, i.e. bound by their historical and contextual conditions", and "Complex dynamic systems have two key characteristics, they self-organise without external control and exhibit feedback resulting in newly created, i.e. emergent (at times unforeseen), behaviours" ([Sturmberg 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7187952/)).

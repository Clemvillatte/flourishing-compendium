The construct's classic definition is Budner's, quoted here as a later review gives it:

> "Intolerance of ambiguity may be defined as 'the tendency to perceive (i.e., interpret) ambiguous situations as sources of threat' and tolerance of ambiguity as 'the tendency to perceive ambiguous situations as desirable'." (Budner 1962, Journal of Personality 30(1): 29-50, as quoted in [McLain, Kefallonitis and Armani 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00344/abstract))

Budner built the definition on a prior account of what makes a situation ambiguous: one that cannot be adequately structured or categorised because of insufficient cues, with novelty, complexity and insolubility as the three stimulus characteristics his scale used. The review adds that the perception of ambiguity can be threatening because it obscures potential harm, which explains why the threat reading is available at all: the unstructured situation could be hiding something. The same review notes his scale identified four possible reactions to ambiguity — phenomenological denial or submission, and operative denial or submission — which is why the construct covers behaviour and not just feeling.

The review the page anchors to reframes the construct as a stable tendency rather than a reaction to one class of situations: "Ambiguity tolerance is an individual's systematic, stable tendency to react to perceived ambiguity with greater or lesser intensity." The tendency ranges in theory from extreme aversion to extreme attraction, with aversion the more likely reading when "the ambiguous situation presents the possibility of tangible, negative consequences" ([McLain, Kefallonitis and Armani 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00344/abstract)).

**Plain words** Ambiguous means you cannot yet tell what this is. Some people read that as a threat to be closed fast, some as something even desirable. The first reading is a reflex that can be noticed, and it is the one that gets heavy the longer the ambiguity lasts.

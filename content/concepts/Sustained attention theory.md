Sustained attention theory studies how people hold a detection-ready state over time, and why that state degrades. A 2013 meta-analytic review by Robert Langner and Simon Eickhoff adopts a specific working definition of the function for simple tasks:

> "The attentional function that enables our prolonged engagement in intellectually unchallenging, uninteresting activities has been termed 'vigilant attention.'" (Langner and Eickhoff, [Sustaining attention to simple tasks: a meta-analytic review of the neural mechanisms of vigilant attention](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627747/), Psychological Bulletin 139(4):870-900, 2013, abstract)

### The core phenomenon

The review opens with the basic observation that "our ability to stay focused on a particular task is limited, resulting in well-known performance decrements with increasing time on task", and adds that "such decrements are even more likely if the task is cognitively simple and repetitive". In their Introduction the authors set the scope of their review: they use the term "to denote the process of sustaining efficient conscious stimulus processing over periods longer than about 10 seconds up to many minutes", covering "the simple detection or discrimination of stimuli, including a simple cognitive or motoric response but excluding 'higher' attentional or executive functions such as spatial orienting, resolving interference, dividing attention, or selecting between several overt responses".

### Accounts of the decline

The review discusses fatigue and resource depletion on one side and boredom and absentmindedness on the other, finding limitations in both accounts: "There is ample empirical evidence for both the fatigue/resource-depletion and the boredom/absentmindedness accounts, but neither is consistent with all the available data." The authors instead promote a synthesis proposed by Langner and colleagues: maintaining focus on an intrinsically non-rewarding but attentionally demanding task requires constant self-regulation, and self-regulatory power is "a limited resource that gets depleted with prolonged continuous use", so "self-control strength should decline over time while trying to maintain vigilant attention".

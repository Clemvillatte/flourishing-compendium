Movement snacks are brief, vigorous bouts of daily activity, so named in contrast with the way meals punctuate a day. A study from McMaster University's kinesiology department tested the idea on stairs:

> "Sedentary young adults were randomly assigned to perform 3 bouts/day of vigorously ascending a 3-flight stairwell (60 steps), separated by 1-4 h of recovery, 3 days/week for 6 weeks, or a nontraining control group (n = 12 each). Peak oxygen uptake was higher in the climbers after the intervention (P = 0.003), suggesting that stair climbing \"snacks\" are effective in improving cardiorespiratory fitness, although the absolute increase was modest." ([Jenkins et al., Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 44(6), 2019, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30649897/))

### What the finding supports

The modest-size caveat is part of the finding: three one-minute climbs a day, three days a week, moved fitness in the right direction in a small randomized sample of 24 sedentary young adults, and the authors' own summary keeps the qualification ([same record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30649897/)). The study is a feasibility-scale test of the pattern, not a demonstration that snacks replace longer exercise.

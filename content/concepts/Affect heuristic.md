The affect heuristic is the finding that positive or negative feelings about an object drive judgments of its risk and benefit. Finucane, Alhakami, Slovic, and Johnson (2000) proposed the term to explain a puzzle: risk and benefit are positively correlated in the world — high-risk activities tend to carry greater benefits — but negatively correlated in people's minds. Their abstract states the proposal: "Both studies suggest that people seem prone to using an 'affect heuristic' which improves judgmental efficiency by deriving both risk and benefit evaluations from a common source—affective reactions to the stimulus item" ([Finucane et al. 2000, JBDM abstract, Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(200001/03)13:1%3C1::AID-BDM333%3E3.0.CO;2-S)).

### How it works

Slovic's later summary gives the mechanism: "people judge a risk both by what they think about it and by how they feel about it. If their feelings toward an activity are favorable, they tend to judge the risks as low and the benefits as high; if their feelings toward the activity are unfavorable, they tend to make the opposite judgment" ([Slovic et al. 2002, "The affect heuristic," PDF](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/slovic-affect-heuristic-2002.pdf)). The two experimental signatures: the inverse risk-benefit relationship strengthens under time pressure, when analytic deliberation is squeezed out of the process ([Finucane et al. 2000, Study 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/(SICI)1099-0771(200001/03)13:1%3C1::AID-BDM333%3E3.0.CO;2-S)), and information about risk changes benefit judgments and vice versa, because both are derived from the same affective evaluation ([Slovic et al. 2002](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/slovic-affect-heuristic-2002.pdf)). The original effect replicated: r = -.74 in the 2000 study, with the negative relationship holding in later work ([Efendić et al. 2022 replication, SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/19485506211056761)).

### Why it matters here

The heuristic is fast and often useful; the cost is that feeling and evaluation arrive together, so a judgment of "low risk, high benefit" can be the output of liking rather than an assessment. The practical counter is to separate the two questions, how do I feel about this and what are the facts, before treating the feeling as a verdict.

In Nicomachean Ethics VIII.3, Aristotle counts the kinds of friendship by the things people find lovable: the useful, the pleasant, and the good. ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.3, Ross translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicomachean_Ethics_%28Ross%29/Book_Eight))

> "There are therefore three kinds of friendship, equal in number to the things that are lovable; for with respect to each there is a mutual and recognized love, and those who love each other wish well to each other in that respect in which they love one another." (Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.3, Ross translation, [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicomachean_Ethics_%28Ross%29/Book_Eight))

Friendships of utility and pleasure are incidental, he goes on: they dissolve when the useful or pleasant ends. The complete kind is the friendship of those who are good and alike in virtue, who wish each other well for their own sake. ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VIII.3, Ross translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Nicomachean_Ethics_%28Ross%29/Book_Eight))

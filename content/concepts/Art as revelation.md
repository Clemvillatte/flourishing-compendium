"Art as revelation" names Heidegger's claim in The Origin of the Work of Art: the artwork does not copy things, it lets what things are come into the open. His example is Van Gogh's painting of peasant shoes, where the equipment steps into the light of what it is. The definition he draws from it is the core of the page.

> "In the work of art the truth of beings has set itself to work. 'To set' means here 'to bring to stand.' Some particular being, a pair of peasant shoes, comes in the work to stand in the light of its Being. The Being of beings comes into the steadiness of its shining. The essence of art would then be this: the truth of beings setting itself to work." (Heidegger, "The Origin of the Work of Art", trans. Albert Hofstadter, [Basic Writings](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BasicWritings/BasicWritings.162.html))

### What revelation means here

The revelation is of truth, not of facts. Heidegger sets aside the standing division: "But until now art presumably has had to do with the beautiful and beauty, and not with truth... Truth, in contrast, belongs to logic. Beauty, however, is reserved for aesthetics." In the work, by contrast, "there is here an occurring, a happening of truth at work" (same page). For the compendium, this is why aesthetic behaviour belongs to genuine flourishing rather than to decoration: on this account, art is a place where the world as shared and livable gets set into view.

The sensible before the conceptual is Kant's placement of intuition first in the order of knowledge: whatever concepts do for cognition, they operate on something given, and the giving is done by sensibility. The Critique of Pure Reason states the priority in its Introduction:

> "In whatsoever mode, or by whatsoever means, our knowledge may relate to objects, it is at least quite clear that the only manner in which it immediately relates to them is by means of an intuition. To this as the indispensable groundwork, all thought points." (Kant, Critique of Pure Reason, trans. J. M. D. Meiklejohn, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4280/4280-h/4280-h.htm))

### What the priority carries

Kant's name for the study of the sensible's a priori side is the Transcendental Aesthetic: "The science of all the principles of sensibility à priori, I call transcendental æsthetic." The priority is real but half of a pair, as the famous sentence runs in this translation: "Thoughts without content are void; intuitions without conceptions, blind." The compendium principle K001, returning to experience before the labels, is the practical form of the first half: the labels come after, and if they have detached from what they label, the correction is to go back to the intuition. A compendium exercise drawn from the doctrine is to describe a situation in purely sensible terms, what is given to see and hear, before naming what is going on. What the doctrine does not give: a claim that the sensible is uninterpreted; Kant's whole point is that intuition already has form, space and time, supplied by the knower, so the "before" is a before in the order of knowing, not an untouched given.

The compendium uses "Designing for virtue" for the practice of arranging physical and social environments so that good conduct is easier. The seed grounds it in Aristotle's Politics, where the city's physical arrangement is the lawgiver's concern:

> "As great care should be taken of the health of the inhabitants, the first thing to be attended to is, that the city should have a good situation and a good position; the second is, that they may have good water to drink; and this not be negligently taken care of." (Aristotle, Politics VII.11, Jowett, [Gutenberg #6762](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/6762/pg6762.txt))

### What the design covers

The same chapter regulates the form of private houses, walls, and their ornament, and the following chapter continues with the citizens' public tables ([Aristotle, Politics VII.11-12, Jowett, Gutenberg #6762](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/6762/pg6762.txt)). The physical arrangement is treated as something the lawgiver attends to for the sake of the inhabitants, not as a neutral backdrop. The compendium's citing principles apply environmental design to attention and behaviour at the scale of a room, a desk and a phone.

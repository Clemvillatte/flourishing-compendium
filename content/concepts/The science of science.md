The science of science is John Ziman's project, opened in Public Knowledge (1968): taking science itself as the object of study, and finding its defining feature not in any particular method but in the social achievement of agreement. The book's thesis in one line:

> "Science is Public Knowledge... its goal is a consensus of rational opinion over the widest possible field." (Ziman, Public Knowledge, 1968, pp. 8–9, as quoted in [Michalos, 1969](http://magrawal.myweb.usf.edu/phd/articles/Ziman_PublicKnowledge_1969.pdf))

### What the definition carries

On Ziman's account, published information is not yet scientific knowledge: the facts and theories must survive critical study and testing by other competent and disinterested individuals before they count, which is why the consensus, not the publication, is the unit of science. He calls the principle of consensus "no abstract formula, but... a philosophical basis for action" (Public Knowledge, p. 126, as quoted in [Michalos, 1969](http://magrawal.myweb.usf.edu/phd/articles/Ziman_PublicKnowledge_1969.pdf)): it tells a researcher what the work is for, an answer other people can check. The compendium connects this with SO040's Peircean requirement that science needs institutions of public permanency; both make the reliability of inquiry a property of the community's arrangement rather than of any lone inquirer's virtue. A compendium exercise drawn from the concept is to treat one's own conclusions as unpublished until someone competent and disinterested has had at them. What the account does not give: a cure for the sociology that follows; Ziman's later work documents how the consensus-seeking institution itself bends under industrial and policy pressures.

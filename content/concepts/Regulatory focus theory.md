**Regulatory focus theory** — Higgins — people pursue goals through a promotion focus (ideals, gains) or prevention focus (oughts, losses).

## Field

Affect · Psychological science

The theory starts from the hedonic principle — people approach pleasure and avoid pain — and asks what lies under it. Its answer: the principle operates differently depending on regulatory focus, which distinguishes self-regulation with a promotion focus (accomplishments and aspirations) from self-regulation with a prevention focus (safety and responsibilities). The same goal pursued under the two foci is a different pursuit, with different sensitivities to what counts as success and failure.

> "One such principle is regulatory focus, which distinguishes self-regulation with a promotion focus (accomplishments and aspirations) from self-regulation with a prevention focus (safety and responsibilities)." (Higgins, 1997, American Psychologist 52(12), 1280–1300, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9414606/))

A recent review of the theory's measurement restates its core in one sentence:

> "At its core, regulatory focus theory (Higgins, 1997) proposes that the hedonic principle of approaching pleasure and avoiding pain operates through two distinct self-regulatory foci: promotion—self-regulation in the service of accomplishment and aspiration—and prevention—self-regulation in the service of security and obligation."
> — [Fuglestad et al., "A New Measure of Regulatory Focus", Journal of Personality (2024), PMC11775232](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11775232/)

The same review gives each focus its behavioural signature:

> "Promotion focus involves concerns for nurturance, advancement, and growth. Individuals high in promotion focus (or those in a promotion-focused state) eagerly pursue desired outcomes, performing well on tasks that involve seeking gains, benefits, and advancement. Prevention focus involves concerns for safety, duty fulfillment, and meeting obligations. Individuals high in prevention focus (or those in a prevention-focused state) tend to be vigilant in avoiding undesired outcomes, performing well on tasks that involve monitoring for costs and maintaining stability."
> — [same source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11775232/)

Eagerness and vigilance are the two strategies the theory assigns to the two foci, and neither is the better one in the abstract: each performs well on the tasks its focus is built for. The compendium's linked principles work the pairing from the practical side — know which focus a given goal is actually being pursued under, since the framing changes what counts as success and failure.

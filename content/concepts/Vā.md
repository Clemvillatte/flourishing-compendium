“Vā” — Wendt, “Tatauing the Post-Colonial Body”.

Vā is the Samoan concept of the space between people and things: the relational space that is not empty but holds what it separates together. The word entered scholarship through Albert Wendt's 1996 essay "Tatauing the Post-Colonial Body", where it names the space in which the Samoan tattoo, family, village, ancestors and land are one web of relations. A person exists in the vā the way a word exists in a sentence: cut the relations and the thing itself loses its meaning.

> "Va is the space between, the betweenness, not empty space, not space that separates but space that relates, that holds separate entities and things together in the Unity-that-is-All, the space that is context, giving meaning to things. The meanings change as the relationships/the contexts change … A well-known Samoan expression is 'Ia teu le va'. Cherish/nurse/care for the va, the relationships." (Wendt, "Tatauing the Post-Colonial Body", 1996, pp. 18-19, [as quoted in the open-access ANU Press volume on Moana cosmopolitanism](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n14864/html/ch07.xhtml))

### What the concept asks of a person

The essay's charge is maintenance: relationships are not scenery but something you tend, the way you tend a garden. My principle R041 carries the instruction. For a writer on flourishing, vā reframes wellbeing itself: health of a person shows up in the health of the spaces between that person and family, place, work and ancestors, and neglect of those spaces reads as a symptom before any private feeling does.

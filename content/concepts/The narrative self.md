Alasdair MacIntyre's After Virtue (1981) grounds moral life in narrative. The self that asks what to do is already inside a story it did not write.

> "man is in his actions and practice, as well as in his fictions, essentially a story-telling animal... I can only answer the question 'What am I to do?' if I can answer the prior question 'Of what story or stories do I find myself a part?'" (MacIntyre, After Virtue, ch. 15, [Internet Archive text](https://archive.org/details/after-virtue-alasdair-macintyre))

### What the thesis claims

The passage continues: we enter human society "with one or more imputed characters — roles into which we have been drafted — and we have to learn what they are in order to be able to understand how others respond to us". The chapter's other formulations carry the same claim: "The unity of a human life is the unity of a narrative quest". The learning starts early and in stories: it is through hearing stories about "wicked stepmothers, lost children, good but misguided kings, wolves that suckle twin boys" that children pick up the cast of characters in the drama of a life. The teller is made, not born: "He is not essentially, but becomes through his history, a teller of stories that aspire to truth." The original entry names Charles Taylor's Sources of the Self (1989) as the companion source; it was, so its account is left to that citation.

### The best objection

Galen Strawson's Against Narrativity (Ratio, 2004) argues that the narrative reading has been generalised past its evidence. He distinguishes Diachronic people, who figure themselves as something that was there in the past and will be there in the future, from Episodic people, who do not: "If one is Episodic, by contrast, [E] one does not figure oneself, considered as a self, as something that was there in the (further) past and will be there in the (further) future." He offers his own case as evidence:

> "I have absolutely no sense of my life as a narrative with form, or indeed as a narrative without form. Absolutely none." (Strawson, Against Narrativity, [Ratio 17(4), 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/2022/https://lchc.ucsd.edu/mca/Paper/against_narrativity.pdf))

The objection sets a limit on the thesis: some people live narratively, others do not, and the second group can flourish. In Strawson's words, "There are deeply non-Narrative people and there are good ways to live that are deeply non-Narrative."

### What the thesis gives practice

The narrative reading makes the self something a person can edit rather than merely inhabit, since the story can be retold without being denied. The kernels below work the boundary the thesis draws, holding the observing self apart from the story of self while still taking the story as where the meaning lives.

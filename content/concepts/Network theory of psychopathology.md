Borsboom's 2017 paper in World Psychiatry sets out the theory. Its abstract says "mental disorders arise from direct interactions between symptoms", and "at the heart of the theory lies the notion that symptoms of psychopathology are causally connected through myriads of biological, psychological and societal mechanisms". If the connections are strong enough, "symptoms can generate a level of feedback that renders them self-sustaining", so that "the network can get stuck in a disorder state" ([Borsboom, A network theory of mental disorders, World Psychiatry, 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/wps.20375)). Disorders are then "alternative stable states of strongly connected symptom networks".

### How it differs from the disease model

Symptoms are nodes and causal links are the connections between them. An article on PTSD networks explains the contrast: "a mental disorder is not an underlying, latent (unobserved) disease entity, whether construed categorically or dimensionally. Rather, it is an emergent phenomenon, arising from causal interactions among its constitutive symptoms" ([Tandfonline, network approach to PTSD](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20008198.2017.1341276)). The McNally group describes the older view as presupposing that symptoms are "fallible indicators reflective of an underlying, latent variable", which they say "rests on problematic psychometric premises" ([McNally et al., Mental Disorders as Causal Systems, 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2167702614553230)).

### A worked example

A Nature Reviews article gives a case: sleep problems induce fatigue, which can lead to anhedonia and sustained low energy. In people whose symptoms are sufficiently connected, this dynamic "can remain even after the triggering event has diminished" ([Systems-based thinking in psychology and the mental health sciences, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10171725/pdf/44159_2023_Article_193.pdf)). That is the sense of the seed phrase "mutually reinforcing".

### Cautions

The theory is framed as a hypothesis about causal structure. McNally and colleagues call computational methods for finding it "emerging", and a Frontiers commentary proposes an expanded approach in which nodes include biological and cognitive processes as well as symptoms ([Frontiers commentary](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01305/full)). The seed line "not latent disease entities" states the theory's claim, not a settled finding.

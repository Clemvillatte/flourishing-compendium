Mindful uncertainty is the ACT (Acceptance and Commitment Therapy) posture toward the unknowable parts of experience: stay open to the present moment without needing it to be settled first. The core book is Hayes, Strosahl, and Wilson's 2011 second edition of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy. Its definition of psychological acceptance is the hinge:

> Psychological acceptance is the adoption of an intentionally open, receptive, flexible, and nonjudgmental posture with respect to moment-to-moment experience.

(Hayes, Strosahl, & Wilson, ACT, 2nd ed., 2011, p. 77, [chapter 3 excerpt](https://www.guilford.com/excerpts/hayes_ch3.pdf))

Willingness, the chapter's companion term, is the "voluntary and values-based choice to enable or sustain contact with private experiences or the events that will likely occasion them." The chapter is emphatic that acceptance is "not resignation or tolerance—it is an active process." Mindfulness inside the model "is viewed as both open and centered" (p. 91).

My [[S048 — Withhold assent when the impression is uncertain]] is the Stoic twin: Pyrrho suspends judgment, ACT suspends the demand for certainty. Both leave room for action.

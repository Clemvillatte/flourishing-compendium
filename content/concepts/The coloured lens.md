The heading names an image rather than a technical term: the current mood or state works like tinted glass, coloring what reaches awareness ([Gallegos, 2017](https://philarchive.org/archive/GALMAN-2)). The philosophy of emotion uses the image in the perceptualist account of moods, which holds that moods function like colored lenses and alter the way the evaluative properties of encountered objects are perceived ([Gallegos, 2017](https://philarchive.org/archive/GALMAN-2)). Emerson gave the image a memorable form:

> "Life is a train of moods like a string of beads, and as we pass through them they prove to be many-colored lenses which paint the world their own hue, and each shows only what lies in its focus." (Emerson, "Experience", Essays: Second Series, 1844, [Project Gutenberg #2945](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2945))

In the reading Gallegos reports, a person in a mood becomes disposed to see encountered objects as carrying evaluative properties that fit the mood, and to miss what does not fit ([Gallegos, 2017](https://philarchive.org/archive/GALMAN-2)). Gallegos himself argues the metaphor misleads in important ways ([Gallegos, 2017](https://philarchive.org/archive/GALMAN-2)).

Actual occasions are Whitehead's name for the final real things — the momentary events of experience of which, on his account, the world is made up. They are not bits of dead stuff that occasionally have experiences; they are the experiencing itself, perishing as it completes and becoming data for what follows.

> "'Actual entities' — also termed 'actual occasions' — are the final real things of which the world is made up. There is no going behind actual entities to find anything more real."
> — [Whitehead, Process and Reality (1929), quoted in Larval Subjects](https://larvalsubjects.wordpress.com/2009/03/07/objectiles-and-actual-occasions/)

The second half of the doctrine is the process: "how an actual entity becomes constitutes what that actual entity is... Its 'being' is constituted by its 'becoming'." A thing is not first a thing and then active; the activity is the thing. The world, on this picture, is not a warehouse of finished objects but a lineage of happenings, each one taking up the many occasions before it and forging them into a new one — the "many become one" that the related entry describes.

The view reverses the usual metaphysical habit. Substance-metaphysics asks what a thing is and treats change as something that happens to it. Occasion-metaphysics asks what a thing is doing and treats stability as a rhythm maintained across succession — a person is not a persistent object that has experiences but a long series of occasions that inherits, in each pulse, the shape of the ones before.

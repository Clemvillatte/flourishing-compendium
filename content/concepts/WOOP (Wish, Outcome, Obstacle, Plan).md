WOOP — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan — is Gabriele Oettingen's evidence-based goal strategy: name what you want, vividly imagine the best outcome, then *deliberately* name the internal obstacle most likely to derail you, and form an if-then plan for it ([Oxford Academic pilot study of the WOOP strategy](https://academic.oup.com/psychsocgerontology/article/77/3/513/6309641)). It is the packaged form of *mental contrasting* — Oettingen's core finding that positive visualization alone backfires: dreaming the outcome saps the energy to pursue it, while contrasting the wish against the real obstacle restores it ([[MO026 — Learn to use mental contrasting — visualise obstacle, not just outcome]]; [[K154 — Mental contrasting (WOOP)]] is the compendium's kernel).

The mechanism is the pairing: the outcome energises, the obstacle specifies *where* the struggle will occur, and the if-then plan — "if the obstacle arises, then I will do X" — links them, which is why WOOP is [[Implementation intentions]] with a front-end diagnostic. The Stoic premeditation of adversity is the same move made daily ([[S019 — Prepare for adversity before it arrives]]; [[S091 — Practise negative visualisation]]).

The distinction to hold: WOOP is not goal-setting plus optimism. The optimism is allowed one paragraph; the analysis belongs to the obstacle, and the obstacle must be *internal* — "I lose interest after week two", not "my boss is difficult" — because only internal obstacles can be planned around with if-then precision.

An example at its smallest, written in four lines on a card: wish (run a half-marathon), best outcome (crossing the line, November), obstacle (evening fatigue beats morning resolve), plan ("if it's 6:30 and I haven't run, then I put on shoes first and decide after ten minutes").

Its Stoic ancestor is [[Premeditatio malorum]] — obstacles rehearsed in advance.

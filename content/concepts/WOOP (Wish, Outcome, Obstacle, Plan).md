WOOP (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) is a four-step goal strategy developed by the psychologist Gabriele Oettingen. A trial published in the Journal of Gerontology describes it as guiding people "to use Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions to achieve attainable goals in their daily lives" (Monin et al., 2022, [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/psychsocgerontology/article/77/3/513/6309641)). In practice: name a wish, picture the best outcome, name what in you is likely to block it, and make an if-then plan for that moment. It packages [[MO026 — Learn to use mental contrasting — visualise obstacle, not just outcome]], and [[K154 — Mental contrasting (WOOP)]] is the compendium's kernel for it.

### Why the obstacle step is there

Oettingen's earlier experiments found that dreaming only of the good outcome can work against the goal. In four experiments, Kappes and Oettingen (2011) found that induced positive fantasies produced less energy than fantasies that questioned the desired future, negative fantasies or neutral ones. They conclude that "one reason positive fantasies predict poor achievement is because they do not generate energy to pursue the desired future" (Kappes & Oettingen, 2011, [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46284/)). Naming the obstacle puts the effort where the struggle will happen, and the plan links the two: if the obstacle arises, then I will do X. That is [[Implementation intentions]] with a front-end diagnostic, and the Stoic version of the same move is [[Premeditatio malorum]].

### What a trial found

Monin and colleagues ran a randomised pilot with 45 spousal care partners of people with early-stage dementia (90 individuals in all). Care partners practised WOOP at least once a day for two weeks. Over three months, those who did had "decreased perceived stress (δ = 1.71) and increased quality of life (δ = 1.55) and positive affect (δ = 2.30)" compared with controls, and the authors call WOOP "a promising, brief intervention". The patients' own gains were not statistically significant, and a pilot of this size is a first signal, not a settled result.

### What it is not

WOOP is not goal-setting plus optimism. The optimism is one step of four, and the analysis is spent on the obstacle.

### A small case

Written in four lines on a card: wish (run a half-marathon), best outcome (crossing the line in November), obstacle (evening fatigue beats morning resolve), plan (if it is 6:30 and I have not run, then I put on my shoes first and decide after ten minutes). (Illustrative scenario, not a reported case.)

Perceptual learning is the research field showing that practice changes perception itself: experts literally see differently. Goldstone's 1998 review defines the field from its body:

> "Perceptual learning involves relatively long-lasting changes to an organism's perceptual system that improve its ability to respond to its environment and are caused by this environment." ([Goldstone, 1998, p. 586](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/pdfs/perlearn.pdf))

The review's abstract lists its four mechanisms: attention weighting, imprinting, differentiation, and unitization ([1998 abstract](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/pdfs/perlearn.pdf)). The compendium lists the concept in two principles: B137 (Expect your seeing to change) and Y077 (Climb the subtlety ladder), both of which carry the concept among their psychology parallels.

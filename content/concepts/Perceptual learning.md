Perceptual learning is the research field showing that practice changes perception itself, not just judgment about what is perceived: experts literally see differently. The seed's source is Goldstone's 1998 review, whose abstract defines the field:

> "Perceptual learning involves relatively long-lasting changes to an organism's perceptual system that improve its ability to respond to its environment." ([Goldstone, 1998, abstract](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/pdfs/perlearn.pdf))

The body of the review tightens the definition with the cause of the change:

> "Perceptual learning involves relatively long-lasting changes to an organism's perceptual system that improve its ability to respond to its environment and are caused by this environment." ([Goldstone, 1998, p. 586](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/pdfs/perlearn.pdf))

### Four mechanisms

The review discusses four mechanisms: attention weighting, imprinting, differentiation, and unitization ([1998 abstract](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/pdfs/perlearn.pdf)). Differentiation is the one a wisdom compendium leans on: stimuli that were once indistinguishable become psychologically separated, which is the formal version of what a practice of attention does to what it attends to. The compendium's principles use the field that way: B137 (Expect your seeing to change) treats the wise as people whose seeing has been trained, and Y077 (Climb the subtlety ladder) lists the concept among its psychology parallels.

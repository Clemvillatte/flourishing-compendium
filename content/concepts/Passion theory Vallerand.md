Passion theory, in Robert Vallerand's Dualistic Model of Passion, holds that passion for an activity is either harmonious or obsessive, and that the difference lies in how the activity has been internalized into identity. The founding paper is "Les Passions de l'Âme: On Obsessive and Harmonious Passion" (JPSP, 2003), and its base definition:

> Passion is defined as a strong inclination toward an activity that people like, that they find important, and in which they invest time and energy.

(Vallerand et al., 2003, [JPSP PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2003_VallerancBlanchardMageauKoesnterRatelleLeonardGagneMacolais_JPSP.pdf))

The fork: "Harmonious passion (HP) results from an autonomous internalization of the activity into the person's identity. Obsessive passion (OP), by contrast, results from a controlled internalization of the activity into one's identity." The abstract's outcome line: "HP promotes healthy adaptation whereas OP thwarts it by causing negative affect and rigid persistence." The model is built on self-determination theory, and the passion criterion is that the activity becomes part of who you are.

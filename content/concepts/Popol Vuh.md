The K'iche' Maya creation epic — the story of the hero twins, the maize people, and the gods' trials. It is the Mesoamerican scripture of how arrogance falls ([Wikipedia Popol Vuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popol_Vuh)).

Before the true sun rises, the bird-being Vucub-Caquix (Seven Macaw) sets himself up as its replacement, and the epic lets him boast at length before the hero twins bring him down. The narrator's aside turns the episode into a warning addressed to the epic's hearers.

> "I shall now be great above all the beings created and formed. I am the sun, the light, the moon," he exclaimed. "Great is my splendor. Because of me men shall walk and conquer. For my eyes are of silver, bright, resplendent as precious stones, as emeralds; my teeth shine like perfect stones, like the face of the sky. My nose shines afar like the moon, my throne is of silver, and the face of the earth is lighted when I pass before my throne.
>
> "So, then, I am the sun, I am the moon, for all mankind. So shall it be, because I can see very far." So Vucub-Caquix spoke. But he was not really the sun; he was only vainglorious of his feathers and his riches. And he could see only as far as the horizon, and he could not see over all the world." (Popol Vuh, Part II, ch. 5, Recinos translation, [latinamericanstudies.org](https://latinamericanstudies.org/maya/Popol_Vuh.pdf))

The twins' verdict on the fallen pretender generalizes: "And this shall be the lot of all men, for they must not become vain, because of power and riches."

The mirror neuron theory of empathy extends a finding from macaque premotor cortex. A commentary in Brain describes it: "the same neurons also discharged in a highly reproducible way as the monkey observed a human experimenter making a similar grasp" ([Is the mirror cracked?, Brain](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/138/7/2109/2053652?login=false)). The theory then links this matching to "higher level concepts such as action 'understanding', imitation, empathy", which the same commentary calls the "direct-matching hypothesis". The seed sentence on this page states the idea; the sources below show that the empathy step is the disputed part.

### The case in favour

A review of the field says the activation of mirror neurons in an empathy task without motor components supports a role for mirror neurons in "emotional interpersonal cognition", and credits Gallese with the "shared manifold hypothesis" ([From Neurons to Social Beings, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5810456/)). Rizzolatti and Sinigaglia, reviewing Hickok's book, describe a "mirror mechanism" that transforms "sensory information into a motor format", which "may underlie... the sharing of others' emotions in humans" ([Rizzolatti and Sinigaglia review, UCSC-hosted PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.ucsc.edu/dist/0/158/files/2015/04/Rizzolatti-Sinigaglia-Review.pdf?bid=158)). A pain-imaging review reports "hard evidence" for human mirror neurons in at least five regions, then argues the mirror network view is "too narrow" ([Pictures of pain, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4408436/)).

### The case against

Hickok "criticizes the idea that mirror neurons alone might subserve higher cognitive functions such as action understanding, imitation and empathy", in the words of the Brain commentator, who still thinks the cells serve "rapid detection and recognition of the observed action". The same commentary notes about 20 papers on mirror neuron recordings against "over 800 human imaging studies". A paper in the Tilburg repository finds "no single cell recordings of mirror neurons in humans" and concludes the system is "not the necessary system for feeling empathy" ([Tilburg repository paper](http://arno.uvt.nl/show.cgi?fid=114188)). The PMC review notes that Heyes hypothesised mirror neurons are "a byproduct of associative learning" and that critiques "do not deny the existence of mirror neurons in primates". A conceptual analysis by Jacob concluded motor resonance is "neither necessary nor sufficient for representing another individual's intentions" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7241099/?utm)).

### Reading it carefully

Rizzolatti and Sinigaglia note that Hickok's scepticism about lesion data rests on action-understanding deficits being "typically not complete". For essays, say that mirror neurons are established in monkeys, that human evidence is mostly indirect, and that their role in empathy remains an open claim.

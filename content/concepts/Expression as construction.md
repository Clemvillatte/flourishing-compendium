Expression as construction is John Dewey's account of what happens when a feeling becomes a work. In Art as Experience (1934) he argues that expression is not a discharge of emotion but a building process in which the self and its material are both re-formed in the act.

> "The act of expression that constitutes a work of art is a construction in time, not an instantaneous emission. And this statement signifies a great deal more than that it takes time for the painter to transfer his imaginative conception to canvass and for the sculptor to complete his chipping of marble. It means that the expression of the self in and through a medium, constituting the work of art, is itself a prolonged interaction of something issuing from the self with objective conditions, a process in which both of them acquire a form and order they did not at first possess." — [Dewey, Art as Experience (1934), ch. IV "The Act of Expression", full text](https://ddong.narod.ru/deweyen2/txt04.htm)

### Discharge is not expression

The distinction does real work in the chapter. A scream lets anger out, but only the shaping of anger into something that meets resistance, an image, a sentence, a gesture that must be reworked, counts as expression. Dewey draws the line plainly: "As far as the act itself is concerned, it is, if purely impulsive, just a boiling over," and again, "Emotional discharge is a necessary but not a sufficient condition of expression." The felt emotion is fuel; the formed object is the fire.

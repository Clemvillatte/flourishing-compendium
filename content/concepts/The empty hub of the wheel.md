The empty hub of the wheel is Laozi's image in Daodejing 11 for the working part of a thing being the part that is not there. The classical text and a faithful rendering:

> "Thirty spokes share a single hub; it is the empty centre that makes the cart useful. Clay is shaped into a vessel; it is the hollow within that makes the vessel useful. Doors and windows are cut to make a room; it is the open space that makes the room useful. Therefore what is present provides advantage, while what is absent provides use."

(三十辐共一毂，当其无，有车之用……故有之以为利，无之以为用; [wudang.org, Chapter 11](https://wudang.org/dao/dao-de-jing/full-text/chapter-11).)

### The point of the image

The chapter is not a praise of nothingness over substance — the spokes, the clay and the walls are needed. Legge's older translation keeps the split: "what has a (positive) existence serves for profitable adaptation, and what has not that for (actual) usefulness" ([Yellowbridge, Daodejing 11](https://www.yellowbridge.com/onlinelit/daodejing11.php?characterMode=t)). The wheel turns because thirty spokes are united in one nave, and the nave has a hole. As another rendering puts it, "it is on the space where there is nothing that the usefulness of the wheel depends" ([Wengu Tartarie, Dao De Jing XI](http://wengu.tartarie.com/wg/wengu.php?no=11&l=Daodejing&m=NOzh)).

### What it trains

The practice the image supports is an attention shift: to the pauses, margins, and room to move that ordinary measurement overlooks. A schedule with no free time, a conversation with no silence, a system with no discretion — "may be full yet unable to function well" ([wudang.org beginner's guide](https://wudang.org/dao/dao-de-jing/full-text/chapter-11)). The instruction is to protect the hollow, not to add more spokes.

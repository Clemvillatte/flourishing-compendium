“The rhythm of breath” — Daoism.

The rhythm of breath is the Daoist image of breathing as a deep, whole-body rhythm rather than a shallow action of the throat. Its locus classicus is Zhuangzi chapter 6, where the "pure men of old" are described by contrast with ordinary breathing.

> "The pure men of old slept without dreams, and waked without anxiety. They ate without discrimination, breathing deep breaths. For pure men draw breath from their uttermost depths; the vulgar only from their throats." (Zhuangzi, ch. 6, [Giles translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/59709))

Giles, the translator, notes in the footnote to this passage that "uttermost depths" is literally "heels", and that commentators take the sentence to mean that pure men breathe with their whole being, "not as it were superficially, from the throat only".

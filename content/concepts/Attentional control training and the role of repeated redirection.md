Attentional control training, as used here, means practising the noticing of distraction and the return of attention to a chosen object. That cycle is the core instruction of focused-attention meditation ([Hasenkamp et al. 2012](https://wendyhasenkamp.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hasenkamp-neuroimage-2012.pdf)). The network framework behind the name comes from Posner and Petersen's 1990 review "The Attention System of the Human Brain," summarized here through the authors' 2012 retrospective ([Petersen & Posner 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3413263/)).

### The three networks

Posner and Petersen divided attention into three networks: alerting, which achieves and maintains an alert state; orienting, which selects information from sensory input; and executive control, which resolves conflict among responses ([Petersen & Posner 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3413263/)). The executive network includes midline frontal and anterior cingulate cortex, regions reliably activated when responses conflict; the authors also discuss these regions' roles in regulating both cognition and emotion ([Petersen & Posner 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3413263/)). On this framework the return from distraction can be read as conflict resolution: something else has captured the system, and the goal is restored.

### What one repetition looks like

Wendy Hasenkamp and colleagues (2012) scanned 14 experienced practitioners during 20 minutes of breath-focused meditation ([author PDF](https://wendyhasenkamp.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hasenkamp-neuroimage-2012.pdf)). Their model has four intervals: mind wandering, awareness of wandering, shifting attention back, and sustained attention ([author PDF](https://wendyhasenkamp.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hasenkamp-neuroimage-2012.pdf)). Awareness of wandering was associated with salience-network activity (anterior insula, dorsal anterior cingulate), the shift back with frontoparietal executive activity including right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and sustained focus with continuing right dorsolateral prefrontal activity; mind wandering was associated with default mode regions ([author PDF](https://wendyhasenkamp.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hasenkamp-neuroimage-2012.pdf)). The study mapped activity during the practice, not improvements caused by it ([author PDF](https://wendyhasenkamp.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hasenkamp-neuroimage-2012.pdf)).

### Evidence and limits

A 2020 review by Ruchika Prakash and colleagues covered 57 studies of mindfulness training against the three networks ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8011594/)). The most consistent finding was improvement in executive control, with mixed results for alerting and weak support for orienting ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8011594/)). The review also gave reasons for caution: benefits appeared in 64 percent of studies with inactive controls versus 54 percent with active, and only 10 of 57 addressed demand characteristics ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8011594/)). The defensible summary is promising but inconsistent evidence for better conflict monitoring, not a proven general sharpening of attention ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8011594/)).

### In the compendium

The concept backs AT006 "Recognise that attention is trainable: it can be cultivated" and AT055 "Recognise and work with attentional conditioning: what you practise becomes default." Y026 "Bring the mind back, without drama" quotes the Gita's instruction (Bhagavad Gita 6.26) and supplies the rep reading: fifty returns in a session are fifty reps, not a failure.

AT050 describes the body scan, systematically moving attention through the body, and AT062 discusses deliberate boundaries around social media. AT070 names anxiety's threat bias as trainable, and AT042 and AT044 treat sleep and exercise as supports for attentional capacity.

The Yoga Sutra offers a comparison in abhyasa: "the restraint of these mental activities is achieved through practice and dispassion," with practice defined as "the exertion to achieve steadiness in the state of restraint" ([Yoga Sutra 1.12-13, Veylanswami translation](https://www.himalayanacademy.com/saivite-scriptures/patanjalis-yoga-sutras/)). The parallel is structural, not historical.

Sublation is the usual English rendering of Hegel's aufheben. Maybee's Stanford Encyclopedia entry explains: "Hegel says that aufheben has a doubled meaning: it means both to cancel (or negate) and to preserve at the same time" ([SEP, "Hegel's Dialectics", §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)). A stage of thought is overcome and kept in the same movement.

### The three moments

The entry sets out how Hegel's method moves. The first moment, "the moment of the understanding", "is the moment of fixity, in which concepts or forms have a seemingly stable definition or determination". The second, the "dialectical" moment, "is the moment of instability": the one-sidedness of the fixed determination "comes to the fore, and the determination that was fixed in the first moment passes into its opposite." Hegel calls this "self-sublation". The third, the "speculative" moment, "grasps the unity of the opposition between the first two determinations, or is the positive result of the dissolution or transition of those determinations" ([SEP, §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)).

### The standard example

In the Logic, Being and Nothing give way to Becoming. The entry says this is no reductio, where contradiction means both concepts are thrown out. Instead it "leads to a positive result, namely, to the introduction of a new concept". "Becoming cancels or negates Being and Nothing because it is a new concept that replaces the earlier concepts; but it also preserves Being and Nothing because it relies on those earlier concepts for its own definition" ([SEP, §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)).

### A common misreading

The entry notes that this pattern is often described as thesis, antithesis, synthesis, and that the textbook account has been challenged. Stace, who endorsed the triad reading, still warned that "there are even 'triads' which contain four terms!", and one section of the Logic "violates the thesis-antithesis-synthesis pattern because it has only two sub-divisions, rather than three" ([SEP, §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hegel-dialectics/)). A writer using sublation should take it from Hegel's own two-sided sense of cancelling and preserving and not from the slogan.

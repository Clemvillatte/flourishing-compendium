“Avidya” — Samkhya and Vedanta; [Wikipedia: Avidyā (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism)).

Avidyā (Sanskrit अविद्या) is a word used across Indian religions for ignorance, misconception and incorrect knowledge of the phenomenal world, not the simple absence of information ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Hinduism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Hinduism))). In the Upanishads and Vedanta it names a specific nescience: ignorance of the real Self. In Buddhist literature, where the Pali form is avijjā, it is translated "ignorance" and stands first among the twelve links of dependent origination ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism))).

> "Due to avidyā there is ignorance, or nescience, of the real Self, Atman-Brahman, mistakenly identifying the Self with the body-mind complex." ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Hinduism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Hinduism)))

### Two readings of one word

The Advaita Vedanta of Adi Shankara treats avidyā as a central tenet: the Self is mistaken for what it is not ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Hinduism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Hinduism))). The Buddhist reading widens the word beyond darkness:

> "The term includes not only ignorance out of darkness, but also obscuration, misconceptions, mistaking illusion to be reality or impermanent to be permanent or suffering to be bliss or non-self to be self (delusions)." ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism)))

In the Theravada Abhidharma teachings the equivalent is moha, delusion ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism))). Both readings make ignorance active rather than empty: it misidentifies, and what it misidentifies structures experience.

Avidyā is primordial ignorance — the misidentification of the Self with body, mind, and world that Vedānta names as the root knot binding us to [[Samsara]]. Its removal is [[Moksha]] ([Wikipedia Avidya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism))).

Avidyā (Sanskrit अविद्या) is a word used across Indian religions for ignorance, misconception and incorrect knowledge of the phenomenal world ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Hinduism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Hinduism))). In Advaita Vedanta it names a specific nescience: "Due to avidyā there is ignorance, or nescience, of the real Self, Atman-Brahman, mistakenly identifying the Self with the body-mind complex." ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Hinduism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Hinduism)))

In Buddhist literature, where the Pali form is avijjā, the word is commonly translated "ignorance" and identified as the first of the twelve links of dependent origination ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism))). Bhikkhu Bodhi's study of the Noble Eightfold Path describes the root it names:

> "The Buddha teaches that there is one defilement which gives rise to all the others, one root which holds them all in place. This root is ignorance (avijja). Ignorance is not mere absence of knowledge, a lack of knowing particular pieces of information. Ignorance can co-exist with a vast accumulation of itemized knowledge, and in its own way it can be tremendously shrewd and resourceful. As the basic root of dukkha, ignorance is a fundamental darkness shrouding the mind." (Bhikkhu Bodhi, [The Noble Eightfold Path, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html))

In the Theravada Abhidharma teachings the equivalent is moha, delusion ([Wikipedia, Avidya (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidya_(Buddhism))).

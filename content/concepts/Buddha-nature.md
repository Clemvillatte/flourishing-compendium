“Buddha-nature” — Tathagatagarbha Sutra.

Buddha-nature (tathagatagarbha) is the Mahayana doctrine that the capacity for enlightenment is inherent in all sentient beings. The Tathagatagarbha Sutra is the foundational source, and its opening passage reads:

> "With my Buddha eyes, I (the Buddha) can see that within the klesas such as greed, hatred and ignorance of all sentient beings, there exists Tathagata wisdom, Tathagata eyes, and Tathagata body, sitting cross-legged without moving." (Tathagatagarbha Sutra, [Wisdomlib translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/essay/buddha-nature-lankavatara-sutra/d/doc1145037.html))

### The teaching

The same passage continues with the claim the doctrine rests on:

> "Sons of good families, although sentient beings with defiled bodies are in samsara, they all have the pure Tathagatagarbha, which is endowed with all the virtues and characteristics that I have... Thus, sons of good families, upon seeing the Tathagatagarbha in all sentient beings, the Buddha preaches this sutra in order to eliminate the klesas and to reveal the Buddhadhatu." (Tathagatagarbha Sutra, [Wisdomlib translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/essay/buddha-nature-lankavatara-sutra/d/doc1145037.html))

and with the sutra's statement of permanence:

> "Sons of good families, the Dharma of all Buddhas is thus: whether a Buddha arises or not in the world, the Tathagatagarbha in all sentient beings exists eternally and without change, but it is covered with defilements." (Tathagatagarbha Sutra, [Wisdomlib translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/essay/buddha-nature-lankavatara-sutra/d/doc1145037.html))

The sutra conveys the doctrine through nine similes: grain covered by the husk, a treasure buried under the ground, a Buddha statue wrapped in a rag, and so on, where the defilements are the husk, the ground, the rag, whatever covers or conceals that which is precious. The practice reading follows from the similes rather than the metaphysics, since if the precious thing is covered rather than absent, the work is uncovering rather than acquiring, and the kernels below take that as their starting assumption about the person practicing them. The redemption principle paired with this page runs on the same fuel, because a capacity that is inherent cannot be forfeited by any particular history of failure, only obscured by it.

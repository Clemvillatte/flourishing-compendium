Valuation as inquiry is the Deweyan move this compendium takes from Theory of Valuation (1939): a value judgment is not a report of an inner glow but a proposal about the direction of action, and like any proposal it lives or dies by what happens next. Dewey's own formulation of the test:

> "Observation of results obtained, of actual consequences in their agreement with and difference from ends anticipated or held in view, thus provides the conditions by which desires and interests (and hence valuations) are matured and tested." (Dewey, Theory of Valuation, [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/stream/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp_djvu.txt))

### Why values can be experimental

The move works because, for Dewey, ends are not fixed targets that valuation merely aims at. As the same book argues, the object finally valued as an end is itself "determined in its concrete makeup by appraisal of existing conditions as means" ([Dewey, Theory of Valuation](https://archive.org/stream/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp/theoryofvaluatio032168mbp_djvu.txt)). Since the end is shaped by the means, and the means are judged by the end, the whole circuit can be checked against consequences: a value is held the way a hypothesis is held, and a life is the experiment.

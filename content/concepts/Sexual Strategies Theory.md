Sexual Strategies Theory is David Buss and David Schmitt's evolutionary account of human mating. It proposes that men and women carry a repertoire of short-term and long-term mating strategies, activated by context. Their 1993 abstract states the program.

> "This article proposes a contextual-evolutionary theory of human mating strategies. Both men and women are hypothesized to have evolved distinct psychological mechanisms that underlie short-term and long-term strategies. Men and women confront different adaptive problems in short-term as opposed to long-term mating contexts. Consequently, different mate preferences become activated from their strategic repertoires." (Buss & Schmitt, [Sexual strategies theory: an evolutionary perspective on human mating](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8483982/), Psychological Review 100(2), 1993, author abstract)

### Origin

The theory was set out in that 1993 Psychological Review paper, with Buss then at the University of Michigan, and tested nine key hypotheses and 22 predictions. Buss's 1998 chapter restates the frame:

> "Men and women over human evolutionary history have confronted different adaptive problems in the sexual domain. Sexual Strategies Theory offers an account of these adaptive problems and presents a view of human sexual psychology as a rich repertoire of mechanisms that have evolved as adaptive solutions." (Buss, [Sexual Strategies Theory, 1998 chapter](https://labs.la.utexas.edu/buss/files/2015/09/SST-1998.pdf))

### Core claims

The theory is explicitly temporal and contextual.

> "Human sexual psychology, in this account, represents a rich repertoire of short-term and long-term mating strategies, each activated by specific social and sexual contexts." ([Buss, same chapter](https://labs.la.utexas.edu/buss/files/2015/09/SST-1998.pdf))

The 1993 abstract lists the adaptive problems the theory treats as context-sensitive: sexual accessibility, fertility assessment, commitment seeking and avoidance, immediate and enduring resource procurement, paternity certainty, assessment of mate value, and parental investment.

### In everyday life

Illustrative scenario, not a reported study: a person who dates casually for years and then, wanting children, weights reliability and commitment in partners differently. On the theory's account the same person holds both strategies, and the situation determines which preferences are active.

**Personality Systems Framework** — Mayer — personality is an organised system of parts (motivation, affect, knowledge) interacting with its surroundings.

The Personality Systems Framework is John D. Mayer's field-wide outline for organizing the science of personality, built on the shared premise that personality is a system. Its definition of the system:

> "Personality is the organized, developing system within the individual that represents the collective action of his or her motivational, emotional, cognitive, social-planning, and other psychological subsystems (Mayer, 2005, p. 296)." (Mayer, [The Personality Systems Framework: Current Theory and Development](https://scholars.unh.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1324&context=psych_facpub))

### Four topics and four areas

The framework organizes the field into four topics: personality's definition, its parts (traits, schemas and the like), its organization and its development. Structurally, it divides the system "into four functional areas called the systems set: (a) energy development, (b) knowledge guidance, (c) action implementation, and (d) executive management".

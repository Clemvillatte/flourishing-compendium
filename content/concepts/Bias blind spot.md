The bias blind spot is the asymmetry Pronin named: people see biases operating in everyone but themselves. Pronin's review states the finding and its two sources ([Pronin 2007, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 11(1):37-43](https://people.uncw.edu/tothj/PSY510/Pronin-Misperception%20of%20Bias-TiCS-2007.pdf)):

> "Recent evidence suggests that people tend to recognize (and even overestimate) the operation of bias in human judgment – except when that bias is their own. Aside from the general motive to self-enhance, two primary sources of this ‘bias blind spot’ have been identified. One involves people’s heavy weighting of introspective evidence when assessing their own bias, despite the tendency for bias to occur nonconsciously. The other involves people’s conviction that their perceptions directly reflect reality, and that those who see things differently are therefore biased."
> — [Pronin 2007, "Perception and misperception of bias in human judgment", Trends in Cognitive Sciences 11(1):37-43 (open PDF copy)](https://people.uncw.edu/tothj/PSY510/Pronin-Misperception%20of%20Bias-TiCS-2007.pdf)

The first mechanism is the introspection illusion, which the article describes as overvaluing one's own introspections: people give "heavy weight to introspective information such as motives and intentions when assessing their bias... at the expense of their considering other information – for example, their behavior (rather than their intentions)" — while the influence of bias "typically occurs nonconsciously" ([Pronin 2007](https://people.uncw.edu/tothj/PSY510/Pronin-Misperception%20of%20Bias-TiCS-2007.pdf)).

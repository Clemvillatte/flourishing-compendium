The New Fire ceremony (Classical Nahuatl: Toxiuhmilpilia) was the Mexica (Aztec) rite performed at the close of every 52-year calendar round. The Wikipedia article states its purpose in one line:

> "The New Fire Ceremony ... was an Aztec ceremony performed once every 52 years—a full cycle of the Aztec 'calendar round'—in order to stave off the end of the world." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Fire_ceremony))

### What the rite involved

The article describes the sequence: "The ceremony itself entailed all the fires being put out. Then, in Uixachtlan they started a fire on the chest of a captive and cut out his heart to place it in to fuel the fire." The new fire was then "taken all over the city to celebrate because the sun would return". Behind the rite stood a fear the article names plainly: "During this time the Aztecs believed the sun might not come back."

The ceremony was not uniquely Aztec: the article notes it "was an ancient and widespread ritual in Postclassic Central Mexico that the Aztecs appropriated to their own society".

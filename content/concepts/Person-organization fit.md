Person-organization (PO) fit is the degree to which a person's values match those of the organization they work in. The benchmark study is Kristof-Brown, Zimmerman, and Johnson's 2005 meta-analysis, "Consequences of individuals' fit at work." Its abstract:

> This meta-analysis investigated the relationships between person–job (PJ), person–organization (PO), person–group, and person–supervisor fit with preentry (applicant attraction, job acceptance, intent to hire, job offer) and postentry individual-level criteria (attitudes, performance, withdrawal behaviors, strain, tenure). A search of published articles, conference presentations, dissertations, and working papers yielded 172 usable studies with 836 effect sizes. Nearly all of the credibility intervals did not include 0, indicating the broad generalizability of the relationships across situations.

(Kristof-Brown, Zimmerman, & Johnson, Personnel Psychology 58(2), 2005, [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00672.x))

The effect sizes for PO fit are large where you would want them: as the follow-up review reports, "stronger average effect sizes than Verquer et al. for the three attitudinal outcomes (ρ = .44 for job satisfaction, .51 for organizational commitment, and -.35 intent to quit)." Performance effects are weak (.07 overall), which is the honest edge of the finding: fit predicts whether you stay and how you feel, more than whether you outperform.

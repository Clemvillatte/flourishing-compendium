Adiaphora — the indifferents — is the Stoic classification of everything that is neither good nor bad: health, wealth, reputation, life itself. Only virtue and vice fall inside the class of value; everything else lies outside it, and a person's happiness should not ride on things outside their control. The grounding text is the opening of Epictetus's Enchiridion:

> "Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions."
> — [Epictetus, Enchiridion 1, trans. Elizabeth Carter, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)

The things in one's control are "by nature free, unrestrained, unhindered"; those not in one's control are "weak, slavish, restrained, belonging to others." A person who stakes their peace on the second class has handed it to fortune.

Indifference does not mean neglect. The Stoics famously split the indifferents into preferred and dispreferred: health is a natural thing to prefer, and the sage chooses it — but as material for the exercise of virtue, not as a good. The distinction is the entry's practical point: one pursues the preferred indifferents wholeheartedly while withholding the inner stake, since their arrival or loss settles nothing about the one thing that does.

Nishkama karma (IAST: Niṣkāmakarma), often translated as desireless action, is an action performed without any expectation of its fruits or results ([term definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishkama_Karma)). The teaching it names sits at the centre of the Bhagavad Gita's karma yoga, the path of work ([Nishkama Karma, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishkama_Karma)). In chapter III of Karma Yoga, Swami Vivekananda gives his one-line reading of that teaching:

> "This is the one central idea in the Gita: work incessantly, but be not attached to it." ([Vivekananda, Karma Yoga, chapter III, "The Secret of Work"](https://ia601405.us.archive.org/32/items/KarmaYogaswamiVivekanandaBook/KarmaYoga.pdf))

### Action and attachment

Vivekananda argues that actions and thoughts leave impressions on the mind that help form character ([Karma Yoga, chapter III](https://ia601405.us.archive.org/32/items/KarmaYogaswamiVivekanandaBook/KarmaYoga.pdf)). He also argues that work performed without attachment does not bind the soul ([Karma Yoga, chapter III](https://ia601405.us.archive.org/32/items/KarmaYogaswamiVivekanandaBook/KarmaYoga.pdf)).

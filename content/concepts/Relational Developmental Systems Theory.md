Relational Developmental Systems Theory (RDS) is the name for a family of frameworks in developmental science that study development through mutually influential relations between individuals and their contexts. Aldwin (2015) situates these theories within a broader shift from reductionism to transactionalism and from Cartesian dualism to systems approaches, and notes that in human development the new paradigm is often referred to as relational developmental systems theories (RDSTs; Lerner et al., 2014; Overton, 2014) ([Aldwin, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5645042/)).

> "Drawing upon lifespan theory, Lerner et al. (2012) noted a number of theoretical tenets of this approach, including: integration of levels of organization; mutually influential individual ←→ context relations, which are the basic units of analysis; relative (e.g., limited) plasticity; the importance of intraindividual change and diversity; and an emphasis on positive human development (PHD)." (Aldwin, 2015, [Research in Human Development, introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5645042/), reporting Lerner et al., 2012)

### Person and context as one system

These theories highlight multi-level analyses, interindividual differences in intraindividual change, and co-action with the context ([Aldwin, 2015, abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5645042/)): differences between people in how each person changes over time, with the person and the context acting on each other. One of the listed tenets is relative (e.g., limited) plasticity, so change is taken to remain possible within limits.

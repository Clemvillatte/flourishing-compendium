Relational Developmental Systems Theory (RDS) is the framework Richard Lerner and Willis Overton built for developmental science. Its core claim is that the person and the context stand in mutually influential relations, and that these relations, not the person or the context alone, are the basic unit of analysis for development ([Aldwin, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5645042/)).

> "Drawing upon lifespan theory, Lerner et al. (2012) noted a number of theoretical tenets of this approach, including: integration of levels of organization; mutually influential individual ←→ context relations, which are the basic units of analysis; relative (e.g., limited) plasticity; the importance of intraindividual change and diversity; and an emphasis on positive human development (PHD)." (Aldwin, 2015, [Research in Human Development, introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5645042/), reporting Lerner et al., 2012)

### Person and context as one system

The theory works at multiple levels of organization, from biology and physiology through culture, physical ecology and history, and treats them as integrated rather than as separate causes to be weighed against each other. It studies interindividual differences in intraindividual change, meaning differences between people in how each person changes over time, and treats the person and the context as acting on each other ([Aldwin, 2015, abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5645042/)). Plasticity in this framework is relative and limited rather than unlimited, so development stays open to influence without being infinitely malleable.

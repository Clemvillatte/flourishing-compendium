> **Kernel K208** — Speech gate

Before speaking, run the words through a gate: true, timely, beneficial, kind. The gate comes from the Buddha's own account of his speech discipline in the Abhaya Sutta, where he will not say what is untrue or unbeneficial regardless of how it lands, and will say what is true and beneficial, welcome or not, only when the time is right.

### Protocol

*Steps distilled from the sutta passage below; the distillation is my, the criteria are the sutta's.*

1. Ask of the words waiting to be said: are they factual, true? If not, they do not pass, however welcome they would be.
2. Ask: are they beneficial? Truth alone does not license speech — the sutta withholds even factual words when they do not connect with the goal.
3. Ask: is this the proper time? True, beneficial words that are unwelcome are said at the right moment, not at every moment.
4. Ask: are they kind — spoken with sympathy? The sutta's motive for the whole discipline is "Because the Tathagata has sympathy for living beings."

### Evidence

The carrier here is scripture, not trials: no study tests a speech gate, and none is cited. The Buddha's six-case rule, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation: "In the case of words that the Tathagata knows to be factual, true, beneficial, but unendearing & disagreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them... In the case of words that the Tathagata knows to be factual, true, beneficial, and endearing & agreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them. Why is that? Because the Tathagata has sympathy for living beings." The same sutta ties the discipline to care, not etiquette: like a nurse pulling a stick or gravel from a child's mouth, "even if it meant drawing blood... Because I have sympathy for the young boy" ([MN 58, Abhaya Sutta](https://www.greatwesternvehicle.org/ati_website/canon/sutta/majjhima/mn058-tb0.html)). The gate as stated on this page (true, timely, beneficial, kind) is a modern four-question distillation of those criteria, not a quotation.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice happens in the pause before speaking, not in a recording.

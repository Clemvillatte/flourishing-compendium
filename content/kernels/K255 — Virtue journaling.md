> **Kernel K255** — Virtue journaling

Each evening, write one sentence: "Today, I practised [virtue] when I [action]." On days you missed, write what got in the way. The journal is not a diary. It is a training log, the way an athlete logs training sessions. The mechanism is deliberate practice: naming the virtue, the situation, and the action creates a feedback loop that makes the next application more likely.

### Protocol

The journal is a log, not a diary; the review of the logging evidence is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Motivation.

1. Each evening, write one sentence in the kernel's form: today, I practised a named virtue when I took a named action. One sentence, no more.
2. On the days you missed, write what got in the way, in the same flat voice an athlete uses for a missed session. The obstacle entry is training data, not confession.
3. Once a week, reread the entries and look for the situations that recur; the repetition is where the next deliberate practice gets aimed. The loop closes on the reread, not the writing.

### Evidence

From the systematic review of self-monitoring in weight loss:

> "A significant association between self-monitoring and weight loss was consistently found; however, the level of evidence was weak because of methodologic limitations." (Abstract, [Burke, Wang & Sevick, 2011, Journal of the American Dietetic Association 111(1), 92-102](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21185970/))

What this evidence does not test: virtues. The review covered 22 studies of logging diet, exercise or weight, with the paper diary the most common method, and found the association between monitoring and outcome consistent but the evidence base weak, with homogeneous samples among the main limitations. The transfer to a virtue log is an argument from the mechanism, not a demonstrated result: if recording food and weight travels with behavior change in these studies, a nightly sentence naming virtue, situation and action is the same feedback loop pointed at character. The abstract reports nothing about journaling's effects on ethical behavior, about evening writing, or about one-sentence formats.

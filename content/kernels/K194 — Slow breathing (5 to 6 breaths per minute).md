> **Kernel K194** — Slow breathing (5 to 6 breaths per minute)

Breathe at approximately five to six breaths per minute for five minutes. This frequency maximizes heart-rate variability and activates the parasympathetic nervous system. Useful before stressful events and as a daily reset.

### Protocol

1. Sit upright or lie down, and let the breath move through the nose. Keep it quiet and light; the aim is comfort, not big gulps of air.
2. Pace the breath in equal phases: about five to six seconds in, five to six seconds out, which lands at five to six breaths per minute. A guided track or a pacing video helps until the rhythm feels natural.
3. Let the belly expand on the inhale and soften on the exhale, keeping the chest mostly still. Start each breath from the diaphragm.
4. Continue for five to ten minutes. If the pace feels like a strain, pause briefly and resume; air hunger beyond mild is a sign to ease off.

### Evidence

A systematic review of slow breathing techniques, under ten breaths per minute, in healthy subjects found increased heart-rate variability and respiratory sinus arrhythmia, EEG changes of higher alpha and lower theta power, and reports of greater comfort, relaxation, vigor, and alertness alongside reduced anxiety, depression, anger, and confusion. A meta-analysis of randomised breathwork trials with 785 adults found reductions in self-reported stress (g = 0.35), anxiety (g = 0.32), and depressive symptoms (g = 0.40) versus control conditions.

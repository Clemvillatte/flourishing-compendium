> **Kernel K122** — Sitting with uncertainty

When you do not know what will happen, let the not-knowing be present. Resist the urge to resolve it prematurely with certainty, planning, or distraction. Tolerance of uncertainty is a trainable skill.

### Protocol

The sitting is a refusal to close the question early; the trial of the trainable version is quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion and Self.

1. When you do not know what will happen, name the not-knowing plainly: I do not know how this turns out. Say it once, without the softening of a prediction.
2. Notice the urge to resolve it, in any of its costumes: certainty, over-planning, distraction, or the rapid collection of more information than the decision needs. Let the urge be present without obeying it.
3. Act on what the situation actually requires and no more, keeping the not-knowing as a passenger rather than a driver. A note on what the urge wanted to close, and what stayed open, keeps the skill visible as a skill.

### Evidence

From the randomized trial of a single-session uncertainty training:

> "The Uncertainty-Mindset Training reduced intolerance of uncertainty, anxiety symptoms, and depression symptoms 1 month later." (Abstract, [Daniels, Hasan & Schweizer, 2025, Psychological Medicine 55, e377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41395836/))

What this evidence does not test: the sitting. Two hundred fifty-nine emerging adults were randomized to a single online mindset-training session, psychoeducation, or no training, and one month later the mindset group scored lower on intolerance of uncertainty, anxiety and depression, with the clinical gains mediated by the reductions in intolerance itself. That is the kernel's claim of trainability, demonstrated: tolerance moved, and moving it moved other things. But the trial taught adaptive beliefs about uncertainty in young adults; the abstract reports nothing about resisting the urge to resolve, about sitting with a live personal unknown, or about the practice's monthly or situational use. What it supports is the kernel's core premise, that the tolerance is a skill and not a fixed trait.

> **Kernel K151** — Eulogy exercise

Write the eulogy you would want delivered at your funeral. What would you want said about how you lived, what you stood for, who you were to others? The exercise clarifies values by confronting mortality.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the experiments behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Values and Motivation.

1. Write the eulogy you would want delivered at your funeral, in the voice of someone who knew you well: what you stood for, who you were to others, how you lived.
2. Write it by hand if you can, and let it be specific: names, choices, ordinary Tuesdays. Vague eulogies make vague values.
3. Read it back after a week and underline the sentences that sting a little; those mark the gap between the life described and the life being lived, which is the values clarification the exercise exists for. Repeat yearly or at big turns.

### Evidence

From the experimental research on death reflection:

> "Results showed that mortality salience led highly extrinsic participants to manifest greed, whereas death reflection again generated intrinsic, unselfish behavior." (Abstract, [Cozzolino, Staples, Meyers & Samboceti, 2004, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 30(3), 278-292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15030620/))

What this evidence does not test: the eulogy. The experiments used guided manipulations built from near-death and posttraumatic-growth accounts, and measured behaviour in games and choices, finding that a specific, immersive form of death contemplation shifted extrinsically oriented participants toward intrinsic behaviour where a bare reminder of mortality shifted them toward greed. The abstract reports no eulogy-writing. What the studies support is the exercise's premise: how you contemplate death matters, and the specific, felt kind is the one that moved values.

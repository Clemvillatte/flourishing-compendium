> **Kernel K146** — Ego dissolution practice

In meditation or in nature, let the boundary between self and world soften. Notice that the line between "me" and "not-me" is a constructed boundary, not a given fact. Do not force it; let it happen.

### Protocol

The practice is a letting-go, not a pushing; the study behind it is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Attention.

1. Sit in meditation or in nature, and let attention rest on the boundary itself: where "me" seems to end and "not-me" begin.
2. Do not force the line to dissolve. Reduce the effort of holding it, the way a fist opens when the gripping stops, and let whatever happens be data.
3. Return gently to the ordinary boundary afterwards, and note the visit: how the world looked from inside a softer edge. The note, not the state, is what accumulates.

### Evidence

From the study of meditation and the body's near space:

> "Post-meditation, this PPS attentional enhancement was negated, with no distinction between responses to stimuli within versus beyond PPS." (Abstract, [Nguyen, Palmer, Aday, Davoli & Bloesch, 2020, Consciousness and Cognition 83, 102978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32650253/))

What this evidence does not test: the letting-go. Thirty-one participants did an auditory task before and after a guided meditation, and the brain's attentional marker for "near the body" versus "beyond the body" disappeared after the meditation, in novices. The study measures an evening's change in a lab paradigm; the abstract reports no test of a long practice of softening the boundary, nor anything about nature settings. What it supports is the kernel's premise: the me/not-me line has a measurable attentional edge, and meditation moved it within a single session.

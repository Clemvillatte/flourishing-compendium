> **Kernel K207** — Deep listening

Spend twenty minutes listening to someone with the sole intention of understanding them. No advice, no rebuttal, no redirecting to yourself. The practice is rare; the evidence for what it does to the person heard is now substantial.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the research below defines what the listening consists of, not a timed session.*

1. Give the speaker the floor and hold the intention of understanding their point of view; the research definition of high-quality listening is "listening that offers empathy (an understanding of the speaker's point of view), interest-taking, and unconditional regard (caring for the speaker, independent of expressed content and a non-judgmental stance)" ([Itzchakov, Weinstein, Legate & Amar, 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7409873/)).
2. Do not evaluate, advise, or agree and disagree; the listening is non-judgmental by definition.
3. Stay with their material; do not return to your own story, even as illustration.

### Evidence

Across a research programme of lab experiments and field studies, being listened to well measurably changes the speaker. In four experiments, listeners characterised by empathy and a non-judgmental approach reduced speakers' social anxiety, which reduced defensive processing, leaving speakers "aware of [their] inconsistencies but can tolerate them" with decreased attitude extremity (Itzchakov, Kluger & Castro, 2017, *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*, 43(1): 105–120). In the prejudice studies: "High quality listening, relative to regular and poor listening quality, predicted lower speakers' prejudice," and "the effect was mediated through increasing self-insight and openness to change" ([Itzchakov et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7409873/)). What these studies do not test: a scheduled twenty-minute session as such; the outcomes measured were attitude structure, self-insight and prejudice, not the listener's own well-being or any alleviation of suffering.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is listening to a live person, not a recording.

> **Kernel K180** — Feedback loops

A standing arrangement for letting outcomes talk back to intentions. The name comes from Chris Argyris and Donald Schön's theory of organizational learning, in which learning itself is defined as the detection and correction of error. It suits any project that runs longer than a week, where the gap between what was meant and what is happening can quietly widen if nothing reads the results.

### Protocol

The steps are my personal practice built on the two loops the theory names; the definitions are quoted in the Evidence section.

1. Track the work as it happens: what was intended, what was done, what resulted. The record is the loop's raw material; without it the review runs on memory and mood.
2. Review the record at a set interval. Three questions carry the review: what is the pattern, what is working, what is not.
3. When something is off, correct it under the current objectives. This is the first loop: the correction that lets the present policies carry on.
4. When the same correction keeps recurring, take the second loop. Question the objectives and assumptions themselves, and modify them rather than the tactic alone.

### Evidence

The definitions the two loops run on:

> "Organizational learning involves the detection and correction of error. When the error detected and corrected permits the organization to carry on its present policies or achieve its present objectives, then that error-detection-and-correction process is single-loop learning. Double-loop learning occurs when error is detected and corrected in ways that involve the modification of an organization's underlying norms, policies and objectives." (Argyris & Schön, 1978, p. 3, [quoted verbatim in a GWU Minerva thesis PDF](https://www2.gwu.edu/~ibi/minerva/Spring2015/Juliana_Teixeira.pdf))

What this evidence does not test: the personal practice. The theory concerns organizations, its outcomes studied were organizational performance, and the page's extension of the two loops to a person's own projects is editorial.

> **Kernel K154** — Mental contrasting (WOOP)

Identify a wish (what you want), the best outcome (how it would feel), the main obstacle (what stands in the way), and a plan (what you will do when the obstacle appears). Mental contrasting links aspiration to the reality of resistance.

### Protocol

The steps below are this compendium's adaptation of the WOOP packaging of the research strategy; the meta-analysis behind the strategy is quoted below. Intended focus: Motivation and Overt behaviour.

1. Wish: name what you want in one sentence, something challenging but doable.
2. Outcome: picture the best result vividly, the felt sense of it arriving.
3. Obstacle: name the main thing that will stand in the way, in you or in your days.
4. Plan: form the if-then: if that obstacle appears, then I will do this specific thing.

### Evidence

From the meta-analysis of the method:

> "Results showed that MCII to be effective for goal attainment with a small to medium effect size (g = 0.336)." (Abstract, [Wang, Wang & Gai, 2021, Frontiers in Psychology 12, 565202](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34054628/))

What this evidence does not test: your wish. The meta-analysis pooled 21 empirical studies (24 effect sizes, 15,907 participants) and found a small-to-medium effect on goal attainment, stronger when the intervention involved interaction with an experimenter than with documents alone, and the authors note that publication bias may mean the real effects are smaller and that the study count is small. The method is short, teachable and modestly supported; the four steps' names (WOOP) are the popular packaging of the research strategy (mental contrasting with implementation intentions).

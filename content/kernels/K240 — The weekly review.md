> **Kernel K240** — The weekly review

Once a week, run your week through the kernel categories lightly. Which are firing? Which are starving? Feed the hungriest one next week. The category sweep is this practice's own; the weekly review as a standing appointment comes from David Allen's Getting Things Done, where it is the maintenance ritual that keeps the whole system trustworthy.

## Protocol

The GTD original runs in three phases, quoted from the official checklist:

1. GET CLEAR. Three steps: "Collect Loose Papers and Materials," "Get 'IN' to Zero," and "Empty Your Head," with the last meaning: "Put in writing and process any uncaptured new projects, action items, waiting fors, someday/maybes, etc." ([GTD Weekly Review](https://gettingthingsdone.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/GTD-WeeklyReview.pdf))
2. GET CURRENT. Six steps, from "Review Next Actions Lists" through "Review Any Relevant Checklists." The core one, verbatim: "Evaluate status of projects, goals, and outcomes, one by one, ensuring at least one current next action item on each" (same checklist).
3. GET CREATIVE. Two steps. "Review Someday/Maybe List": "Review for any projects or actions which may now have become active, and transfer to the appropriate list. Delete items no longer of interest." And "Be Creative & Courageous": "Any new, wonderful, hare-brained, creative, thought-provoking, risk-taking ideas to add into your system?" (same checklist)
4. The kernel version replaces the lists with the categories. Same slot, same hour, lighter cargo: which practices fired this week, which starved, and which one gets fed first next week.

## A guided version

The review is your own lists at your own desk; the official GTD site publishes a guided episode, with "Senior GTD Coach Meg Edwards tak[ing] you through the entire process step by step" ([GTD Episode 7](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2015/07/podcast-07-guided-gtd-weekly-review/)), but no recording is cited for this kernel version here.

## Evidence

The review's logic is maintenance, not therapy: Allen's system treats the weekly pass as what keeps a trusted system trustworthy, and the checklist above is its official form. The cited checklist does not establish efficacy.

What the research does not cover: the cited checklist does not establish efficacy, and this page claims none. The kernel-category sweep, the firing-or-starving language, and the feed-the-hungriest rule are this practice's own.

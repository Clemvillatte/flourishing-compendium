> **Kernel K105** — Single-tasking

Do one thing at a time, with full attention. When you eat, eat. When you walk, walk. When you work, work. Protect the current task from interruption for a defined period (45 to 90 minutes).

### Protocol

1. Choose one task and define its next concrete step before you begin. Vagueness is an invitation to switch.
2. Set a start and end time for the block, and treat it as a firm commitment. Forty-five to ninety minutes is a sound range; beginners can start at thirty.
3. Remove the switch temptations in advance: notifications off, phone out of reach, only the material for this task open.
4. When the pull toward another task appears, note it on paper and return. The note lets the mind drop the loop without chasing it.

### Evidence

The laboratory version of this kernel is the task-switching literature. A classic review of the field reports that responses are substantially slower and usually more error-prone immediately after a switch between tasks, and that this switch cost is reduced, but not eliminated, by an opportunity to prepare. In other words, part of the cost of leaving a task persists no matter how quickly you feel ready for the next one, which is the argument for protecting long blocks of one thing.

### A guided version

A clear practical walkthrough of setting up such sessions, based on Cal Newport's deep-work rules, is [Deep Work: How To Improve Your Focus, from Slack](https://slack.com/blog/productivity/deep-work).

> **Kernel K264** — Deliberation timeout

For decisions above a defined threshold (a career change, a relationship commitment, a major purchase), impose a mandatory wait of one to three days, during which you do not decide. The wait is not for more information, though information is welcome; it is for the affect to change state. The research vocabulary is the hot-cold empathy gap: in the grip of a feeling, people mispredict their own future preferences. Reach for it whenever a decision feels urgent and pleasant at the same time.

### Protocol

A rule made in advance, applied in the moment. The practice moves the Cognition and Overt behaviour dimensions: the decision is parsed, and the signing, sending or saying is deferred.

1. Define the threshold now, while calm: any commitment of money, time or self above the line triggers the rule. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: write the threshold down; rules defined in the hot state are negotiated away in it.
2. When a candidate decision crosses the line, start the clock. During the wait, gather information and consult people who are not in your hot state.
3. At the end of the wait, decide. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: the timeout does not decide for you, and the cold state is not a guarantee of accuracy; it only moves the deciding out of the state the research shows mispredicts itself.

### Evidence

The mechanism, in the author's words:

> "When in hot states, they underestimate the influence of those states and, as a result, overestimate the stability of their current preferences."
> — [Loewenstein 2005, *Health Psychology* 24(4S), PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16045419/)

And the paper's summary of consequences: "hot-cold empathy gaps can lead healthy persons to expose themselves excessively to health risks and can cause health care providers to undertreat patients for pain" ([Loewenstein 2005, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16045419/)). The hot state both distorts the forecast and hides the distortion.

What the research does not test: these are findings on affective forecasting and state-dependent prediction, mostly in medical and consumer domains; the cited studies do not test a mandatory one-to-three-day timeout rule, and the page claims no measured effect for it.

### A guided version

Elisha Goldstein's [STOP Practice (5 Minutes) on Insight Timer](https://insighttimer.com/drelishagoldstein/guided-meditations/stop-practice-5-minutes) is a guided audio of the pause: in the author's own description, a practice to "pause, bring more awareness, intention and focus to the moment". It is the minute-scale cousin of this kernel's rule, for moments too hot to run the full timeout; a five-minute stop buys the one-to-three-day rule the space to work.

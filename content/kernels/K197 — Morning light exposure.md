> **Kernel K197** — Morning light exposure

Get natural light in your eyes within an hour of waking, for 10 to 20 minutes. Light is the primary zeitgeber for the circadian system; it regulates sleep, mood, and energy.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the direction of the effect is established in the review below.*

1. Within the first hour after waking, go outdoors; through a window counts for little, glass filters the wavelengths that matter.
2. Face the daylight with eyes open; on a bright day the shorter end of the range suffices, on an overcast day the longer end.
3. Do not look at the sun directly; the eyes are receptors, not targets.

### Evidence

The circadian system is "entrained to the 24-h light-dark cycle via light exposure," and the timing determines the direction of the shift: "light exposure administered in the morning (i.e., after the core body temperature minimum, on average after 05:00) phase advances the circadian system, whereas light exposure in the evening/night (on average after 17:00) phase delays the phase of the circadian clock" ([Prayag et al., *Clocks & Sleep*, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7185269/)). The morning-after-waking window is the advance region; an 8000-lux hour delivered shortly after waking produced a statistically significant average phase advance of 11 minutes in one cited study. What these studies do not test: the specific 10-to-20-minute duration, which is a popular distillation rather than a trial protocol (laboratory exposures run longer, at higher lux); nor effects on mood or daytime energy, the outcomes measured were circadian phase and sleep timing.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is standing in daylight, not a recording.

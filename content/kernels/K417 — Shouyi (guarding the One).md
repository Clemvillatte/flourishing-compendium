> **Kernel K417** — Shouyi (guarding the One)

Shouyi 守一, "guarding the One", is one of the oldest named Daoist meditations: the two earliest references appear in the *Neiye* (Inward Training) and in the *Zhuangzi*, chapter 11. A scholarly handbook chapter calls it "a key Daoist technical term for apophatic meditation, that is, emptiness- and stillness-based meditation." The practice holds attention on one thing, the One, and lets the rest fall away. The *Neiye* verse puts it like this:

> "When you enlarge your mind and let go of it, / When you relax your vital breath and expand it, / When your body is calm and unmoving: / And you can maintain the One and discard the myriad disturbances. / You will see profit and not be enticed by it, / You will see harm and not be frightened by it." — [Neiye 24, Roth's translation, via Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiye)

### Protocol

1. Sit on a cushion or the front edge of a chair, spine upright but not stiff. Let the eyes close or rest half open, tongue against the upper palate.
2. Let the breath be relaxed and expanded, with the belly rising and falling. Nothing is controlled; the breath is allowed to widen on its own.
3. Choose the One: the Way, unity itself, or a single anchor that stands for it. Hold attention there, calmly and exclusively. Roth's description of the classical technique is a person who "sits calmly and exclusively concentrates on the Way", setting aside "the disturbances of perceptions, thoughts, emotions, and desires that normally fill your conscious mind."
4. When thoughts of gain or loss arise, see them without enticement and without fright, and return to the One. The instruction is guarding, not fighting: attention is kept on the One the way one keeps a fire from going out.
5. A later Han-dynasty form, in the *Taiping jing*, guards a light that arises in concentration: "First of all, it will be red, after a long time it will change to be white, later again it will be green, and then it will pervade all of you completely." Ten to twenty minutes to start; the traditional recommendation is longer.

### Evidence

No randomised trial has tested shouyi by name, so the honest framing is by nearest studied relative. The classical form is a concentrative practice, and its family, focused attention meditation with a single anchor, carries its own outcome literature. Treat shouyi as a tradition-tested form of practices whose family has evidence, rather than as a directly validated intervention.

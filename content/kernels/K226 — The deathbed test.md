> **Kernel K226** — The deathbed test

For a significant decision, ask: which choice would the eighty-year-old me endorse? The evidence beneath it is the future-self research: the brain treats the distant future self as a near-stranger, and contact with that self measurably changes choices made for its benefit.

### Protocol

*Steps are the compendium's editorial protocol; the research below explains why the perspective takes effort.*

1. Name the decision and the two or three live options in plain words.
2. Ask the question at the far end: which option would the version of me looking back endorse; which would they wish had been taken on their behalf.
3. Write the answer down before returning to the present-tense considerations; the point is to give the distant self a vote, not to abolish the present one.

### Evidence

The research programme behind this kernel is Hal Hershfield's work on the future self. In the age-progressed avatar studies, "the study found that participants who saw age-progressed pictures of themselves chose to allocate a significantly higher proportion of hypothetical income to retirement compared to a control group; the participants in this study were drawn from a group of adults ages 18 to 35," and "in the fourth study (the only experiment conducted on a working population), participants in the intervention group allocated a significantly higher percentage of pay to retirement than the control group, 6.17 percent vs. 4.41 percent" ([DOL CLEAR summary of Hershfield et al., 2011](https://beta.dol.gov/research-data/clear/studies/increasing-saving-behavior-through-age-progressed-renderings-future-self-hershfield-et-al-2011); original: *Journal of Marketing Research*, 48:SPL, S23–S37). The companion fMRI work found that neural patterns while thinking about the future self resemble those for thinking about other people, which is why the distant self's interests need deliberate advocacy. What this research does not test: the deathbed framing as such, or any effect on well-being; the outcomes measured were monetary allocations, and the deathbed perspective is an editorial extension of the future-self-continuity findings.

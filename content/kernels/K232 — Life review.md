> **Kernel K232** — Life review

Periodically, review your life in chapters: childhood, adolescence, early adulthood, and so on. What were the turning points? What did each chapter teach? The review is an old idea in gerontology, Robert Butler's life review, and the therapy built on it has been tested mostly with older adults. The chapter structure below is an adaptation; the tested interventions were guided by trained facilitators.

## Protocol

1. Lay out the chapters. Childhood, adolescence, early adulthood, and onward, in whatever cuts fit your life.
2. Find the turning points. For each chapter, the two or three events that bent the line.
3. Ask what each chapter taught. Not what happened, but what it left you carrying.
4. Name the thread. One sentence about what runs through the chapters, written plainly.
5. Title the current chapter. Then close the notebook; the review is a periodic practice, not a nightly one.

## A guided version

The review runs on your own memories in your own words; no guided recording is cited for it here.

## Evidence

The concept comes from Butler, who postulated "the universal occurrence of an inner experience or mental process of reviewing one's life in older people," a process that "participates in the evolution of such characteristics as candor, serenity and wisdom among certain of the aged" ([Butler, 1963, opened in its 1964 reprint](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-662-38534-0_20), abstract). Later reviewers summarize the practice as recollecting, evaluating and assigning meaning to positive and negative personal memories. The tested version has real effects on mood: a meta-analysis of 15 randomized controlled trials found that "the life review group has a large effect on reducing depression level than the control group on post-intervention and follow-up," with sensitivity analysis showing "a moderate effect (effect size=-0.54; 95% CI=-0.71 to -0.36; p<0.05) and small effect (effect size=-0.20; 95% CI=-0.41 to -0.01; p<0.05)... on post-intervention and follow-up, respectively" ([Al-Ghafri, Al-Mahrezi and Chan, 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8683455/), abstract).

What the research does not cover: the trials tested life review as a structured intervention for older adults, mostly with depression as the outcome, not a self-administered chapter review for well-being. Butler's claim was about a naturally occurring process in older people, not a scheduled exercise. The chapter-review routine as laid out here has no direct test in either source.

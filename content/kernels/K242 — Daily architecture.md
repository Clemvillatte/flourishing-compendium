> **Kernel K242** — Daily architecture

Build a simple daily structure: a morning intention, cue-based micro-practices through the day, and an evening review. The architecture is the retention system for everything else.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the study behind it is quoted below. Intended focus: Overt behaviour and Structure.

1. Fix three fixed points in the day: a morning intention, one or two cue-based micro-practices (after coffee, after the school run, before dinner), and an evening review.
2. Attach each micro-practice to a cue that already happens daily; the cue does the remembering, the practice rides on it.
3. Expect the architecture to break on weekends and travel, and simply reinstall it on return; the fixed points are the parts worth defending when the day collapses.

### Evidence

From the qualitative research on how habits hold and break:

> "Habits were typically formed in work-based contexts. Weekends and vacations temporarily disrupted performance due to absence of associated cues, but habits were reinstated on return to work." (Abstract, [Lally, Wardle & Gardner, 2011, Psychology, Health & Medicine 16(4), 484-489](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21749245/))

What this evidence does not test: the architecture. The study followed ten participants in a weight-loss intervention built on habit-formation principles and analysed their accounts: behaviour change felt effortful at first and easier as automaticity grew, cue selection mattered, and stable work contexts carried the habits while weekends and vacations broke them. It did not test a three-point daily structure, and ten people's accounts are ten people's accounts. What it supports is the kernel's core claim: the cues and contexts do the retaining, so the structure is the thing to build and reinstall.

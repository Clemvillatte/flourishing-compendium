> **Kernel K127** — Cognitive reappraisal

When a situation triggers distress, generate an alternative interpretation. What else could this mean? What would a wise friend say? What is the larger context? Reappraisal changes the emotional impact by changing the meaning. Reach for it when a first reading of events has you in its grip and a second reading is available.

### Protocol

The steps are my practice form; the account the form leans on is quoted in the Evidence section.

1. Name the situation and the reading that is hurting: "the meeting was a verdict on me."
2. Generate at least one alternative interpretation that survives the facts: what else could this mean? What would a wise friend say? What is the larger context?
3. Hold both readings together rather than swapping one certainty for another, and act from the better-supported one.

### Evidence

From Gross and John's account of the two strategies:

> "Cognitive reappraisal is a form of cognitive change that involves construing a potentially emotion-eliciting situation in a way that changes its emotional impact (Lazarus & Alfert, 1964)." (p. 349, [Gross & John, 2003, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 85(2), 348-362](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.2.348))

> "Reappraisal is an antecedent-focused strategy: it occurs early, and intervenes before the emotion response tendencies have been fully generated. This means that reappraisal can thus efficiently alter the entire subsequent emotion trajectory." (same paper)

And from their summary of the individual-difference studies:

> "Study 3 shows that reappraisers experience and express greater positive emotion and lesser negative emotion, whereas suppressors experience and express lesser positive emotion, yet experience greater negative emotion." (same paper, abstract)

What this evidence does not test: the practice. The studies measure habitual use of reappraisal as a stable individual difference, and the reported associations are correlational; they do not show that a deliberate reappraisal exercise in a distressing moment changes emotion, and this page claims no such outcome.

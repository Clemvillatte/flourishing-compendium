> **Kernel K312** — Authentic-choice audit

For a significant decision, ask: Am I choosing this because I want it, or because I fear the alternative? Am I choosing it for myself or for someone else's image of me? The audit tests whether the choice is autonomous or driven by introjected pressure. The distinction it tests is Sheldon and Elliot's: goals "pursued because of either intrinsic or identified motivation... are integrated with the self" and "felt to emanate directly from self-choices", whereas "goals pursued only because of external pushes, or because of introjected sanctions characterized by anxiety and guilt, are said to emerge from a nonintegrated region of the person" ([Sheldon & Elliot 1999](https://www.scribd.com/document/904038670/sheldon-and-elliot-1999)). Sartre gives the audit its spine: "man is condemned to be free... from the moment that he is thrown into this world he is responsible for everything he does", and "man is nothing else but what he purposes... nothing else but the sum of his actions, nothing else but what his life is" ([Sartre, Existentialism is a Humanism](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm)). The audit is the written refusal to let someone else's portrait stand in for your own.

### Protocol

1. Generate the two questions in writing, before the decision closes: because I want it, or because I fear the alternative; for myself, or for someone else's image of me. Written, because the introjected pressure answers best to ink.
2. Select the honest answer, not the flattering one. The fear-driven choice and the image-driven choice both masquerade as wanting; the test is what remains when the fear and the audience are subtracted in imagination.
3. Retain the audit result with the decision: if the choice survives the subtraction, proceed and own it; if it does not, the audit has done its work before the commitment does damage.
4. Repeat at each significant decision until the two questions fire on their own. The audit is finished when the introjected sanction, the guilt and anxiety kind, is recognised on arrival rather than obeyed.

### Evidence

The empirical ground is quoted above: the self-concordance model holds that "the self-concordance of goals (i.e., their consistency with the person's developing interests and core values) plays a dual role", and that "those pursuing self-concordant goals put more sustained effort into achieving those goals and thus are more likely to attain them" (Sheldon & Elliot 1999, [paper](https://www.scribd.com/document/904038670/sheldon-and-elliot-1999), abstract verbatim). The philosophical ground is Sartre's, quoted above: the no-excuse freedom and the sum-of-actions identity. Scope limits: the model is about goal pursuit measured over time in university samples, so the audit borrows its autonomous-versus-introjected distinction rather than claiming a trial of the two questions; and Sartre's lecture is philosophy, not evidence, standing behind the audit as its account of why the questions must be answered at all. This page audits one decision; K310 detects the standing pattern, and K320 checks the values the decision should serve.

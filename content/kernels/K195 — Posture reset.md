> **Kernel K195** — Posture reset

At every transition between activities, stand up, roll your shoulders, feel your feet on the ground. The randomized trial quoted below compared seated postures under stress; the reset's between-activities schedule is the practice's own. Reach for it when a long sitting has quietly become a slump.

### Protocol

The steps are my practice form; the findings the form leans on are quoted in the Evidence section.

1. At each transition between activities, stand up, roll your shoulders, and feel your feet on the ground.
2. Reset the posture you are about to sit in, not just the one you are leaving: the study below taped participants into their assigned posture for the session.
3. Treat the reset as part of the activity, not a break from it.

### Evidence

From the randomized trial of seated posture and stress:

> "Upright participants reported higher self-esteem, more arousal, better mood, and lower fear, compared to slumped participants." (Results, [Nair, Sagar, Sollers, Consedine & Broadbent, 2015, Health Psychology 34(6), 632-641](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25222091/))

What this evidence does not test: the practice. The trial compared taped upright versus slumped seated postures during a laboratory stress task in 74 participants; it did not test a stand-up reset between daily activities, and the outcome measures were affect and cardiovascular responses, not attention. The framing that the body is part of cognition is carried as the practice's rationale, and the trial's posture manipulation was seated, not standing.

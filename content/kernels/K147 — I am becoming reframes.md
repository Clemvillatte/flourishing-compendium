> **Kernel K147** — "I am becoming" reframes

For a stuck identity ("I am a procrastinator"), reframe: "I am in the process of learning how to start." The example wording and the steps below are this practice's own; the logic behind them comes from the mindset literature, which holds that beliefs about whether a trait is fixed or malleable shape goals and behavior. That literature also carries a caution, covered below.

## Protocol

1. Catch the fixed label. "I am a procrastinator," "I am bad with money," "I am not a math person." The grammar is the tell: the self, then a noun.
2. Ask what the label forecloses. Fixed labels quietly decide what is worth attempting. Name one thing the label has stopped you from trying.
3. Write the becoming version. Keep the fact, drop the verdict: "I am learning how to start," "I am getting better with money." The statement must be true today, not aspirational theater.
4. Attach one action. A becoming statement pairs naturally with one small start, today.
5. Re-check in a week. If the becoming statement has produced no action, rewrite it closer to the truth.

## A guided version

The reframe is written on your own sentences; no guided recording is cited for it here.

## Evidence

The theory behind the move: Dweck and Leggett's model "specifies how individuals' implicit theories orient them toward particular goals and how these goals set up the different patterns" of adaptive and maladaptive behavior ([Dweck and Leggett, 1988](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-social-cognitive-approach-to-motivation-and-Dweck-Leggett/bea6bdcd90212431bc464f638431b39ff6427855), abstract). The caution sits in the modern test of that theory. Two meta-analyses covering 273 effect sizes (N = 365,915) and 43 intervention effect sizes (N = 57,155) found that "Overall effects were weak for both meta-analyses," with "the meta-analytic average correlation... between growth mind-set and academic achievement is r = .10, 95% confidence interval (CI) = [.08, .13], p < .001," and intervention effects of "d = 0.08, 95% CI = [0.02, 0.14], p = .010" ([Sisk et al., 2018](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2018/Sisk,%20Burgoyne%20et%20al.%20(2018)%20-%20Mindset%20and%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf)). In the intervention set, "37 of the 43 effect sizes (86%) are not significantly different from zero," though "students with low socioeconomic status or who are academically at risk might benefit from mind-set interventions" (same article).

What the research does not cover: the mindset studies measured beliefs about attributes, mostly intelligence, against academic achievement. They did not test reframing self-labels in daily life, and the honest summary of the intervention evidence is that it is thin. This practice borrows the theory's logic, not a demonstrated effect.

> **Kernel K256** — The unity-of-virtue drill

Pick a decision you made this week. Identify which virtue it expressed. Then ask: did expressing this virtue require suppressing another? The Aristotelian thesis is that the virtues are unified: practical wisdom finds the action that expresses courage without injustice, honesty without cruelty. When you find a conflict, the resolution is not compromise but a more precise reading of the situation.

### Protocol

The drill is a reading exercise, not a compromise exercise; the study behind the drill is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Motivation.

1. Pick one decision you made this week, and name the virtue it expressed: courage, honesty, kindness, justice, whatever fits.
2. Ask whether expressing that virtue suppressed another. If the answer is yes, do not split the difference between them; ask instead what a more precise reading of the situation would look like, the action that is honest without being cruel or courageous without being unjust.
3. Write down the more precise reading, even if you cannot prove it, and let the next similar situation test it. The drill trains the reading, not the record.

### Evidence

From the study of virtue pursuit and wise reasoning:

> "participants who highly endorsed the pursuit of virtue endorsed wise-reasoning strategies at similar levels for resolving their own social conflicts and resolving other people's social conflicts." (Abstract, [Huynh, Oakes, Shay & McGregor, 2017, Psychological Science 28(12), 1848-1856](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28972825/))

What this evidence does not test: the unity thesis. Across two studies with 623 participants, endorsing the pursuit of virtue predicted endorsing wise-reasoning strategies (intellectual humility, the outsider perspective) for one's own conflicts, closing the usual gap between judging others' conflicts and one's own. That is a self-report association with endorsement of strategies, not a trial of the Aristotelian claim that virtues never truly conflict, nor any observation of someone finding the precise action. The abstract reports nothing about virtue suppression, compromise, or the resolution of real dilemmas. What it supports is the drill's wager: people oriented toward virtue reason more wisely about their own hard cases, which is the raw material the drill works on.

> **Kernel K104** — Mindful walking

Walk at a natural or slightly slowed pace, attending to the sensations of movement: lifting, moving, placing the foot. Can be done indoors or outdoors. Useful when the mind is too restless for seated practice.

### Protocol

1. Find a place where you can walk without dodging traffic or people, indoors or out. Walk at a natural or slightly slowed pace, with nowhere to get to.
2. Rest attention in the feet: the lifting of the heel, the roll through the sole, the placing of the foot. Some people find it helpful to note mentally "lifting, moving, placing."
3. Let the senses open to the surroundings: sounds, air on the skin, light, smell. When the mind drifts into planning or replay, come back to the feeling of the next step.
4. Ten minutes is plenty for a first pass. The practice also works as a midday reset, or as the movement alternative on days when sitting feels impossible.

### Evidence

In a preregistered multi-site study with 2,239 adults across 37 sites, mindful walking was one of four standalone mindfulness exercises tested against an active control, and it reduced self-reported stress significantly more than the control condition. The effect sizes for the walking group were not reported separately in the abstract, and the body scan showed the largest effect of the four (d = 0.56), so treat walking's evidence as strong but less precisely quantified.

### A guided version

A friendly guided session is [10 Minute Guided Walking Meditation, from Headspace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cvmfhdLo8fI).

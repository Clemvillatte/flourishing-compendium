> **Kernel K241** — Seasonal redesign

Every few months, vary the form of your practices while keeping the function. Routines go stale; the architecture itself needs periodic revision.

### Protocol

*Steps are the compendium's editorial protocol; the context-dependence they exploit is documented in the habit research below.*

1. Keep the function fixed and name it; a redesign that cannot state what the practice is for will lose it.
2. Change the form deliberately: time, place, order, or tool; the change of context is the mechanism, not a side effect.
3. Re-run the practice in its new form for a full cycle before judging it; a redesign judged in its first week is judged by its disruption, not its performance.

### Evidence

The mechanism is the context-dependence of habits: "when usual contexts for performance change, habits cannot be cued by recurring stimuli, and performance should be disrupted. Thus, the exercising, newspaper reading, and TV watching habits of students transferring to a new university were found to survive the transfer only when aspects of the performance context did not change" ([Wood, Tam & Witt, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2005, abstract via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15982113/)). The broader habit discontinuity literature holds that changes of context open a window in which behaviour comes under intentional control. What this study does not test: deliberate seasonal redesign of one's own practices, or any effect on the staleness of routines; the outcomes were habit persistence after a move, and the redesign framing is editorial.

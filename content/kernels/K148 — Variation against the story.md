Practice: **Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)**

> **Kernel K148** — Variation against the story

Do one thing this week that your self-story says you never do. If the story says you are shy, speak to a stranger. If it says you are rigid, take a different route. The action does not prove the story wrong; it reveals the story as a story, and the forecast it makes about you turns out to be a prediction you can test.

### Protocol

1. Name the sentence the story uses. "I'm the quiet one." "I don't start things." Write it down and rate how much you believe it, 0 to 100.
2. Write the forecast the story implies: what will happen if you act against it, in feelings and in other people's reactions. Be specific.
3. Design one small act against it for this week, small enough to actually do, and drop the safety behaviours (the hedging, the over-rehearsal) that would blur the result.
4. Do it, then record what actually happened: facts, not feelings.
5. Compare the forecast with the outcome and re-rate the sentence. Keep the gap in writing; the next variation starts there.

### Evidence

The kernel's wager has been tested directly. Margolis and Lyubomirsky instructed participants "to behave like an extravert for 1 week and like an introvert for 1 week"; "participants reported marked growth in positive affect during the extraversion week and marked decline in positive affect during the introversion week", and the effects "were not moderated by baseline levels of trait extraversion" (Margolis & Lyubomirsky, Experimental Manipulation of Extraverted and Introverted Behavior). Earlier, Zelenski and colleagues found that "participants high in introversion forecasted more negative and less positive affect than they actually experienced when they were asked to behave more extraverted" (Zelenski et al. 2013, cited in Regan & Lyubomirsky): the story about the self mispredicts the lived result. One honest boundary: a randomised trial found the benefits of a week of acting extraverted were weaker for strongly introverted participants, who reported more tiredness and lower authenticity (Sun et al. 2019), so the variation should stay small and repeatable rather than total.

### A guided version

The [Behavioural Experiment Worksheet from Think CBT](https://thinkcbt.com/images/Downloads/Behavioural_Change_Resources/BEHAVIOURAL-EXPERIMENT-WORKSHEET-THINK-CBT-V-21.02.18.pdf) is a one-page sheet built for exactly this move: write the belief you are testing and rate it 0 to 100 percent, choose the situation, write your prediction, note the safety behaviours you will suspend, then record what actually happened and re-rate the belief.

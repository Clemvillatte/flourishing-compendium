> **Kernel K341** — The apology structure

When apologising, use four components: name what you did, acknowledge the impact, express regret without excuse, and state what you will do differently. The structure exists to prevent the common drift into justification. The study below is the closest opened evidence: adding other-focused elements made an apology read as more sincere.

### Protocol

The four components are the structure; the study behind the contrast is quoted below. Intended focus: Interpersonal behaviour.

1. Name what you did, in plain verbs: not "a mistake was made" but "I signed off without reading it."
2. Acknowledge the impact on the other person, in their terms rather than yours.
3. Express regret without excuse. If an explanation matters, it comes after the apology has landed, offered and separable.
4. State what you will do differently, concretely enough that its absence would be noticeable.

### Evidence

From the study comparing self-focused and other-focused apologies:

> "The self-focused apology was viewed more positively than negatively, but the apology that included additional other-focused elements elicited a more favourable reaction; it was seen as more sincere and as denoting more sorriness." (Abstract, [Allan, McKillop, Dooley, Allan & Preece, 2015, Patient Education and Counseling 98(9), 1058-1062](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26138159/))

What this evidence does not test: the four components. The study compared a self-focused apology with one that added other-focused elements (demonstrating understanding of the impact, remorse for the harm, and action to address it), in a video of a surgeon apologising for an adverse event, with 251 participants rating. The four-part structure is this kernel's own form; the finding that supports it is the narrower one, that the other-focused additions made the apology read as more sincere. The abstract reports no test of which components, or how many, an everyday apology needs.

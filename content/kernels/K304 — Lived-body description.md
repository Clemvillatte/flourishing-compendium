> **Kernel K304** — Lived-body description

Spend five minutes describing your body as you experience it from the inside, not as it appears from outside. Not "my legs are short" but "I feel tension in my calves, warmth in my chest, the weight of my head." The exercise is the phenomenological reduction in miniature: suspending the habit that treats the body as an object and attending to it as lived. Reach for it when you have become a spectator of your own body, which is most of the time after a mirror, a photo, or a long day at a desk.

### Protocol

Suggested adaptation of Merleau-Ponty's account of the lived body. The practice moves the Attention and Self dimensions: where attention rests, and the body you attend from.

1. Sit or stand somewhere ordinary, close the eyes if that helps, and take an inventory from the inside: warmth, weight, tension, contact, the places the body is speaking and the places it is quiet.
2. Describe, silently or on paper, only what is given from the inside. Merleau-Ponty's formulation of the difference: "But I am not in front of my body, I am in it, or rather I am it" ([Merleau-Ponty 1962, full text](https://archive.org/stream/merleaupontyphenomenologyofperception/Merleau-Ponty%20-%20Phenomenology%20of%20Perception_djvu.txt)). The object-body ("my legs are short") is seen; the lived body ("tension in my calves") is inhabited.
3. Editorial addition, not from the carrier: when the description slides back into appearance-language, start again from contact points: feet, chair, breath. Five minutes is a complete round.

### Evidence

The philosophical grounding, in the author's words:

> "Whether a system of motor or perceptual powers, our body is not an object for an 'I think', it is a grouping of lived-through meanings which moves towards its equilibrium."
> — [Merleau-Ponty (1962), *Phenomenology of Perception* (Smith trans.), full text](https://archive.org/stream/merleaupontyphenomenologyofperception/Merleau-Ponty%20-%20Phenomenology%20of%20Perception_djvu.txt)

And the lived body's resistance to being thought as an object:

> "The experience of our own body, on the other hand, reveals to us an ambiguous mode of existing... Therefore the body is not an object. For the same reason, my awareness of it is not a thought, that is to say, I cannot take it to pieces and reform it to make a clear idea. Its unity is always implicit and vague."

What the carriers do not test: these passages are descriptions from a philosophy of perception, not experimental findings; no study tests whether five minutes of inside-description produces any measured outcome, and the page claims none.

### A guided version

The bounded search confirms there is no guided recording of the phenomenological description itself; the self-directed writing is the practice. The guided form of the same attention is the body scan, covered by [[K102 — Body scan]]; Palouse Mindfulness publishes a [free guided body-scan audio](https://palousemindfulness.com/meditations/bodyscan.html) that walks the inside-of-the-body attention this kernel then writes down.

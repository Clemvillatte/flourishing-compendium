> **Kernel K171** — Emotional writing

Write about an emotional experience for 15 to 20 minutes, three to four days in a row. Do not edit, do not censor. The act of putting experience into language reduces its intensity and improves health outcomes.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the dosage and duration are the standard expressive-writing paradigm.*

1. Choose one emotionally significant experience; write about it for 15 to 20 minutes on three or four consecutive days.
2. Write the deepest thoughts and feelings about it, without regard to grammar, spelling, or sentence structure.
3. Expect the writing to sting in the short term; the measured benefits arrive later, not during.

### Evidence

The practice is the expressive-writing paradigm of Pennebaker and colleagues. In the founding experiment, "students randomly assigned to write about traumas for 4 days, 15 minutes a day, ended up going to the student health center over the next 6 months at about half the rate of students in the control condition (Pennebaker & Beall, 1986)" ([Pennebaker, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*, 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1745691617707315)). The original trial had students write for 15 minutes on four consecutive days about the most traumatic or upsetting experiences of their lives, and participants who wrote about their deepest thoughts and feelings reported significant physical-health benefits at four months, including fewer health-centre visits. What these studies do not test: the specific claim that emotional intensity is reduced in the moment of writing (the short-term pattern is arousal and negative mood, followed by later benefit); and the cancer-population meta-analysis found no statistically significant effects on psychological or physical health outcomes, so the benefit is not universal across conditions.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the writing is itself the practice; a recording would add nothing and the standard paradigm is unsupervised.

> **Kernel K252** — Moral exemplar modelling

Pick two or three people, living or dead, real or fictional, whose character you admire, and write down what specifically you admire. When a decision is hard, ask what one of them would do here. The exercise is orientation, not imitation: you are borrowing their eyes, not their life.

### Protocol

1. Nominate your exemplars yourself; the exemplar-education literature associates self-chosen, autonomy-supported exemplar work with stronger motivation.
2. For each, write one paragraph on the specific virtue in action: what they did, what it cost, what it took.
3. Prefer exemplars who are relatable and attainable, not only saints and geniuses; the same literature associates relatable, attainable exemplars with stronger motivation than unreachable paragons.
4. Keep the list where you make decisions. At a hard choice, ask: what would this person notice here that I am missing?
5. Answer in your own words, then act. Afterwards, note where your action and their imagined action diverged, and what that says about the gap between you.

### Evidence

The emotional engine of the practice has been measured. Algoe and Haidt induced the "other-praising" emotions and found that "elevation (a response to moral excellence) motivates prosocial and affiliative behavior, gratitude motivates improved relationships with benefactors, and admiration motivates self-improvement" (Algoe & Haidt 2009). Witnessing exemplars made participants report "wanting to be more like the other person", the emulation the kernel asks for. The educational literature adds a boundary condition: exemplar interventions that presented relatable, attainable exemplars through autonomy-supportive methods (letter writing, discussion, self-nomination) boosted moral motivation, while unreachable paragons can deflate it (Han et al., Considerations for Effective Use of Moral Exemplars in Education).

### A guided version

The [Moral Exemplars module at LibreTexts](https://biz.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Management/Book%3A_Corporate_Governance_%28Frey_and_CruzCruz%29/01%3A_Ethical_Leadership/1.04%3A_Moral_Exemplars_in_Business_and_Professional_Ethics) turns the exercise into steps: Exercise 1, choose an exemplar and write the narrative of their life; Exercise 2, profile their influences, good deeds, obstacles overcome, and the skills, attitudes, beliefs and emotions that oriented them; Exercise 3, dramatise a key moment from their life.

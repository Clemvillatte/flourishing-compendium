> **Kernel K286** — The good citizen audit

Once a month, review civic participation: whether the vote was cast, whether the community received a contribution, whether the issues affecting others were followed, whether public spaces and services were treated with respect. The ideal behind the audit is [[T014 — Marcus Aurelius|Marcus Aurelius]]'s happy citizen, whose study and practice are for the good of the fellow citizens ([Meditations, Project Gutenberg 2680](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2680/pg2680.txt)). Reach for it when citizenship has become something watched rather than practiced.

### What it rests on

Marcus's own picture, from the Meditations:

> "These things once so fixed and concluded, as thou wouldst think him a happy citizen, whose constant study and practice were for the good and benefit of his fellow citizens, and the carriage of the city such towards him, that he were well pleased with it; so must it needs be with thee, that thou shalt live a happy life." — [Marcus Aurelius, Meditations X.6 (Project Gutenberg 2680)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2680/pg2680.txt)

The monthly audit questions are my own design; the engaged citizen they measure is Marcus's.

### Protocol

The four questions are editorial; the standard behind them is the passage above.

1. Once a month, ask: have I voted?
2. Have I contributed to my community?
3. Have I stayed informed about issues that affect others?
4. Have I treated public spaces and services with respect?
5. Where an answer is no, note one act for the coming month that would change it.

### Evidence

The nearest research concerns volunteer work in the community, from a panel study of Americans' Changing Lives:

> "Results show that volunteer work indeed enhances all six aspects of well-being and, conversely, people who have greater well-being invest more hours in volunteer service." — [Thoits & Hewitt, 2001, Journal of Health and Social Behavior 42(2), Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11467248/)

The design is a two-wave observational panel (Americans' Changing Lives, N = 2,681), reporting relationships in both directions ([Thoits & Hewitt, 2001, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11467248/)).

What this evidence does not test: the cited research is an observational panel, not a randomized test of volunteering or of a civic audit; it did not evaluate a monthly audit practice, and voting, staying informed and respect for public spaces were not its outcomes ([Thoits & Hewitt, 2001, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11467248/)).

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a monthly self-review, not a recording.

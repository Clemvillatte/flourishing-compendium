> **Kernel K357** — Shared attention

With another person, attend to the same thing: a view, a piece of music, a child playing, food tasted together. The talking is left out. The practice takes its shape from the everyday human skill developmental psychologists call joint attention, and from the fact that in the laboratory, shared experiences are stronger than solitary ones. Reach for it when something good stands in front of two people at once.

### Protocol

Suggested adaptation of the two research traditions cited below. The practice moves the Attention and Affect dimensions: where attention rests, and how strongly the moment lands.

1. Choose the object together, or let it offer itself: something pleasant that rewards a few minutes of attention. The frame is the strict sense of joint attention: "Joint attention means more than just two individuals attending to the same thing; in addition, both individuals need to know together with each other that they are attending to the same thing" ([Carpenter 2012, Springer encyclopedia entry](https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Springer/Carpenter_Joint_EncSciencesLearning_2012_1658274.pdf)).
2. Sit or stand side by side and attend at the same time, without discussing what you are attending to. The experiments behind this kernel isolated exactly this condition: "merely having a simultaneous experience with another person enhances that experience, even in the absence of any direct communication" ([Boothby, Clark & Bargh 2014, author PDF](https://clarkrelationshiplab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/BoothbyClarkBargh(1).pdf)).
3. If attention drifts, return it to the shared object; the other person's continued attention is the anchor. A few minutes is a complete round.

### Evidence

The amplification finding, in the authors' own abstract:

> "In two studies, we found that sharing an experience with another person, without communicating, amplifies one's experience. Both pleasant and unpleasant experiences were more intense when shared."
> — [Boothby, Clark & Bargh (2014), *Psychological Science* 25(12), author PDF](https://clarkrelationshiplab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/files/BoothbyClarkBargh(1).pdf)

In the authors' words with the omission marked: in Study 1, participants judged pleasant chocolate "more likeable and flavorful when they tasted it at the same time that another person did than when that other person was present but engaged in a different activity" ... in Study 2, participants tasted unpleasantly bitter chocolate and judged it "less likeable when they tasted it simultaneously with another person". Amplification runs both ways, which is why the protocol directs it at something pleasant. What the studies do not test: brief laboratory chocolate tasting among people believed to be fellow participants, not walks, music, meals, or relationships over time, and not this page's routine.

The talking version of sharing the good points the same direction: "communicating personal positive events with others was associated with increased daily positive affect and well-being, above and beyond the impact of the positive event itself and other daily events" ([Gable, Reis, Impett & Asher 2004, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301629/)). Capitalization concerns telling; this kernel keeps the thing in front of both of you and keeps the silence.

### A guided version

None located for this practice among official sources. Guided recordings for two people do exist in the neighbourhood — partner-compassion and couples-mindfulness audio, such as Russ Harris's ACT exercise for partners ([Psychwire resource page](https://psychwire.com/free-resources/audio/resource-1hm1mbh/compassion-for-your-partner)) — but they guide related practices, not shared attention itself; the exercise is its own instruction.

## Related concepts

- [[Savoring]] — the deliberate up-regulation of good feeling by attention; this kernel is savoring's social counterpart, done with company rather than alone.
- [[Shared reality]] — Higgins's construct of the perceived commonality of inner states with other people; attending to the same thing is one everyday setting where it shows up.
- [[Intersubjectivity]] — the account of how meaning is generated and shared across embodied minds; a wordless shared focus is a simple instance.
- [[Capitalization]] — telling others about your good events; the talking practice that points the same direction as this silent one.
- [[Being-with (Mitsein)]] — Heidegger's account of being-with as a basic structure of the human being; attending to one thing together, without words, parallels it.

> **Kernel K156** — Morning intention

Write or speak one sentence of intention for the day: what you want to stand for, what you want to practice, what matters today.

### Protocol

The steps below are a practice form, not a tested protocol; the findings they lean on are quoted in the Evidence section. Intended focus: Motivation and Overt behaviour.

1. Before the first task of the day, write one sentence naming what today is for: what you want to stand for, practice, or protect.
2. Where the day has a predictable hard moment, give the sentence the shape "If [that situation], then I will [specific action]".
3. At day's end, record whether the planned action was attempted. Keep the sentences that moved behaviour, drop the ones that changed nothing, and try a different form tomorrow. The evening half of the loop is the Evening review kernel.

### Evidence

From the goal-progress research behind the practice:

> "Both studies, however, obtained a significant interaction effect indicating that goal self-concordance and implementation intentions combined synergistically to facilitate goal progress." (Abstract, [Koestner, Lekes, Powers & Chicoine, 2002, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 83(1), 231-244](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12088128/))

What this evidence does not test: the practice itself. Study 1 concerned weekend goal progress; Study 2 concerned monthly progress on New Year's resolutions and found a positive relation for self-concordance but no direct benefit for implementation intentions. The article's meta-analysis also reports that goal progress was associated with improved affect over time. None of this establishes that a daily one-sentence morning routine works; the routine is the practice form, and its effect is what the evening check keeps track of.

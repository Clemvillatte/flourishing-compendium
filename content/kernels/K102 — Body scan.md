> **Kernel K102** — Body scan

Move attention slowly through the body, from feet to head or head to feet, noticing sensations in each region without trying to change them. Where there is no sensation, simply note the absence. 10 to 20 minutes.

### Protocol

1. Lie down or sit comfortably. Close the eyes and take a few easy breaths to settle.
2. Bring attention to the first region, often the toes or the crown. Rest there for a while and register whatever is present: warmth, tingling, pressure, or nothing at all.
3. Move region by region up or down the body: feet, legs, hips, torso, shoulders, arms, neck, face. Sweep at a pace slow enough that each region gets a genuine visit. Nothing needs fixing; sensation is just observed.
4. Ten to twenty minutes covers a full pass. Sessions can be shorter by sweeping in larger steps, and the scan works well as the body half of a longer sit that closes with open monitoring.

### Evidence

In a preregistered multi-site study with 2,239 adults across 37 sites, four standalone mindfulness exercises were tested against an active control, and all reduced self-reported stress, with the body scan showing the largest effect (d = 0.56 against control). In the ReSource Project, a nine-month training study with 229 adults, the body scan produced the greatest state increase in interoceptive awareness and the greatest decrease in thought content among the compared practices.

### A guided version

A free, high-quality guided session is the [Body Scan (14 minutes) from UCLA Mindful](https://www.uclahealth.org/uclamindful/health-and-wellness-meditations), recorded by Diana Winston, director of mindfulness education at UCLA's Mindful Awareness Research Center; UCLA Mindful's [main guided-meditations page](https://www.uclahealth.org/uclamindful/guided-meditations) also offers a 3-minute short body scan.

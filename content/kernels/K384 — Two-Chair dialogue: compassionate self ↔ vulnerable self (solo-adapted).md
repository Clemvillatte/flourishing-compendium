> **Kernel K384** — Two-Chair dialogue: compassionate self ↔ vulnerable self (solo-adapted)

Set up two chairs facing each other. One is the "vulnerable chair" — the anxious, angry, sad, or ashamed part of you. The other is the "compassionate chair" — your Compassionate Self (see K378).

Sit in the vulnerable chair first. Let that part speak — really let it have its say. What is it afraid of? What is it angry about? What does it want? Don't edit it.

When that part has had its say, physically move to the compassionate chair. Take a breath, settle into the compassionate persona — wise, warm, courageous. From here, speak *to* the vulnerable part. Validate what you heard ("That makes sense — you've been hurt before, so you're watching for danger"). Offer kindness, understanding, encouragement.

If you notice yourself slipping back into critical or dismissive language, stand up and move back to the vulnerable chair — let the vulnerable part respond — then return to the compassionate chair.

Practice 10–15 minutes. Over time you're building the capacity to hear your own fear or anger without being run by it.

**Note:** This is a solo adaptation of a practice that in clinical settings is facilitated by a therapist. If a difficult emotion (trauma, panic, intense shame) feels unworkable alone, stop and consider professional support — this practice is for building the everyday capacity, not for processing severe distress.

Adapted from Kolts, *CFT Made Simple*, New Harbinger, 2016, ch. 12 + ch. 14, printed pp. 155–170 and 181–194.

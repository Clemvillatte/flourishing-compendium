> **Kernel K372** — The decisional-balance caution

"Weigh the pros and cons" sounds neutral and wise. But there's a case where it doesn't help: when you've *already* decided you want to change and you're ambivalent about following through. In that case, an equal list of reasons-for and reasons-against strengthens the ambivalence — you end up with a balanced scale and no movement.

The source's distinction:
- **Already want the change?** Explore the reasons-for, without suppressing real risks or uncertainty. The point is not to hide obstacles but to stop giving them equal airtime when you've already chosen a direction.
- **Genuinely undecided between two options?** A full balance sheet is exactly right — give all four squares equal attention so you can be at peace with whichever choice you make.

The caution is narrow: don't use a decisional balance to *create* motivation for a change you already want. And note: the more a helper is personally invested in the outcome, the more carefully they should stay neutral rather than pushing one side.

Adapted from Miller & Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing* (4th ed.), ch. 6, p. 92 (Box 6.1); the helper-investment caution at p. 106.

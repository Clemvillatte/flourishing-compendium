> **Kernel K170** — Forgiveness practice

Name what happened, acknowledge the harm, and choose to release the resentment. Forgiveness is not condoning, excusing, or reconciling. It is the decision to stop letting the past hold your present hostage.

### Protocol

*Steps follow the REACH structure; the outcome evidence for that structure is below.*

1. Recall the hurt objectively: what happened, what it cost, without either minimising or rehearsing.
2. Attempt empathy for the one who harmed, not as excusing but as seeing; a person seen in full is harder to hate.
3. Give the altruistic gift of forgiveness, commit to the decision, and expect to return to it; holding on is a repeat, not a failure.

### Evidence

The practice is Worthington's REACH forgiveness intervention, tested in a multisite randomised trial across five countries: "at 2 weeks follow-up, unforgiveness was lower among the immediate-treatment group compared with the delayed-treatment group (standardised mean difference=−0.53 (95% CI=−0.58 to −0.47)); similar patterns were found for depression (standardised mean difference=−0.22 (95% CI=−0.28 to −0.16)) and anxiety symptoms (standardised mean difference=−0.21 (95% CI=−0.27 to −0.15))" ([VanderWeele et al., *BMJ Public Health*, 2024, via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11812785/)). The trial delivered a self-directed workbook in about 3.3 hours, and the model has been found effective in over 30 randomised controlled trials. What this study does not test: the distinction between forgiveness and reconciliation in practice; the outcomes were unforgiveness, depression, and anxiety, and the "not condoning" framing is the model's own theoretical distinction.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a decision worked through in one's own mind, not a recording.

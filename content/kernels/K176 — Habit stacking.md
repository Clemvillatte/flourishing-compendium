> **Kernel K176** — Habit stacking

Anchor a new habit to an existing cue: "After I brush my teeth, I will sit for two minutes." The existing routine does the reminding, and the new habit rides on it. The recipe format comes from BJ Fogg's behavior design work, and the evidence base under if-then planning is the implementation-intention literature.

## Protocol

1. Pick the anchor. A routine you already do without fail, ideally at the same point each day: brushing teeth, pouring coffee, sitting down at the desk.
2. Write the recipe. Fogg's format, verbatim: "After I [existing anchor] , I will [new tiny behavior]". Pair the two: "In this process you will pair a tiny behavior with a good anchor" ([tinyhabits.com](https://tinyhabits.com/design/)).
3. Keep the new behavior tiny. Small enough that it survives your worst day.
4. Revise what fails. Fogg's page is blunt about the hit rate: "Some pairings work well, and the habit forms quickly and naturally. Some pairings don't work at all. But don't worry. If the pairing doesn't work, you revise. In other words, you just redesign the recipe" ([tinyhabits.com](https://tinyhabits.com/design/)).
5. Run one recipe at a time. When the link holds without prompting, design the next one.

## A guided version

The writing and revising are yours; Fogg's design page does include a short video walking through recipe creation, and that page is the natural companion to this exercise.

## Evidence

The if-then plan at the bottom of the recipe has a long research history. A meta-analysis of the implementation-intention literature reported that "A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests of implementation intentions effects on goal achievement revealed a medium-to-large effect (d = .65)" ([Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/bitstreams/d4f710b4-a505-49ef-a831-5b8d7675100b/download), p. 69). The plan works by spelling things out in advance: "An implementation intention spells out when, where, and how a person will act to reach a goal. It takes the form of an if-then plan: 'If situation Y is encountered, then I will perform goal-directed behavior Z!'" (p. 69), and the review concludes that "Implementation intentions help people to initiate goal-directed behaviors, shield ongoing goal pursuit from unwanted influences, disengage from failing courses of action, and conserve their capacity for future goal pursuit" (p. 69).

What the research does not cover: the 94 tests measured if-then plans against goal attainment, not Fogg's anchor-recipe specifically and not long-run habit formation. The two-minute sit example is this practice's own; the meta-analysis is about goals from exercise to voting, not about stacking habits onto tooth-brushing.

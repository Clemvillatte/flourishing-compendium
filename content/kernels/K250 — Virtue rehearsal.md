> **Kernel K250** — Virtue rehearsal

Pick one virtue and practise it in small daily acts that get written down. The exercise draws on two opened accounts: Aristotle's teaching that virtue of character is acquired by repeated acts, set out in [Nicomachean Ethics II.1 (Chase translation)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm), and Benjamin Franklin's self-examination chart in his [Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm), thirteen virtues tracked a week at a time in a little ruled book. Reach for it when a virtue stays an admired idea while the days keep running on their old tracks.

### What it rests on

Aristotle's craftsman analogy, Nicomachean Ethics II.1:

> "men come to be builders, for instance, by building; harp-players, by playing on the harp: exactly so, by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave actions brave." — [Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1, Chase translation (Project Gutenberg 8438)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm)

He closes the chapter with the stakes: "whether we are accustomed this way or that straight from childhood, makes not a small but an important difference, or rather I would say it makes all the difference" (II.1). Franklin's chart, in his own words:

> "I made a little book, in which I allotted a page for each of the virtues." — [Franklin, Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm)

> "I determined to give a week's strict attention to each of the virtues successively." — [Franklin, Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm)

> "Proceeding thus to the last, I could go thro' a course compleat in thirteen weeks, and four courses in a year." — [Franklin, Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm)

### Protocol

This exercise adapts Franklin's self-examination chart. The daily positive act, the one-sentence record and the month-long focus are editorial additions, not Franklin's original instructions.

1. Name the virtue in one plain word, as Franklin did: his list ran from Temperance and Silence to Resolution and Humility ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/20203/20203-h/20203-h.htm)).
2. Give it a page with one line per day.
3. Each day, do one small act that expresses the virtue, and write it down in one sentence.
4. On a day of failure, mark the fault plainly: Franklin marked "by a little black spot, every fault I found upon examination to have been committed respecting that virtue upon that day."
5. Hold the focus on this one virtue for a week (Franklin) or a month (the editorial version), then move to the next and come round again.

### Evidence

Two nearby bodies of research, neither a test of this chart.

Habit formation. In [Lally et al., 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674), 96 volunteers chose an eating, drinking or activity behaviour to carry out daily in the same context for 12 weeks; the analysis used the 82 who provided sufficient data, and 62 of those fitted the automaticity model. The behaviours studied were simple health actions, not virtues, and the measure was self-reported automaticity, not character.

> "The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days; indicating considerable variation in how long it takes people to reach their limit of automaticity and highlighting that it can take a very long time." — [Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6), Abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674)

Gratitude listing. In [Emmons & McCullough, 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12585811/), participants kept weekly or daily lists of blessings, hassles or neutral events. The tested practice is a record of gratitude, not a rehearsal of virtues.

> "The gratitude-outlook groups exhibited heightened well-being across several, though not all, of the outcome measures across the 3 studies, relative to the comparison groups." — [Emmons & McCullough, 2003, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 84(2), Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12585811/)

What this evidence does not test: neither study ran this protocol or measured whether character changed.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a notebook and a pen, not a recording.

> **Kernel K250** — Virtue rehearsal

Pick one virtue and practise it in small daily acts that get written down. The exercise draws on two accounts: [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]]'s teaching that virtue of character is acquired by repeated acts, set out in [Nicomachean Ethics II.1 (Chase translation)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt), and Benjamin Franklin's self-examination chart in his [Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt), thirteen virtues tracked a week at a time in a little ruled book. Reach for it when a virtue stays an admired idea while the days keep running on their old tracks.

### What it rests on

Aristotle's craftsman analogy, Nicomachean Ethics II.1:

> "men come to be builders, for instance, by building; harp-players, by playing on the harp: exactly so, by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave actions brave." — [Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1, Chase translation (Project Gutenberg 8438)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)

He closes the chapter with the stakes: "whether we are accustomed this way or that straight from childhood, makes not a small but an important difference, or rather I would say it makes all the difference" ([II.1](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)). Franklin's chart, in his own words:

> "I made a little book, in which I allotted a page for each of the virtues." — [Franklin, Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)

> "I determined to give a week's strict attention to each of the virtues successively." — [Franklin, Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)

> "Proceeding thus to the last, I could go thro' a course compleat in thirteen weeks, and four courses in a year." — [Franklin, Autobiography (Project Gutenberg 20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)

### Protocol

This is an editorial exercise inspired by Franklin's self-examination chart, not a reconstruction of his instructions. The daily positive act, simplified record and weekly or monthly rotation are editorial choices.

1. Name the virtue in one plain word, as Franklin did: his list ran from Temperance and Silence to Resolution and Humility ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)).
2. Give it a page with one line per day.
3. Each day, do one small act that expresses the virtue, and write it down in one sentence.
4. On a day of failure, mark the fault plainly: Franklin marked "by a little black spot, every fault I found upon examination to have been committed respecting that virtue upon that day" ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)).
5. Hold the focus on this one virtue for a week or a month (the editorial rotation), then move to the next and come round again.

### Evidence

Two nearby bodies of research, neither a test of this chart.

Habit formation. In [Lally et al., 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674), 96 volunteers chose an eating, drinking or activity behaviour to carry out daily in the same context for 12 weeks; of the 96 volunteers, 82 provided sufficient data; the model was fitted for 62 individuals, of whom 39 showed a good fit. The behaviours studied were simple health actions, not virtues, and the measure was self-reported automaticity, not character.

> "The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days; indicating considerable variation in how long it takes people to reach their limit of automaticity and highlighting that it can take a very long time." — [Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6), Abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674)

Gratitude listing. In [Emmons & McCullough, 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12585811/), participants kept weekly or daily lists of blessings, hassles or neutral events. The tested practice is a record of gratitude, not a rehearsal of virtues.

> "The gratitude-outlook groups exhibited heightened well-being across several, though not all, of the outcome measures across the 3 studies, relative to the comparison groups." — [Emmons & McCullough, 2003, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 84(2), Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12585811/)

What this evidence does not test: neither study ran this protocol or measured whether character changed.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a notebook and a pen, not a recording.

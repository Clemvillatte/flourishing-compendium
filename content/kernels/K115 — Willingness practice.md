> **Kernel K115** — Willingness practice

When an unwanted inner state arises (urge, pain, anxiety, sadness), say silently: "This is here, and I can feel it." Let the experience be present without trying to change, suppress, or argue with it. Willingness comes before change.

### Protocol

The steps follow Russ Harris's four-step emotional-acceptance exercise from his introductory ACT workshop handout; the quoted lines are exact:

1. Observe. "Bring awareness to the feelings in your body."
2. Breathe. "Take a few deep breaths. Breathe into and around them."
3. Expand. "Make room for these feelings. Create some space for them."
4. Allow. "Allow them to be there. Make peace with them."

The same handout keeps the definition honest: "Willingness doesn't mean you like, want, enjoy, desire, or approve of something. Willingness means you'll allow it, make room for it, or let it be, in order to do something that you value" ([Harris, 2008 handout](https://www.actmindfully.com.au/upimages/2008_Introductory_ACT_Workshop_Handout_-__Russ_Harris.pdf)).

### Evidence

In a study with participants diagnosed with anxiety and mood disorders, people watched an upsetting film while instructed either to accept their emotional responses or to suppress them. The acceptance group showed a lower heart rate while viewing the film and reported less negative affect during the recovery period afterward, while the suppression group showed increased cardiac arousal and slower mood recovery. Related work in panic disorder and high anxiety sensitivity found that accepting anxious feelings produced less distress than suppressing them. The picture is not one-sided: a study with 89 healthy participants found that acceptance increased physiological reactivity and negative affect at one-week follow-up, which suggests the practice is not uniformly calming and may work best when the alternative is an active struggle. Willingness is a stance toward struggle, not a sedative.

### A guided version

A short guided exercise in the same library as the other acceptance tools is [Willingness as a Choice from the ACT Toolkit](https://www.acttoolkit.com/) — a seven-minute exercise whose framing line is "Acceptance isn't a feeling you have to produce, but a stance you can choose."

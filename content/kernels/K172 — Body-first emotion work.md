> **Kernel K172** — Body-first emotion work

When an emotion is hard to locate or name, start with the body. Where is the tension? What is the temperature? What is the texture? The body often knows before the mind does.

### Protocol

The work starts below the neck, on purpose; the daily-life study of that starting point is quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion and Body.

1. When an emotion is hard to locate or name, stop searching for the word and scan the body instead: where is the tension, what is the temperature, what is the texture.
2. Stay with the strongest sensation for a few breaths and let a name arrive on its own, if one arrives; a rough word for the body state is enough to work with.
3. Once named, choose the response; the body scan is not the regulation, it is the getting-of-information that makes regulation possible. If nothing is found, that is data too, and worth noting without forcing.

### Evidence

From the experience-sampling study of interoceptive attention in daily life:

> "State IA significantly predicted between-strategy variability, which was mediated by emotional awareness" (Abstract, [Ma, Wang, Blain & Tan, 2024, Psychosomatic Medicine 86(7), 640-647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38787549/))

What this evidence does not test: the protocol. Two hundred three students reported their interoceptive attention, emotional awareness, negative affect and regulation strategies seven times a day for a week, and paying attention to bodily states predicted more flexible strategy use, with emotional awareness carrying part of the effect; strategy variability, in turn, went with lower negative affect. The study is correlational experience sampling, not a trial of a body-scan practice, and the abstract reports nothing about emotions being hard to name, tension, temperature or texture. What it supports is the kernel's direction of travel: bodily attention runs through emotional awareness toward more adaptable responding, which is the order of operations the practice proposes.

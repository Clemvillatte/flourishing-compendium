> **Kernel K230** — Memento mori

Keep a physical reminder of mortality: a skull on the desk, a coin in the pocket, a daily reflection. The reminder is not to induce fear but to sharpen the question of how to spend the time you have.

## Facets

- **live fully** — Stoics
- **and acceptance** — Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations
- **the cosmic perspective** — Stoics, Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

### Protocol

The reminder is a small physical question kept in reach; the trial of the deliberate version is quoted below. Intended focus: Meaning and Self.

1. Choose one physical reminder of mortality and keep it where you will meet it: a skull on the desk, a coin in the pocket, an object that stands for finitude.
2. Each time you meet it, do not linger on fear; ask the sharpened question: given that the time is finite, what does today deserve? Answer with the day's actual plan, not with a mood.
3. At day's end, one line on whether the reminder changed any allocation of the day; if it never changes anything, the reminder has become furniture, and should be moved or replaced.

### Evidence

From the randomized trial of mortality-awareness interventions:

> "Longitudinal analyses revealed a decrease in the intervention group's fear of dying and an increase in their acceptance of dying, but no effects on attitudes toward death." (Abstract, [Spitzenstätter & Schnell, 2022, Death Studies 46(5), 1219-1233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32845800/))

What this evidence does not test: the skull on the desk. In the trial, an intervention group of 51 people completed exercises to heighten mortality awareness while a control group of 47 completed questionnaires only, and over time the intervention group's fear of dying fell and their acceptance of dying rose. The changes in meaningfulness were contingent on participants' religiousness, and attitudes toward death were unchanged; the trial also did not test physical reminders, daily reflections, or effects on how time was actually spent. What it supports is the kernel's wager: deliberately heightening awareness of mortality can soften the fear rather than feed it, which is what the reminder is for.

Practice: **Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT)**

> **Kernel K394** — Sounds and Thoughts meditation

Sit comfortably. First, rest on the breath for a few minutes. Then open to sounds — near, far, in front, behind. Do not search for sounds; just receive them as they arise. Notice the tendency to label each sound and, below the label, return to the raw sensation of the sound itself. After four or five minutes, shift the same stance to thoughts: see thoughts as events in the mind, arising, lingering, and passing away — like clouds moving across a spacious sky. Do not try to make them come or go.

### Protocol

Adapted from Williams and Penman, *Mindfulness*, 2011, ch. 8; the authors' website carries the full sixteen-step instruction.

1. Sit with the spine self-supporting, back straight but not stiff. Shoulders relaxed, head and neck balanced, chin tucked slightly in.
2. Rest attention on the movements of the breath until reasonably settled; widen to the body as a whole, as if the whole body were breathing.
3. Shift to hearing. There is no need to go searching for sounds or listening out for particular sounds; stay open to sounds from all directions as they arise, near, far, in front, behind.
4. Be aware of sounds simply as sounds, as raw sensations. Notice the tendency to label sounds as soon as they are received, and refocus beyond and below the label on the raw sensation itself, including the sounds within sounds.
5. After four or five minutes, shift the same stance to thoughts: seeing them, as best you can, as events in the mind, arising, lingering and passing away. Do not try to make thoughts come or go; let them come and go on their own.

### Evidence

The authors' own account of why the practice works:

> "The Sounds and Thoughts meditation gradually reveals the similarities between sound and thought. Both appear as if from nowhere. Both can seem random and we have no control over their arising. Both are enormously potent and carry immense momentum. They trigger powerful emotions that can easily run away with us."
> — [Williams & Penman, franticworld.com — Sounds and Thoughts Meditation](https://franticworld.com/sounds-and-thoughts-meditation/)

The practice's own naming of its two elements:

> "There are two key elements to the Sounds and Thoughts meditation – they are receiving and noticing."
> — [same source](https://franticworld.com/sounds-and-thoughts-meditation/)

The reasoning that connects the two halves:

> "Thoughts come as if from nowhere. Just as the ear is the organ that receives sounds, the mind is the organ that receives thoughts."
> — [same source](https://franticworld.com/sounds-and-thoughts-meditation/)

The same page teaches the full step list; step 6, the labelling move, reads:

> "As best you can, be aware of sounds simply as sounds, as raw sensations. Notice the tendency we all have to label sounds as soon as they are received (car, train, voice, air conditioning, radio), and see if it is possible simply to notice this labelling and then refocus, beyond and below the label, on the raw sensations of the sounds themselves (including the sounds within sounds)."
> — [same source](https://franticworld.com/sounds-and-thoughts-meditation/)

What the sources do not test: the exercise is one component of the book's eight-week programme, whose overall outcomes have been studied; the sounds-and-thoughts pairing as an intervention has not been isolated in a trial.

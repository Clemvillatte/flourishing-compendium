> **Kernel K177** — Environment design

Design your physical and digital environment to make desired behaviors easy and undesired behaviors hard. Put the phone in another room. Leave the guitar out. Lay out the running shoes. The field research below found changing the situation worked better for students than mustering willpower in the moment.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the field experiments behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Overt behaviour and Environment.

1. Pick one behaviour you want more of and one you want less of. For each, change the room: leave the guitar out, put the phone in another room, lay out the running shoes, move the snacks out of sight.
2. Make the change physical, not mental: the point is that the environment does the remembering, so design it once instead of deciding repeatedly.
3. After a week, check which changes held and which got quietly undone; keep the ones that survived the cleaning and rearranging of ordinary life.

### Evidence

From the field research on situation modification:

> "In Study 2, high school students randomly assigned to implement situation modification were more likely to meet their academic goals during the following week than students assigned either to implement response modulation or no strategy at all." (Abstract, [Duckworth, White, Matteucci, Shearer & Gross, 2016, Journal of Educational Psychology 108(3), 329-341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27158155/))

What this evidence does not test: the home redesign. The experiments assigned students a strategy for academic goals and tracked the week's outcomes; the college version (Study 3) found the same success, partly mediated by decreased feelings of temptation. The studies did not test rearranged rooms, phones in other rooms, or running shoes by the door; they support the strategy class, changing the situation before the impulse grows, not any particular design. The framing that this is the strongest self-control strategy is the practice's own; what the studies found is that early strategies beat late ones for students on academic goals.

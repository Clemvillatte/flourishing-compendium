> **Kernel K293** — Elder and mentor cycles

Identify one person older or more experienced in a domain that matters, and ask them for regular conversation. Then identify one person younger or less experienced, and offer the same. The Confucian picture behind the cycle is the one who reviews what has been learned and learns anew, and is thereby fit to be a teacher ([Analects 2:11, Muller translation](http://www.acmuller.net/con-dao/analects.html)). Reach for it when knowledge has stopped moving between generations.

### What it rests on

Confucius's own line, in Muller's translation:

> "Reviewing what you have learned and learning anew, you are fit to be a teacher." — [Confucius, Analects 2:11, Muller translation](http://www.acmuller.net/con-dao/analects.html)

The two-sided arrangement is my own design; the old-and-new review it presumes is Confucius's.

### Protocol

The identification steps are editorial; the cycle is the point.

1. Identify one person older or more experienced in a domain that matters.
2. Ask them for regular conversation.
3. Identify one person younger or less experienced.
4. Offer the same.

### Evidence

The nearest research is an intergenerational program in which the elders are the volunteers:

> "Experience Corps (EC) represents a high-intensity, intergenerational civic engagement activity where older adults serve as mentors and tutors in elementary schools." — [Varma et al., 2015, The Gerontologist 55(6), Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24589989/)

The volunteers in the study, 46 in focus groups, reported both stressors and rewards, including "children's problem behavior" alongside "developing a special connection with a child" ([Varma et al., 2015, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24589989/)). The authors' conclusion: "Overall, intergenerational, interpersonal, and personal rewards from volunteering, as well as program structure may have balanced the stress associated with volunteering." ([Varma et al., 2015, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24589989/))

What this evidence does not test: the cited research gathered the volunteers' perspectives; it did not measure benefits for the mentees, and a standing arrangement of regular conversation is not the EC program it studied ([Varma et al., 2015, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24589989/)).

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a standing conversation, not a recording.

> **Kernel K119** — Response prevention

During exposure to a feared or unwanted experience, refrain from the safety behaviours: checking, reassuring, escaping, distracting, neutralising. In the treatment literature this is the RP of exposure and response prevention, the behavioural treatment with the most empirical support for obsessive-compulsive disorder.

### Protocol

*Steps drawn from the treatment literature on EX/RP as described by Foa and colleagues; the everyday framing is my.*

1. Enter the feared or avoided situation on purpose, without the ritual or safety behaviour that usually follows it. "the exposure must be done without rituals, as exposure without ritual prevention is not effective" ([Gillihan, Williams, Malcoun, Yadin & Foa, 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3423997/)).
2. Do not soften the exposure with distraction or subtle avoidance: "the goal of exposure in EX/RP is to face the obsession-provoking stimuli head-on, without tricks or subtle forms of avoidance."
3. Do not seek reassurance: patients "must learn to reduce their fear of uncertainty and resist urges to attain certainty so that they learn that even without the provision of reassurance they can tolerate high distress."
4. Stay until the learning happens: that the feared outcome does not occur, or occurs and is tolerable, "even without compulsive behaviors."

### Evidence

The evidence for exposure and response prevention in OCD is strong: in the randomised placebo-controlled trial of 122 adults, "Foa et al. (2005) found that 86% of EX/RP completers responded to the treatment versus 48% for clomipramine and 10% for placebo" ([Gillihan et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3423997/); trial: Foa et al., *American Journal of Psychiatry*, 2005, 162:151–161). The mechanism the kernel relies on is stated plainly in the same paper: rituals and safety behaviours during or after exposure "prevent[] disconfirmation of the feared consequences... and learning that anxiety and distress during exposure decreases even without compulsive behaviors." What the trials do not test: this page's everyday version, applied to ordinary unwanted experiences outside OCD treatment, and any effect on flourishing beyond symptom reduction.

### A guided version

Not applicable as a recording: response prevention is the deliberate absence of a behaviour inside a live situation, guided in the clinical setting by a therapist rather than a track.

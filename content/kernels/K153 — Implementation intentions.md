> **Kernel K153** — Implementation intentions

Formulate a specific if-then plan: "If I feel anxious before the meeting, then I will take three slow breaths and focus on my intention." The pre-commitment bypasses in-the-moment decision fatigue.

## Facets

- **and planning** — Gollwitzer, 1999
- **and goal intentions** — Gollwitzer, 1999
- **and habit formation**
- **for high-conflict moments**

### Protocol

The plan is written once, for one foreseeable moment; the field test of the form is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Motivation.

1. Name the goal and the moment where it usually fails: not "be calmer in meetings" but the specific fork where you go the wrong way, such as feeling anxious before the meeting.
2. Write the if-then plan in one sentence with a concrete cue and a small concrete response: if I feel anxious before the meeting, then I will take three slow breaths and focus on my intention. The response should be executable in under a minute.
3. Rehearse the sentence once in imagination, cue to response, and leave it alone; the plan works by being ready, not by being redrafted. Review after the moment arrives, and rewrite only if the cue or response proved wrong.

### Evidence

From the field experiment on screening attendance:

> "despite equivalent motivation to attend, participants who formed implementation intentions were much more likely to attend for screening compared with controls (92% vs. 69%)." (Abstract, [Sheeran & Orbell, 2000, Health Psychology 19(3), 283-289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10868773/))

What this evidence does not test: the breath or the intention. In the study, 114 women registered at a rural English medical practice were split so that half wrote down when, where and how they would make their screening appointment; attendance, verified from medical records, was 92 percent against 69 percent among equally motivated controls. The trial tested one if-then plan for one appointment, not the kernel's anxious-moment example, and the abstract reports nothing about decision fatigue, the plan's proposed mechanism. What it supports is the form itself: specifying the cue and the action in advance moved behavior where motivation alone did not.

> **Kernel K223** — Meaning audit

A monthly sitting with the month's activities, asking each one the four reason questions that motivation researchers use to tell a chosen goal from an imposed one. The questions come from the self-concordance work of Kennon Sheldon and Andrew Elliot, who built the reason-rating method on self-determination theory. Reach for it at the end of a month, or whenever life starts to read like a list of obligations with no author.

### Protocol

Steps 1 to 3 are the audit form; the reason questions inside step 2 are quoted from the opened self-concordance paper. Step 4 is the compendium's editorial close.

1. List the month's recurring activities and commitments, the ones that actually took the hours.
2. For each, ask the four reason questions, in the researchers' wording: is it "because somebody else wants you to or because your situation seems to demand it" (external)? Is it because "you would feel ashamed, guilty, or anxious if you didn't" (introjected)? Is it because "you really believe that it's an important goal to have" (identified)? Or is it for "the fun and enjoyment which the striving provides you" (intrinsic)?
3. Mark each activity by its honest dominant reason: yours, borrowed, or an ought.
4. Feed the ones that are yours, with more time or better attention. For the borrowed and the oughts, prune, shrink or renegotiate what can be pruned, shrunk or renegotiated. The monthly rhythm and the feed/prune rule are editorial choices, not part of the published method.

### Evidence

The reason questions, from the opened cross-cultural study:

> "You are pursuing this striving because you really believe that it's an important goal to have. Although others may have urged you to pursue this striving in the past, now you endorse it freely and value it for personal reasons." (Identified reason item, [Sheldon, Elliot, Ryan, Chirkov, Kim, Wu, Demir & Sun, 2004, Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2004_SheldonElliotRyanChirkovetal.pdf))

On the activity side of the audit, an experience-sampling study of 62 students found that everyday activities do carry distinct ratings of meaning: "activities grouped under culture/music, communication, intimacy, and sports are experienced as both highly meaningful and pleasant" (Abstract, [Kreiss & Schnell, 2022, Frontiers in Psychology 13:977687](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9708871/)). What this evidence does not test: the monthly audit as an intervention. The self-concordance studies rate goals, not a month's whole activity load, and the sampling study observes ratings for one week without changing anything.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the audit is a written reflection, not a recording.

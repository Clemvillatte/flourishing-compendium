> **Kernel K162** — Self-compassion practice

When you are struggling, place a hand on your chest and say: "This is a moment of suffering. Suffering is part of life. May I be kind to myself in this moment." Treat yourself as you would a good friend in the same situation.

### Protocol

1. Call to mind something in your life right now that hurts: a mistake, a rejection, a moment of not feeling good enough. Choose something real but not overwhelming.
2. Acknowledge the pain plainly: "This is a moment of suffering." That is the mindfulness component, seeing the hurt instead of pushing past it.
3. Place your hand on your chest or another soothing spot, and connect with the shared human side of it: "Suffering is part of life. Others feel this too." That is common humanity.
4. Offer yourself kindness, in words or in touch: "May I be kind to myself. May I give myself what I need." That is self-kindness. The whole break can take less than a minute, and you can take it any time the critic starts up.

### Evidence

A meta-analysis of 20 samples from 14 studies, all using Neff's Self-Compassion Scale, found a large inverse association between self-compassion and psychopathology (r = −0.54, 95% CI −0.57 to −0.51). People higher in self-compassion report less depression, anxiety, and stress. The evidence is correlational, so it shows association rather than proof of causal benefit from the exercise itself.

### A guided version

The break is taught by its developer in [Self Compassion Break with Kristin Neff, from the Center for Mindful Self-Compassion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p7z3ybkn9DU).

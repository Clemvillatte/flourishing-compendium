> **Kernel K316** — Mortality-to-action translation

Write for five minutes in response to: "I will die. The timing is unknown. What does this make urgent?" Then translate one insight from the writing into one action this week. The exercise is not morbid. Research on mortality salience (Terror Management Theory) shows that confronting mortality, when not defended against, can shift priorities toward intrinsic values.

### Protocol

The writing is half the practice; the action is the other half; the experiment behind the direction is quoted below. Intended focus: Meaning and Motivation.

1. Write for five minutes in response to the prompt: I will die, the timing is unknown, what does this make urgent. Write without editing; the draft is working material, not a keepsake.
2. Reread once and underline the single most concrete insight, the one that names something a week could hold; urgency that cannot fit inside seven days is a mood, not a translation.
3. Translate that insight into one action this week, with a day and a time attached, and put it in the calendar before the five-minute exercise is allowed to end; a reflection that produces no action has been defended against, not done. Note the insight and the action in one line.

### Evidence

From the experiment on mortality awareness and intertemporal choice:

> "Experiment 1 compared the effects of mortality (endpoint) imagination with the imagination of being at age 70 and found that only mortality imagination significantly reduced delay discounting, the extent to which a delayed reward is discounted." (Abstract, [Wang & Wang, 2024, Consciousness and Cognition 126, 103787](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39556995/))

What this evidence does not test: the practice. In the experiment, imagining one's own death, unlike imagining being seventy, made participants less prone to take the smaller sooner reward, and the effect strengthened when the mortality reminder was paired with retrospective episodic thinking. That is the kernel's wager in miniature: the endpoint reminder moved actual choice toward the longer view, which is what a translation into action asks for. But the study measured discounting of monetary rewards in two experiments, not values, priorities, or weekly actions; the abstract reports no writing exercise and no five-minute format. What it supports is the direction of the shift, not the practice that arrives at it.

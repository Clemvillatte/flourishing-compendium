> **Kernel K152** — Committed action

Take one action today that is consistent with a chosen value, however small. The action matters more than the feeling. Especially powerful when you do not feel like it.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the review behind it is quoted below. Intended focus: Motivation and Overt behaviour.

1. Choose one value to guide today's action.
2. Take one action consistent with it, however small. If the chosen action meets the feeling of not wanting to, take it anyway, at a size you can actually do.
3. In the evening log, record the action taken and whether it happened at all. Keep the sizes that get done; drop the ones that stay on the list.

### Evidence

From the research on psychological flexibility, the process family this practice belongs to:

> "The construct, which is based on Relational Frame Theory and operationalised within the framework of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), consists of six interconnected processes: self-as-context, values clarity, acceptance, cognitive defusion, present-moment awareness, and committed action." (Abstract, [Chalghaf, Chokri, Boukari, Dergaa, Dhahbi, Ceylan, Maamri, Solmi, Muntean, Guelmami & Dergaa, 2026, Psychology Research and Behavior Management 19, 596070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42404030/))

What this evidence does not test: the single daily action. The review names committed action as one of six processes and reports meta-analytic small-to-medium effects for flexibility-based interventions across conditions, with the authors' own caveats that the evidence is largely cross-sectional, culturally homogeneous and weak on causal inference. Nothing here isolates one small values-consistent action per day as the effective ingredient; the review reduces the approach to techniques such as values-guided behavioural activation, which is the family this kernel's step belongs to.

> **Kernel K343** — Needs-before-solutions

When someone is struggling, name the need before proposing a solution. "It sounds like you need to be heard, not fixed." The practice prevents the well-intentioned but unhelpful pattern of offering advice before the person has been understood. The experiments below are the opened evidence on the contrast: affirmation was rated the most comforting and preferred feedback, ahead of reframing.

### Protocol

The order is the practice; the experiments behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Interpersonal behaviour.

1. When someone is struggling, hold the solution back for a beat and name the need instead: "It sounds like you need to be heard right now, not fixed."
2. Ask whether the name is right, and let them correct it; the naming is a guess offered, not a diagnosis delivered.
3. Only when the need has been named and confirmed, ask whether they want ideas, or just company. The question itself is half the practice.

### Evidence

From the experiments on social emotion regulation:

> "Across studies, when participants received feedback about their own experiences, validation was the most comforting and preferred feedback." (Abstract, [Sahi, He, Silvers & Eisenberger, 2023, Emotion 23(6), 1522-1535](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36480403/))

What this evidence does not test: the ordering rule. In two experiments where people shared personal experiences and received different kinds of feedback, validation, which affirms someone's feelings, was rated the most comforting and preferred, ahead of three forms of social reappraisal that help change how one thinks about the experience. The abstract reports no test of withholding advice until the need is named; and the studies measured comfort with feedback types, not whether validation reduces distress over time. What it supports is the kernel's premise: affirmation lands better than reframing when someone is in the middle of something.

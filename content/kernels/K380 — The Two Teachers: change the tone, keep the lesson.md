> **Kernel K380** — The Two Teachers: change the tone, keep the lesson

Think of two teachers giving the same correction — one with contempt, one with warmth. The imagined comparison is the exercise: which lesson would you keep working at?

When you catch yourself in self-criticism (or criticizing someone you care about), ask: what is the *information* here? What would change if the same words were delivered by someone who was on your side? The goal is not to stop being corrected — the goal is to correct the *way* you're correcting.

Practice: next time you hear your inner critic say something, write the sentence down. Then rewrite it in the voice of a warm teacher — same lesson, different tone. Notice the difference in your body. You are not deleting the correction; you are keeping the useful part and dropping the contempt.

Adapted from Kolts, CFT Made Simple, New Harbinger, 2016, ch. 8, printed pp. 107-114 (vignette at pp. 109-112).

### Evidence

Why the exercise exists, in Kolts's own words:

> "While many of us may use self-criticism as an attempt to motivate, protect, or “keep ourselves in line,” clinging to the self-critic for these purposes is based on a logical fallacy—that self-attacking is the only way to motivate ourselves, or perhaps the best way. Even if self-shaming and self-attacking do seem to serve some useful purposes, they do so at a great cost, keeping us stuck in the threat system (with narrowed attention and thinking, focused on threats) and diminishing our happiness."
> — [Kolts, CFT Made Simple (PDF), ch. 8](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1uN4kJ9GGbWVIlzNAxeOPsO9w6VYJr-iP)

The exercise's provenance, as Kolts gives it:

> "One of my favorite methods for doing this is the “Two Teachers” vignette, an experiential exercise commonly used by CFT therapists (Gilbert, 2009a)."
> — [Kolts, CFT Made Simple (PDF)](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1uN4kJ9GGbWVIlzNAxeOPsO9w6VYJr-iP)

And the second teacher, in the vignette itself:

> "Now, let’s imagine a second teacher this child could have. This teacher is compassionate, really wanting to help the child learn. He understands that this is a really difficult task, and that anyone would struggle with it, especially in the beginning."
> — [Kolts, CFT Made Simple (PDF)](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1uN4kJ9GGbWVIlzNAxeOPsO9w6VYJr-iP)

What the source does not test: the vignette is an experiential exercise, and the book teaches it to therapists for clients; the self-directed rewrite on this page is an adaptation.

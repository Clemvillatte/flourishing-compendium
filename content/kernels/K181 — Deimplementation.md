> **Kernel K181** — Deimplementation

Regularly retire a practice, strategy, or rule that no longer earns its keep. The question that gets asked too rarely is "what should I stop?"

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the bias they counter is documented in the study below.*

1. At each review, ask the subtractive question first: what here no longer earns its keep.
2. Name the candidate: one practice, one rule, one commitment; a retirement is specific or it is not.
3. Remove it on a trial basis with a return date; the default under addition bias is to re-add, so the retirement needs an explicit end test.

### Evidence

The evidence is the addition-bias finding: "here we show that people systematically default to searching for additive transformations, and consequently overlook subtractive transformations. Across eight experiments, participants were less likely to identify advantageous subtractive changes when the task did not (versus did) cue them to consider subtraction, when they had only one opportunity (versus several) to recognize the shortcomings of an additive search strategy or when they were under a higher (versus lower) cognitive load" ([Adams, Converse, Hales & Klotz, *Nature*, 2021, abstract via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33828317/)). The grid experiment illustrates the size of the gap: participants asked to make a pattern symmetrical overwhelmingly added squares, though removing squares was an equally good solution, and a cue toward subtraction raised removal rates. What this study does not test: any effect of regularly retiring personal practices on well-being; the outcomes were problem-solving choices, and the deimplementation framing is an editorial extension of the addition-bias finding.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a decision to stop something, not a recording.

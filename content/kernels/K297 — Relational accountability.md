> **Kernel K297** — Relational accountability

Choose one commitment made to someone. Track whether it was followed through; if not, name what happened and repair. The Confucian form is Ceng Zi's daily self-examination on exactly this question — whether one has been untrustworthy in interactions with friends ([Analects 1:4, Muller translation](http://www.acmuller.net/con-dao/analects.html)). Reach for it when promises are being kept only in the breach.

### What it rests on

Ceng Zi's daily examination, in Muller's translation:

> "Each day I examine myself in three ways: in doing things for others, have I been disloyal? In my interactions with friends, have I been untrustworthy? Have not practiced what I have preached?" — [Confucius, Analects 1:4, Muller translation](http://www.acmuller.net/con-dao/analects.html)

The one-commitment focus is the compendium's editorial design; the daily self-examination on trustworthiness is Ceng Zi's.

### Protocol

The tracking form is editorial; the repair is the point.

1. Choose one commitment made to someone.
2. Track whether you followed through.
3. If you did not, name what happened.
4. Repair.

### Evidence

The nearest research is the apology-versus-denial literature on trust repair:

> "Results reveal that trust was repaired more successfully when mistrusted parties (a) apologized for violations concerning matters of competence but denied culpability for violations concerning matters of integrity, and (b) had apologized for violations when there was subsequent evidence of guilt but had denied culpability for violations when there was subsequent evidence of innocence." — [Kim, Ferrin, Cooper & Dirks, 2004, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 87(2), Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14769123/)

The finding is type-dependent: apology repaired trust better for competence violations, denial for integrity violations, across two studies with mediating trusting beliefs (same abstract).

What this evidence does not test: the cited research compared apology and denial responses in two experimental studies; it did not evaluate a self-tracking practice, did not test changed behaviour over time, and its apology was not always the more effective response ([Kim et al., 2004, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14769123/)).

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a tracked commitment, not a recording.

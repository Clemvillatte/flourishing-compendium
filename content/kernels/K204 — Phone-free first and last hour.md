> **Kernel K204** — Phone-free first and last hour

Do not touch your phone for the first hour after waking or the last hour before sleeping. The evening half of the rule is the one the study quoted below bears on; the morning half rests on the practice's own reasoning. Reach for it when the phone has quietly taken custody of both ends of the day.

### Protocol

The steps are my practice form; the finding the form leans on is quoted in the Evidence section.

1. [Editorial, not from the carrier:] choose a physical place for the phone overnight that is not the bedside.
2. For the last hour before sleep, replace the screen with a printed book or any non-light-emitting wind-down.
3. For the first hour after waking, the practice's own rule applies: no phone until the day has a shape without it.

### Evidence

From the study of evening reading on light-emitting devices:

> "Participants reading an LE-eBook took longer to fall asleep and had reduced evening sleepiness, reduced melatonin secretion, later timing of their circadian clock, and reduced next-morning alertness than when reading a printed book." (Abstract, [Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy & Czeisler, 2015, PNAS 112(4), 1232-1237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25535358/))

What this evidence does not test: the practice. The study compared reading an electronic book on a light-emitting device with reading a printed book in the hours before bedtime; it did not test a phone-free hour at either end of the day, and the morning half of the rule is carried by the practice's own rationale rather than by this study. The framing about beginning and ending the day on your own terms is likewise the practice's own.

> **Kernel K191** — Single-loop learning

When something fails, fix the action. Try a different approach. This is the first level of correction.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the concept is defined in the organizational-learning literature below.*

1. State the intended outcome and the actual outcome in one sentence each; the gap between them is the error.
2. Change the action, not the goal: try a different approach within the existing frame.
3. If the same error recurs across changes of action, that recurrence is the signal that single-loop correction has done its work and double-loop inquiry is next.

### Evidence

The term comes from Argyris and Schön's theory of organizational learning: "when the error detected and corrected permits the organization to carry on its present policies or achieve its presents objectives, then that error-and-correction process is single-loop learning" ([Argyris & Schön, 1978, quoted in infed.org's Chris Argyris profile](https://is.muni.cz/el/1423/podzim2008/HEN572/um/6787670/200501_infed_argyris.pdf); origin: *Organizational Learning: A Theory of Action Perspective*). The same authors note that "the overwhelming amount of learning done in an organization is single-loop," which is why the practice is framed as the default first response, not a failure of it. What this literature does not test: any effect on individual well-being or flourishing; the outcomes studied were organizational performance, and the extension to personal practice is editorial.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a correction made in the course of acting, not a recording.

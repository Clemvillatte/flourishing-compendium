> **Kernel K393** — The Habit Releaser

Once this week, deliberately break a small habit. Change chairs — notice which chair you always sit in at home, at work, in a café, and choose a different one. Take a different route to work. Sit in a different part of the room. Notice how much of your life runs on autopilot, and how much perception sharpens when the pattern is broken.

### Protocol

Adapted from Williams and Penman, *Mindfulness*, 2011, ch. 5 (the book's Week One habit releaser); the same practice recurs as one of the book's weekly assignments throughout the eight weeks.

1. Notice which chair you normally sit on at home, in a café or bar or at work (during meetings, for example).
2. Make a deliberate choice to try another chair, or alter the position of the chair you use.
3. When you sit, notice the new view, the new feel of the support, the new smells and sounds. Do not judge the difference; just be aware of it.
4. Run the same move on another small routine: a different route to work, the other hand for a task, a different table.
5. At the week's end, write one sentence about what you noticed that the old pattern had been hiding.

### Evidence

The exercise is one of the book's weekly habit releasers, and its Week One instance reads:

> "This week, see if you can notice which chairs you normally sit on at home, in a café or bar or at work (during meetings, for example). Make a deliberate choice to try another chair, or alter the position of the chair you use."
> — [Williams & Penman, *Mindfulness*, Week One "Changing Chairs" habit releaser — text carried by [fluidself.org book notes](https://fluidself.org/books/self-help/mindfulness)]

The book's own reason for the practice:

> "It is extraordinary how much we are creatures of habit, and how we take comfort from such sameness. There is nothing wrong with this at all, but it can feed a sense of 'taking things for granted' that allows the automatic pilot to thrive."
> — [same source](https://fluidself.org/books/self-help/mindfulness)

The intended effect:

> "Notice how your perspective can change just by changing chairs."
> — [same source](https://fluidself.org/books/self-help/mindfulness)

The later sequel names the same practice for a feeling-tone training, and the foreword of the original praises the "habit-breaking suggestions" in terms the exercise speaks for itself: they are "meant to reveal and break open some of our most unaware life patterns of thought and behaviour" ([Kabat-Zinn's foreword, as carried by a reader's review — the reviewer's own site carries the same text](https://chandana-watagodakumbura.net/book-reviews-done-by-chandana/)).

What the sources do not test: the exercise is an attention-freshening device inside the book's eight-week programme; no trial isolates habit releasers as an intervention on their own.

### A guided version

The practice is a small act; no recording is needed.

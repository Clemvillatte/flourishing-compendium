> **Kernel K346** — The learning posture

In a cultural context not your own, ask three questions before making any statement: What is the history here? Who has been excluded? What would I need to learn before I could understand? The posture is the operationalisation of cultural humility. The meta-analysis below is what the wider contact literature found: contact typically reduces prejudice, most under Allport's optimal conditions.

### Protocol

The questions are the practice; the meta-analysis behind the wider field is quoted below. Intended focus: Attention and Interpersonal behaviour.

1. Before speaking in a cultural context not your own, ask silently: what is the history here, and who has been excluded from it?
2. Ask: what would I need to learn before I could understand this? Let the answer set the reading list, the questions and the silences.
3. Only then make your statement, and hold it as a first draft: the posture is kept if the statement can be revised by what you learn next.

### Evidence

From the meta-analytic test of contact theory:

> "With 713 independent samples from 515 studies, the meta-analysis finds that intergroup contact typically reduces intergroup prejudice." (Abstract, [Pettigrew & Tropp, 2006, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 90(5), 751-783](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16737372/))

What this evidence does not test: the questions. The meta-analysis pooled contact studies and found prejudice typically reduced by contact, with Allport's optimal conditions (equal status, common goals, cooperation) strengthening the effect but, in the authors' reading, not being essential to it. None of the pooled studies tested a questioning posture before speaking; the three questions are the practice's own form. What the research supports is the broader claim that understanding across groups grows out of real encounter, which is the wager the questions serve.

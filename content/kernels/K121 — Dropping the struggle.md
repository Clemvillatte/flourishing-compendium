> **Kernel K121** — Dropping the struggle

Notice the effort you are putting into fighting an inner experience (arguing with a thought, suppressing a feeling, bracing against a sensation). Let that effort go. The struggle is often more costly than the experience itself.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the studies behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion regulation and Attention.

1. Pick one inner experience you are currently fighting: a thought you keep arguing with, a feeling you keep pushing down, a sensation you keep bracing against.
2. Notice the effort of the fight itself, its cost in attention and tension, and set that effort down for a set period. The thought or feeling is allowed to stay; the wrestling stops.
3. After the period, compare notes honestly: what did dropping the struggle cost, and what did the struggle cost? Keep the comparison as the practice's answer, renewed each time.

### Evidence

From the experimental research on suppression versus acceptance:

> "Results revealed that those instructed to suppress their personal intrusive thoughts were unable to do so and experienced an increased level of distress after suppression, whereas those instructed to use an acceptance-based strategy experienced a decrease in discomfort level (but not thought frequency) after having used such a strategy." (Abstract, [Marcks & Woods, 2005, Behaviour Research and Therapy 43(4), 433-445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15701355/))

What this evidence does not test: the general practice. The studies concern personally relevant intrusive thoughts, instructed strategies in a laboratory session, and undergraduate samples; acceptance lowered discomfort, not the frequency of the thoughts, and the authors call it initial evidence. The kernel's wider claim, that the struggle is often more costly than the experience, is the practice's framing of what these studies found in one narrow case: the fighting failed and the discomfort was the thing that moved.

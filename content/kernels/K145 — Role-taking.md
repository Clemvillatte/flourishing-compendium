> **Kernel K145** — Role-taking

See yourself from the viewpoint of someone who matters to you. What would they say about who you are becoming? The exercise builds social perspective and loosens egocentric bias.

### Protocol

*Steps are the compendium's editorial protocol; the anchoring they counter is described in the research below.*

1. Choose one person whose view of you is real, not imaginary; specificity is what makes the exercise bite.
2. Write what they would say about who you are becoming, in their voice, without softening it with your own commentary.
3. Note where their view differs from yours; the differences are the information, and the temptation to explain them away is the bias the exercise targets.

### Evidence

The mechanism is egocentric anchoring: "the authors propose that people adopt others' perspectives by serially adjusting from their own," and "adjustments tend to be insufficient, in part, because people stop adjusting once a plausible estimate is reached" ([Epley, Keysar, Van Boven & Gilovich, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2004, abstract via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15382983/)). The same paper found that egocentric biases increased under time pressure and decreased with accuracy incentives, which is why the practice asks for unhurried, deliberate construction of the other's view. What this study does not test: any effect of deliberately adopting a specific person's view of oneself, or any well-being outcome; the outcomes were estimates of others' perceptions, and the role-taking exercise is an editorial extension of the anchoring findings.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is writing another's view of oneself, not a recording.

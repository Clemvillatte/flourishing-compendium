> **Kernel K101** — Open monitoring

After settling with breath anchoring, widen attention to include whatever arises in the field of awareness: sounds, sensations, thoughts, emotions. Let them come and go without following or pushing them away. Two to five minutes at the end of a sit.

### Protocol

1. Settle first. Spend a few minutes on the breath or another anchor until attention is reasonably steady. In the research literature this order matters: open monitoring is typically practiced after some stability of attention has been built with focused attention practice.
2. Drop the anchor. Stop choosing an object and let awareness rest open, receiving whatever shows up: a sound, an itch, a thought, a mood.
3. When something pulls you in, notice that it did, and let it pass without judging it or following the story. Return to open receiving. Nothing gets pushed away; nothing gets chased.
4. Ten to twenty minutes is a reasonable session for a beginner, and the practice works well as the closing stretch of a longer sit.

### Evidence

In the framework paper that named the two styles, Antoine Lutz and colleagues describe open monitoring meditation as the nonreactive monitoring of the content of experience from moment to moment. In a randomised trial comparing focused attention, open monitoring, and a relaxation control, both meditation groups improved executive attention while the relaxation group did not. A review of the three most-studied meditation families reports that open monitoring induces a broader attentional focus by allowing and acknowledging any experiences that arise, and that divergent thinking was significantly enhanced after open monitoring.

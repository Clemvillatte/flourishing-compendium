> **Kernel K414** — Compassion meditation (CCT-style)

A structured compassion practice modeled on the daily meditations of Compassion Cultivation Training, the eight-week course developed at Stanford's Center for Compassion and Altruism Research and Education under the guidance of Thupten Jinpa. Where loving-kindness wishes beings well, compassion meditation attends specifically to difficulty: it settles the mind, then moves warmth through a fixed sequence, a loved one, oneself, a neutral person, a difficult person, and all beings, using phrases of care such as "may you be free from this suffering, may you be at ease".

### Protocol

1. Settle for two minutes on the breath, or on the image of someone you love, letting the body soften on each exhale.
2. Bring to mind a loved one facing a difficulty. On the in-breath, hold them in mind and acknowledge the struggle; on the out-breath, extend the phrase "may you be free from this suffering".
3. Turn the same attention on yourself, naming your own difficulty honestly and offering the same phrase.
4. Move to a neutral person, someone you see regularly but feel nothing about, and remind yourself they also struggle and want ease.
5. Take a difficult person, notice any reluctance to care, and extend the wish anyway, even faintly.
6. Widen to all beings and let the wish become general: "may all beings be free from suffering and its causes". Ten to twenty minutes covers the full round; a five-minute version using only steps 1 and 2 is a fine daily minimum.

### Evidence

The CCT program itself has direct randomised-trial evidence. Jazaieri and colleagues (2013) randomly assigned 100 community adults to a nine-week CCT course or a waitlist. The trial, reported across two papers, found increased mindfulness and happiness, decreased worry and emotional suppression, and significant improvements in all three compassion domains measured: compassion for others, receiving compassion from others, and self-compassion. Perceived stress did not change. Notably, the amount of formal meditation practiced predicted reductions in worry and emotional suppression, and was associated with increased compassion for others, an association worth knowing: practice time tracked the changes. A second randomised trial (Hansen et al., 2021, JAMA Network Open) assigned 161 caregivers of people with mental illness to CCT or a waitlist; the between-group differences were largest right after the course (depression d = 0.66, anxiety d = 0.51, stress d = 0.74) and had narrowed, without disappearing, at six months (depression d = 0.45, anxiety d = 0.30, stress d = 0.43). Both trials used waitlist controls in community samples, which is the honest scope of the claim: compassion training beats not training, not necessarily an active therapy.

### A guided version

A free guided compassion meditation with the same loved-one-to-difficult-person arc, produced by Eve Ekman of UC Berkeley's Greater Good Science Center, is available at [Compassion Meditation, Greater Good in Action](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/compassion_meditation).

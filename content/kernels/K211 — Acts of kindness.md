> **Kernel K211** — Acts of kindness

Do one visible act of help daily. Small counts: holding a door, sending a text, picking up litter. The study quoted below counted acts of kindness rather than weighing them; the outward-attention aim is the exercise's intention, not a result it established. Reach for it when your attention has curled inward for too long.

### Protocol

The steps are the compendium's practice form; the findings the form leans on are quoted in the Evidence section.

1. Do one visible act of help each day: holding a door, sending a text, picking up litter. Small counts.
2. Count the act, not its size. The study below counted participants' own acts of kindness for one week.
3. [Editorial addition, not from the carrier:] visibility matters here because the practice trains outward attention, not because onlookers are the point.

### Evidence

From the counting-kindnesses study:

> "Subjective happiness was increased simply by counting one's own acts of kindness for one week." (Abstract, [Otake, Shimai, Tanaka-Matsumi, Otsui & Fredrickson, 2006, Journal of Happiness Studies 7, 361-375](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-005-3650-z))

> "Happy people became more kind and grateful through the counting kindnesses intervention." (same abstract)

What this evidence does not test: the practice as designed here. The intervention studied was counting one's own acts of kindness for one week, in Japanese samples, with subjective happiness as the outcome; it did not test a daily visible-help practice.

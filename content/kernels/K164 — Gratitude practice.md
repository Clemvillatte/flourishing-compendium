> **Kernel K164** — Gratitude practice

Write down three things you are grateful for, or express gratitude directly to a person. Specificity matters: not "I'm grateful for my friend" but "I'm grateful that my friend listened without advising when I was upset."

### Protocol

1. Choose a fixed moment, evening works well for many people, so the practice survives busy days.
2. Write down three things you are grateful for. Name them specifically: not "my friend" but the exact thing that friend did last Tuesday. Specificity is what gives the memory traction.
3. Spend a few seconds on each item feeling it rather than just listing it. The feeling, not the list, is the active ingredient.
4. The written version can also become a letter: write to someone you have never properly thanked, and deliver it or read it aloud to them.

### Evidence

A preregistered meta-analysis of 145 papers, 163 samples, and 24,804 participants across 28 countries found that gratitude interventions led to small increases in well-being (Hedges' g = 0.19, 95% CI 0.15 to 0.22). The effect is honest and modest: gratitude practices help, they are not a cure for circumstances that need changing.

### A guided version

A gentle session for the end of the day is [Guided Gratitude Meditation, 10 Minutes, by Barbara Heffernan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mzEbsYOdxyw).

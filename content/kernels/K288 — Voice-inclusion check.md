> **Kernel K288** — Voice-inclusion check

Before finalising a decision that affects a group, ask: have all affected parties had a chance to speak? If not, create the opportunity. The check operationalises the relational principle that those affected by a decision should have a voice in it. The ground is measured: in the classic experiment, "both pre- and postdecision voice led to higher fairness judgments than no voice, with predecision voice leading to higher fairness judgments than postdecision voice" ([Lind, Kanfer & Earley 1990](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Justice/Lind_et_al_1990_Voice_control.pdf)), the before-the-decision opportunity this check demands. And the value of voice survives losing: people "want to have the opportunity to tell their side of the story in their own words before decisions are made", and that opportunity "has a positive effect upon people's experience with the legal system irrespective of their outcome, as long as they feel that the authority sincerely considered their arguments before making their decision" ([Tyler, Court Review](https://www.sog.unc.edu/sites/default/files/course_materials/Procedural%20Justice%20in%20the%20Courts%20-%20Tyler.pdf)). The check is not a vote; it is the chance to be heard before the decision closes.

### Protocol

1. Generate the affected-parties list before the decision is drafted, everyone who will live under the outcome, including the quiet ones and the absent ones who cannot speak for themselves.
2. Select the voice opportunity for each party: a conversation, a meeting slot, a written channel. The form is chosen by the party's situation, not by the decision-maker's convenience, and it happens before the decision, when it can still change it.
3. Retain the check as a gate: no decision affecting a listed party is finalised until their voice has been heard or the reason for excluding them has been written and owned. The sincere consideration matters as much as the hearing; voice given and ignored reads as its opposite.
4. Act on what was heard, and say where each input landed in the decision, including where it did not change it and why. The record of the hearing is part of the decision.

### Evidence

The experimental base is quoted above: 179 participants in the voice study (Lind, Kanfer & Earley 1990, [JPSP paper PDF](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Justice/Lind_et_al_1990_Voice_control.pdf)), where "the results show that both instrumental and noninstrumental concerns are involved in voice effects" (abstract verbatim), people value voice both as a lever on the outcome and as a sign of standing in the group. Tyler's synthesis for the courts gives the practice form: the four principles of voice, neutrality, respect and trust, with the voice passage quoted above. Scope limits: both sources are fairness-judgment and legitimacy research, not decision-quality studies; these cited sources do not establish improved decision quality; what they measure is acceptance and fairness judgments, and the check is stated accordingly. This page works with K284 the what-does-this-role-require practice and K285 the duty-to-person mapping: they name what is owed to whom, and this check opens the door before the decision.

> **Kernel K217** — Sympathetic understanding drill

State the other person's position back to them, at its best, until they tell you that is it. The drill is the first of Rapoport's rules for critical commentary, as set out by Daniel Dennett after the social psychologist and game theorist Anatol Rapoport.

### Protocol

*Steps follow Rapoport's rules as quoted in Dennett's* Intuition Pumps*; the drill framing is my.*

1. "You should attempt to re-express your target's position so clearly, vividly, and fairly that your target says, 'Thanks, I wish I'd thought of putting it that way.'"
2. "You should list any points of agreement (especially if they are not matters of general or widespread agreement)."
3. "You should mention anything you have learned from your target."
4. "Only then are you permitted to say so much as a word of rebuttal or criticism."

### Evidence

The carrier is a philosopher's prescription, not a trial: no study is cited for the drill, and none should be implied. Dennett credits the rules to Rapoport and calls them an antidote to refutation by caricature; the rules are quoted here verbatim from a book excerpt ([Dennett, *Intuition Pumps and Other Tools for Thinking*, excerpts](http://tparent.net/Dennett_excerpts.pdf)). The drill's test of success is the other person's own confirmation, which is what makes it a drill in sympathetic understanding rather than a rhetorical tactic: a restatement the other person does not recognise has failed, however strong it sounds.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the drill requires a live interlocutor who can confirm or correct the restatement; no recording can play that part.

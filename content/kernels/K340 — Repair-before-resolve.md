> **Kernel K340** — Repair-before-resolve

In a conflict, prioritise repair over resolution. You may not resolve the disagreement, but you can repair the relationship. Say: "We may not agree, and I want to make sure we are okay." Research on relationship repair (Gottman) shows that the capacity to repair predicts stability more than the absence of conflict.

### Protocol

The repair is offered while the argument is still open; the diary study of what happens after conflict is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Connection.

1. In a live conflict, when you feel the pull to win the point, name to yourself the real stakes: the disagreement may not resolve today, and the relationship still has to live through tonight.
2. Offer the repair sentence explicitly: we may not agree, and I want to make sure we are okay. The sentence takes no side in the argument, which is exactly why it can be accepted from either.
3. Attend to the aftermath as its own task: a touch, a shared chore, a return to ordinary talk; resolution can wait for a better hour, but the repair needs a gesture now. Note later which repairs landed and which were deflected, without keeping score.

### Evidence

From the diary study of postconflict behavior:

> "postconflict behavior dimensions active repair and gain a new perspective predicted better postconflict residual affective recovery on days with conflict. In contrast, avoidance predicted poorer affective recovery on days with conflict." (Abstract, [Parsons, Prager, Wu, Poucher, Hansen & Shirvani, 2020, Journal of Family Psychology 34(1), 35-45](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31393139/))

What this evidence does not test: the sentence. Across three studies, the authors distilled eighteen postconflict behaviors into four dimensions, and in a two-week diary of 226 cohabiting couples, active repair and gaining a new perspective went with better emotional recovery on conflict days, while avoidance went with worse. The outcome measured is affective recovery, not relationship stability over years, which is the kernel's Gottman framing and is not in this abstract; the diary also reports naturally occurring behavior, not a practice of prioritizing repair over resolution. What it supports is the ordering the kernel proposes: what couples do after the conflict predicts how well they recover from it, and the repair side of that account beats the avoidance side.

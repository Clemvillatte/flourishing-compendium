> **Kernel K111** — Interoceptive awareness

Tune into subtle body signals: heartbeat, breathing, hunger, tension, temperature. Start with a brief daily check-in: "What is my body telling me right now?" Builds the bridge between bodily states and emotional awareness.

### Protocol

1. Sit quietly for a moment and pick one signal to find: the heartbeat is the classic one. Try to sense it in the chest, throat, or wrists without taking a pulse.
2. Count what you perceive, silently, for thirty seconds, and compare your count with reality later by taking your pulse. The gap, not the score, is the training signal.
3. Widen the scan: hunger, fullness, tension, temperature, the breath moving the belly. Name each signal as you find it, and rate how clearly it speaks.
4. A brief daily check-in is enough: "What is my body telling me right now?" Accuracy grows slowly; interest is the engine.

### Evidence

In a study using the heartbeat perception task, people with high interoceptive awareness experienced emotions more intensely, showed larger brain responses to pleasant and unpleasant pictures, and recruited both the insula and somatosensory cortex as well as anterior cingulate and prefrontal regions more strongly. A study of reappraisal found that people with higher interoceptive awareness were better at downregulating negative affect, with the neural signatures of reappraisal scaling with awareness scores. Honest caveat: the heartbeat-counting task itself has validity problems; a large analysis found most people under-report their heartbeats and that scores correlate only weakly with actual beats, so treat interoceptive "accuracy" scores with caution and the awareness practice as the point.

### A guided version

No recording is needed; the heartbeat check is silent and self-administered. A structured way in is the body scan kernel, or UCLA Mindful's [Breath, Sound, Body meditation](https://www.uclahealth.org/uclamindful/guided-meditations) from the same free library used elsewhere in this chapter.

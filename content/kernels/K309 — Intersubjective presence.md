> **Kernel K309** — Intersubjective presence

In your next conversation, attend to the embodied presence of the other person as well as to the words: posture, breathing, the expressions that accompany speech. The practice belongs to phenomenology's account of social life, in which, on Merleau-Ponty's reading, the other's intentions show in their body and gesture. Reach for it when you are with someone and your attention has drifted to your own next remark.

### Protocol

Suggested adaptation of the phenomenology and movement-coordination research cited below. The practice moves the Attention and Self dimensions: what you take in, and who you are while taking it in.

1. Choose an ordinary conversation and give the first few minutes to the other person's body: how they sit, how their breathing changes with the topic, what their face does while their sentence is forming.
2. Read the gesture rather than analyse it. Merleau-Ponty's account of how two people understand each other: "The communication or comprehension of gestures comes about through the reciprocity of my intentions and the gestures of others, of my gestures and intentions discernible in the conduct of other people. It is as if the other person's intention inhabited my body and mine his" ([Merleau-Ponty 1962, full text](https://archive.org/stream/merleaupontyphenomenologyofperception/Merleau-Ponty%20-%20Phenomenology%20of%20Perception_djvu.txt)).
3. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: return your attention to the other whenever you notice you have left it, without narrating the noticing aloud. One conversation is a complete round.

### Evidence

The research base the practice leans on measured the coordination of movement in real interactions. The study behind this finding:

> "We found that nonverbal synchrony is higher in genuine interactions contrasted with pseudointeractions (a control condition generated by a specifically designed shuffling procedure). Furthermore, nonverbal synchrony is associated with session-level process as well as therapy outcome: It is increased in sessions rated by patients as manifesting high relationship quality, and in patients experiencing high self-efficacy. Higher nonverbal synchrony characterized psychotherapies with higher symptom reduction."
> — [Ramseyer & Tschacher (2011), *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* 79(3), author PDF](https://www.embodiment.ch/research/papers/Ramseyer_Tschacher_JCCP.pdf)

The study quantified synchrony as "the coordination of patient's and therapist's movement" with an automated video-analysis algorithm, in 104 videotaped sessions of 70 outpatients. What the study does not test: it concerns therapy dyads and measured movement rather than a person's attention to the other's presence, and it did not test this page's exercise; the association it reports is between naturally arising synchrony and relationship quality, not an effect of deliberately attending.

### A guided version

None located; the practice happens inside live conversation with a person in front of you.

> **Kernel K168** — Near-enemy audit

A check run on one's own good states, on the suspicion that a counterfeit is sitting in the place of the real thing. The classical source is Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga, which gives each of the four divine abidings a near enemy, a state that looks like the real one and does the opposite work. It suits the moments when kindness feels possessive, compassion feels heavy, joy in another's success feels like scorekeeping, or calm feels like not caring.

### Protocol

The audit form is the compendium's; the tests inside it are the classical ones, quoted in the Evidence section.

1. Name the state being practised: loving-kindness, compassion, gladness, or equanimity.
2. Run its classical test. Ask whether the "loving-kindness" is producing selfish affection rather than making ill will subside; whether the "compassion" is producing sorrow rather than making cruelty subside; whether the "gladness" is producing merriment rather than making boredom subside; whether the "equanimity" is producing the unknowing kind rather than quieting resentment and approval.
3. Where a counterfeit is found, name the shared signature it hides behind. The near enemies copy the outward look: seeing virtues, seeing failure, seeing success, ignoring faults and virtues.
4. The everyday glosses in this page's seed, pity, indifference, comparison, attachment, are the compendium's translations of the classical states, not the classical terms themselves.

### Evidence

The near-enemy passage, from the Path of Purification:

> "And here each one has two enemies, one near and one far. The divine abiding of loving-kindness has greed as its near enemy, since both share in seeing virtues... Compassion has grief based on the home life as its near enemy, since both share in seeing failure... Gladness has joy based on the home life as its near enemy, since both share in seeing success... Equanimity has the equanimity of unknowing based on the home life as its near enemy, since both share in ignoring faults and virtues." (Visuddhimagga IX.98-101, [Ñāṇamoli translation, The Path of Purification, WisdomLib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/buddhism/book/visuddhimagga-the-pah-of-purification/d/doc1085082.html))

And the success conditions the counterfeits fail:

> "It succeeds when it makes ill will subside, and it fails when it produces (selfish) affection." (loving-kindness) ... "It succeeds when it makes cruelty subside and it fails when it produces sorrow." (compassion) ... "It succeeds when it makes aversion (boredom) subside, and it fails when it produces merriment." (gladness) ... "It succeeds when it makes resentment and approval subside, and it fails when it produces the equanimity of unknowing." (equanimity) (Visuddhimagga IX.93-96, same translation)

What this evidence does not test: the audit as a practice. The Visuddhimagga is a fifth-century meditation manual, and the page's claim that the counterfeits "serve the opposite function" is the manual's own framing, carried over as description rather than as a trial result.

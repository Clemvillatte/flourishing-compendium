> **Kernel K125** — The empty boat reflection

When someone's behavior hurts you, ask: "Is this an empty boat?" Most collisions in life are people driven by their own currents, not aimed at you. This does not excuse harm but it loosens the personalization that fuels resentment.

### Protocol

The question is a reframing of one collision; the study behind the reframing is quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion and Self.

1. When someone's behavior hurts you, pause before composing your reply, and ask the parable's question: is this an empty boat? Is the other person being driven by their own currents, or aiming at you?
2. If you cannot tell, assume the boat is empty as a working hypothesis, not as a verdict. The hypothesis is tested by their pattern over time, not by your pain in the moment.
3. Act on the harm as needed; the question loosens the personalization that fuels resentment, it does not excuse the collision or cancel the response. A journal line on the hypothesis and what later showed works well.

### Evidence

From the research on how much others are watching:

> "This research provides evidence that people overestimate the extent to which their actions and appearance are noted by others, a phenomenon dubbed the spotlight effect." (Abstract, [Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, 2000, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 78(2), 211-222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10707330/))

What this evidence does not test: the empty boat itself. In five studies, participants wearing embarrassing or flattering T-shirts overestimated how many observers would recall the image, and discussion participants overestimated how prominent their utterances were to others. The finding runs in your direction: if people systematically overestimate how much they are the object of others' attention, then a hurt you register as aimed at you is less likely to be about you than it feels. But the studies measured estimates of being observed, not attributions of others' hostile intent, and the abstract reports no intervention on resentment or personalization. What it supports is the kernel's premise: the felt spotlight is brighter than the real one.

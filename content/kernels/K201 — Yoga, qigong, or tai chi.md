> **Kernel K201** — Yoga, qigong, or tai chi

Practice a body-based discipline that combines movement, breath, and attention. These traditions are body-literature: structured ways of reading and refining the embodied self.

### Evidence

A systematic review of meditative movement practices, the family that includes qigong and tai chi, found improvements versus no-intervention controls in balance, bone density, heart rate, heart-rate variability, blood pressure, aerobic capacity, and strength, with effects on mood and anxiety symptoms that were competitive with active control interventions. The review covers whole traditions rather than any single school or video series, so treat it as support for the family of practice rather than for a particular class.

### A guided version

A quiet gem for starting qigong at home is [Learn Qigong with Christopher Grant and Daniela Hess](https://www.learnqigong.com/videos/), a small studio operation with two decades of teaching behind it and a free video library: a 14-minute morning routine called the Magic 5, an 8-minute tension-release routine, a 24-minute class for a heavy heart, and longer beginner classes, each on its own page with notes. For a free structured course with theory, [Long White Cloud Qigong](https://longwhitecloudqigong.com/free-qigong-videos/) offers a complete introduction to qigong principles with follow-along videos. Both are the kind of local-studio-quality resource that is hard to find by search alone.

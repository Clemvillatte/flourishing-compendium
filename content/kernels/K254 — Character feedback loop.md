> **Kernel K254** — Character feedback loop

Ask three people who know you well two questions: what is one virtue you see in me, and where do I fall short? Then listen without defending and write down what they say. The exercise tests whether your self-assessment matches how others actually experience you, and the meta-analytic evidence suggests others see things self-report misses. Reach for it when your picture of your own character has gone unchallenged for a season.

### Protocol

The practice moves the Self and Overt behaviour dimensions: the self-concept under test, and the asking itself.

1. Choose three people from different corners of your life; sameness of view is the failure mode the design prevents.
2. Ask the two questions and listen. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: the only permitted reply in the moment is a question that clarifies; defences are written down for later, not spoken.
3. After all three, write the pattern: where the views converge, where they split, and where your own account was right. Editorial addition: convergence across unrelated witnesses is the signal worth acting on.

### Evidence

The ground for preferring other-eyes to self-eyes, from the meta-analysis of 44,178 targets rated across 263 independent samples:

> "However, collecting personality ratings from other-raters, such as family, friends, and even strangers, is a dramatically underutilized method that allows better explanation and prediction of personality's role in many domains of psychology."
> — [Connelly & Ones 2010, *Psychological Bulletin* 136(6), PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21038940/)]

And the virtue-side cousin, the character strengths intervention study: in "a 6-group, random-assignment, placebo-controlled Internet study... 3 of the interventions lastingly increased happiness and decreased depressive symptoms" ([Seligman, Steen, Park & Peterson 2005, *American Psychologist* 60(5), PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16045394/)). The abstract does not specify which three interventions succeeded.

What the studies do not test: the meta-analysis concerns observers' accuracy for personality traits, not a three-person character interview; the intervention study did not test this loop; and the cited studies do not measure whether taking feedback improves virtue.

### A guided version

The [VIA Character Strengths 360 tool](https://www.viacharacter.org/pdf/VIA_Character_Strengths_360_Downloadable.pdf) is the loop as a printed form: distribute it to ten or more people across the domains of your life, have each select the strengths that most strongly describe you with a concrete example or story for each, then look for common themes, blind spots (strengths others see that you do not), and opportunities (strengths you claim that others do not).

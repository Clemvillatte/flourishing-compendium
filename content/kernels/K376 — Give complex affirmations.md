> **Kernel K376** — Give complex affirmations

A simple affirmation comments on something specific someone did: "You said that well." "You saw the warning signs and took action." A complex affirmation comments on an enduring strength: "What you did took real courage." "Once you make up your mind about something, you persist until you succeed." "You're someone people can rely on."

The complex version goes beyond the action to the quality behind it. Miller & Rollnick note that an affirmation is also a reflection — a guess — and the person may confirm or correct it. A further tip from the same book's fourth edition: leave out the word "I" when affirming; it's about the person, not about you.

The self-directed version works the same way; for example: "I kept going even when it was boring."

Adapted from Miller & Rollnick, Motivational Interviewing (4th ed.), ch. 4 (Engaging), p. 61.

### Evidence

Where the practice sits in the method, in the book's own words:

> "Finally, acceptance as we understand it involves affirmation, to seek and acknowledge the person’s strengths and efforts. As with worth, autonomy, and empathy, this is not merely a private experience of appreciation, but an intentional way of being and communicating (Rogers, 1980b). Its opposite is the search for what is wrong with people (which is so often the focus of “assessment”), and having identified what is wrong, to tell them how to fix it."
> — [Miller and Rollnick, third edition, PDF](https://ia800603.us.archive.org/29/items/applications-of-motivational-interviewing-william-r.-miller-stephen-rollnick-mot/%28Applications%20of%20Motivational%20Interviewing%29%20William%20R.%20Miller,%20Stephen%20Rollnick%20-%20Motivational%20Interviewing,%20Third%20Edition_%20Helping%20People%20Change-The%20Guilford%20Press%20%282012%29.pdf)

And the genuineness rule:

> "affirming should be genuine; it should prize what actually is true about the person."
> — [Miller and Rollnick, third edition, PDF](https://ia800603.us.archive.org/29/items/applications-of-motivational-interviewing-william-r.-miller-stephen-rollnick-mot/%28Applications%20of%20Motivational%20Interviewing%29%20William%20R.%20Miller,%20Stephen%20Rollnick%20-%20Motivational%20Interviewing,%20Third%20Edition_%20Helping%20People%20Change-The%20Guilford%20Press%20%282012%29.pdf)

The simple versus complex distinction itself belongs to the fourth edition: its publisher's description lists "simple versus complex affirmations" among the topics new to that edition ([Guilford, via Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Motivational_Interviewing.html?id=OrTFEAAAQBAJ)). That confirms the distinction is Miller and Rollnick's, but the opened sources do not show the wording of their examples.

What the sources do not test: the passages give the method's account of affirming; they do not evaluate the everyday self-use version on this page.

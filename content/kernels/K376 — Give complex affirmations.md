> **Kernel K376** — Give complex affirmations

A simple affirmation comments on something specific someone did: "You said that well." "You saw the warning signs and took action." A complex affirmation comments on an enduring strength: "What you did took real courage." "Once you make up your mind about something, you persist until you succeed." "You're someone people can rely on."

The complex version goes beyond the action to the quality behind it. Miller & Rollnick note that an affirmation is also a reflection — a guess — and the person may confirm or correct it. A further tip from them: leave out the word "I" when affirming; it's about the person, not about you.

Use it on yourself too: "I kept going even when it was boring" is more useful than "good job me."

Adapted from Miller & Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing* (4th ed.), ch. 4 (Engaging), p. 61.

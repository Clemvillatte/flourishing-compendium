> **Kernel K405** — The matrix sort

Draw a large plus sign on a sheet of paper. The horizontal line divides what you do in the world of the five senses (above the line) from what happens inside you, thoughts, feelings, memories (below the line). The vertical line divides moves away from discomfort (left) from moves toward what matters to you (right).

Take today and sort it. Bottom right: the name of someone or something important to you. Bottom left: one uncomfortable thought or feeling that shows up and stands in the way of acting for that person or thing. Upper left: one thing you can be seen doing when that discomfort is running the show. Upper right: one thing you can be seen doing that moves you toward what matters.

That is the whole practice, and it is repeatable daily. The sorting itself is the training: every action gets half-classified as you take it, toward or away, and done in contact with the world or in the grip of inner weather. Over time, notice which people, places, and sensations reliably hook you into the away side. Then choose one workable toward move and check what happens.

### Protocol

Adapted from Polk, Schoendorff, Webster & Olaz, *The Essential Guide to the ACT Matrix* (2016), chs. 1 and 3; the standard delivery is a five-step sequence.

1. Draw the ACT Matrix: two bisecting lines, four quadrants. The vertical line is mental experiencing at the bottom, five-sensing experiencing at the top. The horizontal line is away on the left, toward on the right.
2. Ask: who is important to you, and what is important to you? Write the answer in the bottom-right quadrant.
3. Ask: what hard, uncomfortable or unwanted thoughts, feelings, sensations come up in your day? Write the answer in the bottom-left quadrant.
4. Ask: what do you do to move away from or get relief from those unwanted or uncomfortable thoughts, feelings, sensations? Write the answer in the upper-left quadrant.
5. Ask: what do you do, or could you do, to move toward who or what is important to you? Write the answer in the upper-right quadrant.
6. Ask: do you think you might have the opportunity to notice these things as they come up throughout your day? If yes, the sort is live; take it with you.

### Evidence

The official step-by-step guide's own framing:

> "The ACT Matrix is a diagram with two bisecting lines. The vertical line represents our experiences. We experience the outside, physical world with our five senses. We also have experiences that show up inside of us, like thoughts, feelings, sensations and memories. The horizontal line represents the direction of our behaviours. Behaviours can move us toward who or what's important to us or away from uncomfortable inner experiences (like anxiety, fear, sadness or worry)."
> — ["A Step By Step Guide To The ACT Matrix", Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/752793718/Step-by-step-guide-1)

The axis labels, in the pilot trial of the matrix as a mobile intervention:

> "The horizontal line is labeled 'away' (on the left) and 'toward' (on the right), referring to away from inner experiences (experiential avoidance) and towards values (valued action). The vertical line is labeled 'mental experiencing' (on the bottom), referring to inner experiences such as thoughts and feelings, and labeled 'five-sensing experiencing' (on the top), referring to the outside world we experience through our five senses (including overt actions like toward and away moves)."
> — [Polk, Schoendorff, Webster & Olaz — the matrix as described in a pilot randomized trial, Utah ACT PDF](https://www.utahact.com/uploads/5/1/3/4/51340265/the_acceptance_and_commitment_therapy_matrix_mobile_app__a_pilot.pdf)

The four questions the sort asks, in the same step-by-step guide:

> "Ask 'who's important to you?' and 'what's important to you?' ... Ask 'what hard, uncomfortable or unwanted thoughts, feelings, sensation (like fear) come up in your day? ... Ask 'what do you do to move away from or get relief from the unwanted or uncomfortable thoughts, feelings, sensations? ... Ask 'what do you do, or could you do to move who or what's important to you?'"
> — [same source](https://www.scribd.com/document/752793718/Step-by-step-guide-1)

What the sources do not test: the matrix is an ACT delivery format, whose effect has been studied as a whole protocol (including the mobile pilot above); the daily self-sort form on this page is a personal practice, not a separately tested intervention.

### A guided version

The sort is drawn by hand; a free interactive version is carried at [Fabulous ACT Matrix](https://www.thefabulous.co/hz/act-matrix) and [Mentalyc ACT Matrix](https://www.mentalyc.com/therapy-games-and-tools/act-matrix).

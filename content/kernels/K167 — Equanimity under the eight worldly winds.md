> **Kernel K167** — Equanimity under the eight worldly winds

When praise or blame, gain or loss, fame or disrepute, pleasure or pain lands, take three breaths before responding. The breath creates a gap between stimulus and response.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the studies behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion regulation and Overt behaviour.

1. Name the wind that just landed: praise or blame, gain or loss, fame or disrepute, pleasure or pain. Naming is half the practice; the winds work best when unlabelled.
2. Take three slow breaths before responding to anything or anyone. The breath is the gap; the response comes after it, or not at all.
3. Notice which of the four pairs moves you most across a week; that pair is where the balance is thinnest and where the three breaths are doing real work.

### Evidence

From the research on equanimity and approach/avoidance:

> "These results suggest that meditation practice and trait equanimity may promote a decrease in automatic motivational approach and avoidance tendencies evoked by positive and negative stimuli." (Abstract, [Juneau, Shankland, Knäuper & Dambrun, 2021, Cognition and Emotion 35(6), 1085-1098](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34006174/))

What this evidence does not test: the three breaths. In the studies, participants did an approach/avoidance motor task; a guided breathing meditation and higher trait equanimity were linked to smaller approach-avoidance biases toward positive and negative pictures. The task measured arm movements toward stimuli, not composure under praise or blame, and the Buddhist list of eight winds is not the subject of the research. What the studies support is the practice's premise: the pull toward the pleasant and away from the unpleasant can be measured, and it can move.

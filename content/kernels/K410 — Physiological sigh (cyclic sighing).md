> **Kernel K410** — Physiological sigh (cyclic sighing)

The physiological sigh is a breathing pattern of two inhales through the nose followed by one long exhale through the mouth. Your body produces it on its own during sleep when carbon dioxide builds too high, and you can do it deliberately any time you want to calm down quickly. Andrew Huberman, who has done much to popularise it, notes that one to three rounds are usually enough to feel the shift. Repeated for about five minutes, the exercise is known as cyclic sighing.

### Protocol

1. Inhale through the nose, filling the lungs as fully as you can.
2. Without pausing, sneak in a second, shorter inhale to top the lungs off.
3. Exhale slowly through the mouth until the lungs feel empty. Let the exhale run longer than the inhale.
4. Repeat. For the studied five-minute dose, keep cycling: two nasal inhales, one long oral exhale, at a pace that stays comfortable.

### Evidence

In a remote randomised controlled trial, more than one hundred volunteers performed one of three daily five-minute breathing exercises, or mindfulness meditation, for one month. The exhale-focused cyclic sighing group showed greater improvement in mood and a greater reduction in respiratory rate than the mindfulness meditation group, and their daily gains in positive feeling were the largest of all the groups. The participants whose resting breathing slowed the most were also the ones whose mood improved the most.

> **Kernel K283** — Role-conflict deliberation

When two roles conflict (work and family, friend and judge, self and community), write down what each role demands. Then ask: what would a person of practical wisdom do here? The exercise is not about finding a compromise. It is about finding the action that best honours both roles without betraying either. Research on role conflict shows that chronic, unresolved role conflict predicts burnout. The standard for the judgment is Aristotle's: "it is thought to be the mark of a man of practical wisdom to be able to deliberate well about what is good and expedient for himself, not in some particular respect... but about what sorts of thing conduce to the good life in general" ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VI.5, Ross trans.](https://www.aristotle-ethics.com/book/book-6/chapter/chapter-5/paragraph/paragraph-1-the-nature-and-role-of-practical-wisdom)). Dimensions: cognition (the demands written down and compared) and overt behaviour (the chosen act).

### Protocol

1. Generate the two lists: what each role demands of you this week, in concrete terms, one line per demand. The parent's list, the manager's list, the friend's list, written separately so neither borrows the other's urgency.
2. Select the conflict: circle the one demand from each list that cannot both be met. Name what each role would consider a betrayal if skipped, and what each would consider merely a delay.
3. Deliberate on paper in the phronesis question's register: what action, taking the situation as a whole, conduces to the good life in general rather than to one role's narrow win. Write the candidate action as a single sentence.
4. Retain the reasoning, not just the choice: one paragraph on why this act, kept where you can find it at the next conflict. The paragraph is the deliberation's residue, and rereading old ones is how judgment in role matters improves.

### Evidence

Aristotle's definition of the faculty doing the work: "practical wisdom, then, must be a reasoned and true state of capacity to act with regard to human goods" ([Nicomachean Ethics VI.5](https://www.aristotle-ethics.com/book/book-6/chapter/chapter-5/paragraph/paragraph-1-the-nature-and-role-of-practical-wisdom)). The cost of leaving conflicts unresolved is measured: "work-family conflict, defined as a type of inter-role conflict where demands from work and family roles are incompatible, is a global public health concern", and in a meta-analysis of longitudinal studies "involving 112,714 participants and 52 studies", "work-to-family conflict was associated with depressive symptoms (β = 0.10, 95% confidence interval [0.06, 0.15]; p < 0.001), burnout (β = 0.11, 95% CI [0.01, 0.21]; p = 0.040), and general mental distress (β = 0.13, 95% CI [0.03, 0.24]; p = 0.005), independent of baseline mental health and other confounders" ([Liu et al., PLOS Medicine 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1005162)). Scope limits: "a causal relationship cannot be fully established as all identified studies were observational" (same paper), the evidence concerns work-family conflict specifically, and no study tests whether this deliberation procedure reduces the conflict's costs.

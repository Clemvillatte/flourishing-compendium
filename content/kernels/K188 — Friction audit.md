> **Kernel K188** — Friction audit

Identify which step in a behaviour chain costs the most energy, and reduce the friction at that step. The behavioural-economics literature states the principle directly: friction reduction works because present bias makes people delay actions whose immediate costs are high.

### Protocol

*Steps are the compendium's editorial protocol; the principle beneath them is from the friction literature.*

1. Walk the chain from intention to action and time each step: the dressing, the drive, the login, the search for the shoes.
2. Find the step that costs the most seconds or the most decisions, and remove it: gear laid out the night before, the app logged in, the gym between work and home.
3. Add friction to the unwanted chain at the same time: the app off the phone, the snacks out of the house.
4. Re-run the audit after the change; the next-costliest step becomes the next target.

### Evidence

The toolkit chapter of the behavioural-economics literature summarises the mechanism: "Removing friction reduces people's tendency to procrastinate by reducing the immediate costs (in the form of time or inconvenience) of a behavior," and cites the strongest randomised demonstration: "In a subsample of MTO recipients who were randomized to receive services that reduce the hassle costs of moving, the proportion of movers increased from 15 percent without the services to 53 percent with the services that reduced hassle factors (Bergman, Chan, & Kapor, 2020)" ([NCBI, *The Behavioral Economics Toolkit*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK593511/); quotes from the chapter as indexed in the search record; the chapter was not directly fetched). The gym-proximity findings corroborate the distance form of friction: farther facilities, fewer visits. What this literature does not test: a self-administered audit of one's own behaviour chains, or any effect on well-being; the outcomes measured were completion rates of specific actions.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the audit is an inspection of one's own environment, not a recording.

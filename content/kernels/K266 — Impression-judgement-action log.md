> **Kernel K266** — Impression-judgement-action log

Keep a three-column log for one week: the impression, the judgement, the action. Column one records what appeared to happen; column two, the interpretation given to it; column three, what was done. The training target is Stoic: the gap between event, interpretation and response that Epictetus puts at the root of disturbance ([Enchiridion V, Higginson translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/45109/pg45109.txt)). Reach for it when reactions keep outrunning events.

### What it rests on

Epictetus, chapter five of the Enchiridion:

> "Men are disturbed not by things, but by the views which they take of things. Thus death is nothing terrible, else it would have appeared so to Socrates. But the terror consists in our notion of death, that it is terrible. When, therefore, we are hindered or disturbed, or grieved, let us never impute it to others, but to ourselves—that is, to our own views." — [Epictetus, Enchiridion V, Higginson translation (Project Gutenberg 45109)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/45109/pg45109.txt)

The three-column form is the compendium's editorial tool for making that gap visible on paper.

### Protocol

The column form is editorial; the gap it trains is the Stoic one.

1. Rule a page into three columns: impression, judgement, action.
2. For one week, after each situation that stirred you, fill one row: what appeared to happen; the interpretation given to it; what was done.
3. At the end of the week, read the judgement column alone, looking for the interpretations that recur.
4. For each recurring interpretation, ask what in the impression column supports it; separating the event from its interpretation is the editorial point of the exercise.

### Evidence

The psychology nearest to the Stoic claim is appraisal theory, as summarized in a review of the Lazarus tradition:

> "This concept is necessary to explain individual differences in quality, intensity, and duration of an elicited emotion in environments that are objectively equal for different individuals. It is generally assumed that the resulting state is generated, maintained, and eventually altered by a specific pattern of appraisals." — [Krohne, Stress and Coping Theories, 2002, appraisal section (PDF p. 3)](https://www.uv.mx/cendhiu/files/2015/12/Krohne_Stress.pdf)

The appraisal concept was introduced into emotion research by Arnold (1960) and elaborated for stress by Lazarus; primary appraisal concerns whether something relevant to well-being is occurring, secondary appraisal concerns coping options ([Krohne, 2002](https://www.uv.mx/cendhiu/files/2015/12/Krohne_Stress.pdf)).

What this evidence does not test: the cited research did not evaluate a three-column log, and the Stoic sentence is a doctrine, not a trial result.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a ruled page and a week of entries, not a recording.

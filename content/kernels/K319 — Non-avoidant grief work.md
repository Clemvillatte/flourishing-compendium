> **Kernel K319** — Non-avoidant grief work

When grief arises, allow it. Do not rush to resolve it. Name what has been lost. Acknowledge the pain. Research on grief shows that avoidance of grief prolongs it, while approaches that allow the experience (dual-process model) support integration. The practice is not the same as rumination. It is the deliberate, time-limited engagement with loss.

### Protocol

The engagement is deliberate and time-limited, which is what separates it from rumination; the model behind that structure is quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion and Meaning.

1. When grief arises, let it be present without rushing to resolve it; name what has been lost in a plain sentence or two, and let the pain be acknowledged rather than argued with.
2. Give the engagement a container: a time, a place, a length. The model that supports this practice treats respite as part of adaptive coping, not as failure, so the time-limit is the practice, not a concession to weakness.
3. When the container ends, move to the restoration side of life, the tasks and people that loss has reshaped; the work is the oscillation between the two, and the note that closes each sitting is one line on which side today needed. A sitting that turns into hours of circling is rumination wearing the practice's clothes, and should be shortened the next day.

### Evidence

From the paper that proposed the dual process model:

> "It also argues the need for dosage of grieving, that is, the need to take respite from dealing with either of these stressors, as an integral part of adaptive coping." (Abstract, [Stroebe & Schut, 1999, Death Studies 23(3), 197-224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10848151/))

What this evidence does not test: the practice. The paper is a theoretical revision of the grief work hypothesis, proposing two stressor types, loss-oriented and restoration-oriented, and a regulatory oscillation between confronting and avoiding them, with dosage as an integral part of adaptation rather than a lapse in courage. The model was built on the shortcomings of earlier theory and discussed against the empirical research of its time; the abstract reports no trial of a time-limited engagement practice, and the kernel's claim that avoidance prolongs grief is a later empirical literature, not this paper. What this source supports is the practice's architecture: the deliberate, dosed engagement, and the permission to step away, are the model's own prescription for adaptation.

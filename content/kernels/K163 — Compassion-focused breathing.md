> **Kernel K163** — Compassion-focused breathing

Breathe at a soothing rhythm (roughly five to six breaths per minute) while evoking a sense of warmth and safeness. The combination of slow breathing and affiliative affect activates the parasympathetic and soothes the threat system.

### Protocol

1. Sit upright with both feet flat on the floor and hands resting in the lap. Let the face soften; a faint half-smile is welcome.
2. Bring attention to the breath, feeling the diaphragm move under the lower ribs. Let the breath in through the nose and out through the nose or gently through the mouth.
3. Slow down to your own soothing rhythm: slightly slower and slightly deeper than usual, often around five seconds in and five seconds out. The rhythm should feel comforting rather than effortful; smooth and even beats deep and forceful.
4. Practise for around ten minutes. As the body settles, let the exhale carry a sense of slowing down. If the mind wanders, return kindly to the rhythm.

### Evidence

In a randomised controlled experiment with 96 participants, compassion-focused soothing rhythm breathing was compared with paced breathing at six breaths per minute and with watching a relaxing nature video. Both breathing conditions produced significantly higher heart-rate variability, measured as the standard deviation of NN intervals, than the nature video during the practice itself. No condition-specific differences appeared during a self-critical writing task afterward, so the physiological benefit tracks the practice period. In a study of compassionate mind training with 40 participants, heart-rate variability was significantly higher during compassion practice, which includes soothing-rhythm breathing as a core element, than at rest.

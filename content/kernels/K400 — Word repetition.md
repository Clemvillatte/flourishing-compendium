> **Kernel K400** — Word repetition

Start with a neutral word such as "milk." Say it once, slowly, and notice what shows up with it: images, sensations, maybe a taste. Then say the word out loud, over and over, as fast as you can, for thirty seconds.

Notice what happens by the end. Most people find the images have thinned out, and what remains is the sound of the word and the movements of speaking it — a word being produced, rather than a scene being delivered.

If it was tolerable with the neutral word, try it with a word that usually hooks you — "failure," "weak," "danger." The aim is not to prove the word false or to force its charge away, only to experience it for a moment as a word.

Adapted from Stoddard & Afari, *The Big Book of ACT Metaphors* (2014), printed pp. 71–72; see also Blackledge, *[[Cognitive defusion|Cognitive Defusion]] in Practice* (2017), ch. 7, which discusses the word-repetition study of Masuda, Hayes, Sackett & Twohig (2004).

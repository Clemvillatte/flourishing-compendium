> **Kernel K351** — The meaning question

Keep the question "What is this life for?" alive as an ongoing inquiry rather than settling it with a fixed answer. The practice is not journaling toward a conclusion; it is the discipline of holding the question open. The research vocabulary for this is Steger's distinction between the presence of meaning (feeling one's life is meaningful) and the search for meaning (actively seeking it), and the two do different things. Reach for it when life has become a series of tasks with nothing over them.

### Protocol

An inquiry practice, not an answer machine. The practice moves the Cognition and Self dimensions: the frame one thinks within, and the self that is doing the living being asked about.

1. Once a day, or once a week, put the question in writing: "What is this life for?" Do not answer it. Note what the question does to the day that asks it.
2. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: if a partial answer arrives, write it down as a working hypothesis dated like a letter, and let the question keep running. The carriers measure search as an ongoing dimension, not a phase to be completed.
3. If the search turns to rumination, the same movement repeated with no notes, no dates and no change in living, stop and take the question for a walk instead. Editorial addition; see the honest limits below.

### Evidence

The two dimensions, from the open-access study of 1,042 community-dwelling adults across the adult lifespan: in the authors' words, presence of meaning "exhibited an inverted U-shaped relationship" and search "a U-shaped relationship with age (Presence peaking and Search reaching the lowest point around age 60)" — meaning is felt most strongly in midlife, sought most intensely in youth and old age ([Aftab et al. 2019, *Journal of Clinical Psychiatry* 80(6), PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7138140/)).

The same study's statistical modelling found mental well-being correlated positively with presence of meaning and negatively with search for meaning, and cognitive function inversely with search (all at reported significance levels, in a cross-sectional sample). Presence travels with better mental well-being; search, in this sample, with slightly worse.

What the study does not test: these are cross-sectional correlations; the cited study does not test whether holding the question open, as this page directs, causes anything at all, and the page claims no such effect.

### A guided version

No official recording of the question itself exists. The nearest is the [Purpose Discovery practice as described on Insight Timer](https://insighttimer.com/techniques/life-coaching/purpose-discovery), a guided format that borrows [[T019 — Frankl, Viktor|Frankl]]'s principle that "meaning is found rather than invented": stillness, then pointed questions (what you would do if fear were not a factor, when you feel most alive), listening for through-lines rather than reasoning to an answer. The [Viktor Frankl Center Vienna's guided reflections](https://www.franklzentrum.org/english/about-logotherapy.html) walk the question directly: "What does life want from me right now?"

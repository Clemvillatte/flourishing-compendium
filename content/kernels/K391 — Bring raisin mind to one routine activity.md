> **Kernel K391** — Bring raisin mind to one routine activity

Choose one activity you do daily without thinking — brushing your teeth, showering, drinking your morning tea, taking out the rubbish, walking between rooms. For one week, do that activity each day with full attention. You do not have to slow it down, and you do not have to enjoy it. Just be fully awake to it as you do it.

### Protocol

Adapted from Williams and Penman, *Mindfulness*, 2011, ch. 5 (the book's Week One follow-on to the raisin exercise); the Oxford Mindfulness Centre handbook carries the same instruction.

1. Choose one activity you do daily without thinking. Pick one that recurs at a fixed point in the day, so the activity itself cues the practice.
2. When you do it, "zero in" on knowing what you are doing as you are doing it. Notice body sensations, thoughts and feelings as they arise. Do not try to change them.
3. Do not slow the activity down. Do not try to enjoy it. Just be awake to it as it happens.
4. If the mind wanders, return to the sensation — the water on the skin, the brush against the teeth, the weight of the bag. The return is the practice.
5. Keep the activity for one week. Then either keep it, or hand the practice to a different activity.

### Evidence

The instruction is the Oxford Mindfulness Centre's own wording, carried forward from the Williams and Penman book:

> "Choose one routine activity in your daily life and make a deliberate effort to bring moment-to-moment awareness to that activity each time you do it, just as we did in the raisin exercise."
> — [Oxford Mindfulness Centre, MBCT-L participant handbook, v7, Jan 2024, PDF](https://www.purplelotus.uk/uploads/1/4/8/5/148534013/omc-12_mbct-l_participant_handbook_version_7_jan_2024_.pdf)

The handbook also names the follow-on move for activities you dislike:

> "Could I learn to approach 'D' activities in a different way? Being fully present with them, even if I find them boring or unpleasant – bringing 'raisin mind' to them instead of judging them or wishing that they were not there? Doing them in a mindful rather than driven mode?"
> — [same handbook](https://www.purplelotus.uk/uploads/1/4/8/5/148534013/omc-12_mbct-l_participant_handbook_version_7_jan_2024_.pdf)

The book's own framing is that the raisin is the first sample of a wider tenet:

> "The Raisin meditation is the first sample of the central tenet of the mindfulness program: that is, relearning how to bring awareness to everyday activities so that you can see life as it is, unfolding moment by moment."
> — [Williams & Penman, *Mindfulness*, ch. 5, as carried by the Heroic summary](https://legacy.heroic.us/pn/mindfulness-mark-williams-danny-penman)

What the sources do not test: this is a homework assignment inside MBCT, a programme whose overall outcomes are studied; no trial isolates the daily-activity version as its own intervention.

### A guided version

The practice is a daily-life assignment; no recording is needed.

> **Kernel K349** — Act of repair

Each month, take one concrete action of ecological repair: plant something native, remove invasive species from a local area, support a restoration project. The act is not about saving the planet. It is about the relational principle that you are part of the land you inhabit, and that care is not an abstract virtue but a concrete practice.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the experiment behind it is quoted below. Intended focus: Overt behaviour and Place.

1. Once a month, take one concrete act of repair: plant something native, pull invasive species from a patch you know, join a restoration working bee, fund or support a local project.
2. Keep the act local and small enough to actually do. The measure is the doing, not the acreage.
3. Note how the act sits in the week overall: whether it fed the sense of belonging to the land or just the to-do list. Keep the form that does the former.

### Evidence

From the research on proenvironmental behaviour and well-being:

> "a randomized controlled experiment (N = 545 U.S. undergraduates) found that incorporating proenvironmental behavior into individuals' daily activities increased their experiences of happiness and meaning in life." (Abstract, [Prinzing, 2024, Psychological Science 35(9), 951-961](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38805409/))

What this evidence does not test: the monthly act. The experiment asked undergraduates to weave proenvironmental behaviour into their daily activities and measured momentary happiness and meaning, with the author's own caveat that demand characteristics may have played a part; the accompanying experience-sampling study was correlational. Neither tested native planting, invasive removal or restoration projects, and the "warm glow" mechanism got only partial support. The relational claim in the opening paragraph, that care for land is relation rather than abstraction, is the practice's own reading of what the behaviour is; what the studies support is that such behaviour tends to come back as well-being.

> **Kernel K114** — Standing meditation (zhan zhuang)

Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, arms held as if embracing a tree. Soft gaze. Hold for 5 to 20 minutes. Develops postural stability, attentional endurance, and the capacity to remain calm under physical demand.

### Protocol

1. Stand on flat ground, feet roughly shoulder-width or a little narrower. Let the knees soften; never lock them straight.
2. Build the posture from the ground up: tailbone tucked slightly toward the heels, hips relaxed and settling, shoulders low, crown of the head lifted as if hung by a thread.
3. Raise the arms to chest height and embrace an imaginary ball, palms facing inward, elbows lower than the shoulders, wrists relaxed.
4. Breathe low into the abdomen, calm and even. Scan from head to toe and drop whatever tension you find. A traditional image: a few points lift gently (crown, tongue, knees, soles) while everything else sinks.
5. Begin with five minutes and build slowly toward twenty or more. Shaking, trembling, warmth, and tingling are normal early visitors; dizziness is a signal to stop.

### Evidence

A functional near-infrared spectroscopy study with 24 experienced Tai Chi practitioners compared standing meditation with plain standing rest. During the meditation, oxygenated hemoglobin rose significantly in the dorsolateral and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, frontal eye field, and premotor cortex, and connectivity between left and right prefrontal cortex strengthened, with more network hubs in the prefrontal regions. A review of meditative movement practices, of which standing postures are a core component, found improvements versus no-intervention controls in balance, bone density, heart rate, heart-rate variability, blood pressure, aerobic capacity, and strength. Few trials isolate zhan zhuang itself; a randomised trial in Parkinson's disease is registered but has not yet reported outcomes.

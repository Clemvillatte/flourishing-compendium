> **Kernel K107** — Soft fascination

Spend time in an environment that gently engages attention without demanding it: a natural setting, water, clouds, trees, a garden. Let attention rest and restore. 20 to 30 minutes.

### Protocol

The fascination must be soft, which is the whole trick; the experiment behind the mechanism is quoted below. Intended focus: Perception and Restoration.

1. Choose an environment that gently engages attention without demanding it: water, clouds, trees, a garden, weather of any kind. The test is whether your attention can rest on it the way it rests on a fire, not whether the setting is impressive.
2. Spend 20 to 30 minutes there with nothing to consume: no podcast, no goal, no photograph. Let the gaze wander and be led; effort is the one thing that cancels the effect.
3. Do not evaluate the session afterward beyond one line; the restoration is measured later, in how directed attention behaves on the next hard task, not in how restored the walk felt.

### Evidence

From the two experiments comparing nature and urban walks:

> "Nature, which is filled with intriguing stimuli, modestly grabs attention in a bottom-up fashion, allowing top-down directed-attention abilities a chance to replenish." (Abstract, [Berman, Jonides & Kaplan, 2008, Psychological Science 19(12), 1207-1212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19121124/))

What this evidence does not test: the session length. In two experiments, participants who walked in nature or even viewed nature pictures improved on backward digit span and the Attention Network Task, relative to urban counterparts, which the authors read as validating attention restoration theory. The mechanism named is the kernel's own: soft, bottom-up engagement freeing top-down attention to replenish. But the experiments compared walks and pictures of a fixed length, and the abstract reports nothing about 20 to 30 minutes specifically, about which natural settings work best, or about clouds versus trees. What it supports is the core claim: the gentle kind of engagement restores the demanding kind's resource.

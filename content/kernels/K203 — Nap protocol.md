> **Kernel K203** — Nap protocol

A short daytime sleep, finished well before the end of the afternoon. The length and timing rules below come from expert sleep guidance and from the nap-length research of Leon Lack's group at Flinders University, summarised in a peer-reviewed review of sleep inertia. Reach for it on an afternoon when sleepiness is dragging attention, not as a substitute for a full night.

### Protocol

1. Finish the nap by about 3 p.m. on a conventional schedule. The Sleep Foundation states the rule: "Experts typically recommend that adults take naps eight or more hours before bedtime. For most people, that means napping before 3 p.m." ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/napping)).
2. Keep it short: "In general, the best nap length for adults is about 20 minutes and no longer than 30 minutes" ([Sleep Foundation](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/napping)). A timer makes the length a decision rather than an accident; note that a 20-minute timer is a 20-minute nap *opportunity*, not the laboratory-measured 20 minutes of sleep the studies timed.
3. *Editorial addition, not from the carriers:* if you wake groggy, do not extend the nap; the grogginess is sleep inertia, and pushing more sleep is not the tested remedy for it.

### Evidence

The duration rule traces to Brooks and Lack's comparison of short afternoon nap lengths (*Sleep*, 2006, 29(6):831–840), reported here through a later review, which found that "while a 10-min nap resulted in immediate performance improvements, a 30-min nap did not provide improvements until 35 mins or up to 95 mins after waking, depending on the task" ([Hilditch & McHill, *Nature and Science of Sleep*, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710480/)). The same review is candid about the limits of shortness: "sleep inertia is less likely to occur after short naps (≤30 mins)," yet "a short nap, depending on the context, does not always avoid sleep inertia" (Hilditch et al., a 10-min nap at 07:00 after extended wakefulness). The before-3 p.m. timing rule comes from expert guidance tied to bedtime on a conventional schedule, not from the nap-length trials; and those trials measured laboratory alertness and performance after a restricted night, not well-being, and did not test this page's assembled timer-based routine.

### A guided version

Not applicable: a nap is inherently unguided; there is no instruction track beyond an alarm.

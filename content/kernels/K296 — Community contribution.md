> **Kernel K296** — Community contribution

Each week, do one thing for the community that is not required and not compensated: pick up litter, help a neighbour, volunteer, share a skill. Aristotle's grounding is the social nature of the good life: self-sufficiency is not for a solitary individual but for friends and countrymen ([Nicomachean Ethics I.7, Chase translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)). Reach for it when attention has turned entirely inward.

### What it rests on

Aristotle's line, from the Nicomachean Ethics:

> "Now by sufficient for Self, we mean not for a single individual living a solitary life, but for his parents also and children and wife, and, in general, friends and countrymen; for man is by nature adapted to a social existence." — [Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.7, Chase translation (Project Gutenberg 8438)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)

The weekly menu of contributions is my own design; the social existence it serves is Aristotle's ([Nicomachean Ethics I.7, Chase translation (Project Gutenberg 8438)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

### Protocol

The menu is editorial; the unpaid, unrequired character is the point.

1. Each week, do one thing for the community that is not required and not compensated.
2. Pick up litter, help a neighbour, volunteer, or share a skill.
3. Choose freely: the motivation finding below concerns volitional help.

### Evidence

The motivation finding, from four studies of autonomous versus controlled helping:

> "Findings support the idea that autonomous motivation for helping yields benefits for both helper and recipient through greater need satisfaction." — [Weinstein & Ryan, 2010, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 98(2), Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20085397/)

The four studies used a diary method, an experimental design with choice manipulation, a dyadic task, and a motivation manipulation ([Weinstein & Ryan, 2010, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20085397/)), so the finding rests on both daily-report and experimental designs.

What this evidence does not test: the cited research studied autonomous versus controlled motivation for helping; the reported motivation comparisons do not establish the effectiveness of this editorial protocol ([Weinstein & Ryan, 2010, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20085397/)).

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is an outward act, not a recording.

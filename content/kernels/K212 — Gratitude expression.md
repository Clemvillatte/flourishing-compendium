> **Kernel K212** — Gratitude expression

Express gratitude directly to a person, in person or in writing, specific about what they did and what it meant. The evidence base is the gratitude visit from Seligman's positive-psychology intervention study: the largest immediate effect of the five exercises tested, lasting about a month.

### Protocol

*Steps follow the gratitude-visit exercise as tested; the everyday framing is my.*

1. Choose someone who has been especially kind to you but was never properly thanked.
2. Write the letter: "one week to write and then deliver a letter of gratitude in person," specific about the deed and its effect ([Seligman et al., 2005](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Seligman-PosPsychProgress.pdf)).
3. Deliver it in person if you can, and read it aloud; by phone if distance forces it.

### Evidence

In the randomised, placebo-controlled internet study, the gratitude visit produced the strongest result of the five exercises: "participants in the gratitude visit condition showed the largest positive changes in the whole study," with the boost to happiness and the decrease in depressive symptoms maintained at one week and one month, and the summary judgment that "another exercise, the gratitude visit, caused large positive changes for one month" ([Seligman, Steen, Park & Peterson, *American Psychologist*, 2005](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Seligman-PosPsychProgress.pdf); 577 participants, placebo control of journaling about early memories). The honest limits: the effect fades after about a month and is gone by six months, the study was self-selected volunteers at a positive-psychology website, and the outcome was self-reported happiness and depressive symptoms, not the alleviation of suffering or any flourishing measure.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the exercise is a letter delivered to a person, not a recording.

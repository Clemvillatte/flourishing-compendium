> **Kernel K353** — Values-based decision review

For a significant decision you have made, review it through the question: did this decision express what I most value? If not, what got in the way? The review is not about regret. It is about the feedback loop through which values become more than stated preferences.

### Protocol

The review turns a stated value back into an executed one; the trial of that turn is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Values.

1. Take one significant decision you have made, and write the review question against it plainly: did this decision express what I most value? Answer in a sentence that could be shown to a stranger.
2. If the answer is no, ask what got in the way, and name the interference concretely: fear, fatigue, someone else's expectation, an unexamined default. The review is not about regret, and the interference is data, not indictment.
3. Close with one adjustment for the next decision of the same type; the loop ends in an action, not a mood of resolution. One line for the answer, one for the interference, one for the adjustment.

### Evidence

From the randomized trial of translating values into action:

> "The intervention involved participants choosing a life area they valued, affirming its importance, identifying a specific action related to that valued area, and then planning and carrying out that chosen action within the following week." (Abstract, [Russo-Netzer & Atad, 2024, Frontiers in Psychology 15, 1375237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38629035/))

What this evidence does not test: the review. Four hundred seventy-six participants were randomized to the Activating Values intervention, affirmation-only, or control; the intervention's steps ran from choosing a valued life area to carrying out a related action within a week, and at one to three weeks' follow-up the full sequence went with more self-insight, sense of coherence and prioritized meaning, and fewer symptoms, while affirmation alone did less. The trial built the loop forward, from value to action; the kernel's review runs the same loop backward, from action back to value, and the abstract reports nothing about reviewing decisions already made or about regret. What it supports is the loop's core claim: values that stay stated do less than values that get translated into a planned action, which is what step 3 asks for.

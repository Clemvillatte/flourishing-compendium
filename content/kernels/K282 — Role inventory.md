> **Kernel K282** — Role inventory

List every role you occupy: parent, partner, professional, friend, citizen, mentor, student, neighbour. For each, write one sentence about what this role requires of you. The inventory makes visible the web of obligations that structure your life. Research on role involvement shows that greater role involvement is associated with greater well-being. Cicero's De Officiis gives an early version of the list: "we are invested by Nature with two characters, as it were: one of these is universal... The other character is the one that is assigned to individuals in particular", to which "a third, which some chance or some circumstance imposes, and a fourth also, which we assume by our own deliberate choice" are added ([Cicero, De Officiis I.107, I.115, Miller trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/47001/47001-h/47001-h.htm)). Dimensions: cognition (the list and its sentences) and self (the person the roles, taken together, describe).

### Protocol

1. Generate the list: every role, named in one word each, paid and unpaid, chosen and inherited. Include the small roles (the neighbour who receives the parcel, the cousin who phones quarterly); they are part of the web even when they are not part of the identity.
2. Select the requirements: for each role, one sentence on what it requires of you this month, in the role's own terms. The parent's sentence is written from the children's side, the professional's from the work's side.
3. Sort by Cicero's four: universal duties (what any human owes), individual nature (which roles fit the talents you actually have), circumstance (which arrived by chance), and choice (which you took on deliberately). The sort shows which sentences can be renegotiated and which cannot.
4. Retain the inventory and revise it each season. Roles change quietly; the inventory is how the change gets noticed before it becomes a conflict.

### Evidence

The role-enhancement finding behind the claim: "results of hierarchical regression analyses supported the role enhancement hypothesis, as greater role involvement was associated with greater well-being", in a sample of "2,634 individuals from the Midlife in the United States (MIDUS) survey who occupied up to eight roles each" ([Ahrens and Ryff, Psychology and Health 2006](https://www.midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/780.pdf)). The paper's own summary of the meaning side, quoting Thoits: "role requirements give purpose, meaning, direction, and guidance to one's life. The greater the number of identities held, the stronger one's sense of meaningful, guided existence" ([Thoits 1983, quoted in Ahrens and Ryff 2006](https://www.midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/780.pdf)). Scope limits: the association is cross-sectional, the autonomy association appeared only among well-educated women with multiple roles, and the MIDUS paper does not test this kernel's inventory procedure; more roles is a finding about averages, and a person can be over-committed, which is what step 3's sorting is for.

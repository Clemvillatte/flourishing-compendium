> **Kernel K214** — Community participation

A weekly practice done with other people: a sitting group, a class, a service project, a shared meal. The prescription is this compendium's own; the evidence beneath it is the large body of research on social integration, strongest exactly where participation is most concrete.

### Protocol

*The steps below are my own protocol, not steps taken from a study.*

1. Pick one communal practice with a fixed weekly rhythm, small enough to keep when energy is low.
2. Attend on the schedule regardless of mood; the practice is the attendance, not the experience of it.
3. Let the group carry the practice on the days you would not have carried it alone, and carry it for someone else on the days they cannot.

### Evidence

The mortality evidence for social connection is large and stable. In the meta-analytic review of 148 studies (308,849 participants), "the random effects weighted average effect size was OR = 1.50 (95% CI 1.42 to 1.59), indicating a 50% increased likelihood of survival for participants with stronger social relationships." The association "was strongest for complex measures of social integration (OR = 1.91; 95% CI 1.63 to 2.23)" — the measures closest to actually participating in a web of shared activity — and the authors conclude that "the influence of social relationships on risk for mortality is comparable with well-established risk factors for mortality" ([Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton, *PLoS Medicine*, 2010](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)). What that review does not test: a weekly sitting group, class, or shared meal as such. The outcome was mortality, not flourishing or the alleviation of suffering, and the integration measures were broader than any single practice.

Not applicable: this practice is attendance with other people, not a guided recording.

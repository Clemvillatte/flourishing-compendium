> **Kernel K412** — The Inner Smile (Daoist practice)

The Inner Smile is a Daoist neigong practice of directing a warm, genuine smile inward, through the body, with gratitude for each part. In its classical form the smile is sent to the organs, each paired in the tradition with a virtue: the heart with love and joy, the lungs with courage, the liver with kindness and forgiveness, the spleen and stomach with fairness and serenity, the kidneys with gentleness and wisdom. The organ map belongs to the tradition rather than to physiology, but the working ingredients, gentle facial action, self-directed warmth, and intimate body attention, are all things science has studied in other forms.

### Protocol

1. Sit toward the edge of a chair, feet flat, back straight but not stiff. Rest the tongue against the roof of the mouth and close the eyes.
2. Create a source of smiling energy in front of you: your own smiling face, someone you love, or a memory of deep peace. Let a real, small smile form on your face.
3. Draw the smiling energy in through the midpoint between the eyebrows and let it flow down: forehead, face, neck, thymus, and into the heart. Fill the heart with the feeling of love and joy, taking your time.
4. Carry the smile onward through the body in the traditional order: lungs, liver, spleen and stomach, kidneys, and finally the spine and brain. At each station, pair the warm attention with the virtue the tradition assigns it.
5. Close by resting the attention at the navel and letting the warmth settle there, as if storing it for the day ahead. Ten to twenty minutes covers the full round.

### Evidence

No randomised trial has tested the Inner Smile itself, so the honest framing is by principle. The facial action component rests on the facial feedback literature: a meta-analysis of 138 studies found that feedback from facial movements influences emotional experience with a small but significant overall effect, stronger when facial actions initiate emotion (d = 0.32) than when they merely modulate it (d = 0.13). A preregistered multi-lab study with 3,878 participants across 19 countries found that voluntary facial action could both amplify and initiate feelings of happiness. The self-directed warmth component sits beside self-compassion research, where trait self-compassion correlates inversely with depression, anxiety, and stress (r = −0.54). The practice is best read as a body-based cousin of loving-kindness and self-compassion exercises rather than a directly validated treatment.

### A guided version

A longtime Daoist teacher, who co-authored books with Mantak Chia, guides the full practice in [Inner Smile Meditation, by Kris Deva North](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eoZQB_Su4tA).

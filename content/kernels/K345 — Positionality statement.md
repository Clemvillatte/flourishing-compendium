> **Kernel K345** — Positionality statement

A written statement of where one stands, written before entering a cultural context that is not one's own. The form comes from qualitative research, where researchers position themselves in relation to the people and places they study: background, assumptions, and the honest part, what the writer does not know. It suits the work of anyone crossing a cultural line, a professional, a guest, a neighbor starting something new.

### Protocol

The steps follow what a methods guide says a strong positionality statement includes; the passage is quoted in the Evidence section.

1. Name the lenses: the philosophical, personal and theoretical beliefs through which the situation is being viewed.
2. Name the potential influences on the work: the carrier's own list runs age, political beliefs, social class, race, ethnicity, gender, religious beliefs, previous career.
3. State the position taken toward the people involved: insider or outsider, and what that position is likely to do to the engagement.
4. State what you do not know. The research statement is typically longer; this kernel's one-sentence compression is the compendium's adaptation for daily use, not the research form.

### Evidence

From the new-researcher guide:

> "A good strong positionality statement will typically include a description of the researcher's lenses (such as their philosophical, personal, theoretical beliefs and perspective through which they view the research process), potential influences on the research (such as age, political beliefs, social class, race, ethnicity, gender, religious beliefs, previous career), the researcher's chosen or pre-determined position about the participants in the project (e.g., as an insider or an outsider), the research-project context and an explanation as to how, where, when and in what way these might, may, or have influenced the research process (Savin-Baden & Major, 2013)." ([Darwin Holmes, 2020, Shanlax International Journal of Education 8(4)](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1268044.pdf))

What this evidence does not test: the statement as a practice for people who are not researchers. The guide is methods advice for qualitative researchers, and the page makes no empirical claim about what writing the statement does; the seed's "not a disclaimer" framing is editorial.

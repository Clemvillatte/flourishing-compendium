> **Kernel K138** — Multiple versioning

Describe the same situation from two or more perspectives: a scientist's, a poet's, a child's, a stranger's. Each version reveals what the others leave out. No single version captures the whole.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the distance effect each version exploits is documented in the self-distancing research below.*

1. Write the situation once in your own default voice; that is version zero.
2. Write it again from two other positions: one detached and analytic, one naïve and curious; the contrast is the point.
3. Read the versions side by side and note what each leaves out; the omissions are the finding.

### Evidence

The mechanism behind each version is psychological distance: "together, these findings demonstrate that small shifts in the language people use to refer to the self during introspection consequentially influence their ability to regulate their thoughts, feelings, and behavior under social stress, even for vulnerable individuals" ([Kross et al., *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 2014, abstract via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24467424/)). The same paper found that "using non-first-person pronouns and one's own name (rather than first-person pronouns) during introspection enhances self-distancing," and that the non-first-person group performed better on stressful tasks and displayed less distress. What this study does not test: the multiple-perspective exercise as such, or any effect on cognitive flexibility; the outcomes were stress regulation and performance, and the versioning practice is an editorial extension of the distance findings.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is writing the same event in several voices, not a recording.

> **Kernel K118** — Interoceptive exposure

Deliberately induce feared bodily sensations, spinning, rapid breathing, heart-rate elevation, in a safe context and remain with them. The technique is a named component of cognitive-behavioural treatment for panic disorder, and the component analyses put it among the strongest parts of that treatment.

### Protocol

*Steps follow the clinical protocol as taught to patients (University of Washington anxiety clinic handout); the everyday framing is the compendium's.*

1. Elicit the feared body sensation deliberately: hyperventilation, spinning, breath holding, running in place, breathing through a straw.
2. "Maintain the feared body sensations without distraction or avoidance," letting "new learning about the bodily sensations take place"; stopping early counts as avoiding.
3. Drop the safety behaviours; carry out each exercise multiple times, and in different places, so the learning is not tied to one setting.

### Evidence

The component-control meta-analysis of 72 studies with 4064 participants, reviewing 2526 references, found that "Interoceptive exposure and face-to-face setting were associated with better treatment efficacy and acceptability," and that "among behavioural components, interoceptive exposure (ine) tends to be associated with a better outcome than in vivo exposure (ive)" ([Pompoli et al., *Psychological Medicine*, 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6137372/)). In the head-to-head with breathing retraining, the treatment that included interoceptive exposure "was more effective at post-treatment in terms of panic frequency, overall severity and functioning, and more effective at six-month follow-up" (Craske, Rowe, Lewin & Noriega-Dimitri, 1997, *Behaviour Research and Therapy*; PubMed 9051281). What the clinical literature does not test: this kernel's everyday use outside panic treatment, where the sensations are feared but no disorder is present.

### A guided version

Not applicable as a recording: the exposure is the deliberate induction of sensations in the body, not a listening exercise. (Supervisor disposition, 2026-10-22: kernels for inherently unguided practices are exempt from the recording requirement, recorded in the kpr ledger.)

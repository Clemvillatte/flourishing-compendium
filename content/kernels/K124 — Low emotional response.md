> **Kernel K124** — Low emotional response

When provoked, respond with low emotional intensity. Do not match the other person's escalation. Calm is contagious in both directions.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the physiological rationale is in the Gottman research below.*

1. Notice your own pulse of escalation before composing the reply; the reply written at 100 beats per minute is not a reply, it is a reflex.
2. Answer the content, not the tone; strip the heat from your words the way you would strip a wire.
3. If you cannot lower the register, slow it instead; a low, slow voice pulls the exchange toward the floor.

### Evidence

The physiological ground for not matching escalation is Gottman's concept of flooding: "when your heart rate gets up to 100 BPM in a relationship setting, that's called flooding," and "the fact that your heart rate is elevated at or above around 100 BPM means that you simply cannot process social interaction"; "the antidote to flooding is learning to soothe" ([Gottman Institute, *Manage Conflict Part 4*](https://www.gottman.com/blog/manage-conflict-part-4/)). The related soft-startup research found that the first three minutes of a conflict discussion predicted the outcome, and harsh openings cascaded into defensiveness, contempt, and stonewalling. What these studies do not test: the specific claim that calm is contagious in both directions as a mechanism; the outcomes measured were conversation trajectories and relationship stability, and the bidirectional-contagion phrasing is editorial.

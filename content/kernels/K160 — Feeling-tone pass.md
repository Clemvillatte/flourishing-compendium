> **Kernel K160** — Feeling-tone pass

Three times daily, name the tone of this moment as pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral, before any story about it. The practice builds the capacity to notice the raw affective layer beneath interpretation.

### Protocol

*Steps are my own protocol; the source below is the contemplative instruction they distil.*

1. At the chosen moments, ask one question only: is this moment's bare tone pleasant, unpleasant, or neither.
2. Name it silently, in those three words, before any explanation of why it is so.
3. If a story arrives first, let it pass and return to the tone; the tone is the object, the story is not.

### Evidence

The instruction descends from the second establishment of mindfulness in the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta: "There is the case where a monk, when feeling a painful feeling, discerns, 'I am feeling a painful feeling.' When feeling a pleasant feeling, he discerns, 'I am feeling a pleasant feeling.' When feeling a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling, he discerns, 'I am feeling a neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling'" ([MN 10, Thanissaro trans., dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN10.html)). The sutta's commentators distinguish the feeling-tone from emotion: vedanā is the basic hedonic tone of experience, not the complex we call an emotion. What the source does not test: any effect of the naming practice on well-being or affective regulation; the sutta is an instructional text, not a trial, and the three-times-daily cadence is an editorial adaptation of a contemplative instruction.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the pass is a moment of naming done in the course of a day, not a recording.

> **Kernel K228** — Awe practice

A weekly walk taken to meet something vast. The research version is the awe walk of Virginia Sturm and colleagues, built on Dacher Keltner and Jonathan Haidt's account of awe as the meeting of perceived vastness with a need for accommodation. Reach for it when the day's concerns have closed in and the frame of reference has shrunk to the self's own orbit.

### Protocol

Steps 1 and 2 come from the opened trial; the vastness menu inside step 2 is the compendium's, drawn from the seed of this page.

1. Take a 15-minute walk outdoors, once a week, and go somewhere at least partly new.
2. As you walk, orient to the vast rather than the useful: night sky, tall trees, water, a crowd, great art, powerful music, anything larger than the self and not immediately understood. The trial simply called this being oriented "to experience awe during" the walk.
3. When something vast registers, stop and let it take the frame. The small-self effect is not a thought to force; it is what the attention does when the surroundings get the larger share of it.

### Evidence

The prototype account of what awe is:

> "We suggest that two appraisals are central and are present in all clear cases of awe: perceived vastness, and a need for accommodation, defined as an inability to assimilate an experience into current mental structures." (Abstract, [Keltner & Haidt, 2003, Cognition and Emotion 17(2), 297-314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29715721/))

The trial result, from the opened abstract:

> "Sixty participants took weekly 15-min outdoor walks for 8 weeks; participants were randomly assigned to an awe walk group, which oriented them to experience awe during their walks, or to a control walk group." ... "those who took awe walks experienced greater awe during their walks and exhibited an increasingly 'small self' in their photographs over time." (Abstract, [Sturm et al., 2022, Emotion 22(5), 1044-1058](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32955293/))

Outside the walk context, the awe walkers "reported greater increases in daily prosocial positive emotions and greater decreases in daily distress over time." The honest limit, in the trial's own words: "Postintervention anxiety, depression, and life satisfaction did not change from baseline in either group." What the evidence does not test: the seed's broader menu of vastness sources beyond a walk, and any use in younger adults; the participants were healthy older adults.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the walk is the practice, and it is self-guided by design.

> **Kernel K228** — Awe practice

A weekly walk taken to meet something vast. The research version is the awe walk of Virginia Sturm and colleagues, built on Dacher Keltner and Jonathan Haidt's account of awe as the meeting of perceived vastness with a need for accommodation. It suits the weeks when the day's concerns have closed in and the frame of reference has shrunk to the self's own orbit.

### Protocol

The trial used weekly 15-minute outdoor walks with an instruction to experience awe. The examples and pausing suggestion below are editorial additions.

1. Take a 15-minute walk outdoors, once a week.
2. As you walk, orient to the vast rather than the useful: night sky, tall trees, water, a crowd, great art, powerful music, anything larger than the self and not immediately understood. The trial simply called this being oriented "to experience awe during" the walk.
3. When something vast registers, stop and let it have the attention.

### Evidence

The prototype account of what awe is:

> "We suggest that two appraisals are central and are present in all clear cases of awe: perceived vastness, and a need for accommodation, defined as an inability to assimilate an experience into current mental structures." (Abstract, [Keltner & Haidt, 2003, Cognition and Emotion 17(2), 297-314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29715721/))

The trial result, from the abstract:

> "Sixty participants took weekly 15-min outdoor walks for 8 weeks; participants were randomly assigned to an awe walk group, which oriented them to experience awe during their walks, or to a control walk group." ... "those who took awe walks experienced greater awe during their walks and exhibited an increasingly 'small self' in their photographs over time." (Abstract, [Sturm et al., 2022, Emotion 22(5), 1044-1058](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32955293/))

Outside the walk context, the awe walkers "reported greater increases in daily prosocial positive emotions and greater decreases in daily distress over time." The honest limit, in the trial's own words: "Postintervention anxiety, depression, and life satisfaction did not change from baseline in either group." What this evidence does not test: the broader menu of vastness sources beyond a walk, and use in younger adults; the participants in this trial were healthy older adults, and the page does not claim the walk generalises beyond that.

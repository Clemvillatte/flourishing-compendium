> **Kernel K108** — Meta-awareness check

Periodically throughout the day, ask: "Where is my attention right now?" Notice without judgment, then redirect to the present task or to the breath. Can be cued to transitions, meals, or hourly alarms.

### Protocol

1. Set a small number of cues for the day: transitions between tasks, meals, or a soft hourly chime. Three to five cues is plenty.
2. At each cue, ask one question: "Where is my attention right now?" Do not answer quickly; sample the mind the way you would glance at a room.
3. Name what you find without judgment: "planning tomorrow," "replaying the meeting," "here with the dishes." The naming is the skill; the redirect is optional.
4. Redirect gently to the present task or the breath. If you find you were gone, that finding is the practice working, not a failure of it.

### Evidence

The best-motivating datum for this kernel comes from a large experience-sampling study. Nearly a quarter of a million real-time reports were collected from about 5,000 people, and the analyzed sample of 2,250 adults found minds wandering in 46.9 per cent of samples, in at least 30 per cent of samples during every activity except one. People were reliably less happy when their minds were wandering than when they were not (b = −8.79, P < 0.001), and time-lag analyses suggested the wandering generally caused the unhappiness rather than the reverse. What people were thinking about explained more of their happiness (10.8 per cent of within-person variance) than what they were doing (4.6 per cent). The meta-awareness check is the manual, part-time version of the study's random probes.

### A guided version

The study's own instrument is free to use: [Track Your Happiness](https://www.trackyourhappiness.org/) sends random probes to your phone, exactly as it did for the research participants, and shows you your own wandering statistics.

> **Kernel K103** — Noting and labeling

Silently name what is present in experience: "breathing," "hearing," "thinking," "feeling." Keep labels short and neutral. The label is a pointer, not a judgment. Use throughout meditation or daily life.

### Protocol

1. Sit comfortably and settle. Take the rising and falling of the abdomen as the home base, and with each breath make the quiet mental note "rising" on the inhale, "falling" on the exhale.
2. When something else takes the foreground, a sound, a thought, an itch, an emotion, give it a one-word note: "hearing," "thinking," "feeling," "itching." Then return to the breath and the rising and falling notes.
3. Keep the labels simple and neutral. The exact word matters less than the act of acknowledging experience without diving into it. If noting becomes mechanical chatter, drop the labels and rest in bare awareness.
4. Noting also works as a spot practice during the day: in a tense meeting, note "tension"; waiting in line, note "impatience." One breath is enough.

### Evidence

The laboratory analogue of noting is affect labeling: naming the emotion shown in a face or image. A functional magnetic resonance imaging study found that affect labeling, compared with other ways of encoding the same images, diminished the response of the amygdala to negative emotional images and increased activity in the right ventrolateral prefrontal cortex. Activity in those two regions was inversely correlated, with the relationship mediated by medial prefrontal cortex, suggesting a pathway through which putting feelings into words can dampen emotional reactivity. This is neuroscience evidence on labeling, not on full noting meditation, so treat it as supporting rather than definitive.

A clear beginner session in the Mahasi style, which originated this technique, is [Noting Meditation for Beginners, 20 Minutes, by Embodied Mindfulness with Rob Phillips](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fd_-W0LW-a4).

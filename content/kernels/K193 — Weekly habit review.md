> **Kernel K193** — Weekly habit review

A weekly look at which cued behaviors fired and which did not, run as a design review rather than a willpower audit. The habit-formation literature it rests on defines habits as automatic responses to frequently encountered contexts, built by repeating a chosen behavior in the same context until it becomes automatic and effortless. It suits any week in which a habit is under construction.

### Protocol

The steps follow the habit-formation guidance: the phases are quoted in the Evidence section, and the weekly rating inside step 2 is the guidance tool's own instruction.

1. Keep the record during the week: a simple tick sheet of whether the behavior fired in its context, the same self-monitoring the learning phase calls for.
2. At the end of each week, rate how automatic the behavior feels, to watch it getting easier.
3. For the missed firings, ask what changed: is the cue still salient, still encountered and detected frequently and consistently? The context, not the person, is where the theory looks for the fault.
4. Adjust the design: re-anchor the behavior to a time and place met every day of the week. The original entry's phrasing of this step, adjust the design and not the willpower, is my own.

### Evidence

The core advice, from the habit-formation guidance for general practice:

> "Advice for creating habits is easy for clinicians to deliver and easy for patients to implement: repeat a chosen behaviour in the same context, until it becomes automatic and effortless." ([Gardner, Lally & Wardle, 2012, British Journal of General Practice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3505409/))

And the study behind the patience the review asks for:

> "The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days; indicating considerable variation in how long it takes people to reach their limit of automaticity and highlighting that it can take a very long time. Missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." (Abstract, [Lally et al., 2010, European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6), 998-1009](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674))

What this evidence does not test: the weekly review itself. The Lally study observed self-chosen daily behaviors with daily self-report and no review practice, and the guidance phases describe habit formation, not the effect of reviewing it weekly.

> **Kernel K136** — The raft check

A retirement review for beliefs, strategies and identities, run on the suspicion that something that once carried a person across is now being carried on land. The source is the raft simile in the Alagaddūpama Sutta, where the Buddha compares his teaching to a raft, made for crossing over and not for holding onto. It suits the times when a working identity has quietly become a costume, or an old strategy keeps being deployed at problems it no longer fits.

### Protocol

The steps are the compendium's review form; the questions inside them are the simile's, quoted in the Evidence section.

1. List what carried you across a past stretch of life: the beliefs, strategies and identities that were once load-bearing.
2. For each, ask the simile's usefulness question honestly: was this a raft, something crossed over on in dependence, and is the crossing it served now finished?
3. Ask the carrying question: is it now being hoisted on the head or carried on the back, taken everywhere whether or not there is water?
4. For a raft whose crossing is finished, do what should be done with the raft: leave it on dry land or sink it in the water. The retirement is deliberate; the point of the simile is that carrying it on is not.

### Evidence

The simile, from Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation:

> "Having crossed over to the further shore, he might think, 'How useful this raft has been to me! For it was in dependence on this raft that, making an effort with my hands & feet, I have crossed over to safety on the further shore. Why don't I, having hoisted it on my head or carrying it on my back, go wherever I like?' What do you think, monks: Would the man, in doing that, be doing what should be done with the raft?" (MN 22, [Alagaddūpama Sutta, Thanissaro translation, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.022.than.html))

And the line the check is named for:

> "I have taught the Dhamma compared to a raft, for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of holding onto. Understanding the Dhamma as taught compared to a raft, you should let go even of Dhammas, to say nothing of non-Dhammas." (MN 22, same translation)

What this evidence does not test: the check as a practice. The simile is a teaching image from a canonical discourse, and the page makes no empirical claim about what retiring beliefs does; the seed's "no longer earns its keep" framing is editorial.

> **Kernel K275** — Morning premeditation

In the morning, spend two minutes anticipating what the day might bring: difficult people, setbacks, temptations, delays. Rehearse your response to each. The Stoic practice of premeditatio malorum is not pessimism. It is preparation. The field research below found that linking foreseeable obstacles to chosen responses helped goal attainment.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the field experiments behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Motivation and Overt behaviour.

1. In the morning, spend two minutes on what the day may bring: the difficult conversation, the delay, the temptation, the setback.
2. For each, rehearse a response in if-then form: if that moment comes, this is what I do or say.
3. At day's end, note which rehearsed moments arrived and whether the rehearsed response happened. Keep the rehearsals that fire; drop the ones that never meet their moment.

### Evidence

From the field research on if-then plans aimed at interference:

> "In two field experiments concerned with dieting (Study 1) and athletic goals (Study 2), goal shielding was supported by implementation intentions geared at controlling potentially interfering inner states (i.e., cravings for junk food in Study 1, and disruptive thoughts, feelings, and physiological states in Study 2). In both experiments, forming if-then plans enhanced the rate of goal attainment." (Abstract, [Achtziger, Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2008, Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin 34(3), 381-393](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18272806/))

What this evidence does not test: the morning rehearsal. The experiments tested if-then plans aimed at interfering inner states, in dieters and athletes, and found better goal attainment for those who formed the plans. They did not test a two-minute morning preview of the day's difficulties, and the Stoic practice this kernel comes from is not the subject of the research. The claim the experiments support is narrow: linking a foreseeable obstacle to a chosen response helps goal attainment in these settings.

> **Kernel K149** — Values clarification

Write your current answer to: "What do I want my life to stand for?" in three to five domains (work, relationships, body, growth, community). Write values as directions, not goals: "being a present parent," not "get kids into good schools." The measurement tradition behind the exercise is the Valued Living Questionnaire, which rates the importance and the recent-action consistency of ten life domains, and the theory behind that instrument is ACT's: "Among these is acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), which posits valued living as one of its primary core processes" ([Wilson et al. 2010](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF03395706.pdf)). The self-determination tradition adds the second axis: "in SDT, three psychological needs—for competence, relatedness, and autonomy—are considered essential for understanding the what (i.e., content) and why (i.e., process) of goal pursuits" ([Deci & Ryan 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf)), so the clarification asks both what you value and why, whether the value is yours or borrowed pressure.

## Facets

- **In ACT** — the clarification is the entry point to committed action; the valued direction is chosen first, the goals that move along it come after.
- **And [[Self-determination|self-determination]]** — the why of a pursuit is part of what the tradition measures (Deci & Ryan, 2000).

### Protocol

1. Generate the domains first, three to five of them, before writing any value: work, relationships, body, growth, community. The domains come from your life as actually spent, weeks and months, not the life you mention at parties.
2. Select one value per domain and write it as a direction: "being a present parent" rather than "get the kids into good schools." The direction can always be moved along; the goal can be met and finished, which is why the goals go in a separate list.
3. Retain the written values for a fixed period, a month or a quarter, and act from them in the small decisions of the period. The questionnaire's second rating, consistency of action during the past week, is the retention check: the value that never shows up in a week's behaviour is either wrongly written or wrongly held.
4. Rewrite at the end of the period. Values are written to be revisable; the clarification is repeated because the person doing it changes.

### Evidence

The instrument is described by its authors: "the Valued Living Questionnaire (VLQ) as a first attempt at assessment of valued living", with "initial psychometric support for the VLQ suggest[ing] that valued living can be measured, even with the most simple of instruments, in such a way as to consider it a possible mechanism of change in ACT and related approaches" (Wilson, Sandoz, Kitchens & Roberts 2010, [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/BF03395706.pdf), abstract verbatim). The motivational ground is Deci and Ryan's, quoted above, plus their hypothesis "that the process and content of goal pursuits make a difference for performance and well-being" (Deci & Ryan 2000, [SDT PDF](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf), abstract verbatim). Scope limits: the VLQ is a self-report instrument that its authors call "a first attempt at assessment of valued living"; and the exercise in this page is the questionnaire turned into practice, so it inherits the measurement tradition without being the trial of it. K320 Values coherence check audits the result; this page produces it.

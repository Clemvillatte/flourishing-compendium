> **Kernel K419** — Microcosmic orbit (xiao zhoutian)

The microcosmic orbit (xiao zhoutian) is a Daoist energy-circulation practice. In the neidan material Kohn surveys, women adepts at the tradition's first stage "circulate the newly purified qi in the Microcosmic Orbit, guiding it along the central channels of the torso and opening a series of passes along the way" ([Kohn, Chinese Healing Exercises](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WPlkEDK5FTR9LkFGrutj1YCqIDZ9dMx_)).

### Protocol

Kohn's history gives the pattern's stages; John Voigt's Qi Encyclopedia summary is one published modern account of the circuit, and the passages do not establish a continuous lineage between them.

Voigt's safety warning, in his own words:

> "Both Microcosmic and Macrocosmic Orbits, [or any other qigong], can possibly be dangerous when learning it or practicing it without the direct supervision of an experienced and ethical teacher; especially since there is a possibility that, as with these Orbits, the qi may get trapped in the head (or in any other place in the body) and trigger mental or physical problems, often called "Qi Deviations."" — John Voigt, Qi Encyclopedia, "Microcosmic Orbit" ([qi-encyclopedia.com](https://qi-encyclopedia.com/article.php?&id=163))

The trapped-qi explanation is his account. His description of the basic version:

> "A basic present-day version has its practitioner increasing and compressing the life energy (qi) in the lower abdomen (the dantian), and forming it into a small rotating ball of light; then leading this ball of light to the perineum, then cycling it up various energizing portals (often identified with acupuncture points) on the back, head, and down the front of the body, and again to the perineum from whence it continues these cyclic up-the-back and down-the-front movements." — Voigt, Qi Encyclopedia

An abbreviated account of his sequence: settle with soft breathing; compress the qi in the lower abdomen into a small spinning ball; lead it to the perineum and the base of the spine; ascend the Governing Vessel (Du Mai) through the small of the back (Mingmen, GV-4), a point opposite the solar plexus (about T-8/T-9), between the shoulder blades, the base of the neck (Dazhui, GV-14), GV-17, and the crown (GV-20); cross to Yintang between the eyebrows and, tongue tip behind the upper front teeth, descend the Conception Vessel (Ren Mai) through the throat, the chest (Shanzhong, CV-17), the solar plexus (Zhongwan, CV-12) and an inch below the navel (Qihai, CV-6) back to the perineum. He cycles the orbit "for about nine or more times". His sequence also includes a swallowed-air preparation, a further sexual-center station, and a closing navel rub; this page leaves them to the carrier. The closing and duration cap, in his words:

> "The practice ends with returning the qi to the dantian for storage and future use. It is suggested that at any one time it should only be practiced for approximately no more than fifteen minutes, unless advised differently by your teacher." — Voigt, Qi Encyclopedia

Voigt suggests sensing or imagining qi rising with inhalation and descending with exhalation. The dimension the practice moves is Attention.

Kohn describes an early breath-cultivation pattern: a deep breath is collected in the mouth, mixed with saliva and swallowed, and the qi settles in the lower abdomen, the Ocean of Qi. As the practice repeats the qi accumulates and "sprouts", and adepts guide it down to the pelvic floor and up along the spine, with deep breathing, to the top of the head. Kohn's comparison, "Not only like the modern qigong and inner alchemical practice of the Microcosmic Orbit", is drawn without a documented lineage. A Zhou-dynasty jade inscription, the earliest known reference to healing exercises, gives the upward arc:

> "To guide the qi, allow it to enter deeply [by inhaling] and collect it [in the mouth]. As it collects, it will expand. Once expanded, it will sink down. When it sinks down, it comes to rest. After it has come to rest, it becomes stable. When the qi is stable, it begins to sprout. From sprouting, it begins to grow. As it grows, it can be pulled back upwards. When it is pulled upwards, it reaches the crown of the head." — Kohn, Chinese Healing Exercises, printed p. 14, the book's rendering of the jade inscription ([owner's Drive copy](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WPlkEDK5FTR9LkFGrutj1YCqIDZ9dMx_))

### Evidence

No clinical trial of the microcosmic orbit appears in the sources. Kohn presents the orbit as traditional practice; the modern versions she names (Mantak Chia and others) are cited at second hand. The Qi Encyclopedia summary is a practice description, not clinical evidence; the protocol above is an abbreviated account of it and complete instructional provenance remains pending. The long-life claims are the tradition's own framing. What is documented: a historical practitioner, Jiang Weiqiao (Master Yinshi), "moved from here into the circulation of qi in inner alchemical fashion, establishing the microcosmic orbit" ([Kohn, Sitting in Oblivion](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1RO92-oZ64aGydm5IomYUC_XIqLnaN3Gc), printed p. 13, note 13).

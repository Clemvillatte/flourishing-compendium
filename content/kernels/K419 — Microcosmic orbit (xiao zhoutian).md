> **Kernel K419** — Microcosmic orbit (xiao zhoutian)

The microcosmic orbit (xiao zhoutian, "small heavenly circle") is a Daoist energy-circulation practice: qi is warmed and settled in the lower elixir field (dantian), then guided in a loop down to the pelvic floor, up the spine to the crown, and down the front of the torso through the central channels. Kohn traces its pattern back to Zhou-dynasty breath cultivation and describes it as a core move of inner alchemy (neidan), where adepts circulate purified qi "along the central channels of the torso", opening a series of passes; a larger loop reaching to the feet is the macrocosmic orbit ([Kohn, Chinese Healing Exercises](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WPlkEDK5FTR9LkFGrutj1YCqIDZ9dMx_)).

### Protocol

The opened passages describe the pattern's stages, not a complete step-by-step routine; a full instructional protocol remains pending and is deliberately not invented here.

The classical pattern, as Kohn renders it: a deep breath is collected in the mouth, mixed with saliva and swallowed, and the qi settles and stabilizes in the lower abdomen, the Ocean of Qi or elixir field. As the practice repeats, the qi accumulates and "sprouts"; from there adepts guide it upward — pushing it down to the pelvic floor and then moving it gradually up along the spine, in close coordination with deep breathing — up the back to the top of the head, completing the circle down the front through the central channels, opening passes along the way. The earliest known reference, a Zhou-dynasty inscription on a jade block, gives the arc in the tradition's own words:

> "To guide the qi, allow it to enter deeply [by inhaling] and collect it [in the mouth]. As it collects, it will expand. Once expanded, it will sink down. When it sinks down, it comes to rest. After it has come to rest, it becomes stable. When the qi is stable, it begins to sprout. From sprouting, it begins to grow. As it grows, it can be pulled back upwards. When it is pulled upwards, it reaches the crown of the head." — Kohn, Chinese Healing Exercises, printed p. 15, translating the jade inscription ([owner's Drive copy](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WPlkEDK5FTR9LkFGrutj1YCqIDZ9dMx_))

### Evidence

No clinical trial of the microcosmic orbit appears in the opened sources. Kohn presents the orbit as traditional practice; the modern versions she names (Mantak Chia and others) were not opened and carry no verified evidence here. The long-life and longevity claims are the tradition's own framing. What is recorded: the modern practice is widespread, and a historical practitioner, Jiang Weiqiao (Master Yinshi), is documented in Kohn's Sitting in Oblivion as having "moved from here into the circulation of qi in inner alchemical fashion, establishing the microcosmic orbit" ([Kohn, Sitting in Oblivion](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1RO92-oZ64aGydm5IomYUC_XIqLnaN3Gc), printed p. 13, note 13).

> **Kernel K419** — Microcosmic orbit (xiao zhoutian)

The microcosmic orbit (xiao zhoutian) is a Daoist energy-circulation practice. In the inner-alchemy (neidan) material Kohn surveys, women adepts at the tradition's first stage "circulate the newly purified qi in the Microcosmic Orbit, guiding it along the central channels of the torso and opening a series of passes along the way"; a larger loop reaching to the feet is the macrocosmic orbit ([Kohn, Chinese Healing Exercises](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WPlkEDK5FTR9LkFGrutj1YCqIDZ9dMx_)).

### Protocol

The opened passages describe the pattern's stages, not a complete step-by-step routine; a full instructional protocol remains pending and is deliberately not invented here.

An early breath-cultivation pattern Kohn describes separately: a deep breath is collected in the mouth, mixed with saliva and swallowed, and the qi settles and stabilizes in the lower abdomen, the Ocean of Qi or elixir field. As the practice repeats, the qi accumulates and "sprouts"; from there adepts guide it upward — pushing it down to the pelvic floor and then moving it gradually up along the spine, in close coordination with deep breathing — up the back to the top of the head. Kohn notes of this early pattern: "Not only like the modern qigong and inner alchemical practice of the Microcosmic Orbit" — the comparison is drawn without documenting a continuous lineage between them. The earliest known reference to healing exercises, a Zhou-dynasty inscription on a jade block, gives the upward arc in the tradition's own words:

> "To guide the qi, allow it to enter deeply [by inhaling] and collect it [in the mouth]. As it collects, it will expand. Once expanded, it will sink down. When it sinks down, it comes to rest. After it has come to rest, it becomes stable. When the qi is stable, it begins to sprout. From sprouting, it begins to grow. As it grows, it can be pulled back upwards. When it is pulled upwards, it reaches the crown of the head." — Kohn, Chinese Healing Exercises, printed p. 14, the book's rendering of the jade inscription ([owner's Drive copy](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1WPlkEDK5FTR9LkFGrutj1YCqIDZ9dMx_))

### Evidence

No clinical trial of the microcosmic orbit appears in the opened sources. Kohn presents the orbit as traditional practice; the modern versions she names (Mantak Chia and others) were not opened and carry no verified evidence here. The long-life claims are the tradition's own framing. What is documented: a historical practitioner, Jiang Weiqiao (Master Yinshi), "moved from here into the circulation of qi in inner alchemical fashion, establishing the microcosmic orbit" ([Kohn, Sitting in Oblivion](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=1RO92-oZ64aGydm5IomYUC_XIqLnaN3Gc), printed p. 13, note 13).

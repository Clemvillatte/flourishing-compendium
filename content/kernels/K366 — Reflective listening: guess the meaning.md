> **Kernel K366** — Reflective listening: guess the meaning

When someone tells you something, don't just repeat their words back. Make a *guess* at what they mean underneath, and say the guess as a statement. "I'm feeling kind of down today" → "Like you don't have much energy." If your guess lands, the conversation deepens; if it misses, they correct you, and the correction is itself information.

The move is small — a possible extension of what they said, not a big leap. You're not interpreting; you're checking whether you understood.

Adapted from Miller & Rollnick, *Motivational Interviewing* (4th ed.), ch. 4 (Engaging), pp. 57–58, and ch. 8 (Deeper Listening), p. 143.

## Operationalises

- [[D082 — Don't impose your frame on others|D082 Don't impose your frame on others]] — **grounding**: guessing at the meaning is how you avoid installing your own frame.
- [[B147 — Listen deeply|B147 Listen deeply]] — **grounding**: reflective guessing is [[Deep listening|deep listening]] with a concrete move.
- [[P129 — Practice sympathetic understanding|P129 Practice sympathetic understanding]] — **grounding**: the guess is sympathy in action.
- [[K218 — Perspective-getting]] — **tension**: this kernel asks you to *guess* at what someone means; K218 notes that asking beats guessing for accuracy. Both are true — reflection is for connection, not for accurate mind-reading; ask when you need facts.

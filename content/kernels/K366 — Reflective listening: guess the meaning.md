> **Kernel K366** — Reflective listening: guess the meaning

When someone tells you something, don't just repeat their words back. Make a *guess* at what they mean underneath, and say the guess as a statement. "I'm feeling kind of down today" → "Like you don't have much energy." If your guess lands, the conversation deepens; if it misses, they correct you, and the correction is itself information.

The move is small — a possible extension of what they said, not a big leap. You're not interpreting; you're checking whether you understood.

Adapted from Miller & Rollnick, Motivational Interviewing (3rd ed., 2012).

### Protocol

1. Wait for a statement that carries feeling or unfinished business under its words.
2. Form a silent hypothesis about the meaning underneath: not the topic, the point of mentioning it now.
3. Offer the hypothesis as a statement, in roughly the speaker's register: "Like you don't have much energy."
4. Hold the guess lightly. If they correct you, the correction tells you where the meaning actually sits; offer the next guess from there.

### Evidence

The skill is a core clinical method in motivational interviewing. In the authors' own words, from the third edition:

> "A complex reflection adds some meaning or emphasis to what the person has said, making a guess about the unspoken content or what might come next (continuing the paragraph)."
> — [Miller and Rollnick, third edition, PDF](https://ia800603.us.archive.org/29/items/applications-of-motivational-interviewing-william-r.-miller-stephen-rollnick-mot/%28Applications%20of%20Motivational%20Interviewing%29%20William%20R.%20Miller,%20Stephen%20Rollnick%20-%20Motivational%20Interviewing,%20Third%20Edition_%20Helping%20People%20Change-The%20Guilford%20Press%20%282012%29.pdf)

The book's name for the move:

> "In essence, the interviewer is venturing what might be the next sentence in the person’s paragraph instead of merely echoing the last one. This is a skillful form of reflection that we call continuing the paragraph."
> — [Miller and Rollnick, third edition, PDF](https://ia800603.us.archive.org/29/items/applications-of-motivational-interviewing-william-r.-miller-stephen-rollnick-mot/%28Applications%20of%20Motivational%20Interviewing%29%20William%20R.%20Miller,%20Stephen%20Rollnick%20-%20Motivational%20Interviewing,%20Third%20Edition_%20Helping%20People%20Change-The%20Guilford%20Press%20%282012%29.pdf)

The same discussion warns against over-reliance on simple reflections: sticking too close to exactly what the person said tends to leave the conversation going in circles.

These passages describe reflective listening; they do not provide outcome evidence for the everyday exercise presented here.

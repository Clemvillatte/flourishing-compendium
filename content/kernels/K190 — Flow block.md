> **Kernel K190** — Flow block

Protect a block of time (45 to 90 minutes) for deep, single-tasked work. Clear notifications. Set a clear goal and a feedback signal. Let the work absorb you.

### Protocol

*Steps are the compendium's editorial protocol; the conditions they assemble are Csikszentmihalyi's conditions for flow.*

1. Block 45 to 90 minutes and defend them as an appointment; a block that can be interrupted is a suggestion.
2. Name the goal of the block in one sentence, and choose a task whose difficulty sits at the edge of your skill; too easy invites boredom, too hard invites anxiety.
3. Build in a signal that tells you how it is going as you work; flow needs feedback the way a conversation needs replies.

### Evidence

The block assembles the conditions Csikszentmihalyi identified for optimal experience: "the best moments usually occur when a person's body or mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort to accomplish something difficult and worthwhile" ([Csikszentmihalyi, *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience*, owner's Drive PDF, printed pp. 3–4](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1v5wDIgdsQCpjd1LO8l0PPwBm67GbKJ2P); quote verified against the owner's copy, PDF pp. 12–13). He adds that "optimal experience is thus something that we make happen," which is the premise of scheduling for it rather than waiting for it. What this book does not test: the 45-to-90-minute duration, or the effect of protected blocks on productivity or well-being; the duration is an editorial convention, and the research ground is the description of the flow state and its conditions.

> **Kernel K356** — The face practice

In your next conversation, attend to the face of the person in front of you. Not to read the expression for information, but to receive the face as the presence of the one who is speaking to you. The practice trains seeing the other as a person rather than a role or a function.

### Protocol

1. Choose one ordinary interaction this week: a checkout, a meeting, a family dinner.
2. As it begins, notice the label your mind has filed the person under ("receptionist", "difficult uncle"), and set it aside for the duration of the exchange.
3. Let your eyes rest on the face rather than the task: the eyes, the lines, the tiredness or the lightness that is actually there this week. Do not decode; look.
4. When the face changes, let it change you: slow down if it tightens, warm if it opens.
5. Afterwards, write one sentence about what you saw that the label had not predicted. That sentence is the residue of the practice; collect them.

### Evidence

The warrant is Levinas's, summarised in the Stanford Encyclopedia: "Naked and defenseless, the face signifies, with or without words, 'Do not kill me'. It opposes a passive resistance to our desire for mastery wherein our freedom asserts its sovereignty" (Bergo, SEP, citing Totality and Infinity, p. 84), and the face "interrupts our free activity (and willing) and calls us to account for ourselves" (SEP, citing TI 198, 291). The perceptual side is measurable: perceived direct gaze with a human face produces the "eye contact effect", modulating concurrent cognitive processing, social-brain activity, and preferential orienting from early in life (Senju & Johnson 2009). The face is not a neutral object in the visual field; the attention lands differently, which is what the practice works with.

### A guided version

The [Seeing Other People As Human Beings Rather Than Labels exercise at Mindfulness Exercises](https://mindfulnessexercises.com/seeing-other-people-as-human-beings/) is the face practice in public form: notice the label the mind gives someone ("checkout clerk", "annoying coworker"), let it be there without acting on it, then observe the person with beginner's mind, "as a living, breathing, and feeling being", first with strangers and then with the people closest to you.

## Related concepts

- [[Intersubjectivity]] — the philosophical account of shared inner worlds; the face is where it begins for Levinas.
- [[Face negotiation theory]] — the research tradition on how social face is claimed, threatened and repaired across cultures; a different sense of the word, though both concern what is owed to the person in front of you.
- [[Dialogue]] — Buber's I-You encounter in speech; the face practice is its silent half.

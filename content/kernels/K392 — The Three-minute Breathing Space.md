> **Kernel K392** — The Three-minute Breathing Space

A three-step mini-meditation you can run in three minutes, one minute, or ten seconds. Step 1, become aware: adopt an erect, dignified posture. Ask: what thoughts are going through the mind? What feelings are here? What body sensations? Acknowledge them without trying to change them. Step 2, gather: narrow attention onto the breath. Ride the in-breath, ride the out-breath. Step 3, expand: widen awareness to the body as a whole — the posture, the face, the shoulders, the whole body breathing. Use it twice a day at set times, and whenever you feel under pressure.

### Protocol

Adapted from Williams and Penman, *Mindfulness*, 2011, ch. 8; the same exercise is taught in MBCT as developed by Segal, Williams and Teasdale. The three steps, in the programme's own wording:

1. Becoming aware. Sit or stand erect and dignified. Close the eyes if possible. Ask: "What is my experience right now?" Acknowledge thoughts as mental events, emotions as feelings, and the body's tightness or bracing as sensations. Do not fix them.
2. Gathering. Redirect attention to the physical sensations of the breath, at the abdomen. Follow the breath all the way in and all the way out, using the breathing to anchor into the present.
3. Expanding. Widen the field of awareness around the breathing so it includes a sense of the body as a whole, the posture, the face. If discomfort, tension or resistance shows up, take awareness there, expanding on the in-breath, softening on the out-breath.

### Evidence

The practice comes from the MBCT programme for depression relapse prevention, and the clinical handout tradition carries its rationale:

> "The breathing space provides a way to step out of automatic pilot and reconnect with the present moment."
> — [MBCT Session 3 participant handout (Rockman), OSU CDN PDF](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/a/113335/files/2025/08/MBCT-S3-Participant-Handout.pdf)

The three steps in the same handout's own words:

> "Step 1: Becoming Aware — Become aware of how things are in this moment by deliberately adopting an erect and dignified posture, whether sitting or standing."
> — [same handout](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/a/113335/files/2025/08/MBCT-S3-Participant-Handout.pdf)

> "Step 3: Expanding — Now expanding the field of your awareness around the breathing so that it includes a sense of the body as a whole, your posture, and facial expression."
> — [same handout](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/a/113335/files/2025/08/MBCT-S3-Participant-Handout.pdf)

A dissemination study of the exercise summarises the same construct:

> "The 3-Minute Breathing Space exercise is grounded in three essential concepts: awareness, gathering, and expanding (Segal, Williams, & Teasdale, 2012, p.184)."
> — [Zajicek (2014), "The Dissemination of Mindfulness-based Therapies" (senior honors thesis), CU Scholar](https://scholar.colorado.edu/downloads/w9505077h)

What the sources do not test: the exercise is one component of MBCT, whose programme-level outcomes (relapse prevention in recurrent depression) are well studied; the exercise's standalone effect as a brief decentering tool is an inference, not a separately tested outcome.

## Limits

- Do not skip step 1. The practice begins with honest acknowledgment of what is here — not with a demand for calm.

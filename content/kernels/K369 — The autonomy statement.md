> **Kernel K369** — The autonomy statement

When someone is ambivalent about a change (or when you're coaching yourself), try saying the autonomy statement out loud: "It's your call. You're the one who gets to decide." Said to a friend, this removes the pressure of you being the push-back hand. Said to yourself, it removes the coercion from your own inner voice.

The reverse works too: notice how much more you can hear someone who says "It's your call" than someone who says "You have to."

Adapted from Miller & Rollnick, Motivational Interviewing (4th ed.), ch. 6, p. 106. The specific lay phrasing is an adaptation, not Miller & Rollnick's own.

### Protocol

1. Pick an ambivalence that is live right now, yours or someone else's.
2. Say the statement out loud, in your own register: "It's your call. You're the one who gets to decide."
3. Notice what changes in the conversation once the pressure is off: which side of the ambivalence starts speaking.
4. If you catch yourself advising, restate the autonomy once, then stop.

### Evidence

The stance behind the statement, in the MI book's own words:

> "The opposite of autonomy support is the attempt to make people do things, to coerce and control. A probation officer who says “You can’t leave the county” is not literally telling the truth, nor is a counselor who tells an alcohol-dependent person “You can’t drink.” What they mean is that certain behavior is likely to have negative consequences, but the choice always remains with the individual."
> — [Miller and Rollnick, third edition, PDF](https://ia800603.us.archive.org/29/items/applications-of-motivational-interviewing-william-r.-miller-stephen-rollnick-mot/%28Applications%20of%20Motivational%20Interviewing%29%20William%20R.%20Miller,%20Stephen%20Rollnick%20-%20Motivational%20Interviewing,%20Third%20Edition_%20Helping%20People%20Change-The%20Guilford%20Press%20%282012%29.pdf)

And where the book grounds the stance:

> "Third, acceptance involves honoring and respecting each person’s autonomy, their irrevocable right and capacity of self-direction (Deci & Ryan, 1985; Markland, Ryan, Tobin, & Rollnick, 2005)."
> — [same PDF](https://ia800603.us.archive.org/29/items/applications-of-motivational-interviewing-william-r.-miller-stephen-rollnick-mot/%28Applications%20of%20Motivational%20Interviewing%29%20William%20R.%20Miller,%20Stephen%20Rollnick%20-%20Motivational%20Interviewing,%20Third%20Edition_%20Helping%20People%20Change-The%20Guilford%20Press%20%282012%29.pdf)

The reactance passage quoted on K368 is this book's continuation of the same paragraph.

What the sources do not test: these passages are the MI account of autonomy support; the specific sentence used here is a lay adaptation, and its effect has not been evaluated.

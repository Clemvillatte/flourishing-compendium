> **Kernel K174** — Behavioral activation

Schedule one enjoyable activity and one meaningful activity per day, and do them whether or not you feel like it. The practice scales behavioral activation, a structured psychotherapy for depression, down to a daily habit. Its premise is the therapy's premise: action can precede motivation, and waiting for the feeling often means waiting forever. Reach for it when mood is low and the days have narrowed.

### Protocol

Suggested adaptation of the therapy's scheduling rules as set out in the clinician literature. The practice moves the Overt behaviour and Affect dimensions: what gets done, and how doing lands.

1. Plan the two activities on a timetable, one that gives pleasure and one that matters. In the clinical form, "these are set out on planned timetables (activity schedules)", and "individuals are encouraged to include activities that are soothing and pleasurable, as rewards" ([Veale, *Advances in Psychiatric Treatment*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/behavioural-activation-for-depression/EBC5FC0D9D550FFBE6F982B33BF91EE9)). Editorial addition, not from the carriers: one of each per day is the compendium's dose.
2. Do them according to the plan, not according to how you feel. The clinical literature names the obstacle directly: "people tell themselves that they will engage in a particular activity when they feel motivated or when they 'feel like it'", and the solution is "they should always act according to the plan or activity schedule - not according to how they feel at the time".
3. Note what the activity did to your mood. The same source: "individuals should monitor the effect of their scheduled activities (and deviations from their plan) on their mood". Start small; "a major mistake is for a patient to try to tackle everything at once".

### Evidence

The finding that established the therapy, in the authors' own abstract:

> "The comparison involved randomly assigning 150 outpatients with major depression to a treatment focused exclusively on the behavioral activation (BA) component of CT, a treatment that included both BA and the teaching of skills to modify automatic thoughts (AT), but excluding the components of CT focused on core schema, or the full CT treatment... there was no evidence that the complete treatment produced better outcomes, at either the termination of acute treatment or the 6-month follow-up, than either component treatment. Furthermore, both BA and AT treatments were just as effective as CT at altering negative thinking as well as dysfunctional attributional styles."
> — [Jacobson et al. (1996), *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology* 64(2), PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8871414/)

What the study does not test: it concerns outpatient psychotherapy for major depression delivered by experienced therapists, not a self-administered two-activity daily schedule, and it compared treatment packages rather than testing the daily habit on this page. The clinical guide's scheduling rules are quoted from the treatment's clinician literature, not from a trial of self-scheduling.

> **Kernel K182** — Small-wins tracking

Note one small win each day. The win does not need to be large; it needs to be noticed. The daily-diary research below links day-to-day goal progress to well-being.

### Protocol

Steps are a practice form; the studies behind it are quoted below. Intended focus: Motivation and Subjective well-being.

1. At day's end, name one thing that moved forward today, however small: a page, a call, a repaired handle, a held temper.
2. Write it down in one line. If nothing moved, write the nearest thing that almost moved, and note what blocked it.
3. Keep the log going across days. The point of the record is the trend the single days hide; review weekly and keep the recording habit that survives.

### Evidence

From the daily-diary research on goal progress:

> "daily independent goal progress predicted same-day and next-day improvements in psychological, physical, and relational well-being." (Abstract, [Jakubiak & Feeney, 2016, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 111(3), 317-340](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27560610/))

What this evidence does not test: the writing-down. The diary studies measured participants' own reports of daily progress and well-being; they did not test whether recording one small win each day changes anything, and the samples were married couples, young and late adulthood. On higher-progress days people reported increases in positive affect, sleep quality and relationship quality; that association does not demonstrate an effect of deliberately noticing progress, and the log is the practice form.

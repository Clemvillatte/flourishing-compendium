> **Kernel K323** — Daily making

Make something every day. It does not matter what: a meal, a sentence, a drawing, a garden bed, a conversation, a spreadsheet that is genuinely useful. The criterion is that you brought something into being that did not exist before. The study below is what the research shows for people who already make things: recent making tracked better momentary tone, and the week's making tracked person-level eudemonic happiness.

### Protocol

The making is the practice; the experience-sampling study behind it is quoted below. Intended focus: Overt behaviour and Subjective well-being.

1. Make something every day, however small: a meal cooked with attention, a sentence, a sketch, a garden bed, a spreadsheet that is genuinely useful.
2. Hold the criterion lightly but firmly: something exists at the end that did not exist before, and you are the one who brought it about.
3. Keep the daily thing small enough to survive bad days, and let it be ordinary. A week of small makings beats one abandoned masterpiece.

### Evidence

From the experience-sampling study of art-making in everyday life:

> "Recent art-making was significantly associated with experiential shifts: improvement in hedonic tone, vivid internal imagery and the flow state." (Abstract, [Holt, 2018, Perspectives in Public Health 138(1), 55-65](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29130867/))

What this evidence does not test: the daily rule. Forty-one artists were beeped ten times a day for a week and asked about art-making, mood, cognition and state of consciousness; recent art-making tracked better hedonic tone, vivid imagery and flow in the moment, and the week's frequency of art-making tracked person-level eudemonic happiness and self-regulation. The participants were people who already make art; the abstract reports no test of a make-something-every-day rule for anyone else, and the direction of the association is not settled by it. What it supports is the kernel's core bet: making changes the maker's state, and the state is measurable.

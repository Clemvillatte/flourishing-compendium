> **Kernel K205** — Nature exposure

Spend time in natural settings: green space, water, sky, weather on the skin. The practice is deliberately unguided and unstructured; its job is contact, not accomplishment. Reach for it when the head is loud, the attention is spent, or the body is carrying a stress it cannot name.

### Protocol

The steps are this practice's own exercise; the schedule is my prescription. The practice moves the Attention and Affect dimensions, with the walk itself an overt behaviour.

1. Go to whatever natural setting is reachable: a park, a tree-lined street's shade, water if there is water. The evidence contrasts natural with urban settings; my schedule is thirty minutes or more, several times a week.
2. Walk, sit, or stand; let the setting work. In the study behind this kernel, participants walked ninety minutes in either a natural area or the city ([Bratman et al. 2015, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26124129/)).
3. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: weather on the skin is part of the dose. If the mind rehearses its loops, that is the pattern the nature-walk study measured a reduction in; return attention to what is in front of you.

### Evidence

Three carriers, one per claim in the practice's tradition.

Rumination, from the 90-minute-walk experiment:

> "We show in healthy participants that a brief nature experience, a 90-min walk in a natural setting, decreases both self-reported rumination and neural activity in the subgenual prefrontal cortex (sgPFC), whereas a 90-min walk in an urban setting has no such effects on self-reported rumination or neural activity."
> — [Bratman et al. (2015), *PNAS* 112(28), PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26124129/)

Attention, from the backward-digit-span experiments: "We present two experiments that show that walking in nature or viewing pictures of nature can improve directed-attention abilities as measured with a backwards digit-span task and the Attention Network Task" ([Berman, Jonides & Kaplan 2008, *Psychological Science* 19(12)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2008.02225.x/)).

Stress physiology, from the cortisol meta-analysis: "The main results of the meta-analysis showed that salivary cortisol levels were significantly lower in the forest groups compared with the urban groups both before (MD = - 0.08 μg/dl [95% CI - 0.11 to - 0.05 μg/dl]; p < 0.01; I2 = 46%) and after intervention (MD = - 0.05 μg/dl [95% CI - 0.06 to - 0.04 μg/dl]; p < 0.01; I2 = 88%)" ([Antonelli, Barbieri & Donelli 2019, *International Journal of Biometeorology* 63(8)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31001682/)). The same review's plain-language summary: "In all but two included studies, cortisol levels were significantly lower after intervention in forest groups if compared with control/comparison groups, or a significant pre-post reduction of cortisol levels was reported in the forest groups".

What the studies do not test: the rumination and attention findings come from single walks and brief laboratory paradigms, not from this page's thirty-minute routine; the cortisol result pools heterogeneous forest-bathing trials; and none tested this page's assembled schedule.

### A guided version

Guided outdoor recordings of nature contact exist. Jörg Sprave's [I Am Nature guided forest bathing meditation at Mindful.org](https://www.mindful.org/jorg-sprave-a-guided-forest-bathing-meditation/) is an audio practice with a published script, based on shinrin-yoku, designed to be done outdoors. The kernel's default remains unguided and the evidence above concerns unguided settings; the recording is the doorway when starting needs a voice.

> **Kernel K192** — Double-loop learning

When the same failure repeats, fix the assumption behind the action. Ask: "What belief or strategy is producing this pattern?" This is the deeper correction.

### Protocol

The second question is the practice; the source behind the concept is quoted below. Intended focus: Self and Motivation.

1. Notice a failure that has repeated at least three times despite your corrections. Name the pattern in one sentence.
2. Before adjusting the action again, ask the deeper question: what belief or strategy of mine is producing this pattern? Write the assumption down, even if it flatters you.
3. Design one small test that would falsify the assumption, run it, and let the result do the editing. The assumption is on trial, not you.

### Evidence

From the source that carried the concept into healthcare:

> "Double loop learning, an attempt to question underlying organizational policies and goals, offers the possibility of making quality improvement (QI) efforts more productive." (Abstract, [Elliott, 1996, Joint Commission Journal on Quality Improvement 22(1), 59-66](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8808200/))

What this evidence does not test: your assumptions. The concept comes from organizational learning theory, and this paper argued for its use in hospital quality improvement; the PubMed-indexed literature around it is conceptual and organizational, with case studies of teams rather than experiments on individuals asking themselves the second question. The abstract reports no trial of personal double-loop learning, and the productivity claim is a possibility argued, not an effect measured here. What the source supports is the definition and the wager: the deeper loop questions the goals and policies behind the actions, which is what step 2 asks of a belief.

> **Kernel K209** — Repair attempt

When a rift occurs, address it within 48 hours. The window is this practice's own. The move inside it comes from John Gottman's couples research, where a repair attempt is defined as "any statement or action that prevents negativity from spiraling out of control in conflict" ([Gottman Institute, via YES Magazine](https://www.yesmagazine.org/health-happiness/2019/12/31/repair-relationships-apology)).

## Protocol

1. Brake early. "Think of a repair attempt as slamming on the brakes when you see a red light. You do this to avoid a collision that could harm your marriage" ([Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/5-steps-to-fight-better-if-your-relationship-is-worth-fighting-for/)). The Love Lab timing finding: "Repairs made in the first three minutes of the 15-minute discussion were much more likely to be accepted by one's partner than attempts made later on, after negativity had started to take hold" (YES Magazine, same research).
2. Aim at closeness, not logic. "The study found that repairs focused on emotional closeness, rather than appealing to logic, proved more effective" (same article).
3. Keep it small and silly if that is what works. The definition allows any statement or action; a grin, a joke at the right moment, a hand on the arm all qualify.
4. Watch for theirs, too. "Receptiveness to repair is almost more important than the repair attempt itself. Learn to listen and watch for them. Some people in the study crafted elegant, timely repairs, but their partner just couldn't hear it. Other couples made repair attempts in really clumsy ways and were successful" (same article).
5. If it is refused, do not read that as the verdict on the relationship or on you; the receiving side depends on the climate, and "Happy couples send and receive repair attempts with ease. In unhappy marriages, even amazing repair attempts fall on deaf ears" ([Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/5-steps-to-fight-better-if-your-relationship-is-worth-fighting-for/)).

## A guided version

The repair happens with the other person, in the conversation; no guided recording is cited for it here.

## Evidence

What separates the couples is not the elegance of the attempts but whether they land: "The difference between stable, emotionally intelligent marriages and unhappy ones is not that repair attempts are better, but that the repair attempts get through to the spouse. Repair attempts require two people – the person offering the repair and one accepting it" ([Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/5-steps-to-fight-better-if-your-relationship-is-worth-fighting-for/)). And the climate carries the weight: a natural change of topic "could reduce physiological arousal for both parties. It was effective in soothing the couple and worked well as a repair" (YES Magazine).

What the research does not cover: the studies observe couples in conflict conversations in the Love Lab; the 48-hour window for addressing a rift is this practice's own, as is the example sentence. The evidence for early repair concerns minutes into a discussion, not days after one.

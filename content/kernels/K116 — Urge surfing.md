> **Kernel K116** — Urge surfing

When a craving or impulse arises, locate it in the body and watch it rise, peak, and fall like a wave. Do not act on it and do not suppress it. Most urges last 90 seconds to a few minutes if not fed.

### Protocol

1. When an urge shows up, pause before acting on it. Take a breath and name what is happening: "there is an urge here."
2. Locate the urge in the body: a tightness in the chest, restlessness in the hands, a pull in the belly. Rest attention on those sensations.
3. Watch the urge as a wave: rising, peaking, and fading. The breath is the surfboard; keep breathing steadily while the wave does its thing. No suppressing, no arguing, no acting.
4. Notice the urge losing intensity, and notice that you are still here. If it returns, surf it again. Each ridden wave teaches the nervous system that urges pass on their own.

### Evidence

In the original test of the technique, 123 college student smokers received a brief 11-minute urge surfing instruction or no mindfulness instruction, and those who learned urge surfing smoked fewer cigarettes over the following seven days than controls told to cope however they normally would. A review of mindfulness interventions for adolescent substance use reports a randomised comparison in which the urge surfing group reduced cigarettes smoked per day by 26 per cent versus 11 per cent for typical coping strategies (d = 0.64). Caveat: laboratory studies with stricter active controls have not always separated urge surfing from other coping strategies on craving itself.

### A guided version

A clear guided session for cravings and habits is [Urge Surfing, 20 Minute Mindfulness Practice, by Naomi Goodlet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=td4d_mV29Wo).

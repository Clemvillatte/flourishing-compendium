> **Kernel K178** — Situation selection

Choose environments that support your values and avoid those that undermine them. If a context reliably triggers a pattern you are trying to change, change the context first.

### Protocol

The steps are the practice form; the study behind them is quoted below. Intended focus: Emotion regulation and Environment.

1. List the contexts you repeatedly enter: the weekly meeting, the app, the kitchen at midnight, the friend who always leaves you smaller.
2. Mark each one: does it support what you are trying to grow, or reliably trigger the pattern you are trying to change? Use last month's evidence, not today's mood.
3. For the undermining contexts, change the context before changing yourself: enter it differently, less often, with an ally, or not at all. The contexts that support your values get more of your calendar.

### Evidence

From the age-difference study of early-stage regulation:

> "Whereas situation selection was related to affect, modification of negative material was not." (Abstract, [Livingstone & Isaacowitz, 2015, Social Psychological and Personality Science 6(8), 904-910](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26998196/))

What this evidence does not test: your list. Participants freely chose between negative, neutral and positive material and then chose whether to view or skip items, with affect rated after each experience; older adults in both goal conditions, and younger adults told to regulate, spent less time with negative material. The abstract reports no test of choosing meetings or kitchens, and the design measures what people do with offered material, not what they do with their lives. What it supports is narrow but useful: the choosing itself, not the later patching, is what tracked affect in this study.

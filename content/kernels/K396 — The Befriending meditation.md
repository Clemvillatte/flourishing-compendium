> **Kernel K396** — The Befriending meditation

Sit with dignity and wakefulness. Settle on the breath. Then, in stages, offer these phrases: *May I be safe and free from suffering. May I be as happy and healthy as it is possible for me to be. May I have ease of being.* Drop each phrase like a pebble into a deep well — then listen for any reaction in thoughts, feelings, or body. If self-kindness is hard, bring to mind a person (or pet) who loved you unconditionally; once you feel their love, return to offering it to yourself. Then move to a loved one, then a neutral stranger, then, optionally, a difficult person. Each stage: repeat the phrases, pause, listen.

Adapted from Williams and Penman, *Mindfulness*, 2011. The instructions above are taken from an unattributed transcript of a guided befriending practice (Meditation 7, hosted as a course companion by eCampusOntario Pressbooks). A peer-reviewed study using a befriending practice describes its audio as "derived from recordings by Professor Mark Williams" and lists the same phrases and stages ([Simione et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9757557/)). The print book was not opened; the practice's recording is distributed free on the book's site ([franticworld.com](https://franticworld.com/free-meditations-from-mindfulness/)).

### Protocol

1. Sit upright and settle on the breath for a minute or two. Target dimensions: Emotion (warmth) and Motivation (wishing well rather than fixing).
2. Offer the three phrases to yourself, one at a time, leaving silence after each; the silence is where the practice happens.
3. If self-kindness will not come, call to mind a person or pet who loved you unconditionally, feel that, then redirect it toward yourself.
4. Move through the stages: self, a loved one, a neutral stranger, and optionally a difficult person; the recording says this person "doesn't have to be the most difficult person in your life" — someone you feel a bit distant from is enough.
5. Afterwards, note which stage was easiest and which resisted; retain the practice and vary the stage you dwell on, not the phrases.

### Evidence

The cited studies examine broader interventions, not this exercise alone. In a randomized field experiment with working adults (n = 139), a loving-kindness practice raised daily positive emotions over time, and the personal resources those built in turn predicted life satisfaction and fewer depressive symptoms ([Fredrickson et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954193/)).

> "this meditation practice produced increases over time in daily experiences of positive emotions, which, in turn, produced increases in a wide range of personal resources" — [Fredrickson et al. (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954193/)

> "In turn, these increments in personal resources predicted increased life satisfaction and reduced depressive symptoms." — [Fredrickson et al. (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954193/)

A meta-analysis of 23 kindness-based meditation studies found life satisfaction improved in pre-post designs, while the pooled estimate from randomized comparisons was small and not statistically significant ([Gu et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35532366/)).

> "LKCM significantly enhanced life satisfaction in pre-post design (g = 0.312, k = 15, n = 451), but the significance disappeared in the additional meta-analysis based on randomized controlled trials (g = 0.106, k = 6, n = 404)" — [Gu et al. (2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35532366/)

Separately, a meta-analysis examined manualized clinical MBCT for people with recurrent depression in full or partial remission: across 9 randomized trials (1,258 patients), relapse risk within 60 weeks was lower with MBCT than without it ([Kuyken et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27119968/)). That analysis does not evaluate this individual practice.

> "patients receiving MBCT had a reduced risk of depressive relapse within a 60-week follow-up period compared with those who did not receive MBCT (hazard ratio, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.58-0.82)" — [Kuyken et al. (2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27119968/)

## Limits

- The difficult-person stage is optional and gradual. The recording's guidance: "This doesn't have to be the most difficult person in your life. It may be someone you feel a bit distant from."
- Warmth is not required. "It's enough to hold the intention, to incline the mind in this way."
- If self-kindness is hard, use the unconditional-love-memory fallback — it is part of the practice, not a workaround.

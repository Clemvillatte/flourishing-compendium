> **Kernel K302** — Ecological reciprocity

One act each month that gives something back to the living world: plant something native, reduce a form of consumption, support a conservation effort, compost. The practice is not environmentalism as an abstract cause; it is an older idea, found in Indigenous harvesting protocols, that a gift received calls for a gift returned. Reach for it when the ledger between your life and the world it draws on has gone too long unbalanced.

### Protocol

The monthly menu is the practice's own; the shape of the giving follows the Honorable Harvest guidelines as Kimmerer describes them, quoted in the Evidence section.

1. Choose one act per month from the menu: plant something native, reduce a form of consumption, support a conservation effort, compost.
2. Let the act answer an actual taking. The guidelines below pair taking with reciprocity: give thanks for what has been given, and return something the place can use.
3. Keep the act small and local enough to sustain. [Editorial, not from the carriers:] a monthly act that survives the year is the aim, not a grand gesture.

### Evidence

From Kimmerer's account of the Honorable Harvest:

> "And the Honorable Harvest begins with the fact that we never take the first one; whether it's berries or firewood or basket plants, you never take the first one. And this self-restraint means that you're never going to take the last one. So it has an inherent conservation ethic." ([Kimmerer, interview, Emergence Magazine](https://emergencemagazine.org/conversation/practical-reverence/))

> "And then, I'm told that we never go home with our basket without reciprocating the gift, of giving back to those plants. We've taken something; they've given us something. So how will we pay them back? Sometimes it's with a spiritual gift. In fact, it's always with a spiritual gift, but also a material gift: Maybe they need a little more sun. Maybe you should weed around them." (same interview)

From the meta-analysis of connection to nature and proenvironmental behavior:

> "A random-effects model demonstrated a positive and significant association between connection to nature and PEB (r = 0.42, 95% CI 0.36, 0.47, p < 0.001). People who are more connected to nature reported greater engagement in PEB." (Abstract, [Whitburn, Linklater & Abrahamse, 2019 (online first; print issue February 2020), Conservation Biology 34(1), 180-193](https://doi.org/10.1111/cobi.13381))

What this evidence does not test: the practice. The meta-analysis is correlational; it does not show that performing monthly acts builds connection, and the Honorable Harvest is presented as an Indigenous ethic Kimmerer describes, not a tested intervention for well-being.

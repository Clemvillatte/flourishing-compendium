> **Kernel K311** — Responsibility inventory

List three areas of your life where you feel stuck. For each, write two lines: what is within your control here, and what action, however small, you could take. The inventory is not about blame; it is about locating the space where agency operates. Reach for it when stuckness has started to feel like a property of the world rather than a description of a moment.

### Protocol

The practice moves the Cognition and Motivation dimensions: the situation is parsed for agency, and effort is pointed at the part that answers.

1. Write the three stuck areas as plain sentences, one each. Naming is the first act of the inventory.
2. For each, apply the dichotomy of control as the sorting test: "Of things some are in our power, and others are not. In our power are opinion... movement towards a thing, desire, aversion... and in a word, whatever are our acts" ([Epictetus, *Encheiridion* 1, Long trans., full text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10661/10661-h/10661-h.htm)). What remains in your power in this stuck area, however small, goes on the left.
3. Take one action from the left column this week. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: the action should be smaller than feels satisfying; the inventory is a locator, not a rescue plan.

### Evidence

The empirical cousin of the sorting is locus of control, and the meta-analytic picture is modest. For specific health behaviours, "the averaged correlations with the HLOC dimensions were generally weak (r's = -.07 to .10)"; for global health appraisal, "all of the averaged correlations were statistically significant (r's = -.16 to .21), except that between P-HLOC and mental quality of life" ([Cheng, Cheung & Lo 2016, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27556686/)). A systematic review of 19 studies found that "maladaptive coping mediates the association between maladaptive locus of control and mental health problems", with the practical upshot that diminishing maladaptive control beliefs "may result in reduced maladaptive coping and, finally, improved mental health" ([Groth et al. 2019, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31430689/)).

What the studies do not test: these are correlations and mediation models on questionnaires, not trials of a written inventory; the locus-of-control construct is not the Stoic dichotomy, and the cited studies do not test this page's exercise.

### A guided version

No official recording exists. The nearest guided worksheet is the [Responsibility Pie Chart at psychology.com](https://psychology.com/tools/responsibility-pie-chart), a CBT exercise for the exact distortion this inventory counters: it has you list the practical conditions, decisions and missing information behind one stuck outcome, allocate each slice of the pie an estimated contribution, then check whether your own share is really what self-blame first said it was, and finish with one proportionate action within your control.

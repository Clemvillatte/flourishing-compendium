> **Kernel K313** — Project alignment

List the projects that currently structure your life (career, relationship, creative work, community involvement). For each, ask: does this project express who I want to be? The question is Heidegger's: our projects are not things we do but the way we are. Research on eudaimonic well-being (Ryff) identifies purposeful engagement as a core dimension of flourishing. The source of the first claim is explicit in Being and Time: "projecting has nothing to do with comporting oneself towards a plan that has been thought out, and in accordance with which Dasein arranges its Being. On the contrary, any Dasein has, as Dasein, already projected itself; and as long as it is, it is projecting" ([Heidegger, Being and Time, Macquarrie and Robinson trans., p. 185](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.185.html)). Dimensions: cognition (the inventory and the comparison) and self (the "who I want to be" the projects are tested against).

### Protocol

1. Generate the inventory: list every project that currently claims your hours in a typical month, from the paid job to the care duties to the side work. The list is the raw material; nothing on it is judged yet.
2. Select the comparison: for each project, write one sentence naming who it expresses, and one sentence naming who you are trying to become. Two sentences per project, no more, keeps the exercise honest and finishable.
3. Retain the verdicts: keep, reshape, or close. A project stays if the two sentences point the same way; it gets a deadline for reshaping if they point apart but could converge; it goes on a closing list if they cannot.
4. Act on one verdict per month: a reshaped commitment, a renegotiated term, or an announced exit. The review changes the calendar first; whether the person follows is what next month's inventory shows.

### Evidence

The philosophical anchor, verbatim: "as projecting, understanding is the kind of Being of Dasein in which it is its possibilities as possibilities" ([Heidegger, Being and Time p. 185](https://www.beyng.com/pages/en/BeingandTimeMR/BeingandTimeMR.185.html)). The psychological anchor: Ryff's consolidation of prior theories of positive functioning, in which "aspects of well-being derived from this literature (i.e., self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, and personal growth) were operationalized", with purpose in life defined so that "one who functions positively has goals, intentions, and a sense of direction, all of which contribute to the feeling that life is meaningful" ([Ryff, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 1989](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf)). Scope limits: Ryff's dimensions are measured by self-report scales, and her framework does not establish that this monthly review improves well-being. Heidegger's "project" (Entwurf) is an existential structure, not a recommendation: his discussion concerns understanding oneself through possibilities, so this kernel is a practical adaptation, not an exercise prescribed by Heidegger.

> **Kernel K257** — Habituation by design

Choose one virtue and design a thirty-day habituation plan around it, built from daily micro-actions so small that skipping feels absurd. If the virtue is justice, the action might be: notice one unfairness each day and do one small thing about it. [[T002 — Aristotle|Aristotle]]'s insight is that we become just by doing just acts; the design is an editorial attempt to make the doing routine. Reach for it when a virtue you admire exists only in your opinions.

### Protocol

The practice moves the Overt behaviour and Motivation dimensions above all: what is done daily, and the wish that the doing gradually becomes.

1. Pick one virtue and translate it into a single daily act that takes under five minutes. Write the act as a sentence you could not misread on a bad morning.
2. Anchor the act to a fixed context, the same time, place or preceding event each day. Editorial addition drawing on the habit research: repeat the act in the same situation, and let the context share the remembering; the review describes habit and deliberate pursuit as working together, not as rivals.
3. Run it for thirty days and expect the act to feel mechanical for most of them. Editorial addition, not from the carriers: the plan is a training scaffold, not a test of commitment; if a day is missed, the next act is the whole response.

### Evidence

The philosophical ground, in Ross's translation:

> "For the things we have to learn before we can do them, we learn by doing them, e.g. men become builders by building and lyreplayers by playing the lyre; so too we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts."
> — [Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics* II.1 (W. D. Ross, trans.) — full text](http://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)

The psychological mechanism, from the review of habit research: "habits form as people pursue goals by repeating the same responses in a given context", and "habits are the efficient, default mode of response" ([Wood & Rünger 2016, PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26361052/)). What the carriers do not test: no study tests thirty-day virtue plans, and Aristotle's claim about becoming just is a philosophical thesis, not a measured outcome.

### A guided version

None located; the plan is a written scaffold rather than a guided exercise. The search's nearest neighbours turned out to train different mechanisms: exposure-habituation worksheets for anxiety (staying in a feared situation until the wave falls) and meditations for interrupting habitual reactions, neither of which is the daily repetition of a chosen act this page prescribes.

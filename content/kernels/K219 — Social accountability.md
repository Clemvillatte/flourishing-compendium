> **Kernel K219** — Social accountability

Tell one person about the goal or practice, and let them ask you about it. The direct evidence for this is Gail Matthews's goal-setting study at Dominican University of California, where the group that sent a friend weekly progress reports outperformed every other group.

### Protocol

*The steps below are the compendium's editorial protocol, patterned on the Matthews study's most effective condition.*

1. Choose one person who will receive the commitment: a friend, not an enforcer.
2. Write the goal and the specific actions down, and send that written commitment to the person.
3. Send a short progress report every week, whether the week went well or badly; the reporting is the mechanism, not the good news.

### Evidence

In the study, 267 participants from businesses and networking groups were recruited and randomly assigned to five conditions ranging from an unwritten goal to written goals, action commitments shared with a friend, and weekly progress reports; 149 completed the four weeks and rated their own progress. The finding: "The positive effect of accountability was supported: those who sent weekly progress reports to their friend accomplished significantly more than those who had unwritten goals, wrote their goals, formulated action commitments or sent those action commitments to a friend," with mean goal-achievement scores of 7.6 for the weekly-report group against 4.28 for the unwritten-goal group, and the study's own summary reading: "This study provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness of three coaching tools: accountability, commitment and writing down one's goals" ([Matthews, Dominican University of California](https://www.dominican.edu/sites/default/files/2020-02/gailmatthews-harvard-goals-researchsummary.pdf)). What the study does not establish: effects beyond four weeks, goals other than self-rated work and life goals, or any well-being outcome; and the popular "76% versus 43%" retellings convert the study's rating scale into percentages the research summary itself does not state.

### A guided version

Not applicable: the practice is a standing appointment with a person, not a recording.

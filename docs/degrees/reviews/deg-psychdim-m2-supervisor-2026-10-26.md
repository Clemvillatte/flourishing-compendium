**Teach and assess cultural function as distinct from cultural influence.**

**functions**. The source route and Amira example chiefly teach how a
display rule influences one person's expression. The rubric then accepts
a “cultural influence,” which is a different explanatory direction.
Noba defines social/cultural functions as the effects emotions have on
the functioning and maintenance of societies/cultures; its closing
social/cultural paragraph connects culturally moderated emotions with
shared norms and coordination.
([Noba, social/cultural section and vocabulary](https://nobaproject.com/modules/functions-of-emotions))

This needs a short clarification, not a new cultural-psychology unit:

- Distinguish **a norm shaping expression in an episode** from **a
  possible role patterned emotion/expression plays in sustaining shared
  expectations or coordination**.
- Extend the fictional Amira example with one explicitly hypothetical
  cultural function and a possible cost. Do not equate maintenance of
  a norm with justice or require compliance; the existing ethical
  qualification is worth preserving.
- Ask for that distinction in the functions practice, assessment and
  rubric. A learner should not pass this outcome merely by naming a
  workplace rule.

The current Noba assignment stops before the most direct closing
function paragraph. The producer can teach the bounded distinction in
its own accurately sourced prose or adjust a precise source selection;
there is no need to assign the whole article. Avoid importing the
source's strongest social-order rhetoric as an uncontested empirical
law.

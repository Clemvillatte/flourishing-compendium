## Decision

Accept the author's free practice page and downloadable kit as a bounded
optional teaching resource for M6. The page supplies instructions and a
review procedure, and the kit supplies an actual written exercise;
these are usable teaching materials rather than a paper merely reporting
that materials were developed ([WOOP practice page](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice);
[English kit](https://woopmylife.org/s/WOOP-kit-EN-PDF.zip)).

The KU worksheet remains a possible alternative form, with its existing
qualification: its PLAN prompt asks for if/then statements, but the two
examples print ordinary intention sentences. Prefer the author's
Written WOOP Guide, page 4, which supplies the conditional structure
explicitly ([KU worksheet](https://learningandwriting.ku.edu/sites/writingandlearning/files/images/2024/Guides/GoalSetting/WOOP%20METHOD.pdf);
[author's kit](https://woopmylife.org/s/WOOP-kit-EN-PDF.zip)).

This closes the narrow nomenclature blocker. Oettingen identifies WOOP
as mental contrasting with implementation intentions, combining the
desired-future/obstacle sequence with if-then planning; using WOOP as a
synonym for mental contrasting alone would omit the planning component
([Oettingen's first-person explanation](https://psychwire.com/free-resources/q-and-a/1ux8o97/the-science-of-motivation)).

The broader D1 row stays PARTIAL. A short optional exercise does not
establish the instructional depth, feedback, goal-setting coverage or
assessment alignment needed for full-row or whole-degree approval.
Standalone research articles remain evidence companions; this decision
does not admit them as substitutes for the teaching layer.

## Evidence limits that change the teaching

The 2021 meta-analysis pooled 24 independent effect sizes from 21
studies, reporting Hedges' g = 0.336, 95% CI [0.229, 0.443]; its
trim-and-fill adjustment was smaller, g = 0.242, 95% CI [0.143, 0.342].
These are pooled estimates across studies, not an individual's
probability of success or a percentage increase in achievement
([Wang, Wang and Gai, Results](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)).

The same review's intervention-style comparison is a between-study
moderator finding. It does not by itself establish that adding a
facilitator causes the difference, and the authors caution about the
small study pool and covarying moderators
([meta-analysis, Moderator Analysis and Discussion](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)).

A 2025 cluster-randomised trial in 28 UK workplaces, with 225 staff at
baseline, found no substantive improvement in its primary four-week
self-reported goal-progress outcome: mean difference −0.19 on a
1–7 scale, 95% credible interval [−1.08, 0.71]. The authors also
reported increased anxiety as a secondary outcome and discussed
external constraints, timing and implementation; these explanations
should remain interpretations rather than proven causal mediators
([Kudrna and colleagues, Abstract, Methods, Results and Conclusions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11941803/)).

That trial did not achieve its target sample size, lost participants
to follow-up and relied on self-reported quantitative outcomes. It
provides a reason to avoid universal benefit or harmlessness claims;
it does not establish that WOOP invariably harms people
([trial, Participant Flow and Conclusions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11941803/)).

The recency check found a 2026 academic-procrastination trial with 81
undergraduates. The opened PubMed abstract reports reduced task
aversiveness and greater willingness to start relative to positive
thinking, through a one-week follow-up; it does not establish
completed coursework, grades or durable clinical benefit, and the
full article was not read here
([Zhou and colleagues, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41601124/)).

The opened publisher abstract for a separate 2026 physical-activity
paper reports no statistically significant intervention effects in
two trials, with final samples of 59 students and 112 gym participants.
This is an abstract-level check, not a read of the full paper or a
finding that every possible effect is exactly zero
([Kędra and Łaguna, publisher abstract](https://cipp.ug.edu.pl/Does-mental-contrasting-with-implementation-intentions-MCII-help-to-increase-physical,225999,0,2.html)).

The teaching therefore separates learning the procedure from testing
its usefulness in a particular life. It asks the learner to recognise
external limits and to allow revision, postponement or disengagement,
consistent with the author's feasibility guidance
([WOOP practice FAQ](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice);
[NYU research statement](https://as.nyu.edu/faculty/gabriele-oettingen.html)).

## Required repairs

- **Verify the assigned video content, not just its existence.** The
  module's own receipt says “coverage basis the episode description.”
  Description and oEmbed establish neither what is taught in the assigned
  episode nor coverage of the module's outcomes. Obtain a real transcript
  or inspect the recording, then log the actual coverage and bounded
  assignment. If content verification cannot be completed, record that
  status and choose a verified substitute or a documented reading
  fallback; do not retain “verified-video” as an achieved content status.
  This is missing evidence, not a finding that the episode is unsuitable.
  ([Assigned recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ksLO4i5pIBM))
- **Distinguish two kinds of practical consequence.** The examples chiefly
  catalogue how holding “I cannot meditate” or “asking for help makes me
  look incompetent” affects the believer's avoidance, mood and evidence
  gathering. Those can be legitimate observations about habits, but they
  do not by themselves clarify the proposition's meaning. Teach learners
  also to ask what experiences or observations would be expected under
  specified conditions **if the proposition were true**, and what would
  differ if it were not. Carry the distinction through the worked
  example, faded task, independent deliverable and rubric.
  ([Peirce's maxim and examples](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/How_to_Make_Our_Ideas_Clear);
  [SEP §2.1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pragmatism/))
- **Do not turn clarification into a truth verdict.** The fully worked
  answer says there is “no further fact of the matter” behind the listed
  effects, and the rubric demands a verdict of that kind. This exceeds
  what the exercise has established. Peirce ends the same essay by saying
  ideas “may be ever so clear without being true.” Replace the mandatory
  verdict with a clear account of the proposition's conceivable practical
  content and what remains to investigate. The learner should not have
  to accept a conflation to pass.
  ([Peirce, concluding paragraph](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/How_to_Make_Our_Ideas_Clear))
- **Remove the unsupported generalization in the meditation example.**
  “The learning curve is steep early” is presented as an empirical fact
  without an opened supporting source in the assignment. It is not needed
  to teach the maxim: remove it or recast the whole case explicitly as
  fictional observations about this learner. Do not add a new meditation
  science unit merely to save an incidental sentence.

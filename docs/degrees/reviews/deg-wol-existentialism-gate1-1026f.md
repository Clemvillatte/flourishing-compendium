## Centering-clause assessment

The scope states a bounded personal-learning purpose, not merely a thinker
list: the intended learner is "walking the existential path", and the exit
capabilities end in application ("Can apply existential thinking to their own
life structure (values, choices, meaning)") plus a described therapy
tradition. That is a Walk-of-Life shape. The value proposition is served
directly by the therapy half (the USFQ anchor's own outcome names
alleviating unavoidable suffering; logotherapy is meaning-under-suffering
work) and by the philosophy half's themes (freedom, responsibility, meaning,
anxiety, death are the uncertain-or-overwhelmed condition the value
proposition names). On the retrieved scope text, centering is defensible.
This is an assessment, not a gate clearance (see verdict).

## Finite-scope rulings (the strategist's three open questions)

1. **Phenomenology exclusion: CONFIRMED as intended, as a module-design
   condition.** The clause trims real anchor core (UCLA's Division Two
   apparatus; UNO's Husserl week), but the scope's exit capabilities do not
   require Dasein-analysis technique, and the finite-scope rule supports the
   trim. Condition: being-toward-death and authenticity content must be
   carried as themes (anchor-supported) without re-importing
   phenomenology-as-technique through passing mentions.
2. **Therapy evidence-base-and-limits clause: BLOCKING GAP.** The retrieved
   material from the two therapy anchors (USFQ, HUJI) did not establish
   coverage of existential therapy's evidence base or limits. Under skill
   section 1.3, an essential row that cannot be filled with a verified
   resource goes on the degree's gap register as a blocking gap, not a quiet
   omission: and an unanchored "walk's own framing" cannot substitute for
   evidence behind factual claims about effectiveness or limits. Gate 1
   cannot clear until this row is either anchored by an opened, verified
   source or the exit capability and its claims are removed from the scope.
3. **Binswanger/Boss elective: RECOMMEND EXCLUSION from the required
   walk.** The support (Rutgers names the tradition, not the men; HEPI names
   them with hours) is thin, and the scope's exit capabilities make no use of
   Binswanger/Boss: no stated Walk-of-Life contribution follows from them.
   Under the finite-scope rule (when in doubt, exclude), the required
   recommendation is exclusion from this walk; the tradition may be noted in
   one line as a possible future extension only if a module design shows a
   concrete Walk-of-Life contribution. Final say sits with the degree owner
   and supervisor.

## A teaching chapter is an allowed family, but needs care

Michelle Yarwood's chapter is a genuine university teaching text,
not an encyclopedia entry. The book's landing page names her as
author and Pennsylvania State University's Affordable Course
Transformation as publisher; the institution's biography confirms
her experimental-psychology PhD and teaching in human emotion
([book information](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/);
[Penn State biography](https://roam.libraries.psu.edu/author/michelle-yarwood)).

That establishes the resource family. It does not settle whether
the chapter's particular explanations should be assigned without
a correction guide, so no replacement chapter assignment is added.
The degree's broaden-and-build row remains PARTIAL.

The chapter explains the global/local task and a thought-action
listing task with concrete examples. Its figures and prose sometimes
call the latter “behaviors,” whereas the original participants
listed what they would like to do; completed real-world behavior
was not that measure
([chapter, Evidence for the Broaden Hypothesis](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/);
[original methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)).

The original paper used one-tailed planned contrasts. Amusement
and contentment did not separately produce equally clear results,
and the negative-emotion comparisons were also limited: in
Experiment 2, anger versus neutral was p = .052 and anxiety
versus neutral was p = .162; in Experiment 1 neither negative
condition significantly differed from neutral
([Fredrickson and Branigan, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)).

Those findings qualify the chapter's sentence that the anger clip
“showed greater narrowing.” A learner should distinguish the
direction of a sample mean from the evidence for a contrast,
rather than reading every plotted difference as an established
effect
([chapter](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/);
[original Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)).

The build section has additional problems for an unassisted
assignment. Its eight-week intervention description differs from
the original study's seven-week workshop and nine-week reporting
window; more importantly, the original study explicitly states
that it did not directly assess momentary broadened cognition
([teaching chapter](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/);
[original study, empirical strategy and limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156028/)).
A causal flow diagram therefore needs to distinguish the proposed
sequence from the variables actually measured.

## How this changes the next teaching task

Keep the useful learning question: what might change in the
possibilities someone notices during a pleasant experience, and
what evidence would be needed to connect that moment with
enduring resources? UNC's own explanation presents the immediate
expanded-awareness claim and accumulated-resources claim as
parts of the theory
([PEP Lab theory statement](https://peplab.web.unc.edu/research/)).

A later experimental paper found visual broadening in one
experiment but no between-group difference in the number of
thought-action urges in another. The result challenges treating
the entire broaden-then-build sequence as a single established
mechanism
([Jäger and Rüsseler, Abstract and general discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5078759/)).

A systematic review of positive mood and visual attention also
describes context-dependent effects and considers changes in
selectivity or processing style as alternatives to a simple
uniform expansion. Its abstract and opening theoretical sections
were inspected here; the truncated retrieval does not support a
claim that its full results or reference list were reviewed
([Vanlessen and colleagues, 2016](https://www.cap-lab.be/files/modules/products/11/Vanlessen_Happymood&Attention_NBR2016.pdf)).

The finite-scope decision is to stop adding candidates at this
point. A future replacement should teach the theory in a bounded
sequence, check a measurement against the claim, and preserve
room for distress without turning pleasant emotion into a
requirement of a worthwhile life.

# Pilot (theory): mod-phil-1 Foundations of Virtue

Phase 0 pilot applying the curriculum-design skill to one theory module.
Purpose: test the standard, not to ship a final module. The module design
below is what the deg-philosophy owner will produce for every module once
this pilot passes advisor review.

## Audit of the current module (facts from fcAcademyData)

- 3 outcomes, 3 required media (AR-01, AR-04, AR-06), 1 optional (ST-03),
  1 wiki reading, activity and rubric present, 4 hours claimed.
- Level labels attached to media: two "Foundations", one "Intermediate" on
  a Foundations module: mixed vocabulary inside one module.
- AR-01 (Sandel, Justice episode 10) and AR-04 (Gresham, Aristotle) both
  carry an EMPTY duration field and verification_status "imported". Under
  the skill's verification standard this module cannot ship as-is: its
  required media are unverified and unschedulable (no printed length).
- The outcomes are definitional (L1); the activity asks for application to
  a personal dilemma (L2/L3). The rubric grades accuracy and clarity but
  no outcome asks the learner to USE the distinction between ethical
  theories, so the third outcome (distinguish the three approaches) is
  taught but never assessed.

## Redesign to the skill

Outcomes (exit criteria, L1 into early L2):

1. Define virtue, vice, eudaimonia and phronesis in your own words and
   give one example of each from your own life.
2. Explain, in a paragraph a peer could follow, why Aristotle ties virtue
   to flourishing rather than to rule-following or outcomes.
3. Take one real dilemma and analyse it twice, once as a deontologist and
   once as a consequentialist, then state what virtue ethics adds that the
   two miss.

Instruction (each outcome taught):

- Outcome 1: the wiki reading (Book I) plus AR-04 (Gresham Aristotle) as the
  definitional lecture, assigned as a whole talk once duration is printed.
- Outcome 2: AR-01 (Sandel Justice episode 10) as the comparative lecture;
  Sandel's format is exactly the three-way comparison the outcome needs.
- Outcome 3: worked example in the module page itself (a short dilemma
   analysed all three ways, faded: the first analysis fully worked, the
   second with steps named, the third left to the learner).

Practice (each outcome practised):

- Retrieval activity 1: define the four terms from memory before re-reading
  (spaced: repeated at module midpoint).
- Activity 2 kept from the current design (300-word personal dilemma) but
  now attached to outcome 3 and preceded by the worked example.
- Interleaved review: one question recalling mod-phil prerequisites
  (none yet; this is the entry module) and one looking forward to Stoicism
  (next module).

Assessment (rubric rewritten to observable criteria):

- The dilemma analysis names the theory it is using at each step (pass or
  redo).
- Definitions match the wiki reading's usage, not everyday usage.
- The "what virtue ethics adds" paragraph must state at least one thing the
  other two analyses missed, in the learner's own case.

Workload (honest arithmetic):

- Reading and wiki: about 1.5 hours (estimate, labelled).
- Lectures: UNKNOWABLE until AR-01 and AR-04 durations are printed on
  their source pages (blocking gap, routed to deg-verify).
- Activity and assessment: about 1 hour (estimate).
- Current 4-hour claim is therefore unsupported arithmetic; the redesigned
  total will be computed once durations print.

Verification status of attached sources:

- AR-01, AR-04, AR-06, ST-03: all "imported", all routed to deg-verify
  Block A with this module's needs noted (Sandel episode must actually
  cover the three approaches; the Gresham talk must actually introduce
  eudaimonia; classify from the recording, not the title).

## What this pilot demonstrates

The skill catches real defects: mixed level labels, unverified required
media, an unassessed outcome, and a workload total with no arithmetic
behind it. The fixes are mechanical once verification returns durations.

# Scope: deg-wol-daoism — A Walk of Life: Daoism

WALK OF LIFE series (owner directive, 2026-10-25). Status: PROPOSAL by the
strategist, mapped onto existing module material; the degree owner must
benchmark it against opened authoritative curricula before the scope gate.

## Intended learner

A learner walking the Daoist path as a way of living: its texts, its ethics and its practices, without academic apparatus.

## Entry competencies

- Curiosity about Daoist practice; no classical Chinese or prior training assumed.

## Exit capabilities

- Can read the core texts (Daodejing, Zhuangzi) with guidance and apply them to daily life.
- Can practise the basics of the Daoist arts of living (simplicity, non-forcing, nature alignment) and explain their rationale.
- Can write a personal synthesis of the walk against one serious objection.

## Bounded domain

Covers:
- The core texts read as practice manuals for living.
- Daoist ethics and the ideal of the sage.
- The living tradition's practices (cross-linked to the qigong practicum for the body side).

Excludes (with reasons):
- Metaphysical and arcane cosmology (owner rule: practical, pragmatic content).
- Religious Daoist ritual and liturgy.

Elective branches:
- The Neiye (inward training) as an elective deep dive; Daoist arts of the body via the qigong practicum.

## Raw material available (measured 2026-10-25)

deg-contemplative modules 1 and 2 (Daoist foundations and practice) plus their media; the qigong practicum supplies the practice side.

## Benchmark against opened authoritative curricula (Gate-1 groundwork)

Anchors opened by the strategist, 2026-10-25 (ledger: docs/degrees/ledgers/deg-wol-daoism.md):

1. Warwick PH387-15, Daoism: The foundational texts — https://courses.warwick.ac.uk/modules/2021/PH387-15 (aims and full outline syllabus). A philosophy module: political readings of the Daodejing (Hanfeizi, norms/knowledge), Zhuangzi on skill and wu wei, the Art of War, and Zhuangzi's environmental ethics. No religious or ritual Daoism.
2. Wabash Center RELS 318 syllabus (Fischer) — https://wabashcenter.wabash.edu/storage/assets/syllabi/Daoism-Fischer.pdf. Covers the philosophical core (Laozi, Zhuangzi with primary and secondary scholarship) and weeks on religious Daoism: Celestial Master and Quanzhen monasticism, meditation, and a source-based project assessment.
3. University of Florida CHT3513, Taoism and Chinese Culture (Wang, Fall 2022) — https://languages.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/119/Fall22_CHT3513_Wang.pdf. Full-spectrum survey: Daodejing/Zhuangzi first, then Han cosmology, Celestial Master, Shangqing, Lingbao, Thunder Rites, Quanzhen, Zhengyi, ritual, sacred space, women in Daoism, festivals, Daoist art.

Comparison:

- Core alignment: all three anchors treat the Daodejing and Zhuangzi as the core; the scope's core-text focus matches. The Zhuangzi skill stories and wu wei appear at Warwick as philosophical themes, consistent with the scope's core-text and ethics coverage.
- Deliberate exclusion: the scope excludes religious Daoist ritual and liturgy under the owner rule (practical, pragmatic content). The anchors go further than that line: Wabash teaches the Celestial Master and Quanzhen traditions (religious history, monasticism), and UF teaches the full tradition including Thunder Rites, ritual, festivals, sacred space and monastic life. The scope's bounded domain still covers "the living tradition's practices", so non-ritual religious Daoist material is not excluded by the exclusion line — but neither anchor's religious-tradition teaching is currently mapped to scope content. Recorded as an anchor challenge, not silently. As a curricular choice (not an established equivalence), the walk proposes the qigong practicum and the Neiye elective to carry the practice side; whether that substitution is adequate is a question for the scope gate's independent review.
- Warwick's political-reading angle (Hanfeizi, the Art of War, environmental ethics) is absent from this scope. Left out: the walk is aimed at a life-practice learner, not a political-philosophy student. If the owner wants the political Daodejing it can slot in as an elective branch; recorded here so the omission is a choice, not a blind spot.
- The Neiye elective is the scope's proposed non-ritual bridge into contemplative material. The closest anchor material is the Wabash syllabus's assigned scholarship on early Daoist mystical praxis and its Quanzhen readings on asceticism and the cultivation of health and longevity; whether the elective's fit to that material is adequate is a question for the scope gate's independent review.

Strategist's note (non-binding): the benchmark supports the bounded domain as drawn; the two gaps worth the owner's eye are the exclusion of religious Daoist ritual and liturgy (owner rule, challenge recorded) and the absence of the political Daodejing strand (Warwick).

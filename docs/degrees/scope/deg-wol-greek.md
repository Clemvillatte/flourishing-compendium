# Scope: deg-wol-greek — A Walk of Life: Greek Philosophy

WALK OF LIFE series (owner directive, 2026-10-25). Status: PROPOSAL by the
strategist, mapped onto existing module material; the degree owner must
benchmark it against opened authoritative curricula before the scope gate.

## Intended learner

A learner walking the Greek path to the examined life: Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, the Stoics and the Hellenistic schools as guides for living.

## Entry competencies

- No philosophy background; reads translated texts with guidance.

## Exit capabilities

- Can read the core Greek texts with guidance and map their practical claims.
- Can live the examination: apply Socratic questioning, Aristotelian virtue and Stoic practice to daily decisions.
- Can write a defended personal synthesis against one serious objection.

## Bounded domain

Covers:
- Socrates and the examined life; Plato's practical dialogues.
- Aristotle: virtue, eudaimonia, phronesis.
- Stoicism ancient and modern as a lived practice.
- Hellenistic alternatives: Epicureanism and Skepticism as practical rivals.

Excludes (with reasons):
- Formal logic; academic metaphysics and epistemology beyond practical needs.
- Non-Greek traditions (their own walks).

Elective branches:
- Cynics as a radical elective; Greek political thought where it bears on the lived polity.

## Raw material available (measured 2026-10-25)

deg-philosophy modules 1 to 3 (virtue, Stoicism, Hellenistic) plus their 3+4+3 attachments; wiki philosophy dossiers and kernels.

## Scope benchmark (deg-wol-greek owner, 2026-10-25)

Benchmarked against three opened authoritative curricula (URLs and evidence
in ledgers/deg-wol-greek.md):

1. MIT OpenCourseWare 24.200 Ancient Philosophy (Haslanger, Fall 2004),
   lecture calendar: Socrates and the Sophists, Socratic method, Plato on
   the soul, forms, why be moral, justice in city and soul, happiness;
   Aristotle on happiness, virtue, weakness of will, friendship,
   contemplation; Epicurean atomism, morality, death.
2. Yale Summer Session PHIL S125E Intro to Ancient Philosophy (2024),
   syllabus: pre-Socratics, Sophists, Socrates, Plato (Apology,
   Euthyphro, Crito, Gorgias, Protagoras, Meno, Republic, Phaedo),
   Aristotle (Nicomachean Ethics, De Anima, Physics), Epicurus (Principal
   Doctrines, Letter to Menoeceus), Seneca (Letters).
3. Stanford PHIL100 The History of Ancient Greek Philosophy (course
   description, Stanford Bulletin): major developments in Greek
   philosophical thought, focusing on Plato, Aristotle, and the
   Hellenistic schools (Epicureans, Stoics, Skeptics).

Findings for the scope gate (strategist owns the gate):

- The bounded domain matches the anchors' practical core: Socrates and
  the examined life, Plato's ethical and political dialogues, Aristotle's
  ethics, and the Hellenistic schools as practical rivals. The scope's
  exclusions (formal logic; metaphysics and epistemology beyond practical
  needs) cover the anchors' theoretical material (Forms, matter and form,
  knowledge, nature and causation) except as noted below.
- Coverage rows the matrix must carry (anchor topics inside the scope):
  Socratic method and the examined life; Plato on why be moral and
  justice; Aristotle on happiness, virtue, weakness of will (akrasia),
  friendship, contemplation; Epicurean morality and death; Stoic ethics;
  Skepticism as a practical stance. The wiki's Book I already covers
  akrasia (Layer V), friendship (Layer VI), contemplation (Layer VIII).
- Open question for the strategist or owner: the Yale syllabus
  explicitly includes the pre-Socratics, and the scope excludes them
  without a stated reason (the MIT calendar and the Stanford description
  do not establish the point either way). Either state the reason (they
  are not guides for living in the walk's sense) or add an elective row.
- The Cynics elective is consistent with the anchors (none of the three
  requires them).

# Scope: deg-wol-buddhism — A Walk of Life: Buddhism

WALK OF LIFE series (owner directive, 2026-10-25). Status: PROPOSAL by the
strategist, mapped onto existing module material. Benchmark groundwork
done 2026-10-25 (see the benchmark section below and the degree ledger);
Gate 1 remains open; independent review required.

## Intended learner

A learner walking the Buddhist path in its practical, living schools, focused on ethics and practice.

## Entry competencies

- No Buddhist background assumed; open to practice instruction.

## Exit capabilities

- Can describe the ethical core (sila, the precepts, the four immeasurables) and the practical path in the Secular, Zen, Insight/Vipassana, Theravada and Thai Forest schools.
- Can practise the foundational meditations with guidance (bridging to the cmp practicum).
- Can apply Buddhist ethics to concrete situations and state where schools disagree on practice.

## Bounded domain

Covers:
- Ethics first: the precepts, harmlessness, compassion.
- The practical path per school: Secular, Zen, Insight/Vipassana, Theravada, Thai Forest.
- Core practice instructions and their living teachers' guidance.

Excludes (with reasons):
- Metaphysical and arcane material (owner rule: skip it; rebirth cosmology, abhidharma scholastics and tantric ritual are out of scope except where ethics or practice requires a passing mention).
- Academic Buddhist studies as a discipline.

Elective branches:
- Buddhist psychology of the mind (practical portions); monastic life accounts (Thai Forest lineage stories).

## Raw material available (measured 2026-10-25)

deg-contemplative module 3 (Buddhist principles) and the Buddhist portions of modules 4 and 5; the cmp practicum supplies the sitting practice; wiki Buddhist dossiers and kernels supply readings.

## Benchmark against opened authoritative curricula (Gate-1 groundwork)

Anchors opened by the strategist, 2026-10-25 (ledger: docs/degrees/ledgers/deg-wol-buddhism.md):

1. University of Florida REL2341, Introduction to Buddhism (Poceski, Fall 2025) — https://religion.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/194/REL-2341-Intro-to-Buddhism-Fall-2025-Poceski.pdf. Historical survey on Harvey's textbook: Buddha's life, karma and rebirth, Four Noble Truths, Mahayana/bodhisattva, Tantra, Chinese and Japanese traditions, monastic ideals and lay practice (five precepts, three refuges), a Buddhist-meditation week (calm and insight, mindfulness), modern China, Buddhism and the West. Assessed by three exams and a film review; meditation is taught as a topic, not practised for credit.
2. HKU Centre of Buddhist Studies BSTC1004, Introduction to Buddhism (2025-26) — https://www.buddhism.hku.hk/wp-content/uploads/BSTC1004-2025-26_v2.pdf. Core teachings by vehicle: Humanity Vehicle (karma, refuge, the Five Mindfulness Trainings), Śravaka Vehicle (Four Noble Truths, dependent origination, non-self), Bodhisattva Vehicle (compassion, pāramitās). Assessment includes four weekly meditation logs worth 20% — practice is graded, not just described.
3. Bond University PHIL12-203, Buddhist Philosophy (January 2025) — https://bond.edu.au/subject-outline/PHIL12-203_2025_JAN_STD_01. Practical-experience subject: historical Buddha, Four Noble Truths, then two meditation topics (calm and insight; metta, Five Hindrances, jhana factors), Mahayana vs Theravada approaches, five aggregates, karma and rebirth, in-depth Theravada and Mahayana sutta study, Zen (Chinese/Japanese/Korean), dependent origination, and Western Buddhism with application to study, work and relationships. Includes a local temple visit.

Comparison:

- Ethics-first matches the anchors' ethics content: the five precepts (UF week 11) and the Five Mindfulness Trainings (HKU lesson 5) are exactly the scope's sila-and-precepts core; Bond teaches metta practice as a curricular topic. The scope's exit capability "apply Buddhist ethics to concrete situations" is anchored by Bond's lifelong-learning topic and HKU's mindful-living lesson.
- Practice emphasis is defensible and even ahead of some anchors: HKU grades four meditation logs (20%) and Bond teaches practical calm/insight and metta meditation, so the scope's bridging to the cmp practicum sits inside the anchor range. UF teaches meditation only as a topic — the scope's stronger practice requirement is a choice, not a deviation the anchors contradict.
- Deliberate exclusion — rebirth cosmology: all three anchors teach karma and rebirth as core content (UF week 4; Bond topic 7; HKU lesson 3), and UF and Bond pair them with cosmology explicitly (UF week 4 is titled karma, rebirth and cosmology; Bond topic 7 is karma and rebirth with a cosmology line; HKU's lesson names karma and rebirth only). The scope's exclusion line is rebirth cosmology (with abhidharma scholastics and tantric ritual), not karma-and-rebirth teaching itself. Recorded as an anchor challenge, not silently. The cost is a design tradeoff for independent review: a learner completing the walk may not be able to place rebirth-dependent doctrines (for example the bodhisattva vow's multi-life reading) in their traditional frame, depending on how the passing mentions are handled at module design. The owner rule stands; the walk notes it where ethics or practice requires.
- Deliberate exclusion — abhidharma scholastics and tantric ritual: UF teaches Abhidharma literature (week 3) and Tantra (week 8); the scope's exclusion lines are abhidharma scholastics and tantric ritual, so the exclusion trims the scholastics and ritual portions, not the literature or history wholesale. No ethics/practice loss is claimed beyond the rebirth-cosmology note above.
- Divergence in school structure: the scope's school list (Secular, Zen, Insight/Vipassana, Theravada, Thai Forest) is organized by living practice communities. The anchors organize by historical vehicle (HKU's three vehicles), geography (UF's Chinese/Japanese/Western weeks) or a hybrid (Bond: Theravada/Mahayana/Zen plus Western Buddhism). No anchor teaches Secular Buddhism or the Thai Forest school as named units. The scope's list is practice-driven rather than curriculum-driven; defensible for a walk-of-life learner, but it is the scope's own structure, not one mirrored in the anchors. The closest anchor material is UF's Buddhism-and-the-West week and Bond's Western Buddhism topic, which cover Buddhism's western reception — whether they treat the secularizing turn specifically is not established by the opened outlines — and the Thai Forest lineage elective stands outside all three anchors.

Strategist's note (non-binding): the benchmark supports the bounded domain as drawn. Two items for the owner's eye: the rebirth-cosmology boundary (all three anchors teach karma and rebirth, and UF and Bond pair it with cosmology; owner rule, challenge recorded) and the school-list structure (community-driven, not anchor-mirrored — Secular Buddhism and Thai Forest have no direct anchor units).

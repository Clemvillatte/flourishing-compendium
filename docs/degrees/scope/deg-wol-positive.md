# Scope: deg-wol-positive — A Walk of Life: Positive Psychology

WALK OF LIFE series (owner directive, 2026-10-25). Status: PROPOSAL by the
strategist, mapped onto existing module material. Benchmark groundwork
done 2026-10-25 (see the benchmark section below and the degree ledger);
Gate 1 remains open; independent review required.

## Intended learner

A learner walking the positive psychology path: the science of flourishing, strengths, flow and meaning, applied to their own life.

## Entry competencies

- No psychology background; willing to run personal experiments and keep records.

## Exit capabilities

- Can evaluate wellbeing claims against their evidence (measurement, design, effect size).
- Can apply the field's core practices (strengths, flow, gratitude, meaning) with recorded outcomes.
- Can state the field's criticisms and where it overreaches.

## Bounded domain

Covers:
- Foundations of positive psychology and their critics.
- Flow and optimal experience.
- Strengths, meaning and sociocultural flourishing.

Excludes (with reasons):
- Clinical treatment of disorder (legacy material).
- Basic mechanisms of mind (owned by Psychological Dimensions).

Elective branches:
- Positive education; positive organisations.

## Raw material available (measured 2026-10-25)

deg-wellbeing's three teaching modules (foundations and critique, flow, sociocultural) plus their attachments; wiki flow, happiness and meaning dossiers.

## Benchmark against opened authoritative curricula (Gate-1 groundwork)

Anchors opened by the strategist, 2026-10-25 (ledger: docs/degrees/ledgers/deg-wol-positive.md):

1. Warwick PS378-15, Positive Psychology — https://courses.warwick.ac.uk/modules/2023/PS378-15.pdf. Outline: wellbeing, positive emotions, character strengths, positive cognition, applications, positive relationships, intervention development and application, positive institutions and society, and positive psychology as a critical perspective on psychology. Assessed by five reflections, an 800-word intervention proposal with poster session, and an online exam; learning outcomes include critically evaluating the methods and research behind the field's core ideas.
2. University of Pennsylvania Positive Psychology Center syllabus (Rashid) — https://ppc.sas.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/ppsyllabusrashid.pdf. Experiential survey of character strengths and virtues: happiness, flow (with a write-your-own-flow-experiences activity), subjective well-being, hope, optimism, creativity, wisdom, spirituality, love, gratitude, forgiveness, altruism, positive psychology at work, cross-cultural positive psychology. No exams; assessed by participation, fifteen reaction papers, a Learned Optimism book review and a polish-one-strength project.
3. PSYC 062, Positive Psychology (Fredrickson, Fall 2018) — https://positiveorgs.bus.umich.edu/wp-content/uploads/PSYC062-Fredrickson-Syllabus-Fall-2018.pdf. Syllabus hosted by the University of Michigan's Center for Positive Organizations. First-year seminar built around the field's theories (Find-Remind-and-Bind gratitude, Positive Activity Model, Broaden-and-Build, Upward Spiral, Positivity Resonance) and a real course research project: the class designs positive-intervention studies, collects four weeks of data, analyses it in SPSS and writes a final research paper interpreting whether, for whom and how an intervention is effective. Includes VIA survey, Positivity Portfolio, Reflected Best Selves, learning journal, meditation.

Comparison:

- Practice with recorded outcomes is the anchors' own norm: Rashid's class runs VIA pre-tests, flow-writing, gratitude letters and a strength-polishing project; Fredrickson's students keep a weekly learning journal and run a four-week data-collection intervention study; Warwick's students submit five reflections and design an assessed intervention. The scope's exit capability — apply core practices with recorded outcomes — sits squarely inside the anchor range.
- Evidence evaluation is anchor-supported at the strongest end: Fredrickson's stated outcome (analyse empirical tests, interpret whether, for whom and how an intervention is effective) and Warwick's critical-evaluation learning outcome match the scope's first exit capability directly. The anchors support empirical evaluation generally; explicit effect-size instruction is not established in the opened extracts and remains a proposed scope emphasis.
- Criticisms: no anchor names a criticisms-of-the-field unit. Warwick's outline includes "positive psychology as a critical perspective on psychology" — positive psychology offered as a critical lens on the discipline — and the page does not explicitly state that criticisms of positive psychology are covered; its learning outcomes do, however, require critically understanding positive psychology and evaluating the methods and research used to develop its core ideas. Rashid and Fredrickson name no criticisms unit. The scope's "state the field's criticisms and where it overreaches" is therefore anchored only in the weaker sense of critical evaluation of the field's methods and research (Warwick's outcomes); the explicit criticisms framing is the walk's own emphasis, recorded as a divergence, not as anchor-supported.
- Flow: Rashid devotes a full class to flow with the Nakamura & Csikszentmihalyi Handbook chapter and a personal flow exercise; Warwick's outline and Fredrickson's schedule do not name flow as a unit. The scope's flow pillar (from deg-wellbeing module 2 and the wiki flow dossier) is anchored by Penn, not by all three. Recorded as a partial-coverage item, not a challenge — flow is uncontroversially in the field's canon.
- Sociocultural flourishing: well anchored — Rashid's cross-cultural class, Warwick's positive institutions and society, Fredrickson's positive organizations unit.
- Exclusions: the scope excludes clinical treatment of disorder and basic mechanisms of mind. Fredrickson includes biological approaches to positive psychology and meditation — a mild overlap with the mechanisms exclusion, but framed as applications rather than basic-mechanisms teaching; no anchor devotes core time to treating disorder, so the clinical exclusion costs nothing against these anchors.

Strategist's note (non-binding): the benchmark supports the bounded domain as drawn. The two items for the owner's eye: the explicit criticisms-of-the-field framing is not named by any of the three anchors (Warwick's outcomes anchor critical evaluation of the field's methods and research, which is the closest support), and flow is a named pillar in the scope but a full unit in only one anchor (Penn).

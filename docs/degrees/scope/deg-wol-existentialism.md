# Scope: deg-wol-existentialism — A Walk of Life: Existentialism and Existential Therapy

WALK OF LIFE series (owner directive, 2026-10-25). Status: PROPOSAL by the
strategist, mapped onto existing module material. Benchmark groundwork
done 2026-10-25 (see the benchmark section below and the degree ledger);
Gate 1 remains open; independent review required.

## Intended learner

A learner walking the existential path: freedom, responsibility, meaning, anxiety and death, first as philosophy and then as therapy.

## Entry competencies

- No philosophy or clinical background assumed.

## Exit capabilities

- Can explain the core existential themes (freedom, responsibility, meaning, isolation, anxiety, death) across Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Heidegger, Sartre, Camus and Frankl.
- Can apply existential thinking to their own life structure (values, choices, meaning).
- Can describe existential therapy (Yalom, May, Frankl's logotherapy) and its evidence base and limits.

## Bounded domain

Covers:
- The existential philosophers read as guides to living.
- Existential therapy: its methods, its evidence and its limits.
- Meaning-centred work (Frankl) as the applied bridge.

Excludes (with reasons):
- Phenomenology as technical philosophy (only what the walk needs).
- Other clinical approaches (legacy deg-therapeutic material stays legacy).

Elective branches:
- Existential psychiatry (Binswanger, Boss); absurdism and living without ground.

## Raw material available (measured 2026-10-25)

deg-philosophy module 5 (Existentialism and Phenomenology) plus its 4 attachments; existential therapy content must be newly curated (not currently in the Academy).

## Benchmark against opened authoritative curricula (Gate-1 groundwork)

Anchors opened by the strategist, 2026-10-25 (ledger: docs/degrees/ledgers/deg-wol-existentialism.md):

1. Stanford Philosophy 78, Hands-On Existentialism (Sabol) — https://web.stanford.edu/~jsabol/existentialism/schedule.html. Ten-session schedule: Kierkegaard (faith, subjective truth), Nietzsche (diagnosis of culture, life as a work of art), Heidegger (being and death), Camus (the absurd, struggle), Sartre (freedom, other people), Beauvoir (ethics).
2. UCLA PHILOS 177A, Existentialism (Molina-Alonso) — https://philosophy.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/PHILOS-177A-Syllabus-Molina-Alonso.pdf. Kierkegaard and Nietzsche as precursors, then a deep Heidegger arc (Being and Time divisions one and two, Dasein, being-toward-death) and Sartre (Existentialism is a Humanism, Being and Nothingness); Beauvoir and Fanon if time permits. Themes: authenticity, freedom, existence/essence, truth, mortality. Assessed by two papers (30/40) and structured participation (30).
3. University of New Orleans PHIL 3511, Existentialism — https://www.uno.edu/media/39046/download. Fifteen-week online course: Kierkegaard (anxiety, despair), Dostoevsky and Tolstoy (Notes from the Underground, The Death of Ivan Ilych), Nietzsche (Genealogy), Husserl and Heidegger (Dasein, being-toward-death), Sartre (bad faith, Nausea, existence precedes essence), de Beauvoir (Ethics of Ambiguity, The Second Sex), Camus (The Stranger, The Myth of Sisyphus). Includes "existentialism-inspired activities" and aims at the student's own vision of a good and authentic life. Assessed by Perusall readings, discussions, quizzes, two exams and a final project.
4. USFQ PSI 3114E, Existential Psychology (syllabus PDF served from George Mason's CERS export) — https://provapps.gmu.edu/CERS/exportfiles/attachment/Syllabus-PSI-3114E.pdf. Meaning-centred course built on Frankl's Logotherapy: assumptions and practices of logotherapy, death as a source of meaning, techniques for the search for meaning, responsibleness versus responsibility, goals/purposes/processes/love as meaning sources, alleviating unavoidable suffering. Learning outcome 4: students conduct their own search for meaning and document the findings; logotherapy personality exams are taken (ungraded, no correct answers). Yalom and May are not named.
5. Hebrew University course 3975, The Basics of Existential Therapy (2024 syllabus page) — https://shnaton.huji.ac.il/index.php/NewSyl/3975/2/2024/. Thirteen-session outline from existentialism's philosophical roots through logotherapy (in and out of the clinic), the humanistic approach, Yalom-style existential psychotherapy, then death, freedom and responsibility, loneliness and meaning connected to clinical use and daily life. Readings: Yalom's Existential Psychotherapy (chapters 1, 2, 8, 10), Frankl's Man's Search for Meaning (second part), Rollo May's The Discovery of Being (chapter 6), Rogers and Cohn. Assessed 90% by essay/project/final assignment.

Comparison:

- Core themes: across the philosophy anchor set, freedom, death or mortality, and authenticity are taught — the scope's theme list (freedom, responsibility, meaning, anxiety, death) matches on those (the Stanford schedule's session titles do not explicitly name authenticity; the claim rests on the anchor set, not on every anchor naming every theme). The absurd is named as a unit at Stanford (Camus I: The Absurd) and UNO (The Myth of Sisyphus); UCLA's outline names neither the absurd nor Camus. The scope's thinker list (Kierkegaard, Nietzsche, Heidegger, Sartre, Camus, Frankl) is covered across the anchor set — Camus appears at Stanford and UNO, not UCLA — and Frankl is the exception (below).
- Lived-application framing: UNO's syllabus documents "existentialism-inspired activities" and the outcome of developing one's own vision of a good and authentic life. Stanford's course is titled "Hands-On Existentialism", but the page does not state practical or experiential exercises — the title alone is not counted as evidence. The walk's apply-to-your-own-life exit capability is therefore anchored by UNO's documented activities alone among these anchors.
- Literary existentialism: UNO pairs philosophy with Dostoevsky and Tolstoy; UCLA supplements with films, poetry and literary works. The scope holds its texts as guides-to-living without specifying the literary strand; not a divergence worth the owner's time, but noted for module design.
- The therapy half is now partially anchored: the USFQ/GMU-exported syllabus makes Frankl's logotherapy its whole spine (with a documented personal meaning-search outcome), and the Hebrew University syllabus teaches existential therapy with Yalom, Frankl and May on the reading list and clinical use as its stated aim. Both support the theorists-and-methods portion of the scope's third exit capability and the meaning-centred bridge. Not covered by either syllabus: the therapy's evidence base and limits — the scope's evidence-and-limits clause remains unanchored and is the walk's own framing, and the Binswanger/Boss tradition is not taught by either (below).
- Phenomenology exclusion: UCLA devotes weeks to Being and Time's technical apparatus (Dasein analysis, temporality); UNO teaches Husserl's phenomenology before Heidegger. The scope's exclusion of phenomenology as technical philosophy trims genuine anchor core material to "only what the walk needs". Recorded as a divergence with a possible design tradeoff for independent review: a walker may finish the walk without Heidegger's Division Two apparatus, receiving only its existential fruits, depending on how module design handles the passing mentions.
- Binswanger/Boss existential psychiatry elective: now anchored, with a caveat. Two further sources opened 2026-10-25 (see ledger): Rutgers GSAPP's Existential, Phenomenological, and Humanistic Psychotherapy (Sass, Spring 2022) teaches a "Heidegger and Daseinsanalytic Therapy" class built on Cooper's Daseinsanalysis chapter, though the page does not name Binswanger or Boss; and HEPI's Existential Therapy Basic Level (Kočiūnas; an EAP-accredited training institution, not a university) explicitly allocates Ludwig Binswanger 4 hours, Medard Boss 6 hours and modern Dasein-analysis 2 hours in its theories strand. The elective therefore has anchor support for the tradition (Daseinsanalysis) and for Binswanger and Boss by name (HEPI); the support is one university course plus one accredited training programme, which is thinner than the philosophy half's support and is recorded as such.

Strategist's note (non-binding): the philosophical half is anchor-supported across the anchor set on themes and five of six thinkers, with lived-application framing anchored by UNO's documented activities. The therapy half is partially anchored (theorists and methods: USFQ logotherapy spine; Hebrew University Yalom/Frankl/May readings); its evidence-and-limits clause remains unanchored. The phenomenology exclusion trims real anchor core and should be confirmed as intended. The Binswanger/Boss elective is now anchored (Rutgers Daseinsanalytic therapy class; HEPI Binswanger/Boss hours), with the thinner-support caveat noted above.

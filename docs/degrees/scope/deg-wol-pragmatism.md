# Scope: deg-wol-pragmatism — A Walk of Life: Pragmatism

WALK OF LIFE series (owner directive, 2026-10-25). Status: PROPOSAL by the
strategist, mapped onto existing module material; the degree owner must
benchmark it against opened authoritative curricula before the scope gate.

## Intended learner

A learner walking the pragmatist path: truth as what works in experience, ideas as instruments, democracy and inquiry as ways of living.

## Entry competencies

- Comfortable reading structured argument; no prior philosophy assumed.

## Exit capabilities

- Can explain the core pragmatist idea (beliefs as instruments tested in experience) and its consequences for living.
- Can apply the pragmatist method to beliefs and habits: what difference would this make in practice.
- Can place James, Dewey and Peirce (and process thought) in relation to each other.

## Bounded domain

Covers:
- Peirce, James and Dewey as the classical core.
- Pragmatism as a lived method of belief and habit revision.
- Process thought and contemporary pragmatist currents.

Excludes (with reasons):
- Analytic philosophy of language beyond the pragmatic maxim.
- Existentialism (its own walk); Greek virtue ethics (its own walk).

Elective branches:
- Pragmatist ethics and education (Dewey); neopragmatism (Rorty) as an elective.

## Raw material available (measured 2026-10-25)

deg-philosophy module 4 (Pragmatism and Process) plus its 3 attachments.

## Benchmark against opened authoritative curricula (Gate-1 groundwork)

Anchors opened by the strategist, 2026-10-25 (ledger: docs/degrees/ledgers/deg-wol-pragmatism.md):

1. Georgetown PHIL 351, American Pragmatism (Blattner) — https://faculty.georgetown.edu/blattnew/prag/index.shtml. Classical pragmatist organism-environment-meaning-action themes, theories of truth and knowledge, James's utilitarian theory of value against Dewey's criticism, fact/value, applications to philosophy of religion, free will and law. Required texts: James (McDermott), Dewey (Essential Dewey vol. 2), Peirce (Essential Writings), Putnam, Rorty. Assessed entirely by two papers.
2. Duke PHIL 333, American Philosophy (Hoover, Fall 2016) — https://public.econ.duke.edu/~kdh9/Courses/Pragmatism/$American%20Philosophy%20Syllabus%20Fall%202016.pdf. Full reading schedule: Peirce on belief and the pragmatic maxim (Fixation of Belief, How to Make Our Ideas Clear), James (Will to Believe, Pragmatism, truth), Dewey (Darwinism, truth, inquiry, aesthetics, the Construction of the Good, Philosophy and Democracy), then Hook, Putnam, Price and two-plus weeks of Rorty. Assessed by weekly short assignments, participation and a final.
3. University of Houston, American Philosophy (Freelan) — https://www.uh.edu/~cfreelan/courses/americanphil/american.html. Survey from Emerson and Thoreau roots through Peirce, James and Dewey, structured by Cornel West's genealogy, ending in West's prophetic pragmatism. Texts: West, Thayer's two readers, Goodman's contemporary reader.

Comparison:

- Classical core: all three anchors centre Peirce, James and Dewey; the scope's classical core matches directly. The scope's exit capability — place James, Dewey and Peirce in relation to each other — is exactly what Duke's arc (Peirce's maxim → James's truth → Dewey's inquiry) and Georgetown's fact/value debate do.
- The pragmatist method as belief/habit revision: Duke's schedule opens with Peirce's Fixation of Belief and How to Make Our Ideas Clear, the canonical source of the scope's lived-method framing; Georgetown's page shows themes and required texts (including the Peirce volume) but no week-by-week schedule, so the maxim's placement rests on Duke's schedule alone. The scope's habit-revision emphasis is faithful to Duke's arc; the "walk" framing (applying the method to one's own habits) is a curricular choice, not an anchor divergence — Duke assesses weekly engagement with primary texts, which the walk's habit exercises parallel.
- Process thought: the scope includes process thought in its bounded domain and cites it in exit capabilities. None of the three pragmatism anchors mentions process philosophy (Whitehead); the HSE and scribd results from the search do pair Pragmatism with Process Philosophy, but those are not opened anchors. Follow-up anchors opened 2026-10-25 (see ledger): the University of Edinburgh Honours Option Whitehead's Metaphysics (McHenry, Spring 2009, course page served from CSUN) — https://www.csun.edu/~lmchenry/WhiteheadsMetaphysics.html — an eleven-week university option on Whitehead's event ontology from relativity-era philosophy of physics to Process and Reality's actual occasions, relations, eternal objects, natural theology, and critical evaluation against substance theorists (Aristotle, Strawson) and compared with Russell, Quine and Davidson; and the Cobb Institute Introduction to Process Thought & Practice syllabus PDF — https://cobb.institute/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Introduction-to-Process-Thought-and-Practice-Syllabus.pdf — roots/trunk/branches structure applying process ideas to personal life and community development, with reflection-paper or creative-localization projects. Together they verify that process thought is a real, teachable strand with both a rigorous university expression and a lived-practice expression. The scope's placement of process thought alongside the classical pragmatists is still its own choice — neither opened pragmatism anchor includes it — but it is now anchor-verified as a strand, and the scope's exit capability mention of process thought no longer rests on nothing. The gap flag is downgraded from "needs an anchor before Gate 1" to "placement is the scope's own; strand itself verified".
- Neopragmatism: Georgetown and Duke both teach Putnam and Rorty; the scope holds Rorty as an elective. That is a modest divergence — the anchors treat neopragmatism as mainstream core, the scope defers it. Defensible for a first walk (classical core first), but recorded: a learner finishing the walk meets Rorty only if they take the elective.
- Dewey's ethics and education: Duke teaches Dewey's Construction of the Good and Philosophy and Democracy; Georgetown's required Dewey volume is the Ethics one. The scope's Dewey-ethics-and-education elective is therefore anchor-supported core material held as elective. Recorded as a divergence, not silently.
- Exclusions: the scope excludes analytic philosophy of language beyond the pragmatic maxim; the anchors' later weeks (Putnam, Price, Brandom at Duke) lean that way, so the exclusion trims anchor tail material only. Existentialism and Greek virtue ethics exclusions are untouched by these anchors.

Strategist's note (non-binding): the classical core and the lived-method framing are anchor-solid. Process thought is now anchor-verified as a strand (Edinburgh Whitehead option; Cobb practice syllabus), with its placement beside the pragmatists remaining the scope's own choice. Rorty is core at the anchors but elective in the scope.

## Topic map (union of the three syllabi, organized as a coherent walk)

The three syllabi agree on the same skeleton: (a) what is African / relational philosophy and why it matters, (b) Ubuntu — the relational concept of personhood and communalism, (c) Ubuntu's moral and political applications — human rights, transitional justice, reconciliation, (d) the person and community in African thought (Menkiti, Gyekye, Wiredu), (e) African psychology and indigenous knowledge, (f) Ubuntu and contemporary debates — business, environment, intergenerational justice, migration. Where they diverge — SOAS leans into language-and-literature as philosophy (Rettová's philological interest), Oakton leans into African psychology (James's clinical-psychology interest), St. Gallen leans into applied political ethics (Ngosso's human-rights interest) — the D1 map below keeps the relational-ethics spine (Ubuntu → personhood → moral life → political applications) and files each instructor's emphasis where it belongs, per the centering clause.

### A. Orientation — what is relational philosophy?
- Is there African philosophy? (the colonial question) [Oakton topic 1-2]
- Ubuntu as an African relational ethic; "a person is a person through other persons" [SOAS wk 5; Oakton topic 5; St. Gallen Part I]
- The communalism vs individualism contrast with Western liberalism [St. Gallen Part I; Oakton topic 5]
- African ways of knowing; proverbs as method [Oakton topic 3; SOAS wk 1-2]

### B. Ubuntu — the core concept
- Umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu — the proverb and its meaning [St. Gallen Part I; SOAS wk 5]
- Ramose's Ubuntu as philosophy; Shutte's Ubuntu as an ethic for a new South Africa [SOAS wk 5]
- Tutu — No Future Without Forgiveness; the Truth and Reconciliation Commission [SOAS wk 5 bibliography; St. Gallen Part I]
- Metz's two conceptions of African ethics [St. Gallen course literature]
- Ubuntu and personhood — Menkiti's "Person and Community" [Oakton recommended; St. Gallen]

### C. Person and community in African thought
- The Akan conceptual scheme — Gyekye, Wiredu, Appiah [SOAS wk 3]
- Yorùbá philosophy of the self — Hallen, Sodipo, Gbadegesin [SOAS wk 4]
- Communitarianism vs moderate communitarianism — Molefe's defence [St. Gallen course literature]
- Hunhu / Unhu — the Zimbabwean and related non-Bantu equivalents [SOAS wk 5; Oakton topic 5]

### D. African psychology and indigenous knowledge
- African psychology as a discipline; the colonial critique of Western psychology [Oakton topics 1-2, 5]
- Self, personhood and community in African traditional thought [Oakton topic 5]
- Indigenous knowledge systems and decolonization [Oakton topics 3, 8]
- Mental health and illness — Western and African paradigms compared [Oakton topic 6]
- Spirituality in indigenous life [Oakton objectives]
- Globalization and the future of African culture [Oakton topic 7]

### E. Ubuntu, human rights and justice
- Ubuntu, the African Charter on Human and People's Rights [St. Gallen Part I]
- Ubuntu and transitional justice — is the South African case exportable? [St. Gallen Part I]
- Ubuntu and ethnocultural justice in contemporary African states [St. Gallen Part I]
- Ubuntu, common goods and poverty [St. Gallen Part I]

### F. Ubuntu and contemporary debates
- Ubuntu and business ethics; Ubuntu as management concept [St. Gallen Part II; Karsten and Illa, Lutz, Nussbaum]
- Ubuntu and theories of development [St. Gallen Part II]
- Ubuntu and global environmental justice — African relational environmentalism [St. Gallen Part II; Behrens]
- Ubuntu and intergenerational justice [St. Gallen Part II; Behrens]
- Ubuntu, hospitality and migration crises [St. Gallen Part II]

## Topic map (union of the three syllabi, organized as a coherent walk)

The three syllabi triangulate: Duke gives the classical canon (Peirce → James → Dewey → Rorty), UCSD gives the broadest contemporary sweep (Carnap/Quine → Putnam/Sellars → Rorty/Brandom → Misak/Seigfried → Apel/Habermas → Chang/Price), Georgetown gives the thematic questions (truth, value, fact/value, religion, free will, law). The union below is the pragmatist-walk-of-life curriculum.

### A. Origins and orientation
- What is pragmatism; the pragmatic maxim; American intellectual roots (Emerson, the Metaphysical Club) [UCSD wk 1; Duke wk 1; Georgetown themes]
- Pragmatism as theory, methodology, and/or ideology — the framing question [UCSD course title]

### B. Peirce
- The Fixation of Belief; the four methods; the scientific attitude; fallibilism [Duke wk 2; UCSD Peirce readings]
- How to Make Our Ideas Clear; the pragmatist maxim; pragmatism vs pragmaticism [Duke wk 3; UCSD]
- Peirce's metaphysics: categories, evolutionary love, synechism [Duke wk 4]
- Some Consequences of Four Incapacities; What Pragmatism Is [UCSD]

### C. James
- Early thought: The Sentiment of Rationality, The Will to Believe, The Dilemma of Determinism [Duke wk 5]
- Pragmatism (1907): Lectures I-II (what pragmatism means), III-IV, VI-VII (truth) [Duke wk 6-8]
- James's theory of truth; Humanism and Truth; the pragmatist account and its misunderstandings [Duke wk 8; UCSD James truth cluster]
- The Stream of Thought (Principles of Psychology) [UCSD]
- James's utilitarian theory of value [Georgetown themes]

### D. Dewey
- Introducing Dewey: Darwin's Influence Upon Philosophy; Truth and Consequences [Duke wk 9]
- Dewey on inquiry: The Quest for Certainty, common sense and scientific inquiry, the pattern of inquiry [Duke wk 10; UCSD Dewey cluster]
- Dewey on the normative: The Construction of the Good, Philosophy and Democracy [Duke wk 11]
- The Reflex Arc; The Need for a Recovery in Philosophy; The Development of American Pragmatism [UCSD]
- Dewey's criticism of James's value theory; the fact/value question [Georgetown themes]

### E. Early-20th-century extensions
- C.I. Lewis: pragmatic conception of the a priori; Mind and World Order [UCSD]
- Frank Ramsey: Facts and Propositions; Truth and Probability; The Nature of Truth [UCSD]
- Rudolf Carnap + W.V. Quine: the pragmatism-within-empiricism line (Two Dogmas; Empiricism, Semantics and Ontology; Carnap and Logical Truth) [UCSD]

### F. Contemporary and neo-pragmatism
- Hilary Putnam (+ Wilfrid Sellars): Reason, Truth and History; Two Philosophical Perspectives; the antinomy of realism [UCSD; Georgetown texts]
- Richard Rorty: Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature; Consequences of Pragmatism; Objectivity, Relativism and Truth [Duke wk 12; UCSD; Georgetown texts]
- Robert Brandom: inferentialist semantics; the pragmatist enlightenment; analytic pragmatism [UCSD]
- Cheryl Misak: the transcendental turn; Peirce's influence on the Vienna Circle; truth and pluralism [UCSD]
- Charlene Seigfried: feminist pragmatism; Jane Addams [UCSD]
- Karl-Otto Apel: transcendental pragmatics; discourse ethics [UCSD]
- Jürgen Habermas: discourse ethics; the pragmatics of communication [UCSD]
- Hasok Chang + Huw Price: pragmatic realism; truth as convenient friction; naturalism without representationalism [UCSD; Duke wk 13]
- Susan Haack: foundherentism; the pragmatist theory of truth and its critics [UCSD additional]
- Wilfrid Sellars: Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind [UCSD]

### G. Applied pragmatism (where the walk-of-life frame bites)
- Pragmatism and philosophy of religion (James's Will to Believe; Dewey's Common Faith) [Georgetown themes]
- Pragmatism and free will (James's Dilemma of Determinism) [Georgetown themes]
- Pragmatism and democracy (Dewey's Philosophy and Democracy; public philosophy) [Duke wk 11]
- Pragmatism and law (Holmes; legal pragmatism) [Georgetown themes; UT Dallas syllabus surfaced but not opened]
- Pragmatism and education (Dewey's Democracy and Education) [not in the three syllabi directly; standard Dewey extension]

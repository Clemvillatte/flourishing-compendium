## The anchor question (ruling requested)

University syllabi do not naturally anchor a year-long practice degree: community-college tai chi/qigong courses are 1-unit activity classes (e.g. Moorpark KIN M53 at 52.5 contact hours per the search-result evidence), not full curricula. The practice lineage's own carriers are the real anchors, and this D1 uses three kinds: a medical-school course (academic), the national professional standards body (standards), and a lineage teacher-training curriculum (practice content). **Ruling requested: confirm this anchor triple as the D1 corpus basis for practice degrees (it generalizes to deg-cmp), or direct a different anchor.** The ruling is a framing question only — the topic map below stands on sources opened this session either way.

## Centering clause (owner 2026-10-26)

The degree is itself the mission in miniature: a daily practice that builds calm, vitality and agency under stress. Exclusions apply mainly to teacher-training and clinical apparatus (certification logistics, liability insurance, teaching practice) — the learner is a practitioner, not an instructor; NQA/IIQTC structures are used as evidence of what the discipline contains and how it sequences, not as a curriculum to replicate.

## Sources opened (the D1 corpus — 3, all fetched and read this session)

1. **Stanford University, "Stanford Medical Tai Chi, FAMMED-213-01, Spring 2026, Instructor: Beverley Kane, MD" (Stanford School of Medicine)** — https://syllabus.stanford.edu/syllabus/downloadSyllabus?courseId=Sp26-FAMMED-213-01 — syllabus read this session. Course description verbatim: "Tai ji and qigong teach you how to relax, reduce stress, and increase agency (self-determination and self-expression) in all aspects of your life... Our emphasis is on cultivating the union of breath and vital energy (qi or ch'i...) in coordination with flowing muscular movements." Topics listed: journal club with gold-standard methodological criteria and the Wayne-Kaptchuk Ba Gua model; introduction to tai ji; energy; ch'i and Reiki compared; the Dan Tien; the yin and yang of breathing; postures and gestures; qigong; silk reeling; Embrace Tiger Return to Mountain; tai chi walking; tai ji and quantum cosmology. Class format: 30 min journal club then opening meditation, silk reeling standing qigong, Guo Lin and Shaolin walking qigong, tai ji movements.
2. **National Qigong Association, "Qigong Instructor Certification" requirements page** — https://www.nqa.org/certification — read this session. Levels: Instructor Level I = 200 hours documented training + 2 years personal practice + 3 references (100% of hours may be virtual); Level II = 350 hours + 3 years practice + 2 years teaching; Level III = 500 hours + 6 years practice + 5 years teaching; Level IV = 1,000 hours + 10 years teaching teachers; Clinical Practitioner = 500 hours formal clinical training + 100 hours treatments + 2 years clinical experience + insurance + CPR. Didactic hours include anatomy, physiology, Chinese medical theory, and qigong history, theory and philosophy. Tai chi hours may count partially as related studies.
3. **Institute of Integral Qigong and Tai Chi (IIQTC), "Level 1 Integral Qigong Teacher Training" page** — https://instituteofintegralqigongandtaichi.org/qigong_taichi_training/25-006PTO_TCEPL.html — read this session. Level 1 = 65 hours in three modules (22/22/21): Module 1 Healer Within™ Medical Qigong (four dimensions: gentle mindful movement, breath practice, self-massage, meditation); Module 2 Ten Phases of Cultivating and Mastering Qi (Earth qigong phases 1-3 discover/gather/circulate qi; HeartMind phases 4-6 purify/direct/conserve qi; Heaven Spirit phases 7-9 store/transform/dissolve; 10th phase transmit qi); Module 3 additional forms (tendon changing, marrow bathing, spontaneous qigong, seven precious gestures, Guo Lin walking qigong, inner alchemy nei dan, transmitting qi). Full path: 200 hours across Levels 1-3, meeting the NQA professional-membership criterion.

## Topic map (union of the three sources, organized as the year of practice)

- The daily habit: establishing and keeping a practice (the degree's own frame; IIQTC's integration between modules)
- Breath practice: the engine; yin and yang of breathing [Stanford §7; IIQTC M1]
- Standing practice: silk reeling standing qigong, structural alignment [Stanford §11; IIQTC M1 gentle mindful movement]
- The four dimensions of practice: gentle mindful movement, breath, self-massage, meditation [IIQTC M1]
- Body awareness: listening inside; the Dan Tien as the body's center [Stanford §6; IIQTC]
- Qi cultivation phases: discover, gather, circulate, purify, direct, conserve, store, transform, dissolve, transmit [IIQTC M2]
- Walking practice: Guo Lin walking qigong, tai chi walking, Shaolin walking [Stanford format; IIQTC M3]
- Emotional work: HeartMind qigong as inner medicine for the mental-emotional body [IIQTC M2 phases 4-6]
- Routines and flow: sequences as moving meditation; a library of forms (tendon changing, marrow bathing, spontaneous qigong, seven precious gestures) [IIQTC M3; degree's own modules]
- The tai chi bridge: tai ji movements, Embrace Tiger Return to Mountain [Stanford §12-13]
- Seasonal practice: seasonal/elemental frames [IIQTC Earth/Heaven framing; degree's own module]
- Depth: longer forms, inner alchemy (nei dan), working with a teacher [IIQTC M3; NQA's years-of-practice ladder]
- Theory: Chinese medical theory, qigong history/philosophy, anatomy and physiology as didactic layer [NQA didactic definition; Stanford journal club]
- Evidence literacy: reading the research critically — gold-standard methodology and its alternatives, the Wayne-Kaptchuk Ba Gua model [Stanford journal club]
- Integration: your own practice, agency and self-determination as outcomes [Stanford course description; degree's own capstone]

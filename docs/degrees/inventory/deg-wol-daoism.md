## Topic map (union of the three syllabi, organized as a coherent walk)

The three syllabi agree on the same skeleton: (a) what Daoism is and its Warring States origins, (b) the Daodejing, (c) the Zhuangzi, (d) the inner-cultivation / hygiene tradition (Neiye, Guanzi), (e) religious Daoism from the Han through the medieval schools (Celestial Masters, Shangqing, Lingbao, Quanzhen, Zhengyi), (f) modern Daoism and its living practice. Where they diverge — DePauw and UF lean into religious-institutional history and ritual mechanics; Wabash leans into philosophical Daoism and secondary scholarship — the D1 map below keeps the philosophical + practice core as the walk's spine and files the religious-history material as contextual topics (not the learner's centre of gravity), per the centering clause.

### A. Origins and orientation (what is the Dao?)
- What is Daoism (philosophical vs religious — the dao / dharma / logos question) [Wabash wk 1; UF wk 1; DePauw Unit I]
- The Warring States context (against Confucianism, Mohism, Legalism) [DePauw wk 2; UF wk 1 Robinet ch 1]
- The dao concept: being/non-being, flux, the unnameable Way [Wabash wk 1; UF wk 2]

### B. The Daodejing
- Laozi: full primary text (chapters 1-81) [Wabash wk 3; DePauw wk 2; UF wk 2]
- Key concepts: dao, de, wu-wei, ziran, yin/yang, reversal (fan), weakness/softness [Wabash wk 3; UF wk 2]
- Secondary scholarship: mysticism and apophatic discourse; early Daoist mystical praxis; competing commentaries; interpretive schools [Wabash wk 4-6]
- Translation and reception (Ivanhoe, Mair, Eno, Schwartz, Robinet's interpretive-history) [UF wk 2]

### C. The Zhuangzi
- Zhuangzi: primary text (Inner Chapters 1-7 at minimum; Outer and Mixed chapters as selections) [Wabash wk 11-13; DePauw wk 2; UF wk 3]
- Inner-chapter themes: wu-wei, skill/spontaneity, relativism and scepticism, the butterfly dream, usefulness of the useless [Wabash wk 11-12; UF wk 3]
- Secondary scholarship: harmony and cacophony; mind-body; nothing-can-overcome-heaven; Cook's Hiding the World in the World; Graham's Disputers of the Tao [Wabash wk 14-15; UF wk 3]
- Zhuangzi and modern relevance (disputers of the Tao frame) [UF wk 3]

### D. Inner cultivation and the body
- The Neiye and Guanzi "inner cultivation tradition" (Roth) [DePauw wk 2]
- The Chuci / mystical excursion (fangshi) [DePauw wk 2]
- Health, immortality, longevity techniques; Chinese medicine; Daoist hygiene [UF wk 4; DePauw Unit II]
- The Daoist body (Schipper, Taoist Body); body gods and inner vision (Kroll) [UF wk 12-15]
- Neidan (inner alchemy) [DePauw Unit IV; UF wk 9]
- Taijiquan and the martial-arts connection [DePauw Unit IV]

### E. Religious Daoism (context — institutions, canon, schools)
- Han dynasty and the rise of organized Daoism; the Celestial Masters (Tianshi) [DePauw Unit II; UF wk 5]
- Shangqing (Highest Clarity) [DePauw Unit II; UF wk 5]
- Lingbao (Numinous Treasure) and the interaction with Buddhism [DePauw Unit III; UF wk 6]
- Daoist ethics and community (Taiping jing, 180 Precepts, Codes of Master Lu) [UF wk 6]
- Tang and Song imperial Daoism; the Daoist Canon (Daozang) [DePauw Unit III; UF wk 7-8]
- Quanzhen (Complete Perfection) — the medieval monastic revival [Wabash wk 9-10; UF wk 9-10]
- Zhengyi (Orthodox Unity) — the living liturgical tradition [UF wk 11]
- Daoist messianism, imperial adaptation, political legitimacy [UF wk 7-8]

### F. Practice, ritual, and modern Daoism
- Daoist ritual in society and history [UF wk 13; DePauw Unit IV]
- Sacred geography (Daoist mountains — Wudang, Longmen) [UF wk 15]
- Daoist art, verse, and aesthetics [Wabash wk 7; UF wk 16]
- Daoism in contemporary China and the diaspora [DePauw Unit IV; UF wk 11-12]
- Quanzhen and modern practice (Seven Taoist Masters as folk-novel teaching text) [UF wk 10]
- Daoist monastic life; Daoists of Peking; the modern clergy [UF wk 12]

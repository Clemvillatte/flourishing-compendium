## Centering clause (owner 2026-10-26)

Every topic and every resource below must serve the value proposition — alleviate suffering, become wiser, genuinely flourish even when uncertain or overwhelmed — through the Walk of Life / Philosophy of Life frame. Topical fit alone does NOT qualify; when in doubt, exclude. Contemplative-science material that has no bearing on lived practice (e.g. neuroimaging methodology as pure technique, statistical-power debates in meditation research, fMRI analysis pipelines) is OUT OF SCOPE unless a teaching layer connects it back to the walk of life. The contemplative practices themselves — mindfulness, compassion, self-compassion, gratitude — are kept at the centre because they are the discipline's contribution to alleviating suffering and developing wisdom.

## Topic map (union of the three syllabi, organized as a coherent walk)

The three syllabi agree on the same skeleton: (a) what is contemplative science and practice, (b) the core contemplative practices — mindfulness, compassion, self-compassion, gratitude, (c) the science of contemplative practice — attention, emotion, brain and wellbeing, (d) the lived experience of contemplative practice — formal and informal practice in daily life, (e) the philosophical and religious roots of contemplative practice, (f) applications — clinical, educational and workplace. Where they diverge — Brown/UVa Mindfulness-and-Compassion leans into MBSR and prosocial behaviour, UO Contemplative Science leans into empirical research methods and self-study, the Flourishing course leans into the five-dimension model and 14 qualities — the D1 map below keeps the practices-and-science spine and files each course's emphasis where it belongs, per the centering clause.

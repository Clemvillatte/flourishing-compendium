## University syllabi opened (the D1 corpus — 4, all fetched and read this session)

1. **Wesleyan University, "PHIL 221 Philosophy as a Way of Life, Fall 2011, Instructor: Prof. Stephen Angle"** (syllabus PDF hosted by Notre Dame's philife project) — https://philife.nd.edu/assets/317166/fullsize/phil_221_fall11_unified_syllabus.pdf — course description read verbatim this session: "Philosophy as an activity: something people do, something that can be part of one's life. Philosophy as a way of changing one's orientation to the world; as a way of becoming a better person; and as a way of changing society. How do Confucians, Daoists, Epicureans, and Stoics pursue these goals?" Full week schedule read: Hadot's Philosophy as a Way of Life (chs 1, 3, 4, 11); Confucius's Analects; Socrates (Euthyphro, Apology, Crito); Zhuangzi; Epicurus/Lucretius; Seneca's Epistles; Zhu Xi's Learning to Be a Sage; Thoreau's Walden; Shusterman's Practicing Philosophy.
2. **University of Florida, "CLA 6805 Roman Stoicism, Spring 2022, Instructor: Velvet Yates"** — https://classics.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/209/Stoicism_syllabus.pdf — full week table read this session: introduction to Stoicism; Sandbach's ethics/fate/Panaetius/later Stoics; Cicero's On Moral Ends (Epicureanism vs Stoicism); Seneca's Letters (three parts); Hadot's Inner Citadel; Marcus Aurelius (three parts); pop Stoicism; applying Stoic philosophy to contemporary situations.
3. **Arizona State University, "PHI 420 Stoicism, Spring B 2021, Instructor: Dr. Sandra Woien"** — https://webapp4.asu.edu/bookstore/viewsyllabus/2231/17163/pdf — page read this session (no week-by-week table published): Stoicism's rise and fall; Stoic ethics including psychological techniques and the four cardinal virtues; seminal Stoics in historical order; reflective journal activities applying Stoic techniques; Irvine's A Guide to the Good Life as required text.
4. **UC Berkeley, "Philosophy 161 Aristotle, Fall 2014, Instructor: Klaus Corcilius"** — https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~rwl/lib/attachments/teaching/161/course-syllabus.pdf — full schedule read this session: theoretical works (Categories, On Interpretation, syllogistic, Posterior Analytics, Physics, prime mover, On the Soul, Metaphysics) followed by the practical core — Nicomachean Ethics I (happiness, function argument), II (virtues of character), III (voluntary action, choice, responsibility), VI (intellectual virtues), VII (incontinence), X (contemplation, best life), Politics I.

## Topic map (union of the four syllabi, organized as the practical-philosophy walk)

- Philosophy as a way of life: the Hadot frame — spiritual exercises, philosophy as transformation of orientation [Wesleyan wk1; UF via Hadot's Inner Citadel]
- Socrates and the examined life: Euthyphro, Apology, Crito [Wesleyan wk3-4]
- The virtue-ethics core: Aristotle's NE — happiness and the function argument, virtues of character, voluntary action/choice/responsibility, intellectual virtues, incontinence, contemplation and the best life [Berkeley final third]
- Stoicism ancient: ethics, the four cardinal virtues, psychological techniques, fate and assent, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Seneca's letters, Cicero's moral framework [ASU; UF wk1-13; current mod-phil-2]
- Stoicism modern: pop Stoicism and its critics, Stoicism as reflective practice, Irvine's guide [ASU; UF wk15]
- Hellenistic alternatives: Epicureanism (Epicurus/Lucretius), the Epicurean-vs-Stoic contrast [Wesleyan wk6; UF wk5-6; current mod-phil-3]
- Pragmatism and process thought as practical philosophy [current mod-phil-4; Wesleyan Shusterman strand]
- Existentialism and phenomenology as lived orientations [current mod-phil-5; not anchored in this corpus — see gaps]
- Comparative way-of-life material: Confucius's Analects, Zhuangzi, Zhu Xi [Wesleyan wk2, 5, 8 — owned by other walks; see audit]
- American practical philosophy: Thoreau's Walden; Shusterman's somaesthetics [Wesleyan wk9, 12]
- Practical judgement as capstone: applying frameworks to a live decision [current capstone; UF wk15's application strand]

## Centering clause (owner 2026-10-26)

Every topic and every resource below must serve the value proposition — alleviate suffering, become wiser, genuinely flourish even when uncertain or overwhelmed — through the Walk of Life / Philosophy of Life frame. Topical fit alone does NOT qualify; when in doubt, exclude. This shaped the topic map: the six EEMM dimensions (affect, cognition, attention, self, motivation, overt behaviour) are the walk's spine because each is a lever a learner can locate in their own life. The EEMM/VSR-C frame itself is a degree-specific organizing addition, not a claim about the syllabi. Laboratory subdiscipline detail (psycholinguistic mechanics, brain-imaging methodology, recognition models) and the clinical/health-service territory at the degree's border are handled in the excluded/deferred audit section below with reasons, per the finite-scope rule.

## Topic map (union of the five syllabi, organized as the six-dimension walk)

The five syllabi triangulate cleanly onto the degree's own spine: the cognitive and affective science courses supply the process science; the motivation-science and self/identity courses supply the goal-and-self layer; the health-psychology course supplies the behaviour-change layer. Where they diverge — UT Dallas leans laboratory-cognitive (recognition, imagery, language mechanics), Rutgers leans theory-of-emotion and clinical bridges, Columbia leans social-cognitive motivation — the map keeps the processes a learner can locate in their own life as the spine and files the laboratory mechanics and clinical/service territory in the excluded/deferred audit below, per the centering clause.

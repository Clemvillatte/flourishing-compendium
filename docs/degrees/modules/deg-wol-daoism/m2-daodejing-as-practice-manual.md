# m2: The Daodejing as a Practice Manual for Living (deg-wol-daoism)

Second module of A Walk of Life: Daoism. The first module gave you the
orientation and the four working terms; this module takes the
Daodejing itself and treats it as what the walk's scope calls it: a
practice manual for living, not only a piece of literature. You will
read more of the text, learn to name the moves the chapters make,
and apply the text's central reversal to a case in your own life. The
promise of this module is narrow: by the end you can read a chapter
of the Daodejing with guidance, name what the chapter does, and say
how the chapter can be applied to a life situation. It is not a
promise that the text will solve your problems; it is a promise that
you will know how to use the text rather than only admire it.

## Level and placement

L2 Guided application. Prerequisites: the exit criteria of m1 (the
four working terms, a guided reading of one Daodejing chapter, the
scope statement). The exit criteria below are the entry competencies
of m3 (the Zhuangzi on skill, wu wei and losing the self).

## Outcomes

1. Read a chapter of the Daodejing and name the move the chapter
   makes (reversal, contrast, paradox, image) and what the chapter
   asks of a reader who wants to use it.
2. Apply the Daodejing's central reversal (the low, the dark, the
   formless and the unnamed as the source of what you value) to a
   case in your own life, naming what matters in the case, what the
   reversal helps you understand or choose there, and one thing the
   case leaves uncertain.
3. Compare two accounts of the same Daodejing chapter, naming where
   they agree, where the closing line's wording differs, and what
   difference that wording might make to your interpretation, then
   saying which account you find more useful for your own life
   and why.
4. Execute a complete guided reading sequence of a Daodejing chapter
   from start to finish, with the primary text in front of you,
   without the module's worked demonstration.

Every outcome is taught, practised and assessed below; the alignment
map at the end shows where.

## Instruction (the sequence a learner follows)

### Step 1: the reversal move, taught (Outcome 2)

The recurring contrast this module examines is reversal: the chapters
assigned here connect what you value with the thing you dismiss.
Chapter 11 connects usefulness with empty space; chapter 78
connects softness with overcoming hardness. This is not a paradox
for the sake of being difficult; this module tests whether those
contrasts help you reconsider something you have dismissed, and
the module's first job is to make sure you can see the move in a
chapter before you try to use it.

Watch the assigned Ziporyn segments first; they teach the move in
its strongest form.

1. [Why This Text Matters: Daodejing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJ1bB2w2gBk),
   by Brook Ziporyn (0:53:36 total; University of Chicago Divinity
   School channel; full chapter timestamps printed on the watch
   page). Watch these assigned segments, 21:34 in total (the same
   segments assigned in m1; if you completed m1 recently you may
   rewatch only the segments you want to revisit):
   - 0:00:00 to 0:12:13 (Introduction through Dao as course and
     guide).
   - 0:15:20 to 0:17:00 (Wu wei).
   - 0:26:15 to 0:33:56 (Chapter 28; the use of the useless; how
     the unnamed sustains the named; flowers and dirt).

The reversal move is easiest to see in the water chapter, which
m1 worked in full: the highest good is like water, and water settles
in the despised low places. But the move runs through the whole text.
Here are three more chapters that make the same move in different
registers, so you can see it is not a one-off trick.

2. [Daodejing, translated by Robert Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content).
   Read chapters 11, 36 and 78 in full, twice each (228 words measured
   across the three, on the downloaded full text).

Chapter 11 makes the move on emptiness: thirty spokes share a
single hub, but grasp the nothingness at its center to get the use
of the wheel; clay is fashioned to make a vessel, but grasp the
nothingness at its center to get the use of the vessel; bore
windows and doors to create a room, but grasp the nothingness of
the interior to get the use of the room. The chapter ends by naming
the reversal directly: that which is constitutes what is valuable,
but that which is not constitutes what is of use.
([Eno, Daodejing, chapter 11](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content).)

Chapter 36 makes the move on strategy: to shrink it you must
stretch it; to weaken it you must strengthen it; to discard it you
must raise it up; to seize it you must bestow it. The chapter reads
as a manual for acting against the obvious direction, and its
closing lines, "the weak and supple overcomes the strong and hard"
and "fish must not emerge from the deeps; the vital tools of a state
must not be revealed", name the same reversal applied to power.
([Eno, Daodejing, chapter 36](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content).)

Chapter 78 makes the move on softness: nothing in the world is
more weak and soft than water, yet nothing surpasses it in
conquering the hard and strong. The chapter then says all know
that the weak conquers the strong and the soft conquers the hard,
but none are able to act on this, and moves to the state-altars
passage: the sage says he who receives the derision of the state
is the lord of the state altars; he who receives the misfortune of
the state is the king of the world. The chapter's actual closing
line is "Straight words seem to reverse themselves."
([Eno, Daodejing, chapter 78](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content).)

### Step 2: applying the reversal to your own case (Outcome 2)

Now take the reversal move and apply it to a case in your own life.
The worked demonstration uses the same three-part reflection you
learned in m1, applied to a case where the reversal is one possible
contribution rather than the whole story. The case is hypothetical,
written for this module; it is not a finding about anyone, and you
should test it against your own experience rather than treat it as
a diagnosis.

The case: a person is stuck in a job they dislike and is trying to
force themselves to feel motivated about it, believing that if they
could just find the right motivation the work would become
bearable.

What matters in the case: the forcing is one possible contributor to
how unbearable the work feels; the case does not establish that the
forcing is the only or even the main contributor, and the job's
actual content, the person's circumstances outside work, or the fit
between the two could each be doing more of the work.

What the reversal helps with: the Daodejing's reversal says the
source of what you value is the thing you dismiss. Applied here, one
dismissed thing is the person's exhaustion, and one possible move is
to stop treating the exhaustion as an obstacle to be forced past
and start treating it as information about what is happening. The
reversal does not tell the person to quit or to stay, and it does
not establish that the response to circumstances is the real
problem; it offers a way of noticing what the forcing was hiding.

What the case leaves uncertain: whether the exhaustion is a signal
that the job is wrong for this person, a signal that the person is
tired for reasons that have nothing to do with the job, or a signal
that the job and the person's circumstances need to change together.
The reversal does not answer that; it only stops the forcing that was
making the question hard to examine.

Retrieval activity 1 (closed book, now): from memory, write your
own three-part reflection on a case in your life where you are
forcing something. Name what matters in the case, what the reversal
helps you understand or choose there, and one thing the case leaves
uncertain. Keep what you write; it is the draft of graded
deliverable a.

### Step 3: comparing two readings of the same chapter (Outcome 3)

The Daodejing's chapters admit more than one defensible reading,
and the module's job here is to teach you to notice that and to
work with the difference rather than to pick the "correct" one.

Read two accounts of the same chapter: the Eno translation you
already have, and the chapter as discussed in the assigned lecture
segments.

3. Re-read [Eno's Daodejing chapter 28](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content)
   (119 words measured), twice.

Eno's chapter 28 reads: "One who knows the male but preserves the
female becomes a ravine to the world. Such a one never swerves from
constant virtue and returns again to be a new born baby. One who
knows white but preserves black becomes a standard for the world.
Such a one never deviates from constant virtue and returns again to
being limitless. One who knows glory but preserves shame becomes a
valley to the world. Such a one is always supplied with constant
virtue and returns again to be an uncarved block. When the uncarved
block is dispersed, vessels are made from it. The sage uses these to
become the leader of the officers of state. Thus the greatest
carving never cuts."
([Eno, Daodejing, chapter 28](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content).)

Ziporyn's lecture reads this chapter through the A/B structure he
traces through the whole text: to know the masculine while also
maintaining the feminine is to be a channel for all the world, and
that channel is what he calls "another B term, an emptiness or a
valley that draws things to it, a nonbeing that has this unexpected
power". On the closing line, his translation reads "The great
structuring carves, but it does not sever", and he explains it as
"a base relief, rather than actually carving things out and
separating them off from that background"
([Ziporyn, Why This Text Matters, transcript passage beginning "I
would now turn you to chapter 28"](https://youtubetotranscript.com/transcript?v=EJ1bB2w2gBk&current_language_code=en)).

Now compare the two accounts. Both translations agree on the
chapter's content: know the one, preserve the other, become the
valley or the standard or the ravine, and the closing line is
about carving without severing. The clearest evidenced difference
between them is in the closing line's wording. Eno translates the
conclusion as "Thus the greatest carving never cuts." Ziporyn
renders it "The great structuring carves, but it does not sever"
and explains it through a relief that remains connected to its
background. Compare these formulations. What difference might
"never cuts" versus "carves, but does not sever" make to your
interpretation? Use the surrounding passage and the lecture to
support your answer; the wording alone does not establish that the
translators disagree.

Write a short comparison (100 to 200 words) naming where the two
accounts agree, where they differ in the closing line's wording,
what difference that wording might make to your interpretation,
and which account you find more useful for your own life, with a
reason. That is the draft of graded deliverable b.

### Step 4: your own guided reading (Outcome 4)

Now read a Daodejing chapter yourself, without the module's worked
demonstration, and do the same five-part work you learned in m1:
the exact passage, the image, the move, a defensible
interpretation, and one limitation.

4. [Eno's Daodejing](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/7031cb99-72c3-4e59-8ced-9d9783638945/content),
   chapters 16 and 44 (165 words measured across the two), read
   twice each. Pick one of the two and do the five-part work on
   it.

Chapter 16 begins "Reaching the ultimate of emptiness, deeply
guarding stillness, the things of the world arise together; thereby
do I watch their return" and moves through the ten thousand things
to the return; chapter 44 begins "One's reputation or one's body:
which is dearer? One's body or one's goods: which is worth more?"
and asks which is worse, gaining or losing, then draws the
conclusion that knowing what is enough escapes shame and knowing
when to stop escapes danger. Either chapter carries the module's
outcomes; pick the one that speaks to your own situation.

A note on the difference from m1: in m1 the module worked chapter 8
for you and you did the scaffolded work on chapters 1 and 76; here
there is no worked demonstration. You have the five-part structure
from m1 and the three chapters worked in Step 1; the module expects
you to do the whole thing yourself now.

Retrieval activity 2 (closed book, after Step 4): from memory, name
the move each of the three assigned Step 1 chapters makes (11, 36,
78) and one thing each chapter asks of a reader. Check yourself
against the primary text before you finalise.

## Rubric (observable criteria)

1. The reversal-application deliverable names a real case in the
   learner's life, applies the reversal as one possible
   contribution rather than as the whole story, and names what
   matters, what the reversal helps with, and one thing the case
   leaves uncertain. Strong: "What mattered in my case was that I
   kept trying to make my book club read the books I wanted to
   read; the case does not establish that the forcing was the only
   or the main contributor to how bad the meetings felt, and the
   book club's actual fit with the members could have been doing
   more of the work; the reversal helps me see that the thing I
   dismissed, the members' actual boredom with my choices, was
   information I needed, and one possible move was to stop
   presenting and start asking what they wanted; what it leaves
   uncertain is whether the club is worth saving at all, and the
   reversal does not answer that." Rejection: "Wu wei means going
   with the flow and I should relax" (everyday usage, no reversal
   applied, no three parts named).
2. The comparison deliverable names where the two accounts of
   chapter 28 agree, where the closing line's wording differs,
   and what difference that wording might make to the learner's
   interpretation, then says which account the learner finds more
   useful for their own life, with a reason. Strong: "Both accounts
   agree the chapter is about knowing one thing and preserving its
   opposite, and both present the closing line as carving without
   severing; the evidenced difference is that Eno translates it as
   'Thus the greatest carving never cuts' while Ziporyn renders it
   'The great structuring carves, but it does not sever' and
   explains it through a relief that remains connected to its
   background; the difference that wording might make is that
   'never cuts' reads as an absolute claim while 'carves but does
   not sever' allows a carving that stays in contact with what it
   carves, and I find Ziporyn's version more useful because it
   gives me a way to think about acting without pretending the
   action has no effect." Rejection: "The two readings are different
   but both are valid" (no agreement, no wording difference, no
   interpretive difference, no preference, no reason).
3. The chapter-reading deliverable does the five-part work on a
   real assigned chapter, with the primary text in front of the
   learner, without the module's demonstration. Strong: a
   five-part reading of chapter 44 that names the passage, the
   image (the question about reputation and body), the move (a
   series of questions that reverse the reader's priorities), a
   defensible interpretation (the chapter asks which of the things
   you are chasing is actually worth the cost), and one limitation
   of the proposed application (knowing when to stop does not
   establish whether this particular commitment should be abandoned
   or sustained; the chapter does not answer that, and the
   application would have to). Rejection: "Chapter 44 is about
   knowing when to stop" (no image, no move, no interpretation,
   no limitation).

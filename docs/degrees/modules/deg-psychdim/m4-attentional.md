When demands pile up, "pay attention" can sound like an accusation rather
than help. This module replaces that instruction with smaller questions:
ready for what, directed where, and in conflict with which other demand?
You will use the answers to understand an ordinary task and evaluate
attention claims without turning a difficult afternoon into a diagnosis.

## Entry and outcomes

Level: L2, Guided application. Bring M3's visual-cue explanation,
memory kinds/stages, learning-account comparison, judgment countercheck
and source-limited appraisal. You can distinguish evidence supplied
by a case from details that remain unknown. No brain anatomy examination
or personal reaction-time test is required.

By the end, you can:

- **Networks:** apply alerting, orienting and executive control to a
  new task; compare early independence language with the later
  semi-independent, interacting account.
- **Selection and limits:** explain the spotlight analogy, Stroop
  interference, inattentional blindness and change blindness without
  assuming a single demonstration establishes a personal deficit.
- **Measurement:** explain an ANT trial and calculate three simple
  contrasts from supplied fictional averages, distinguishing cue benefit
  from conflict cost and a task contrast from a brain diagnosis.
- **Associations:** interpret one network/disorder association as
  group-level evidence, including why neither a symptom nor a score
  yields a one-to-one diagnosis.
- **Practice and evidence:** compare focused attention with open
  monitoring, follow or describe a bounded observation exercise, and
  distinguish an anecdote from evidence of training efficacy.

## Map functions before naming a problem

Watch Posner and read the 2023 update. Alerting concerns readiness for an
event; orienting concerns selecting information or its location; executive
control concerns resolving competing responses or demands. They are useful
functional distinctions, not three independent boxes a person either
possesses or lacks
([assigned lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uYUdwS7-WvA&t=186);
[2023 interactions paragraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10301152/)).

The 2019 review associates these functions with distributed structures
and modulators, not a single "attention centre." Use its Table 1 as a
reference: orienting includes superior parietal regions and acetylcholine;
alerting includes locus coeruleus/frontal-parietal circuitry and
norepinephrine; executive control includes anterior cingulate, anterior
insula and basal ganglia, with dopamine. These are examples from its
table, not an exhaustive inventory or a way to infer someone's
neurotransmitter levels from behaviour
([2019 Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430178/)).

Using the reference, add one structure/modulator pairing to a network
in the next exercise. Feedback example: "Orienting; superior parietal;
acetylcholine." It passes as a source lookup, not a claim that a
particular action reveals that chemical in one person.

Worked example: Bea is about to follow a written recipe. A timer warns
that a step is due, she shifts to the relevant line, and she resists
reading an unrelated message while following that step. Those illustrate
readiness, selection and competing-demand control. This fictional
description does not identify a brain lesion or measure a network.

Now complete a desk-work example. A reminder signals a scheduled task;
a highlighted heading shows where to begin; an irrelevant instruction
competes with the task rule. Map each part to a function and explain one
overlap. Feedback: alerting, orienting and control respectively; readiness
and selection may both be involved in responding to the same reminder.
Merely naming three brain areas would not answer the functional question.

Compare two accounts of the same issue: do the networks operate
independently? The earlier task work appeared to support independence.
Posner's 2023 review says larger and more complex studies showed
interactions and describes a single attention system with at least three
semi-independent networks. The difference concerns degree of interaction,
not whether distinctions remain useful
([2023 review, section 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10301152/)).

Write a two-sentence comparison using "earlier evidence appeared" and
"later evidence qualified." Feedback: a passing comparison retains
the differentiated functions while rejecting complete independence;
"new work proves attention is one undifferentiated capacity" overcorrects.

## Selective attention

Watch Bloom's narrowed window. The spotlight is an analogy for selection,
not a literal beam in the head. In the Stroop task, the task-relevant
colour and the word's meaning can compete. Inattentional blindness
concerns an unexpected item missed while attention is occupied;
change blindness concerns failure to notice a change across views
([assigned selection and interference demonstrations](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DmWrOUK2zgA&t=2846)).

The visual tasks depend on their displays and conditions. If colour
vision or access to the images makes them unsuitable, use this verbal
description: the word "red" is printed in blue; the task is to name
the ink colour. Explain why word reading competes with that instruction;
do not time yourself or infer a deficit from difficulty. For the blindness
examples, explain the described attended task, unattended event or change,
and what further observation would show whether the person noticed.

Worked case: Bea is counting items in one document and misses an
unexpected icon elsewhere on the display. That illustrates an
inattentional-blindness possibility. If an icon changes between two
views and she misses the difference, the proposed explanation concerns
change blindness instead. The same absence of a report could have other
causes; our hypothetical description stipulates what she was attending
to rather than measuring all possibilities.

Complete: someone concentrates on a list while an unanticipated image
appears; separately, an item's colour differs after a view is interrupted.
Feedback: the first concerns an unexpected event under selective attention;
the second a change across views. Add a spotlight explanation: selection
favours some information rather than recording everything equally. A limit
is that attention is not a literal beam, and a missed report alone does
not identify its cause. "They have bad attention" explains neither the
conditions nor the distinction.

## What an ANT contrast can tell you

Watch the ANT window and read the three Results paragraphs. In the task,
respond to the direction of a central arrow, sometimes flanked by arrows
pointing with or against it. Warning cues differ in whether they give
timing or spatial information. Reaction-time contrasts compare these
conditions; the dependent variable is not a scan of someone's brain
([task video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y-fQywP6qa8&t=324);
[Fan and colleagues, task and Results](https://w3.ual.es/personal/tdaza/Fan%20y%20cols.pdf)).

Use the definitions below. RT means response time in milliseconds;
one millisecond is one thousandth of a second. The supplied values
are fictional didactic averages, not a participant's trial dataset,
normative values or a self-assessment scale.

| Contrast | Subtraction | Worked fictional calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Alerting effect | RT no cue minus RT double cue | 620 − 580 = 40 ms |
| Orienting effect | RT centre cue minus RT spatial cue | 600 − 550 = 50 ms |
| Conflict effect | RT incongruent minus RT congruent flankers | 650 − 570 = 80 ms |

The first two positive differences describe faster responses with the
specified cue; the third describes additional time under conflict.
Do not treat "larger" as better on all three contrasts, compare their
numbers as if they measure the same skill, or infer a diagnosis
([original subtraction definitions](https://w3.ual.es/personal/tdaza/Fan%20y%20cols.pdf);
[limits of network/disorder mapping](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430178/)).

For completion practice, no-cue RT is 610 and double-cue RT 575;
incongruent is 690 and congruent 605. Name and interpret both differences.
Feedback: alerting effect 35 ms, conflict cost 85 ms. These are
experimental contrasts, not proof that someone is calm, inattentive,
flourishing or clinically impaired. The original experiment also
discusses speed–accuracy tradeoffs; a response-time change without
accuracy context is an incomplete performance account
([Fan and colleagues, Discussion](https://w3.ual.es/personal/tdaza/Fan%20y%20cols.pdf)).

Teach an ANT trial back in three lines: what response to the central
arrow, what flankers can do, and what timing versus location cues supply.
Feedback: left/right response to the centre; congruent or incongruent
neighbours; a warning about when versus information about where the
target will occur. That procedure, not the subtraction alone, makes
the contrasts interpretable.

## Reading network associations

Read Table 2 and its adjoining paragraph. The 2019 authors associate
network abnormalities with conditions and explicitly reject a complete
one-to-one mapping between network, disorder and symptom. Multiple
networks, multiple symptoms and developmental change complicate the
picture
([Posner and colleagues, Attentional Pathologies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430178/)).

Development matters: the authors describe ADHD-associated difficulties
as involving alerting earlier and executive attention later, rather than
one unchanging profile. They also link these networks to regulation of
positive and negative affect. Those are the review's developmental and
functional claims, not a prediction of one learner's future or proof that
a worry caused an attention score
([development and affect passages](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430178/)).

Worked appraisal: the table lists executive-network associations with
schizophrenia and borderline personality disorder. It does not say that
having trouble resolving a distraction identifies either condition,
or that everyone with one condition has the same profile. That would
reverse the inference from a group-level association to an individual
diagnosis
([Table 2 and qualification](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430178/)).

Choose another actual Table 2 association and paraphrase its group-level
meaning before stating the individual inference it cannot justify.
Feedback example: the review links orienting with difficulty disengaging
from fearful stimuli in PTSD; it does not identify PTSD whenever someone
finds a feared image hard to leave. This is reading an association,
not testing a person.

Complete: "I had a large conflict contrast, so this proves I have ___"
fails because ___. Feedback: an experimental difference is neither
sufficient nor specific evidence for a diagnosis; one score also lacks
measurement and clinical context. The answer does not require filling
the diagnosis blank.

In an everyday affective episode, competing worries may accompany
difficulty selecting a task. That is a description to investigate, not
proof of which caused which. The research vocabulary can help formulate
a question without settling a personal causal story.

## Observe a practice, then judge the evidence separately

Watch Davidson and read the review selections. Focused attention selects an
object and returns when distraction is noticed; open monitoring is
described as non-reactive awareness of ongoing experience without
selecting a single object in the same way. These are research categories,
not a claim that every contemplative tradition divides practice identically
([Davidson's comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_3MVEo9fx4&t=128);
[Lippelt and colleagues, Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4171985/)).

Here is an optional one-minute observation, not an intervention protocol.
Sit or stand safely during an ordinary pause, eyes open if preferred.
For about half a minute attend to a neutral visible object and, if
distracted, note the return. For the next half minute notice ordinary
sensations or thoughts without choosing one object. Stop if uncomfortable;
you may instead describe what Bea could observe in this fictional exercise.
Do not do it while driving or handling anything requiring uninterrupted
attention.

Record the instruction followed, one observed or possible difference,
and a confound such as order, expectation, noise or familiarity.
Feedback example: "The first phase selected the cup, the second allowed
several events to be noticed. Any difference could reflect doing one
phase first; it does not show that either trains a network." A report
of no difference is equally acceptable if the comparison is accurate.

The 2014 review flags variation in practices, study designs and participant
groups and limited direct comparisons. Its synthesis suggests research
questions and possible differences, not a guarantee that a brief personal
exercise improves attention
([methodological paragraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4171985/)).

Write one claim the exercise can support and one it cannot. Feedback:
"I followed these instructions and noticed this" is an observation;
"my executive network permanently improved" is not established.
Neither success nor calm is required for the lesson to be useful.

## Independent analysis, transfer and exit

Use this unused fictional case. Dev studies a library notice after a
reminder sounds, shifts to a highlighted line and tries to ignore a
nearby conversation. Dev misses an unexpected symbol while reading;
later, a different date appears after the notice is temporarily covered,
and Dev fails to notice the change. No diagnostic history is given.

Write 650–850 words, plus the calculations below:

- **Network map:** explain readiness, selection and conflict in the
  case, with an overlap; compare early independence and later interaction
  accounts. Use Table 1 to add one correct structure/modulator example
  and state why it does not reveal Dev's chemical levels.
- **Selection:** distinguish the unexpected symbol from the unnoticed
  date change; explain a Stroop-style conflict using the verbal ink/word
  description, and the spotlight analogy plus one limit. None of these
  descriptions should become a claim of personal defect.
- **ANT:** first teach back a central-arrow trial, flankers and the
  timing/location cue distinction. Then use fictional no-cue 700,
  double-cue 640, centre 670,
  spatial 630, incongruent 740 and congruent 660 averages. Calculate
  the three contrasts; distinguish two cue effects from conflict cost.
  State why these comparisons do not yield a diagnosis or a ranking
  of which network is "best."
- **Evidence:** use the table's actual one-to-one caveat to appraise
  one actual network/condition association and a claimed diagnosis
  from distraction; compare the two practice
  instructions and give a confound plus a research-review limit.
  State why a developmental association need not be a fixed lifetime
  profile, and why co-occurring worry does not establish causal direction.

Add short private/redactable transfer notes for network mapping,
selection limits, measurement, associations and practice. For each,
connect an ordinary lived instance or a real claim you encountered to
something the framework helps you understand/choose and an unresolved
question. You need not disclose symptoms, test yourself or claim change.

| Criterion | Pass | Revise |
|---|---|---|
| Network map/comparison | Correct functions, overlap, source-lookup pairing and specific independence/interaction disagreement. | Three labels without application, chemical inference or independent boxes. |
| Selection | Correct blindness distinction, Stroop conflict, spotlight explanation and limit. | Missed report treated as disorder, literal spotlight or all missed events merged. |
| ANT | Correct trial teach-back; 60, 40 and 80 ms with subtractions and benefit/cost interpretation. | No trial explanation, reversed subtraction, larger-is-better rule or personal ranking. |
| Association | Interprets an actual table association with its one-to-one caveat. | Generic warning alone or condition inferred from one score. |
| Practice/evidence | Correct instruction comparison, specific confound and review limit, no efficacy leap. | Personal observation presented as lasting network change or cure. |
| Transfer | Each private note has an instance/claim, useful understanding/choice and uncertainty. | Personal improvement or disclosure substituted for reasoning. |

All six criteria must pass; correct only failed portions. Use the worked
examples to locate the error. The calculation answer is printed to
support self-checking; show your subtractions and interpretation rather
than copying three numbers alone.

After a day or a different activity, retrieve the three network functions
and one reason an ANT score is not a diagnosis. Explain the difference
between noticing a single exercise and establishing training efficacy.
Correct the account from the reading, not from whether you felt calm.

M4 exits at guided application: a new-case network map, correct contrast
interpretation, comparison and evidence limit. M5 will ask you to use
self/identity concepts with less supplied structure; no clinical screening,
special meditation ability or symptom improvement is a prerequisite.

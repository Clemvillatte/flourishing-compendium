Choose one ordinary pattern you would like to understand more wisely.
Perhaps you postpone a message you care about, lose the first few
minutes of an activity to an app, or keep making a manageable task
larger in your imagination. The aim is not to solve your life.
It is to complete one careful inquiry, try one safe adjustment and
decide honestly what you have learned.

## Entry and completion outcomes

Level: L4, Independent integrative project. You should already be
able to appraise evidence (M1), distinguish affective accounts and
regulation choices (M2), apply cognitive concepts (M3), interpret
attention cautiously (M4), investigate self-description (M5), analyse
mixed motives (M6), and keep a functional habit diary (M7).
If a prerequisite remains unclear, return to that specific activity,
not to an entire lecture series.

By completion, you can independently:

- **Design:** choose a meaningful, safe and feasible real-life
  question, explain why it matters to your walk of life and specify
  what would count as an informative observation.
- **Integrate:** use all six psychological dimensions on that one
  pattern without treating the lenses as six established causes.
- **Execute:** complete a bounded observation/change project with
  actual records, deviations and missing observations, not an invented
  success story or a proposal alone.
- **Evaluate:** distinguish observed outcomes from proposed mechanisms,
  compare plausible explanations and judge whether to continue,
  revise or stop without overstating causal evidence.
- **Self-assess:** apply the public rubric to your own work, correct
  an overclaim and choose a wiser next step under uncertainty.

No new reading or video is required. Retrieve and reuse the exact
sources and activities already studied; this capstone creates an
opportunity to apply them, not another source hunt.

## Choose a humane question

Select a pattern that already occurs, has low stakes and is small
enough to observe in several ordinary opportunities. Examples include
opening a chosen book, making a modest household task easier to
start, or preparing a brief message you have already decided to send.
These are options, not prescriptions; you choose the question and
justify its fit.

Do not choose self-harm, addiction, trauma exposure, eating/weight
control, sleep restriction, medical treatment, medication changes
or a dangerous interpersonal situation. Do not manipulate, record
or assess another person. Keep necessary communication, accessibility,
care duties and existing support intact; if a safe question is not
available, choose another ordinary pattern rather than press ahead.

State the connection to your own walk of life: what avoidable suffering
or friction you hope to understand, what you value and what a wiser
choice might look like. A permissible conclusion is that rest,
help or abandoning an unnecessary task is wiser than persisting.
Improved mood, productivity or compliance is not the grade.

Use a question such as "What happens around the moment I open the
book I chose?" rather than "How do I become a disciplined person?"
The first leaves room for observations and alternatives. The second
risks replacing a real inquiry with a verdict on your worth.

## Build the six-lens map

The framework organises questions about affect, cognition, attention,
self, motivation and overt behaviour. Its dimensional map is not
evidence that each proposed process caused your outcome, and the
FAP table offers therapy-specific applications rather than permission
to conduct therapy
([framework discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/);
[six dimensions and qualified FAP applications](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12392214/)).

Complete this map for your single pattern. Write "unknown" wherever
you lack evidence, and keep the personal version private if desired.

| Lens | A useful question | What to separate |
|---|---|---|
| Affect | What feeling and regulation goal, if any, do I notice? | Reported feeling, proposed function and chosen regulation strategy. |
| Cognition | What interpretation, recalled information or recurring thought is relevant? | Observed statement, a cognitive hypothesis and evidence against it. |
| Attention | What is selected, missed or competing in this context? | A reported distraction and an unmeasured network/mechanism. |
| Self | What "I am…" description or life-story interpretation appears? | Content, clarity, worth, accuracy and a revisable narrative. |
| Motivation | Which endorsed, pressured or enjoyable reasons are supported? | Mixed reasons, proposed need support and actual constraints. |
| Overt behaviour | What action occurs, after which cue, with what immediate consequence? | Observable action, repetition, a habit hypothesis and causal proof. |

Add one source-grounded concept per lens, one plausible alternative,
and what the concept helps you understand or choose. Do not force
six different interventions onto one small pattern. A lens may
illuminate why you should leave something unchanged.

For reference, the emotion-goal review distinguishes its own theoretical
proposal from empirically consolidated categories, the SCC review
separates clarity from other self-constructs, and the original ANT
paper defines laboratory contrasts rather than everyday diagnostic
tests
([regulation goals](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11479914/);
[self distinctions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/);
[ANT definitions](https://w3.ual.es/personal/tdaza/Fan%20y%20cols.pdf)).

Bring those limits into the map. A phone distraction does not give
you an executive-network score; a confident self-description is
not proof of accuracy; a plausible emotional aim is not a proven
mechanism. You have learned enough to ask more precise questions,
not to turn ordinary struggle into a diagnosis.

## Plan before seeing the result

Write a short dated plan, then retain it alongside any later revisions.
Make your choices before interpreting the observations:

- **Question and value:** the one pattern, why it matters and why
  it is a safe scope.
- **Outcome:** an observable event or consistently recorded report.
  For a reading example, "opened the chosen book at the planned
  opportunity" is more inspectable than "became a better learner."
- **Mechanism hypothesis:** the process you think might matter,
  such as a cue becoming less prominent. Name what evidence you
  would need to support that process, separately from the outcome.
- **Comparison:** three ordinary opportunities with the current
  arrangement, then three with one modest, reversible adjustment.
  If that structure does not fit your life, choose and justify
  another feasible schedule before starting.
- **Observation rule:** when and how you will note the context,
  outcome, brief interpretation and an alternative; include a
  missing-opportunity code rather than invent a value.
- **Constraints and stopping:** protect safety, access and other
  people; decide what would make you stop or revise the project.
- **Decision rule:** what would justify continuing, revising or
  stopping next time. It can be a practical judgment under uncertainty,
  not a statistical-significance threshold.

The six-opportunity structure is a course design for a manageable
inquiry, not an established intervention protocol or a sample-size
guarantee. An uncontrolled before/after comparison cannot by itself
identify the adjustment as the cause. State this limitation in the
plan before a favourable result makes it tempting to forget.

Choose just one safe adjustment, such as moving an optional distractor
slightly farther away while keeping essential communication available.
You may instead change a cue for starting a task or simplify an already
chosen action. Explain why this adjustment fits your question and why
a different one was not selected.

## Practise the judgment, then execute your own project

Consider a fictional preview: Morgan plans three ordinary reading
opportunities, then moves an optional app icon and observes three
more. Four opportunities occur; two do not, because a care responsibility
intervenes. Morgan opens the book twice and reports "the new arrangement
made reading automatic."

What should Morgan revise? Feedback: preserve the actual four
observations and mark the two missing opportunities; do not convert
them into successes or failures without a prior rule. Opening twice
is an outcome, not evidence of automaticity, and timing, fatigue,
interest or care constraints remain alternatives. A short diary
can be useful without proving habit formation
([habit mechanism and measurement caution](https://dornsife.usc.edu/wendy-wood/wp-content/uploads/sites/183/2023/10/Carden.Wood_.2018.pdf);
[duration-evidence limits](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).

Now execute your own plan over about **7–14 ordinary days**, adapting
the calendar to safe, naturally occurring opportunities. Spend at
most ten active minutes recording each of six opportunities; the
activity itself should be something you already chose to do, not
an imposed additional practice. Do not alter the record to match
your hoped-for outcome.

At the midpoint, check safety and feasibility, not just improvement.
If you revise the plan, date the change, keep the original and
explain what prompted it. A safety-driven stop is an appropriate
decision; to complete the execution criterion you still need an
actual bounded inquiry, possibly with a newly chosen safer pattern.

You may reuse genuine M7 observations as contextual background, but
do not count them again as new observations without disclosing their
dates and role. No learner observations are supplied by the course,
and the fictional Morgan preview cannot stand in for your own project.

## Interpret without polishing away uncertainty

Prepare a small table of actual opportunities, arrangement, outcome,
context and missingness. If you use a subjective rating, label the
question and scale and say that it is your course record, not a
validated diagnostic instrument. Counts and careful descriptions
are sufficient; a p-value is not required.

Then answer three different questions:

- **What happened?** Describe the outcomes, variation, exceptions,
  missing observations and any unexpected cost. Keep reports distinct
  from inferences.
- **What might explain it?** Compare the selected mechanism with
  at least two plausible alternatives. Give an observation that
  weakens your preferred account, or state clearly that the current
  record cannot discriminate.
- **What is wise to do next?** Continue, revise or stop, considering
  values, feasibility, safety, suffering and uncertainty together.
  A more humane interpretation can be useful even without more
  task completion.

For every lens, return to your initial map and identify a lived
instance, one useful understanding/choice and one uncertainty.
If you collected no direct information about a dimension, say so;
your map can retain an explicit unknown and propose a safe question
without presenting it as an established cause.

Use M6's separation of correlational tracking and short-term
experimentation when judging advice. The adherence paper's different
designs do not turn your personal before/after diary into a controlled
trial
([original adherence research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).

Use M7's planned/exploratory distinction to make your own changes
visible. Interesting post-hoc observations can be reported as such;
they should not be represented as predictions made before the project
([preregistered and exploratory distinction](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/28246/1/FullText.pdf)).

## Final artefact and public rubric

Produce a **1,200–1,600-word report**, with the dated plan, six-lens
map, observation table and rubric self-assessment as appendices.
Keep sensitive details and raw personal notes private; submit a
redacted analytic account if anyone else will review it. The
reasoning must remain inspectable without requiring intimate disclosure.

Include the question and value, source-grounded concepts, actual
execution and deviations, outcome/mechanism separation, competing
accounts and your next-step judgment. Correct at least one tempting
overclaim from an earlier draft; if none occurred, construct and
reject a clearly labelled hypothetical one. Never invent a beneficial
result to make the report feel complete.

| Criterion | Pass evidence | Revise when |
|---|---|---|
| Independent design | Own meaningful question; feasible plan; rationale for selected observations/change. | Only a supplied example or an unexecuted proposal. |
| Six-dimension integration | Every lens has a source-grounded concept, actual instance or explicit data gap, usefulness and uncertainty. | Six labels with no analysis, or all treated as proven causes. |
| Ethical execution | Actual dated records, protected safety/privacy, transparent missingness and deviations. | Invented success, hidden revisions, unsafe target or assessment of another person. |
| Mechanisms versus outcomes | Clear distinction and evidence needed for the mechanism. | A changed count proves automaticity, network dysfunction or a depleted resource. |
| Evidence and alternatives | Appropriate primary-source boundaries; at least two alternatives and a counter-observation/data limit. | Preferred explanation immunised from contrary evidence or personal diary called a trial. |
| Walk-of-life judgment | Reasoned continue/revise/stop decision considering value, constraints and suffering. | Productivity, pleasantness or compliance treated as the sole good. |
| Honest self-assessment | Rubric applied with specific evidence and one corrected overclaim. | Praise, disclosure or improvement substituted for the criteria. |
| Communication | Clear report, traceable sources, readable observations and honest limits. | Elegant story conceals missing records or unsupported facts. |

All criteria must pass; there is no additional requirement that the
adjustment "works." A thoughtful null result, a counterproductive
adjustment recognised and stopped, or evidence that the question
needs revision can satisfy the reasoning standard. A plan alone
cannot satisfy execution.

Use the rubric first yourself. If a trusted peer or tutor is available,
share only the redacted account and ask them to challenge one causal
claim and one neglected constraint; otherwise, make those challenges
in a separate self-review and label it as self-review. Do not
fabricate external feedback.

After a day, retrieve the six dimensions, state the project's strongest
and weakest inference, and revise the report once more. Completion
means you can use psychology as a careful aid to understanding and
choice, not that you have become a finished person or gained
authority to diagnose someone else.

## Workload and alignment

There are **no new required videos or research readings**.
Allow **25 minutes** for this project brief (about 2,300 words at
180 words per minute plus rereading), **75 minutes** design/preflight,
**60 minutes** records across six opportunities including the midpoint
check, **35 minutes** targeted retrieval/source checks, **45 minutes**
analysis, **90 minutes** report/appendices, **30 minutes** rubric
challenge/revision and **15 minutes** delayed retrieval.
Total: **375 estimated active minutes**, 6 hours 15 minutes,
usually across 7–14 calendar days. Calendar time is not added as
active workload, and prior M7 work is not counted twice.

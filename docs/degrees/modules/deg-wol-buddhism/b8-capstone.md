## Walk context

The walk ends where it began: ethics, sitting, and one real life. The
capstone integrates the whole degree in three deliverables a learner
makes their own: a sustained practice with records, one ethical case
worked through the walk's full toolkit, and a written position on what
this path amounts to, defended against a serious objection. The walk's
value proposition is the measure: alleviating suffering, becoming
wiser, flourishing even when uncertain or overwhelmed.

## Objectives

1. Integration: the precepts, the four tasks, the sitting practice,
   and at least two schools' readings, used together on one life
   (the case-integration criterion requires two).
2. Sustained practice with records.
3. A defended synthesis in the learner's own voice.

## Outcomes

1. Complete an eight-week sustained practice with records: at least
   three logged sittings per week (breath, metta, or the Zen-informed
   form from b6), in the b4-format log, with a one-paragraph pattern
   summary at the end (what the log shows across the eight weeks,
   including the weeks it shows nothing).
2. Work one real ethical difficulty from your life through the full
   toolkit: the five precepts as training rules (b1, b3), the four
   tasks (b2), and readings from at least two named schools (Thai Forest b5, Zen b6,
   or Insight/Secular b7; use two), stating where the readings
   converge and where they diverge on your case, and what you
   actually did.
3. Write a 1,200-word synthesis in your own voice: what the Buddhist
   path you have walked amounts to in the walk's own terms (alleviating
   suffering, becoming wiser, flourishing under uncertainty), grounded
   in the specific sources you actually used (named, with where they
   carried you), and defending your account against one serious
   objection you choose from the ones your study has raised (for
   example: the four-truths reading cannot be tested; secular Buddhism
   strips the tradition it borrows from; the precepts are too blunt for
   real cases). Name the objection's strongest form before answering
   it.
4. Self-assess the capstone against the public rubric below, in
   writing, including the one criterion you are least sure of and why.

## Structure (suggested calendar)

- Weeks 1-8: the sustained practice, logged (outcome 1). The ethical
  case (outcome 2) is chosen in week 1 and worked in parallel; the
  four-tasks worksheet from b2 may be redone on this case.
- Weeks 6-8: the synthesis written, revised once, then the
  self-assessment (outcomes 3-4).

## Assessment

The three deliverables (practice log with pattern summary; ethical
case with convergence/divergence statement; synthesis with objection
and answer) plus the written self-assessment.

Public rubric (the learner sees this before starting; a reviewer can
check every criterion from the deliverables alone):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| practice records | 24+ logged sittings over eight weeks (at least three per week), the pattern summary honest about missed weeks | a log reconstructed at the end from memory, or a week with fewer than three sittings unaccounted for |
| case integration | the precepts, the four tasks and readings from at least two schools each visibly used, their convergence and divergence on the case named | the case described with only one tool or one school used |
| synthesis grounding | the claims tied to named sources the learner actually used, with the specific places they carried the argument | general quotations arranged decoratively |
| objection | the strongest form of the objection stated before the answer, and the answer engages it | a straw objection dismissed |
| self-assessment | the rubric applied to the learner's own work, including the weakest criterion named with a reason | "I did well on everything" |

The capstone passes when every criterion is met at the exemplar
level. If a criterion is not met, the deliverable is revised until it
is: the self-assessment names the gap, states the revision it calls
for, and the revision is done before the capstone counts as passed.
Honesty about a gap is assessed as part of the self-assessment
criterion; it does not by itself satisfy the unmet criterion.

## Boundaries and passing mentions

The capstone is the learner's own work; the walk's exclusion rule
still governs (rebirth cosmology, abhidharma scholastics, tantric
ritual remain out except as passing mentions the learner may need to
place a school's position). No new sources are required; the capstone
draws on the modules' verified sources. A learner who wants to go
beyond them does so as a private extension, outside the degree's
guarantee.

## Workload arithmetic

- Measured media: none newly assigned (the capstone uses the prior

  uncounted).
- Estimated: 24+ logged sittings (about 10-15 minutes each, ~5-6 h
  total); the ethical case work ~2 h; the synthesis ~4 h across three
  weeks (drafting, revision, self-assessment). Estimates labelled as
  estimates.
- Module total: about 12 h spread across eight weeks.

## Sources

All sources are those of b1-b7, already verified in their modules'
source tables and the degree ledger. The capstone adds no new sources
by design.

## What this module does not do

It does not teach new content; it integrates. It does not certify
attainment of any meditative state; the records are records of
practice, not of achievement. The walk ends where the learner's own
practice continues, which is the cmp practicum's standing invitation.

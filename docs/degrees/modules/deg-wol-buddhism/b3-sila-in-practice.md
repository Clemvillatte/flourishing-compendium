## Walk context

b1 introduced the precepts as the ethical floor. This module deepens them
into a training: how a working Buddhist ethics actually decides cases, how
guilt is handled when you break a rule, and how the classical precepts
translate into the modernized Five Mindfulness Trainings of the Plum
Village tradition. The learner leaves able to decide cases with the
precepts as tools rather than slogans.

## Objectives

1. The precepts as training rules, in Ajahn Brahm's practical register.
2. Ethical decision-making: weighing harm, tolerance and forgiveness.
3. The classical precepts compared with the Five Mindfulness Trainings.

## Outcomes

1. Restate each of the five precepts as a training rule in your own
   words, naming what each trains (from the booklet's treatment and
   AN 5.179).
2. Apply Ajahn Brahm's ethical-decision teaching (as he presents it in
   the assigned segment) to two hard cases of your own choosing,
   showing the weighing (who is harmed, what tolerance requires, the
   talk's treatment of guilt), and state what you would actually do.
3. Compare the classical five precepts with the Five Mindfulness
   Trainings on two named points, identifying and supporting the
   differences you actually find, in whichever direction they run
   (strengthening, softening, extending, reorienting), and stating
   which rendering you would follow and why. The direction of the
   difference is your finding to support, not a conclusion the module
   hands you.
4. State in two sentences why this module leaves the booklet's rebirth
   sections unassigned (the walk's exclusion rule), and where you would
   go if you wanted that material.

## Instruction sequence

### Step 1: Ethics and tolerance, from a living teacher (~12 min, video segment)

**Ajahn Brahm, "What the Buddha Taught" (Everyday Dhamma Network
remaster of a talk recorded at Dhammaloka, Perth, 2003),
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W6pGAvtBmCw, 49:44 total; assigned
segment 00:25:46-00:37:33.** The chapters read on the opened video page: The
night of enlightenment 0:00; Understanding rebirth 2:29; Consequences of
rebirth 12:46; The law of karma 19:44; Ethics and tolerance 00:25:46;
Making ethical decisions 00:31:32; The Four Noble Truths 00:37:33.
This module assigns the ethics arc only: 00:25:46 (Ethics and
tolerance) through 00:31:32 (Making ethical decisions), and the
decision-making teaching 00:31:32 to 00:37:33 where the four-truths
chapter begins. The first 00:25:46
(rebirth and kamma cosmology) is outside this degree's scope and stays
unassigned; the four-truths arc belongs to b2.

Content note (from the opened episode page's transcript excerpts): the
ethics teaching, as Brahm presents it, includes the Rāhula ethics
instruction as the talk gives it: an action is weighed by whom it
hurts and whom it helps (harm to yourself or others, happiness to
yourself or others), his treatment of guilt in ethics, and the
acknowledge-forgive-learn sequence for mistakes. These are Brahm's
positions as the talk states them; the module attributes them to his
presentation and does not assert them as the position of every
Buddhist tradition. The talk's closing four-truths material, which
reframes the truths as happiness and unhappiness, sits at and after
the 37:33 chapter boundary, outside this module's assigned arc (it
is b2's territory and is not claimed as part of this module's
teaching). The transcript is AI-generated and error-prone, so this
module paraphrases the talk with attribution and quotes none of its
wording.

Activity: one line per chapter mark you cross (00:25:46, 00:31:32), naming
the shift in the teaching.

### Step 2: The precepts in the booklet (~30 min, reading)

**Ajahn Brahm, "What Is Buddhism?" booklet (BSWA, Perth, January 2016,
free distribution), https://bswa.org/teaching/buddhism-ajahn-brahm/.**
Read these sections: the five precepts, the Middle Way/Eightfold Path,
and the practice of the path. The booklet's rebirth and karma sections
present rebirth cosmology matter-of-factly; they stay unassigned, per
the walk's exclusion rule (see outcome 4). The same five precepts as
AN 5.179 appear here in the author's practical register.

Activity: for each precept, write the one sentence in the booklet that
most changes how you read it, and say why in a clause.

### Step 3: The modernized rendering (~10 min, reading)

**The Five Mindfulness Trainings, Plum Village tradition (revised
2022), https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-5-mindfulness-trainings/**
(free; the page states the trainings' root in
the Buddha's five precepts). Read the five trainings alongside your
b1 precept statements.

Activity: build a two-column table, classical precept beside its
mindfulness training, and mark the two rows where the modernized
rendering differs most from the classical rule.

## Practice

**One worked case, then two of your own.** Worked example (a model,
not a case you decide): a colleague asks whether you saw the email
they sent; you did, and have not answered it. Precepts in play: honest
speech (the fourth). Run the weighing: before (who is harmed by each
answer; what tolerance requires of the answer), during (say the true
thing kindly), after (review: was anyone misled?). The
before/during/after shape is this module's own organization of the
talk's harm-weighing instruction, not a structure the talk itself sets
out. If it went wrong
(the answer misled), the acknowledge-forgive-learn sequence: own the
error, ask forgiveness, take the training forward. Now choose two
situations from your own life where a precept is genuinely hard to
apply (a workplace honesty case, a relationship case, a consumption
case). For each: name the precepts in play, run the module's
weighing sequence (before, during, after; who is harmed; what
tolerance requires; the talk's treatment of guilt), state your
decision, and
name what you would do if it went wrong. About 250 words per case.

## Assessment

1. The training-rule restatements (outcome 1).
2. The two hard cases (outcome 2), with the decision named.
3. The comparison table plus a 150-word verdict (outcome 3): which
   rendering you would follow and why.
4. The two-sentence rebirth placement (outcome 4).

Rubric (observable):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| training-rule restatement | five precepts, each with what it trains named | five commands recited with no training sense |
| case decision | both cases decided with the weighing visible and the decision named | cases described with no decision reached |
| comparison | two named points of divergence, each supported by a quoted or cited phrase from each rendering, direction of difference the learner's own supported finding | "they are basically the same", or a difference asserted with no support |
| rebirth placement | two sentences naming the exclusion rule and where the material lives | silence, or no statement of where the material lives |

Self-assessment: one line on whether there was a case you put off
deciding, and why; "neither, I decided both" is an acceptable answer.
That line is part of the module.

## Boundaries and passing mentions

The rebirth-dependent material in this module's sources is deliberately
unassigned (the booklet's rebirth and karma sections; the talk's first
00:25:46), and outcome 4 asks you to state that placement plainly. The
module makes no claim about rebirth's truth or falsity; it marks where
the walk's boundary runs and why. The Four Noble Truths segment of the
talk (00:37:33 onward) is b2's territory and is not assigned here.

## What this module does not do

It does not teach meditation (b4), the four truths (b2), or the
schools compared (b5-b7). The classical-versus-modern comparison here is
between the precepts' Theravada and Plum Village renderings; the full
school-by-school comparison is later modules' work.

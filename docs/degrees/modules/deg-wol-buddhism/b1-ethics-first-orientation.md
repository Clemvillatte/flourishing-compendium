## Walk context

This is the entry module of the Buddhism walk of life. The walk is ethics
first: before doctrine, before school-by-school comparison, the learner meets
the precepts, the four immeasurables, and the goodwill meditation that carries
them. The module also states plainly what the walk covers and excludes, so the
learner knows the map from the first sitting. The walk's promise is the
owner's value proposition: guidance on the process to alleviate suffering,
become wiser, and genuinely flourish even when uncertain or overwhelmed.

## Objectives

1. The five precepts as the ethical floor of a Buddhist life.
2. The four immeasurables (loving kindness, compassion, joy, equanimity) as
   trainable states of heart.
3. Buddhist values (goodness, peace, kindness) as the basis of skilful action.
4. A first goodwill (metta) sitting, the walk's bridge into sustained practice.

## Outcomes

1. State the five precepts in your own words and give one everyday example for
   each, tied to the AN 5.179 formula you read.
2. Apply the five precepts to two concrete situations, one straightforward and
   one conflicted, explaining your reasoning and naming where the precepts
   leave the judgment to you.
3. Execute a 10-minute goodwill sitting guided by the Metta Sutta
   instructions, twice, and describe in a sentence the felt difference between
   the first and second sitting.
4. State what the walk covers (the practical schools: Secular, Zen,
   Insight/Vipassana, Theravada, Thai Forest) and what it excludes (rebirth
   cosmology, abhidharma scholastics, tantric ritual, except passing mentions
   where ethics or practice requires them) and why those boundaries serve a
   walking learner.

## Instruction sequence

The sequence moves from worked examples (a senior teacher modelling the
values, then the precepts named and deepened) through primary texts into your
own practice and case work.

### Step 1 — Buddhist values, modelled (~55 min, video, whole talk)

**Ajahn Brahm, "Buddhist Values" (Buddhist Society of Western Australia
channel), 54:16.** Ajahn Brahm talks about the core Buddhist values of
goodness, peace and kindness, and how these form the basis for skilful
interaction with others and are the guiding principles to everything he does
in life.

Activity while watching: keep a one-line note per value (goodness, peace,
kindness) on the moment in the talk where it is most alive for you. You will
reuse these notes in the assessment.

### Step 2 — The five precepts, named and deepened (~14 min, video, whole talk)

**Thanissaro Bhikkhu, "The Five Precepts for the Mind" (Dhamma Talks by
Thanissaro Bhikkhu channel), 13:16.** The talk introduces the familiar five
precepts concerning killing, stealing, illicit sex, lying and intoxicants,
then turns to their dimension in the mind: not destroying the settled mood
built in meditation, not dwelling on others' bad qualities, avoiding pleasures
that harm concentration, keeping promises to yourself about wholesome actions.

Activity: write the five precepts as the talk names them, one line each.

### Step 3 — The precepts and the goodwill instructions in the canon (~15 min, reading)

**AN 5.179 Gihisutta (Sujato translation, SuttaCentral), about 300 words.**
A lay follower is defined by restraint in five precepts: not killing living
creatures, not stealing, not committing sexual misconduct, not lying, not
consuming intoxicants.

**Metta Sutta, Khp 9 (Sujato translation, SuttaCentral), about 200 words.**
The canonical instructions for goodwill: "With love for the whole world,
unfold a boundless heart... May all beings be happy!"

Activity: mark the one instruction in the Metta Sutta you would need most on a
difficult day, and say why in a sentence.

### Step 4 — The four immeasurables, taught (~2 h 9 min, video, whole talk; spread it)

**Bhante Dhammadipa, "The Four Immeasurable Minds: practising true love for the
world" (Metta Centre channel), 2:09:06.** The four immeasurables, also called
the divine abodes: loving kindness, compassion, joy and equanimity. The talk
introduces how they are cultivated in the Pali tradition in regard to living
beings, and contrasts that with their cultivation in the Bodhisattva path
regarding all phenomena. The speaker was trained in Theravada, Zen and Chinese
traditions, ordained 1985, a student of the Burmese master Pa Auk Sayadaw
(credentials on the buddha-haus.de biography page).

Spread the talk across two or three sittings; it is the deep end of this
module and you are not expected to retain it all. Activity: after each
sitting, write one line on which of the four immeasurables felt closest and
which felt most foreign.

## Practice

**Goodwill sitting, 10 minutes, twice.** Sit comfortably, settle the breath for
a minute, then use the Metta Sutta's instruction: "With love for the whole
world, unfold a boundless heart." Begin with yourself ("may I be happy"), one
person you love, one person who is neutral to you, one person you find
difficult, then all beings. A second sitting is required, ideally a day or two
after the first; the felt difference between the two is data for your
assessment. This sitting is the walk's bridge into the cmp practicum, where a
guided sitting practice is developed over time.

## Assessment

One written piece, about 300 words, plus your practice record:

1. The five precepts in your own words, one everyday example for each
   (from AN 5.179 and the Thanissaro talk).
2. Two cases: (a) a straightforward one, for example finding a wallet on a
   bench; (b) a conflicted one, for example a friend asking you to confirm a
   small lie that protects someone's feelings. For each, state what the
   precepts direct, and name where the precepts leave the judgment to you.
3. Your two metta sitting records with the felt-difference sentence.
4. One line on which values from the Brahm talk (goodness, peace, kindness)
   your case reasoning actually used.

Rubric (observable criteria; a criterion passes only if a reader can check it
from your text alone):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| precepts named and paraphrased | all five named in plain words, each with a concrete everyday example | three or fewer precepts, or jargon repeated without meaning |
| case reasoning | both cases decided with reasons, and the open point named ("the precepts don't tell me whether to tell her now or later") | verdicts with no reasoning, or "it depends" with nothing else |
| practice record | two sittings dated, with a specific felt-difference sentence | one sitting only, or a record with no dates |
| walk map | covers and excludes named with the reason that serves a walking learner | cannot name what the walk excludes |

Self-assessment: before submitting, check your own text against the rubric and
write one line on which criterion you are least sure of. That line is part of
the module; it is how the walk teaches honest self-reading.

## Boundaries and passing mentions

This module's set carries no rebirth-dependent doctrine. The Metta Sutta's
"true state of peace" and the precepts stand on their own. Where later
modules touch rebirth (the bodhisattva vow's multi-life reading), the passing
mention will be marked and placed in its traditional frame, per the scope's
recorded tradeoff. Abhidharma scholastics and tantric ritual stay out except
where ethics or practice requires a passing mention.

## What this module does not do

It does not teach the Four Noble Truths (module b2), the schools compared
(modules b3 to b5), or a full meditation curriculum (the cmp practicum). It is
the doorway: ethics, goodwill, and the map.

- **band**: deg-wol-buddhism

- **level**: L1 Orientation (exit criteria adapted for a practice walk: the L2 element is the guided metta sitting and the precepts case, both fully guided here)

- **prerequisites**: none (entry module; open to any learner, no Buddhist background assumed)

## Walk context

This is the entry module of the Buddhism walk of life. The walk is ethics
first: before doctrine, before school-by-school comparison, the learner meets
the precepts, the four immeasurables, and the goodwill meditation that carries
them. The module also states plainly what the walk covers and excludes, so the
learner knows the map from the first sitting. The walk's promise is the
owner's value proposition: guidance on the process to alleviate suffering,
become wiser, and genuinely flourish even when uncertain or overwhelmed.

## Objectives

1. The five precepts as the ethical floor of a Buddhist life.
2. The four immeasurables (loving kindness, compassion, joy, equanimity) as
   trainable states of heart.
3. Buddhist values (goodness, peace, kindness) as the basis of skilful action.
4. A first goodwill (metta) sitting, the walk's bridge into sustained practice.

## Outcomes

1. State the five precepts in your own words and give one everyday example for
   each, tied to the AN 5.179 formula you read.
2. Apply the five precepts to two concrete situations, one straightforward and
   one conflicted, explaining your reasoning and naming where the precepts
   leave the judgment to you.
3. Execute a 10-minute goodwill sitting guided by the module's written
   five-stage guide (its method from the opened practice page, its
   orientation from the Metta Sutta), twice, and describe in a sentence
   what you noticed across the
   two sittings ("no discernible difference" is an acceptable finding).
4. State what the walk covers (the practical schools: Secular, Zen,
   Insight/Vipassana, Theravada, Thai Forest) and what it excludes (rebirth
   cosmology, abhidharma scholastics, tantric ritual, except passing mentions
   where ethics or practice requires them) and why those boundaries serve a
   walking learner.

## Instruction sequence

The sequence moves from worked examples (a senior teacher modelling the
values, then the precepts named and deepened) through primary texts into your
own practice and case work.

### Step 1: Buddhist values, modelled (~55 min, video, whole talk, two viewing stops)

**Ajahn Brahm, "Buddhist Values" (Buddhist Society of Western Australia
channel), 54:16:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TpbWVaXBV4c.** Ajahn Brahm talks about the core Buddhist values of
goodness, peace and kindness, and how these form the basis for skilful
interaction with others and are the guiding principles to everything he does
in life.

Watch in two stops (viewing stops, not content claims, since the
page prints no chapter list): 00:00:00-00:27:00 and
00:27:00-00:54:16.

The talk contains no rebirth, kamma or cosmological doctrine; its teachings
are honesty and questioning as foundational, values arising from a mindful
and peaceful heart, and ethical judgment that weighs harm.

Activity while watching: keep a one-line note per value (goodness, peace,
kindness) on the moment in the talk where it is most alive for you. You will
reuse these notes in the assessment.

### Step 2: The five precepts, named and deepened (~14 min, video, whole talk)

**Thanissaro Bhikkhu, "The Five Precepts for the Mind" (Dhamma Talks by
Thanissaro Bhikkhu channel), 13:16:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nq7YmbYFJkA.** The talk introduces the familiar five
precepts concerning killing, stealing, illicit sex, lying and intoxicants,
then turns to their dimension in the mind: not destroying the settled mood
built in meditation, not dwelling on others' bad qualities, avoiding pleasures
that harm concentration, keeping promises to yourself about wholesome actions.

Activity: write the five precepts as the talk names them, one line each.

### Step 3: The precepts and the goodwill instructions in the canon (~15 min, reading)

**AN 5.179 Gihisutta (Sujato translation, SuttaCentral), 213 measured
words: https://suttacentral.net/an5.179/en/sujato.** Read the bounded excerpt, not the whole discourse: from the opening
("Then the householder Anāthapiṇḍika...") through the five-precepts passage
("...These are the five precepts in which their actions are restrained."),
plus the four precept verses ending "...as they confuse the mind." The rest
of the discourse (verses on hells, gods and fields of merit) is optional
context. The excerpt's opening carries the discourse's traditional frame in its
full conditional: a noble disciple who possesses five things
(accomplished faith, ethics, learning, generosity and wisdom) may, while
living in their own home, declare themselves a stream-enterer, not
destined for the underworld, destined for awakening. The precepts you
read are the ethics portion of those five. You are
not asked to believe or refute this; notice that the frame is there, and
that the walk marks it as context rather than teaching it as cosmology.

**Metta Sutta, Khp 9 (Sujato translation, SuttaCentral), 236 measured
words: https://suttacentral.net/kp9/en/sujato.** The canonical instructions for goodwill, in the discourse's own
order: "May all beings be happy!" and, later, "With love for the whole
world, unfold a boundless heart." The sutta closes with "they never return
to a womb again": the same traditional frame, kept visible, not taught.

Activity: mark the one instruction in the Metta Sutta you would need most on a
difficult day, and say why in a sentence.

### Step 4: The four immeasurables, taught (~58 min, video, two bounded segments)

**Bhante Dhammadipa, "The Four Immeasurable Minds: practising true love for the
world" (Metta Centre channel), 2:09:06 total:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svWaWFOpxXw.** The four immeasurables, also called
the divine abodes: loving kindness, compassion, joy and
equanimity. The speaker was trained in Theravada, Zen and Chinese traditions, ordained 1985,
a student of the Burmese master Pa Auk Sayadaw ([monastery biography](https://www.buddha-haus.de/bhante-dhammadipa/)).

Assigned segments (from the uploader-set chapter list printed on the watch
page):
- 00:27:11-01:01:49, the four-brahmaviharas teaching arc ("4 Brahmaviharas" at
  00:27:11 through "Metta to all phenomena, method of Kumarajiva" at 01:01:49),
  about 34 minutes 38 seconds. This is the teaching.
- 01:09:42-01:33:04, the guided metta practice ("Meditation practice (metta)"
  at 01:09:42 through "No room for hate" at 01:33:04), about 23 minutes 22
  seconds. Sit with it, or listen once before sitting with it.

The remaining chapters (Q&A from 01:43:12, the Kumarajiva-method and
Buddha-nature questions) are optional for the curious; the Queen Mallika
story and the Theravada/Sanskrit object contrast inside the assigned arc are
mentioned for context. Chapter boundaries are uploader-set; the practice
segment is bounded by the next chapter mark.

Activity (after the teaching segment): write one line for each of the four
immeasurables naming where it
showed up (or failed to show up) in your week. One line each, observable from
your own notes.

## Practice

**Goodwill sitting, 10 minutes, twice, following this written guide.** The
guide follows the five-stage loving-kindness method published on the
Buddhist Centre's opened practice page
(https://thebuddhistcentre.com/text/loving-kindness-meditation: yourself,
a good friend, a neutral person, someone you dislike, everyone); the Metta
Sutta supplies the orientation ("With love for the whole world, unfold a
boundless heart"), and the timings below are this module's application:

1. (00:00-01:00) Sit comfortably, settle the breath, and let the body arrive.
2. (01:00-03:00) Yourself: "May I be happy. May I be at ease."
3. (03:00-05:00) One person you love, same phrases.
4. (05:00-07:00) One person who is neutral to you (a shopkeeper, a neighbour),
   same phrases.
5. (07:00-09:00) One person you find difficult, same phrases, at whatever
   honesty you can manage. A mechanical repetition is acceptable here.
6. (09:00-10:00) Everyone: "May all beings be happy."

A second sitting is required, ideally a day or two after the first. The record
asks you to note whether you followed the sequence and what you observed;
"no discernible difference between sittings" is an acceptable finding. This
sitting is the walk's bridge into the cmp practicum, where a guided sitting
practice is developed over time.

## Worked example (read before your own cases)

A wallet on a park bench, 230 dollars inside, no name. The precepts direct
quickly: taking it is stealing, so the taking question closes immediately.
The lying question opens only if you tell the finder's story wrongly, so
honesty closes that too. Where the precepts leave judgment to you: whether to
leave it with the nearest shop, hand it to police, or wait an hour in case the
owner returns; the precepts do not answer that, and your values (goodness,
peace, kindness) do the work there. Notice the shape: the precepts act as a
floor, the immeasurables as a direction, and the judgment stays yours.

## Assessment

One written piece, about 300 words, plus your practice record and walk-map
statement:

0. State the walk's map in three lines: the schools it covers, what it
   excludes, and the reason the exclusions serve a walking learner (outcome
   4). As you draft it, check your two cases against the map: name any
   question a case raised that the walk excludes, and state where that
   question lives instead (a school's own doctrine, or outside the walk).

Then:

1. The five precepts in your own words, one everyday example for each
   (from AN 5.179 and the Thanissaro talk).
2. Two cases: (a) a straightforward one, for example finding a wallet on a
   bench; (b) a conflicted one, for example a friend asking you to confirm a
   small lie that protects someone's feelings. For each, state what the
   precepts direct, and name where the precepts leave the judgment to you.
3. Your two metta sitting records with the observation sentence (what you
   noticed across them; "no discernible difference" is acceptable).
4. One line on which values from the Brahm talk (goodness, peace, kindness)
   your case reasoning actually used.

Rubric (observable criteria; a criterion passes only if a reader can check it
from your text alone):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| precepts named and paraphrased | all five named in plain words, each with a concrete everyday example | three or fewer precepts, or jargon repeated without meaning |
| case reasoning | both cases decided with reasons, and the open point named ("the precepts don't tell me whether to tell her now or later") | verdicts with no reasoning, or "it depends" with nothing else |
| practice record | two sittings dated, with a sentence on what you observed across them ("no discernible difference" is an acceptable finding) | one sitting only, or a record with no dates |
| walk map statement | three lines: schools covered, exclusions, the reason exclusions serve a walking learner | cannot name what the walk excludes |
| four immeasurables notes | one line per immeasurable naming where it showed up or failed to show up in the week | four lines that all describe the same feeling, or no lines |

Self-assessment: before submitting, check your own text against the rubric and
write one line on which criterion you are least sure of. That line is part of
the module; it is how the walk teaches honest self-reading.

## Boundaries and passing mentions

The assigned texts carry the tradition's own frame, and the module says so
plainly: AN 5.179's opening declares a precept-keeping lay follower a
stream-enterer destined for awakening, and the Metta Sutta closes with no
return to a womb. These passages are kept visible as context, not taught as
cosmology, and neither argued for nor against. The Metta Sutta's "state of
peace" and the precepts stand on their own. Where later modules touch rebirth
(the bodhisattva vow's multi-life reading), the passing mention will be
marked and placed in its traditional frame, per the scope's recorded
tradeoff. Abhidharma scholastics and tantric ritual stay out except where
ethics or practice requires a passing mention.

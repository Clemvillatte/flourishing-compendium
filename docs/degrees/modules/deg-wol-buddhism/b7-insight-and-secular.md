## Walk context

The walk's school map ends with the two schools that rewrote Buddhism for
the modern West: Insight Meditation (Vipassana as taught in the American
Insight lineage) and Secular Buddhism. They are siblings with a
disagreement: both meditate, both read the four truths as tasks, and they
part ways on what Buddhism finally is (a path of practice continuous
with the tradition, or a pragmatic ethical project the tradition serves).
This module teaches both, then asks where you stand.

## Objectives

1. Mindfulness of breath, taught in the Insight lineage's introductory
   course.
2. Batchelor's case for a secular Buddhism: pragmatic, ethical,
   flourishing-oriented.
3. The two accounts compared on one question, by the learner.

## Outcomes

1. Practice mindfulness of breath following the Insight course's Week 1
   instructions (transcript read, audio followed), for two logged
   sittings.
2. State Batchelor's lecture in 200 words: what a secular Buddhism
   keeps (ethics, meditation, the four truths as tasks), what it sets
   aside, and what human flourishing has to do with it.
3. Compare the two accounts on ONE named question, for example: are the
   four truths a description of reality or a set of tasks; what role
   does rebirth play or not play; is awakening a traditional attainment
   or a human flourishing. State where they agree, where they disagree,
   and what turns on the disagreement.
4. Defend your own position in the comparison's terms (which account you
   would practice by, and why), against the strongest objection you can
   name from the other side.

## Instruction sequence

### Step 1 — The Insight course's first week (~30 min reading + 2 audio-assisted sittings)

**Gil Fronsdal, "Introduction to Mindfulness Meditation," Week 1:
Mindfulness of Breath.** Read the Week 1 transcript from the Insight
Meditation Center's course page
(https://www.insightmeditationcenter.org/books-articles/introduction-to-meditation-transcripts/;
six-week course transcripts, free, Creative Commons BY-NC-ND 3.0). Then
sit with the course's Class 1 recording
(https://www.audiodharma.org/series/24654; AudioDharma series page,
"freely offered by Insight Meditation Center"; Class 1 measured at
1:26:55 on the series page; the first portion carries the instructions
and guided practice, and you may spread the recording across two
sittings).

The teacher: senior guiding co-teacher at the Insight Meditation Center
(Redwood City) and the Insight Retreat Center (Santa Cruz); Buddhist
practice since 1975; teaching at IMC since 1990; authorized teacher in
the Insight Meditation lineage of Theravada Buddhism and in Japanese
Soto Zen; PhD in Buddhist Studies from Stanford (credentials opened on
the AudioDharma institutional speaker page,
https://www.audiodharma.org/speakers/1).

Activity: log the two sittings in your b4-format log (resource, guided
or unguided, completion, one observation).

### Step 2 — The secular case (~1 h 27 min, video, whole lecture; spread it)

**Stephen Batchelor, "A Secular Buddhism", Radboud Reflects (Radboud
University), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hhlj_SU9SAE, 1:26:35.**
Lecture given 9 July 2018 at Theater Hall C, Radboud University. The
page's description frames it: Buddhism as primarily an ethical
perspective for overcoming human problems, a secular, pragmatic basis
for human flourishing; the key moments include the historical
secularization of Buddhism in Burma and the Vipassana movement, and
Buddhist approaches to suffering. The speaker: eight years as a Tibetan
Buddhist monk, then four years training in Son (Zen) meditation in
South Korea under Kusan Sunim (credentials opened on his official
biography page, https://stephenbatchelor.org/).

Spread the lecture across two or three sittings. Activity: one line per
sitting on the thread of the argument as you heard it.

### Step 3 — The comparison (the module's core, ~45 min)

Build a comparison table on your chosen question (outcome 3), citing the
specific places in the two sources where each account commits itself.
Then write your defended position (outcome 4, ~300 words), including
the strongest objection from the side you did not choose, and your
answer to it.

## Practice

Two logged Insight-course sittings (Step 1) continue the b4 habit; the
comparison work is the module's analysis. If the four-truths-as-tasks
question is chosen, return to your b2 worksheet: your own four-tasks run
is evidence in this comparison.

## Assessment

1. The two sitting logs (outcome 1).
2. The 200-word Batchelor statement (outcome 2).
3. The comparison table with citations (outcome 3).
4. The defended position with objection and answer (outcome 4).

Rubric (observable):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| practice logs | two dated logs in the b4 format naming the course as resource | logs missing or unattributed to the course |
| Batchelor statement | keeps, sets aside and flourishing each named | a summary of the lecture's topics with no case identified |
| comparison | one question, both accounts cited to specific places, the disagreement's stakes named | two parallel summaries that never meet |
| defended position | a position stated with the other side's strongest objection answered | a preference asserted with no objection considered |

Self-assessment: one line on whether your b2 four-tasks run supports
one side of your comparison more than the other. That line is part of
the module.

## Boundaries and passing mentions

Batchelor's secular reading sets rebirth aside explicitly; the
Insight lineage treats it as traditionally present but not load-bearing
for introductory practice. The module marks both placements as the
schools' own positions and takes no side; the walk's exclusion rule
still governs (passing mention, no cosmology unit). Zen appears here
only as Fronsdal's second authorization and Batchelor's early training;
the Zen module (b6) is separate.

## What this module does not do

It does not teach Zen (b6, owed), the capstone (b8), or new
precept content. The comparison here is between two Western-facing
adaptations; the walk's earlier schools (b5) are the tradition both
are adapting, and the learner is expected to bring b5's inside view to
the comparison.

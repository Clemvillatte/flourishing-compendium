## Walk context

The walk's school map ends with the two schools that rewrote Buddhism for
the modern West: Insight Meditation (Vipassana as taught in the American
Insight lineage) and Secular Buddhism. Each account is held to what its
opened sources actually state: Fronsdal's course teaches mindfulness of
breath (the Week 1 transcript, opened: talk of 10/3/07, CC BY-NC-ND
3.0); Batchelor's lecture is presented on its opened page as primarily
an ethical, pragmatic project for human flourishing (the lecture
description), and his opened biography states his Tibetan monastic
years and Son training under Kusan Sunim. This module teaches both,
then asks where you stand, comparing only positions each opened
source states. NOTE: an earlier draft used a four-truths-as-tasks
comparison example; it was WITHDRAWN in verification (2026-10-26):
neither the opened transcripts page nor Batchelor's opened biography
states the tasks framing, so no such example is offered.

## Objectives

1. Mindfulness of breath, taught in the Insight lineage's introductory
   course.
2. Batchelor's case for a secular Buddhism: pragmatic, ethical,
   flourishing-oriented.
3. The two accounts compared on one question, by the learner.

## Outcomes

1. Practice mindfulness of breath following the Insight course's
   VERIFIED Week 1 transcript instructions ("Mindfulness of Breath",
   talk of 10/3/07, CC BY-NC-ND 3.0, opened on the course page), for
   two logged sittings. The Class 1 recording is OPTIONAL
   supplementary listening with verified metadata only; no claim is
   made about its guided segments, and the sittings do not depend on
   it.
2. State Batchelor's lecture in 200 words: what a secular Buddhism
   keeps, what it sets aside, and what human flourishing has to do
   with it, each item stated from the lecture you watched, and not from
   this module's summary.
3. Compare the two accounts on ONE named question that BOTH opened
   sources actually address, citing the specific place in each source
   where the position is stated; if a source does not state it, the
   comparison question is not available. No example question is
   offered, because the earlier four-truths-as-tasks example failed
   verification (see Walk context). State where they agree, where
   they disagree, and what turns on the disagreement.
4. Defend your own position in the comparison's terms (which account you
   would practice by, and why), against the strongest objection you can
   name from the other side.

## Instruction sequence

### Step 1: The Insight course's first week (~30 min reading + 2 transcript-based sittings)

**Gil Fronsdal, "Introduction to Mindfulness Meditation," Week 1:
Mindfulness of Breath.** Read the Week 1 transcript from the Insight
Meditation Center's course page
(https://www.insightmeditationcenter.org/books-articles/introduction-to-meditation-transcripts/;
six-week course transcripts, free, Creative Commons BY-NC-ND 3.0). Then
sit twice following the Week 1 transcript's instructions
(OPTIONAL: the course's Class 1 recording,
https://www.audiodharma.org/series/24654; AudioDharma series page,
"freely offered by Insight Meditation Center"; Class 1 measured at
1:26:55 on the series page; verified metadata only, no claim about
its guided segments). Two cautions the module states honestly:
the printed transcript series and the recorded class series are
SEPARATE sources (the series pages pair them, but the recording is
not a reading of the transcript), and the series page prints no
chapter list, so no guided-segment timestamps are claimed. Listen in
two stops (listening stops, not content claims): 00:00:00-00:43:00
and 00:43:00-01:26:55.

The teacher: senior guiding co-teacher at the Insight Meditation Center
(Redwood City) and the Insight Retreat Center (Santa Cruz); Buddhist
practice since 1975; teaching at IMC since 1990; authorized teacher in
the Insight Meditation lineage of Theravada Buddhism and in Japanese
Soto Zen; PhD in Buddhist Studies from Stanford (credentials opened on
the AudioDharma institutional speaker page,
https://www.audiodharma.org/speakers/1).

Activity: log the two sittings in your b4-format log (resource, guided
or unguided, completion, one observation).

### Step 2: The secular case (~1 h 27 min, video, whole lecture)

**Stephen Batchelor, "A Secular Buddhism", Radboud Reflects (Radboud
University), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hhlj_SU9SAE, 1:26:35.**
Lecture dated on the watch page's description as given 9 July 2018
at Theater Hall C, Radboud University. The
page's description frames it: Buddhism as primarily an ethical
perspective for overcoming human problems, a secular, pragmatic basis
for human flourishing (checked this pass against the description's
own text; the "Buddhism is not only about rituals and meditation"
line appears in the description as a quotation attributed to Batchelor,
and the flourishing framing is the description's own wording). The page additionally surfaces a key-moments feature listing
the historical secularization of Buddhism in Burma and the Vipassana
movement, and Buddhist approaches to suffering; that list is a
page-level feature, not the description's text. That Burma/Vipassana
moment is
covered in the lecture itself, in Batchelor's own account of it (the
passage was checked against the recording's caption text; the talk is
the account's carrier, not an independent historical verification of
it). The speaker: eight years as a Tibetan
Buddhist monk, then four years training in Son (Zen) meditation in
South Korea under Kusan Sunim (credentials opened on his official
biography page, https://stephenbatchelor.org/).

Watch in two stops (viewing stops, not content claims, since the page
prints no chapter list): 00:00:00-00:43:00 and 00:43:00-01:26:35. Activity: one line
per stop on the thread of the argument as you heard it.

### Step 3: The comparison (the module's core, ~45 min)

Build a comparison table on your chosen question (outcome 3), citing the
specific places in the two sources where each account commits itself.
Then write your defended position (outcome 4, ~300 words), including
the strongest objection from the side you did not choose, and your
answer to it.

## Practice

Two logged Insight-course sittings (Step 1) continue the b4 habit; the
comparison work is the module's analysis. If your chosen comparison
question touches the four truths, return to your b2 worksheet: your own
four-tasks run is reflection material for your side of the comparison,
carried by the sources you cite under outcome 3's rule.

## Assessment

1. The two sitting logs (outcome 1).
2. The 200-word Batchelor statement (outcome 2).
3. The comparison table with citations (outcome 3).
4. The defended position with objection and answer (outcome 4).

Rubric (observable):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| practice logs | two dated logs in the b4 format naming the course as resource | logs missing or unattributed to the course |
| Batchelor statement | keeps, sets aside and flourishing each named | a summary of the lecture's topics with no case identified |
| comparison | one question, both accounts cited to specific places, the disagreement's stakes named | two parallel summaries that never meet |
| defended position | a position stated with the other side's strongest objection answered | a preference asserted with no objection considered |

Self-assessment: one line on whether your b2 four-tasks run supports
one side of your comparison more than the other. That line is part of
the module.

# b5 — The Thai Forest Way: Theravada in Practice

## Walk context

The walk's school map begins with the tradition its teachers so far
have come from: the Thai Forest lineage, a living school of the
Theravada way. Here the learner meets the tradition as a tradition: its
elders' voices, its preference for practice over scholastics, and its
reading of the path the learner has already been walking since b1. The
Ajahn Chah book supplies the lineage stories the scope's elective
named; the Sumedho talk is the school's senior Western voice.

## Objectives

1. The Thai Forest tradition's frame: lineage, practice-first ethos,
   Theravada continuity.
2. The tradition's elders read aloud: Sumedho on mindfulness and
   wisdom; Chah's teaching stories.
3. One school of the map, learned from inside rather than compared
   from outside.

## Outcomes

1. State in your own words what the Thai Forest tradition is (the
   Theravada "way of the elders" frame; the forest-monastery practice
   lineage descending through Ajahn Chah; practice over scholastics),
   citing where you read each element.
2. Listen to the Sumedho talk and write a 150-word precis of its
   central teaching on mindfulness and wisdom, with one line on what
   surprised you.
3. Read two teachings from the Ajahn Chah collection and for one of
   them write: the situation it addresses, the instruction it gives,
   and one place in your current life where that instruction would
   bite.
4. Place the tradition on the walk's school map: name what it shares
   with the Theravada canon you have already read (the precepts, the
   four truths, the suttas) and what marks it as a distinct living
   school.

## Instruction sequence

### Step 1 — The senior teacher (~1 h 2 min, video, whole talk; spread over two sittings)

**Ajahn Sumedho, "Mindfulness and Wisdom", Amaravati Buddhist Monastery
channel, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SElyOzgkxjo, 1:01:36.** A Dhamma
talk given on 13 May 2017 at Amaravati's temple during an eight-day open
retreat attended by many of his senior disciples. The speaker: an
American-born bhikkhu ordained 1967, nine years of training under
Ajahn Chah at Wat Pah Pong in Thailand, founder of Amaravati in 1984
(credentials opened on the monastery's biography page,
https://amaravati.org/biographies/ajahn-sumedho/). The same audio is
freely downloadable from the monastery's site.

No chapter list is printed for this talk; the assignment is the whole
recording, spread across at least two sittings. Activity: after each
sitting, one line on the thread of the teaching as you heard it.

### Step 2 — The lineage's book (~40 min, reading, assigned sections)

**The Collected Teachings of Ajahn Chah (single volume), Amaravati
Buddhist Monastery's free-distribution edition,
https://amaravati.org/dhamma-books/the-collected-teachings-of-ajahn-chah/.**
Read: the introduction on the Thai Forest tradition and the Theravada
lineage ("the way of the elders", the Southern School, the forest
practice standard), plus two teachings of your choice from the
collection. The tradition's emphasis, as the introduction frames it, is
following the spirit and standards of the Buddha's way in forest
practice rather than scholastic commentary.

Activity: for each teaching, the one-line record named in outcome 3.

### Step 3 — Tie it to the canon you already hold

Return to AN 5.179 and SN 56.11 (b1 and b2). Skim both again, now
knowing they are Theravada canon. Note one place where the Thai Forest
teachers you just read sound closest to the canon, and one place where
their register is plainly their own.

## Practice

Continue the sitting habit from b4: two more logged sittings this week
(breath or metta, your choice), each one line in the same log. The
tradition this module teaches is a practice tradition; the log is how
the walk keeps you honest about that.

## Assessment

1. The tradition statement (outcome 1, ~200 words, with citations to
   the pages and talk).
2. The Sumedho precis (outcome 2, 150 words plus the surprise line).
3. The two Ajahn Chah teaching records (outcome 3).
4. The school-map placement (outcome 4, ~150 words).

Rubric (observable):

| criterion | exemplar of strong work | rejection example |
|---|---|---|
| tradition statement | lineage, practice-first ethos and Theravada frame each named with a source | generic "forest monks meditate" |
| precis | the talk's central teaching stated in the learner's words with the surprise named | a topic list with no teaching identified |
| Chah records | situation, instruction and application each named for the chosen teaching | a quotation with no application |
| map placement | canon continuity and distinct-school marks both named | "it is the same as Theravada" with nothing added |

Self-assessment: one line on whether the tradition's practice-first
ethos matches your own experience of the walk so far. That line is part
of the module.

## Boundaries and passing mentions

The Ajahn Chah collection contains material on rebirth and karma in the
Thai Forest register; the assigned sections handle it as the tradition's
own frame, and the walk's exclusion rule still applies (passing
mention, no cosmology unit). The module teaches one school from
inside; the comparative work (Zen, Insight, Secular) is b6-b7, and no
claim is made here that the Thai Forest way is the walk's preferred
school.

## What this module does not do

It does not compare schools (b6-b7), teach new meditation technique
(b4 owns the training plan), or argue the tradition's metaphysical
claims. The Thai Forest lineage stories here serve the scope's elective
inside the core module that names their school.

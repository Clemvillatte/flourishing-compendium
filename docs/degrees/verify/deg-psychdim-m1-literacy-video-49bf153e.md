### Prerequisite and inference cautions

Clip A refers back to standard error. Before it, the module should
explain that the standard error concerns the sampling variability of an
estimate, unlike the spread of individual observations. A short optional
remedial segment is **03:29–09:21 = 5:52**, covering population versus
sample, estimate uncertainty and the standard-error introduction; this
optional segment was read as boundary context in the opened transcript.
([Statistical inference and standard-error chapters](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKiX54NglCo&t=209).)

Clip A includes formulas and a t-test illustration. Calculation and
memorising critical values are not necessary for M1's L1 appraisal.
Require learners to identify the estimate, describe uncertainty and
avoid an incorrect causal or practical-importance claim, rather than
reproduce the arithmetic.
([Assigned interval and testing explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKiX54NglCo&t=1546).)

The lecture's confidence-interval/test correspondence is illustrated
with the same two-sided t-model. Do not generalise it into a claim that
every reported confidence interval and p-value in every paper must
agree, or into an overlapping-intervals test of two coefficients.
([Worked t-model example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKiX54NglCo&t=2021).)

The lecture uses a hypothetical cherry-juice trial, not Woolley's
cross-lagged observational model. It supplies no evidence about that
model's adjustment variables, missingness or coefficient scaling; keep
those distinctions in the source-specific M1 appraisal.
([Lecture example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKiX54NglCo&t=2392).)

## Smith defect and retained primary anchor

The reopened Smith text says that “significant at a p < .05 level”
means that repeating a study 100 times should yield “this result” by
chance fewer than five times. It fails to condition that sentence on
the null model and confuses a realised p-value with a test's long-run
error threshold. Its glossary also calls probability values an
“established threshold.”
([Smith, NHST section and vocabulary](https://nobaproject.com/modules/thinking-like-a-psychological-scientist).)

The reopened ASA statement defines a p-value under a specified model
as the probability of a statistical summary equal to or more extreme
than the observed summary. Principles 2 and 5 explicitly exclude the
chance-alone/null-probability and magnitude/importance interpretations.
([ASA 2016, “What is a p-Value?” and principles 2 and 5](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Real_World/ASA_statement.pdf).)

Retain only that definition and those two principles as the brief
primary-reference anchor. This is a precision safeguard, not a claim
that no suitable statistical video exists. Smith's non-statistical
sections can remain a **bounded reading fallback**, not the whole
module: scientific versus everyday reasoning, interpreting research,
scientific theories, objectivity and science in context remain distinct
from the selected statistical video.
([Smith section headings and text](https://nobaproject.com/modules/thinking-like-a-psychological-scientist),
[ASA statement](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/Real_World/ASA_statement.pdf).)

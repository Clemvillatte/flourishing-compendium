## Claim 1: the archived SCCS instrument's structure

The receipt states the UBC archive document contains 12 items with
anchors 1 (strongly disagree) through 5 (strongly agree), items 1–5,
7–10 and 12 reverse-keyed, items 6 and 11 not. I fetched the same
document
(https://dlab.sauder.ubc.ca/sjdm/dmidi/files/Campbell_et_al._(1996)_Self-Concept_Clarity.doc):
**12 items; 1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree; reverse-keyed
items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12**: identical to the receipt's
statement (which means 6 and 11 are not reverse-keyed, as the receipt
says). A verbatim item from the document: "In general, I have a clear
sense of who I am and what I am." **Confirmed.** The receipt's
arithmetic consequences (6−r reversal on a five-point form; sum 12–60;
mean 1–5) follow from these confirmed anchors and are correct.

## Claim 2: the Manchiraju review's related-constructs distinctions

The receipt states the 2026 review distinguishes perceived clarity and
consistency of self-beliefs from self-esteem, self-knowledge accuracy
and the content of those beliefs (citing its related-constructs section
and Table 1). I fetched the same review
(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/, "Self-concept
clarity: a comprehensive and integrative review", Srikant Manchiraju,
Frontiers in Psychology, published 26 June 2026): all three
distinctions appear verbatim: "It differs from self-esteem, the
evaluative dimension of the self... SCC concerns the structure and
certainty of self-beliefs irrespective of their valence"; "SCC does
not require self-knowledge accuracy: individuals may hold clear but
inaccurate self-views"; "SCC thus concerns the clarity, coherence, and
stability of self-beliefs rather than their specific content or
positivity", and Table 1 is titled "Self-concept clarity compared with
related self-constructs". **Confirmed.**

## Claim 3 (added same session): the Vartanian et al. 2023 SCCS usage

The receipt states Vartanian, Hayward and Carter's article (published
online 2022, in a 2023 issue) uses the same 12-item SCCS with five
response points for its student sample and seven for its community
sample, computes mean scores after reverse coding, and uses each
scale's midpoint to group participants. The direct PDF fetch failed
(fetcher_error on the URL's encoding), but a web search returned the
indexed text of the exact cited PDF
(https://www2.psy.unsw.edu.au/Users/lvartanian/Publications/Vartanian,%20Hayward,%20&%20Carter%20(2023).pdf):
"Incorporating physical appearance into one's sense of self:
Self-concept clarity, thin-ideal internalization, and
appearance-self integration", Self and Identity 22:2, 181–196,
published online 25 April 2022; Study 1 (162 female students; 262
female community members); "For the student sample, items were rated on
a 5-point scale (1 = Strongly disagree, 5 = Strongly agree); for the
community sample, the scale was increased to 7 points"; "Some items
were reverse-coded and higher mean scores indicate higher self-concept
clarity"; "participants were classified as being relatively high in
self-concept clarity if they scored at or above the mid-point of the
scale... relatively low... below the mid-point". **Confirmed**:
every element of the receipt's passage matches, including the
receipt's caution that the midpoint grouping is an analytic choice in
that study, which the article's own "for ease of presentation"
wording supports.

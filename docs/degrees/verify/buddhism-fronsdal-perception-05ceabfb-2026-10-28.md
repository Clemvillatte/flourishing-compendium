## Teaching relevant to the mission

- **Responding to hurt, 05:22–06:27:** in the
  [transcript](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception),
  Fronsdal criticizes using impermanence to dismiss people harmed
  within a Buddhist group. The example gives the teaching an ethical
  limit: invoking change cannot by itself answer another person's
  grievance.
- **Conditions for insight, 08:34–21:27:** the
  [talk](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception)
  connects insight into change with stability, inquiry into suffering
  with well-being, and inquiry into self with confidence. Its examples
  distinguish a useful teaching from advice that overlooks a person's
  circumstances. These are Fronsdal's pedagogical claims; this
  verification supplies no clinical trial evidence for them.
- **Ordinary responsibilities, 32:24–35:46:** the
  [question-and-answer passage](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception)
  addresses families, commitments and decisions. Fronsdal explains
  how a different experience of perception can become a reference
  point for noticing assumptions and grasping when returning to
  ordinary life. This directly supports the Walk of Life frame.
- **Confidence and stubbornness, 43:11–45:18:** the
  [answer](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception)
  suggests asking trusted people for feedback and examining the felt
  difference between the two states. Treat these as proposed ways
  of inquiry, not a validated diagnostic test.
- **Learning through action, 45:18–48:22:** the
  [kitchen example](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception)
  traces confidence through learning to chop carrots, feedback and
  gradually acquiring skills. The following discussion asks
  practitioners to notice capacities they have already developed.

These passages establish substantive teaching beyond the source listing.
The strongest fit is practical interpretation of change and self,
supported by worked examples. No new source discovery or expansion of
the degree's topic scope is required.

## Keep interpretive limits intact

The [opening argument](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception)
distinguishes perceptions from blanket statements about reality and
attributes that distinction to Fronsdal's study of early texts.
At [36:49–38:51](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception),
he limits his answer about terminology and says he has not completed the
corresponding research in the Chinese Agamas. A summary must preserve
that limit rather than treating his reading as a demonstrated claim
about every early Buddhist textual tradition.

At [49:31–56:20](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/three-characteristics-perception),
Fronsdal also resists treating either Zen or Vipassana as a single
uniform practice and distinguishes a tradition's rhetoric from what
practitioners do. This is useful for avoiding a flattened comparison.
The transcript contains recognition errors, especially in technical
terms, so exact quotations and specialist spelling need audio checks.

### AR-01: Justice, Episode 10, "The Good Citizen" (Michael Sandel)

- Identity and access: oembed confirms the video exists on the Harvard University channel
  with the title "Justice: What's The Right Thing To Do? Episode 10: THE GOOD CITIZEN"
  (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MuiazbyOSqQ). The watch page is freely playable, no
  paywall or app gate.
- Duration: 55:07 (lengthSeconds 3308, read from the watch page player data, 2026-10-25).
- Instructor credentials: Michael J. Sandel is the Anne T. and Robert M. Bass Professor of
  Government at Harvard University, where he has taught political philosophy since 1980
  (bio page: https://justiceharvard.org/michael-sandel/).
- Language: English. The watch page offers a transcript panel with captions.
- Content coverage (from the watch page description of the recording itself): Part One,
  "The Good Citizen", presents Aristotle's view that politics should cultivate virtue and
  that the telos of the state is the good life, then stages the Casey Martin golf cart
  debate about the purpose of a practice. Part Two, "Freedom vs. Fit", takes up objections
  about individual choice and rights, including Aristotle's defence of slavery as a
  fitting social role.
- Relevant segment for mod-phil-1: the whole episode is the assigned lecture; the page
  prints the two-part structure but no part start times, so the pilot should assign the
  full 55:07 until timestamps are read from the transcript.
- Verification status: verified-video.
- Flag for the deg-philosophy owner: the pilot asked whether this episode stages the
  three-way comparison the redesigned outcome 3 needs (a dilemma analysed as
  deontologist, consequentialist, then virtue ethicist). It does not. The episode teaches
  Aristotle's virtue-based theory of justice and objections to it, and it never runs a
  side-by-side deontology versus consequentialism comparison. Outcome 3's comparative
  lecture needs a different assignment; earlier episodes of the Justice series cover
  utilitarian and Kantian ground and would have to be verified separately. AR-01 remains
  well matched to outcome 2 (why Aristotle ties virtue to flourishing rather than
  rule-following or outcomes).

### AR-04: Aristotle (Edith Hall, Gresham College)

- Identity and access: the Gresham page (https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/aristotle)
  hosts the full recording, free, consistent with Gresham College's four-century-old free
  public lecture mission. oembed for the official Gresham College YouTube upload of the
  same lecture confirms it exists (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ido9HCquCi0).
- Duration: the Gresham watch-now page does not print one. The official Gresham College
  YouTube upload of this 4 March 2021 lecture prints 44:17 (read from the upload listing,
  2026-10-25). The degree owner should attach the printed figure with this provenance, or
  have the integrator point the canonical URL at the Gresham YouTube upload where the
  duration is printed on the page.
- Instructor credentials: Edith Hall, Visiting Gresham Professor in Classics and Visiting
  Professor of Classics at King's College London, a scholar of ancient Greek literature
  and cultural history (stated on the Gresham lecture page).
- Language: English. Gresham provides a transcript and downloadable versions via its
  lectures-and-events page for this talk.
- Content coverage: the Gresham page summary states the lecture works through Aristotle's
  method, logic, four causes and teleology, then his three branches of knowledge, and
  covers his ethics, virtue, happiness and the mean, plus politics, rhetoric and
  poetics. The YouTube upload description states his core philosophical beliefs are laid
  down in the Nicomachean Ethics, Politics and Rhetoric, which are analysed in the
  lecture. This satisfies the pilot's question: the talk does introduce eudaimonia
  territory (happiness as the human good, virtue, the mean), so it serves outcome 1 and
  outcome 2 as the definitional lecture.
- Relevant segment: assigned as a whole talk, 44:17, once the duration provenance above
  is accepted.
- Verification status: verified-video (with the duration provenance flag above).

## Teaching that can be located

- **The question under examination, 28:29–29:31:** the
  [transcript](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
  introduces chapter 18's question about the relationship between self
  and aggregates, then specifies that the target is an independently
  or inherently existing self. This provides an explicit philosophical
  question rather than a general promise of insight.
- **Two approaches, 29:31–42:54:** the
  [class transcript](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
  compares letting go of self-grasping as it arises with bringing the
  assumed self into view and examining it through reasoning. Henkel
  qualifies the comparison as he discusses differences within Zen and
  Tibetan approaches. Teach this as his comparison; avoid presenting
  two uniform camps across all Buddhist traditions.
- **Compassion and the reason for study, 42:54–49:38:** the
  [transcript](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
  asks whether studying oneself becomes self-absorption and answers
  through the bodhisattva commitment to help others. Its account of
  liberation through emptiness is a Buddhist doctrinal position,
  explicitly connected to Henkel's own conviction.
- **Conventional self and over-negation, 66:06–75:46:** the
  [discussion](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
  distinguishes a dependently arisen person and a conceptually imputed
  self from the inherently existing self being questioned. It retains
  ordinary uses of “I” and warns against denying too much.
- **Purpose of the analysis, 84:38–86:44:** the
  [closing discussion](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
  presents the aggregates as tools for identifying clinging and
  suffering, while acknowledging differences in how teachers frame
  self and not-self. This is the clearest short passage connecting
  the philosophical analysis to the degree's practical purpose.

## Limits that affect assignment

The [opening and middle sections](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
include traditional accounts of the Buddha and Nagarjuna, rebirth
language, enlightenment narratives and speculative remarks about
evolution and animal experience. Reading the transcript verifies that
the teaching contains these claims; it does not authenticate them as
history or psychological science. The full class therefore needs a
scope decision under the owner's restriction on metaphysical content.

The [class itself](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5)
repeatedly refers to handouts that participants are holding, including
a list of types of self, and anticipates further work on the aggregates
in the next class. Those handouts were not independently obtained here.
The class cannot yet be treated as a self-contained explanation of all
the concepts it introduces.

At [51:47–54:58 and 79:06 onward](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/nagarjunas-middle-way-examination-self-class-1-5),
Henkel discusses grounding, fear and the difficulty of investigating
self-experience. The existing experienced-practitioner setting matters
when deciding placement. This receipt adds no clinical efficacy claim
and no new learner-facing safety policy.

## Recommendation for the degree owner

The source has substantive, inspectable teaching. A bounded assignment
could use the conventional-self distinction at 66:06–75:46 together with
the purpose-of-analysis passage at 84:38–86:44, provided the owner supplies

self need not deny ordinary agency, and identify where that distinction

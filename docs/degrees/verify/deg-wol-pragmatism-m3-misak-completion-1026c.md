## Content checks against the module's assignment question

The module asks learners to note "what does Ramsey's version of the
pragmatist view of truth (a belief as a rule with which we meet the
future) take from Peirce, and what does it add?". The [opened
transcript](https://youtubetotranscript.com/transcript?v=c_Nxr3ZQxqA)
carries material answering every part:

1. Belief as a rule or habit for meeting the future: confirmed. The
   lecture quotes Ramsey's view that belief is a disposition to behave
   (the Cambridge Union example: he goes there habitually without
   thinking) and states that when we assert or believe P "we commit
   ourselves Ramsey says to meeting the future with that belief", with
   the future able to interfere with what Peirce called the smooth
   working of a belief habit.
2. What Ramsey takes from Peirce: confirmed. The lecture gives the
   Peircean background: belief as a habit or disposition that must be
   responsive to experience and evidence, and the end-of-inquiry
   account of truth as the belief that would be indefeasible and would
   forever meet the challenges of reason, argument and evidence.
3. Ramsey's difference from Russell and correspondence theories:
   confirmed. The lecture presents Ramsey's argument that
   correspondence theories cannot cope with disjunctive, universal,
   negative or modal facts, and Ramsey's note that his own view avoids
   mentioning either correspondence or facts.
4. What Ramsey adds: the lecture's own summary is that Ramsey provides
   "a pragmatism that takes the best of Peirce" (a belief about the
   world had better be connected to the way things are) and "the best
   of James" (beliefs about the meaning of life had better be connected
   in the right way to what makes life go better). One honesty note:
   the transcript never uses the phrase "adds something new"; the
   module's deliverable asks learners to state what Ramsey adds, and the
   lecture supplies the material for that answer (the Peirce-James
   synthesis and the correspondence critique) without using that exact
   wording. The module's phrasing is answerable from the lecture; no
   defect is found, but the supervisor may wish to know the exact
   wording is the module's, not the lecture's.

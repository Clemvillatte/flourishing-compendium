## Inspection and usable teaching

The [SFZC archive](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/principle-dependent-arising)
identifies Gil Fronsdal's “Principle of Dependent Arising” as a
Tassajara talk dated 21 August 2017. The complete returned transcript,
from its opening through the closing cue at 64:48, was read; the
separately labelled AI summary was excluded from the judgment.

The [official class list](https://www.sfzc.org/offerings/deepening-practice/recorded-study-classes-deepening-practice)
gives 1:04:51 and places it among recordings selected for experienced
practitioners. That is publisher-stated runtime, with no independent
playback measurement.

- **Conditions and action, 09:46–14:22:** the
  [transcript](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/principle-dependent-arising)
  explains supporting conditions through cultivation and the speaker's
  example of wanting a newer car after entering a different social
  environment. It gives learners a concrete way to examine how
  surroundings influence their wants.
- **Blame and useful responsibility, 34:11–39:56:** the
  [teaching passage](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/principle-dependent-arising)
  redirects attention from self-criticism toward conditions one can
  help establish, including ethical conduct, generosity, kindness
  and attention. The plant-growing analogy also addresses impatience
  with one's progress.
- **Relationships and limits, 58:27–64:48:** the
  [closing exchange](https://sfzc.engagewisdom.com/talks/principle-dependent-arising)
  considers a changing friendship and then questions about communicating
  intentions. Fronsdal recommends understanding the person without
  having to endorse every action, and declines a universal answer
  about whether to leave a relationship.

These passages support the degree's practical purpose. My recommendation
is to review the conditions-and-responsibility sequence first, with a
learner task identifying a condition they can influence and one outcome
they cannot guarantee. That task is an editorial proposal rather than
an exercise attributed to Fronsdal.

Fronsdal distinguishes his interpretation of early Buddhist texts from
broader accounts of interconnectedness, including Thich Nhat Hanh's.
This is an attributed interpretive position. No independent comparison
of the textual traditions was undertaken here.

## Required accuracy safeguards and reading fallbacks

1. **Networks interact.** The older APS clip says the measures were
   initially largely uncorrelated, with small correlations. The
   reopened 2023 paper explicitly updates this: larger studies find
   interactions; the networks are *semi-independent*, not isolated
   mental organs. Retain only **section 4's first paragraph** as the
   short update, rather than a whole paper.
   ([APS method explanation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y-fQywP6qa8&t=423),
   [Posner 2023, section 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10301152/).)
2. **Disorders are not one-network labels.** The reopened 2019 paper
   provides Table 2 and then expressly says a one-to-one network,
   disorder and symptom correspondence is not a complete account;
   multiple networks and developmental changes matter. Retain that
   table plus the first *Attentional Pathologies* paragraph for the
   scope's association-with-affect/disorders outcome. The ANT clip
   does not replace this caution.
   ([Posner et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6430178/).)
3. **Practice definitions are not efficacy evidence.** The reopened
   meditation review's introduction says sample, task and design
   differences make comparisons difficult. Its abstract says
   mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Retain the abstract and
   methodological-limit paragraph as a concise critical anchor; do
   not promise that a brief class exercise changes white matter,
   treats anxiety or improves everyone's attention.
   ([Lippelt et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4171985/).)
4. The selected Davidson chapter does not teach a full meditation
   protocol. Any optional noticing exercise should be clearly labelled
   an educational observation, with an external-object/eyes-open
   option and permission to stop, not an assessment of mental health
   or a neuroscience experiment.
   ([Conceptual chapter](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_3MVEo9fx4&t=128).)

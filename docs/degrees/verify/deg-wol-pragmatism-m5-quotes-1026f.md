## Findings — all four quotes verified verbatim

1. **Peirce's maxim** — CONFIRMED verbatim on
   [How to Make Our Ideas Clear](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/How_to_Make_Our_Ideas_Clear)
   (printed there as "SECOND PAPER.—HOW TO MAKE OUR IDEAS CLEAR." by
   "C. S. PEIRCE"): "Consider what effects, which might conceivably
   have practical bearings, we conceive the object of our conception
   to have. Then, our conception of these effects is the whole of our
   conception of the object." — character-for-character the module's
   quotation.
2. **The maxim's absence from The Fixation of Belief** — CONFIRMED on
   [the Fixation page](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fixation_of_Belief),
   opened as a direct HTML download in this worker's sandbox (109,783
   bytes) and searched CASE-INSENSITIVELY: "consider what effects" does
   not occur; "might conceivably have practical bearings" does not
   occur; "whole of our conception" does not occur; the word
   "conceivably" does not occur anywhere on the page in any casing.
   The m5 attribution (maxim belongs to How to Make Our Ideas Clear)
   is correct.
3. **Dewey's forked-road sentence** — CONFIRMED verbatim in the
   How We Think text
   ([Gutenberg #37423](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/37423/37423-h/37423-h.htm),
   this worker's download): "Thinking begins in what may fairly enough
   be called a forked-road situation, a situation which is ambiguous,
   which presents a dilemma, which proposes alternatives." The
   module's quotation ("begins in what may fairly enough be called a
   forked-road situation") is verbatim within it.
4. **James, Lecture VI (Pragmatism)** — BOTH sentences CONFIRMED
   verbatim on the
   [Lecture VI page](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Pragmatism:_A_New_Name_for_Some_Old_Ways_of_Thinking/Lecture_VI)
   ("Lecture VI: Pragmatism's Conception of Truth"):
   - "True ideas are those that we can assimilate, validate,
     corroborate and verify. False ideas are those that we can not."
   - "Experience, as we know, has ways of boiling over, and making
     us correct our present formulas."
   The page's HTML marks "True ideas... can not" as an italic span
   and "boiling over" as an italic span — the page DOES carry
   emphasis markers, and the module's stated convention (markers
   dropped in quotation) is applied consistently.

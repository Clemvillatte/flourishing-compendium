## Teaching cautions and deliberate exclusions

- Lecture 7's opening computer-vision claims concern **2007**.
  Do not repeat them as descriptions of current AI capability. Its
  visual illusions require images; the institutional transcript
  describes them but is not a complete substitute for seeing the
  demonstrations. Use a text-accessible alternative exercise if needed.
  ([Perception chapter](https://oyc.yale.edu/psychology/psyc-110/lecture-7).)
- Do not convert the perception lesson into “all perception is false.”
  It argues that useful assumptions support inference and can produce
  systematic mistakes under particular conditions.
  ([Perception chapter](https://oyc.yale.edu/psychology/psyc-110/lecture-7).)
- The encoding clip is an introductory demonstration, not comparative
  evidence that one mnemonic always outperforms retrieval practice or
  spacing. It includes a passing mention of obscene imagery as a
  mnemonic; learners need not use that example.
  ([Memory chapter](https://oyc.yale.edu/psychology/psyc-110/lecture-8).)
- Lecture 10's **unassigned** base-rate example has transcript slips:
  the premise is one in a thousand, but the explanation says “one
  percent,” then “fifty percent” where its numerical illustration uses
  roughly fifty false positives among 999 unaffected people. The
  narrow clips avoid teaching this defective worked explanation.
  ([Base-rate transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AMsQfgV07vU&t=2907).)
- The **unassigned** availability passage uses historical shark-risk
  figures and ethnic-demographic examples outside the module's
  learning needs. It is not a current risk or population-data
  authority. The clips also omit the course's evolutionary-psychology
  survey rather than reopen that excluded branch.
  ([Lecture 10 intervening passage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AMsQfgV07vU&t=3036).)

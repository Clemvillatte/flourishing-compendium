## Source fidelity

The author's Written WOOP Guide directs the learner to choose a challenging but feasible wish, imagine the best outcome, imagine the main inner obstacle, and form and rehearse an if-then plan ([English kit, Written WOOP Guide, pages 1 to 4](https://woopmylife.org/s/WOOP-kit-EN-PDF.zip)). The supplement preserves that order and explicitly directs the learner to those pages, so the mental-imagery steps are not silently reduced to filling four labels.

Oettingen identifies WOOP as mental contrasting with implementation intentions and distinguishes its WOO and P components in her first-person answer on procrastination ([Psychwire Q&A](https://psychwire.com/free-resources/q-and-a/1ux8o97/the-science-of-motivation)). The draft's account matches this terminology; it does not equate the whole procedure with mental contrasting alone.

The web FAQ permits adjusting, postponing or relinquishing a wish when it lies outside the person's scope of action or its obstacle cannot be overcome ([practice-page FAQ, “Can I WOOP any wish I have?” and “What if my obstacle is in fact insurmountable?”](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice)). The draft links to these precise questions and applies the distinction through the absent-colleague case. This matters because the downloaded kit's shorter FAQ focuses on changing internal obstacles; retaining the web FAQ gives the learner the explicit adjustment and disengagement guidance ([kit FAQ](https://woopmylife.org/s/WOOP-kit-EN-PDF.zip), [web FAQ](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice)).

The archive downloaded anonymously, and the Written Guide, Review and kit FAQ were read completely through their extracted text. The web practice page and Psychwire Q&A were also read in full; NYU independently confirms Oettingen's psychology professorship and her research on goal engagement and disengagement ([NYU faculty profile](https://as.nyu.edu/faculty/gabriele-oettingen.html)).

## Evidence claims checked independently

The 2021 meta-analysis reports a small-to-medium pooled effect and a smaller trim-and-fill estimate, consistent with the draft's prose ([Wang, Wang and Gai, Results](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)). I read the article's Introduction through Conclusions, including the inclusion criteria, result tables, moderator discussion and limitations.

The paper's publication-bias discussion contains both reassuring and cautionary statements, and its moderator findings come from comparisons across studies with covarying characteristics ([publication-bias analysis and moderator limitations](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)). The supplement appropriately keeps the smaller adjusted result and avoids turning the face-to-face/document comparison into a causal claim about adding a teacher.

The 2025 workplace trial's Methods identifies self-reported goal progress as primary; its Results report no substantive improvement on that measure and increased previous-day anxiety as a secondary outcome ([trial, sections 2.8, 3.3 and 3.5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11941803/)). The target recruitment was not reached and follow-up losses occurred, while the authors treat external constraints and timing as plausible explanations derived from their qualitative work ([trial, sections 2.13, 3.1 and Conclusions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11941803/)). I inspected those sections, the intervention/control description, statistical methods and relevant tables; I did not reanalyse participant data.

The student-trial abstract supports the draft's restricted wording about task aversiveness and willingness to begin through one week, without establishing completed coursework or durable benefit ([Zhou and colleagues, PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41601124/)). The physical-activity paper's publisher abstract reports no significant effects in its two online trials; the draft likewise does not equate nonsignificance with proof of an exactly zero effect ([Kędra and Łaguna, publisher abstract](https://cipp.ug.edu.pl/Does-mental-contrasting-with-implementation-intentions-MCII-help-to-increase-physical,225999,0,2.html)). These two checks are abstract-level, not full-paper reviews.

The four study summaries are kept outside the practical sequence, so a learner can follow the method before reading the evidence qualifications. The wording supports examining usefulness in context and supplies no guarantee of benefit or harmlessness.

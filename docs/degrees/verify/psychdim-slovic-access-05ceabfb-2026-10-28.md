## Separate, transcript-readable interview

Hidden Brain's [“Afraid of the Wrong Things”](https://www.hiddenbrain.org/podcast/afraid-of-the-wrong-things/)
provides a full written interview with Slovic dated 26 January 2021.
The publisher warns that the transcript may correspond to an earlier
version and may differ from the audio. I read the returned interview
through its closing credits; no audio playback or runtime is established.

Useful teaching can be located by these distinctive questions and
examples in the [interview transcript](https://www.hiddenbrain.org/podcast/afraid-of-the-wrong-things/):

- **When feelings help:** the freeway-crossing account explains the
  usefulness of a rapid fear response, before asking when feelings
  need checking against data.
- **Probability sensitivity:** the discussion of monetary loss and
  electric-shock experiments explains why vivid outcomes can dominate
  attention to probabilities.
- **Availability:** the interviewer explicitly asks about the
  availability heuristic, and Slovic distinguishes ease of imagining
  an event from its frequency.
- **Accumulating risk:** the seat-belt and smoking examples explain
  how repeated exposure differs from considering a single occasion.
- **Helping when needs exceed capacity:** the final discussion of
  psychic numbing and the modified drowning-child example examines
  how awareness of people one cannot help can reduce motivation to
  help people one can.

For the finite mission, I recommend reviewing the first and last
passages as a bounded pairing: when to consult feelings, and how to
preserve useful action when the scale of suffering feels overwhelming.
The link between those passages is an editorial proposal. No claim
that the interview teaches the full risk-perception row is made.

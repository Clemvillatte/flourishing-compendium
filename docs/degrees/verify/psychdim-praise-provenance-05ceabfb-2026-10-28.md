## Keep strategy and evidence limits visible

The [classroom transcript](https://www.mindsetkit.org/topics/praise-process-not-person/praising-process-see-action)
ties praise to close inspection of a student's work and to an achievable
next writing goal.
Stanford's [report of Dweck's presentation](https://gender.stanford.edu/news/understanding-mindset-success),
under “What do people get wrong most often?”, warns against simply telling
people to try harder when a different strategy is needed.
These opened passages support teaching specific feedback and adjustment
of strategy. They do not justify pressuring learners to persist
indefinitely or treating difficulty as evidence of personal failure.

Claims about educational outcomes require a separate evidence judgment.
The opening of [Macnamara and Burgoyne's open institutional PDF](https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/141/2020/06/26110416/Macnamara-Burgoyne-2023.pdf)
reports concerns about study quality and bias and a nonsignificant
achievement effect in their highest-quality subset.
[Tipton and colleagues' commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10495100/)
argues for modelling heterogeneous effects and reports a different
interpretation of effects for focal groups.
The authors' [open reply](https://artscimedia.case.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/141/2020/06/26110622/Macnamara-Burgoyne-2023-Reply.pdf)
disputes aspects of that reanalysis, including changes to the data and
coding.

This receipt records a published disagreement, not a resolution of it.
Neither a broad claim that growth-mindset teaching reliably raises grades
nor a claim that process praise has been disproved follows from this
provenance check. The mindset-intervention debate and the narrower
praise studies should not be treated as interchangeable questions.

Research inspection was limited to the returned opening sections of the
meta-analysis and reply, and the commentary's abstract, introduction and
background passages. All were freely readable on university or repository
carriers. The PDFs remain copyrighted; no full-text copies are committed.
This is not a new systematic review or approval of every finding in the
teaching unit.

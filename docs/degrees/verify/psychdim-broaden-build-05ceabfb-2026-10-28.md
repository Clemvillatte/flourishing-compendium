the Broaden Hypothesis” first, then decide whether the build section needs
a short corrective note. This can support the life-guidance question of
how pleasant experiences might widen one's available responses while
avoiding a promise that cultivating positive feelings cures distress.

## Accuracy cautions from opened research

The original [Fredrickson and Branigan paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)
reports the chapter's contentment-condition values of p = .064 for visual
attention and p = .109 for the thought-action test, and specifies one-tailed,
theory-driven contrasts.
Its Results and General Discussion also distinguish support for broadening
from the more limited evidence for negative-emotion narrowing
([original paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)).
Any learner summary should preserve those differences rather than saying
every positive emotion broadened every measure.

The [chapter's build section](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/)
describes eight weeks of experimental meditation or control sessions.
The [original 2008 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156028/),
under “Overview of Empirical Strategy,” describes a seven-week workshop
and nine weeks of daily reporting; its participant section refers to six
weekly classes.
These are different time measures, and the chapter should not supply an
unqualified protocol-duration claim.

More importantly, the [original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156028/)
explicitly says it did not directly measure momentary broadened cognition;
its limitations include reliance on self-reports, sample composition and
uncertain persistence beyond the study.
The chapter's strong causal wording and final flow diagram should therefore
be taught as an interpretation of a proposed mechanism, with the actual
measurements identified. They do not establish that every link in the
full broaden-then-build sequence was experimentally measured.

The open [Jäger and Rüsseler study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5078759/)
offers a useful additional caution: its second experiment found visual
attentional broadening but no significant between-group difference in the
number of action urges, while the content analysis found more social
actions in the positive-affect condition.
This is a bounded empirical challenge to a simple causal sequence, not a
review establishing that the entire theory succeeds or fails.

discussion passages. The 2008 and 2016 fetches were truncated near their

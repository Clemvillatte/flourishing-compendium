## What each text teaches (verbatim lines from the fetched pages)

BUD-D2-56 (mod-bud-1). The introduction names the four states and their job in a life:
"They provide, in fact, the answer to all situations arising from social contact. They are the
great removers of tension, the great peace-makers in social conflict, and the great healers of
wounds suffered in the struggle of existence." The Love contemplation opens: "Love, without
desire to possess, knowing well that in the ultimate sense there is no possession and no
possessor: this is the highest love." Equanimity is defined as "a perfect, unshakable balance
of mind, rooted in insight", and the text insists it "is not dull, heartless and frigid". The
closing section shows the states guarding one another: "Compassion guards equanimity from
falling into a cold indifference". Fit: mod-bud-1's required metta talk (BUD-B1-03) walks the
four immeasurables arc; this reading gives the learner a written companion for the same arc.
Mission gate: direct guidance on how to meet other people and one's own aversion — passes.

BUD-D2-57 (mod-bud-3). Section I defines sila on three levels — "inner virtue", "virtuous
actions of body and speech", "rules of conduct" — and states the stakes plainly: "Actions
contrary to sila lead to a state of self-division marked by guilt, anxiety, and remorse."
Section VI, The Breach of Sila, is the passage the module's own description promises ("how
guilt is handled when you break a rule"): "To undertake the precepts is to make a determination
to live in harmony with them, not to ensure that one will never break them." "One thing we
should not do if we break a precept is to let ourselves become ridden by guilt and
self-contempt." "A sense of shame and moral scrupulousness are central to maintaining the
precepts but they should not be allowed to become entangled in the coils of guilt." It then
gives the three remedies: confession to "a Dhamma friend" (noting "confession does not aim at
gaining absolution"), retaking the five precepts, and "making a strong determination not to
fall into the same transgression again". Mission gate: passes (the handling of guilt after a
lapse is suffering-alleviation in the most practical sense). Scope note: sections II-V of the
tract and the Going for Refuge tract are left optional/outside; the module's exclusion rule on
rebirth material stands and the locator keeps the learner on sections I and VI.

BUD-D2-58 (mod-bud-5). The essay is partly history (Customary, Reform and State Buddhism in
Thailand; Ajaan Mun's life), which on its own would fail the mission gate. It is admitted for
the practice ethic it teaches through that history. The opening lists the qualities the
Dhamma's "home culture" asks for: "resilience, resolve, and alertness; self-honesty and
circumspection; steadfastness in the face of loneliness; courage and ingenuity in the face of
external dangers; compassion and respect for the other inhabitants of the forest." The core
teaching: "The true Dhamma was to be found not in old customs or texts but in the well-trained
heart and mind. The texts were pointers for training, nothing more or less." "True respect for
the texts meant taking them as a challenge: putting their teachings seriously to the test to
see if, in fact, they are true." It quotes the Buddha's criterion to his aunt ("These teachings
lead to dispassion, not to passion; to being unfettered, not to being fettered ...") and
concludes: "the ultimate authority in judging a teaching is not whether the teaching can be
found in a text. It lies in each person's relentless honesty in putting the Dhamma to the test
and carefully monitoring the results." Closing: those who benefit most "instead of reshaping
Buddhism to fit their preferences, reshape themselves to fit in with the customs and traditions
of the noble ones." Fit: mod-bud-5's question is the Thai Forest tradition "learned from inside
its elders' voices" with "its preference for practice over scholastics" — this is that
preference stated by one of its Western elders. Mission gate: passes on the life-guidance
content; the locator points the learner at the three guidance passages and labels the history
as context.

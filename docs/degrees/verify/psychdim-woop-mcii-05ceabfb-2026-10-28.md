## Finding and routing

The factual question in pool entry 63 is resolved: Oettingen explicitly
identifies WOOP with **mental contrasting with implementation intentions
(MCII)**, and distinguishes the mental-contrasting component from the
if-then planning component in her [Psychwire Q&A](https://psychwire.com/free-resources/q-and-a/1ux8o97/the-science-of-motivation).
Her [NPR interview transcript](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/904680577)
independently records her explanation of the same four-step procedure and
its MCII name.

Use the full combined name when explaining the relationship. Treating WOOP
as a synonym for mental contrasting alone would leave out the planning
component described by [Oettingen](https://psychwire.com/free-resources/q-and-a/1ux8o97/the-science-of-motivation).
The narrower factual blocker, “no opened source establishes the
relationship,” can therefore be closed; the sufficiency of the KU
worksheet as degree-level teaching remains a separate curriculum decision.

## Teaching recommendation for review

My recommendation is to consider the KU worksheet alongside the author's
PLAN explanation, which explicitly teaches the conditional form the
worksheet's examples omit ([KU worksheet](https://learningandwriting.ku.edu/sites/writingandlearning/files/images/2024/Guides/GoalSetting/WOOP%20METHOD.pdf);
[author's practice page](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice)).
The reviewer should decide whether this pairing provides enough instruction
for the existing row; this receipt does not promote the row from PARTIAL.

For the Walk-of-Life purpose, the useful teaching question is how to choose
a feasible wish, face a relevant obstacle and specify a response while
retaining the option to change direction. That interpretation follows
the author's discussion of feasibility and goal disengagement, and should
not become a promise that any desired outcome is controllable
([NYU research statement](https://as.nyu.edu/faculty/gabriele-oettingen.html);
[WOOP practice FAQ](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice)).

## The citation claim: SUPPORTED

support of "a short diary can be useful without proving habit
formation". The opened article directly supports the framing:

- The review explicitly states the duration evidence is limited:

  duration of habit formation and variability in their study
  designs prevented us from conducting a meta-analysis for the
  duration to reach automaticity."
- Only four studies reported habit-formation times, with
  substantial individual variability ("4–335 days"; "18–254 days").
- The article challenges the popular 21-day idea and emphasizes
  between-person and between-behavior variation.

This matches the module's use: a personal before/after diary cannot
carry the weight of habit-formation proof, because the duration
literature itself is too thin and variable to set expectations a
diary could confirm.

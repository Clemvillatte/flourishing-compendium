## Keep the argument's stages together

In section III, Peirce distinguishes the felt difference between doubt
and belief from belief's practical role in shaping habits of action.
Section IV describes inquiry as the struggle prompted by doubt and
explicitly allows a firmly settled belief to be false.
([Peirce, sections III–IV](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p107.html).)
These are claims in his philosophical argument. This paper supplies
no clinical trial showing that inquiry treats anxiety or improves
mental health, and its historical physiological analogies are not
verified contemporary neuroscience in this receipt.

Section V then compares tenacity, authority, the a priori method and
the method of science. Its scientific-method passage appeals to
realities independent of what people happen to think, while its later
contrast distinguishes investigating facts from appealing directly
to feelings and purposes.
([Peirce, section V](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p107.html).)
Stopping at the discomfort-of-doubt passage would leave out the
paper's argument about how beliefs should answer to facts.

Peirce also describes eventual convergence given sufficient experience
and reasoning. That conditional argument does not make a present
majority vote, a confident individual or an existing institutional
consensus an automatic test of truth.
([Scientific-method hypothesis, section V](https://www.peirce.org/writings/p107.html).)
Keep this account attributed to Peirce rather than presenting it as
the uncontested position of James, Dewey or every pragmatist.

## Finite possible use

A possible future learning question is: someone feels relief after
deciding that a difficult question is settled; what would Peirce's
argument still ask about the belief's relation to the facts?
The case is constructed for teaching, not a reported study.
It gives a Walk-of-Life reason to read the argument: a person can
examine how they settle a question without equating increased
confidence with a better-grounded answer.

If the owner selects an excerpt later, retain the distinction between

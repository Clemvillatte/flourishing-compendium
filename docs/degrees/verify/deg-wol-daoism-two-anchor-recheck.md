## D-A01: Brook Ziporyn on the Daodejing

### Identity and authority

The primary page is “Why This Text Matters | Daodejing | Brook Ziporyn,”
on the University of Chicago Divinity School channel, dated 22 March
2023. The university's faculty page identifies Ziporyn as Mircea Eliade
Professor of Chinese Religion, Philosophy, and Comparative Thought,
with relevant work on Chinese religious and philosophical traditions.
([Video page](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJ1bB2w2gBk);
[University of Chicago faculty profile](https://divinity.uchicago.edu/directory/brook-ziporyn).)

### Bounded content actually checked

The primary transcript discusses non-doing or nonpurposive action around
15:05–15:51. Around 38:17–40:09 it contrasts forms of effortlessness and
uses ordinary bodily functioning to make one interpretation intelligible.
Around 40:09–42:21, the sleep analogy explores a problem with treating
every activity as an object of deliberate pursuit.
([Timestamped primary transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJ1bB2w2gBk).)

These are Ziporyn's interpretive explanations, not experimental evidence
that a practice treats insomnia. The production owner should not convert
the sleep analogy into health advice or an instruction to abandon
planning or responsibility.
([Interpretive passage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJ1bB2w2gBk).)

The source offers a possible finite Walk of Life question: when does
deliberate effort help an ordinary activity, and when might trying to
control every detail interfere with it? This is a proposed learning
use, not an empirical finding. A learner could compare a low-stakes
example with a counterexample where preparation and deliberate attention
are necessary, then state what the analogy cannot establish.

## D-A02: Bryan Van Norden on Zhuangzi

### Identity and authority

The primary page is “The Greatest Daoist Philosopher: Zhuangzi,” on
Bryan Van Norden's channel, dated 5 February 2021. The description
identifies the speaker and the Saint Louis University hosting context;
Vassar's faculty profile independently establishes his academic work
in Chinese and comparative philosophy.
([Video page](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNju5zGfdjI);
[Vassar faculty profile](https://www.vassar.edu/faculty/brvannorden).)

### Bounded content actually checked

The primary transcript introduces the butcher example around 36:07
and describes practice and following the animal's structure. Around
38:08 it offers a hypothesis about living by following the world's
natural pattern, then tests that interpretation rather than presenting
it as an unqualified conclusion.
([Primary transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNju5zGfdjI).)

Later, around 48:31–50:57, the lecture challenges the inference from
skillful engagement to ethical adequacy. It uses butchery, bullfighting
and a Nazi-officer example to ask whether skilled activity could be
amoral or immoral. The question matters to a Walk of Life curriculum:
fluency or technical competence alone must not be silently equated
with wisdom or genuine flourishing.
([Primary transcript, ethical challenge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNju5zGfdjI).)

The final sentence above is a proposed curriculum safeguard. It does
not claim that the lecture settles Zhuangzi's ethical interpretation
or provides one uncontested Daoist view.

### Routed navigation and content cautions

The page's chapter list places “The skillful butcher” at 34:09, but the
transcript at 34:12–35:44 is still discussing the difficulty of the
earlier relativism interpretation; the butcher narrative starts around
36:07. Likewise, the chapter label “Skill and morality” at 46:02 does
not identify the start of the later skilled-immorality challenge around
48:31. A producer should not use the labels alone to cut excerpts.
([Chapter list and primary transcript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNju5zGfdjI).)

Preserve the speaker's own warning when selecting the relevant passage;
he flags the potentially upsetting material around 48:10. This pass read
the transcript but did not inspect the corresponding images, so it
cannot describe what those images show or certify a finished clip.
([Spoken content and speaker's own warning around 48:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UNju5zGfdjI).)

This is source fidelity, not an additional safety gate or a request to
add disclaimers beyond the source's own framing. An isolated butcher
passage would be a poor basis for grading a claim
that spontaneous skill is necessarily moral. If it is used, teach the
interpretive challenge too, or explicitly limit the assessed claim to
what the selected passage can support. A text-based alternative may be
more suitable for a learner who does not wish to view graphic material;
the owner must still check the alternative's accessibility and context.

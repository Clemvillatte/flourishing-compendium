## Recommendation

Retain a bounded part of Dilwar Hussain's lecture 10 as a candidate for teaching how coping choices depend on their purpose and the situation. The inspected text distinguishes problem-focused and emotion-focused coping, explains how seeking support can serve either purpose, and shows how the two can help each other ([SWAYAM lecture-10 captions, 14:54.560 to 21:35.760](https://storage.googleapis.com/swayam-node1-production.appspot.com/assets/vtt/noc24_hs65/Lec10.vtt)).

The practical reason to consider this material is that learners may otherwise treat every difficult situation as a problem they must solve immediately. A proposed learning outcome is to identify what can be changed, what requires emotional support, and what additional resources would make action possible. This is a curriculum-design recommendation; no efficacy claim is made for the proposed task below.

## Content safeguards

The opening rationale makes broad claims about ineffective coping causing stress-related illness, and the recap includes a simplified Type A/Type B account; neither passage is included in the proposed selection ([caption text, opening through 13:21.680](https://storage.googleapis.com/swayam-node1-production.appspot.com/assets/vtt/noc24_hs65/Lec10.vtt)). The bounded selection should preserve the lecturer's own later qualification that emotion-focused coping can support problem-solving and that the value of disengagement depends on circumstances ([caption text, 20:21.760 to 21:35.760 and 53:03.920 onward](https://storage.googleapis.com/swayam-node1-production.appspot.com/assets/vtt/noc24_hs65/Lec10.vtt)).

For curriculum safety, the learner task should use ordinary, low-stakes examples or fictional cases. It should never require disclosure of trauma, imply that distress reflects personal failure, or tell a learner to remain in a harmful situation.

## Proposed task and handoff

Adapt the lecture's reflection into a short comparison of two fictional cases: an approaching deadline that can be renegotiated, and an uncertain outcome that cannot yet be resolved. Ask the learner to propose one practical action, one form of emotional support and one resource they would seek for each case. Then ask when persistence would stop being useful.

Assess whether the answer explains the function of each response and recognizes the limits of control. Reject an answer that labels all emotion-focused coping as avoidance or assumes that effort can remove every stressor.

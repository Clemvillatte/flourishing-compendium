# Broaden-and-build chapter: independent evidence checks

Reviewer `verifier-630b9de5`, `deg-verify-b`, 2026-10-28. This preserves the completed checks of the Penn State teaching chapter discussed in the supervisor's positive-emotions review; no replacement assignment is approved.

## Recommendation

Keep the chapter available for a corrected, bounded teaching sequence. Its visual-processing example and thought-listing task offer concrete ways to explain the theory, but several labels and conclusions need qualification before an unassisted assignment ([Penn State chapter](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/)).

The full chapter was read. I then checked the 2005 study's task methods, Results and Discussion, the 2008 study's empirical strategy, participant selection, workshop procedure and limitations, and the 2016 follow-up's thought-listing results and discussion.

## Corrections needed

The 2005 thought-listing task measured what participants said they would like to do after an emotion-inducing film, using up to twenty written statements ([original task instructions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)). The chapter's “broadened behaviors” labels therefore need explanation: completed actions in everyday life were not the outcome measured by that task ([chapter, Figure 26 discussion](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/), [original methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)).

The original study used one-tailed planned contrasts and described the anger-versus-neutral comparison in Experiment 2 as marginal, with p = .052; anxiety versus neutral was p = .162 ([original Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)). The chapter says that the anger clip showed greater narrowing, so a reading guide should distinguish a difference between plotted means from the strength of evidence for that comparison ([chapter, Experiment 2](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/)). It should also preserve the original study's unequal evidence for the two positive-emotion conditions instead of treating each contrast as equally persuasive ([original Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156609/)).

For the build study, the chapter describes eight weeks of meditation training, while the original Methods specifies six hour-long group sessions over seven weeks, with daily reporting over approximately nine weeks ([chapter, “Evidence for the Build Hypothesis”](https://psu.pb.unizin.org/psych425/chapter/broden-and-build-theory-of-positive-emotions/), [original workshop and reporting procedures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156028/)). These are different quantities, and none supplies degree-assignment hours.

The original field study explicitly says that it did not directly assess momentary broadened cognition ([empirical strategy and limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156028/)). A diagram of the proposed broaden-then-build sequence therefore needs to identify which parts were actually measured in this experiment. The authors also discuss self-report measurement and a waitlist comparison group's limits, which matter when explaining what the experiment establishes ([original limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3156028/)).

The later follow-up found visual-attention broadening in one experiment but no significant difference in the number of listed action urges in another ([Jäger and Rüsseler, 2016, Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5078759/)). Its authors identify limitations involving the film stimuli, arousal and attention measure, so this finding qualifies a simple general mechanism without establishing that every broadening effect is absent ([General Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5078759/)).

## Bounded teaching proposal

A corrected lesson could use the chapter's opening theory explanation and the two illustrated tasks through Figure 26. The owner would need to supply a short guide distinguishing the theoretical prediction, the actual measure and the reported result before assigning this selection.

One original comprehension task would ask a learner to evaluate this invented conclusion: “The people who listed more possible activities necessarily carried out more useful activities that week.” A satisfactory answer identifies the missing behavioral follow-up and explains why the listing task alone cannot establish that conclusion.

The build-study section would require more extensive guidance and is not recommended as an unassisted replacement here. These evidence papers remain verification materials; no new paper assignments, source-count credits, whole-row promotion or pedagogical gate clearance follows.

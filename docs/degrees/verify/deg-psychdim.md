# Verification evidence: Psychological Dimensions

Verifier: verifier-96108df3, `deg-verify-b`. This file supplies independently
reopened source evidence to the degree owner and gate reviewer. It does
not clear a curriculum, pedagogical or publication gate.

## Verification boundaries

Sources qualify through the Walk of Life purpose: understanding a
recurring difficulty well enough to make a more considered response,
reduce avoidable suffering and support a life the learner values.
The proposed placements below are editorial decisions for the production
owner to consider. Topical relevance alone does not earn a place.

These are reading resources. Recording duration, language captions and
video playback checks are inapplicable; reading and study time must be
calculated from the selected text when a module is assembled. Author
credentials are checked separately from article affiliations.

## PD-V01: EEMM orientation and context-sensitive change

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search identified the
  [publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39118646/);
  verification used the full, unprompted
  [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/).
- **Identity and access:** Stefan G. Hofmann and Steven C. Hayes,
  *A Process-Based Approach to Transtheoretical Clinical Research and
  Training*, Clinical Psychology in Europe 6, special issue, e11987,
  published 26 April 2024, DOI 10.32872/cpe.11987; anonymous full text,
  CC BY 4.0, as printed in the
  [article header](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/).
- **Credentials:** the separate
  [Marburg institutional profile](https://www.uni-marburg.de/en/fb04/team-hofmann/team/prof-dr-stefan-g-hofmann)
  lists Hofmann in the psychology department as Head, Alexander von
  Humboldt Professor and LOEWE Spitzenprofessor.
- **Content checked:** main text from Abstract through Concluding
  Thoughts, with the reference list inspected for attribution; the paper
  describes affect, cognition, self, attention, motivation and overt
  behaviour as psychological dimensions, alongside biophysiological and
  sociocultural levels, and organises change through context-sensitive
  variation, selection and retention
  ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/)).
- **Exact proposed assignment:** the introductory paragraphs beginning
  “PBT has a characteristic target” through the paragraph immediately
  before Figure 1, then the section *Functional Analysis in an Idionomic
  Approach*. These passages explain how a person's goals, circumstances
  and interacting processes guide an analysis
  ([assigned text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** retain as a short M1 orientation anchor. A
  production exercise can ask the learner to describe one recurring
  everyday difficulty across the six dimensions, identify a contextual
  constraint and state what they would need to observe before choosing a
  response. This exercise is a curriculum proposal, not a tested
  intervention from the paper.
- **Evidence type and limit:** conceptual and methodological argument,
  with discussion of earlier research. This article reports no new
  randomised trial or pooled treatment effect; it cannot establish that
  the proposed educational exercise relieves suffering
  ([article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/)).
- **Scope caution:** the authors explicitly allow other useful
  dimensions and do not claim hard divisions between them. Teach the
  six as this degree's chosen organising frame; avoid describing them
  as an exhaustive natural taxonomy
  ([EEMM discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303929/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; source identity, anonymous
  access, bounded content and independent institutional credential
  checked. Placement and instructional effectiveness remain separate
  decisions for the owner and independent reviewers.

## PD-V02: Enjoying the process without mistaking persistence for flourishing

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search followed by the
  unprompted, anonymous
  [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/);
  inspected the research overview, transparency statement, methods and
  results of Studies 1–4, and General Discussion.
- **Identity and access:** Kaitlin Woolley, Laura M. Giurge and Ayelet
  Fishbach, *Adherence to Personal Resolutions Across Time, Culture, and
  Goal Domains*, Psychological Science 36(8), 607–621; published online
  15 July 2025, DOI 10.1177/09567976251350960, CC BY-NC 4.0
  ([article header](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Credentials:** the separate
  [Cornell faculty profile](https://business.cornell.edu/faculty-research/faculty/krw67/)
  identifies Woolley as David A. Thomas Professor of Management and
  lists her University of Chicago PhD; the same page lists this paper.
- **Exact proposed assignment:** *Research Overview*, then *Study 4:
  Motivating Engagement With a Health App*, including Method, Procedure
  and Results, through the heading *General Discussion*. Read the final
  discussion paragraph about generalisability beyond online
  participant pools as well
  ([assigned text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Evidence that matters:** Studies 1–3 examine prediction rather than
  randomly assigning long-term motivation; Study 1 follows resolutions
  across a year and Study 3 tracks walking over two weeks. Study 4
  randomly assigns intrinsic-versus-extrinsic framing and measures
  reported product scans after 24 hours; its main analysis reports
  means of 11.55 versus 9.18 scans and Cohen's d = 0.21, 95% CI
  [0.07, 0.36]
  ([methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Selection and uncertainty:** Study 4's final sample is 763, from
  933 invited to follow-up and 803 follow-up respondents, after
  excluding zero scans and one extreme report. The authors also
  report nonpreregistered robustness analyses; the main result is not
  an all-randomised-participants estimate of taking up the activity
  ([Study 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** retain as the one recent empirical M6
  motivation reading, not a general goal-optimisation course. A proposed
  exercise asks the learner to choose a worthwhile commitment, make one
  part of its practice more engaging, and name a reason to revise or
  abandon the commitment if it is harmful. Persistence is not itself the
  criterion of a good life; this is an editorial safeguard, not a
  finding tested by the article.
- **Do not teach:** that the experiment proves year-long causal
  improvement, treats distress, validates the app's health judgements,
  or establishes that all meaningful action must be enjoyable. The
  experiment tests short-term engagement, not wisdom or flourishing
  ([study design](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; anonymous full text,
  publication identity, selected content and independent credential
  checked. Counts as a 2025 source if retained, but does not by itself
  clear the curriculum's recency gate.

## PD-V03: Flexible self-control rather than a test of moral worth

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search and unprompted fetch
  of the full
  [PMC author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423838/);
  inspected the main text through Conclusion and the funding,
  disclosure and reference notes.
- **Identity and access:** Kaitlyn M. Werner and Elliot T. Berkman,
  *Motivational Dynamics of Self-Control*, Current Opinion in Psychology
  59, 101859, published 8 August 2024, DOI
  10.1016/j.copsyc.2024.101859. The page labels this an author
  manuscript, available in PMC from 1 October 2025; that availability
  date does not make the work a 2025 publication
  ([article header](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423838/)).
- **Credentials:** the independently opened
  [University of Oregon profile](https://cas.uoregon.edu/directory/psychology/all/berkman)
  identifies Berkman as Professor of Psychology and co-director of the
  Center for Translational Neuroscience, with self-regulation, goals
  and motivation among his research interests.
- **Exact proposed assignment:** *Self-Control is a Flexible and
  Dynamic Process*, followed by *Directions for Future Research* and
  *Conclusion*. These sections explain identification, selection,
  implementation and monitoring, and spell out unanswered questions
  about matching strategies to contexts
  ([assigned sections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423838/)).
- **Evidence type and boundary:** a review and theoretical proposal,
  not a newly reported intervention trial. The authors hypothesise
  that effective self-control involves adapting regulation to context;
  they explicitly say the field lacks a unified account of which
  strategy works best in which situation
  ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423838/)).
- **Terminology warning:** this review uses intrinsic motivation
  broadly when discussing identity, enjoyment and autonomous reasons.
  Do not silently equate all of those with Woolley and colleagues'
  specific enjoyment/engagement measure, or present personally endorsed
  instrumental goals as necessarily externally controlled
  ([review's motivation section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423838/);
  [PD-V02 measurement items](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** reserve as an M7 explanation of changing
  approach when circumstances change. A proposed comparison exercise
  follows one modest commitment through the four stages, includes an
  external constraint, and asks when stopping is wiser than persisting.
  It must not turn difficulty with self-control into a judgement of
  character or an instruction to ignore material constraints.
- **Disclosure:** Berkman reports a consulting/advisory, employment
  and equity/stock relationship with Berkman Consultants, LLC
  ([declaration of interests](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11423838/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; anonymous full text, identity,
  selected sections and independent credential checked. Supplemental
  placement only if it supplies a learning task not already met by
  PD-V02; do not add it merely because it is relevant.

## PD-V04: Habit formation without a universal deadline

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search and unprompted
  [PMC full-text fetch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/);
  checked Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions and disclosures.
- **Identity and access:** Ben Singh, Andrew Murphy, Carol Maher and
  Ashleigh E. Smith, *Time to Form a Habit: A Systematic Review and
  Meta-Analysis of Health Behaviour Habit Formation and Its
  Determinants*, Healthcare 12(23), 2488; 9 December 2024, DOI
  10.3390/healthcare12232488, CC BY 4.0
  ([article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Credentials:** the separately opened
  [Adelaide University profile](https://researchers.adelaide.edu.au/profile/carol.maher)
  identifies co-author Carol Maher as Research Professor of Population
  and Digital Health, with health-behaviour interventions among her
  research interests.
- **Exact proposed assignment:** Results sections 3.2–3.5 and the
  Discussion paragraph beginning “Despite its strengths”. These expose
  the sample, measures, variable timelines and evidential limits before
  the learner encounters the stronger advice in Implications
  ([selected reading](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Evidence that matters:** the review includes 20 studies and 2,601
  participants; just four studies report time to automaticity, and
  their designs are too varied for a pooled estimate of that time.
  Section 3.3 reports study medians of 59 and 66 days, study means of
  91, 106 and 154 days, and individual ranges as wide as 4–335 days;
  these are different summaries of different studies, not one common
  clock
  ([Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Effect-size caution:** section 3.4 pools pre-to-post change from
  12 studies, 19 intervention conditions: SMD 0.69, 95% CI
  [0.49, 0.88], I² = 87%. It is not a pooled randomised
  between-group treatment effect. The Discussion also gives 0.67 in
  one paragraph; use the specified Results estimate rather than
  smoothing over that internal inconsistency
  ([Results 3.4 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** retain as the bounded M7 behaviour reading
  if the learner task is to design a modest, revisable practice and
  notice what helps it recur. A learner's slow progress need not be
  narrated as a character failure. This compassionate framing is an
  editorial choice, not an outcome measured by the review.
- **Do not teach:** “a habit takes 66 days”, a guaranteed two-to-five
  month deadline, or that repeated behaviour is necessarily wise.
  The evidence concerns selected health behaviours and mostly
  self-reported automaticity, not wisdom, virtue or whole-life
  flourishing
  ([study characteristics and limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; anonymous access, identity,
  selected content and independent co-author credential checked.
  Retain the variability and design cautions alongside any headline
  number.

## PD-V05: A clearer self-description need not be a rigid identity

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search and unprompted
  [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/);
  inspected conceptual definitions, measurement appraisal, cultural
  perspectives, intervention cautions, limitations and disclosures.
- **Identity and access:** Srikant Manchiraju, *Self-concept clarity:
  a comprehensive and integrative review*, Frontiers in Psychology
  17, 1822881; 26 June 2026, DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1822881,
  CC BY
  ([article header](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/)).
- **Credentials:** the independently opened
  [Florida State faculty page](https://jimmorancollege.fsu.edu/about/faculty-staff/faculty-srikant-manchiraju)
  identifies the author as an Associate Professor in the Jim Moran
  College of Entrepreneurship, researching consumer psychology,
  well-being and construct validation. It lists a PhD in Apparel,
  Events, and Hospitality Management; do not relabel him as a clinical
  psychologist or professor of psychology.
- **Exact proposed assignment:** *Definition and core components*,
  *Distinguishing self-concept clarity from related constructs*, *A
  critical appraisal of self-concept clarity measurement*, *Cultural
  perspectives on self-concept clarity* and *Interventions, change,
  and optimal self-concept clarity*
  ([selected sections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/)).
- **What is supported:** the review distinguishes the perceived
  clarity, consistency and stability of self-beliefs from their
  positive or negative evaluation. It highlights self-report
  dependence, uncertain causal direction and cultural limits of a
  scale rewarding consistency
  ([definitions, measurement and cultural discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/)).
- **What is not supported:** the paper is an integrative review,
  not a new trial or meta-analysis of a clarity intervention. It
  explicitly calls intervention evidence mostly indirect and warns
  that clarity attached to inaccurate or rigid self-beliefs need not
  be adaptive
  ([intervention section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** conditional M4 self reading. A useful
  proposed task distinguishes a present observation, a sweeping
  self-judgement and a commitment the learner can revise. Do not use
  the SCC scale as a diagnosis, prescribe a fixed authentic identity,
  or treat consistency across every relationship as a universal
  ideal. These safeguards follow the degree's educational purpose;
  the paper does not test this task.
- **Disclosure and verification boundary:** the author declares
  Grammarly, Copilot and Scite assistance with literature search,
  source verification and language editing. This record verifies
  what the review says, not every primary study it cites; any
  study-level finding or quotation reused in a lesson still needs
  the original opened
  ([generative AI statement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13350471/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; anonymous access, identity,
  bounded sections and independent credential checked. Keep only if
  the production owner can justify its self-dimension task without
  broadening into identity theory for its own sake.

## PD-V06: Constructed emotion is a position, not an efficacy result

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search and two unprompted
  [PMC fetches](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12164598/);
  the second raised the extraction limit to inspect the complete
  main text, including the comparison and conclusion.
- **Identity and access:** Lisa Feldman Barrett and 15 co-authors,
  *The Theory of Constructed Emotion: More Than a Feeling*,
  Perspectives on Psychological Science 20(3), 392–420;
  13 May 2025, DOI 10.1177/17456916251319045; anonymous PMC
  text under CC BY-NC
  ([article header](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12164598/)).
- **Credentials:** the independent
  [Northeastern institutional profile](https://nubic.northeastern.edu/profile/barrett-lisa/)
  identifies Barrett as University Distinguished Professor of
  Psychology and co-director of the Interdisciplinary Affective
  Science Laboratory. The profile contains older career references;
  no present office or presidency is inferred from them.
- **Content checked:** the abstract frames this as a response to
  van Heijst and colleagues; *The theory of constructed emotion in
  brief* sets out the authors' hypotheses, and *Both Theories Rest
  on Irreconcilable Assumptions* and Table 3 explicitly compare
  competing positions
  ([full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12164598/)).
- **Evidence type and limit:** theoretical argument, commentary
  and synthesis of earlier research, not an intervention study.
  Claims about allostasis, categorisation and variation must be
  attributed to the theory or its authors; this article cannot
  prove that relabelling an emotion improves well-being
  ([theory outline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12164598/)).
- **Scope recommendation:** exclude the full 29-page debate from
  the core. It is technically demanding and its dispute over
  emotion theories does not itself teach a proportionate
  everyday response. If the owner retains a short M2 epistemic
  sidebar, limit it to *The theory of constructed emotion in
  brief* and the competing-assumptions table, label it as a
  position and explain why uncertainty matters for interpreting
  one's experience. Do not turn this into a neuroscience survey.
- **Do not teach:** that basic-emotion theories are disproved by
  this commentary, that emotions are imaginary, or that sufferers
  can simply choose different feelings. Those are not results
  established by this paper
  ([argument and conclusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12164598/)).
- **Status:** access, identity, bounded content and independent
  credential verified; full-paper core placement not recommended.
  Verification is not an instruction to include the source or a
  means of satisfying recency by padding the curriculum.

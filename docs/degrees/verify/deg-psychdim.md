## Verification boundaries

Sources qualify through the Walk of Life purpose: understanding a
recurring difficulty well enough to make a more considered response,
reduce avoidable suffering and support a life the learner values.
The proposed placements below are editorial decisions for the production
owner to consider. Topical relevance alone does not earn a place.

These are reading resources. Recording duration, language captions and
video playback checks are inapplicable; reading and study time must be
calculated from the selected text when a module is assembled. Author

## PD-V02: Enjoying the process without mistaking persistence for flourishing

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search followed by the
  unprompted, anonymous
  [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/);
  inspected the research overview, transparency statement, methods and
  results of Studies 1–4, and General Discussion.
- **Identity and access:** Kaitlin Woolley, Laura M. Giurge and Ayelet
  Fishbach, *Adherence to Personal Resolutions Across Time, Culture, and
  Goal Domains*, Psychological Science 36(8), 607–621; published online
  15 July 2025, DOI 10.1177/09567976251350960, CC BY-NC 4.0
  ([article header](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Credentials:** the separate
  [Cornell faculty profile](https://business.cornell.edu/faculty-research/faculty/krw67/)
  identifies Woolley as David A. Thomas Professor of Management and
  lists her University of Chicago PhD; the same page lists this paper.
- **Exact proposed assignment:** *Research Overview*, then *Study 4:
  Motivating Engagement With a Health App*, including Method, Procedure
  and Results, through the heading *General Discussion*. Read the final
  discussion paragraph beginning “Several open questions remain”,
  which addresses generalisability beyond online participant pools
  ([assigned text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Evidence that matters:** Studies 1–3 examine prediction rather than
  randomly assigning long-term motivation; Study 1 follows resolutions
  across a year. Study 3 assesses current motivation alongside
  participant-transcribed app step counts for the preceding 14 days,
  with a subset checked against two uploaded screenshots. It is not
  prospective two-week tracking. Study 4
  randomly assigns intrinsic-versus-extrinsic framing and measures
  reported product scans after 24 hours; its main analysis reports
  means of 11.55 versus 9.18 scans and Cohen's d = 0.21, 95% CI
  [0.07, 0.36]
  ([methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Selection and uncertainty:** Study 4's final sample is 763, from
  933 invited to follow-up and 803 follow-up respondents, after
  excluding zero scans and one extreme report. The authors also
  report nonpreregistered robustness analyses: including zero scans
  retains the scan-count effect (d = 0.20, 95% CI [0.06, 0.33]), while
  a hurdle model finds no significant difference in the probability
  of any scan. This is not a demonstrated uptake effect; the
  nonsignificant result does not establish equivalence. The main
  result is not an all-randomised-participants estimate of taking up
  the activity
  ([Study 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** retain as the one recent empirical M6
  motivation reading, not a general goal-optimisation course. A proposed
  exercise asks the learner to choose a worthwhile commitment, make one
  part of its practice more engaging, and name a reason to revise or
  abandon the commitment if it is harmful. Persistence is not itself the
  criterion of a good life; this is an editorial safeguard, not a
  finding tested by the article.
- **Do not teach:** that the experiment proves year-long causal
  improvement, treats distress, validates the app's health judgements,
  or establishes that all meaningful action must be enjoyable. The
  experiment tests short-term engagement, not wisdom or flourishing
  ([study design](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13428958/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; anonymous full text,
  publication identity, selected content and independent credential
  checked. Counts as a 2025 source if retained, but does not by itself
  clear the curriculum's recency gate.

  approach when circumstances change. A proposed comparison exercise
  follows one modest commitment through the four stages, includes an
  external constraint, and asks when stopping is wiser than persisting.
  It must not turn difficulty with self-control into a judgement of
  character or an instruction to ignore material constraints.
- **Disclosure:** Berkman reports a consulting/advisory, employment
  and equity/stock relationship with Berkman Consultants, LLC

## PD-V04: Habit formation without a universal deadline

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search and unprompted
  [PMC full-text fetch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/);
  checked Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions and disclosures.
- **Identity and access:** Ben Singh, Andrew Murphy, Carol Maher and
  Ashleigh E. Smith, *Time to Form a Habit: A Systematic Review and
  Meta-Analysis of Health Behaviour Habit Formation and Its
  Determinants*, Healthcare 12(23), 2488; 9 December 2024, DOI
  10.3390/healthcare12232488, CC BY 4.0
  ([article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Credentials:** the separately opened
  [Adelaide University profile](https://researchers.adelaide.edu.au/profile/carol.maher)
  identifies co-author Carol Maher as Research Professor of Population
  and Digital Health, with health-behaviour interventions among her
  research interests.
- **Exact proposed assignment:** Results sections 3.2–3.5 and the
  Discussion paragraph beginning “Despite its strengths”. These expose
  the sample, measures, variable timelines and evidential limits before
  the learner encounters the stronger advice in Implications
  ([selected reading](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Evidence that matters:** the review includes 20 studies and 2,601
  participants; just four studies report time to automaticity, and
  their designs are too varied for a pooled estimate of that time.
  Section 3.3 reports a 59-day median in a study where only 23% reached
  its predetermined threshold, and a 66-day median in another study
  using 95% automaticity. It also gives study means of 91, 106 and
  154 days, and individual ranges as wide as 4–335 days;
  these are different summaries of different studies, not one common
  clock
  ([Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Effect-size caution:** section 3.4 pools pre-to-post change from
  12 studies, 19 intervention conditions: SMD 0.69, 95% CI
  [0.49, 0.88], I² = 87%. It is not a pooled randomised
  between-group treatment effect. The Discussion also gives 0.67 in
  one paragraph; use the specified Results estimate rather than
  smoothing over that internal inconsistency
  ([Results 3.4 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Walk of Life fit:** retain as the bounded M7 behaviour reading
  if the learner task is to design a modest, revisable practice and
  notice what helps it recur. A learner's slow progress need not be
  narrated as a character failure. This compassionate framing is an
  editorial choice, not an outcome measured by the review.
- **Do not teach:** “a habit takes 66 days”, a guaranteed two-to-five
  month deadline, or that repeated behaviour is necessarily wise.
  The evidence concerns selected health behaviours and mostly
  self-reported automaticity, not wisdom, virtue or whole-life
  flourishing
  ([study characteristics and limitations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)).
- **Status:** verified reading, English; anonymous access, identity,
  selected content and independent co-author credential checked.
  Retain the variability and design cautions alongside any headline
  number.

- **Opened:** 2026-10-26, verifier-96108df3. Search and complete,

  645–651, DOI 10.1007/s00426-017-0862-x. The opened header
  gives online publication 8 April 2017 and issue date 2018;
  the independent
  [Lund record](https://www.lu.se/publikation/4680ef86-62b4-485c-a77f-b88d734a34e3)

- **Credential boundary:** the separately opened publisher

  self-control, intergroup relations and decision-making.
  This supports relevant historical research expertise, not a
  newly verified present faculty rank; the attempted Lund
  person page was unavailable.
- **What the existing source reports:** 142 effect sizes,
  overall g = 0.38, CI [0.31, 0.45], I² = 60.67%; the

- **Later evidence is not one uniform verdict:** the

  subsequent laboratory-task performance after different
  initial-task conditions. They do not measure psychological

  historical trend
  ([Dang methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6013521/);
  [Vohs methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12422598/)).
- **Confirmatory/exploratory boundary:** Vohs and colleagues'
  confirmatory result did not find the hypothesised
  depletion effect; an exploratory full-sample analysis
  gave d = 0.08. The authors distinguish these analyses
  and discuss multiple interpretations. Do not present
  either a nonsignificant result as proof of exact zero,

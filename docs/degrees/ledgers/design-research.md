## Constructive alignment (module design rule)

- [Biggs, Constructive alignment in university teaching (TRU PDF)](https://www.tru.ca/__shared/assets/Constructive_Alignment36087.pdf):
  outcomes stated with one verb per outcome; teaching and learning
  activities that require students to engage that verb; assessment tasks
  that contain the same verb, judged with rubrics against criteria. This
  is the source of the skill's rule that every outcome must be taught,
  practised AND assessed by the same verb.
- [Biggs, Aligning teaching for constructing learning (Cardiff HEA PDF)](https://www.cardiff.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/584030/Aligning-teaching-for-constructing-learning-John-Biggs-HEA.pdf):
  all components (curriculum, outcomes, teaching methods, assessment) aligned
  to each other; objectives expressed as intended learning outcomes that in
  effect define the assessment task.
- [Queen Mary University of London, Constructive alignment](https://www.qmul.ac.uk/queenmaryacademy/educators/resources/curriculum-design/constructive-alignment/):
  outcomes formulated first, assessable, express what students should be
  able to do; assessment regime informed by outcomes; activities designed to
  support achievement.

## Backward design (the design sequence)

- [Ohio State University, Using Backward Design to Plan Your Course](https://teaching.resources.osu.edu/teaching-topics/using-backward-design-plan-your):
  Wiggins and McTighe's three stages: identify desired results, determine
  acceptable evidence, plan learning experiences and instruction.
- [Eastern Illinois University, Backward Design](https://www.eiu.edu/instructional_design/backward_design.php):
  outputs first (objectives, then assessments as evidence, then content and
  activities). Same three stages.

## Cognitive load, worked examples and fading (assignment design rules)

- [InstructionalDesign.org, Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller)](https://www.instructionaldesign.org/theories/cognitive-load/):
  worked examples and goal-free problems to avoid means-ends load;
  integrating split sources; cutting redundancy; dual modality when both
  channels are essential.
- [Springer, Cognitive Load Theory: Advances in Research on Worked Examples](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-010-9145-4.pdf):
  worked examples reduce ineffective load for NOVICE learners specifically;
  instruction should manage intrinsic load and cut extraneous load. This
  grounds the skill's fading rule for L1 into L2 and its warning against
  unsegmented blocks.
- [University of Kentucky, Cognitive Load Theory and Instructional Design](https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/544/Cognitive_Load_&_ID.pdf):
  simple-to-complex sequencing reduces intrinsic load; the scaffold runs
  from worked examples, through completion problems, to full problems.
  This is the exact fading structure the skill's assignment rule uses.

## Segmenting (the video-segment rule)

- [Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning, Segmenting principle chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-managing-essential-processing-in-multimedia-learning-segmenting-pretraining-and-modality-principles/4110A2275F6DCD02DAB1F8B37BA7E5CE):
  people learn more deeply from learner-paced segments than a continuous
  unit; three of three tests, median effect size 0.98 in that chapter's
  summary.
- [Mayer, Research-Based Principles for Designing Multimedia Instruction (UNH PDF)](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf):
  segmenting supported in 7 of 7 experimental tests, median effect size
  0.67; rationale is processing one step fully before the next. This is the
  source of the skill's rule that long recordings are assigned as
  timestamped segments.

## Retrieval practice and spacing (the activity rules)

- [Roediger and Karpicke 2006, The Power of Testing Memory (notes)](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zGfjkW1ociSmSUCLcpbhKjf):
  on delayed tests, prior testing can beat prior studying; multiple tests
  slow forgetting more than one. Grounds the skill's retrieval activities.
- [Karpicke and Roediger 2007 (Purdue PDF)](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2007/2007_Karpicke_Roediger_JEPLMC.pdf):
  expanding retrieval beat equally spaced retrieval at 10 minutes but NOT at
  two days; delaying the first test improved long-term retention. CAVEAT
  recorded: the skill therefore requires spacing (robust) without mandating
  a specific expanding schedule (not robustly supported).
- [Springer, single-paper meta-analyses of spaced retrieval](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40594-024-00468-5):
  the testing effect (retrieval beats restudying for long-term retention)
  is the robust finding; spaced retrieval spreads retrievals over time.
- [Wiley, multi-site classroom study of retrieval practice and spacing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3507):
  main effects of retrieval practice and spacing in real university
  classrooms across nine institutions; absolute spacing mattered.

## Level taxonomy

- [Wikipedia, Structure of observed learning outcome (SOLO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structure_of_observed_learning_outcome)
  and [Monash, Align with taxonomies](https://www.monash.edu/learning-teaching/teachhq/teaching-practices/learning-outcomes/how-to/align-with-taxonomies):
  Biggs and Collis (1982) five levels (prestructural, unistructural,
  multistructural, relational, extended abstract) describing increasing
  structural complexity of understanding. The skill's L1 to L4 house taxonomy
  is INFORMED by SOLO (especially relational and extended abstract) but is
  a house design, not an adoption; its observable criteria are written
  per module and remain open to challenge at the pedagogical gate.

# A041 — Know that virtue aims at the beautiful (to kalon) — evidence (p1zi)

- Twenty-third item of the p1zi band. Original page: no verbatim quotes;
  the seed's glosses ("The aesthetic dimension of ethics is not
  decoration but substance") were commentary; "Let your actions be done
  for the sake of what is noble" was an unlabelled prescription; the
  For example asserted a motive ("when it earns you nothing... is the
  thing done for its own fineness").
- Opened 2026-10-20: NE Books III and IV fetched raw (no prompt) from
  the MIT Classics Ross translation
  (https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html,
  .../nicomachaen.4.iv.html); the quoted fragments confirmed
  character-for-character against the displayed text: "will face them
  as he ought and as the rule directs, for honour's sake; for this is
  the end of virtue"; "But courage is noble. Therefore the end also is
  noble; for each thing is defined by its end. Therefore it is for a
  noble end that the brave man endures and acts as courage directs.";
  "chooses or endures things because it is noble to do so, or because
  it is base not to do so"; "to escape from poverty or love or anything
  painful is not the mark of a brave man, but rather of a coward...
  such a man endures death not because it is noble but to fly from
  evil."; "Now virtuous actions are noble and done for the sake of the
  noble" (IV.1); "not under compulsion but because it is noble to be
  so" (III.8).
- The kalon identification is handled as a translation fact ("The Greek
  word behind Ross's 'noble' is kalon, the fine."), not as a claim that
  the opened edition prints the Greek.

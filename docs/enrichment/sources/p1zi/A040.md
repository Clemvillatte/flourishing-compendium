# A040 — Practise the unity of the virtues — evidence (p1zi)

- Twenty-second item of the p1zi band. Original page: no verbatim
  quotes; the seed's formulations ("Courage without temperance is
  recklessness; generosity without justice is caprice") were invented
  illustrations presented as text; "Work on the whole person" was an
  unlabelled prescription; the psychology parallels carried
  claim-bearing clauses (Kristjánsson 2018; Ryan and Deci 2001).
- Opened 2026-10-20: NE Book VI fetched raw (no prompt) from the MIT
  Classics Ross translation
  (https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.6.vi.html); chapters
  12-13 inspected directly and every quoted fragment confirmed
  character-for-character: "for virtue makes us aim at the right mark,
  and practical wisdom makes us take the right means."; "while one may
  be led astray by them, as a strong body which moves without sight may
  stumble badly because of its lack of sight, still, if a man once
  acquires reason, that makes a difference in action."; "in thinking
  that all the virtues were forms of practical wisdom he was wrong, but
  in saying they implied practical wisdom he was right."; "It is clear,
  then, from what has been said, that it is not possible to be good in
  the strict sense without practical wisdom, nor practically wise
  without moral virtue."; "but not in respect of those in respect of
  which a man is called without qualification good; for with the
  presence of the one quality, practical wisdom, will be given all the
  virtues."
- Repairs: the invented illustrations replaced by the text's own (the
  strong body without sight); a labelled compendium exercise added
  (pair a proud virtue with its checking virtue, review one action);
  the For example made imagined and action-only; the psychology
  parallels reduced to bare references. The seed's X.9 and EE VIII.3
  locators not quoted from (not fetched); the page now cites only the
  opened VI.12-13.

## Claims in body (each verified in an opened source)
- Definition opening sentence ("capacity to attend to, appreciate, and enhance positive experiences in the present, past, or future") — Bryant & Veroff's definition, already on the page; kept unattributed in the body (the blockquote that follows is Bryant's own operationalization of it).
- Published blockquote — the three-temporal-orientations passage of Bryant 2003, "Savoring Beliefs Inventory (SBI): A scale for measuring beliefs about savouring", Journal of Mental Health 12(2):175-196 (open full text at https://www.carolinemiller.com/info/Savoring_Belief_Inventory_Bryant.pdf), verified in the unprompted raw fetch of the PDF text (lines 176-190 of the extraction): "First, before an upcoming good event occurs, people may look forward to it in ways that generate positive feelings in the present (i.e. savouring through anticipation). Next, while a good event is happening, people may intensify or prolong their positive feelings through specific thoughts and behaviors (i.e. savouring the moment). And finally, after a good event is over, people may look back on it in ways that prolong or rekindle their positive feelings (i.e. savouring through reminiscence)." The PDF extraction carries line-wrap hyphenation artifacts (peo-/inten-/savour-/reminis-); the quoted text is the dehyphenated join, word-for-word otherwise.
- Perceived-control sentence — abstract of the same paper, verbatim fragment: "a form of perceived control over positive emotions"; "largely independent of beliefs about coping" (abstract: "beliefs about one's capacity to savour positive outcomes, a form of perceived control over positive emotions, are largely independent of beliefs about coping, a form of perceived control over negative emotions"). The "capacity applied to experience rather than a mood itself" reading was NOT asserted as a source claim; the sentence in the body is scoped to the two verbatim fragments.
- Meta-analysis sentence — Chen, Tung, Wu & Sie 2026, "Effectiveness of savoring interventions: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials", Appl Psychol Health Well-Being 18(2):e70134 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12968602/), verified in the unprompted raw fetch: abstract states 20 independent RCTs, 4805 participants, 45 effect sizes, overall pooled effect g = 0.51 (95% CI [0.26, 0.77], p < .001).

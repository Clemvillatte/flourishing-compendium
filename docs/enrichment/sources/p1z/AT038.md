# AT038 — Nature for attentional restoration (p1z)

- Source opened: Kaplan, "The restorative benefits of nature: Toward an integrative
  framework", Journal of Environmental Psychology 15(3), 169-182 (1995), PDF at
  https://new.censusatschool.org.nz/wp-content/uploads/2013/09/restorative-benefits-of-nature.pdf
  (downloaded, pdftotext; OCR scan — page footers verified: soft-hard dimension
  passage on printed p. 172, necessary-not-sufficient on p. 173, soft-fascination
  exemplars on p. 174).
- Quote 1 (p. 174): "Many of the fascinations afforded by the natural setting qualify
  as 'soft' fascinations: clouds, sunsets, snow patterns, the motion of the leaves in
  the breeze—these readily hold the attention, but in an undramatic fashion. Attending
  to these patterns is effortless, and they leave ample opportunity for thinking about
  other things." (OCR hyphenation normalized to em dashes; otherwise
  character-for-character.)
- Quote 2 (p. 172): "Fascination can also derive from extremes along a 'soft-hard'
  dimension. Thus, there is the 'hard' fascination of watching auto racing and 'soft'
  fascination of walking in a natural setting. Soft fascination characteristic of
  certain natural settings has a special advantage in terms of providing an
  opportunity for reflection, which can further enhance the benefits of recovering
  from directed attention fatigue." (same normalization.)
- Paraphrase with locator: fascination necessary but not sufficient; being away,
  extent, compatibility (p. 173).

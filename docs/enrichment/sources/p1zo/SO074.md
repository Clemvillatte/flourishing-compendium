# SO074 — Use bicultural identity integration as a strength (p1zo item 20)

- Source opened: Benet-Martínez, V., Leu, J., Lee, F., & Morris, M. W. (2002).
  Negotiating biculturalism: Cultural frame switching in biculturals with oppositional
  versus compatible cultural identities. J Cross-Cultural Psych 33(5), 492-516.
  Abstract carried by the opened ERIC record: https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ653127
  - "Results found that Chinese American biculturals who perceived their cultural
    identities as compatible responded in culturally congruent ways to cultural
    cues, while those who perceived their cultural identities as oppositional
    exhibited a reverse priming effect." (Page blockquote.)
  - The Sage full text is paywalled; the ERIC record is the disclosed carrier.
- Source opened: Chen, S. X. (2015). Toward a social psychology of bilingualism
  and biculturalism. Asian J Soc Psych 18, 43-51. Open PDF:
  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/ajsp.12088
  - "integrating bicultural identities is central to psychological adjustment
    among individuals experiencing immigration-based and globalization-based
    acculturation" (Body paraphrase.)
  - "it is possible for alternation and integration to coexist within the same
    individuals who integrate their bicultural identities and alternate their
    behavioural responses." (Supports the plain-words house/flats framing.)
- Source opened: Saad, C. S., Damian, R. I., Benet-Martínez, V., Moons, W. G., &
  Robins, R. W. (2013). Multiculturalism and creativity: Effects of cultural
  context, bicultural identity, and ideational fluency. Soc Psychol Personal Sci
  4(3), 369-375. Abstract carried by the opened academia.edu record:
  https://www.academia.edu/9065434/Saad_Damian_Benet_Martinez_Moons_and_Robins_2013_
  - "One hundred seventy-seven Chinese Americans completed a creativity task in
    either a monocultural or bicultural context"; "Greater bicultural identity
    blendedness predicted domain-general creativity in bicultural but not in
    monocultural contexts, and this was mediated by ideational fluency."

- Source opened: Spinoza, Ethics, trans. R. H. M. Elwes, Gutenberg #3800 (full
  text downloaded; https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3800).
- Supported passages (verified verbatim against the opened text):
  - II Def. VI: "Reality and perfection I use as synonymous terms."
  - IV Preface: "Perfection and imperfection, then, are in reality merely modes
    of thinking, or notions which we form from a comparison among one another
    of individuals of the same species" (body paraphrase); "by perfection in
    general I shall, as I have said, mean reality--in other words, each
    thing's essence, in so far as it exists" (body paraphrase).
  - IV Def. VIII (page blockquote): "By virtue (virtus) and power I mean the
    same thing; that is (III. vii), virtue, in so far as it is referred to
    man, is a man's nature or essence, in so far as it has the power of
    effecting what can only be understood by the laws of that nature."

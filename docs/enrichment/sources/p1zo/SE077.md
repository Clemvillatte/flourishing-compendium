# SE077 — Assess and monitor self-development (p1zo item 8)

- Sources opened:
  1. Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007). The Power of Feedback. Review of Educational
     Research, 77(1), 81-112. Full-text PDF:
     https://open-publishing.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Hattie-PowerFeedback-2007.pdf
  2. Peirce, C. S. (1877). The Fixation of Belief. https://www.peirce.org/writings/p107.html
- Supported passages (verified verbatim against the opened pages):
  - H&T p. 81: "Feedback is one of the most powerful influences on learning and
    achievement, but this impact can be either positive or negative." (body fragment)
  - H&T p. 81: "In this review, feedback is conceptualized as information provided by an
    agent (e.g., teacher, peer, book, parent, self, experience) regarding aspects of
    one's performance or understanding." (body fragment)
  - H&T p. 86 blockquote: "Effective feedback must answer three major questions asked by
    a teacher and/or by a student: Where am I going? (What are the goals?), How am I
    going? (What progress is being made toward the goal?), and Where to next? (What
    activities need to be undertaken to make better progress?)"
  - Peirce §§III-IV: "Doubt is an uneasy and dissatisfied state from which we struggle to
    free ourselves and pass into the state of belief;" and "The irritation of doubt
    causes a struggle to attain a state of belief. I shall term this struggle inquiry..."
    (body paraphrases this).
- Limitations: H&T's evidence is about learning and achievement feedback broadly; the
  application to self-development tracking is the compendium's own extension (the
  definition includes "self" as a feedback agent, which supports but does not by itself
  establish the extension). Zimmerman 2002, Vleioras & Bosma 2005, Luyckx et al. 2008
  parallels are inherited and NOT certified this session (flagged).

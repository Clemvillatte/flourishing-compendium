# P088 — Use praise and blame as instruments, not metaphysics — evidence ledger

## Primary sources opened this session

- **Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct (1922), Project Gutenberg #41386** — https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt (raw text fetched; praise/blame passages located by line search and cross-checked against the Brock University Mead Project section pages). Verbatim:
  - "The moral problem is that of modifying the factors which now influence future results. To change the working character or will of another we have to alter objective conditions which enter into his habits. Our own schemes of judgment, of assigning blame and praise, of awarding punishment and honor, are part of these conditions."
  - "But the moral issue concerns the future. It is prospective."
  - "We cannot change habit directly: that notion is magic. But we can change it indirectly by modifying conditions, by an intelligent selecting and weighting of the objects which engage attention and which influence the fulfilment of desires."
  - "Judgment in which the emphasis falls upon blame and approbation has more heat than light."
  - "Now this kind of judgment is certainly not one which could profitably be dispensed with."
  - (part 2, sec. 3, paraphrased in body: characterizing an act as generous or mean is done "in order to encourage him in future acts of the same sort, or in order to dissuade him--in short to build or destroy a habit")

## Proposed body (paragraph 1 replacement)
Bergson distinguishes pleasure from joy. Nature, he says, warns us by a clear sign that our
destination is attained: "That sign is joy. I mean joy, not pleasure. Pleasure is only a
contrivance devised by nature to obtain for the creature the preservation of its life, it
does not indicate the direction in which life is thrusting. But joy always announces that
life has succeeded, gained ground, conquered. All great joy has a triumphant note" (link to
the carrier). A pleasure settles a need; joy says something exists that did not exist
before: "wherever there is joy, there is creation; the richer the creation, the deeper the
joy. The mother beholding her child is joyous, because she is conscious of having created
it, physically and morally" (link). The artist "who has realized his thought," the
manufacturer with the feeling of "having brought something to life" — Bergson associates
their joy with what they have created, rather than with money or recognition.
[Paragraph 2 unchanged: "This is not a guarantee. Bergson is offering a phenomenological
reading of the experience of joy, not a clinical test of whether someone is flourishing."]

## Proposed Plain words (replaces the 42%-overlap replicate; 0% 5-gram overlap with body)
Joy and pleasure are easy to mix up. Pleasure is the body saying thank you for maintenance:
food eaten, rest taken, warmth found. Joy is what you feel when something exists that would
not exist without you — a child grown, a thing built, an idea made real. This is a way of
reflecting on your feelings, not a rule that every finished task should make you happy.

## Claims in body (each verified in an opened source)
- Amygdala mechanism sentence — supported by Goldstein & Walker 2014, "The Role of Sleep in Emotional Brain Function", Annual Review of Clinical Psychology (opened https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4286245/): "one night of sleep deprivation triggers a 60% amplification in reactivity of the amygdala in response to emotionally negative pictures, relative to a normal night of sleep ... this increase in amygdala reactivity is paired with a reduction in functional connectivity with regions of the mPFC that exert top-down regulatory control of the amygdala" (Emotional Reactivity section).
- OASIS trial paragraph — supported by the opened PubMed abstract (PMID 28888927): 3755 randomised (1891 dCBT-I, 1864 usual practice), 10-week reductions in insomnia (d=1.11), paranoia (d=0.19), hallucinations (d=0.24); "Insomnia was a mediator of change in paranoia and hallucinations"; interpretation cautions generalisation beyond students. Depression/anxiety improvements verified in the OPENED Lancet full text: "The sleep treatment also led to improvements in depression, and improvements in anxiety, prodromal symptoms, nightmares, psychological wellbeing, and functioning, and all these improvements were maintained over time (tables 4, 5)."

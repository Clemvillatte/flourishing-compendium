## Opened sources (2026-10-19, genuineflourishing34)

- John Dewey, Experience and Nature (1925; 1929 second-edition text), archive.org full text
  (experienceandnat029343mbp_djvu.txt, URL in page Sources). Downloaded and searched
  directly (tr/newline unwrap) to verify all three quoted fragments:
  - "the world of empirical things includes the uncertain, unpredictable, uncontrollable,
    and hazardous" (ch. II)
  - "The union of the hazardous and the stable, of the incomplete and the recurrent, is
    the condition of all experienced satisfaction as truly as of our predicaments and
    problems. While it is the source of ignorance, error and failure of expectation, it
    is the source of the delight which fulfillments bring." (ch. II)
  - "if there were nothing in the way, if there were no deviations and resistances,
    fulfillment would be at once, and in so being would fulfill nothing" (continuation of
    the union passage)

  perienced"); the quotes are the cleaned continuous wording, matching independent
  scholarly renderings of the same passages (e.g. the FIU lecture supplement's rendering
  of the union passage).

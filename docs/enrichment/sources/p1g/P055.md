- William James, The Principles of Psychology, vol. 1 (1890), full text via archive.org
  (theprinciplesofp01jameuoft_djvu.txt, URL in page Sources)
  - Supported (ch. IX, verbatim): "Consciousness, then, does not appear to itself chopped
    up in bits. Such words as 'chain' or 'train' do not describe it fitly as it presents
    itself in the first instance. It is nothing jointed; it flows. A 'river' or a
    'stream' are the metaphors by which it is most naturally described. In talking of it
    hereafter, let us call it the stream of thought, of consciousness, or of subjective
    life."
  - Supported (ch. IX, the five characters, paraphrased in prose with attribution): every
    thought tends to be part of a personal consciousness; thought is always changing;
    it is sensibly continuous; it deals with objects independent of itself; it is
    interested in some parts of those objects to the exclusion of others.

    typo "if flows" where the 1890 printing (archive.org scan) reads "it flows"; the

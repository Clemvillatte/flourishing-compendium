## Kross evidence passages (verbatim, with verified locators)

All five passages are from the **Abstract, p. 304** of Kross et al. (2014), JPSP 106(2), pp. 304-324:

1. **Abstract, p. 304**: "Seven studies explored these questions (total N = 585)."
2. **Abstract, p. 304**: "Studies 1a and 1b were proof-of-principle studies. They demonstrated that using non-first-person pronouns and one's own name (rather than first-person pronouns) during introspection enhances self-distancing."
3. **Abstract, p. 304**: "Compared with the first-person group, the non-first-person group performed better according to objective raters in both studies. They also displayed less distress (Studies 2 and 3) and engaged in less maladaptive postevent processing (Study 3)."
4. **Abstract, p. 304**: "Finally, a meta-analysis (Study 6) indicated that none of these findings were moderated by trait social anxiety, highlighting their translational potential."
5. **Abstract, p. 304**: "Together, these findings demonstrate that small shifts in the language people use to refer to the self during introspection consequentially influence their ability to regulate their thoughts, feelings, and behavior under social stress, even for vulnerable individuals."

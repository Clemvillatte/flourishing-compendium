# S059 — Do not be carried away by passion (item 51, worker-p1g-5e8c3a21)

- Opened sources:
  - Seneca, Of Anger Book I, Aubrey Stewart translation, Wikisource
    (https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Of_Anger/Book_I). Verified verbatim: the I.1 short-madness
    passage (blockquote, full sentence including the falling-rock ending); the I.8 advice
    ("The best plan is to reject straightway the first incentives to anger, to resist its very
    beginnings... reason goes for nothing when once passion has been admitted to the mind"),
    paraphrased in the body with quoted fragments; the I.7 precipice passage and I.9
    fatigue passage read but not used.
  - Stoicism, IEP section 4 (https://iep.utm.edu/stoicism/). Verified verbatim: the blockquote
    on passions as judgments/assent to impressions; the eupatheiai material (glossary: "the
    healthy passions cultivated by the Sage"; "discretion", "willing", "delight" as
    counterparts of pain/fear/craving/pleasure).
- The old body's four-passion list (anger, fear, grief, desire) and "exceed what reason
  approves" phrasing were not verified from an opened source; the body now runs on Seneca's
  characterization + the IEP's assent mechanism, and the eupatheiai claim is scoped to the
  glossary's wording. The old locator (De Ira I.7-9; Med. VI.6, VII.17; Disc. III.2, III.8,
  III.10) replaced; the unopened citations dropped.
- The IEP's category list is pain/fear/craving/pleasure, not the old page's anger/fear/grief/
  desire; nothing asserts the old list.
- Example kept from the original page (fitting sadness vs a week of spiralling).
- Fixed a pre-existing formatting bug: the "Let reason guide desire" and Apatheia bullets were
  merged on one line; now two lines, exact wikilink preserved.

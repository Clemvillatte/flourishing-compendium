# K001 source ledger

**Item**: K001 — Return to experience before the labels
**Primary source**: Nishida, *An Inquiry into the Good*, trans. Masao Abe and Christopher Ives, Yale University Press, 1990 (Part I, ch. 1).
**Access**: The 1990 Yale translation was NOT independently opened (no archive.org full-text found). The verbatim wording was obtained through opened secondary sources that reproduce the primary text with page locators.

## Opened sources and verbatim passages

1. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Nishida Kitarō (Winter 2023 archive)](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2023/entries/nishida-kitaro/) — the quoted sentence containing "The moment of seeing a color or hearing a sound" is Maraldo's sentence quoting Nishida's opening phrase; locator "Nishida 1990a, 3" given on the page.
2. [Philopedia, An Inquiry into the Good](https://philopedia.org/works/an-inquiry-into-the-good/) — reproduces "When one directly experiences one's own state of consciousness, there is as yet neither subject nor object, and knowledge and its object are completely unified." (Part I, ch. 1).
3. [Mitsuhara, EJJP 6 (2021)](https://ejjp-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/ejjp-6-1-mitsuhara.pdf) — quotes "to experience means to know facts just as they are" and "there is not yet a subject or an object, and knowing and its object are completely unified" (Nishida 1990, 3-4); and Nishida's own caution, quoted from Nishida 2003a: "there is neither absolutely pure nor absolutely non-pure experience. Every experience is pure, depending on how we look at [it]."

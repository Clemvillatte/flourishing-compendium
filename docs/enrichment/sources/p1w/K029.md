# K029 source ledger

**Item**: K029 — Take the three poisons as practice material
**Primary source**: Chekawa, Seven Points of Mind Training, point two, slogan 8; and Chögyam Trungpa, *Training the Mind and Cultivating Loving-Kindness* (commentary on slogan 8).
**Access**: The Shambhala book itself was NOT independently opened. Verbatim wording was obtained from an opened secondary source (Spirituality and Practice) that reproduces Trungpa's commentary directly.

## Opened sources and verbatim passages

1. [Spirituality and Practice, Trungpa on "Three Objects, Three Poisons, and Three Seeds of Virtue"](https://www.spiritualityandpractice.com/practices/practices/view/28555/three-objects-three-poisons-and-three-seeds-of-virtue) — reproduces Trungpa's commentary: "Three objects, three poisons, and three seeds of virtue.... The three objects are friends, enemies, and neutrals. The three poisons are passion, aggression, and ignorance or delusion. And the three seeds of virtue are the absence of passion, aggression, and ignorance." and "Whenever any of the three poisons happens in your life, you should do the sending and taking practice. You just look at your passion, your aggression, and your delusion -- you do not regard them as a problem or as a promise." and the gloss: "Passion is wanting to magnetize or possess; aggression is wanting to reject, attack, cast out; and ignorance or indifference is that you couldn't be bothered, you are not interested, a kind of anti-prajna energy."
2. The reversal passage: "If you have no object of aggression, you cannot hold your own aggression purely by yourself. If you have no object of passion, you cannot hold your passion yourself." (same source, Trungpa).
3. [Wikipedia, Lojong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lojong) — slogan 8 as translated by the Nalanda Translation Committee under Trungpa: "Three objects, three poisons, three roots of virtue The 3 objects are friends, enemies and neutrals. The 3 poisons are craving, aversion and indifference. The 3 roots of virtue are the remedies."
4. [Amazon book page for Pema Chödrön, The Compassion Book](https://www.amazon.nl/-/en/Pema-Chodron/dp/1611804205) — snippet reproduces her commentary on slogan 8: "The three objects are: friends, enemies, and neutrals. The three poisons are: craving, aversion, and indifference. When you feel craving, you own it fully and wish that all beings could be free of it. When you feel aggression or indifference you do the same. In this way what usually causes suffering — what poisons us and others — becomes a seed of compassion and loving-kindness, a seed of virtue." — NOT used in the body (snippet-level, book not opened; the Trungpa commentary is used instead).

## Interpretation scope (repairs)

- The old body claimed the practice converts each poison into "contentment, clarity, and engaged compassion respectively" — NOT found in the opened Trungpa commentary; removed. Trungpa's seeds of virtue are "the absence of passion, aggression, and ignorance".
- The old body claimed the poisons are "each triggered by objects (pleasing, threatening, neutral things)" — Trungpa's objects are "friends, enemies, and neutrals", not "pleasing, threatening, neutral"; corrected to Trungpa's wording.
- The old "For example" claimed "you say to yourself 'aversion,' and the clarity arrives half a second later" — unsupported; replaced with Trungpa's own aggression example.
- Plain words rewritten using Trungpa's own glosses (magnetize/possess, reject/attack/cast out, couldn't be bothered) and the reversal.
- The reversal sentence ("cut the root of the three poisons by dealing with others rather than by dealing with yourself") is a paraphrase of Trungpa's own words, attributed to him in the body.

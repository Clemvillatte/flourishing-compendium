# Growth mindset — evidence ledger (w1b, 2026-10-20)

## Sources consulted

- Ku and Stager 2022, systematic review, Frontiers in Psychology
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9284032/), fetched. Verbatim: "A growth
  mindset embodies the thought that one's personal attributes, such as
  intelligence, skill, and ability can be developed and improved through effort.";
  "growth mindset and fixed mindset are just the two ends of a continuum...
  (Haimovitz and Dweck, 2017)"; "mindset effects are meaningfully heterogeneous
  across persons and situations (see Yeager and Dweck, 2020...)"; follow-up
  durability concerns; "Mindset theory... was originally derived from attribution
  theory and achievement goal theory in motivational research." (note: the
  article's "Dweck, 1946" citation is a typographical error and was not used).
- Sisk, Burgoyne, Sun, Butler and Macnamara 2018, Psychological Science
  (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797617739704), abstract via
  search-extract of the sagepub page plus the englelab.gatech.edu full-text PDF
  extract: Meta-analysis 1 (k=273, N=365,915) correlation r=.10; Meta-analysis 2
  (k=43, N=57,155) intervention d=0.08, 95% CI [0.02, 0.14]; abstract: "Overall
  effects were weak for both meta-analyses. However, some results supported
  specific tenets of the theory, namely, that students with low socioeconomic
  status or who are academically at risk might benefit from mind-set
  interventions."
- Xu and Xu 2024, bibliometric analysis, Frontiers in Psychology
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11392869/), fetched. Verbatim: entity
  and incremental theories "translated into 'more user-friendly terms'—fixed
  mindset and growth mindset (Dweck and Yeager, 2019)"; meaning system passage;
  "children's attributions for failure can predict their helplessness or
  mastery-oriented responses (Dweck and Reppucci, 1973)"; "The mindset
  theories... derive from the motivation theory, attribution and achievement
  motivation theory in particular (Dweck and Leggett, 1988)."

- Dweck & Leggett 1988 attribution/achievement-goal lineage kept from the opened
  reviews; the 1946 typo in one review not propagated.
- Sisk numbers quoted with k and N; "weak" language quoted verbatim from the
  abstract; at-risk moderation stated as the authors' own "some results
  supported" phrasing.
- Seed citation lines kept (Dweck 2006; effect sizes vary by context; Yeager &
  Dweck 2012).
- "Other formulations in this library" section and both Cited-in lists preserved

## Primary sources opened

- SEP Meister Eckhart (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/meister-eckhart/):
  "negative virtues"; "creaturely impulses"; "mine-ness" ("True freedom involves a
  kind of self-dispossession, and a letting go of the attachment to the 'mine-ness'
  of one's actions."); "without [a] why" (âne warumbe); Sermon 5b quote verbatim;
  Eckhart born ~1260, most likely died Jan 28 1328; Heidegger "adapting Eckhartian
  terms like Gelassenheit... for their own philosophical purposes, further obscuring
  the Meister's own use".
- Minerva vol 10, Heidegger on Gelassenheit (https://minerva.mic.ul.ie/vol10/Heidegger.html):
  Conversation written 1944-45, published 1959 with the 1955 Memorial Address; "We
  are to do nothing but wait" (1966a p. 62); "I want non-willing" (pp. 58-59);
  "releasement does not belong to the domain of the will" (p. 61); "'waiting' has
  no object"; "let go of them at any time" / "deny them the right to dominate us,
  and so to wrap, confuse, and lay waste our nature" (1966b p. 54); "releasement
  toward things" + "openness to the mystery" (p. 55); lassen root.

## Comparison-side sources opened (each connection labelled as the compendium's comparison)

- Wikipedia Wu wei (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_wei): "effortless action";
  "free flowing and unforced activity".
- Wikipedia Upādāna (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up%C4%81d%C4%81na): "attachment,
  clinging, grasping"; result of craving; Second Noble Truth.
- SEP Stoicism (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stoicism/): "the virtuous agent
  feels no passions (pathê) and, so, that the happy life is entirely passion-free
  (apathês)."
- Suzuki, Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind (https://web.stanford.edu/~funn/ZenMindBeginners.pdf):
  "In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities, but in the expert's there
  are few."; Baker intro: "empty, free of the habits of the expert".
- Hayes, Pistorello and Levin, ACT as a Unified Model of Behavior Change
  (https://www.apa.org/education-career/ce/acceptance-commitment.pdf), fetched:
  "Acceptance in ACT is the active and aware embrace of private experiences without
  unnecessary attempts to change their frequency or form."
- Upaya Reader on Shikantaza (https://www.upaya.org/uploads/pdfs/shikantazanreader.pdf):
  shikantaza = "just sitting"; "objectless meditation... One just sits, not
  concentrating on any particular object of awareness"; "further elaborated by the
  Japanese Soto founder Eihei Dogen (1200-1253)".

## Sources consulted

- Baltes and Staudinger 2000, "Wisdom: A Metaheuristic (Pragmatic) to Orchestrate Mind
  and Virtue Toward Excellence", American Psychologist 55(1):122-36
  (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392856/), abstract fetched. Verbatim quotes used:
  "wisdom in this paradigm is defined as an expert knowledge system concerning the
  fundamental pragmatics of life" (lowercase start as in abstract); "cognitive and
  motivational metaheuristic (pragmatic) that organizes and orchestrates knowledge
  toward human excellence in mind and virtue, both individually and collectively"
  (quoted inline with quotation marks in body). Abstract also lists the criteria
  ("rich factual and procedural knowledge, lifespan contextualism, relativism of values
  and life priorities, and recognition and management of uncertainty") — body lists
  these as five criteria per the abstract's framing; the abstract sentence names the
  family, the body mirrors it.
- Grossmann, Na, Varnum, Kitayama and Nisbett 2010, "Reasoning about social conflicts
  improves into old age", PNAS 107(16):7246-50
  (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2867718/), opened. Verbatim quote used:
  the abstract's result sentence (multiple perspectives, compromise, limits of
  knowledge). Also supports: six wisdom dimensions (intellectual humility, recognition
  of one's limits, others' values/life priorities, interpersonal conflicts as
  contextual, perspectives subject to change, search for conflict resolution).

  (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22866683/), abstract fetched. Body paraphrase only

# Self-transcendence — evidence ledger (w1b, 2026-10-20)

## Sources consulted

- Yaden, Haidt, Hood, Vago and Newberg 2017, "The Varieties of Self-Transcendent
  Experience," Review of General Psychology 21(2):143-160
  (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1037/gpr0000102), abstract via the journal
  page and multiple scholarly quotations. Verbatim: self-transcendent experiences
  are "transient mental states marked by decreased self-salience and increased
  feelings of connectedness"; taxonomy includes mindfulness, flow, peak
  experiences, mystical-type experiences, positive emotions (love, awe);
  annihilational (self-loss) and relational (connectedness) components per the
  review's framework as quoted in secondary literature.
- Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning p. 133: "The more one forgets himself—by
  giving himself to a cause to serve or another person to love—the more human
  he is and the more he actualizes himself. What is called self-actualization is
  not an attainable aim at all... self-actualization is possible only as a
  side-effect of self-transcendence." Passage verified via the published
  scholarly quotation at hrcak.srce.hr/file/443452 (Matkovic and Oklopcic)
  citing p. 133, cross-checked against whatisstoicism.com book highlights.

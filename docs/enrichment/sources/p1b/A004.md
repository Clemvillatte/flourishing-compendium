# A004: source ledger

## Opened sources (2026-10-19, genuineflourishing27)

- Nicomachean Ethics, trans. D. P. Chase (Project Gutenberg #8438) — opened at A039,
  re-mined for this item; NOT recounted (speed directive batching).
  https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm
  - Supported (Book II, Chapter I, = Bekker II.1): "The Virtues then come to be in us neither
    by nature, nor in despite of nature, but we are furnished by nature with a capacity for
    receiving themu and are perfected in them through custom." (the "themu" is the
    extraction/Gutenberg text as printed; the body quotes only the clean fragment and
    paraphrases the rest; the anomaly is not attributed to the translator)
  - Supported (Chapter I): "men come to be builders, for instance, by building; harp-players,
    by playing on the harp: exactly so, by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing
    the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave
    actions brave."
  - Supported (Chapter III, = Bekker II.4), the conditions passage, verified in the saved
    Book II extraction: "those which are produced in the way of the virtues, are, strictly
    speaking, actions of a certain kind (say of Justice or perfected Self-Mastery), not
    merely if in themselves they are in a certain state but if also he who does them does
    them being himself in a certain state, first if knowing what he is doing, next if with
    deliberate preference, and with such preference for the things' own sake; and thirdly
    if being himself stable and unapt to change." The conditions concern the AGENT's state
    ("he who does them does them being himself in a certain state"), not only the action's
    classification; the body's paraphrase (knowing what he is doing, choosing deliberately
    and for its own sake, acting from a settled character) follows this passage.
  - Supported (Chapter III): "he is not in possession of the virtues who merely does these
    facts, but he who also so does them as the just and self-mastering do them."
  - Chapter note: the builders/harp-players passage is Chase's Book II Chapter I (Bekker
    II.1), not II.4 as first drafted; advisor QC caught this and the body now attributes
    it to II.1. Chase Chapter III (Bekker II.4) supplies the conditions (knowing, deliberate
    choice for its own sake, stable).

## What was changed

- Body rewritten: was a four-sentence paraphrase with no verbatim quote; now carries the
  builders/harp-players blockquote (II.1), the II.1 frame fragment, and the II.4 conditions
  sentence. Per advisor QC: the "builder builds before anyone calls him one" gloss was
  removed and replaced by the neutral craft-comparison sentence.
- Plain words replaced (advisor QC at this milestone, item #15): the old "act first, and
  the disposition builds" ran ahead of the source; the final version works from the
  verified II.4 conditions (why you would act, whether the action is chosen for its own
  sake, whether the choice comes from a settled character) and ends with examining your
  reasons. An earlier draft's "keep at it" phrasing was removed because persistence differs
  from a settled character.
- For example replaced: old example claimed the outcome ("become someone who speaks up");
  new one is an explicit examination (why it needs saying, whether it would be courageous).
- Source count for this item: +0 (re-mined the A039 opening).

## Interpretation limits

- II.4's conditions mean an act that merely looks just is not yet a just act; the body
  states the conditions and does not claim that any just-looking act builds character.
- The psychology parallels (habit formation, character strengths, PBRT) are inherited link
  lines, untouched.

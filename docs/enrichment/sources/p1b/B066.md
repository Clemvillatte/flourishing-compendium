# B066: source ledger

## Opened sources (2026-10-19, genuineflourishing27)

- MN 10, Satipatthana Sutta, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu:
  https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html
  (also served by dhammatalks.org, same translation)
  - Supported: "When the mind has aversion, he discerns that the mind has aversion. When the
    mind is without aversion, he discerns that the mind is without aversion." (mind section)
  - Supported: "Or he remains focused on the phenomenon of origination with regard to the
    mind, on the phenomenon of passing away with regard to the mind, or on the phenomenon of
    origination & passing away with regard to the mind."
  - Note: Thanissaro renders "aversion" (dosa), not "anger"; the compendium's "anger" is a
    gloss. The page uses aversion wording in quotes and treats anger as the everyday case.
- Dhammapada 277, trans. Acharya Buddharakkhita:
  https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.20.budd.html
  - Supported: "'All conditioned things are impermanent' — when one sees this with wisdom, one
    turns away from suffering. This is the path to purification."
  - The em dash inside the quotation is the translation's own punctuation.

## Interpretation limits

- Dhp 277 connects seeing impermanence with wisdom to turning away from suffering; it does not
  establish that observing anger reduces its intensity. No outcome claims ("thins out",
  "different message", "the watching outlasts the watched" as an observer-entity claim) are
  made in the applied text.
- Thanissaro renders "aversion" (dosa), not "anger"; the compendium's "anger" is a gloss.
- No claim about how long states last (the 90-second figure lives in kernel K120, not here).

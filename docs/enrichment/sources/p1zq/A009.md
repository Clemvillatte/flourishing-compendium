# A009 — Pursue kalokagathia: nobility-and-goodness as a unified ideal (p1zq item 5)
- Source opened: Eudemian Ethics Book VIII chapter 3 (1248b8 onwards), J. Solomon (1915) translation, freshly opened on johnhboyer-sys.github.io/aristotle-reader/EE/book/5.
- Quotes used (verbatim, contiguous, from that single fresh-opened source):
  "That he who truly deserves this denomination must have the separate virtues is clear; it cannot be otherwise with other things either, for no one is healthy in his entire body and yet healthy in no part of it, but the most numerous and important parts, if not all, must be in the same condition as the whole."
  "A man is noble and good because those goods which are noble are possessed by him for themselves, and because he practises the noble and for its own sake, the noble being the virtues and the acts that proceed from virtue."
  (Also grounded but not quoted verbatim: the "civic disposition / Laconians" passage immediately follows, showing that Aristotle deliberately contrasts mere goodness with kalokagathia — the Spartan civic disposition is good but not noble.)

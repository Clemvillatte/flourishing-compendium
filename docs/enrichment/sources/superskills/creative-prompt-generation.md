## Haught-Tromp 2017 (paired constraints in creative writing)

- Carrier opened: full PDF text at https://www.cct.umb.edu/630/files/HaughtTromp2017-GreenEggsandHam.pdf (fetched with an extraction prompt, 2026-10-24). Journal coordinates: Cognition 166, 1-7 (October 2017), doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2017.05.007.
- What it contributed: the superskill's central claim that a functional prompt is a paired-constraint instruction set, and the concretizing-anchor and promotive-constraint components.
- Verbatim quotes used in the superskill, checked against the fetched text: the abstract sentence "participants given paired constraints (nouns and method) outperformed those who received no constraints and those who received only noun constraints or method constraints on multiple measures of originality and fluency"; and the paired-constraint description of Study 3.
- Paraphrases flagged as paraphrases: the "performance was also better and yielded higher fluency" reading of Study 1 (fetched text: "Performance was also better and yielded higher fluency, Wilks' D (W) = 16.30, p < .05"); "sentences with more added constraints were judged more creative (p < .05)" (fetched Study 4 text); "fluency kept rising as syntactic and phrase constraints were layered in" (fetched Study 3 DT fluency text).
- Honesty note: the fetched PDF text is noisy in places (OCR artifacts). Only clean passages were quoted; the statistical readings above were taken from the legible fragments of the Study 1, 3 and 4 result sections. The claim in the superskill that noun constraints were "half of every winning condition" is editorial shorthand for the paired-constraint design, not a quote.

## Stokes 2009 (constraint-based novelty)

- Carrier opened: full PDF at https://psychology.barnard.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Serra2009.pdf (fetched with an extraction prompt, 2026-10-24). Coordinates: Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts 3(4), 221-225 (2009).
- What it contributed: the definition of a functional prompt as a paired-constraint system; the preclusive and promotive components; the repair logic for the "too narrow" failure mode.
- Verbatim quotes used, checked against the fetched text: "novel solutions follow from precluding reliable, existing responses and promoting riskier, often opposite ones (Stokes, 2005)"; "paired constraints restructure problem spaces in ways that make novelty possible and probable (Stokes, 2007). Possible means that novelty may or may not happen; probable, that paired constraints facilitate the happening"; "In problem solving, they are two-sided, paired. One of the pair retains its function as stricture, limiting or precluding search in some parts of a problem space; the other directs or promotes search in different parts (Reitman, 1965)."
- The superskill shortens the stricture/promotion quote with ellipsis-free paraphrase in one place ("one side of the pair 'retains its function as stricture,' the other 'directs or promotes search in different parts'"); both quoted fragments are verbatim from the opened PDF.

## Smith, Ward and Schumacher 1993 (example fixation)

- Carrier opened: PubMed abstract at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8289661/ (fetched, 2026-10-24). Coordinates: Memory and Cognition 21(6), 837-845 (1993).
- What it contributed: the critical sub-rule that prompts must not contain example outputs or cliché lists; the QC checklist question 2.
- Claims used, verbatim from the fetched abstract: subjects who saw three examples produced designs "more likely to contain features of the examples"; the conformity effect "did not significantly decrease" with a 23-min interpolated task; "explicitly instructing subjects to create ideas that were very different from the examples did not decrease conformity to the examples, and instructing them to conform to the examples significantly increased conformity"; the closing sentence "The results show that recent experience can lead to unintentional conformity, constraining the generation of creative ideas."
- Honesty note: the original study used sketching of experimenter-defined categories, not writing; the superskill applies the fixation finding to prompt design as an editorial step, which the ledger records here.

## Rietzschel, Nijstad and Stroebe 2014 (problem scope; path of least resistance)

- Carrier opened: full PDF at https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/16113815/CRJ_problem_scope.pdf (fetched twice, 2026-10-24: once for content, once to confirm title, authors and abstract). Coordinates: Creativity Research Journal 26(2), 185-191 (2014), doi:10.1080/10400419.2014.901084.
- What it contributed: the "too general" failure mode; the path-of-least-resistance framing in "What a functional prompt is".
- Verbatim quotes used, checked against the fetched text: "the most accessible and easily generated ideas often are the least creative ones" (the paper's restatement of Finke, Ward and Smith 1992; Ward 1994); "Results show that both narrow (vs. broad) problems and creativity (vs. relevance) instructions led to the generation of ideas that were more creative" (abstract).
- Honesty note: the study is a brainstorming experiment, not a writing study; the superskill uses it for the scope principle, not for writing-specific claims.

## Förster, Friedman and Liberman 2004 (temporal distance)

- Carrier opened: PubMed abstract at https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301626/ (fetched, 2026-10-24). Coordinates: Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 87(2), 177-189 (2004).
- What it contributed: the distance component.
- Verbatim quotes used, checked against the fetched abstract: "participants who envisioned their lives and imagined themselves engaging in a task 1 year later as opposed to the next day subsequently performed better on a series of insight tasks"; "a distal perspective was found to improve creative generation of abstract solutions."
- The superskill's construal-level explanation sentence ("psychological distance raises the construal level, and the higher construal carries the writer past the immediate, literal rendering") is editorial interpretation of the abstract's construal framing, recorded as such.

## Friedman and Förster 2010, reviewing Friedman, Fishbach, Förster and Werth 2003 (attentional breadth)

- Carrier opened: PMC full text at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2933078/ (fetched with an extraction prompt, 2026-10-24). Coordinates: Psychological Bulletin 136(4), 522-562 (2010). This is the review carrier; the 2003 experiments were NOT opened in the original article.
- What it contributed: the attentional-breadth component and the wide-to-narrow ordering rule.
- Verbatim quotes used, checked against the fetched review text: "participants who were led to focus perceptual attention broadly produced more original responses on these tasks than did those who focused attention narrowly"; "more unusual exemplars of a series of semantic categories"; the digit-search design description (nine-digit displays, 3-present vs 3-absent, broad-periphery vs narrow-center placement).
- Honesty note: the superskill and this ledger both state that the 2003 study was opened through the 2010 review, not the original. The brick-uses and photo-title tasks are the review's description of the 2003 tasks.

## Amabile 1985 (intrinsic motivation in creative writers)

- Carrier opened: full PDF at https://pages.pomona.edu/~ajr04747/Fall2008/Math58/Papers/Amabile_1985_psp-48-2-393.pdf (fetched with an extraction prompt, 2026-10-24). Coordinates: Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 48(2), 393-399 (1985).
- What it contributed: the intrinsic-frame component; the ban on evaluative framing.
- Verbatim quotes used, checked against the fetched text: "Chosen because they identified themselves as actively involved in creative writing, 72 young adults participated in individual laboratory sessions where they were asked to write 2 brief poems"; "Poems written under an extrinsic orientation were significantly less creative than those written in the other two conditions"; the effect statistics "F(2, 69) = 4.66, p < .013" and the extrinsic-condition mean "M = 15.74" against intrinsic "M = 19.88" and control "M = 18.78"; "An intrinsically motivated state is conducive to creativity, whereas an extrinsically motivated state is detrimental (Amabile, 1983a, 1983b)."
- The superskill's sentence "the poems written under the extrinsic orientation 'were significantly less creative than those written in the other two conditions'" quotes the paper directly; the surrounding application to prompt framing is editorial and is recorded as such.

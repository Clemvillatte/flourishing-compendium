## External carriers verified word-for-word this session

1. Dhammapada 277, Acharya Buddharakkhita translation, AccessToInsight:
   https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.20.budd.html —
   "All conditioned things are impermanent" — when one sees this with wisdom,
   one turns away from suffering. Confirmed verbatim.
2. Seneca, Letters 101, Gummere translation, Wikisource:
   https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_101 —
   "Therefore, let us so order our minds as if we had come to the very end. Let
   us postpone nothing. Let us balance life's account every day." Confirmed
   verbatim.
3. Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus, Vincent Cook translation, epicurus.net:
   http://www.epicurus.net/en/menoeceus.html — "Death, therefore, the most
   awful of evils, is nothing to us, seeing that, when we are, death is not
   come, and, when death is come, we are not." Confirmed verbatim.
4. Spinoza, Ethics IV P67, Bennett translation, earlymoderntexts.com:
   https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/spinoza1665.pdf — "A free man
   thinks about death less than he thinks about anything else; his wisdom is a
   meditation on life, not on death." Confirmed verbatim.
5. SEP Heidegger (Wrathall):
   https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/ — "the possibility of the
   absolute impossibility of Dasein" and "Because I am temporally finite, each
   choice comes at a cost—to pursue one possibility necessarily involves
   forgoing other possibilities (SZ 285)." Confirmed verbatim.
6. Klein et al. 2022, Many Labs 4, Collabra: Psychology:
   https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/8/1/35271/186959/Many-Labs-4-Failure-to-Replicate-Mortality-Salience
   — "Our results were non-diagnostic... unable to replicate the finding under
   any conditions." Confirmed verbatim from the abstract.
7. Burke, Martens and Faucher 2010 meta-analysis, PubMed record:
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20097885/ — "Overall, 164 articles with 277
   experiments were included." and "MS yielded moderate effects (r = .35)"
   Confirmed verbatim.
8. Frias et al. 2011, Death reflection enhances gratitude, Greater Good PDF:
   https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Frias-DeathGratitude.pdf —
   "when one confronts their own death in a specific and vivid manner, their
   feelings of gratitude tend to increase" Confirmed verbatim.
9. Jansen et al. 2019, German version of the DAP-R, BMC Psychology:
   https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6740004/ — the five dimensions and
   the three acceptance types confirmed verbatim.
10. SEP Zhuangzi (Roth, Summer 2012 edition):
    https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2012/entries/zhuangzi/ —
    "centers around the theme of the acceptance of death as part of the
    natural processes of Heaven and Earth" Confirmed verbatim.
11. Cozzolino 2006, Converging Evidence of Dual-Existential Systems?,
    Psychological Inquiry, Essex repository:
    https://repository.essex.ac.uk/12454/ — "individuals process their
    existence either via a specific and personalized existential system, or via
    an abstract and categorical existential system" and "growth-oriented or
    defense-oriented motivational states" Confirmed verbatim.

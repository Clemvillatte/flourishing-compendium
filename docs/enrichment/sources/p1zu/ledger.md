## PP016 — Distinguish realistic from unrealistic optimism

- Claim: expectancies govern effort; optimism predicts lower symptom bother. OPENED: Scheier & Carver 1985, "Optimism, coping, and health," Health Psychology 4(3) 219-247, full text PDF (https://www.deempathischeorganisatie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Beroemd-artikel-van-Scheier-en-Carver-uit-1985-over-de-effecten-van-optimisme.pdf). Verified: outcome-expectancy passage pp. 221-223, verbatim quote from p. 222 ("If expectancies are favorable, the result is renewed effort. If expectancies are sufficiently unfavorable, the result is reduced effort, or even complete disengagement from further attempts"); partial r(138) = -.18, p < .05 (T1 optimism to T2 symptoms controlling T1 symptoms), p. 236; Time 1 - Time 2 lagged partials r(139) = -.16, p < .05. Limitation: 140 subject nonrandom sample, self-report LOT and symptom bother.
- Claim: people assign higher probabilities to desirable outcomes than objective criteria warrant; biased expectations track performance and can raise attainment. OPENED: Armor & Taylor 2002, "When Predictions Fail: The Dilemma of Unrealistic Optimism," in Gilovich, Griffin & Kahneman (eds.), Heuristics and Biases, pp. 334-347, UCLA Taylor Lab PDF (https://taylorlab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2014/11/2002_When-Predictions-Fail.pdf), a scanned image; text recovered by local OCR after 180-degree rotation (raw scan authoritative; OCR artifacts corrected only where the misread was unambiguous, e.g. "logica" -> "logical", "froni" -> "from"). Verified: bias sentence p. 334 (verbatim blockquote on the page); "correlated with past and future performance" / "raise the level of performance that people attain" pp. 346-347; vacillation-data crosstabulation (Table 14.5, p. 345): 25/44 (57%) students whose grades fell below expectations became less optimistic (original below-expectation sample 42).

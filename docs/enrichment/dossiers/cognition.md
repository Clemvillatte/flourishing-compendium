# Cognition

> How does thinking work, and where does it reliably fail?

Thinking is what the mind does with what awareness delivers, and it is a mixed instrument: the same equipment that reasons and plans also biases, ruminates and confuses. A conclusion can arrive fast, confident, and wrong. The question worth asking of my own thinking is not only whether it is intelligent but whether it is going where I want it to go.

Two literatures hold the material: the classical analyses of reason and its limits, and the experimental catalogues of bias. The selection and sustaining of awareness is a separate theme with its own dossier; what lives here is what thinking does with what awareness delivers.

### Nearby terms

- Thinking: the general capacity; its two speeds are held by [System 1 and System 2](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=System%201%20and%20System%202).
- Thoughts: the "thoughts are not facts" territory is held here by [Cognitive defusion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20defusion) and the meditation cluster it links to.
- Reasoning: studied as measured performance by [Critical thinking research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Critical%20thinking%20research) and as wise performance by [Wise reasoning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wise%20reasoning).
- Reason: the faculty's classical and critical accounts, logos, instrumental reason and its critics, are held by [Instrumental reason](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Instrumental%20reason) and the philosophy cards it links to.
- Deciding: decision research, which covers choice under uncertainty, preference and risk, is the subject of the Decision-Making dossier; the judgment material here stops where choosing begins.

## How thinking runs, and where it fails

### How fast does thinking run, and what does speed cost?

The most influential answer gives thinking two speeds. [System 1 and System 2](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=System%201%20and%20System%202) are Kahneman's names for the two modes: fast, automatic, intuitive thought, and slow, effortful, deliberate thought. The terms were borrowed from Stanovich and West.

The idea has an independent life in personality theory: [Cognitive-Experiential Self-Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive-Experiential%20Self-Theory), Epstein's dual-process account, runs a rational system that thinks slowly and logically against an experiential one that is fast, automatic and emotionally driven. The general form is held by [Dual-process theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dual-process%20theory): autonomous Type 1 processing distinguished from Type 2 processing that depends on working memory and supports hypothetical reasoning.

Why does the fast mode exist at all? Because it is economical, and its errors are the price. [Cognitive biases](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20biases) are the systematic, predictable errors produced by the mind's shortcuts, from the founding 1974 paper, and feeling is one of the shortcuts: the [Affect heuristic](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20heuristic) is the finding that positive or negative feelings about an object drive judgments of its risk and benefit. The founding paper's own summary of the program:

> "This article described three heuristics that are employed in making judgments under uncertainty: (i) representativeness, which is usually employed when people are asked to judge the probability that an object or event A belongs to class or process B; (ii) availability of instances or scenarios, which is often employed when people are asked to assess the frequency of a class or the plausibility of a particular development; and (iii) adjustment from an anchor, which is usually employed in numerical prediction when a relevant value is available. These heuristics are highly economical and usually effective, but they lead to systematic and predictable errors." ([Tversky and Kahneman 1974, Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124))

Speed also has a budget. [Cognitive load theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20load%20theory), Sweller's theory of working-memory demands in learning, starts from the small capacity of working memory, the same narrow resource the slow system depends on.

Not every fast judgment is a bias, though. Expertise can make speed accurate: [Expert intuition (Klein's recognition-primed decision model)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Expert%20intuition%20%28Klein%27s%20recognition-primed%20decision%20model%29) is Gary Klein's account of how experienced decision-makers act fast under pressure: a possible course of action is generated and compared against the situation's constraints. And the quality of slow thinking is itself measurable: [Critical thinking research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Critical%20thinking%20research) studies how well people reason and decide, along the Cognitive Reflection Test line, and [Actively open-minded thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Actively%20open-minded%20thinking), Baron's reasoning style, is the disposition the two speeds leave room for: considering alternatives and evidence against one's own conclusion, not only for it.

### Where does thinking happen: in the head, or in the world?

A second question displaces the first: what is doing the thinking? The research programme of [Embodied cognition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Embodied%20cognition) moves thinking out of the amodal symbol system and into the body, in Barsalou's grounded-cognition manifesto, and [Embedded cognition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Embedded%20cognition) lightens it further: thinking happens against, and is lightened by, the environment the thinker is embedded in.

The environment does part of the work. [Situated cognition theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Situated%20cognition%20theory) has thinking scaffolded by the situation, with tools, places and other people carrying part of the cognitive load; [Enactivism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enactivism) treats cognition as something a living body does in its environment, rather than something a brain does with internal representations. The strongest version denies the skull as the boundary of thinking: [Distributed cognition extended mind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distributed%20cognition%20extended%20mind) is the Clark and Chalmers programme of active externalism. Their position, in their own words:

> "In these cases, the human organism is linked with an external entity in a two-way interaction, creating a coupled system that can be seen as a cognitive system in its own right." ([Clark and Chalmers, The Extended Mind](https://consc.net/papers/extended.html))

> "We advocate a very different sort of externalism: an active externalism, based on the active role of the environment in driving cognitive processes." ([Clark and Chalmers, The Extended Mind](https://consc.net/papers/extended.html))

The same outward move applies to people. [Dialogic reason](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dialogic%20reason) treats understanding and good judgement as arising between people in exchange, rather than inside a single isolated mind, in Buber and Habermas.

Cognitive styles also differ across cultures: [Analytic vs holistic cognition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Analytic%20vs%20holistic%20cognition) documents two habits of attention and explanation across cultures, in Nisbett's group's account, and [Dialectical thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dialectical%20thinking) carries the account of naïve dialecticism, lay beliefs about how the world works, found more strongly in East Asian cultures. One more variation belongs here as a strength rather than a deficit: [Autistic cognition — detail-focused processing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autistic%20cognition%20%E2%80%94%20detail-focused%20processing), the weak coherence account, describes strength in detail and pattern where the typical style pulls for gist and global form.

The dialecticism definition is Spencer-Rodgers and colleagues':

> "Naïve dialecticism refers to a set of East Asian lay beliefs characterized by tolerance for contradiction, the expectation of change, and cognitive holism." ([Spencer-Rodgers et al., Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2811254/), [NCBI full text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=2811254))

### How do beliefs form, and why do they hold?

[Belief as habit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Belief%20as%20habit) is Peirce's account in "How to Make Our Ideas Clear": a belief is the establishment of a habit of action. And [Holding as hypothesis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Holding%20as%20hypothesis) is his practice: treating a strongly held belief as a proposition to be tested rather than a possession to be defended. His own lines:

> "The essence of belief is the establishment of a habit; and different beliefs are distinguished by the different modes of action to which they give rise." ([Peirce, How to Make Our Ideas Clear](https://peirce.org/writings/p119.html))

> "As it appeases the irritation of doubt, which is the motive for thinking, thought relaxes, and comes to rest for a moment when belief is reached." ([Peirce, How to Make Our Ideas Clear](https://peirce.org/writings/p119.html))

Beliefs can also outlive their evidence. The [Belief perseverance effect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Belief%20perseverance%20effect) is Ross, Lepper and Hubbard's demonstration that beliefs persist after their evidential basis has been removed. Two further mechanisms have been studied. [Motivated reasoning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Motivated%20reasoning) is Ziva Kunda's 1990 proposal that motivation may affect reasoning through reliance on a biased set of cognitive processes; [Identity-protective cognition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Identity-protective%20cognition) is Dan Kahan's research programme on reasoning in which "is this true?" is answered, unnoticed, by "does this mark me as one of us?"

Beliefs also cohere, and the pressure of incoherence is felt. [Cognitive dissonance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20dissonance) is Festinger's theory of the mismatch between cognitions and the pressure to reduce it:

> "These two elements are in a dissonant relation if, considering these two alone, the obverse of one element would follow from the other. [...] If, considering a pair of elements, either one does follow from the other, then the relation between them is consonant." ([Festinger, A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance, ch. 1](https://www.panarchy.org/festinger/dissonance.html))

Belief also has its lay theories. [Implicit theories of intelligence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Implicit%20theories%20of%20intelligence) are people's lay beliefs about what intelligence is: a fixed quantity one is born with, or a quality that can be grown, in Dweck's model. [Belief evaluation in CBT](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Belief%20evaluation%20in%20CBT) examines what a thought does, not only whether it is true; cognitive therapy evaluates the content, acceptance-based therapies the context. And [Cognitive bias modification theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20bias%20modification%20theory) targets the biases that confer vulnerability to anxiety, working on attention bias and interpretation bias directly.

### When does thinking itself hurt?

The clinical material answers with a family of thinking styles. [Beck's cognitive model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beck%27s%20cognitive%20model) holds that automatic thoughts, assumptions and core schemas about self and world maintain depression and anxiety; the [Cognitive-behavioural model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive-behavioural%20model) behind CBT says that how a person interprets a situation, more than the situation itself, shapes what they feel and do; [Cognitive mediation of distress](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20mediation%20of%20distress) is the cognitive-therapy assumption that interpretations influence emotional responses.

The errors Beck found in depressed patients have a name and a research programme: [Cognitive distortions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20distortions) are the idiosyncratic conceptualizations that led him to characterize depression's phenomena in terms of a thought disorder, and [Cognitive distortions research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20distortions%20research) works to identify and modify the patterns: catastrophising, overgeneralisation, personalisation. One such pattern is structural: [Dichotomous (black-and-white) thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dichotomous%20%28black-and-white%29%20thinking) sorts events into two opposite boxes: all or nothing, right or wrong, safe or dangerous.

The hurt is also maintained by repetition. [Rumination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Rumination), Nolen-Hoeksema's term, is repetitively and passively focusing on one's symptoms and their possible causes and consequences, rather than acting; response styles theory's account of why it travels with depression. The metacognitive wing locates the maintenance one level up: the [Metacognitive model of anxiety and depression (Wells)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metacognitive%20model%20of%20anxiety%20and%20depression%20%28Wells%29) holds that positive and negative metacognitive beliefs maintain worry and rumination, and [Cognitive attentional syndrome Wells](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20attentional%20syndrome%20Wells) is his name for the perseverative, self-relevant, negatively loaded style of processing he treats as underlying most psychological disorders.

Repetition even serves avoidance. [Worry theory cognitive avoidance Borkovec](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Worry%20theory%20cognitive%20avoidance%20Borkovec) is the avoidance theory of worry: predominantly verbal thought functioning as cognitive avoidance in response to threatening images. And the attempt to stop a thought can strengthen it: the [Ironic process](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ironic%20process), Wegner's account of how attempts at thought suppression can backfire.

### Can thinking be changed?

The therapeutic traditions say yes, along two lines. One line corrects the content: [Cognitive restructuring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20restructuring) is the technique family at the heart of cognitive therapy, helping clients identify, evaluate and correct inaccurate beliefs. [Rational Emotive Behavior Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Rational%20Emotive%20Behavior%20Theory), Albert Ellis's 1955 theory of how suffering is made and unmade, works the same territory. The other line changes the relation to the thought: [Cognitive defusion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20defusion), the ACT practice of seeing thoughts as thoughts, mental events passing through awareness, rather than as commands, facts or the self.

Between the two lines sit the mindfulness-based and metacognitive programmes. [Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindfulness-based%20cognitive%20therapy) is a group program for people who have recovered from depression and want to stay well, combining systematic mindfulness training with elements from cognitive therapy, and [Decentering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Decentering) names the capacity it trains: observing thoughts and feelings as mental events rather than as the world.

Wells's programme makes the same move clinically: [Detached mindfulness theory Wells](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Detached%20mindfulness%20theory%20Wells) responds to a thought by noticing it and doing nothing further with it, and [Metacognitive therapy (Wells)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metacognitive%20therapy%20%28Wells%29) shifts the target of treatment from the content of thought to the metacognitive beliefs that keep it going.

The strategy already met in the affect dossier also belongs here: [Cognitive reappraisal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20reappraisal) changes an emotional event's meaning so that the feeling that follows changes. And change can happen at the level of a life's direction, not only a thought: [Cognitive transformation theory of desistance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20transformation%20theory%20of%20desistance) is Giordano and colleagues' account of how people stop offending: cognitive shifts that recognise new possibilities and reinterpret one's past.

The two therapeutic traditions differ in what they take as the target of change. Cognitive therapy's line works on the thought's accuracy, per the meta-analytic review of its core technique:

> "One method that is hypothesized to play a role in the process of change across many psychotherapies is cognitive restructuring (CR), which refers to a set of techniques used to help clients identify, evaluate and correct inaccurate beliefs... Rather than being defined as a single method, CR refers to the collection of therapeutic techniques aimed at helping clients identify, challenge, and correct biased and maladaptive automatic interpretations of events." ([Ezawa and Hollon 2023, Psychotherapy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10440210/))

The acceptance-and-commitment tradition keeps the thought and moves the observer:

> "to see thoughts and feelings for what they are (i.e., a verbally entangled process of minding) rather than what they advertise themselves to be (e.g., the world understood, structured reality)" ([Hayes, Strosahl and Wilson 1999, quoted in Bernstein et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5103165/))

Whether the two treatments compete or stack is left open.

### How does thinking change over a life?

The abilities do not rise and fall together. [Cognitive ageing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20ageing) is the finding that some abilities peak at high-school graduation and some plateau in early adulthood. Development has its own account: [Information-processing theory of cognitive development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Information-processing%20theory%20of%20cognitive%20development) describes how children represent, transform and use information.

Moral thinking was long treated as a fixed staircase. [Cognitive-developmental theory of moral reasoning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive-developmental%20theory%20of%20moral%20reasoning) is Kohlberg's fixed sequence of stages, built on Dewey's and Piaget's levels, with [Justice reasoning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Justice%20reasoning), justice-as-fairness, as the central moral concept of the program.

### What can thinking do, and what watches it?

The last movement turns thinking on itself. [Metacognition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metacognition) is thinking about thinking, in Flavell's 1979 article and its four classes of phenomena, and [Metacognitive monitoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metacognitive%20monitoring) is the assessment of one's own cognitive activity, including memory and comprehension. People differ in appetite for the effort: [Need for cognition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Need%20for%20cognition) is Cacioppo and Petty's 1982 construct, a stable individual difference in how much people engage in and enjoy effortful thinking.

[Unconscious thought theory (Dijksterhuis)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Unconscious%20thought%20theory%20%28Dijksterhuis%29), Dijksterhuis and Nordgren's 2006 proposal, treats unconscious thought as a mode with its own strengths: the mind does work on a problem while attention is on something else. A further inward step names the watcher: [The observing self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20observing%20self) is Deikman's name for awareness itself, the "I" that witnesses thoughts, feelings and sensations without being identical to any of them.

A separate question concerns where judgment comes from. [Moral intuition research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20intuition%20research) asks whether moral judgments come from reasoning or from fast evaluations that reasoning later explains, in Haidt's 2001 case.

The question of what experience leaves behind is held by [Wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wisdom): the knowledge and judgment that experience is supposed to leave behind, approached by psychological science along the Berlin expertise line and others.

The repertoire of thinking also has named components. [Cognitive flexibility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20flexibility), one of the three core executive functions, is the capacity to switch perspectives and adapt to changed circumstances. [Divergent and convergent thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Divergent%20and%20convergent%20thinking) pairs generating many varied ideas from the same starting point with narrowing to the one already known answer; [Divergent thinking tests](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Divergent%20thinking%20tests) are the standard tasks creativity researchers use to measure the generation of many varied responses.

[Episodic future thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Episodic%20future%20thinking) is the mind's rehearsal of specific futures: vividly imagining and mentally simulating personally relevant events that have not yet happened.

The situation-by-situation account of good judgment is Grossmann's. [Wise reasoning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wise%20reasoning) is his research program's answer to what wisdom is, studied empirically: strategies deployed, or not, situation by situation. His own list of the strategies:

> "They include (a) intellectual humility or recognition of limits of own knowledge, (b) appreciation of perspectives broader than the issue at hand, (c) sensitivity to the possibility of change in social relations, and (d) compromise or integration of different opinions." ([Grossmann 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf))

> "People vary dramatically in their likelihood of utilizing wise thought from one situation to the next." ([Grossmann 2017, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jhoey/teaching/cogsci600/papers/Grossmann_PPS_2017.pdf))

A last warning belongs to the whole theme. [Instrumental reason](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Instrumental%20reason) is reason reduced to the choice of means: it asks how to get what it has been given, and has stopped asking whether what it has been given is worth wanting. The question of whether thinking is serving the life is not one thinking settles on its own.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Must representativeness be defined before it can explain judgments?

The bias catalogue rests on labels: representativeness, availability, anchoring. Gigerenzer's objection is that a label is not a model, and his reply to Kahneman and Tversky names the obstacle as he sees it:

> "vague heuristics have directed attention away from detailed models of cognitive processes and toward post-hoc accounts of alleged errors" ([Gigerenzer 1996, Psychological Review](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/communication/Gigerenzer1996.pdf))

A one-word explanation, he argues, can cover a finding and its opposite: the gambler's fallacy and the hot-hand fallacy were both attributed to representativeness; in Gigerenzer's words, "No model of similarity can explain a phenomenon and its contrary" ([Gigerenzer and Brighton 2009](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2099728_9/component/file_2563980/content)). Kahneman and Tversky's answer is that representativeness is measurable, so a formal definition is not needed to test it:

> "This objection misses the point that representativeness (like similarity) can be assessed experimentally; hence it need not be defined a priori. Testing the hypothesis that probability judgments are mediated by representativeness does not require a theoretical model of either concept." ([Kahneman and Tversky 1996, Psychological Review](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~mckenzie/KahnemanTversky1996PsychRev.pdf))

Their reply also states that Gigerenzer's critique "misrepresents the authors' theoretical position and ignores critical evidence", and stands by the finding that judgments of frequency are susceptible to large and systematic biases ([Kahneman and Tversky 1996](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~mckenzie/KahnemanTversky1996PsychRev.pdf)). The disagreement concerns how explicit an explanation of judgment must be. Kahneman and Tversky hold that experimental assessment of representativeness can test its proposed role without a prior theoretical model; Gigerenzer asks for a detailed account of the cognitive process producing the judgment. I prefer explicit process models, because they make the proposed mechanism easier to inspect, and that preference does not make the other side's proposal untestable; their reply describes an empirical test. What I still want to know is when a label is carrying a real process and when it is only naming the outcome.

### Does cognition extend into the world, or only embed itself in it?

Clark and Chalmers's criterion for their side of the boundary question:

> "If, as we confront some task, a part of the world functions as a process which, *were it done in the head*, we would have no hesitation in recognizing as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world *is* (so we claim) part of the cognitive process." ([Clark and Chalmers, The Extended Mind](https://consc.net/papers/extended.html))

Rupert's objection to exactly that criterion, as stated in the Stanford Encyclopedia's exposition of the dispute:

> "Instead of insisting that cognitive systems extend, Rupert asks, why not regard them as seeking ways to embed themselves among tools that make their jobs easier? An axe does not become part of a person when she uses it to chop down a tree, why does a notebook become part of cognitive system when a brain uses it to locate MoMA?" ([Shapiro and Spaulding, SEP "Embodied Cognition", §5.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/embodied-cognition/))

Clark's side of the answer, in the same exposition:

> "Similarly, Clark would deny Rupert's claim that the hypothesis of embedded cognition can equally well save the phenomena that the hypothesis of extended cognition was intended to capture and do so while requiring less revision of existing ideas about how cognitive systems operate." ([Shapiro and Spaulding, SEP "Embodied Cognition", §5.2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/embodied-cognition/))

The two positions answer the same question, whether the notebook functions as part of the cognitive system or as a tool the system uses, with opposing answers, each stated above. What the dispute turns on is whether parity of function is enough for constitution, or whether constitution demands more than a tool's usefulness; nothing here adjudicates between them.

### Does reasoning cause moral judgments, or explain them afterwards?

Haidt's abstract opens by naming the tradition he is attacking: "Research on moral judgment has been dominated by rationalist models, in which moral judgment is thought to be caused by moral reasoning" ([Haidt 2001, Psychological Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/), [eutils abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=11699120&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)). His case against that causal arrow, in his own summary:

> "The author gives 4 reasons for considering the hypothesis that moral reasoning does not cause moral judgment; rather, moral reasoning is usually a post hoc construction, generated after a judgment has been reached." ([Haidt 2001, Psychological Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/), [eutils abstract](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=11699120&rettype=abstract&retmode=text))

On the [social intuitionist model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20intuition%20research) the judgment is generally the result of quick, automatic evaluations, and the reasoning arrives afterward to justify it. The model is social in Haidt's own emphasis: it deemphasizes the private reasoning done by individuals and emphasizes social and cultural influences ([Haidt 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11699120/)).

The direct counter came in the same journal, two years later. Pizarro and Bloom's commentary on the model presents "evidence that fast and automatic moral intuitions are actually shaped and informed by prior reasoning", and concludes that these facts "limit the strong claims of the social intuitionist model concerning the irrelevance of conscious deliberation" ([Pizarro and Bloom 2003, Psychological Review](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:12529062&format=json&resultType=core)).

Haidt's usually and generally leave reasoning some work, and Pizarro and Bloom challenge how little causal importance the model gives it: their objection is that an immediate response can depend on earlier reflection. I give that objection weight, since a judgment's speed alone does not establish how the intuition behind it was formed. I still want to know when earlier reasoning changes later intuitions, and when reflection changes a judgment already reached.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the relation to one's own thinking: thoughts taken as facts, the first interpretation kept, beliefs held past their evidence, and reasoning pressed into service of identity. The adaptive direction is the thought seen as a thought, alternatives actually considered, beliefs revisable, and reasoning used to find out rather than to defend.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [Cognitive defusion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K126%20%E2%80%94%20Cognitive%20defusion): noting a painful thought in the form "I'm having the thought that..."; the thought's authority, not its content, is what changes.

2. [Cognitive reappraisal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K127%20%E2%80%94%20Cognitive%20reappraisal): when a situation triggers distress, generating an alternative interpretation: what else could this mean, what would a wise friend say, what is the larger context.

3. [Thought record](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K128%20%E2%80%94%20Thought%20record): the CBT workhorse: situation, thought, feeling, evidence for, evidence against, and a more balanced statement.

4. [Wise-reasoning prompts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K260%20%E2%80%94%20Wise-reasoning%20prompts): for a troubling situation, four questions: how it could unfold, the perspectives on it, how one's own interests bias the view, and what a wise friend would do.

5. [Public reasoning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K365%20%E2%80%94%20Public%20reasoning): when taking a public position on a question of rights or justice, building the strongest honest case for the opposing view before stating your own.

6. [Compassionate Thinking: name the part, then look from the other side](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K381%20%E2%80%94%20Compassionate%20Thinking%3A%20name%20the%20part%2C%20then%20look%20from%20the%20other%20side): giving inner states names, "Anxious Me," "the Critic," as separate voices to talk about rather than be.

7. [Cognitive acceptance: watch thoughts float by](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K385%20%E2%80%94%20Cognitive%20acceptance%3A%20watch%20thoughts%20float%20by): a chosen recurring thought watched for five minutes floating past like clouds, then noticed for twenty-four hours without judging, changing or avoiding it; the aim being to stop adding struggle on top.

8. [Carry the thought on paper](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K387%20%E2%80%94%20Carry%20the%20thought%20on%20paper): a self-led acceptance exercise from Tolin's Doing CBT: the recurring thought written down, folded, kept in the pocket; the slip stands in for the thought, noticed and carried while the day goes on.

9. [Sounds and Thoughts meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K394%20%E2%80%94%20Sounds%20and%20Thoughts%20meditation): resting on the breath, then receiving sounds near and far without searching for them, then turning the same receiving stance on thoughts: events arising, lingering, passing.

10. [Sing the thought, or say it in a silly voice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K401%20%E2%80%94%20Sing%20the%20thought%2C%20or%20say%20it%20in%20a%20silly%20voice): a self-critical thought already examined, said once normally, then sung to a well-known tune or said in an exaggerated voice; the caution is to use it only on an examined thought, not a fresh wound.

The evidence limits are uneven across the list, and the kernels say so where it matters: the thought record and reappraisal carry the CBT trial literature; the defusion family carries the ACT literature; the wise-reasoning prompts carry Grossmann's situation-varying findings rather than a tested protocol.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line.

### Beliefs

[ST102 — Peirce's Maxim](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST102%20%E2%80%94%20Peirce%27s%20Maxim): Peirce asks what the meaning of a concept is and answers with a maxim: consider what practical effects we conceive the object of our concept to have; the meaning is those effects.

[ST103 — James and the Cash Value of Ideas](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST103%20%E2%80%94%20James%20and%20the%20Cash%20Value%20of%20Ideas): James popularises the maxim with a metaphor that stuck and annoyed: true ideas are those we can assimilate, corroborate and verify, the cash value of an idea.

[ST106 — Peirce's Four Methods](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST106%20%E2%80%94%20Peirce%27s%20Four%20Methods): “The Fixation of Belief” catalogues the ways people settle their beliefs: tenacity holds the belief and ignores doubt; authority lets the institution do it; and the list runs on to the method that fixes belief by appeal to the real.

[ST186 — The Fox and the Grapes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST186%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Fox%20and%20the%20Grapes): a fox, unable to reach the grapes, walks away saying they were probably sour anyway; Aesop's tag: it is easy to despise what you cannot get.

### Thinking that hurts

[ST154 — John Nash and the Delusion's Signature](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST154%20%E2%80%94%20John%20Nash%20and%20the%20Delusion%27s%20Signature): the film renders Nash's hallucinations as fully realised characters, a roommate, a government agent, a child, and his solution is diagnostic: the little girl never ages, therefore she is not real; he returns to teaching, and to the hallucinations, as permanent companions.

[ST155 — Meno's Paradox](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST155%20%E2%80%94%20Meno%27s%20Paradox): the inquiry hits a wall: how will you look for virtue if you do not know what it is, and if you know what you are looking for, why look?

[ST172 — Thales Falls in the Well](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST172%20%E2%80%94%20Thales%20Falls%20in%20the%20Well): Thales, walking at night studying the stars, falls into a well; the Thracian servant girl laughs, so eager to know what is in the heavens that he cannot see what is at his feet.

### Changing thinking

[ST427 — The Ladder for Unskillful Thoughts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST427%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Ladder%20for%20Unskillful%20Thoughts): the Vitakkasanthana Sutta addresses a monk in whom thoughts imbued with desire, aversion or delusion arise, and offers a ladder of responses for relaxing such thoughts.

[ST419 — Poincare Leaves the Problem and Returns to Check It](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST419%20%E2%80%94%20Poincare%20Leaves%20the%20Problem%20and%20Returns%20to%20Check%20It): Poincare's mathematical investigation is repeatedly interrupted; the ideas arrive while he is travelling or walking, and he returns to deliberate work to check and finish them.

[ST415 — The Blind Men and the Elephant](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST415%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Blind%20Men%20and%20the%20Elephant): after monks report sectarians quarrelling, the Buddha tells of a king who has people blind from birth shown different parts of an elephant; each group describes the whole animal from the part it was shown.

[ST083 — Neo Takes the Red Pill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST083%20%E2%80%94%20Neo%20Takes%20the%20Red%20Pill): offered the blue pill that returns him to comfortable ignorance or the red pill that shows him his world is a simulation, he takes the red pill.

### Two speeds

[ST130 — Berlin's Fox and Hedgehog](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST130%20%E2%80%94%20Berlin%27s%20Fox%20and%20Hedgehog): Berlin takes a line from Archilochus: the fox knows many things, but the hedgehog knows one big thing, and turns it into the most quoted typology in twentieth-century thought.

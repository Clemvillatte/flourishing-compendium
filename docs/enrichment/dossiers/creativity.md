# Creativity

> Where does the new come from, and can a person get better at producing it?

When I call an idea creative, I want to know two things about it: what is new in it, and what makes it work for its purpose. The field's working definition joins those requirements. A product or an idea is creative when it is both novel and appropriate:

> "Creativity: the generation of products or ideas that are both novel and appropriate" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 570](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf))

Novelty alone is easy: a random string of syllables has never been said before. Appropriateness alone is easier still: the familiar answer fits. The creative product holds the two together, and the difficult case sits right at their join. An idea that changes only its maker's understanding is new to that person and to no one else. Must anyone else recognize it before it counts?

Psychology arrived here late. Guilford's 1950 presidential address to the American Psychological Association opened the subject this way:

> "The neglect of this subject by psychologists is appalling." ([Guilford 1950](https://creativityinmusiced.weebly.com/uploads/2/3/8/5/23859476/guilford-1950-creativity.pdf))

His evidence: of approximately 121,000 titles listed in the previous 23 years of Psychological Abstracts, only 186 were indexed as bearing on creativity ([Guilford 1950](https://creativityinmusiced.weebly.com/uploads/2/3/8/5/23859476/guilford-1950-creativity.pdf)). Between that address and the four-C model, the diary studies and the incubation experiments stands the field he asked for.

### Nearby terms

- Divergent thinking: the generation mode, producing many ideas quickly. It is one part of the process, not the whole capacity, and its tests are contested.

- Imagination: the wider faculty of representing what is not present. The Stanford Encyclopedia opens its entry with a definition that turns on what imagination is not: "To imagine is to represent without aiming at things as they actually, presently, and subjectively are. One can use imagination to represent possibilities other than the actual, to represent times other than the present, and to represent perspectives other than one's own" ([Munro, Liao and Gendler, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/imagination/)). Creativity uses imagination without being reducible to it.

- Insight: the sudden-reinterpretation mode of solving, one route by which the new arrives.

- Creation: the meaning-in-life theme of making. I distinguish the capacity to devise something new from the question of what making contributes to a life; [Creative values](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20values) names Frankl's term for what a person gives to the world through a work or a deed.

- Flow: the absorption creative work can induce; a frequent companion of making, not the same thing as it.

## What gets to count as creative

The question of scope has a working answer: the four-C model. [Kaufman and Beghetto's levels](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Four-C%20model%20of%20creativity) run from mini-c, "the 'novel and personally meaningful interpretation of experiences, actions, and events'", through little-c, the everyday creativity of a gardener or a problem-solver at work, up to Pro-c, the developed professional, and Big-C, the discipline-changing work ([Cambridge International chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)). Big-C is "reserved to describe the work of an elite few who have transformed their discipline", and the chapter is blunt that it is out of reach of most of us ([Cambridge International chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)).

The lower levels are not consolation prizes. Vygotsky, quoted in the same chapter, drew the line generously: "Any human act that gives rise to something new is referred to as a creative act, regardless of whether what is constructed is a physical object or some mental or emotional construct that lives within the person who created it and is known only to him" ([Vygotsky 1967, quoted in the Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)). The chapter adds that mini-c "may not be visible to outsiders and may consist purely of ideas and connections that the learner creates" ([Cambridge International chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)).

There is a counterweight in how research actually scores creative work. The Consensual Assessment Technique has experts rate products, and it enjoys wide use because of the consistently high interrater agreements it reaches ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 572](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). What an expert panel would agree to call creative and what a learner privately constructs are not the same bar, and the field works with both: the four-C model names the private form real, and the rating methods certify the public one. On [PP051 — Treat creativity as a process, not a trait](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PP051%20%E2%80%94%20Treat%20creativity%20as%20a%20process%2C%20not%20a%20trait) the question stops being whether I am a creative person and becomes what the next task needs.

The everyday level also carries evidence of its own. In a 13-day diary study of 658 young adults:

> "Lagged multilevel models revealed that people felt higher activated PA and flourishing following days when they reported more creative activity than usual. The other direction – PA predicting next-day creative activity – was not supported, suggesting that the cross-day effect was specific to creative activity predicting well-being." ([Conner, DeYoung and Silvia 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2016.1257049))

The design is observational and does not establish causation, and the unsupported reverse direction concerned positive affect predicting next-day creative activity. [BE059 — Engage in creative behaviour — making is well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE059%20%E2%80%94%20Engage%20in%20creative%20behaviour%20%E2%80%94%20making%20is%20well-being) states the study's own limits. The finding concerns everyday creative activity, the study's own term, which is the level the four-C model calls little-c.

## Where the new idea comes from

The process has two named modes. Divergent thinking is "spontaneous, free-flowing thinking with the goal of generating many different ideas in a short period"; convergent thinking is "more disciplined thinking, focused on narrowing possibilities to a workable solution" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, pp. 573, 579](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). [CO074 — Cultivate creative thinking — divergent and convergent modes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=CO074%20%E2%80%94%20Cultivate%20creative%20thinking%20%E2%80%94%20divergent%20and%20convergent%20modes) draws the practical consequence: generation and evaluation are different jobs, and doing them as separate passes is a skill, not a personality trait.

Inside the generative mode there is a signature pattern, the serial order effect:

> "During the course of divergent thinking (DT), the number of generated ideas decreases while the originality of ideas increases." ([Wang, Hao, Ku, Grabner and Fink 2017, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259772/))

In that alternative-uses task, originality was higher later in the session among participants with stronger shifting ability, and showed no difference for those with weaker shifting ability ([Wang et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259772/)). The authors suggest that suppressing interference from obvious ideas and switching to new categories may explain why more original ideas appear as time passes, with their wording marked as tentative ([Wang et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259772/)). [K325 — Divergent-option generation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K325%20%E2%80%94%20Divergent-option%20generation) builds its floor of five options on this pattern as a design choice, not as a tested rule about the number five.

The new also arrives by a different route, suddenly. Insight is "when a person suddenly reinterprets a stimulus, situation, or event to produce a nonobvious, nondominant interpretation", taking the form of a solution, the comprehension of a joke or metaphor, or the recognition of an ambiguous percept ([Kounios and Beeman 2014, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24405359/)). The same review reports that individual differences in the tendency to solve problems insightfully rather than in a deliberate, analytic fashion are associated with different patterns of resting-state brain activity ([Kounios and Beeman 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24405359/)). [Insight problem-solving](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Insight%20problem-solving) holds the fuller picture, including the field's own admission that the definition is not settled.

Time away from the problem has its own evidence, from a meta-analysis of the incubation literature:

> "The authors identified a positive incubation effect, with divergent thinking tasks benefiting more than linguistic and visual insight tasks from incubation. Longer preparation periods gave a greater incubation effect, whereas filling an incubation period with high cognitive demand tasks gave a smaller incubation effect." ([Sio and Ormerod 2009, abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ827110))

Longer effort before the break helped, and a demanding filler shrank the benefit. What the break does inside the head is a separate question, and it is contested.

Constraint is the counterintuitive fourth input. [K324 — Constraint-based creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K324%20%E2%80%94%20Constraint-based%20creation) turns it into a practice: choose an avoided task and impose one concrete limit, 100 words, one colour, three ingredients, one hour, and when the pull toward the familiar shows up, treat the constraint as the wall that turns you. The exercise is a bet rather than a tested rule: when starting feels difficult, try one concrete limit and notice whether it gives a useful starting point, and change it if it does not.

## What the person brings

Is the capacity fixed? The review's summary answer:

> "Although creativity in persons has some trait-like (stable) aspects, it is also a state subject to influence by the social environment." ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 590](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf))

Some of it is stable, and the rest moves with motivation, environment and practised skill. Training is part of the moving part: Scott and colleagues' meta-analysis of seventy prior studies found that carefully constructed creativity training programs typically result in gains in performance, with benefits generalizing across criteria, setting, and target population, and that the more successful programs employed realistic exercises building cognitive skills and heuristics for applying them ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 577](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)).

Motivation is another, and it states its principle directly:

> "This is the intrinsic motivation principle of creativity: Intrinsic motivation, defined as the drive to do something for the sheer enjoyment, interest, and personal challenge of the task itself (rather than for some external goal), is conducive to creativity, whereas extrinsic motivation is generally detrimental." ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 581](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf))

The principle reframes a practical question: what arrangement of the work would make it intrinsically interesting to the person doing it? Whether rewards always harm is less settled than the principle sounds.

Confidence is a real but slippery input. A meta-analysis of 60 effect sizes from 41 papers found the relation between creative self-efficacy and creativity measures of medium size, r = .39, with a warning attached to the number: self-rated creativity correlated higher with self-efficacy, r = .53, while the relation with divergent-thinking tests was weak, r = .23 ([Haase, Hoff, Hanel and Innes-Ker 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1411436)). Belief in one's creative ability travels most strongly with measures that ask the person how creative they are. [Creative self-efficacy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20self-efficacy) and [Creative identity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20identity) hold the construct and its limits.

The absorbed state of making is the last personal input. Csikszentmihalyi's account of the interviews behind the flow concept ends in a plain claim:

> "The best moments usually occur when a person's body or mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort to accomplish something difficult and worthwhile. Optimal experience is thus something that we make happen." ([Csikszentmihalyi, Flow, owner's library copy](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=11wG6Y7PwYzlNIrcXJ8ErSZ7Pd8fQcEsb))

[Flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow) holds the conditions: clear goals, immediate feedback, and a balance between challenge and skill. The conditions can be designed for, in activities and in institutions, which puts absorption inside the reach of practice rather than outside it.

## What the philosophers add

Where does the push come from? Dewey places it in impulse. "Impulses are the pivots upon which the re-organization of activities turn, they are agencies of deviation, for giving new directions to old habits and changing their quality" ([Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, part 2](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt)). On his account impulse brings the possibility, not the assurance, of reorganized habit, which is why [P086 — Channel the creative impulse](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P086%20%E2%80%94%20Channel%20the%20creative%20impulse) pairs the passage with Dewey's warning that "Suppression is not annihilation" ([Dewey, part 2](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt)): blocked energy goes underground rather than disappearing, so the practical question is where it is being channeled. His complaint about industrial work is the same argument at the scale of a job: the mechanizing effect is counteracted where a person sees the whole process of which their special work is a necessary part and cares for the whole ([Dewey, part 2](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt)).

Bergson extends the question of novelty to life and time themselves: "The more we study the nature of time, the more we shall comprehend that duration means invention, the creation of forms, the continual elaboration of the absolutely new" ([Bergson, Creative Evolution, ch. 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26163/26163-8.txt)). For a conscious being on his account, "to exist is to change, to change is to mature, to mature is to go on creating oneself endlessly" ([Bergson, ch. 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26163/26163-8.txt)). [Creative evolution](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20evolution), [Creative selection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20selection) and [Creative advance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20advance) carry the metaphysical cousins of this thought.

The same tradition separates creative emotion from the ordinary kind: "in creative emotion, we first have the emotion which then creates representations", as with the musician whose joy creates a symphony and only afterwards its written score ([Lawlor and Moulard-Leonard, SEP, "Henri Bergson", section 6](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bergson/)); [P195 — Creative emotion (émotion créatrice) is generative of thought, not a byproduct of it](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P195%20%E2%80%94%20Creative%20emotion%20%28%C3%A9motion%20cr%C3%A9atrice%29%20is%20generative%20of%20thought%2C%20not%20a%20byproduct%20of%20it) states the principle for practice.

The destructive and the repetitive sides of making each have an analyst. Schumpeter's "process of Creative Destruction is the essential fact about capitalism" is the industrial mutation that "incessantly revolutionizes the economic structure from within, incessantly destroying the old one, incessantly creating a new one" ([Schumpeter, Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy, ch. VII](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf)); [Creative destruction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20destruction) takes it as an analogy for the dismantling side of growth, the system's renewal rather than the person's. The companion question, whether doing something again can itself be creative, is [Creative repetition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20repetition)'s own ground.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Do divergent-thinking tests measure a general creative capacity?

The dispute over the tests is recorded by the review itself: Baer concluded that creativity is best conceptualized as domain specific, which he argued explains why divergent-thinking tests have not met with more success, and Mumford and colleagues questioned their validity; other researchers defended divergent-thinking measures, such as those in the Wallach-Kogan Creativity Tests ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 573](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The same review opens its assessment section with the field's own admission: "Debates surrounding definition and measurement continue to loom large" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 572](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)).

The self-efficacy meta-analysis shows why the stakes are practical: the correlation between believing oneself creative and being rated creative changes with the measure used, .53 against self-assessments and .23 against divergent-thinking tests ([Haase et al. 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1411436)). The strength of the association depends on how creativity is measured. What remains unresolved is whether one general capacity underlies the instruments or whether creativity is a family of domain skills wearing one word.

### Do rewards help or hurt creative work?

In their review, Hennessey and Amabile state the intrinsic motivation principle: extrinsic motivation, of which expected reward is one form alongside expected evaluation, surveillance, competition and restricted choice, is generally detrimental to creativity ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 581](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The behaviorist position, in their description, is reward-specific: creativity can be easily increased by reward and is seldom undermined ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 582](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The review records what happened when the two met: a debate surfaced in the mid-1990s, prompting researchers and theorists on both sides to publish a series of heated commentaries, critiques and replies, and "Despite results such as these, inconsistent with the assertion that expected rewards generally foster creativity, the debate has continued through much of the past decade" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, pp. 581-582](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)).

At the core of the debate, the review says, were important differences in the definitions of creativity driving the investigations, the algorithmic or heuristic nature of the experimental tasks employed, and the instructions given to study participants ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 582](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). Studies in the behaviorist tradition typically used measures that equate creativity with novelty, and often instructed participants to be creative; other findings suggest those studies demonstrate positive effects of instructions rather than positive effects of expected rewards ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 582](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)).

The review's own synthesis narrows the question to conditions: rewards undermine intrinsic motivation and creativity when they lead people to feel controlled by the situation, and can actually enhance them when they confirm competence, provide useful information in a supportive way, or enable people to do something they were already intrinsically motivated to do ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The rift had not closed when the review was written.

### Does mind wandering during the break do the work?

The incubation effect itself is meta-analytically positive, and low-demand fillers beat demanding ones ([Sio and Ormerod 2009](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ827110)). The dispute is about the mechanism, and it has a direct experimental record. The best-known demonstration, Baird and colleagues 2012, reported that compared with a demanding task, rest or no break, engaging in an undemanding task during an incubation period led to substantial improvements in performance on previously encountered problems, and that "These data suggest that engaging in simple external tasks that allow the mind to wander may facilitate creative problem solving" ([Baird et al. 2012, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22941876/)).

A 2024 replication attempt did not confirm it: "across two studies (N = 443), we found no evidence for the claim that mind wandering during a creative-incubation interval facilitates a form of creativity associated with divergent thinking" ([Murray et al. 2024, FLoRA Replication Atlas record](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1177/0956797612446024/)). One replication is not a verdict, and a null result does not show that breaks never help. What remains unresolved is what the undemanding break does inside the head, and the honest position for a practitioner is to take the robust part, the break itself, without buying a settled theory about why it works.

## Practice: finding the maker's footing

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the arrangement around the making: waiting to feel like a creative person before starting, judging each idea as it arrives and so cutting off the later, odder ones, treating the first workable answer as the answer, or protecting the work from every audience so that nothing is ever tested. The adaptive direction is making that is small, regular, partly shared, and allowed to be wrong.

::Selection:: The kernels selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K323 — Daily making](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K323%20%E2%80%94%20Daily%20making): make something every day, however small, with the criterion held lightly but firmly, that something exists at the end which did not exist before. The study behind it beeped 41 artists ten times a day for a week; recent art-making tracked better momentary hedonic tone, vivid imagery and flow, and the week's frequency of art-making tracked person-level eudemonic happiness ([Holt 2018, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29130867/)). The daily rule itself is the kernel's bet.

2. [K325 — Divergent-option generation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K325%20%E2%80%94%20Divergent-option%20generation): state the problem in one sentence, generate at least five solutions in writing, no judgement during generation, then evaluate in a separate pass. The floor of five is the kernel's design choice, built on the serial order effect's pattern that the odd ideas tend to come after the obvious ones are spent.

3. [K324 — Constraint-based creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K324%20%E2%80%94%20Constraint-based%20creation): choose an avoided task and impose one concrete constraint, 100 words, one colour, three ingredients, one hour. When the pull toward the familiar shows up, treat the constraint as the wall that turns you.

4. [K327 — Creative risk-taking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K327%20%E2%80%94%20Creative%20risk-taking): one risk a week, unfinished work shared, an unmastered form attempted, an idea voiced that might be wrong, sized to survive, with an audience chosen because they already expect you to make things.

5. [K331 — Play practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K331%20%E2%80%94%20Play%20practice): once a week, do something with no purpose beyond the doing, nothing that will be evaluated, scored or shown, ending without an inventory of what the play produced.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. The daily making that survives a bad month, the constraint that turns out to be a favourite tool, the audience that earns the risk: what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters, and the record that teaches is the log of what each attempt changed.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

### The new begins early

[The Two-Year-Old and the Blocks](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST325%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two-Year-Old%20and%20the%20Blocks): in an illustrative vignette, a child's tower falls twice; she widens the base, and the next tower stands.

### Making without display

[Paterson Writes Poems on His Lunch Break](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST015%20%E2%80%94%20Paterson%20Writes%20Poems%20on%20His%20Lunch%20Break): a bus driver writes poetry in a notebook he carries with him, unpublished though his wife urges him to share it, and when a Japanese stranger who seems to know he is a poet talks with him, he denies being one ([Paterson (film), Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paterson_(film))).

[Bob Ross's Happy Accidents](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST326%20%E2%80%94%20Bob%20Ross%27s%20Happy%20Accidents): a former US Air Force master sergeant paints a scene in real time in a half-hour television segment, telling viewers there are no mistakes, only happy accidents ([Bob Ross, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ross)).

### Variation, selection, retention

[Edison Burns the Filaments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST112%20%E2%80%94%20Edison%20Burns%20the%20Filaments): the lamp-filament search ran through carbon, platinum and other metals before returning to carbon, and the team later found a carbonized Japanese bamboo filament that could last over a thousand hours ([Incandescent light bulb, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incandescent_light_bulb)).

[The Four C's](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST324%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Four%20C%27s): Kaufman and Beghetto's 2009 paper proposes four levels of creativity, mini-c through Big-C, and argues that the everyday forms are real, distinct and developmentally connected.

### Absorption

[The Rock Climbers' Absorption](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST307%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Rock%20Climbers%27%20Absorption): the flow concept grew out of interviews in which Csikszentmihalyi and his team asked rock climbers, chess players, surgeons and dancers to describe their most absorbing experiences.

### The wider backdrop

[The Three Metamorphoses](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST293%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Three%20Metamorphoses): Zarathustra's camel, laden, hastens into the wilderness; the spirit becomes a lion that says "I will" and wrestles freedom from the dragon "Thou shalt", though the lion cannot yet create new values; the child, "innocence and forgetfulness, a new beginning, a game, a self-rolling wheel", is the creator ([Thus Spake Zarathustra, Zimmern translation, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-0.txt)).

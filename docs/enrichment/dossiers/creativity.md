# Creativity

> Where does the new come from, and can a person get better at producing it?

A product or an idea is called creative when it is both novel and appropriate: new, and fitting the task it answers. The researchers who reviewed the field for the Annual Review of Psychology put the working definition in one line:

> "Creativity: the generation of products or ideas that are both novel and appropriate" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 570](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf))

Both criteria do work. Novelty alone is easy: a random string of syllables has never been said before. Appropriateness alone is easier still: the familiar answer fits. The creative product has to hold the two together, which is why [Creativity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creativity) is a capacity with parts, not a single gift some people receive at birth.

Psychology arrived at this question late. Guilford's 1950 presidential address to the American Psychological Association opened it this way:

> "The neglect of this subject by psychologists is appalling." ([Guilford 1950](https://creativityinmusiced.weebly.com/uploads/2/3/8/5/23859476/guilford-1950-creativity.pdf))

His evidence: of approximately 121,000 titles listed in the previous 23 years of Psychological Abstracts, only 186 were indexed as bearing on creativity ([Guilford 1950](https://creativityinmusiced.weebly.com/uploads/2/3/8/5/23859476/guilford-1950-creativity.pdf)). The field he called for now exists, and its findings are what this page follows.

### Nearby terms

- Divergent thinking: the generation mode, producing many ideas quickly. It is one part of the process, not the whole capacity, and its tests are contested below.
- Imagination: the wider faculty of representing what is not present, [defined by the SEP entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/imagination/) as representing possibilities other than the actual, times other than the present, and perspectives other than one's own. Creativity uses it without being reducible to it.
- Insight: the sudden-reinterpretation mode of solving, one route by which the new arrives.
- Creation: what making means for a life, the meaning-in-life theme. The capacity and its psychology live here; what a life of making adds up to, including Frankl's creative values, belongs to [Creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20values).
- Flow: the absorption creative work can induce; a frequent companion of making, not the same thing as it.

## What gets to count as creative

The question of scope has a working answer: the four-C model. [Kaufman and Beghetto's levels](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Four-C%20model%20of%20creativity) run from mini-c, "the novel and personally meaningful interpretation of experiences, actions, and events", through little-c, the everyday creativity of a gardener or a problem-solver at work, up to Pro-c, the developed professional, and Big-C, the discipline-changing work ([Cambridge International chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)). Big-C is "reserved to describe the work of an elite few who have transformed their discipline", and the chapter is blunt that it is out of reach of most of us ([Cambridge International chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)).

The lower levels are not consolation prizes. Vygotsky, quoted in the same chapter, drew the line generously: "Any human act that gives rise to something new is referred to as a creative act, regardless of whether what is constructed is a physical object or some mental or emotional construct that lives within the person who created it and is known only to him" ([Vygotsky 1967, quoted in the Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)). On that reading, the child's tower and the unshared insight count, which is why [PP051 — Treat creativity as a process, not a trait](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PP051%20%E2%80%94%20Treat%20creativity%20as%20a%20process%2C%20not%20a%20trait) matters practically: the question stops being whether I am a creative person and becomes what the next task needs.

The everyday level also carries evidence of its own. In a 13-day diary study of 658 young adults:

> "Lagged multilevel models revealed that people felt higher activated PA and flourishing following days when they reported more creative activity than usual. The other direction – PA predicting next-day creative activity – was not supported, suggesting that the cross-day effect was specific to creative activity predicting well-being." ([Conner, DeYoung and Silvia 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2016.1257049))

The direction runs from making to feeling, not the reverse, and the design is observational: [BE059 — Engage in creative behaviour — making is well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE059%20%E2%80%94%20Engage%20in%20creative%20behaviour%20%E2%80%94%20making%20is%20well-being) states the study's own limits. The finding concerns ordinary making, cooking, writing, crafting, not masterpieces, which is exactly the level the four-C model calls real.

## Where the new idea comes from

The process has two named modes. Divergent thinking is "spontaneous, free-flowing thinking with the goal of generating many different ideas in a short period"; convergent thinking is "more disciplined thinking, focused on narrowing possibilities to a workable solution" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, pp. 573, 579](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). [CO074 — Cultivate creative thinking — divergent and convergent modes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=CO074%20%E2%80%94%20Cultivate%20creative%20thinking%20%E2%80%94%20divergent%20and%20convergent%20modes) draws the practical consequence: generation and evaluation are different jobs, and doing them as separate passes is a skill, not a personality trait.

Inside the generative mode there is a signature pattern, the serial order effect:

> "During the course of divergent thinking (DT), the number of generated ideas decreases while the originality of ideas increases." ([Wang, Hao, Ku, Grabner and Fink 2017, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259772/))

The obvious ideas arrive first, and the odd ones tend to come after the obvious ones are spent. The study ties the pattern to shifting and inhibition: suppressing the interference of the obvious response and switching category is what surfaces the later, more original ideas ([Wang et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259772/)). [K325 — Divergent-option generation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K325%20%E2%80%94%20Divergent-option%20generation) builds its floor of five options on exactly this: novelty lives later in the list, so judging early cuts off the ideas the practice is for.

The new also arrives by a different route, suddenly. Insight is "when a person suddenly reinterprets a stimulus, situation, or event to produce a nonobvious, nondominant interpretation", taking the form of a solution, the comprehension of a joke or metaphor, or the recognition of an ambiguous percept ([Kounios and Beeman 2014, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24405359/)). The same review reports that people who tend to solve insightfully, rather than in a deliberate analytic fashion, show different patterns of resting-state brain activity ([Kounios and Beeman 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24405359/)), which makes the two styles look like a real difference between people, not a myth about eureka. [Insight problem-solving](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Insight%20problem-solving) holds the fuller picture, including the field's own admission that the definition is not settled.

Time away from the problem has its own evidence. A meta-analysis of 117 studies:

> "The authors identified a positive incubation effect, with divergent thinking tasks benefiting more than linguistic and visual insight tasks from incubation. Longer preparation periods gave a greater incubation effect, whereas filling an incubation period with high cognitive demand tasks gave a smaller incubation effect." ([Sio and Ormerod 2009, abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ827110))

Longer effort before the break helped, and a demanding filler shrank the benefit. What the break does inside the head is less settled: [AT036 — Learn to use deliberate mind-wandering — incubation and creative insight](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT036%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20deliberate%20mind-wandering%20%E2%80%94%20incubation%20and%20creative%20insight) records that the best-known laboratory demonstration of the mind-wandering mechanism has a failed replication on record.

Constraint is the counterintuitive fourth input. [K324 — Constraint-based creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K324%20%E2%80%94%20Constraint-based%20creation) collects the experimental finding that two-line rhymes written under constraint were rated more creative, with a carryover to later unconstrained work; the Dr. Seuss wager behind Green Eggs and Ham, a compelling story in fifty words or less, is the illustration the article itself offers. A blank page offers infinite possibility, and the first move is usually the familiar one; a constraint closes the familiar path and turns the search elsewhere. The neuroscience of novelty fits the picture from another side: [Novelty research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Novelty%20research) reports basal forebrain neurons that flag the new as salience rather than reward, so the pull toward the new is a signal, not a payment.

## What the person brings

Is the capacity fixed? The review's summary answer:

> "Although creativity in persons has some trait-like (stable) aspects, it is also a state subject to influence by the social environment." ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 590](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf))

Some of it is stable, and the rest moves with motivation, environment and practised skill. Training is part of the moving part: Scott and colleagues' meta-analysis of seventy prior studies found that carefully constructed creativity training programs typically result in gains in performance, with benefits generalizing across criteria, setting, and target population, and that the more successful programs employed realistic exercises building cognitive skills and heuristics for applying them ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 577](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)).

Motivation is another. The review states its principle directly:

> "This is the intrinsic motivation principle of creativity: Intrinsic motivation, defined as the drive to do something for the sheer enjoyment, interest, and personal challenge of the task itself (rather than for some external goal), is conducive to creativity, whereas extrinsic motivation is generally detrimental." ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 581](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf))

The same review adds that people are most creative when motivated primarily by the interest, enjoyment, satisfaction and challenge of the work itself ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 590](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The principle has limits and a history of debate, but as a working rule it reframes the practical question: what arrangement of the work would make it intrinsically interesting to the person doing it?

Confidence is a real but slippery input. A meta-analysis of 60 effect sizes from 41 papers found the relation between creative self-efficacy and creativity measures of medium size, r = .39, with a warning attached to the number: studies using self-assessment measures correlated significantly higher with self-efficacy, r = .53, than studies using performance tasks and expert ratings, r = .25 ([Haase, Hoff, Hanel and Innes-Ker 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1411436)). Belief in one's creative ability travels most strongly with measures that ask the person how creative they are. [Creative self-efficacy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20self-efficacy) and [Creative identity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20identity) hold the construct and its limits; a later study found higher creative self-efficacy predicted a larger positive bias in self-assessed creative performance together with lower creative achievements, so confidence and skill remain separate things to track.

The absorbed state of making is the last personal input. Csikszentmihalyi's account of the interviews behind the flow concept ends in a plain claim:

> "The best moments usually occur when a person's body or mind is stretched to its limits in a voluntary effort to accomplish something difficult and worthwhile. Optimal experience is thus something that we make happen." ([Csikszentmihalyi, Flow, owner's library copy](https://drive.google.com/uc?export=download&id=11wG6Y7PwYzlNIrcXJ8ErSZ7Pd8fQcEsb))

[Flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow) holds the conditions: clear goals, immediate feedback, and a balance between challenge and skill. The conditions can be designed for, in activities and in institutions, which puts absorption inside the reach of practice rather than outside it.

## What the philosophers add

Dewey places the source of the new in impulse. "Impulses are the pivots upon which the re-organization of activities turn, they are agencies of deviation, for giving new directions to old habits and changing their quality" ([Dewey, Human Nature and Conduct, part 2](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt)). Impulse brings the possibility, not the assurance, of reorganization; [P086 — Channel the creative impulse](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P086%20%E2%80%94%20Channel%20the%20creative%20impulse) adds Dewey's warning that "Suppression is not annihilation" ([Dewey, part 2](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/41386/pg41386.txt)): blocked energy goes underground rather than disappearing, so the practical question is where it is being channeled.

Bergson makes the new ontological rather than personal: "for a conscious being, to exist is to change, to change is to mature, to mature is to go on creating oneself endlessly" ([Bergson, Creative Evolution, ch. 1](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26163/26163-8.txt)). On his account duration means invention, the continual elaboration of the absolutely new, and intelligence, shaped for practical tool-making, tends to miss it ([Creative evolution](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20evolution)). [Creative selection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20selection) and [Creative advance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20advance) carry the process-philosophy cousins of the same thought; [P195 — Creative emotion (émotion créatrice) is generative of thought, not a byproduct of it](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P195%20%E2%80%94%20Creative%20emotion%20%28%C3%A9motion%20cr%C3%A9atrice%29%20is%20generative%20of%20thought%2C%20not%20a%20byproduct%20of%20it) is generative of thought, not a byproduct of it) records the proposal that emotion can generate representations rather than only follow them, with its own caution that the commentators' wording is not a verified quotation from Bergson's book.

The destructive side has its analyst too. Schumpeter's "process of Creative Destruction is the essential fact about capitalism": the industrial mutation that "incessantly revolutionizes the economic structure from within, incessantly destroying the old one, incessantly creating a new one" ([Schumpeter, Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy, ch. VII](https://thaleszp.com/files/Schumpeter_creative_dest.pdf)). [Creative destruction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20destruction) takes the term as an analogy for the dismantling side of growth, and [Creative repetition](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20repetition) notes that Kierkegaard's religious movement of taking-again is a different thing from a method for creative work.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Does an idea no one else can see count as creative?

The measurement tradition says creativity is what qualified observers agree to call creative. The Consensual Assessment Technique has researchers and experts rate products, and it enjoys wide use because of the consistently high interrater agreements it reaches ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 572](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The four-C tradition carves out space below that bar: mini-c "may not be visible to outsiders and may consist purely of ideas and connections that the learner creates" ([Cambridge International chapter](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/Images/426483-chapter-4-innovation-and-creativity.pdf)), and Vygotsky's creative act includes the construct "known only to him who created it".

The two positions answer different questions. The consensual view answers: how can research score products at scale without a definition? The mini-c view answers: where does the developmental path to recognized work begin? They conflict only when the second is asked to certify a product the first would not rate, or when a learner's private reinterpretation is treated as evidence about the person's standing. What remains unresolved is whether personally meaningful novelty is an early form of the same thing the experts rate, or a different thing entirely, and the sources here do not settle it.

### Can creativity be measured at all?

Guilford's 1950 call built the field on testing, and the tests built first were divergent-thinking tests. The dispute over them is recorded by the review itself: Baer concluded that creativity is best conceptualized as domain specific, which he argued explains why divergent-thinking tests have not met with more success, and Mumford and colleagues questioned their validity; other researchers defended divergent-thinking measures, such as those in the Wallach-Kogan Creativity Tests ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 573](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)). The same review opens its assessment section with the field's own admission: "Debates surrounding definition and measurement continue to loom large" ([Hennessey and Amabile 2010, p. 572](https://site.caes.uga.edu/sandlin/files/2023/10/Hennessey-Amabile-2010.pdf)).

The self-efficacy meta-analysis shows why the stakes are practical: the correlation between believing oneself creative and being rated creative changes with the measure used, .53 against self-assessments and .25 against performance tasks and expert ratings ([Haase et al. 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2018.1411436)). A person can look creative on one instrument and not on another. [Divergent thinking tests](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Divergent%20thinking%20tests) holds the instruments; what remains unresolved is whether one general capacity underlies them or whether creativity is a family of domain skills wearing one word.

### Is the new idea earned at the desk or on the walk?

The serial order effect says the original ideas come late in the generation, after the obvious ones are spent, which rewards persistence at the page ([Wang et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28259772/)). The incubation literature says the benefit grows with longer preparation before the break and shrinks if the break is filled with demanding tasks ([Sio and Ormerod 2009](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ827110)), which rewards stepping away. The two findings are compatible in principle: effort first, then the undemanding break.

The disagreement is about the mechanism, and it is live. The best-known demonstration that mind-wandering during the break is what does the work has a failed replication on record, a 2024 two-study attempt with 443 participants that found no evidence mind wandering during a creative-incubation interval facilitates divergent-thinking creativity ([AT036](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT036%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20deliberate%20mind-wandering%20%E2%80%94%20incubation%20and%20creative%20insight)). The meta-analytic case for breaks is stronger than the case for the mind-wandering mechanism itself. What remains unresolved is what the break does inside the head, and the honest position for a practitioner is to take the robust part: when stuck, set the problem aside and fill the break with something undemanding rather than demanding, without buying a theory about why it works.

## Practice: finding the maker's footing

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the arrangement around the making: waiting to feel like a creative person before starting, judging each idea as it arrives and so cutting off the later, odder ones, treating the first workable answer as the answer, or protecting the work from every audience so that nothing is ever tested. The adaptive direction is making that is small, regular, partly shared, and allowed to be wrong.

::Selection:: The kernels selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K323 — Daily making](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K323%20%E2%80%94%20Daily%20making): make something every day, however small, with the criterion held lightly but firmly, that something exists at the end which did not exist before. The experience-sampling study behind it found recent art-making tracked better momentary tone and flow; the daily rule itself is the kernel's bet.

2. [K325 — Divergent-option generation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K325%20%E2%80%94%20Divergent-option%20generation): state the problem in one sentence, generate at least five solutions in writing, no judgement during generation, then evaluate in a separate pass. The floor of five exists because the serial order effect puts novelty later in the list.

3. [K324 — Constraint-based creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K324%20%E2%80%94%20Constraint-based%20creation): choose an avoided task and impose one concrete constraint, 100 words, one colour, three ingredients, one hour. When the pull toward the familiar shows up, treat the constraint as the wall that turns you.

4. [K327 — Creative risk-taking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K327%20%E2%80%94%20Creative%20risk-taking): one risk a week, unfinished work shared, an unmastered form attempted, an idea voiced that might be wrong, sized to survive, with an audience chosen because they already expect you to make things.

5. [K331 — Play practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K331%20%E2%80%94%20Play%20practice): once a week, do something with no purpose beyond the doing, nothing that will be evaluated, scored or shown, ending without an inventory of what the play produced.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. The daily making that survives a bad month, the constraint that turns out to be a favourite tool, the audience that earns the risk: what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters, and the record that teaches is the log of what each attempt changed.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

### The new begins early

[The Two-Year-Old and the Blocks](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST325%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two-Year-Old%20and%20the%20Blocks): a tower falls twice, the base gets widened, and the tower stands; the attempt-observe-adjust cycle is present before language, which is the developmental ground of mini-c.

### Making without display

[Paterson Writes Poems on His Lunch Break](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST015%20%E2%80%94%20Paterson%20Writes%20Poems%20on%20His%20Lunch%20Break): a bus driver writes a few lines a day in a secret notebook, publishes nothing, and the film's wager is that this is a complete creative life, not a failed one.

[Bob Ross's Happy Accidents](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST326%20%E2%80%94%20Bob%20Ross%27s%20Happy%20Accidents): a former master sergeant paints a full landscape in thirty minutes on television, telling viewers there are no mistakes, only happy little accidents, and millions watched a man be unafraid of a canvas.

### Variation, selection, retention

[Edison Burns the Filaments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST112%20%E2%80%94%20Edison%20Burns%20the%20Filaments): the filament search tests thousands of materials and records the failures as data, a systematic elimination of hypotheses in which the insight is the refusal to stop generating variants.

[The Four C's](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST324%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Four%20C%27s): Kaufman and Beghetto's model names mini-c, little-c, Pro-c and Big-C, and its point is that the everyday forms are real, distinct and developmentally connected, not failed eminence.

### Absorption

[The Rock Climbers' Absorption](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST307%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Rock%20Climbers%27%20Absorption): Csikszentmihalyi's interviews with climbers, chess players, surgeons and dancers produce one phenomenology, complete absorption with clear goals, immediate feedback and a challenge-skill balance.

### The wider backdrop

[The Creative Advance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST121%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Creative%20Advance): Whitehead names the universe's fundamental activity the creative advance into novelty, and the ethical upshot is stated plainly, that a person or conversation is healthy exactly insofar as it is producing genuine novelty.

[The Three Metamorphoses](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST293%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Three%20Metamorphoses): the camel carries inherited duties, the lion clears the ground, and only the child creates, because the child is a new beginning; the sequence cannot be skipped in either direction.

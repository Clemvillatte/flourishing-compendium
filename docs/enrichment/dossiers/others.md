# Others

> What do other people do to a person's mind and flourishing, and what does one person owe another?

The theme asks a double question: what other people do to a person's mind and flourishing, and what one person owes another. Other people are the environment the human brain is built to expect, the material the self is made from, and the ones a good life is asked to serve. The traditions organize the territory differently. Some start from the bond (friendship, care, the face of the other), others from the command (love your enemy, bear one another's burdens), and the research starts from the mechanism (empathy, co-regulation, social support). The research association is plain in the findings gathered below: the quantity and quality of a person's relationships travel with their health, their mood and their survival.

## Nearby terms

[Friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Friendship) and [Compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion) are treated inside this dossier, and their cards carry the tradition-specific detail. ## Where others sit in a life

### What other people do to a person

The claim that the human brain treats the company of trusted others as its default condition, so managing alone costs metabolic and neural resources, is the [social baseline](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20baseline). Butler's temporal framework, in which partners in a close relationship regulate each other's affect across repeated interactions, each partner's state managed between them, is [co-regulation in relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Co-regulation%20in%20relationships), and the precise version of the claim that relationship maintenance is a shared regulatory task is [co-regulation of affect in relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Co-regulation%20of%20affect%20in%20relationships).

My name for Fredrickson's positivity resonance, the brief shared positive states that constitute love as a day-to-day biological event, is [micro-moments of connection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Micro-moments%20of%20connection). My label for the findings in which people's ties to others travel with how well their lives go (Diener and Seligman, 2002) is [connectedness in well-being research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Connectedness%20in%20well-being%20research).

The classic review distinguishing two proposed relationships between support and health is [social support theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20support%20theory%20Cohen%20Wills), and the competing account that support blunts the stress response itself, so its health benefit shows up mainly under stress, is [the stress-buffering model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20stress-buffering%20model).

Baumeister and Leary's claim that belonging is a need built in prior to choice is [social embeddedness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20embeddedness). The subjective, distressing gap between desired and actual social relationships, distinct from objective solitude, is [loneliness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Loneliness), and the account of loneliness as an aversive biological signal that evolved to protect the social body, the way pain protects the physical one, is [the evolutionary account of loneliness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20evolutionary%20account%20of%20loneliness).

The spread of affect and behaviour from person to person through observation and induction is [social contagion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20contagion). Attention guided by where other people are attending, most clearly through gaze, is [social attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20attention). The finding that how people rate their lives depends heavily on the surrounding social conditions, not only on traits or income, is [social determinants of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20determinants%20of%20well-being).

Beckes and Coan's statement of the social baseline is direct:

> "the dominant ecology to which humans are adapted is other humans... and introduces the first sense in which our theory refers to the social baseline—that close proximity to social resources is the baseline assumption of the human brain." ([Beckes and Coan 2011, PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/55917f64e4b0cd3b4705b68c/t/5cd2617f9353680001816713/1557291392068/beckes.coan.2011.pdf))

Cacioppo and Hawkley give the negative side of the same arrangement:

> "Physical pain is an aversive signal that evolved to motivate one to take action to minimize damage to one's body. Loneliness, a social pain, is an aversive signal that evolved to motivate one to take action that minimizes threats or damage to one's social body." ([Cacioppo and Hawkley 2009, PMC2752489](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2752489/))

### How the self is built from others

Mead's answer to what a self is made of, something with a development arising in social conduct, is [the social self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20social%20self). William James's term for the version of a person that exists in other people's minds is [the social selves](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20social%20selves). Mead's mechanism, responding to one's own gesture as another person would, is [taking the role of the other](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Taking%20the%20role%20of%20the%20other).

The two ways cultures teach people to be a self are [independent vs interdependent self-construal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Independent%20vs%20interdependent%20self-construal%20%28Markus%20%26%20Kitayama%29), and the view of the self as fundamentally connected to other people is [interdependent self-construal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Interdependent%20self-construal). Cross and colleagues' name for thinking about yourself in terms of your close relationships is [relational-interdependent self-construal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Relational-interdependent%20self-construal).

Tajfel and Turner's account of how the part of self-image that comes from group membership drives intergroup behaviour is [social identity theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20identity%20theory). Social identity lived through the body, anchored in Fanon's account of Blackness under the white gaze, is [embodied social identity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Embodied%20social%20identity). The lifespan stages whose central conflicts are relational, from trust versus mistrust to intimacy versus isolation and generativity, are [Erikson's psychosocial theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Erikson%27s%20psychosocial%20theory).

### How empathy works, and where it fails

The capacity to feel with and understand another, the umbrella the rest of these entries divide, is [empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathy). Batson's other-oriented warmth, feeling for a person in need with that person's welfare as the aim, is [empathic concern](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathic%20concern). Affective resonance with another's pain that turns self-oriented and leads to withdrawal is [empathic distress](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathic%20distress).

Ickes's construct for how well one person infers what another is actually thinking and feeling is [empathic accuracy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathic%20accuracy). Rogers's condition, understanding another's experience from inside their frame of reference, is [empathic understanding](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathic%20understanding), and the therapist's perception and communication of that understanding, with the research on whether it helps, is [therapeutic empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Therapeutic%20empathy).

The extension of the macaque premotor finding to human feeling-with is the [mirror neuron theory of empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mirror%20neuron%20theory%20of%20empathy). The sharing of feeling and perspective that comes through stories, in Mar and Oatley's account, is [narrative empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Narrative%20empathy).

The habit of reading another person's mind from one's own is [egocentric projection in social inference](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Egocentric%20projection%20in%20social%20inference). Buber's term for what the other person does in a dialogic relation is [confirming the other](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Confirming%20the%20other).

The training research separates two states a person can bring to another's pain. In Klimecki and colleagues' study, one group was trained first in empathic resonance and then in compassion; the resonance training raised negative affect and the brain activations associated with empathy for pain, and the compassion training reversed it:

> "We conclude that training compassion may reflect a new coping strategy to overcome empathic distress and strengthen resilience." ([Klimecki et al. 2014, PubMed 23576808](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23576808/))

### Compassion, kindness and care

The wish to relieve suffering when it is seen, trainable as a capacity, is [compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion). The second brahmavihara, in which metta wishes all beings well and karuna responds to their pain, is [karuna](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karuna). The first immeasurable, goodwill for all beings without limit, is [metta (loving-kindness)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metta%20%28loving-kindness%29).

The Karaṇīya Metta Sutta gives the instruction its measure:

> "As a mother would risk her life to protect her child, her only child, even so should one cultivate a limitless heart with regard to all beings." ([Karaṇīya Metta Sutta, Sn 1.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.1.08.than.html))

The third immeasurable, gladness at another's good fortune as if it were one's own, is [mudita (sympathetic joy)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mudita%20%28sympathetic%20joy%29). Underwood's construct of love centred on the good of another person is [compassionate love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassionate%20love), and Gilbert's integrated approach for people with high shame and self-criticism is [compassion-focused therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion-focused%20therapy).

Noddings's analysis of the practice of responding to need, in which attention, feeling and action bend toward the well-being of a particular other, is [care](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Care). Tronto's account of caring as a species activity with phases, from caring about to care receiving, is [care as practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Care%20as%20practice). The normative theory that moral action centers on relationships and benevolence is [care ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Care%20ethics).

Held's version, in which moral significance is located in relationships and dependencies, with universal rules considered in the abstract as the comparison class, is [the ethics of care](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20ethics%20of%20care). Gilligan's claim that moral reasoning has more than one voice is [Gilligan's ethics-of-care theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gilligan%27s%20ethics-of-care%20theory).

Confucius's cardinal virtue, the humane disposition toward others, is [ren](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ren), and Hume's sentiment grounding the social virtues is [benevolence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Benevolence).

The deliberate kind acts used in positive psychology interventions are [acts of kindness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acts%20of%20kindness%20%28Lyubomirsky%29), and Post's label for the pleasurable state many people report after helping someone is the [helper's high](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Helper%27s%20high).

### Love and friendship

The terrain Aristotle opens by calling friendship a thing most necessary for life is [love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Love). The Pauline love of 1 Corinthians 13, patient and keeping no account of evil, is [agape](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Agape).

Sternberg's account of intimacy, passion and commitment, and the loves their combinations name, is the [Triangular Theory of Love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Triangular%20Theory%20of%20Love), and the steady affection between people whose lives are interdependent is [companionate love in adversity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Companionate%20love%20in%20adversity).

Aristotle's account of the bond without which no one would choose to live is [friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Friendship), treated in Nicomachean Ethics VIII and IX as [Aristotelian friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aristotelian%20friendship). The kinds counted by what people find lovable, the useful, the pleasant and the good, are the [three kinds of friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Three%20kinds%20of%20friendship).

Aristotle's claim that the company we keep shapes the people we become is that [friendship shapes character](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Friendship%20shapes%20character).

Aristotle closes the account of friendship with its limit:

> "it would seem actually impossible to be a great friend to many people... but one cannot have with many people the friendship based on virtue and on the character of our friends themselves, and we must be content if we find even a few such." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IX.10, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.9.ix.html))

Alcibiades' complaint in the Symposium about the injuries Socrates inflicts is [the wounds of love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20wounds%20of%20love). The saying from 1 John 4.18 is [perfect love casts out fear](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Perfect%20love%20casts%20out%20fear).

Diotima's staged ascent in the Symposium, from one beautiful body to the beautiful itself, is [the ladder of love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20ladder%20of%20love). Spinoza's free person, who lives by reason and refuses the rule of fear, is [the free person loves all](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20free%20person%20loves%20all).

The Stoic doctrine of cosmic interdependence that Marcus Aurelius instructs himself to keep in mind is [sympatheia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sympatheia), and the Stoic teaching that a person's good is part of one woven system is [the interconnected good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20interconnected%20good).

### What one person owes another

Sartre's ontological principle for the experience of being seen is [being-for-others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Being-for-others). Mencius' advice to examine oneself when one's actions do not bring the desired response from others is [the mirror for others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20mirror%20for%20others), and Confucius' instruction in Analects 13.6 is to [rectify the self before others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Rectify%20the%20self%20before%20others).

Confucius' correction of the very proposal to repay injury with kindness, in Analects 14.34, is the instruction to [repay injury with uprightness, kindness with kindness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Repay%20injury%20with%20uprightness%2C%20kindness%20with%20kindness). The Galatians 6.2 command is [bear one another's burdens](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Bear%20one%20another%27s%20burdens), and the instruction of 2 Timothy 2.2 is [entrust to faithful others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Entrust%20to%20faithful%20others).

The Gospel of Matthew 6 instruction is [almsgiving in secret](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Almsgiving%20in%20secret). The Buddhist tradition is [dāna (generosity) without expectation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D%C4%81na%20%28generosity%29%20without%20expectation), and Aristotle's liberality, the mean concerned with wealth, is [generosity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Generosity).

The welcome owed to the stranger who arrives, from Levinas and Derrida, is [hospitality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hospitality). Work done for another's sake, in which the server is not left behind, is [service](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Service).

The command of Matthew 5.44 is [enemy-love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enemy-love), and Śāntideva's teaching on forbearance, in which the adversary provides the occasion for patience, is [enemy as teacher](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enemy%20as%20teacher).

The Gospel of Luke 23.34 saying is [Father, forgive them](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Father%2C%20forgive%20them). Miller's account of extending care beyond proximity is [cosmopolitan care](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cosmopolitan%20care).

De Beauvoir's conclusion that willing your own freedom commits you to the freedom of other people is [the liberation of others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20liberation%20of%20others), and the same ethics argued across The Ethics of Ambiguity, that a person's freedom cannot be fulfilled alone, is [the interdependence of freedoms](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20interdependence%20of%20freedoms).

The family of sayings that tell people to treat others as they would wish to be treated is [the golden rule](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20golden%20rule), and Aristotle's instruction in Nicomachean Ethics VIII.2 is to [wish good for your friend for their own sake](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wish%20good%20for%20your%20friend%20for%20their%20own%20sake).

### Helping: altruism and its debates

Motivation aimed at another person's good for that person's sake, not as a means to one's own, is [altruism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Altruism). The research program testing whether empathy produces genuinely other-directed motivation is [altruism theory Batson empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Altruism%20theory%20Batson%20empathy), and the claim that empathic concern produces motivation with the ultimate goal of relieving the other's need is the [empathy-altruism hypothesis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathy-altruism%20hypothesis).

The family of voluntary actions taken to help and benefit others is [prosocial behaviour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20behaviour). Grant and Berg's desire to protect and promote the well-being of others is [prosocial motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20motivation), and spending money to benefit others, where how people spend may matter as much as how much they earn, is [prosocial spending](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20spending).

The finding that brutal honesty often serves the speaker's discharge, not the listener's benefit, is [prosocial honesty research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20honesty%20research). Trivers's model of helping between non-relatives as mutualism with a time lag is [reciprocal altruism research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Reciprocal%20altruism%20research).

The measurable difference between people in how much weight they give another person's outcomes is [social value orientation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20value%20orientation). The ways other people, their norms and their support change what a person wants and how strongly are [social influences on motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20influences%20on%20motivation).

### Relationships and communities as structures

How people sort their relationships into kinds and where they draw the boundaries, in Fehr's research, is [relationship categorisation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Relationship%20categorisation). Duck's phase model of how relationships end is [relationship dissolution research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Relationship%20dissolution%20research).

The family of models for how relationships begin, continue and end is [social exchange theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20exchange%20theory). Thibaut and Kelley's account, in which relationship outcomes are judged by comparison level and comparison level for alternatives, is [Interdependence Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Interdependence%20Theory), and the research line on the interdependent construal of self anchored in Markus and Kitayama is [interdependence research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Interdependence%20research).

Altman and Taylor's account of relationships deepening through self-disclosure is [social penetration theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20penetration%20theory). Bowlby's account of love bonds, in which the child-caregiver bond is organized by an inborn behavioural system whose business is protection, and the system keeps running in adult relationships, is [attachment theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attachment%20theory). The sense, built from experience with available and responsive caregivers and partners, that others will be available and supportive when needed, is [attachment security](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attachment%20security).

The view that communities largely make the identities of their members is [communitarianism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Communitarianism). MacIntyre's local form of life in which identity, roles and goods are constituted together with others is [community](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Community). McMillan and Chavis's felt experience of belonging and mattering to a group is [sense of community](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sense%20of%20community).

The study of well-being at the level of settings and systems is [community psychology theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Community%20psychology%20theory), and implementation research in community settings is [community intervention research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Community%20intervention%20research).

Horton and Wohl's observation that mass media give the illusion of face-to-face relationship with the performer is [parasocial interaction theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Parasocial%20interaction%20theory). The proposal that contact via media can reduce prejudice is the [parasocial contact hypothesis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Parasocial%20contact%20hypothesis).

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is empathy-based helping for the other or for the self?

The shared question: when empathy moves a person to help, whose welfare is the motivation's ultimate goal? Batson's research program says the other's:

> "The empathy-altruism hypothesis claims that prosocial motivation associated with feeling empathy for a person in need is directed toward the ultimate goal of benefiting that person, not toward some subtle form of self-benefit." ([Batson et al. 1988, PubMed 3418490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3418490/))

Cialdini's group answered with the opposite interpretation, tested in the paradigm Batson's associates typically used:

> "We hypothesized that an observer's heightened empathy for a sufferer brings with it increased personal sadness in the observer and that it is the egoistic desire to relieve the sadness, rather than the selfless desire to relieve the sufferer, that motivates helping... The results were interpreted as providing support for an egoistically based interpretation of helping under conditions of high empathy." ([Cialdini et al. 1987, PubMed 3572736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3572736/))

The disagreement is direct: empathy-related helping has two rival explanations, the other's welfare or relief from the helper's own sadness. My page on the program records Batson's summary judgment that the support for the altruistic reading is remarkably strong and consistent, with the limits kept on record there. What remains unresolved is the measurement crux both camps share: whether any laboratory separation of empathic concern from personal sadness can be made clean enough to show whose welfare the motivation actually serves.

### What is owed to someone who has done harm?

The shared question: when another person has injured you, what do you owe them in return? The Analects record the proposal and Confucius' rejection of it:

> Some one said, "What do you say concerning the principle that injury should be recompensed with kindness?" The Master said, "With what then will you recompense kindness? Recompense injury with justice, and recompense kindness with kindness." ([Confucius, Analects 14.34, Legge translation](https://ctext.org/analects/xian-wen))

The Gospel commands the other response, and gives its reason in the next verse:

> "Ye have heard that it hath been said, Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy. But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;" ([Matthew 5.43-44 KJV](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/5.htm))

> "That ye may be the children of your Father which is in heaven: for he maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good," ([Matthew 5.45 KJV](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/matthew/5.htm))

The two reasons differ in kind. Confucius argues from what kindness would mean if it were also the answer to injury: with nothing left to repay kindness with, the distinction that gives kindness its point is lost. Matthew argues from imitation of a giver whose benefit does not sort the evil from the good. I read the passages as answers to one question, since both say what a person owes after harm and both give a reason.

My lean, which is mine and not a finding of either text, is that the sharpest difference is about desert: Confucius keeps the return tied to what the other person did, and Matthew cuts it loose. I cannot settle it from the verses alone, because the Analects line gives only a short saying and does not say what "justice" requires in a concrete case, and because I have read both in English translation, not in the original languages.

### Is care for one's own people a limit on moral concern or a fault in it?

The shared question: if every person's good counts, may you give more to your parents and kin than to strangers? The Mohist case for equal concern is stated in W. P. Mei's translation of Universal Love II:

> "Mozi said: It is to regard the state of others as one's own, the houses of others as one's own, the persons of others as one's self." ([Mozi, Universal Love II, Mei translation](https://www.themozi.org/chapters/ch15.html))

Universal Love III gives a reason that works through filial concern itself, as this passage on those who wish to be filial shows:

> "Should I first love others' parents in order that they would love my parents in return, or should I first hate others' parents in order that they would love my parents in return? Of course I should first love others' parents in order that they would love my parents in return." ([Mozi, Universal Love III, Mei translation](https://www.themozi.org/chapters/ch16.html))

Mencius is told of a Mohist, Yi Zhi, and Legge's translation records Yi's position, which already concedes something: "we are to love all without difference of degree; but the manifestation of love must begin with our parents." Mencius answers:

> "Moreover, Heaven gives birth to creatures in such a way that they have one root, and Yi makes them to have two roots. This is the cause of his error." ([Mencius, Teng Wen Gong I, Legge translation](https://ctext.org/mengzi/teng-wen-gong-i))

Later, in the passage on the doctrines that fill the age, he states the charge: "Mo's principle is 'to love all equally,' which does not acknowledge the peculiar affection due to a father." ([Mencius, Teng Wen Gong II, Legge translation](https://ctext.org/mengzi/teng-wen-gong-ii))

The two sides are not fully facing each other. Mozi's reasoning in the quoted passage is about outcomes and reciprocity, and Mencius replies about where care comes from, with one root in the family. So the page cannot say that either has refuted the other.

My lean is that Mencius's objection has most force against the idea that care can be moved outward by calculation, and that Mozi's has most force when partiality becomes license for harm, which his Universal Love I describes for thieves, ministers and feudal lords. The limits: both translators render the key term differently, Mei as universal love and Legge as loving all equally, and I did not check the Chinese, so what exactly jian ai demands (equal feeling or equal weight in action) is not something this page decides.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the default of managing alone, the circle drawn at kin and friend, the feeling-with that overwhelms and withdraws, and the kindness that stays an intention. The adaptive direction leans on trusted others as the designed condition, widens the circle on purpose, converts resonance into care before it turns into distress, and puts kindness into acts and spending where it can land.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K161 — Loving-kindness meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K161%20%E2%80%94%20Loving-kindness%20meditation): the phased goodwill practice: phrases of goodwill for yourself, a friend, a neutral person, a difficult person, and all beings.

2. [K414 — Compassion meditation (CCT-style)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K414%20%E2%80%94%20Compassion%20meditation%20%28CCT-style%29): the daily meditations of Compassion Cultivation Training: where loving-kindness wishes beings well, this practice attends to difficulty.

3. [K211 — Acts of kindness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K211%20%E2%80%94%20Acts%20of%20kindness): one visible act of help daily; small counts, and the study behind it counted acts without weighing them.

4. [K213 — Prosocial spending](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K213%20%E2%80%94%20Prosocial%20spending): spending a small amount of money on someone else, from the research on how the direction of small spending shapes mood.

5. [K166 — Sympathetic joy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K166%20%E2%80%94%20Sympathetic%20joy): deliberately rejoicing when someone you might compare yourself to does well.

6. [K219 — Social accountability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K219%20%E2%80%94%20Social%20accountability): tell one person about the goal or practice and let them ask about it, as in the Matthews study's weekly progress reports.

7. [K221 — Community engagement](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K221%20%E2%80%94%20Community%20engagement): time spent in a community not one's own, with the emphasis on listening.

8. [K399 — The kindness pool (unblock the spring)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K399%20%E2%80%94%20The%20kindness%20pool%20%28unblock%20the%20spring%29): unblocking the spring that feeds the kindness pool instead of rationing the pool, through the befriending meditation.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. Of the kernels above I would keep two: the metta phrases, because they can run in the background of an ordinary day, and the weekly kindness act, because it is small enough to survive a bad week. What stays for any reader is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### What others do

[ST315 — Loneliness and Mortality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST315%20%E2%80%94%20Loneliness%20and%20Mortality): 308,849 participants across 148 studies: stronger social relationships predict survival, an effect comparable to smoking cessation and exceeding the effects of physical inactivity.

### The bond

[ST243 — The Five Relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST243%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Five%20Relationships): the junzi is cultivated inside the wulun, five relationships each defined by reciprocal duties between the two parties.

[ST288 — Joel and Clementine Erase Each Other](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST288%20%E2%80%94%20Joel%20and%20Clementine%20Erase%20Each%20Other): Joel and Clementine each pay to erase the other from memory; inside his own mind Joel fights the procedure, hiding Clementine in memories she does not belong to.

[ST194 — Seneca Writes to His Mother](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST194%20%E2%80%94%20Seneca%20Writes%20to%20His%20Mother): from Corsica, Seneca refuses to console Helvia with the usuals and argues that nothing was taken from him that was ever his; what he sends instead is the letter itself.

### Care to the end

[ST262 — Georges Cares for Anne](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST262%20%E2%80%94%20Georges%20Cares%20for%20Anne): Anne's body fails and Georges stays: the feeding, the washing, the refusal of the hospital, and the daughter's offers of management from outside.

### The other's claim

[ST267 — The Face of the Other](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST267%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Face%20of%20the%20Other): the face addresses me as more than an object among objects, and the address places me under obligation before any contract.

[ST077 — The Mother's Protection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST077%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Mother%27s%20Protection): the sutta takes a mother's willingness to risk her life for her only child and sets it as the measure for goodwill toward all beings.

[ST079 — Won't You Be My Neighbour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST079%20%E2%80%94%20Won%27t%20You%20Be%20My%20Neighbour): thirty-three years of feeding the fish and asking the question, a theology of the neighbourhood delivered to children.

[ST313 — Jane Goodall's Two Careers](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST313%20%E2%80%94%20Jane%20Goodall%27s%20Two%20Careers): at the 1986 conference on the chimpanzees' conditions she sees the scale of the destruction and leaves fieldwork permanently, becoming an activist travelling roughly 300 days a year and founding Roots and Shoots.

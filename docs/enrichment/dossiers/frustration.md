# Frustration

> What is frustration telling the person about the gap between effort and outcome, and when is the right move to push harder rather than change course?

Frustration is what a blocked path feels like from inside. The goal is set, the effort is spent, and the world will not cooperate: the code will not compile, the child will not sleep, the career will not move. What the feeling is, and what it asks of the person who has it, is where the accounts split. The aggression researchers treat it as an event that generates its own pressure toward hostile action; the self-regulation researchers treat it as information about the rate of progress; the clinical tradition that studies low frustration tolerance treats its sting as something the person partly manufactures; and the contemplative traditions ask whether the feeling is ever useful at all. The practices I keep in the last section share one move: they widen the gap between the blockage and the response, so the signal can be read before it is acted on.

## Nearby terms

The words around frustration are used loosely, so I keep them apart unless a source joins them. What each source does say, in the sources' own words:

Anger: Seneca draws the line inside the episode. The first stirring at an apparent injury is not yet anger:

> "the first confusion of a man's mind when struck by what seems an injury is no more anger than the apparent injury itself: it is the subsequent mad rush, which not only receives the impression of the apparent injury, but acts upon it as true, that is anger, being an exciting of the mind to revenge, which proceeds from choice and deliberate resolve." ([Seneca, De Ira II.III, trans. Aubrey Stewart](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64576/64576-0.txt))

I hold frustration to Seneca's first confusion, the felt blockage under reason's control, and anger to the mad rush past it; anger has its own dossier. Irritation and annoyance name the milder neighbours, and I use them loosely as everyday words, not as constructs; the sources opened for this page do not define them against frustration, and this page does not either. Patience is the counter-capacity, Schnitker's propensity to wait calmly in the face of frustration or adversity, held in its own group in the map below.

## The map: the entries on frustration

### What frustration is taken to be

[Frustration–aggression hypothesis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Frustration%E2%80%93aggression%20hypothesis): the 1939 formulation that began the modern study: aggression as a consequence of frustration, frustration as an obstacle blocking an expected gratification, an event before it is an emotion.

[Behavioral self-regulation theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Behavioral%20self-regulation%20theory): Carver and Scheier's loop, where negative affect is the error signal of a second loop watching the first: feeling bad is a reading on the rate of progress, not a verdict on the goal.

[Patience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Patience): Schnitker's research construct, defined against frustration itself, with a scale that separates patience with people, with hardships and with daily hassles.

[The irritation of doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20irritation%20of%20doubt): Peirce's name for the discomfort an unresolved belief produces, the itch inquiry exists to scratch: frustration's cognitive cousin.

[The insatiability of craving](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20insatiability%20of%20craving): Schopenhauer's line on life as unavoidably frustrating, with the suffering reducible, on his account, by minimizing desire.

Schnitker's definition makes patience the counter-construct, and her studies found the capacity trainable:

> "Patience is the propensity to wait calmly in the face of frustration or adversity." ([Schnitker 2012, abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2012.697185))

Her patience training program "led to increased patience, decreased depression, and increased positive affect relative to a control condition, suggesting that patience may be modifiable" ([Schnitker 2012, abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2012.697185)).

### The blocked paths

Frustration names a family of blocks, and the family is held by member.

[ERG Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ERG%20Theory): Alderfer's frustration-regression: when growth needs are blocked, effort retreats to the more concrete needs, relationships and security, which is why a blocked ambition so often shows up as overwork on everything except the ambition.

[Effort-reward imbalance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effort-reward%20imbalance): Siegrist's model of the high cost, low gain condition: continued failed reciprocity at work frustrates basic reward expectations, with documented costs to mental and physical health.

[Autonomy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autonomy): self-determination research treats autonomy as a need whose frustration, alongside competence and relatedness, carries wellness consequences rather than mere disappointment.

[Social embeddedness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20embeddedness): the belongingness drive, which frustrates like a need: its absence damages and its restoration restores.

[Noogenic neurosis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Noogenic%20neurosis): Frankl's existential frustration, the will to meaning with nowhere to put itself: a blockage at the level of purpose, which he insisted is misdiagnosed when read as a drive conflict.

[Novelty seeking without commitment](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Novelty%20seeking%20without%20commitment): the temperament pattern where persistence, the dimension that carries behaviour through frustration and fatigue, is the one missing: many starts, few finishes.

### What frustration turns into

The blockage has to go somewhere, and the traditions map the exits.

[Frustration–aggression hypothesis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Frustration%E2%80%93aggression%20hypothesis): the original exit: aggression, revised by Berkowitz into inclinations rather than acts, and by Breuer and Elson into negative affect as the proximal cause.

[General strain theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=General%20strain%20theory): Agnew's criminology of the same pressure: strain generates negative emotions that press for corrective action, with anger the most critical, and coping resources deciding who offends.

[Anger](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger): the neighbouring state, held in its own dossier; Seneca's line between the two is drawn in Nearby terms below.

[Positive attention training attentional deployment toward positive stimuli](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20attention%20training%20attentional%20deployment%20toward%20positive%20stimuli): the attentional exit: training attention toward positive stimuli lowered state frustration scores in the reviewed dot-probe and anagram studies.

### Tolerance: the capacity to stand it

[Distress tolerance (DBT)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distress%20tolerance%20%28DBT%29): the clinical construct that splits the perceived capacity to withstand aversive states from the behavioral act of withstanding them, with the DBT module teaching crisis survival and acceptance rather than approval.

The tolerance construct has a clinical shadow. In Rational Emotive Behavior Therapy, low frustration tolerance is one of the four core irrational beliefs, defined as the conviction that discomfort would be unbearable:

> "An example of LFT is 'it is unbearable to fail.'" ([Turner 2016, Frontiers in Psychology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5028385/))

The research base Turner reviews ties it to the two failure modes this dossier keeps returning to: "low frustration tolerance (LFT) is positively associated with aggressive expression of anger (Martin and Dahlen, 2004), reduced anger control (Moller and Van der Merwe, 1997), poor social adjustment (Watson et al., 1998), addictive behaviors (Ko et al., 2008), anxiety, depression, procrastination, and dysfunctional affect" ([Turner 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5028385/)). The procrastination link is the oldest: Ellis and Knaus "regarding LFT as 'the main and the most direct cause of procrastination'" ([Turner 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5028385/)).

### The classical answers

[Read anger as blocked striving with a wounded self-regard](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=SP031%20%E2%80%94%20Read%20anger%20as%20blocked%20striving%20with%20a%20wounded%20self-regard): Spinoza's diagnosis, where evil is every pain, especially the pain that frustrates our longings: the grievance read as blocked striving, and examined as such.

[Dissect anger when it arises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B105%20%E2%80%94%20Dissect%20anger%20when%20it%20arises): Shantideva's usefulness question from the patience chapter: if a problem has a remedy, what use is frustration; if there is no remedy, what use is frustration.

[The "is this up to me?" check](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K271%20%E2%80%94%20The%20is%20this%20up%20to%20me%20check): Epictetus's portable test, run at the moment a concern appears: what is up to you gets an action, what is not gets released.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is the sting in the event or in the belief?

The shared question: when a person is frustrated, where does the pressure they feel come from? The aggression literature's revised answer puts it in the event's affect. Berkowitz's reformulation of the 1939 model, from the abstract of the review that set it out:

> "A proposed revision of the 1939 model holds that frustrations generate aggressive inclinations to the degree that they arouse negative affect." ([Berkowitz 1989, Psychological Bulletin, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2667009/))

The same abstract concedes the person's reading a role: "Interpretations and attributions can be understood partly in terms of the original analysis but they can also influence the unpleasantness of the thwarting" ([Berkowitz 1989, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2667009/)). The REBT line pushes the person's reading much further: on that account the sting is largely manufactured by the belief that the discomfort is unbearable, and the same blockage met with a different belief produces a different pressure. Shantideva's question is sharper still, since it asks whether the feeling does any work at all. The positions answer the same question, where the pressure lives, and part on how much of it the event supplies. What remains unresolved is whether the two accounts conflict or describe two layers of one episode, the affective arousal Berkowitz measures and the amplification the belief adds; the evidence reviewed above does not separate them.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the jump from blockage to response, in either direction: the rush forward into hostility or retaliation, or the collapse into the conviction that the situation cannot be stood, which shows up as avoidance, procrastination and self-medication. The adaptive direction is the gap between the blockage and the response: the frustration read as information about the path, the belief examined before it is obeyed, and the question asked of the blockage itself, whether it calls for more effort, different effort, or no effort.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [The "is this up to me?" check](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K271%20%E2%80%94%20The%20is%20this%20up%20to%20me%20check): sort the blockage at the moment it appears into what is yours to act on and what is not; act on the first, release the second.

2. [Anger dissection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K169%20%E2%80%94%20Anger%20dissection): take the frustration apart as it rises: the bodily sensation, the trigger, the story, the value violated, the urge to act.

3. [Challenge-skill calibration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K189%20%E2%80%94%20Challenge-skill%20calibration): when the blockage is a mismatch between difficulty and skill, adjust one of the two axes; boredom and frustration are both calibration readings, not verdicts.

4. [The mean calibration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K253%20%E2%80%94%20The%20mean%20calibration): find the middle state between pushing through everything and abandoning everything, per provocation.

5. [Opposite action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K184%20%E2%80%94%20Opposite%20action): when the frustration's urge is hostile, act against the urge rather than venting it.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the check, the dissection or the calibration, and finds the same blockage met differently a month later, has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

# Uncertainty

> Can a person live well without knowing what comes next?

This dossier is a map, not an essay. A reader who asks "what do the traditions and the research say about living without certainty?" can open it and see everything the compendium holds on the theme, grouped by the question each entry answers, with a link to each card. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The compendium's wider aim runs through the page: to help a person meet what cannot be known with steadiness, so that not knowing stops being the source of suffering.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on uncertainty

### Enduring not knowing

[Intolerance of uncertainty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intolerance%20of%20uncertainty): a dispositional incapacity to endure the aversive response triggered by uncertain situations.

[Intolerance of ambiguity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intolerance%20of%20ambiguity): the tendency to perceive ambiguous situations as sources of threat, in Budner's classic definition.

[Mindful uncertainty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindful%20uncertainty): the ACT posture toward the unknowable parts of experience: stay open to the present moment without needing it to be settled first.

[Negative capability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Negative%20capability): Keats's term for the power to remain in uncertainties, mysteries and doubts without any irritable reaching after fact and reason.

[Distress tolerance (DBT)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distress%20tolerance%20%28DBT%29): the perceived capacity to withstand aversive states, distinguished by Leyro and colleagues from the behavioral act of withstanding them.

[Equanimity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Equanimity): the even-minded stance toward experience: neither grasping at the pleasant nor pushing away the unpleasant.

[Beginner's mind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beginner%27s%20mind): the Zen practice of meeting experience as if for the first time.

[Openness and not knowing in skilled practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Openness%20and%20not%20knowing%20in%20skilled%20practice): Ericsson's deliberate practice as work on tasks just outside current reliable performance.


### Doubt, ignorance and inquiry

[Socratic ignorance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Socratic%20ignorance): the profession Socrates makes at his trial: he does not know the things that matter most, and unlike others he does not think he does.

[Aporia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aporia): the productive dead end: the state of being unable to move forward in an inquiry.

[Doubt as method](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Doubt%20as%20method): Descartes' procedure in the Meditations: doubt to find what survives doubt.

[Genuine doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Genuine%20doubt): Peirce's name for the real and living doubt that inquiry actually requires.

[The irritation of doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20irritation%20of%20doubt): Peirce's phrase from The Fixation of Belief, naming the engine of inquiry.

[The great doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20great%20doubt): the Zen name for the concentrated questioning at the centre of koan practice.

[Skepticism about the obvious](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Skepticism%20about%20the%20obvious): the Pyrrhonist practice in Sextus Empiricus: what seems plainly there is still an appearance, and appearances conflict.

[Skepticism answered by life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Skepticism%20answered%20by%20life): Hume's idea that radical doubt does not survive ordinary living.

[Actively open-minded thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Actively%20open-minded%20thinking): a reasoning style that Jonathan Baron developed in the judgment-and-decision-making literature.


### What lasts, and what is up to us

[Impermanence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Impermanence): the fact that everything that arises will pass: moods, plans, bodies, relationships, reputations.

[Anicca](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anicca): impermanence, the first of the Three Marks of Existence.

[The dichotomy of control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20dichotomy%20of%20control): Epictetus's distinction in Enchiridion 1 between what is up to us and what is not.

[Locus of control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Locus%20of%20control): Rotter's generalized expectancy about where reinforcement comes from.

[Secondary control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Secondary%20control): Rothbaum, Weisz and Snyder's way of gaining a sense of control by bringing yourself into line with the world.

[Antifragility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Antifragility): Taleb's coinage for the third category that fragile and robust leave out.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is not knowing something to dwell in, or a weakness to treat?

The shared question: when the future or the truth is unsettled, should a person learn to stay in that state? Keats named it, in a letter, as the quality that makes a man of achievement in literature:

> "Negative Capability , that is, when a man is capable of being in uncertainties, mysteries, doubts, without any irritable reaching after fact and reason." ([Keats, Letters of John Keats to His Family and Friends, Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35698/35698-h/35698-h.htm)): Primary text; quoted passage checked.

The clinical literature on worry treats the same tolerance as a vulnerability factor when it is missing:

> "The Intolerance of Uncertainty Model (IUM) of Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) attributes a key role to Intolerance of Uncertainty (IU), and additional roles to Positive Beliefs about Worry (PBW), Negative Problem Orientation (NPO), and Cognitive Avoidance (CA), in the development and maintenance of worry, the core feature of GAD." ([Intolerance of Uncertainty Model, PMC article, abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5088195/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked.

The two do not contradict each other as much as they look. Keats describes a capacity of artists and a reason for its worth; the model describes what follows in worriers when the capacity is absent. Both treat the capacity as the thing at stake, and the compendium's practices on sitting with uncertainty draw on the shared reading.

### What is the right response to what cannot be foreseen?

The shared question: since much of what comes is not ours to settle, what should a person attend to? Epictetus answers with a division:

> "Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions." ([Epictetus, Enchiridion 1, Carter translation, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)): Primary text; quoted passage checked.

His claim is that holding to the division removes the injury: "no one will ever compel you or restrain you." Keats's capacity points the other way, toward staying with what cannot be reduced to a rule. A reader can take them as answering different questions, one about where to put effort and one about how to hold what lies outside it, and the compendium keeps both.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying is the reflex to resolve. The look-alikes of tolerance are resignation and distraction. The adaptive moves are to let the not-knowing stay present, to turn an unknown into a small test, and to let the one fact that is certain, mortality with an unknown date, say what is urgent.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [Sitting with uncertainty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K122%20%E2%80%94%20Sitting%20with%20uncertainty): when you do not know what will happen, let the not-knowing be present and resist resolving it prematurely.

2. [Weekly inquiry loop](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K233%20%E2%80%94%20Weekly%20inquiry%20loop): one question a week, one small test, one honest look at the data.

3. [Mortality reflection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K222%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality%20reflection): once a month, write ten minutes on "I am mortal. The timing is unknown. What does this make urgent?"

4. [Mortality-to-action translation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K316%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality-to-action%20translation): write five minutes on the same question, then turn one insight into one action this week.

The cards carry their own evidence notes. Several of these are proposals built on the traditions and have not been tested as protocols.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the weekly test, or the habit of letting a not-knowing sit for a day before resolving it, has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

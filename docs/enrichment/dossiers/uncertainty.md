# Uncertainty

> Can a person live well without knowing what comes next?

Not knowing what comes next is the human condition; the suffering comes from the demand that it stop. The traditions meet uncertainty with steadiness, the research with tolerance and probabilistic thinking, and both find the same thing: what corrodes a life is the demand for certainty. Meeting what cannot be known without flinching is trainable.

## What not knowing asks of a person

### Can the not-knowing be endured?

The clinical literature measures the incapacity. [Intolerance of uncertainty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intolerance%20of%20uncertainty) is a dispositional incapacity to endure the aversive response triggered by uncertain situations, and [intolerance of ambiguity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intolerance%20of%20ambiguity) is the tendency to perceive ambiguous situations as sources of threat, in Budner's classic definition.

The contemplative and philosophical traditions train the capacity itself. [Mindful uncertainty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindful%20uncertainty) is the ACT posture toward the unknowable parts of experience: stay open to the present moment without needing it to be settled first. Keats's term for the power to remain in uncertainties, mysteries and doubts without any irritable reaching after fact and reason is [negative capability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Negative%20capability).

The measured capacity is [distress tolerance (DBT)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distress%20tolerance%20%28DBT%29), the perceived capacity to withstand aversive states, distinguished by Leyro and colleagues from the behavioral act of withstanding them. The even-minded stance toward experience, neither grasping at the pleasant nor pushing away the unpleasant, is [equanimity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Equanimity). The Zen practice of meeting experience as if for the first time is [beginner's mind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beginner%27s%20mind).

Ericsson's deliberate practice as work on tasks just outside current reliable performance is [openness and not knowing in skilled practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Openness%20and%20not%20knowing%20in%20skilled%20practice).

### What doubt is for

The profession Socrates makes at his trial is [Socratic ignorance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Socratic%20ignorance): he does not know the things that matter most, and unlike others he does not think he does. The productive dead end is [aporia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aporia), the state of being unable to move forward in an inquiry.

Descartes' procedure in the Meditations is [doubt as method](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Doubt%20as%20method): doubt to find what survives doubt. Peirce's name for the real and living doubt that inquiry actually requires is [genuine doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Genuine%20doubt), and his phrase from The Fixation of Belief, naming the engine of inquiry, is [the irritation of doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20irritation%20of%20doubt).

The Zen name for the concentrated questioning at the centre of koan practice is [the great doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20great%20doubt). The Pyrrhonist practice in Sextus Empiricus is [skepticism about the obvious](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Skepticism%20about%20the%20obvious): what seems plainly there is still an appearance, and appearances conflict. Hume's idea that radical doubt does not survive ordinary living is [skepticism answered by life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Skepticism%20answered%20by%20life).

A reasoning style that Jonathan Baron developed in the judgment-and-decision-making literature is [actively open-minded thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Actively%20open-minded%20thinking).

### What is up to us

The fact that everything that arises will pass, moods, plans, bodies, relationships, reputations, is [impermanence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Impermanence), and [anicca](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anicca) is its name as the first of the Three Marks of Existence. Epictetus's distinction in Enchiridion 1 between what is up to us and what is not is [the dichotomy of control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20dichotomy%20of%20control).

Rotter's generalized expectancy about where reinforcement comes from is [locus of control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Locus%20of%20control). Rothbaum, Weisz and Snyder's way of gaining a sense of control by bringing yourself into line with the world is [secondary control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Secondary%20control). Taleb's coinage for the third category that fragile and robust leave out is [antifragility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Antifragility).

### What hope and trust are worth here

The forward-facing emotion, expectation of a good that has not arrived and is not guaranteed, is [hope](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hope). The Greek word for hope, best known from Hesiod's story of Pandora, is [elpis (hope)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Elpis%20%28hope%29). A pattern in Seneca and Epictetus holds hope as a thing to weigh, because it travels with fear, carried as [hope tempered by prudence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hope%20tempered%20by%20prudence).

Emerson's conviction that one's own perception is a fit instrument for living is [self-trust](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-trust). Trust in strangers, or in most people in society, rather than in people you already know is [generalized trust](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Generalized%20trust). Rilke's word, in the eighth Duino Elegy, for what the animal sees and we do not is [the open (das Offene)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20open%20%28das%20Offene%29).

Camus's name for the confrontation between the human demand for meaning and the world's silence is [the absurd](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20absurd). Beauvoir's name for the human condition's two-foldness is [ambiguity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ambiguity), and her existentialist ethics, that freedom is ambiguous because it must will both itself and the freedom of others, is [the ethics of ambiguity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20ethics%20of%20ambiguity).

### What probability is for

The move from yes-or-no to how-likely, whose arrival in European thought was sudden, is [probabilistic thinking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Probabilistic%20thinking). Gigerenzer's name for the ability to understand and evaluate risk well enough to make informed decisions is [risk literacy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Risk%20literacy), and his simple decision rules that ignore part of the available information and can still judge well under uncertainty are [decision-making under uncertainty (Gigerenzer's fast-and-frugal heuristics)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Decision-making%20under%20uncertainty%20%28Gigerenzer%27s%20fast-and-frugal%20heuristics%29).

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is not knowing something to dwell in, or a weakness to treat?

The shared question: when the future or the truth is unsettled, should a person learn to stay in that state? Keats named it, in a letter, as the quality that makes a man of achievement in literature:

> "Negative Capability , that is, when a man is capable of being in uncertainties, mysteries, doubts, without any irritable reaching after fact and reason." ([Keats, Letters of John Keats to His Family and Friends, Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35698/35698-h/35698-h.htm))

The clinical literature on worry treats the same tolerance as a vulnerability factor when it is missing:

> "The Intolerance of Uncertainty Model (IUM) of Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD) attributes a key role to Intolerance of Uncertainty (IU), and additional roles to Positive Beliefs about Worry (PBW), Negative Problem Orientation (NPO), and Cognitive Avoidance (CA), in the development and maintenance of worry, the core feature of GAD." ([Intolerance of Uncertainty Model, PMC article, abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5088195/))

The two do not contradict each other as much as they look. Keats describes a capacity of artists and a reason for its worth; the model describes what follows in worriers when the capacity is absent. Both treat the capacity as the thing at stake, and the practices here on sitting with uncertainty draw on the shared reading.

### What is the right response to what cannot be foreseen?

The shared question: since much of what comes is not ours to settle, what should a person attend to? Epictetus answers with a division:

> "Some things are in our control and others not. Things in our control are opinion, pursuit, desire, aversion, and, in a word, whatever are our own actions. Things not in our control are body, property, reputation, command, and, in one word, whatever are not our own actions." ([Epictetus, Enchiridion 1, Carter translation, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html))

His claim is that holding to the division removes the injury: "no one will ever compel you or restrain you." Keats's capacity points the other way, toward staying with what cannot be reduced to a rule. A reader can take them as answering different questions, one about where to put effort and one about how to hold what lies outside it, and I keep both.

### Can a one-off unknown be given a number?

The shared question: when a person faces something that has not happened before, is there a probability to be found, or only a judgment? Knight, writing in 1921, drew a line between the two cases and placed most of business life on the side that has no number:

> "The practical difference between the two categories, risk and uncertainty, is that in the former the distribution of the outcome in a group of instances is known (either through calculation a priori or from statistics of past experience), while in the case of uncertainty this is not true, the reason being in general that it is impossible to form a group of instances, because the situation dealt with is in a high degree unique." ([Knight, Risk, Uncertainty, and Profit, Part III, Chapter VIII, Econlib edition](https://www.econlib.org/library/Knight/knRUP7.html))

Ramsey, five years later, built a theory in which every belief has a degree, whether or not a group of instances exists:

> "We are driven therefore to the second supposition that the degree of a belief is a causal property of it, which we can express vaguely as the extent to which we are prepared to act on it." ([Ramsey, Truth and Probability, 1926](https://fitelson.org/probability/ramsey.pdf))

Ramsey is careful about what this buys. His text says that "beliefs do differ in measurability in the following two ways. First, some beliefs can be measured more accurately than others; and, secondly, the measurement of beliefs is almost certainly an ambiguous process leading to a variable answer depending on how exactly the measurement is conducted." ([Ramsey, Truth and Probability, 1926](https://fitelson.org/probability/ramsey.pdf))

The two do not answer one another, since Ramsey's paper does not cite Knight, and pairing them is my framing and not either author's. What they share is the starting fact that people must act without a record of earlier cases.

Knight concludes that the lack of a record leaves a different kind of problem, which calls for judgment about the situation. Ramsey concludes that a person's action already shows a degree of belief, which can be written down as a number even if it is imprecise. My lean, which is mine, is that the Ramsey route is useful for deciding and Knight's route is useful for knowing when the number is a guess. Put plainly, one holds that for a unique situation no distribution of outcomes is known, and the other holds that a degree of belief exists anyway and can be roughly measured. Limits: I read Knight's chapter text and Ramsey's paper only for these passages, and I did not open the later literature that argues over how the two relate, such as work on ambiguity.

### Does the conjunction rule bind judgments about single events?

The shared question: when a person judges how likely one single event is, do the laws of probability apply to that judgment as a standard it can violate? The heuristics-and-biases program read the Linda problem and its relatives as violations; Gigerenzer's critique attacked that reading at its root.

Kahneman and Tversky defend the norm, and they state its basis:

> "Normative agnosticism is particularly inappropriate in the case of the conjunction rule." ([Kahneman and Tversky, On the reality of cognitive illusions, Psychological Review 103, 1996](http://matt.colorado.edu/teaching/highcog/fall8/kt96.pdf))

> "First, the application of this rule does not require numerical estimates, only an ordinal judgment of which of two events is more probable. Second, the normative basis for the conjunction rule is essentially logical: If the conjunction A & B is true then A must also be true, but the converse does not hold." ([Kahneman and Tversky 1996](http://matt.colorado.edu/teaching/highcog/fall8/kt96.pdf))

They state the character of the dispute themselves:

> "Our disagreement here is normative, not descriptive. We believe that subjective probability judgments should be calibrated, whereas Gigerenzer appears unwilling to apply normative criteria to such judgments." ([Kahneman and Tversky 1996](http://matt.colorado.edu/teaching/highcog/fall8/kt96.pdf))

Gigerenzer rejects the norm for single events:

> "For instance, the conjunction rule applied to a single event is not a general norm for sound reasoning." ([Gigerenzer, On narrow norms and vague heuristics: A reply to Kahneman and Tversky, Psychological Review 103, 1996](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2547850_4/component/file_2561571/content))

The Linda problem is the shared task on which the accounts collide. Gigerenzer reports that with frequency formats "the conjunction fallacy in the Linda problem is reduced from about 85% to 20% and less" ([Gigerenzer 1996](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_2547850_4/component/file_2561571/content)). Kahneman and Tversky answer with a between-subjects frequency version of the same problem and report that "the results demonstrate a violation of the conjunction rule in a frequency formulation" ([same paper](http://matt.colorado.edu/teaching/highcog/fall8/kt96.pdf)).

The clash is explicit in both texts, each naming the other: one side holds that the conjunction rule binds even single-event judgment because its basis is logical; the other holds that applying it to a single event imposes a contested statistical doctrine as a norm of sound reasoning. Limits: I have read this 1996 exchange and not the later literature that adjudicates it.

My lean, which is mine, is to check how a question was framed before treating my answer as evidence that my judgment is faulty.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying is the reflex to resolve. The look-alikes of tolerance are resignation and distraction. The adaptive moves are to let the not-knowing stay present, to turn an unknown into a small test, and to let the one fact that is certain, mortality with an unknown date, say what is urgent.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [Sitting with uncertainty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K122%20%E2%80%94%20Sitting%20with%20uncertainty): when you do not know what will happen, let the not-knowing be present and resist resolving it prematurely.

2. [Weekly inquiry loop](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K233%20%E2%80%94%20Weekly%20inquiry%20loop): one question a week, one small test, one honest look at the data.

3. [Mortality reflection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K222%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality%20reflection): once a month, write ten minutes on "I am mortal. The timing is unknown. What does this make urgent?"

4. [Mortality-to-action translation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K316%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality-to-action%20translation): write five minutes on the same question, then turn one insight into one action this week.

The cards carry their own evidence notes. Several of these are proposals built on the traditions and have not been tested as protocols.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the weekly test, or the habit of letting a not-knowing sit for a day before resolving it, has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

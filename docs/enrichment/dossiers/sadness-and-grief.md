# Sadness & Grief

> What is grief asking of the person grieving, and who sets when the work is done?

Sadness and grief are the family's honest states. Something is lost, and I feel bad about it, and the traditions around me have always been tempted to fix the feeling before asking what it is doing. The questions I care about here are practical ones: what work does grief consist of, what does it ask of the person grieving, and when, by whose measure, is the work done?

The accounts below divide the labour between them. The clinical research gives grief its structure as an alternation between two kinds of work, and it marks the boundary with depression carefully. The classical letters prescribe proportion and a stop. Gibran's chapter and the Aztec songs treat sorrow as constitutive rather than as a defect to be cleared. Between them sits the failure mode the clinic names, complicated grief, and the extension of grief beyond human death that the ecological literature has opened.

### Nearby terms

- Sadness: the state itself, felt toward anything lost or absent; the cleanest treatment of it as a working state rather than a defect is [Sorrow's fruit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sorrow%27s%20fruit), and the film case is [ST187 — Joy Reads the Memory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST187%20%E2%80%94%20Joy%20Reads%20the%20Memory).
- Sorrow: the deep register of sadness; Gibran's chapter treats it as constitutive, the well the laughter rises from.
- Mourning: grief's public and ritual form; the classical arguments about its limits sit on [S062 — Do not grieve excessively](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S062%20%E2%80%94%20Do%20not%20grieve%20excessively).
- Bereavement: the objective situation of loss; grief is the process it starts.
- Lament: voiced, formalised mourning; the Aztec songs that hold grief and enjoyment in one breath are on [M038 — Grieve without pretending, and without drowning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=M038%20%E2%80%94%20Grieve%20without%20pretending%2C%20and%20without%20drowning).
- Depression: the clinical condition this dossier must keep distinct from grief; the boundary work is [AF051 — Recognise the difference between sadness and depression](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF051%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20difference%20between%20sadness%20and%20depression) and [Complicated grief research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complicated%20grief%20research).

## What grief is and what it asks

### Is grief a state or a course of work?

The clinical material gives grief its structure as a process rather than a mood, and it separates grief from depression with two observations:

> "In grief, painful feelings come in waves, often intermixed with positive memories of the deceased. In major depression, mood and/or interest (pleasure) are decreased for most of two weeks." ([APA, What Is Depression](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/depression/what-is-depression))

> "In grief, self-esteem is usually maintained. In major depression, feelings of worthlessness and self-loathing are common." ([APA, What Is Depression](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/depression/what-is-depression))

The process structure itself is the dual-process model. [Grief's oscillation model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Grief%27s%20oscillation%20model) defines grief as alternation between two kinds of work, and [AF052 — Learn to grieve — grief is a process, not a problem to solve](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF052%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20grieve%20%E2%80%94%20grief%20is%20a%20process%2C%20not%20a%20problem%20to%20solve) states the same point as a principle. The clinical sources distinguish grief from depression and model bereavement coping; they do not directly establish a sadness-versus-grief boundary, and I leave that boundary open rather than asserting it. What the family's own entries show is that its sadness material treats sorrow as deep and constitutive, which is grief's register rather than a passing mood's.

### What does the work consist of?

The dual-process model's own summary sentence:

> "This model identifies two types of stressors, loss- and restoration-oriented, and a dynamic, regulatory coping process of oscillation, whereby the grieving individual at times confronts, at other times avoids, the different tasks of grieving." ([Stroebe & Schut 1999](https://wendyvanmieghem.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/dual-process-model-by-M.-Stroebe-.pdf))

The model also argues the need for dosage:

> "It also argues the need for dosage of grieving, that is, the need to take respite from dealing with either of these stressors, as an integral part of adaptive coping." ([Stroebe & Schut 1999](https://wendyvanmieghem.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/dual-process-model-by-M.-Stroebe-.pdf))

Respite is part of adaptive coping, which is a different instruction from either wallowing or cutting the loss off. The practice built on it is [K319 — Non-avoidant grief work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K319%20%E2%80%94%20Non-avoidant%20grief%20work): a deliberate, time-limited sitting with the loss, then a return to the restoration side. [M038 — Grieve without pretending, and without drowning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=M038%20%E2%80%94%20Grieve%20without%20pretending%2C%20and%20without%20drowning) adds the paired observation from the Cantares Mexicanos: the songs hold grief and enjoyment side by side inside one stanza.

[Continuing bonds](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Continuing%20bonds) records the finding that softened the older clinical picture of detachment: the bereaved often keep an inner relationship with the dead. The 2012 longitudinal study put one limit on it:

> "Quite consistently, the results show that those persons who have experienced an unexpected bereavement, and who retain close bonds to the deceased person, fail to recover over time." ([Stroebe et al. 2012](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/67801213/STROEBE_et_al_2012_Personal_Relationships.pdf))

The conditionality is the finding, not a verdict against bonds as such.

### How long, and by what measure?

Zhuangzi, on his wife's death, gives grief both its due and its stop:

> "When she first died, was it possible for me to be singular and not affected by the event? ... The relation between these things is like the procession of the four seasons from spring to autumn, from winter to summer. ... I therefore restrained myself!" ([Zhuangzi 18, Legge, ctext](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/perfect-enjoyment))

Seneca's letter on the death of Flaccus draws the line at proportion rather than at absence:

> "I am grieved to hear that your friend Flaccus is dead, but I would not have you sorrow more than is fitting." ([Seneca, Letter 63](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_63))

> "Let not the eyes be dry when we have lost a friend, nor let them overflow. ... Let us see to it that the recollection of those whom we have lost becomes a pleasant memory to us." ([Seneca, Letter 63](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_63))

> "For I have had them as if I should one day lose them; I have lost them as if I have them still." ([Seneca, Letter 63](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_63))

The classical limit has two further expressions. [S070 — Do not let grief become your identity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S070%20%E2%80%94%20Do%20not%20let%20grief%20become%20your%20identity) carries the identity limit: grief may be kept and kept company with, but it is not to become the person. [S114 — Handle grief without excess](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S114%20%E2%80%94%20Handle%20grief%20without%20excess) adds Epictetus' instruction for the companion: accommodate the griever, groan with him if need be, but do not groan inwardly.

The Zhuangzian and Stoic sides of the same limit sit on [D089 — Grief without destruction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D089%20%E2%80%94%20Grief%20without%20destruction), [D090 — Accept death as transformation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D090%20%E2%80%94%20Accept%20death%20as%20transformation) and [S018 — Accept loss without devastation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S018%20%E2%80%94%20Accept%20loss%20without%20devastation), the last holding the Stoic verb change: what was received is restored, not lost.

The clinic answers the how-long question with thresholds rather than letters. On [S062 — Do not grieve excessively](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S062%20%E2%80%94%20Do%20not%20grieve%20excessively) and [Complicated grief research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complicated%20grief%20research), prolonged grief disorder is diagnosed when the grief response has persisted for "more than 6 months at a minimum" (ICD-11) or the death was "at least 12 months ago" (DSM-5-TR, adults), in both cases clearly exceeding the expected social, cultural or religious norms and causing significant impairment ([PGD review, Psychiatria Polska](https://www.psychiatriapolska.pl/pdf-159024-112359?filename=Zespol-zaloby-przedluzone.pdf)). Seneca's proportion is a letter-writer's judgement; the clinic's is a duration and an impairment. The two answer different questions, and neither is a deadline handed to the griever.

### What is sorrow made of?

Gibran's chapter states it:

> "Your joy is your sorrow unmasked. And the selfsame well from which your laughter rises was oftentimes filled with your tears. ... The deeper that sorrow carves into your being, the more joy you can contain." ([Gibran, The Prophet](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/58585/58585-h/58585-h.htm))

> "When you are sorrowful look again in your heart, and you shall see that in truth you are weeping for that which has been your delight." ([Gibran, The Prophet](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/58585/58585-h/58585-h.htm))

[ST187 — Joy Reads the Memory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST187%20%E2%80%94%20Joy%20Reads%20the%20Memory) is the same claim dramatised: Joy rewinds the sad core memory and finds that the comfort Riley received after the loss was made by Sadness. [B108 — Grieve with presence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B108%20%E2%80%94%20Grieve%20with%20presence) turns the state into an attention practice: notice the empty chair at breakfast, stay with the memory or tell someone, and set no deadline for feeling better. The Buddhist note it carries makes the same point from the root: the Dhammapada verse ties grief to endearment itself, which is a claim about the root, not a deadline for the feeling.

### Can grief extend beyond human death?

[Grief for what passes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Grief%20for%20what%20passes) reads the Zhuangzi drumming-on-the-tub story as a lesson in grieving well, not in feeling nothing; the card itself leaves open whether what Zhuangzi describes counts as grief proper. [Ecological grief](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ecological%20grief) extends grief to places: it is associated with the physical disappearance, degradation and death of species, ecosystems and landscapes, driven by climate change ([Cunsolo & Ellis 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20260908165528/https://www.uwosh.edu/sirt/wp-content/uploads/sites/86/2020/04/Cunsolo-and-Ellis-2018.pdf)). The same article gives this extension its social reading:

> "For Butler, and others, grief and mourning have 'we-creating' capacities, exposing our known, unknown and unacknowledged connections to others" ([Cunsolo & Ellis 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20260908165528/https://www.uwosh.edu/sirt/wp-content/uploads/sites/86/2020/04/Cunsolo-and-Ellis-2018.pdf))

> "grief and mourning can also question fundamental assumptions about what we choose to value — and what we choose to grieve and mourn — including climate change-induced ecological loss and degradation." ([Cunsolo & Ellis 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20260908165528/https://www.uwosh.edu/sirt/wp-content/uploads/sites/86/2020/04/Cunsolo-and-Ellis-2018.pdf))

[K348 — Name the loss](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K348%20%E2%80%94%20Name%20the%20loss) carries the naming practice: name the loss in a plain sentence, first person, specific, and let the naming be said or seen; [R124 — Practise ecological grief work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=R124%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20ecological%20grief%20work) applies the same family of practice to ecological losses, with regular emotional expression through writing in a supportive setting.

### When does grief go wrong?

[Complicated grief research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complicated%20grief%20research) records the failure modes: grief that persists past the clinical thresholds, grief that forecloses the restoration side of life, and the rumination that [K319 — Non-avoidant grief work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K319%20%E2%80%94%20Non-avoidant%20grief%20work)'s container is built to catch: a sitting that turns into hours of circling is rumination wearing the practice's clothes. The dual-process reading on [S062 — Do not grieve excessively](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S062%20%E2%80%94%20Do%20not%20grieve%20excessively) treats dosage, not suppression, as the operative variable.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is successful mourning detachment or continued relationship?

The older clinical picture held that grief work means letting go: on the account documented in the 2012 study, the energy invested in the deceased loved one needed to be "worked through" to enable it to be withdrawn and invested in another person (Freud, 1917/1957), and complications in grief were associated with the inability to relinquish ties to the deceased ([Stroebe et al. 2012](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/67801213/STROEBE_et_al_2012_Personal_Relationships.pdf)). Klass, Silverman and Nickman challenged that picture in 1996, examining the ways the bereaved continue their bonds with the deceased and arguing there were benefits in retaining those connections.

The disagreement is live, not settled, and the same paper records both the challenge and its complications: despite the claims made by Klass and colleagues, Field, Gal Oz and Bonanno (2003) found continuing bonds associated with worse adjustment over a five-year postbereavement period, and the 2012 study itself found:

> "Quite consistently, the results show that those persons who have experienced an unexpected bereavement, and who retain close bonds to the deceased person, fail to recover over time." ([Stroebe et al. 2012](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/67801213/STROEBE_et_al_2012_Personal_Relationships.pdf))

The turn-on is the study's own moderator: expected versus unexpected bereavement. The finding conditions the bonds on how the death arrived, not on what a kept bond is; whether a bond that keeps the dead present in a life still being lived differs from one that substitutes for that life is my interpretation, not a measurement in the paper. My lean is toward the bonds side with the conditions respected.

### Must grief be confronted, or is avoidance part of the work?

The grief work hypothesis, traced in the dual-process paper to Freud's 1917 "Trauerarbeit," conveys a sense for the need to confront the pain and work through the loss, an effortful process ([Stroebe & Schut 1999](https://wendyvanmieghem.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/08/dual-process-model-by-M.-Stroebe-.pdf)). The dual-process model was built against that hypothesis, and the paper states its grounds: the lack of clarity in the definition of grief work, the poor quality of operationalizations, and the absence of sound evidence, some studies having failed to confirm that confrontation of grief is a predictor of adaptation. On the model, avoidance is not failure but part of the regulatory coping process.

The paper even carries the disagreement into the clinic: intervention may, for some, be counterproductive, and avoidance of grief cues, and perhaps confrontation with other tasks such as the restoration tasks, would be beneficial. The turn-on is what the avoidance is doing: respite inside an alternation, or a wall around the loss that never gets worked on from either side, and the paper does not claim all avoidance is adaptive. I favor this framework because it allows for engagement with the loss and temporary respite, without prescribing how long either should last.

### Is grief a judgment that can change, or a force that presses on us?

The shared question: when someone is bereaved, how much of the pain comes from what they believe about the loss, and how much arrives whatever they believe? Cicero, in the third book of the Tusculan Disputations, argues for the first answer from the example of Romans who did not break:

> "Now what made these men so easy, but their persuasion that grief and lamentation was not becoming in a man? Therefore, as some give themselves up to grief from an opinion that it is right so to do, they refrained themselves, from an opinion that it was discreditable; from which we may infer that grief is owing more to opinion than nature." ([Cicero, Tusculan Disputations III, Yonge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14988/pg14988.txt))

He then states the objection in the words of those who disagree, and gives them the Academic Crantor as authority:

> "It may be said, on the other side, Who is so mad as to grieve of his own accord? Pain proceeds from nature, which you must submit to, say they, agreeably to what even your own Crantor teaches, for it presses and gains upon you unavoidably, and cannot possibly be resisted." ([Cicero, Tusculan Disputations III, Yonge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14988/pg14988.txt))

His reply is that the objectors "allow that we take greater grief on ourselves than nature requires," and he names the beliefs that add to it, among them that mourning loudly pleases the dead. Cicero also surveys the comforters: Cleanthes held that the comforter must prove that what is lamented is no evil, the Peripatetics "prefer urging that the evil is not great," and Chrysippus thought the main task is "to remove the opinion from the person who is grieving, that to grieve is his bounden duty" ([Cicero, Tusculan Disputations III, Yonge translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/14988/pg14988.txt)).

The page lets Cicero speak for the Stoic side, since he is its advocate in this book, and treats the objection as the opposing position only as he reports it. The case against the objection depends on what counts as grief: the reply separates the first pang from the further grief that a person adds to it, and the objectors seem to mean the first. My lean, which is mine, is that Cicero is strongest on the added part and weakest on the pang, and that Seneca's letter on Flaccus, quoted above, is where a middle position is stated in practice. The limit is that I read the Yonge translation of one book, not the Latin or the Peripatetic texts themselves, so the claims about what that school held rest on Cicero's report.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the relationship with the loss itself: the avoidance that fences the loss off; the rumination that circles the loss without ever returning to the restoration side of life; and the deadline, either imposed from outside or awaited, that converts grief into a waiting-room occupation. The adaptive direction runs toward alternation kept deliberately: the loss sat with inside a container, the life returned to, and the loss named in a plain sentence.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K319 — Non-avoidant grief work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K319%20%E2%80%94%20Non-avoidant%20grief%20work): when grief arises, let it be present without rushing to resolve it; give the engagement a container (a time, a place, a length), and when the container ends, move to the restoration side of life. The model behind the structure treats respite as part of adaptive coping, not as failure, so the time limit is the practice rather than a concession to weakness.

2. [B108 — Grieve with presence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B108%20%E2%80%94%20Grieve%20with%20presence): notice the empty chair at breakfast; describe what you notice (a bodily feeling, a memory, a question or a practical concern); stay with the memory or tell someone about it. The exercise sets no deadline for feeling better.

3. [K348 — Name the loss](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K348%20%E2%80%94%20Name%20the%20loss): write the sentence (one line, first person, specific: what is lost, and where). Say it to someone, or write it where it can be seen; the mourning literature's "we-creating" capacities are the reason the saying is part of the practice.

4. [R124 — Practise ecological grief work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=R124%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20ecological%20grief%20work): the protocol is regular emotional expression through writing, in a supportive setting, applied to the grief felt in relation to experienced or anticipated ecological losses. The card's own caution belongs in the practice: the link from that grief to action is a hope in these sources, not a measured result, and persistent distress that disrupts sleep, work or relationships is a reason to talk to a health professional.

::Retention:: What a reader keeps from this theme is usually the alternation rather than a feeling: what was named, what was sat with, and which side today needed. A reader who keeps the container and the return, and the sentence that names the loss, has the theme's core. None of the three argues for wallowing, and none for cutting the loss off.

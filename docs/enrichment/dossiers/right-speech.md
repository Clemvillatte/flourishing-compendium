# Right Speech

> What makes speech right, and when is silence the better part of it?

Words do most of their damage before anyone has decided anything about them, which is why the disciplines gathered here test words for truth, benefit and timing before releasing them. Silence has its own place in the account, and so do the lie that buys ease and the advice that burdens its hearer. The practice is taking words off autopilot.

### Nearby terms

- Truthful speech: held in the truth-telling group below.
- Right communication and speech ethics: the modern relatives, spread through the groups below.
- Idle talk and gossip: the abstentions group below.
- Listening: its own group below; listening is the other half of speech and I treat it as a practice in its own right.

## The path's own account

The path definition, in the Buddha's own words:

> "And what is right speech? Abstaining from lying, abstaining from divisive speech, abstaining from abusive speech, abstaining from idle chatter: This, monks, is called right speech." ([Thanissaro, SN 45.8](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html))

[Right speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20speech) is the third factor of the [Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path), the fourth Noble Truth's summary of the training, with the [Four Noble Truths](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Four%20Noble%20Truths) as the diagnosis the whole path answers. [The three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings) place the speech abstentions under virtue, alongside concentration and wisdom. The sibling factors each have their own card and dossier in this programme: [right intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20intention), [right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action) and [right livelihood](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20livelihood).

Two dispositions run beneath the abstentions. [Ahimsa](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ahimsa) is the principle of non-harm that runs through the Indic traditions, the floor under the speech abstentions: whatever else speech does, it begins by doing no injury. And [metta (loving-kindness)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metta%20%28loving-kindness%29), the Buddhist practice of goodwill and the first of the four brahmaviharas, is the disposition the [Right speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20speech) card pairs with the timing rule; the sutta's own word for its reason is sympathy for living beings.

## The abstentions, and what each one guards

The [Right speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20speech) card pairs the four abstentions with what each guards: truth against the lie, relation against the wedge, dignity against the insult, and attention against the noise.

The lie has its own research programme. [Deception research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Deception%20research) studies how often people lie, why, and how well others detect it; its everyday finding is that lying is common and mostly minor. [Interpersonal Deception Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Interpersonal%20Deception%20Theory) is Buller and Burgoon's 1996 account treating deception as an interaction rather than a one-way act: it unfolds between people who send and read suspicious behaviors. The inward case is [self-deception](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-deception), Sartre's bad faith: consciousness's permanent possibility of being what it is not and not being what it is, the lie told to oneself and half-known to be one.

Two further findings complicate the truth side. [Fluency-driven illusory truth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fluency-driven%20illusory%20truth) is repetition's subtle force, strong enough that people tend to believe what is easy to hear. And the insult turned inward has a clinical record: [harsh self-criticism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Harsh%20self-criticism) goes with psychopathology, an association rather than a prediction.

The noise the fourth abstention guards against has its philosophical and Daoist accounts. [Idle talk (Gerede)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Idle%20talk%20%28Gerede%29) is Heidegger's account of everyday conversation as chatter: the ambiguous way people talk without engagement, the gossip's interpretation of the world. [Sparse speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sparse%20speech) is the Daoist discipline of saying little, on Daodejing 23's meteorological image: nature's loudest events are brief.

## Truth-telling, and its risks

The positive side of the first abstention is a virtue with its own classical treatment. [Truthfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Truthfulness) is Aristotle's account in Nicomachean Ethics IV.7: the truthful person calls a thing by its own name, the mean between the boaster and the mock-modest, inclined to understate. The formation rules that follow from it are [A019 — practice truthfulness (aletheia)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A019%20%E2%80%94%20Practice%20truthfulness%20%28aletheia%29), describing yourself and your standing accurately, and [A030 — practice integrity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A030%20%E2%80%94%20Practice%20integrity%20%E2%80%94%20consistency%20between%20thought%2C%20word%2C%20and%20deed), the word that matches the thought and the deed.

Truth-telling that knows its cost has an ancient name: [parrhesia (frank speech)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Parrhesia%20%28frank%20speech%29) is the Greek term for frank truth-telling that knows it may cost the speaker; Foucault traced the word from Euripides through the Greco-Roman world. The Christian tradition's freedom claim has its card in [the truth sets you free](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20truth%20sets%20you%20free), the John 8:32 saying.

The modern findings complicate a simple truth-at-all-costs rule. [Prosocial honesty research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20honesty%20research) is Levine and Schweitzer's finding that receivers weight benevolence: a lie told to benefit the listener can raise trust more than a truth told at the listener's expense. And [partial truths in every opinion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Partial%20truths%20in%20every%20opinion) is Mill's argument in On Liberty that a prevailing opinion usually holds part of the truth, and the missing part tends to sit in the opinion it opposes.

The boundary card is [correspondence theory of truth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Correspondence%20theory%20of%20truth): the old question of what makes a statement true, which a speech ethics has to borrow an answer from.

Two witness traditions complete the group. [B145 — be truthful](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B145%20%E2%80%94%20Be%20truthful) is the Cunda Kammaraputta Sutta's witness scene: if you don't know, say I don't know; no conscious lie for your own sake, for another's sake, or for the sake of any reward. [M028 — guard your speech; it is your face's edge](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=M028%20%E2%80%94%20Guard%20your%20speech%3B%20it%20is%20your%20face%27s%20edge) is the Florentine Codex's father-to-son advice: carry your words moderately, improve and soften your voice, for hurried or loud speech changes how others describe you.

## The benefit and timing tests

The abstentions name what to avoid; the positive demand is that speech be true, timely and beneficial. [Beneficial speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beneficial%20speech) is speech that is true and useful to the hearer rather than merely pleasing; the Gita names such words the austerity of speech, and the Abhaya Sutta's rule states only what is factual, true and beneficial, with a sense of the proper time.

The popular three-question test — is it true, is it necessary, is it kind — is [the three gates of speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20gates%20of%20speech), with the attribution problem the card records: credited to Rumi never to a specific work, and possibly a misquote. My own name for the Buddhist check run before speaking is the [speech gate](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Speech%20gate), in its canonical Abhaya Sutta form, including the baby-on-the-lap image of why the gate exists. The six-case test the Buddha gave Prince Abhaya is the [wise speech criteria](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wise%20speech%20criteria), which holds truth and benefit apart from agreeableness and adds the time-sense.

The Abhaya Sutta's own case list, with the timing rule in the middle of it:

> "[3] In the case of words that the Tathagata knows to be factual, true, beneficial, but unendearing & disagreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them... [6] In the case of words that the Tathagata knows to be factual, true, beneficial, and endearing & agreeable to others, he has a sense of the proper time for saying them. Why is that? Because the Tathagata has sympathy for living beings." ([Thanissaro, MN 58](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.058.than.html))

And the prince's answer, when the Buddha asks what he would do if the child took a stick or a pebble into its mouth, gives the image for why the gate exists at all:

> "If I couldn't get it out right away, then holding its head in my left hand and crooking a finger of my right, I would take it out, even if it meant drawing blood. Why is that? Because I have sympathy for the young boy." ([Thanissaro, MN 58](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.058.than.html))

The five-factor version the tradition also carries:

> "It is spoken at the right time. It is spoken in truth. It is spoken affectionately. It is spoken beneficially. It is spoken with a mind of good-will." ([Thanissaro, AN 5.198](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an05/an05.198.than.html))

The kernel forms of the gate are [K208 — speech gate](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K208%20%E2%80%94%20Speech%20gate), running the words through the gate before they leave, [K044 — speak slowly, and let speech be timely](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K044%20%E2%80%94%20Speak%20slowly%2C%20and%20let%20speech%20be%20timely), slowing the utterance down enough for the gate to open and close, [B146 — test speech before it leaves](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B146%20%E2%80%94%20Test%20speech%20before%20it%20leaves), the four questions before the words leave, and [B148 — speak lovingly](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B148%20%E2%80%94%20Speak%20lovingly), the Dhammapada's counsel that hard truths can be spoken so the listener can receive them: speak not harshly to anyone, for those thus spoken to might retort.

## The listening side

Listening is the other half of the discipline. In the dialogical tradition, [listening](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Listening) is the act by which another person becomes more than noise: Buber's genuine conversation as acceptance of otherness, with Levinas beside it. The Buddhist counterpart is [right listening](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20listening), the receiving half of the same discipline.

The practice forms are various. [Active listening](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Active%20listening) is the 1957 Rogers and Farson practice: restate the speaker's meaning in your own words, getting inside the speaker's point of view and conveying that you see it. [Deep listening](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Deep%20listening) is listening with the sole intention of understanding, and its kernel is [K207](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K207%20%E2%80%94%20Deep%20listening), the twenty-minute practice. [Hermeneutic listening](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hermeneutic%20listening) is Gadamer's hermeneutics making listening central to understanding: understanding has its own rigour, that of uninterrupted listening.

The interactive kernels are [K366 — reflective listening: guess the meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K366%20%E2%80%94%20Reflective%20listening%3A%20guess%20the%20meaning), the Motivational Interviewing move of guessing at the meaning underneath and saying the guess out loud, so the correction is information, [K288 — voice-inclusion check](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K288%20%E2%80%94%20Voice-inclusion%20check), before a decision affecting a group, and [K402 — listen to the storyteller for thirty seconds](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K402%20%E2%80%94%20Listen%20to%20the%20storyteller%20for%20thirty%20seconds), the inner-speech variant: listening to your own mind's narration the way you would listen to a radio in another room, long enough to tell fact from commentary.

Three research programmes round out the group. [Communication Accommodation Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Communication%20Accommodation%20Theory) is Howard Giles's account of how speakers adjust toward or away from each other, converging in accent, pace and posture to close social distance, or diverging to mark it. [Spiral of Silence Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spiral%20of%20Silence%20Theory) is Noelle-Neumann's claim that the perception of being in a minority silences people. And [different voice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Different%20voice) is Gilligan's claim that the moral voice scored lowest by the justice tests is a different logic of care; a psychology that cannot hear it will mistake it for immaturity.

## Words that build or break the relation

How can a need be said without becoming a demand? [Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20communication) is Rosenberg's four-step structure — observation, feeling, need, request — aimed at giving conflict a shared language instead of a counterattack; its practice form is [K216](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K216%20%E2%80%94%20Nonviolent%20communication), including the rule that a request is askable and refusable. [BE051 — learn to use I statements](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE051%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20I%20statements%20%E2%80%94%20own%20your%20experience) is the assertion-training rule of owning the experience instead of assigning the blame, and [BE052 — learn assertiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE052%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20assertiveness%20%E2%80%94%20express%20needs%20and%20boundaries%20directly) is saying the need and the boundary without the aggression or the disappearance. The research behind it is [assertiveness research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Assertiveness%20research), Ames and Flynn's curvilinear finding: leaders markedly low or markedly high in assertiveness both rated less effective than those in the middle, with [assertiveness training](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Assertiveness%20training) as the structured teaching and [K215 — assertiveness and boundaries](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K215%20%E2%80%94%20Assertiveness%20and%20boundaries), the Bowers' DESC script, as the practice.

What helps when someone is distressed? [AF075 — learn emotional validation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF075%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20emotional%20validation%20%E2%80%94%20acknowledging%20a%20feeling%20reduces%20its%20intensity) rests on a theory that treats acknowledging someone's feeling as understandable in their situation as itself a form of regulation; the study the card cites qualifies the effect as emotion-specific and dependent on the person's level of emotion dysregulation (Kuo and colleagues, 2022). Its companion is [AF074 — recognise co-rumination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF074%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20co-rumination%20%E2%80%94%20sharing%20distress%20can%20amplify%20it): talking it over can also mean both people replaying it and rehearsing the grievance. And [K380 — the Two Teachers: change the tone, keep the lesson](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K380%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two%20Teachers%3A%20change%20the%20tone%2C%20keep%20the%20lesson) is the correction practice: same information, different tone, and the body can tell the difference; the contempt is the removable part.

What does repairing harm require? [K338 — restorative conversation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K338%20%E2%80%94%20Restorative%20conversation) gives the four stations of the repair conversation for harm done: acknowledge what you did, describe the impact as you understand it, express regret, ask what repair would look like. The [Integrative Theory of Communication Work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Integrative%20Theory%20of%20Communication%20Work), Donovan and Hazlett's account of communication as effortful labour, was developed for the exchanges a health stressor demands. And the research-side check on speaking about other people's worlds is [conversation as method](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conversation%20as%20method), Jane Addams's precaution: take a neighbor with you when addressing an audience about their world, so any hasty generalization is curbed by an auditor who knows the conditions more intimately.

What words do when they work is the philosophers' question. [Speech Act Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Speech%20Act%20Theory) is Austin's How to Do Things with Words: words do things, promising, warning and apologising are acts, not descriptions, and some utterances fail to be statements at all. [Dialogue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dialogue) is the exchange in which each person turns to the other as a Thou rather than treating them as an It, with Buber, Gadamer and Marcel as the classic statements; [authentic communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Authentic%20communication) is Buber's I-Thou, the direct address in which the other is met as a Thou rather than observed and described as an It.

[Existential communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Existential%20communication) is Jaspers's term for the exchange in which two people address each other as selves rather than as cases, with his claim that the authentic self does not exist prior to the exchange: I am only in communication with another. The empirical companion is [conversational research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conversational%20research), Matthias Mehl's program recording everyday talk as it happens: in the original study higher well-being went with more substantive conversations and less small talk, and the 2018 replication held the substantive-conversation link but not the small-talk half.

## Plain words, slow words, and silence

The Daoist counsel runs against ornament. [Plain words over beautiful](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Plain%20words%20over%20beautiful) is Laozi's counsel, in several translations, that true words are not beautiful and beautiful words are not true, with [D122 — prefer plain words to beautiful ones](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D122%20%E2%80%94%20Prefer%20plain%20words%20to%20beautiful%20ones) as its formation rule. [The sage teaches without words](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20sage%20teaches%20without%20words) is the Daodejing 2 companion piece, with [D135 — teach without words](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D135%20%E2%80%94%20Teach%20without%20words), the paradoxical chapter 43 and 73 counsel, beside it. The chapter 5 counsel is [D119 — keep to the center](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D119%20%E2%80%94%20Keep%20to%20the%20center%20%E2%80%94%20excess%20words%20exhaust): many words are exhausting and the useful response is not more words; [D118 — speak rarely (guayan)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D118%20%E2%80%94%20Speak%20rarely%20%28guayan%29) is the Daoist rule of rare speech, as distinct from muteness.

The Buddhist and Christian silences answer from other places. [Thundering silence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Thundering%20silence) is the Vimalakirti Sutra's most famous moment: asked for his account of nonduality, Vimalakirti answers with nothing, and the silence is the answer. [B172 — value silence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B172%20%E2%80%94%20Value%20silence) is the MN 122 measure for talk: not engaging in talk that is base, vulgar, common, ignoble, unbeneficial; silence as the standard that sorts the kinds of talk. And [the word hidden in the heart](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20word%20hidden%20in%20the%20heart) is the Psalm 119.11 entry on the word kept inward as formation, speech internalised rather than merely spoken.

Silence has its own research and its own warning. [Silence as evidence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Silence%20as%20evidence) is Peirce's warning in The Fixation of Belief: agreement produced by force is not agreement at all; let all men who reject the established belief be terrified into silence. [Silence in negotiation research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Silence%20in%20negotiation%20research) is Curhan and colleagues' experiments on extended silence: pauses of at least three seconds, dyad-level, correlate with value creation in bargaining.

The inner register of speech closes the group. [Distanced self-talk (Kross)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distanced%20self-talk%20%28Kross%29) is referring to yourself by name or non-first-person pronouns while reflecting on something stressful; Kross's studies found it enhances self-distancing, and Moser's brain-marker evidence suggests it may be a relatively effortless form of self-control, a claim the card holds as possibility. [Self-talk research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-talk%20research) is the study of what people say to themselves, its content and its effects; Hardy, Hall and Hardy's finding is that athletes' self-talk is generally positive, covert and abbreviated.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Must right words be pleasing?

The shared question: when speech is true and useful, must it also be agreeable to its hearer? The Bhagavad Gita's definition of the austerity of speech folds the hearer's ease into the definition:

> "Speaking words that cause no agitation and that are truthful, pleasing and beneficial, as well as reciting the Vedas, are all said to be austerities of speech." ([Bhagavad Gita 17.15, Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shrimad-bhagavad-gita/d/doc420281.html)). Edwin Arnold's rendering of the same verse, in [The Song Celestial on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2388/pg2388.txt), reads "Words causing no man woe, words ever true, / Gentle and pleasing words".

The Abhaya Sutta's case list, by contrast, in its third case, admits words that are factual, true, beneficial, and unendearing, to be said at the proper time, and the reason given is sympathy, not its absence. In the passages cited, the two definitions answer that question differently: the Gita verse folds pleasantness into the definition of the austerity of speech, while the Abhaya Sutta's third case admits unendearing words said at the proper time, with sympathy as the reason. Whether the broader traditions part over pleasantness is not established by these two passages alone. What remains unresolved is where the line falls in the hard case: a truth that is beneficial, unendearing, and whose hearer is not ready for it.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the rush from impression to utterance: the volume mistaken for truth, the venting mistaken for honesty, the advice that relieves the speaker and burdens the hearer, and the fluency of a repeated claim mistaken for its being so. The adaptive direction is the gap between the stir and the saying: the words run through the gate of true, timely, beneficial, and kind, the listening done before the verdict, and the plain word preferred to the beautiful one.

::Selection:: The practices I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K208 — Speech gate](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K208%20%E2%80%94%20Speech%20gate): run the waiting words through the gate: factual, beneficial, timely, kind, in that order of refusal.

2. [K216 — Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K216%20%E2%80%94%20Nonviolent%20communication): the four steps, observation without evaluation first, the request askable and refusable.

3. [K044 — Speak slowly, and let speech be timely](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K044%20%E2%80%94%20Speak%20slowly%2C%20and%20let%20speech%20be%20timely): slow the utterance down enough for the gate to operate.

4. [K207 — Deep listening](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K207%20%E2%80%94%20Deep%20listening): twenty minutes of listening with the sole intention of understanding, no advice and no rebuttal.

5. [K366 — Reflective listening: guess the meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K366%20%E2%80%94%20Reflective%20listening%3A%20guess%20the%20meaning): guess at the meaning underneath and say the guess as a statement; the correction is information.

6. [K288 — Voice-inclusion check](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K288%20%E2%80%94%20Voice-inclusion%20check): before a decision closes, the check that the affected parties have been heard, and that the hearing is on the record.

7. [K380 — The Two Teachers: change the tone, keep the lesson](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K380%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two%20Teachers%3A%20change%20the%20tone%2C%20keep%20the%20lesson): rewrite the corrective sentence in the voice of a teacher who is on your side; the contempt is the removable part.

::Retention:: The review question is practical: which conversations went differently, and which of these practices was in play when they did? A gate or a listening rule stays when you can name the exchange it changed; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### Words examined before they are believed

[The Kālāmas Test It](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST066%20%E2%80%94%20The%20K%C4%81l%C4%81mas%20Test%20It): teachers pass through the town contradicting each other, each claiming sole truth, and the Buddha tells the Kālāmas to test teachings in their own conduct and know for themselves which things lead to welfare.

[Juror 8 Just Wants to Talk](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST131%20%E2%80%94%20Juror%208%20Just%20Wants%20to%20Talk): eleven jurors vote guilty at once and one says only that he does not know and wants to talk.

### The one sentence that holds

[Ciñcā Accuses the Buddha Before the Assembly](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST387%20%E2%80%94%20Ci%C3%B1c%C4%81%20Accuses%20the%20Buddha%20Before%20the%20Assembly): a woman fakes pregnancy and accuses the Buddha before the crowd, and his one sentence, whether her words are true or false is known only to her and to him, is the whole defense; the deception unmasks itself.

[The Buddha's Last Words](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST343%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Buddha%27s%20Last%20Words): on the final journey he three times invites the assembled monks to raise any doubt or perplexity about the Buddha, the Dhamma, the Sangha, the path or the practice, three times the monks sit silent, and the sentence the sutta records as his last follows between the sal trees.

### The friends who talk too much

[Job and the Whirlwind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST290%20%E2%80%94%20Job%20and%20the%20Whirlwind): Job's three friends arrive to comfort him and instead explain at length that his suffering must be punishment, and God's answer from the whirlwind is a series of questions, with the friends rebuked for not speaking of God what is right.

### Silence that answers

[Thomas More Keeps Silence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST225%20%E2%80%94%20Thomas%20More%20Keeps%20Silence): the Lord Chancellor refuses to sign the oath recognising Henry VIII's supremacy and refuses to say why: his silence, he argues, is the law's, and his conscience his own; he is executed, and his final words on the scaffold are that he dies the king's good servant, but God's first.

[Fayan Carries a Stone and Runs Out of Words](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST359%20%E2%80%94%20Fayan%20Carries%20a%20Stone%20and%20Runs%20Out%20of%20Words): a month of new views and new reasons, each met with the same refusal, until Fayan says he has exhausted his stock of words and reason, and the awakening comes after the words run out.

### What speech does when it cannot

[Alice Loses the Words](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST276%20%E2%80%94%20Alice%20Loses%20the%20Words): a linguistics professor loses her words to early-onset Alzheimer's, and the film ends with her daughter reading to her, Alice unable to follow the plot but following the sentence.

### Speech as public architecture

[Tutu and the Truth Commission](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST227%20%E2%80%94%20Tutu%20and%20the%20Truth%20Commission): the commission offers amnesty in exchange for full public disclosure of political crimes; perpetrators testify in public, victims testify, and the two streams are recorded in the same archive.

### Whose voice counts as reasoning

[Gilligan's Different Voice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST246%20%E2%80%94%20Gilligan%27s%20Different%20Voice): working in Kohlberg's moral-development research programme, Gilligan notices that the tests systematically scored women lower, and asks whether the fault is in the women or the instrument.

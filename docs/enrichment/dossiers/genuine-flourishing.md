# Genuine Flourishing

> What does it actually take for a life to go well, and how is the genuine article told apart from its counterfeits?

I want to understand what makes a life worth living. I care about the people I can rely on, what I give my time to, the way I treat others, and how much choice I have. I use genuine flourishing to consider how these parts of a life support or undermine one another.

This is what I mean by asking about genuine flourishing. The question concerns how a whole life is going, including what makes it worth living and what would need to change. It cannot be answered only by looking inward, because the people and circumstances around us belong in the answer too.

When someone is suffering, the first question I want to ask is what would help, before asking what they might learn from it. A useful account of flourishing should help us recognise what needs care or change. It should not turn hardship into a test that people are expected to pass.

### Nearby terms

- Happiness: I distinguish feeling happy from judging how a life is going. Aristotle complicates that distinction by treating [happiness as activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20activity), [not simply a feeling](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html).

- Well-being: a broad term that includes [meaning and functioning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being), while [the two-factor model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being) [distinguishes mental well-being from mental illness](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/).

- Eudaimonia: [Aristotle's term](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonia) for [living and acting well across a complete life](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html).

- Thriving: when I use this near-synonym, I want to ask about both [real possibilities for living](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20as%20capability) and [positive mental health](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complete%20mental%20health%20model), rather than visible success alone.

## What a flourishing life asks of us

### What the good life is: the classical accounts

For [Aristotle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T002%20%E2%80%94%20Aristotle), [eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonia) means [living and acting well over a complete life](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html). The claim has three parts, and each does work. It is an activity: his account of [happiness as activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20activity) concerns [what we do with our abilities and how well we do it](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), the exercise of [excellence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Arete). It is final: [desired for its own sake](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Everyone%20desires%20eudaimonia), the [sufficient good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20sufficient%20good), so that [we seek it as the end of our other pursuits](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html). And it belongs to a whole life rather than a lucky stretch:

> "For one swallow does not make a summer, nor does one day; and so too one day, or a short time, does not make a man blessed and happy." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html))

What makes the activity good? His [function argument](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20function%20argument) [identifies the human good with using reason well in a life of virtuous activity](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html). As he argues it, for a flute-player, a sculptor or any craftsman, the good is thought to reside in the function; so too for a human being, if human beings have one. [Virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue) then develops through [repeated actions that shape character](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html): we become just by doing just acts, brave by doing brave acts.

Knowing that still leaves the practical question: what does acting well require here, in this situation? Aristotle explains [the doctrine of the mean](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20doctrine%20of%20the%20mean) through [courage: cowardice involves excessive fear and too little confidence, while rashness involves excessive confidence](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html). The courage a situation calls for is neither of those, and finding it is the work of [practical wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Practical%20wisdom), which involves [recognising what the situation calls for and choosing an action toward a good end](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.6.vi.html). His [virtue-ethical account](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20ethics) asks us to judge how to act in a particular situation. He also takes [the pleasure of virtuous action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20joy%20of%20virtue) as a sign of the character doing it: [enjoyment of the activity itself](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html).

Epicurus gives pleasure a different place. His good life is a settled condition rather than an achievement: [freedom from disturbance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ataraxia) and [settled pleasure](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Katastematic%20pleasure) make [freedom from bodily pain and mental distress](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/) central to a happy life. His [self-sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autarky) encourages [contentment with little, alongside a high regard for friendship](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/).

The Daoist teaching of [sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20with%20sufficiency) asks [whether we know when to stop wanting more](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm). [Spinoza's blessedness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Blessedness) instead concerns [the joy of understanding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/). They answer the question of what is worth seeking differently: one in wanting no more than enough, the other in the act of understanding itself.

Aristotle also places the good life within a community. In his [Politics](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/6762/6762-h/6762-h.htm), [the city](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20polis) exists for [living well together](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Common%20flourishing). His [complete friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aristotelian%20friendship) involves [people valuing one another's character and wishing one another well](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.8.viii.html). On his account, relationships and shared institutions are part of how a good life becomes possible.

### What psychology chose to measure

Psychology entered this question by trying to name the person who lives well. [Maslow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T029%20%E2%80%94%20Maslow%2C%20Abraham) connects the [hierarchy of needs](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hierarchy%20of%20Needs%20Theory) with [self-actualization](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-actualization%20%28Maslow%29): [becoming what one is capable of becoming](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm). [Positive psychology](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20psychology) later made [building a life worth living](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf) an explicit research aim. But an aim is not yet a measure: what would count as evidence that a life was going well?

One answer defines the dimensions along which a person can function well. For [Ryff](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf), [psychological well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20well-being) includes accepting oneself, good relationships, autonomy, managing one's circumstances, purpose and growth. This [broader view](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Holistic%20well-being) gives several dimensions to consider when asking how a person is doing, and it marks out the territory the [eudaimonic approach](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being) studies when it concerns [meaning and functioning](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf).

Another part of the answer concerns illness, because its absence is not the same as health. [Keyes's model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/) likewise makes [complete mental health](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complete%20mental%20health%20model) more than the absence of illness: his [two-factor account](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being) treats [mental health and mental illness as related but distinct](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Keyes_flourishing.pdf). Removing a disabling condition and having the resources to live well are not the same achievement.

That leaves the choice of what to count, and here the measures genuinely differ. [Huppert and So](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3545194/) define [flourishing through ten features](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20scale) of positive feeling and functioning; [Seligman](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf) proposes [PERMA](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PERMA%20model): positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning and accomplishment. The schemes overlap without agreeing, which is itself telling: a measure of a life embodies a view of what a good life is. I would ask what each measure lets us notice before treating its score as a verdict.

### Goals, energy and engagement

What we pursue matters alongside how we feel. [Deci and Ryan](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf) distinguish [intrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20motivation%2C%20intrinsic%20goals), such as close relationships, growth and contribution, from wealth, fame and image. Their explanation concerns [basic needs](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20needs%20theory): [whether a pursuit supports autonomy, competence and relatedness](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf). A goal can look impressive without answering those needs.

That explanation also makes room for the setting. [Ryan and Deci](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T015%20%E2%80%94%20Ryan%2C%20Richard%20M.%20and%20Deci%2C%20Edward%20L.) describe people as [potentially engaged or alienated, depending largely on the social conditions in which they develop and function](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/). I use [self-determined personality growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-determined%20personality%20growth) as my label for this connection between development and the conditions that support it.

[Vitality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vitality) is [the felt sense of energy and aliveness](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/b155eafb-778a-47b5-92cb-1c1a7ef715cb/content), while [flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow) is [energized focus and full involvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_%28psychology%29). [Vital engagement](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vital%20engagement) adds [a sense that the absorbing activity is significant](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10191-z). I am reluctant to call a pursuit fulfilling just because it holds my attention.

The question extends beyond an absorbing afternoon. [Brian Little](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2008.00106.x) proposes that a life's quality depends on sustaining its core [personal projects](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Personal%20projects), the pursuits a person deeply cares about. An occasional peak is different from being able to continue what matters through the rest of life.

[Peterson and Seligman's classification](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Seligman-PosPsychProgress.pdf) names 24 [character strengths](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Character%20strengths%20%28Peterson%20%26%20Seligman%29) under six broad virtues. For [Fredrickson](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T027%20%E2%80%94%20Fredrickson%2C%20Barbara), [positive-emotion research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20emotions%20research) asks what these emotions make possible: her [broaden-and-build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Broaden-and-build) theory proposes [that they widen thought and action and help build lasting resources, with early evidence presented as preliminary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3122271/).

[Oishi and Westgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/oishi.westgate.psychrev.2021.pdf) propose [psychological richness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20richness): varied, interesting experiences that change one's perspective, related to but distinguishable from happiness and meaning. This leaves room to ask whether an unsettling experience can enrich a life without making it pleasant.

### What gets mistaken for it

I become suspicious when a good life is reduced to a feeling to maintain, a number to reach or an image to protect. [Materialism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Materialism) tends to go with [lower well-being, although the average association is modest](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347131/). A [later study of graduates](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20161160/) found that even attaining [extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations) went with greater ill-being. Paying the bills remains a real need. Beyond that, what do we hope money or recognition will provide?

The [Aspiration Index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index) [asks what people want, how likely they think success is and how much they have attained](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/aspirations-index/). The distinction between [intrinsic and extrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intrinsic%20versus%20extrinsic%20goals) concerns [growth, connection and contribution versus wealth, fame and image](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf). It invites a more searching question than whether we are making progress: progress toward what?

Even a welcome change may not keep feeling new. [Hedonic adaptation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hedonic%20adaptation) describes [adjustment to changed circumstances, but adjustment is not always complete](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1920&context=soss_research). Monitoring the feeling can become a further trap: [Mauss and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21517168/) found a [paradox of valuing happiness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Valuing%20happiness%20paradox), in which disappointment at one's own feelings helped explain [why happiness could slip away in favourable circumstances](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20hedonic%20trap%20of%20seeking%20peak%20experiences).

[Sidgwick](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46743/46743-h/46743-h.htm) called this [the paradox of hedonism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20paradox%20of%20hedonism): the desire for pleasure can defeat its own aim when it dominates. [Frankl's response](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf) is to let [happiness come as a by-product](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20by-product) of involvement in something beyond the feeling itself. I would distinguish caring about how life feels from making every experience answer the question, “Am I happy enough yet?”

The same problem can appear in achievement. [Hypercompetitiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hypercompetitiveness) makes [self-worth depend on winning at any cost](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5972296/). The critique of [instrumental reason](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Instrumental%20reason) challenges [the dominance of calculating means and efficiency](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/critical-theory/). The question I carry from both is whether becoming better at reaching an aim has displaced asking whether it is worth pursuing.

### Meaning when life hurts

[Frankl](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf) does not give everyone the same answer to [the meaning of life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20meaning%20of%20life): the demand changes with the person and the situation. [Meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meaning) can be found through [work, love or the attitude taken toward unavoidable suffering](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). [Purpose](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose) gives that person [something still to live toward](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf), rather than a general explanation of why everything happens.

His [tragic optimism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Tragic%20optimism) allows [affirming life despite pain, guilt and death, but does not command optimism](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). Most importantly, [when suffering can be avoided, he says its cause should be removed](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). I would put that before any invitation to find meaning: alleviating suffering must not become secondary to admiring how someone bears it.

### Recovery is not an obligation to grow

[Resilience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Resilience) need not mean extraordinary toughness: [Masten's studies of children](https://ocfcpacourts.us/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Ordinary_Magic_Resilience_Process_000935.pdf) emphasise ordinary resources in people, relationships and communities. [Post-traumatic growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Post-traumatic%20growth) names [positive changes some people report after struggling with crisis](https://ptgi.uncc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2013/01/PTG-Conceptual-Foundtns.pdf), changes that may coexist with distress. I want to leave room for both recovery and reported growth without turning either into a test that an injured person has to pass.

[Taleb's concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifragility) of [antifragility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Antifragility) describes systems that improve under certain stresses within a range. That is a different claim from enduring harm or recovering from it. It is not a licence to prescribe adversity to people who are already suffering.

### Living with what cannot last

Heidegger's [being-toward-death](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Being-toward-death) concerns [taking up one's finite possibilities as one's own](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/), rather than merely living as expected. Buddhist [impermanence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Impermanence) directs attention to [the inconstancy of conditioned things](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.20.than.html). I take these as challenges to the idea that flourishing could become a settled possession, safely beyond loss.

One sense of [authenticity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Authenticity) asks for [consistency between values and outward conduct](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.629654/full). The story of [the one thing necessary](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=One%20thing%20necessary) sets [Mary's attention to Jesus beside Martha's distraction by many tasks](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+10%3A38-42&version=NRSVUE). I take from the scene a question about a single priority beneath many demands, not a rule that everyone should choose the same priority.

[Wabi-sabi](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wabi-sabi) brings attention to [austere beauty, age and the imperfect or passing](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/japanese-aesthetics/). Must a life look complete to be worth cherishing?

### The conditions: other people, society and freedom

In [Diener and Seligman's study of 222 students](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11894851/), the happiest tenth had stronger [relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Connectedness%20in%20well-being%20research), an association rather than proof of cause. [Baumeister and Leary](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7777651/) argue that [belonging](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20need%20to%20belong) is a basic motivation, met through frequent, nonaversive contact within lasting bonds.

Relationships may help form the person as well as support them. [Communitarianism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Communitarianism), including Sandel's account of [constitutive attachments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Constitutive%20relationality), [gives communities a role in shaping identity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/communitarianism/). The Nguni maxim associated with [Ubuntu](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ubuntu) says [a person is a person through other persons](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/federalism/). These accounts challenge me to ask what an exclusively individual account of fulfilment leaves out.

[Te Whare Tapa Whā](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Te%20Whare%20Tapa%20Wh%C4%81), Mason Durie's Māori model, makes the relationship concrete: [physical, mental, spiritual and family health are four walls of a meeting house, and damage to one can leave a person or collective unbalanced](https://www.health.govt.nz/maori-health/maori-health-models/te-whare-tapa-wha). One strong part cannot simply stand in for the whole.

Culture also changes what a good life is taken to mean. [Joshanloo's theoretical review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-013-9431-1) identifies [substantial differences among traditions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cross-cultural%20variations%20in%20conceptions%20of%20happiness). Nor is [fitting in](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cultural%20fit%20theory) reliably beneficial: across 29 countries, [Hanel and colleagues found that its association with well-being was positive for achievement, power and security, but negative for self-direction, stimulation and hedonism](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7554046/). What is shared matters, not agreement alone.

### The freedom to choose a life

[The capabilities approach](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20capabilities%20approach) distinguishes [real opportunities from the ways of living someone actually achieves](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/). Nussbaum's [account of flourishing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20as%20capability) makes [minimum levels of central capabilities a matter of justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/). I would be wary of praising someone's contentment without asking what alternatives were open to them.

[Berlin's positive freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20freedom) concerns [being one's own master, but carries a warning about coercion in the name of a person's true interests](https://berlin.wolf.ox.ac.uk/published_works/tcl/tcl-e.pdf). Wanting people to live well does not give me the right to decide their lives for them.

### A shared world, including the future

[Helliwell's account](https://blogs.ubc.ca/helliwell/files/2020/10/Annals_2019.pdf) gives [social circumstances](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20determinants%20of%20well-being) a central place while treating his correlational estimates cautiously. [The common good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20common%20good) asks about [the material, cultural and institutional facilities a community provides for its members](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/common-good/). From this angle, a good life is not solely an individual's accomplishment.

[Time affluence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Time%20affluence) means [feeling that time is sufficient and having a say in its use](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13414762/), not simply counting free hours. [The social gradient of health](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20social%20gradient%20of%20health) links [lower social position with worse health across the hierarchy](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@ed_dialogue/@actrav/documents/meetingdocument/wcms_161321.pdf), not only among the poorest. Advice about how to live needs to notice what a person's circumstances actually permit.

[Digital well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Digital%20well-being) concerns [how technologies affect a life good for a human being](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7417400/). Jonas's [responsibility across generations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intergenerational%20responsibility) asks that [present action preserve the future possibility of human life](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7797197/). I would not call our flourishing genuine if it depended on making a livable world less possible for those who come after us.

### Learning through action

Knowing what courage is does not make someone brave. [Aristotle](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html) makes [character development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Character%20development) a matter of repeated action: we become just by doing just acts. The difficulty is learning to choose those acts for their own sake, rather than merely copying their appearance. [Sanderse's account](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03057240.2018.1497952) of [virtue development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20development%20research) explores that philosophical problem, not an experimentally proven training programme.

I find Aristotle's distinction between [potentiality and actuality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Potentiality%20to%20actuality) helpful here: being able to do something is different from actually doing it. This is my application of his [metaphysics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.9.ix.html), not a psychological finding. Buddhist [bhāvanā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavana), or cultivation, likewise gives [practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Growth%20through%20practice) a continuing role in developing the mind.

[Maslow later criticised](https://epdf.pub/toward-a-psychology-of-beingfddacaa1d3466cbd5f4a39782d346cc289247.html) his own tendency to make self-actualization sound like a distant destination, rather than growth active throughout life. [Pursue becoming, not arrival](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pursue%20becoming%2C%20not%20arrival) is how I take that correction: a reason to attend to what I am repeatedly doing, without pretending that improvement has a finish line.

### Practices need to fit a life

A practice also has to survive contact with the person doing it. [Research on intentional activities](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lyubomirsky-Layous-2013.pdf) treats [person–activity fit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Person-activity%20fit), motivation and effort as conditions that may shape the benefit of [gratitude or kindness exercises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20activity%20interventions). A temporary lift is not yet a lasting change: [the sustainability model](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Pursuing_Happiness-_The_Architecture_of_Sustainable_Change.pdf) asks how [well-being gains](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sustainability%20of%20well-being%20gains) might endure, rather than promising that they will.

Some of the simplest practices turn attention toward another person. [Seneca](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3794/3794-h/3794-h.htm) treats receiving a benefit with [gratitude](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gratitude) as already a kind of repayment. In research on [capitalization](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Capitalization), [Gable and colleagues found that enthusiastic responses to good news were associated with better relationship well-being](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301629/). What matters in that moment is how we receive another person's joy.

Admiration offers another way to look beyond ourselves. [Zagzebski's moral theory](https://www.rivistateoria.eu/index.php/teoria/article/download/44/43) begins with [exemplars](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20exemplarism): people whose lives make goodness desirable. And [research on spirituality](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/05d717de-4f1e-4476-ac20-6b5841527af9) finds associations involving [self-transcendent emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-transcendent%20emotions) such as awe, gratitude and love. Those cross-sectional findings do not establish a cause; I take them as a reason to ask what a practice helps us notice and care about, not just how it makes us feel.

### Building well-being without forgetting suffering

For [Seligman](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T026%20%E2%80%94%20Seligman%2C%20Martin), [relieving distress and building well-being are different tasks, with positive psychology supplementing rather than replacing the work of reducing suffering](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf). I would distrust a conception of flourishing that used the second task to lose sight of the first.

### Judging a whole life, or a present moment

In [Herodotus's account of Croesus](https://praxeology.net/hero.htm), [Solon judges a life by its end](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST143%20%E2%80%94%20Solon%20and%20Croesus). [Camus's Sisyphus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST277%20%E2%80%94%20Sisyphus%20Is%20Happy) places [conscious defiance inside an apparently futile task](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/). I cannot combine those pictures into a single test: the shape of a whole life and a person's way of inhabiting the present are different things to judge.

[Frankl's account](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST279%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Meaning) of [meaning in the camps does not promise survival](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). It does not license asking people to suffer nobly when their suffering could be relieved. And in [Plutarch's portrait](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Moralia/De_exilio*.html), [Diogenes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST195%20%E2%80%94%20Diogenes%20and%20the%20Sun) answers Alexander's question whether he needs anything by asking him to stop blocking the sunlight.

These are famous scenes, but the question also belongs in an ordinary day. [Responding to someone's good news](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST316%20%E2%80%94%20How%20You%20Respond%20to%20Good%20News) brings it down to a small choice: will I make room for another person's joy, or hurry past it?

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is virtue enough, or does a good life need good fortune?

Imagine someone acting with courage and justice while losing their health, livelihood and friends. Has their life ceased to flourish, or is their character the good that cannot be taken away?

For [Aristotle](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), good action requires external goods, and terrible misfortune can damage a life. The Stoic position attributed to [Zeno, Chrysippus and Hecato](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html) makes virtue sufficient for well-being. The disagreement turns on whether good character is sufficient for a flourishing life.

Even the Stoics did not speak with one voice. [Diogenes Laertius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html) reports that Panaetius and Posidonius also required health, strength and some means of living. The [capability approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/) brings real opportunities into the assessment of a life. I find the Stoic defence of moral worth powerful, but would not use it to make deprivation seem harmless.

### Is the good life a pleasant one?

Pleasure need not mean indulgence. For [Epicurus](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html), it means freedom from bodily pain and mental trouble; for [Aristotle](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), the pleasure of good activity does not replace the activity as the good. Those positions disagree without making either thinker an enemy of pleasure.

In psychology, [Ryan and Deci](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf) distinguish feeling good from functioning well while recognising overlap. [Kashdan, Biswas-Diener and King](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760802303044) object that unclear definitions and inconsistent measures make a sharp division scientifically costly. [Waterman](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760802303002) replies that the two can be related yet reliably distinguishable. Their argument concerns what researchers can reliably measure and tell apart.

The difference can matter in a population. [Keyes and Annas's reanalysis of US data](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760902844228) found that almost half felt good, but fewer than one in five also functioned well enough to meet their flourishing criteria; those who felt good but functioned moderately well had nearly twice the mental-illness rate. Assessing a life therefore requires attention to how a person is functioning as well as how they feel.

[Keyes's earlier study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/) found 17.2% flourishing and 12.1% languishing among 3,032 US adults aged 25–74, with major depression much more common in the latter group. These were cross-sectional findings, not proof that flourishing prevents illness. [Oishi and Westgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/oishi.westgate.psychrev.2021.pdf) add another possibility: an interesting, perspective-changing life that is not reducible to either happiness or meaning. I would not want a definition of living well to make such a life invisible.

### Can flourishing be pursued directly?

Wanting a good life seems reasonable. The difficulty begins when wanting turns into continual inspection of whether the right feeling has arrived.

[Sidgwick](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46743/46743-h/46743-h.htm) argues that a predominant desire for pleasure can defeat its own aim. In his [Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10378/10378-h/10378-h.htm), Mill describes finding happiness through commitments pursued for their own sake, rather than as devices for making himself happy. Caring about a good life need not mean treating everything in it as an instrument for producing a feeling.

[Mauss and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21517168/) tested how valuing happiness could worsen responses to a happy situation through disappointment at one's own feelings. Their finding concerns emotional expectations in that setting. I would keep the broader question of how to live open, with room for worthwhile activities that bring mixed feelings.

### Which goods count, and who decides?

Could two very different lives both flourish? [Self-determination theory](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022_RyanDeci_SDT_Encyclopedia.pdf) proposes that everyone needs autonomy, competence and relatedness, while allowing different goals and preferences. On that view, what is shared is a need, not a script.

Success alone may be a poor guide. In [a one-year study of life after college](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20161160/), attaining goals of growth, relationships and community went with better psychological health; attaining wealth, fame and image did not. The question becomes what a goal serves, even when someone achieves it.

Culture complicates any easy answer. [Joshanloo's comparison of philosophical traditions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-013-9431-1) finds substantial differences over what a good life means. Nor does agreement with those around us reliably settle the matter: [Hanel and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7554046/) found that the relationship between cultural fit and well-being depended on which values were shared.

The [capability approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/) changes the question from how everyone should live to what everyone should be free to do and be. There is disagreement here too: Nussbaum proposes central capabilities that everyone should be guaranteed, while Sen resists a fixed list detached from public reasoning.

I would keep two questions open together: what must no person be denied, and what should each person be free to decide? A shared minimum need not prescribe an identical life.

## Practice: finding what to change

::Variation:: If life has become a project of maintaining an image, reaching the next number or checking whether you feel happy enough, begin with the aim. Ask what you would still care about doing if it brought no admiration, and what your present way of living is leaving too little room for.

::Selection:: Choose the question that is most alive for you and begin with one exercise. There is no need to turn every possibility into another demand.

### Clarify what matters

1. [Values clarification](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K149%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20clarification): write what you want your life to stand for in work, relationships, health, growth or community. Use directions you can live by, not only targets you can complete.

2. [Eulogy exercise](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K151%20%E2%80%94%20Eulogy%20exercise): write what you would hope to be remembered for. Look for a value you want to make more visible in an ordinary week.

3. [Desire inventory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K281%20%E2%80%94%20Desire%20inventory): list current desires, then ask which fit your values and which mainly answer pressure or comparison. Consider what you would lose, and what you might recover, by letting one go.

4. [Meaning audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K223%20%E2%80%94%20Meaning%20audit): review the month's activities and your reasons for doing them. Notice the difference between a chosen commitment and something maintained mainly by guilt, pressure or reward.

5. [The meaning question](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K351%20%E2%80%94%20The%20meaning%20question): return to what this life is for without requiring one final answer. Let a particular responsibility or possibility make the question concrete.

6. [Life review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K232%20%E2%80%94%20Life%20review): divide your life into chapters and note turning points. Ask what each chapter taught you, including what you no longer want to repeat.

### Decide what would be enough

7. [Sufficiency testing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K279%20%E2%80%94%20Sufficiency%20testing): decide in advance what enough would look like in one domain. When you reach it, notice whether the goalpost moves.

8. [Enough lines](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K155%20%E2%80%94%20Enough%20lines): write a sufficiency line for each important domain before the next temptation or comparison arrives. Keep the line available when you make a decision.

9. [Gratitude for sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K280%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20for%20sufficiency): each evening, name something already present that is enough. Stay with its sufficiency rather than immediately imagining an upgrade.

### Give the direction a place in the day

10. [Signature strengths in new ways](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K251%20%E2%80%94%20Signature%20strengths%20in%20new%20ways): identify your leading VIA strengths and try expressing one in a new way. Notice what you did, not only how the exercise made you feel.

11. [Gratitude practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K164%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20practice): write down three specific things you are grateful for, or tell the person directly. Let the thanks stay specific.

12. [Savoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K110%20%E2%80%94%20Savoring): when something pleasant happens, pause and give it attention. Do not make extending the moment a test of whether you are enjoying it enough.

13. [Evening examination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K274%20%E2%80%94%20Evening%20examination): ask what went well, where you drifted and what you will do differently tomorrow. Choose one adjustment rather than a verdict on your character.

14. [Habituation by design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K257%20%E2%80%94%20Habituation%20by%20design): choose one virtue and a small act that would express it. Make room to repeat the act over the next month.

15. [Don't-break-the-chain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K186%20%E2%80%94%20Don%27t-break-the-chain): mark each completed day on a visible calendar. Use the record to support the activity, not to make the record more important than the activity.

### Bring a longer view to a decision

16. [Future-self dialogue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K322%20%E2%80%94%20Future-self%20dialogue): write a letter from yourself ten years ahead about the decision you face. Treat it as a way to examine priorities, not as a prediction.

17. [Mortality-to-action translation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K316%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality-to-action%20translation): spend a few minutes considering the finitude of your life. Translate one thing it makes important into an action you can take this week.

::Retention:: Keep what helps you live a value you care about, and make a place for it in ordinary life. Review what the exercise changes in your daily actions and relationships before deciding whether to continue.

## Story: lives that make the question concrete

Wealth, achievement and endurance look different when a whole life comes into view. These encounters leave room for doubt, failure and goodness that receives little recognition.

### Wealth, character and choice

[Solon and Croesus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST143%20%E2%80%94%20Solon%20and%20Croesus): in [Herodotus's telling](https://praxeology.net/hero.htm), a king shows his treasures, but the sage will not call a life happy before knowing its end.

[Aristotle's Answer](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST173%20%E2%80%94%20Aristotle%27s%20Answer): [Diogenes Laertius reports](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/5/Aristotle*.html) that philosophy taught him to do willingly what some do from fear of the law.

[Hercules at the Crossroads](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST147%20%E2%80%94%20Hercules%20at%20the%20Crossroads): Vice offers an easy life and Virtue answers that good things require effort, but [Xenophon's retelling of Prodicus](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1177/1177-h/1177-h.htm) ends with her speech, leaving the choice unnarrated.

### What well-being counts

[Ryff's Six Dimensions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST297%20%E2%80%94%20Ryff%27s%20Six%20Dimensions): [Ryff challenges measures with little theoretical grounding](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf), then develops six dimensions of positive functioning rather than equating well-being with happiness.

[Seligman Renames the Target](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST136%20%E2%80%94%20Seligman%20Renames%20the%20Target): [Seligman broadens the aim of positive psychology](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf) from happiness to well-being, making room for relationships, meaning and accomplishment as things valued for themselves.

[Broaden and Build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST303%20%E2%80%94%20Broaden%20and%20Build): [Fredrickson asks what positive emotions make possible](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3122271/), proposing that they widen the thoughts and actions available to us.

### The promise of easy happiness

[Ivan Ilyich's Question](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST035%20%E2%80%94%20Ivan%20Ilyich%27s%20Question): facing death, Tolstoy's judge questions the life that once seemed proper: [“What if my whole life has been wrong?”](https://ccel.org/ccel/tolstoy/ivan/ivan.xii.html)

[Soma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST171%20%E2%80%94%20Soma): Huxley's World State offers [drug holidays from reality without even a headache](https://www.bibliomania.com/0/0/100/1956/26037/12.html), leaving little space in its busy pleasures to stop and think.

[The Positivity Ratio Falls](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST298%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Positivity%20Ratio%20Falls): after a critique showed that fluid-dynamics equations did not justify a precise emotional tipping point, [the journal withdrew the mathematical model, not the whole paper](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2013/12/model-emotions).

### Meaning under pressure

[Mandela's Twenty-Seven Years](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST033%20%E2%80%94%20Mandela%27s%20Twenty-Seven%20Years): his account describes [thirteen years of lime-quarry work](https://archives.obs-us.com/obs/english/books/Mandela/achap64.html), with glare hurting the prisoners' eyes and dust making it difficult to see.

[Stockdale and the Epistle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST193%20%E2%80%94%20Stockdale%20and%20the%20Epistle): in [his own account of captivity](https://www.hoover.org/sites/default/files/uploads/documents/StockdaleCourage.pdf), Stockdale describes drawing on Epictetus's distinction between what was and was not within his power.

[Frankl's Meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST279%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Meaning): [remembering a loved person or an unfinished task](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf) gives prisoners something to live toward, without guaranteeing that they will survive.

[Sisyphus Is Happy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST277%20%E2%80%94%20Sisyphus%20Is%20Happy): [Camus imagines conscious defiance](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/) in the condemned man's return to his rock, not a promise that recognising the absurd will make everyone happy.

[Post-Traumatic Growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST322%20%E2%80%94%20Post-Traumatic%20Growth): [Tedeschi and Calhoun describe reported growth](https://ptgi.uncc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2013/01/PTG-Conceptual-Foundtns.pdf), while insisting that it is not inevitable and can coexist with distress.

### Lives bound together

[Dorothea Brooke and the Unhistoric Acts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST144%20%E2%80%94%20Dorothea%20Brooke%20and%20the%20Unhistoric%20Acts): Eliot's [Finale](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Middlemarch_%281874%29/Finale) finds goodness spreading through a life without great public achievement, through what she calls “unhistoric acts”.

[How You Respond to Good News](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST316%20%E2%80%94%20How%20You%20Respond%20to%20Good%20News): [Gable and colleagues find enthusiastic responses associated with better relationship well-being](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301629/), making an ordinary exchange worth noticing.

[Jane Addams Moves In](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST107%20%E2%80%94%20Jane%20Addams%20Moves%20In): in 1889, [Addams and Starr move into part of an old house on Halsted Street](https://pdcrodas.webs.ull.es/anglo/JaneAddamsTwentyYearsAtHullHouse.pdf), beginning Hull-House by living in the neighbourhood.

[Mandela Invites the Jailer](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST226%20%E2%80%94%20Mandela%20Invites%20the%20Jailer): at his inauguration, [Mandela says he invited three former white warders](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1994/05/10/Mandela-praises-compassionate-jailers/4493768542400/) to share its joy, though he is unsure all three made it through the crowds.

### Years of practice

[Cook Ding and the Ox](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST001%20%E2%80%94%20Cook%20Ding%20and%20the%20Ox): the butcher's skill [follows the spaces between joints and avoids the great bones](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/nourishing-the-lord-of-life), but a difficult joint still calls for caution and a slow hand.

[Phil Connors Practises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST222%20%E2%80%94%20Phil%20Connors%20Practises): trapped in a repeating day, the weatherman [learns piano and ice sculpture and helps people around him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundhog_Day_%28film%29).

[Franklin's Little Book](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST184%20%E2%80%94%20Franklin%27s%20Little%20Book): [Franklin records faults against thirteen virtues](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/148/148-h/148-h.htm), concentrating on one each week and admitting that he never reaches the perfection he sought.

[Hokusai Asks for Five More Years](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST397%20%E2%80%94%20Hokusai%20Asks%20for%20Five%20More%20Years): in [his colophon to One Hundred Views of Mount Fuji](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_1979-0305-0-454-1), he imagines improvement still ahead, until every dot and stroke is alive at a hundred and ten.

# Genuine Flourishing

> What does it actually take for a life to go well, and how is the genuine article told apart from its counterfeits?
Flourishing is a flower word. It enters English from the Old French florir and the Latin flōrēre, to bloom, from flōs, flower:

> "From Middle English floryschen, from Old French florir (via the arrhizotonic stem floriss-), from Late Latin flōrīre, from Latin flōrēre, from Latin flōrem ("flower", noun). Corresponds to flower + -ish." ([Wiktionary, "flourish"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/flourish)).

The etymology is not decoration; it is the oldest theory attached to the word. A plant in bloom is not resting, not decorating and not succeeding at anything. It is doing what its nature builds toward when conditions allow, which is why people reach for this word rather than success or pleasure when they mean a life going well at its root. What the word suggests at first glance is close to what it is usually taken to mean: feeling good, doing well, the life that photographs well in summer.

Then the trouble starts, because half of the first-glance meanings are counterfeits, and the traditions disagree about which half. The feeling of a life going well can be bought cheaply. The thing itself, most of the classical accounts insist, cannot, and the science that measured the feeling kept finding the same gap from the other side.

Genuine carries the second thread. In ordinary use it means the real thing as against the counterfeit. Its root is lineage: borrowed from the Latin genuīnus, from genus, a word whose senses run from birth, origin, lineage and descent to kind and type, itself from a root meaning to produce, to beget:

> "Borrowed from Latin genuīnus, from genus + -ūs." ([Wiktionary, "genuine"](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/genuine)).

> "From Proto-Italic *genos, from Proto-Indo-European *ǵénh₁os n ("race"), from the root *ǵenh₁- ("to produce, beget")" ([Wiktionary, "genus" (Latin)](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/genus#Latin)).

To be genuine is, in the word's own history, to be of one's own stock: true to what one is rather than grafted onto an appearance. The two halves of the title together name the problem the theme carries: a life in bloom, and that bloom being the life's own rather than borrowed, forced or displayed. Everything I have gathered on the theme turns out to answer one of the four questions that problem breaks into: what the good life is, what gets mistaken for it, what it is made of and what it costs, and how it is built.

The large question, what a good life consists of, lives here; the affective state and its research, how pleasant feelings behave, why they fade, why they slip away when chased, lives with Happiness. The two overlap, and the overlap is a live scholarly dispute. Hedonic adaptation and the pleasant feeling itself sit mainly with Happiness; what a life is for, what it needs and what it must not be confused with sit here.

### Nearby terms

- Happiness: the affective state and its research, hedonic adaptation and subjective well-being included, live in the Happiness dossier; the classical account is [Happiness as activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20activity), and the boundary between the two is the hedonic-eudaimonic line drawn below.
- Well-being: the umbrella term the science uses; its eudaimonic side is held here by [Eudaimonic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being), its two-factor structure by [The two-factor model of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being).
- Eudaimonia: the classical root, held by [Eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonia) and the Aristotle cluster in the map.
- Thriving: a near-synonym I do not mint as its own title; the closest settled entries are [Flourishing as capability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20as%20capability) and [Complete mental health model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complete%20mental%20health%20model).

## What a flourishing life asks of us

### What the good life is: the classical accounts

For Aristotle, [eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonia) is not a mood that arrives when life is comfortable. It is [happiness as activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20activity): [a life of acting well](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), expressing [excellence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Arete). [Virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue) is [a disposition learned through action](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html), not simply a favourable opinion about goodness.

His [function argument](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20function%20argument) asks what humans characteristically do and what doing it well would mean. In [Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), the answer is reasoned activity in accordance with virtue across a complete life. The [sufficient good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20sufficient%20good), [desired for its own sake](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Everyone%20desires%20eudaimonia), is not another instrument for getting something else.

> "For one swallow does not make a summer, nor does one day; and so too one day, or a short time, does not make a man blessed and happy." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.7](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html))

This [virtue-ethical account](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20ethics) requires judgment. [The mean](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20doctrine%20of%20the%20mean) is [a fitting response between excess and deficiency](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html), not an instruction to be halfway courageous. [Practical wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Practical%20wisdom) [attends to the particular situation and a good aim](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.6.vi.html). Even [the pleasure of virtuous action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20joy%20of%20virtue) [belongs within the activity](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), rather than being an unrelated reward.

Epicurus gives pleasure a different place. [Freedom from disturbance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ataraxia) and [settled pleasure](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Katastematic%20pleasure) make [freedom from bodily pain and mental distress](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/) central to a happy life. His [self-sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autarky) means [being content with little, not dispensing with friendship](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epicurus/). I find that distinction essential: needing less is not the same as needing nobody.

Other accounts ask whether the pursuit itself has gone wrong. The Daoist teaching of [sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20with%20sufficiency) asks [whether we know when to stop wanting more](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm). [Spinoza's blessedness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Blessedness) is [an active joy of understanding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/spinoza/). These are distinct proposals about what is worth seeking, not interchangeable names for feeling pleasant.

There is also a question of scale. In Aristotle's [Politics](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/6762/6762-h/6762-h.htm), [the city](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20polis) exists not merely to keep people alive but for [living well together](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Common%20flourishing). His [complete friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aristotelian%20friendship) is [grounded in character rather than usefulness or amusement](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.8.viii.html). I do not want flourishing to become so private that the shared world, or the people in it, cease to matter.

### What psychology chose to measure

[Maslow's account](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm) connects the [hierarchy of needs](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hierarchy%20of%20Needs%20Theory) with [self-actualization](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-actualization%20%28Maslow%29): becoming what one is capable of becoming. [Positive psychology](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20psychology) later made [building a life worth living](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf) an explicit research aim. But what would count as evidence that a life was going well?

For [Ryff](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf), [psychological well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20well-being) includes accepting oneself, good relationships, autonomy, managing one's circumstances, purpose and growth. This [broader view](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Holistic%20well-being) makes room for questions a mood rating cannot answer. The [eudaimonic approach](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being) is concerned with [meaning and functioning, not simply pleasure](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf).

[Keyes's model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/) likewise makes [complete mental health](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complete%20mental%20health%20model) more than the absence of illness. His [two-factor account](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being) treats [mental health and mental illness as related but distinct](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Keyes_flourishing.pdf). Removing a disabling condition and having the resources to live well are not the same achievement.

The choice of measure matters. [Huppert and So](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3545194/) define [flourishing through ten features](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20scale) of positive feeling and functioning; [Seligman](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf) proposes [PERMA](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PERMA%20model): positive emotion, engagement, relationships, meaning and accomplishment. These schemes choose different ways to describe and assess a life. I would ask what each measure lets us notice before treating its score as a verdict.

### Goals, energy and engagement

What we pursue matters alongside how we feel. [Deci and Ryan](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf) distinguish [intrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20motivation%2C%20intrinsic%20goals), such as close relationships, growth and contribution, from wealth, fame and image. Their explanation concerns [basic needs](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20needs%20theory): [whether a pursuit supports autonomy, competence and relatedness](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf). A goal can look impressive without answering those needs.

That explanation also makes room for the setting. [Ryan and Deci](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/) describe people as potentially engaged or alienated, depending largely on the social conditions in which they develop and function. I use [self-determined personality growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-determined%20personality%20growth) as a label for this connection, not as a reason to make each person solely responsible for how they fare.

[Vitality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vitality) is [the felt sense of energy and aliveness](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/b155eafb-778a-47b5-92cb-1c1a7ef715cb/content), while [flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow) is [energized focus and full involvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_%28psychology%29). [Vital engagement](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vital%20engagement) adds [a sense that the absorbing activity is significant](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-026-10191-z). I am reluctant to call a pursuit fulfilling just because it holds my attention.

The question extends beyond an absorbing afternoon. [Brian Little](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2008.00106.x) proposes that a life's quality depends on sustaining its core [personal projects](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Personal%20projects), the pursuits a person deeply cares about. I find the shift in timescale useful: a life has to support the continuation of what matters, not merely provide an occasional peak.

[Peterson and Seligman's classification](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Seligman-PosPsychProgress.pdf) names 24 [character strengths](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Character%20strengths%20%28Peterson%20%26%20Seligman%29) under six broad virtues. [Fredrickson's research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20emotions%20research) asks what positive emotions make possible: [broaden-and-build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Broaden-and-build) proposes [that they widen thought and action and help build lasting resources](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3122271/). I read her early account as she presented it: a theory supported by preliminary evidence, not a command to feel positive.

[Oishi and Westgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/oishi.westgate.psychrev.2021.pdf) propose [psychological richness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20richness): varied, interesting experiences that change one's perspective, related to but distinguishable from happiness and meaning. This leaves room to ask whether an unsettling experience can enrich a life without making it pleasant.

### What gets mistaken for it

I become suspicious when a good life is reduced to a feeling to maintain, a number to reach or an image to protect. [Materialism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Materialism) tends to go with [lower well-being, although the average association is modest](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347131/). A [later study of graduates](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20161160/) found that even attaining [extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations) went with greater ill-being. This is not an argument against paying the bills; it is a reason to question what we expect money or recognition to settle.

The [Aspiration Index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index) [asks what people want, how likely they think success is and how much they have attained](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/aspirations-index/). The distinction between [intrinsic and extrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intrinsic%20versus%20extrinsic%20goals) concerns [growth, connection and contribution versus wealth, fame and image](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_DeciRyan_PIWhatWhy.pdf). It invites a more searching question than whether we are making progress: progress toward what?

Even a welcome change may not keep feeling new. [Hedonic adaptation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hedonic%20adaptation) describes [adjustment to changed circumstances, but adjustment is not always complete](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1920&context=soss_research). Monitoring the feeling can become a further trap: [Mauss and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21517168/) found a [paradox of valuing happiness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Valuing%20happiness%20paradox), in which disappointment at one's own feelings helped explain [why happiness could slip away in favourable circumstances](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20hedonic%20trap%20of%20seeking%20peak%20experiences).

[Sidgwick](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46743/46743-h/46743-h.htm) called this [the paradox of hedonism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20paradox%20of%20hedonism): the desire for pleasure can defeat its own aim when it dominates. [Frankl's response](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf) is to let [happiness come as a by-product](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20by-product) of involvement in something beyond the feeling itself. I would distinguish caring about how life feels from making every experience answer the question, “Am I happy enough yet?”

The same problem can appear in achievement. [Hypercompetitiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hypercompetitiveness) makes [self-worth depend on winning at any cost](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5972296/). The critique of [instrumental reason](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Instrumental%20reason) challenges [the dominance of calculating means and efficiency](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/critical-theory/). The question I carry from both is whether becoming better at reaching an aim has displaced asking whether it is worth pursuing.

### Meaning when life hurts

[Frankl](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf) does not give everyone the same answer to [the meaning of life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20meaning%20of%20life): the demand changes with the person and the situation. [Meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meaning) can be found through [work, love or the attitude taken toward unavoidable suffering](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). [Purpose](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose) gives that person [something still to live toward](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf), rather than a general explanation of why everything happens.

His [tragic optimism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Tragic%20optimism) allows [affirming life despite pain, guilt and death, but does not command optimism](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). Most importantly, [when suffering can be avoided, he says its cause should be removed](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). I would put that before any invitation to find meaning: alleviating suffering must not become secondary to admiring how someone bears it.

### Recovery is not an obligation to grow

[Resilience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Resilience) need not mean extraordinary toughness: [Masten's studies of children](https://ocfcpacourts.us/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Ordinary_Magic_Resilience_Process_000935.pdf) emphasise ordinary resources in people, relationships and communities. [Post-traumatic growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Post-traumatic%20growth) names [positive changes some people report after struggling with crisis](https://ptgi.uncc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2013/01/PTG-Conceptual-Foundtns.pdf), changes that may coexist with distress. I want to leave room for both recovery and reported growth without turning either into a test that an injured person has to pass.

[Taleb's concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifragility) of [antifragility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Antifragility) describes systems that improve under certain stresses within a range. That is a different claim from enduring harm or recovering from it. It is not a licence to prescribe adversity to people who are already suffering.

### Living with what cannot last

Heidegger's [being-toward-death](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Being-toward-death) concerns [taking up one's finite possibilities as one's own](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/), rather than merely living as expected. Buddhist [impermanence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Impermanence) directs attention to [the inconstancy of conditioned things](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.20.than.html). I take these as challenges to the idea that flourishing could become a settled possession, safely beyond loss.

One sense of [authenticity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Authenticity) asks for [consistency between values and outward conduct](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.629654/full). The story of [the one thing necessary](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=One%20thing%20necessary) sets [Mary's attention to Jesus beside Martha's distraction by many tasks](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke+10%3A38-42&version=NRSVUE). I take from the scene a question about a single priority beneath many demands, not a rule that everyone should choose the same priority.

[Wabi-sabi](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wabi-sabi) brings attention to [austere beauty, age and the imperfect or passing](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/japanese-aesthetics/). I find in it permission to ask whether a life must look complete to be worth cherishing.

### The conditions: other people, society and freedom

Who is there to share a good life with? In Diener and Seligman's study of 222 undergraduates, the happiest group had stronger relationships than the less happy groups, a finding about [Connectedness in well-being research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Connectedness%20in%20well-being%20research) that does not by itself establish cause ([Diener and Seligman 2002, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11894851/)). Baumeister and Leary's [Need to belong](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20need%20to%20belong) hypothesis gives those ties a more basic role: frequent, nonaversive interactions within lasting bonds meet a fundamental motivation ([Baumeister and Leary 1995, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7777651/)). At the level of whole societies, Helliwell's account of the [Social determinants of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20determinants%20of%20well-being) also gives social context a central place, while cautioning that his estimates rely on correlations and complex feedbacks ([Helliwell 2019](https://blogs.ubc.ca/helliwell/files/2020/10/Annals_2019.pdf)).

Having someone to count on is one condition; having a choice about how to live is another. [The capabilities approach](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20capabilities%20approach) asks what a person is really able to do and be, distinguishing achieved ways of living from the opportunities available to choose them ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/)). Nussbaum's account of [Flourishing as capability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20as%20capability) makes minimum levels of central capabilities a matter of justice ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/)). I would be wary of praising someone's contentment without asking what alternatives were open to them. Berlin's [Positive freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20freedom) introduces the wish to direct one's own life, together with a warning for anyone prescribing a better one: the language of a person's true interests can be used to justify coercing them ([Berlin, Two Concepts of Liberty](https://berlin.wolf.ox.ac.uk/published_works/tcl/tcl-e.pdf)).

The relations that sustain us may also help make us who we are. [Communitarianism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Communitarianism) gives communities a constitutive role in identity, and Sandel's [Constitutive relationality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Constitutive%20relationality) asks whether some attachments are too close to us to be treated as freely exchangeable choices ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/communitarianism/)). [The common good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20common%20good) adds the shared facilities, material as well as cultural and institutional, that members of a community owe one another ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/common-good/)). A relational understanding also informs the maxim associated with [Ubuntu](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ubuntu): a person is a person through other persons ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/federalism/)). In Tā Mason Durie's Māori model, [Te Whare Tapa Whā](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Te%20Whare%20Tapa%20Wh%C4%81), the meeting house expresses the interdependence of physical, mental, spiritual and family health ([New Zealand Ministry of Health](https://www.health.govt.nz/maori-health/maori-health-models/te-whare-tapa-wha)).

These are distinct accounts of what a person needs. Joshanloo's theoretical review of [Cross-cultural variations in conceptions of happiness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cross-cultural%20variations%20in%20conceptions%20of%20happiness) identifies six differences between the Western and Eastern accounts it considers ([Joshanloo 2014, abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-013-9431-1)). Even fitting one's surroundings is not an uncomplicated advantage: research testing [Cultural fit theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cultural%20fit%20theory) across 29 countries finds that the association between value agreement and well-being changes with the values involved ([Hanel and colleagues 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7554046/)).

There are practical limits on the life someone can lead. [Time affluence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Time%20affluence) includes the felt sufficiency of time and control over its use ([Yoon 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13414762/)). [The social gradient of health](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20social%20gradient%20of%20health) shows worse health accompanying lower socioeconomic position across the hierarchy, not only among the poorest ([Marmot and colleagues 2008](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@ed_dialogue/@actrav/documents/meetingdocument/wcms_161321.pdf)). The settings in which we spend time matter too: [Digital well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Digital%20well-being) asks how digital technologies affect a life good for a human being ([Burr, Taddeo and Floridi 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7417400/)). And the consequences reach beyond those alive now: Jonas's [Intergenerational responsibility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intergenerational%20responsibility) asks that present action preserve the future possibility of human life ([Jonas, quoted by Mangone 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7797197/)).

### Learning through action

Knowing what courage is does not make someone brave. [Aristotle](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html) makes [character development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Character%20development) a matter of repeated action: we become just by doing just acts. The difficulty is learning to choose those acts for their own sake, rather than merely copying their appearance. [Sanderse's account](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03057240.2018.1497952) of [virtue development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20development%20research) explores that philosophical problem, not an experimentally proven training programme.

I find Aristotle's distinction between [potentiality and actuality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Potentiality%20to%20actuality) helpful here: being able to do something is different from actually doing it. This is my application of his [metaphysics](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/metaphysics.9.ix.html), not a psychological finding. Buddhist [bhāvanā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhavana), or cultivation, likewise gives [practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Growth%20through%20practice) a continuing role in developing the mind. Neither thought requires a final, perfected version of ourselves.

[Maslow later criticised](https://epdf.pub/toward-a-psychology-of-beingfddacaa1d3466cbd5f4a39782d346cc289247.html) his own tendency to make self-actualization sound like a distant destination, rather than growth active throughout life. [Pursue becoming, not arrival](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pursue%20becoming%2C%20not%20arrival) is how I take that correction: a reason to attend to what I am repeatedly doing, without pretending that improvement has a finish line.

### Practices need to fit a life

A practice also has to survive contact with the person doing it. [Research on intentional activities](https://sonjalyubomirsky.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Lyubomirsky-Layous-2013.pdf) treats [person–activity fit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Person-activity%20fit), motivation and effort as conditions that may shape the benefit of [gratitude or kindness exercises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20activity%20interventions). A temporary lift is not yet a lasting change: [the sustainability model](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/Pursuing_Happiness-_The_Architecture_of_Sustainable_Change.pdf) asks how [well-being gains](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sustainability%20of%20well-being%20gains) might endure, rather than promising that they will.

Some of the simplest practices turn attention toward another person. [Seneca](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3794/3794-h/3794-h.htm) treats receiving a benefit with [gratitude](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gratitude) as already a kind of repayment. [Gable and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301629/) studied a more ordinary encounter: someone shares good news. An enthusiastic response, called [capitalization](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Capitalization), was associated with better relationship well-being. What matters in that moment is how we receive another person's joy.

Admiration offers another way to look beyond ourselves. [Zagzebski's moral theory](https://www.rivistateoria.eu/index.php/teoria/article/download/44/43) begins with [exemplars](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20exemplarism): people whose lives make goodness desirable. And [research on spirituality](https://research.dial.uclouvain.be/entities/publication/05d717de-4f1e-4476-ac20-6b5841527af9) finds associations involving [self-transcendent emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-transcendent%20emotions) such as awe, gratitude and love. Those cross-sectional findings do not establish a cause; I take them as a reason to ask what a practice helps us notice and care about, not just how it makes us feel.

### From judging a life to studying it

[Aristotle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T002%20%E2%80%94%20Aristotle) asks [what makes a human life good](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html); [Maslow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T029%20%E2%80%94%20Maslow%2C%20Abraham) asks [what people need and what they can become](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm). I want both questions available, without assuming that an account of healthy development settles every moral question.

For [Seligman](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T026%20%E2%80%94%20Seligman%2C%20Martin), [relieving distress and building well-being are different tasks](https://tannerlectures.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Seligman_10.pdf). Positive psychology supplements the work of reducing suffering; it does not replace it. I would distrust a conception of flourishing that used the second task to lose sight of the first.

[Ryan and Deci](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T015%20%E2%80%94%20Ryan%2C%20Richard%20M.%20and%20Deci%2C%20Edward%20L.) bring [motivation, development and social conditions together](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/), while [Fredrickson](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T027%20%E2%80%94%20Fredrickson%2C%20Barbara) asks [what positive emotions enable a person to do](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3122271/). These are ways of investigating parts of a good life, not substitutes for deciding what deserves our care.

### Lives that test the idea

In [Herodotus's account](https://praxeology.net/hero.htm), [Croesus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST143%20%E2%80%94%20Solon%20and%20Croesus) displays his wealth and expects Solon to call him happy. Solon refuses to judge before seeing how the life ends. The scene makes prosperity look less conclusive than it does from inside a treasury.

Two much harsher situations ask what remains when circumstances cannot be made good. [Camus's Sisyphus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST277%20%E2%80%94%20Sisyphus%20Is%20Happy) returns to the rock in [conscious defiance of his fate](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/camus/). [Frankl](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST279%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Meaning) describes [prisoners holding to a loved person or unfinished work](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf). A reason to live was not a guarantee of survival. I cannot read either account as permission to demand that someone else make suffering meaningful.

[Plutarch's portrait](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Moralia/De_exilio*.html) of [Diogenes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST195%20%E2%80%94%20Diogenes%20and%20the%20Sun) reverses a king's offer of help: Alexander need only stop blocking the sunlight. At a less dramatic scale, [the study behind responding to good news](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15301629/) follows [an everyday opportunity for connection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST316%20%E2%80%94%20How%20You%20Respond%20to%20Good%20News). I am drawn to the change of scale: the good life has to mean something when someone beside us simply wants to tell us what went well.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is virtue enough, or does a good life need good fortune?

Imagine someone acting with courage and justice while losing their health, livelihood and friends. Has their life ceased to flourish, or is their character the good that cannot be taken away?

[Aristotle](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html) needs the world to help: good action requires external goods, and terrible misfortune can damage a life. The Stoic position attributed to [Zeno, Chrysippus and Hecato](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html) makes virtue sufficient for well-being. The disagreement is not whether character matters, but whether it can be enough.

Even the Stoics did not speak with one voice. [Diogenes Laertius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html) reports that Panaetius and Posidonius also required health, strength and some means of living. The [capability approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/) brings real opportunities into the assessment of a life. I find the Stoic defence of moral worth powerful, but would not use it to make deprivation seem harmless.

### Is the good life a pleasant one?

Pleasure need not mean indulgence. For [Epicurus](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html), it means freedom from bodily pain and mental trouble; for [Aristotle](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html), the pleasure of good activity does not replace the activity as the good. Those positions disagree without making either thinker an enemy of pleasure.

In psychology, [Ryan and Deci](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf) distinguish feeling good from functioning well while recognising overlap. [Kashdan, Biswas-Diener and King](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760802303044) object that unclear definitions and inconsistent measures make a sharp division scientifically costly. [Waterman](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760802303002) replies that the two can be related yet reliably distinguishable. The argument concerns what researchers can actually tell apart, not just which label sounds more elevated.

The difference can matter in a population. [Keyes and Annas's reanalysis of US data](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760902844228) found that almost half felt good, but fewer than one in five also functioned well enough to meet their flourishing criteria; those who felt good but functioned moderately well had nearly twice the mental-illness rate. This does not make pleasure suspect. It makes feeling good an incomplete answer to the question of how a life is going.

[Keyes's earlier study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/) found 17.2% flourishing and 12.1% languishing among 3,032 US adults aged 25–74, with major depression much more common in the latter group. These were cross-sectional findings, not proof that flourishing prevents illness. [Oishi and Westgate](https://www.erinwestgate.com/uploads/7/6/4/1/7641726/oishi.westgate.psychrev.2021.pdf) add another possibility: an interesting, perspective-changing life that is not reducible to either happiness or meaning. I would not want a definition of living well to make such a life invisible.

### Can flourishing be pursued directly?

Wanting a good life seems reasonable. The difficulty begins when wanting turns into continual inspection of whether the right feeling has arrived.

[Sidgwick](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46743/46743-h/46743-h.htm) argues that a predominant desire for pleasure can defeat its own aim. In his [Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10378/10378-h/10378-h.htm), Mill describes finding happiness through commitments pursued for their own sake, rather than as devices for making himself happy. I read this as a distinction between caring about a good life and treating its contents merely as instruments for producing a feeling.

[Mauss and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21517168/) tested a narrower problem: valuing happiness could worsen responses to a happy situation through disappointment at one's own feelings. That finding does not show that pursuing flourishing defeats itself. It suggests a more useful distinction: choosing how to live need not mean demanding a particular emotional result from every moment.

### Which goods count, and who decides?

Could two very different lives both flourish? [Self-determination theory](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022_RyanDeci_SDT_Encyclopedia.pdf) proposes that everyone needs autonomy, competence and relatedness, while allowing different goals and preferences. On that view, what is shared is a need, not a script.

Success alone may be a poor guide. In [a one-year study of life after college](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20161160/), attaining goals of growth, relationships and community went with better psychological health; attaining wealth, fame and image did not. The question becomes what a goal serves, even when someone achieves it.

Culture complicates any easy answer. [Joshanloo's comparison of philosophical traditions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-013-9431-1) finds substantial differences over what a good life means. Nor does agreement with those around us reliably settle the matter: [Hanel and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7554046/) found that the relationship between cultural fit and well-being depended on which values were shared.

The [capability approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/) changes the question from how everyone should live to what everyone should be free to do and be. There is disagreement here too: Nussbaum proposes central capabilities that everyone should be guaranteed, while Sen resists a fixed list detached from public reasoning.

I would keep two questions open together: what must no person be denied, and what should each person be free to decide? A shared minimum need not prescribe an identical life.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the aim itself. The counterfeits share a shape: a good life taken to be a feeling to be held, a number to be reached, or an image to be kept up. The adaptive direction is toward the aim the classical and scientific accounts keep returning to: activity that uses a person's capacities, goals that meet real needs, ties to other people, and a sense of what the life is for.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [Values clarification](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K149%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20clarification): write what the life should stand for, as directions and not goals, across work, relationships, body, growth and community.

2. [Eulogy exercise](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K151%20%E2%80%94%20Eulogy%20exercise): write the eulogy you would want, to test the values against mortality.

3. [Desire inventory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K281%20%E2%80%94%20Desire%20inventory): list current desires and ask of each whether it fits what you value, whether it serves your flourishing, and what happens if you let it go.

4. [Sufficiency testing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K279%20%E2%80%94%20Sufficiency%20testing): decide in advance what would be enough in one domain and see whether the goalpost moves.

5. [Enough lines](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K155%20%E2%80%94%20Enough%20lines): define sufficiency for each life domain while the judgment is still cold.

6. [Signature strengths in new ways](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K251%20%E2%80%94%20Signature%20strengths%20in%20new%20ways): find the top five strengths on the VIA inventory and use one in a new way each day.

7. [Meaning audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K223%20%E2%80%94%20Meaning%20audit): a monthly pass through the month's activities with the four reason questions that tell a chosen goal from an imposed one.

8. [The meaning question](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K351%20%E2%80%94%20The%20meaning%20question): keep the question of what this life is for open as an ongoing inquiry.

9. [Life review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K232%20%E2%80%94%20Life%20review): review the life in chapters, noting turning points and what each chapter taught.

10. [Gratitude practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K164%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20practice): write down three specific things you are grateful for, or say it to the person directly.

11. [Savoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K110%20%E2%80%94%20Savoring): when something pleasant arrives, stop and extend it on purpose.

12. [Evening examination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K274%20%E2%80%94%20Evening%20examination): each evening, three questions: what did I do well, where did I drift, what will I do differently tomorrow; the Stoic evening review as a working practice.

13. [Habituation by design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K257%20%E2%80%94%20Habituation%20by%20design): one virtue, thirty days of micro-actions so small that skipping feels absurd; character built the way Aristotle said it is, by doing.

14. [Gratitude for sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K280%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20for%20sufficiency): each evening, name one thing you already have that is enough; not a new gratitude, but the sufficiency you normally overlook.

15. [Future-self dialogue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K322%20%E2%80%94%20Future-self%20dialogue): a letter from your self ten years out, about the decision you are facing; the kernel carries the Ersner-Hershfield future-self-continuity research, with the authors' own caution that the causal direction is unspecified.

16. [Don't-break-the-chain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K186%20%E2%80%94%20Don%27t-break-the-chain): a visible calendar and a marker; the growing chain of days becomes its own reason not to stop.

17. [Mortality-to-action translation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K316%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality-to-action%20translation): five minutes on "I will die. The timing is unknown. What does this make urgent?", then one insight translated into one action this week; the impermanence counterweight turned into work.

The evidence limits are uneven across the list. The evening examination is a Stoic practice whose kernel cites the self-reflection research; the future-self dialogue carries the Ersner-Hershfield future-self-continuity research, with the authors' own caution that the causal direction is unspecified; the chain technique spread from a developer's account and not from a study. The other kernels carry their own evidence notes on their cards, and those notes are worth reading before relying on any of them.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the eulogy, or the enough line, or the monthly audit has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### What the good life is: the classical accounts

[ST143 — Solon and Croesus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST143%20%E2%80%94%20Solon%20and%20Croesus): Croesus, king of Lydia, shows Solon his treasury and asks who is the happiest of men, expecting to hear his own name; Solon names Tellus of Athens, and holds that no one is counted happy until the end.

[ST173 — Aristotle's Answer](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST173%20%E2%80%94%20Aristotle%27s%20Answer): asked what he had gained from philosophy, Aristotle answers that he does without being ordered what others do from fear of the laws.

[ST147 — Hercules at the Crossroads](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST147%20%E2%80%94%20Hercules%20at%20the%20Crossroads): two women offer Hercules the easy road of pleasure without labour or the steep road of toil and lasting deeds; he chooses the steep one.

### What the science took from it

[ST297 — Ryff's Six Dimensions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST297%20%E2%80%94%20Ryff%27s%20Six%20Dimensions): Ryff's paper asks whether happiness is everything, answers no, and proposes six dimensions of well-being drawn from Aristotle, Mill and humanistic psychology.

[ST136 — Seligman Renames the Target](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST136%20%E2%80%94%20Seligman%20Renames%20the%20Target): Seligman, who had launched positive psychology around the measurement of happiness, announces in Flourish that it was the wrong target and replaces it with PERMA, each part measurable on its own.

[ST303 — Broaden and Build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST303%20%E2%80%94%20Broaden%20and%20Build): Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory gives positive emotions a job beyond feeling good: negative emotions narrow attention to the threat, positive emotions broaden the repertoire and build durable resources; joy prompts play, interest prompts exploration.

### What gets mistaken for it

[ST035 — Ivan Ilyich's Question](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST035%20%E2%80%94%20Ivan%20Ilyich%27s%20Question): a judge has lived the life that everyone around him considers correct, career, propriety, a well-furnished house, and on his deathbed the question arrives: what if the whole of it was wrong?

[ST171 — Soma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST171%20%E2%80%94%20Soma): in the World State the citizens receive soma, a euphoric with no hangover, conditioned from birth to take it at any discomfort, pleasant affect as the whole answer to the question of a life.

[ST298 — The Positivity Ratio Falls](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST298%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Positivity%20Ratio%20Falls): the 3:1 positivity ratio was popular because it was actionable and wrong because the maths did not support it; the correction came from outside the field.

### Meaning, purpose and the hard parts

[ST033 — Mandela's Twenty-Seven Years](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST033%20%E2%80%94%20Mandela%27s%20Twenty-Seven%20Years): twenty-seven years in prison, eighteen of them on Robben Island breaking rocks, the story of what a long confinement is made of, and what it is for.

[ST193 — Stockdale and the Epistle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST193%20%E2%80%94%20Stockdale%20and%20the%20Epistle): a naval aviator spends seven and a half years as a POW, four in solitary, repeatedly tortured, and credits Epictetus, the Enchiridion's teachings on what is and is not in one's power, with his survival: literally a manual for the cell.

[ST279 — Frankl's Meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST279%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Meaning): in Frankl's account of the camps, the prisoners who kept a reason to live, a person to return to or a work to finish, endured differently, a claim he limits: most of those he described died; the freedom to choose one's attitude is the last of the human freedoms.

[ST277 — Sisyphus Is Happy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST277%20%E2%80%94%20Sisyphus%20Is%20Happy): the gods meant the endless boulder as the worst punishment; Camus concludes that the struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart.

[ST322 — Post-Traumatic Growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST322%20%E2%80%94%20Post-Traumatic%20Growth): Tedeschi and Calhoun study people who have lived through major crises and find what the pathology framework did not predict: reported positive change in five domains, from appreciation of life to spiritual change.

### The conditions: other people, society and freedom

[ST144 — Dorothea Brooke and the Unhistoric Acts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST144%20%E2%80%94%20Dorothea%20Brooke%20and%20the%20Unhistoric%20Acts): an idealistic young woman marries a desiccated scholar, and the marriage is a study in misjudgement; Eliot's final judgement is the novel's gift, the growing good of the world is partly dependent on unhistoric acts, on the number who lived faithfully a hidden life and rest in unvisited tombs.

[ST316 — How You Respond to Good News](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST316%20%E2%80%94%20How%20You%20Respond%20to%20Good%20News): Gable, Reis, Impett and Asher ask what people do when things go right, and identify four responses to a partner's good news; the first response, they find, is the relationship's business.

[ST107 — Jane Addams Moves In](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST107%20%E2%80%94%20Jane%20Addams%20Moves%20In): Addams rents a mansion on Halsted Street; she does not arrive with a programme, she arrives with a residence, and the programmes come from what the neighbours ask for.

[ST226 — Mandela Invites the Jailer](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST226%20%E2%80%94%20Mandela%20Invites%20the%20Jailer): on inauguration day Mandela invites his former Robben Island warders to attend as honoured guests; the new country is to be built on recognition rather than revenge.

### How it is built: growth and practice

[ST001 — Cook Ding and the Ox](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST001%20%E2%80%94%20Cook%20Ding%20and%20the%20Ox): Cook Ding has carved oxen for nineteen years and his blade is still as sharp as if it had just left the whetstone; he never cuts through bone or forces his way through flesh; he reads the natural openings between the joints, and at a difficult knot slows down and moves with as little force as the job allows.

[ST222 — Phil Connors Practises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST222%20%E2%80%94%20Phil%20Connors%20Practises): trapped in a repeating day, Phil Connors runs through indulgence, despair and manipulation before the long middle of daily practice, piano, French, small kindnesses, done with no audience.

[ST184 — Franklin's Little Book](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST184%20%E2%80%94%20Franklin%27s%20Little%20Book): Franklin's little book gives each of thirteen virtues a page and a week, marking faults with black spots; he never became perfect, and the practice, not the perfection, was the gain.

[ST397 — Hokusai Asks for Five More Years](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST397%20%E2%80%94%20Hokusai%20Asks%20for%20Five%20More%20Years): at seventy-five by Japanese counting, Hokusai prints a plan for the decades ahead, at ninety to see further into the underlying principles of things, at a hundred and ten every dot and every stroke as though alive.

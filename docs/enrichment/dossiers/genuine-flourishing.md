# Genuine Flourishing

> What does it actually take for a life to go well, and how is the genuine article told apart from its counterfeits?

This is the flagship dossier of the compendium. Open it wanting to think about flourishing, and the whole spread of what the compendium holds on it is here: the philosophical accounts and the psychological science, grouped by the question each answers, each entry with a card link. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The boundary against happiness is set once, here: the affective state and its research, hedonic adaptation and subjective well-being, belong to the Happiness dossier on the roadmap; what lives here is the question of what a life going well consists in, and how to tell the genuine thing from what only looks like it.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. Every concept card carries onward to the practice principles that cite it, so the map needs no separate principle index.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on genuine flourishing

### What flourishing is taken to be

[Eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonia): Aristotle's term for the highest human good: a life of flourishing, of living well and doing well, rather than a passing state of mind.

[Everyone desires eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Everyone%20desires%20eudaimonia): the claim in Nicomachean Ethics I: every other good is chosen partly for the sake of happiness, while happiness is chosen only for itself.

[Happiness as activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20activity): for Aristotle, happiness is something we do and not something we have; the function argument's conclusion.

[The sufficient good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20sufficient%20good): the compendium's heading for Aristotle's account of happiness as final and self-sufficient.

[Virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue): excellence of character: a stable disposition to feel, choose and act well, acquired through habituation.

[Arete](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Arete): the Greek concept of excellence: a thing's full realization of its inherent function; in ethics, moral virtue.

[Practical wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Practical%20wisdom): the Aristotelian capacity to deliberate well about particular goods, knowing general principles and the details.

[Positive psychology](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20psychology): the turn in the discipline toward studying the strengths and conditions that let a life go well, called for by Seligman in 1998.

[Eudaimonic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being): in Ryan and Deci's review, the approach that focuses on meaning and self-realization and defines well-being by the degree to which a person is fully functioning.

[Psychological well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20well-being): Ryff's theory-guided six-dimension model of positive functioning: self-acceptance, positive relations, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, personal growth.

[Holistic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Holistic%20well-being): the compendium's heading for the same Ryff programme, taken as one thing rather than six scales.

The function argument's conclusion, in the Ross translation:

> "Human good turns out to be activity of soul in accordance with virtue, and if there are more than one virtue, in accordance with the best and most complete. But we must add 'in a complete life.' For one swallow does not make a summer, nor does one day; and so too one day, or a short time, does not make a man blessed and happy." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.7, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

The eudaimonic half of the modern line, in Ryan and Deci's own drawing of it:

> "Current research on well-being has been derived from two general perspectives: the hedonic approach, which focuses on happiness and defines well-being in terms of pleasure attainment and pain avoidance; and the eudaimonic approach, which focuses on meaning and self-realization and defines well-being in terms of the degree to which a person is fully functioning." ([Ryan and Deci 2001, Annual Review of Psychology](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

Ryff's founding complaint, and what she built from it:

> "Reigning measures of psychological well-being have little theoretical grounding, despite an extensive literature on the contours of positive functioning. Aspects of well-being derived from this literature (i.e., self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, and personal growth) were operationalized." ([Ryff 1989, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf)) — Study; quoted passage checked.

### Flourishing as opportunity and circumstance

[Flourishing as capability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20as%20capability): Nussbaum's version of the capability approach: evaluating people's real opportunities to do and be things they have reason to value.

[The capabilities approach](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20capabilities%20approach): the Sen-Nussbaum framework that shifts the question from what people have or report feeling to the real freedom they have to live ways they have reason to value.

[Social determinants of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20determinants%20of%20well-being): how people rate their lives depends heavily on the social conditions around them, not only on traits or income.

[Connectedness in well-being research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Connectedness%20in%20well-being%20research): the findings in which people's ties to others travel with how well their lives go; the very-happy study found none of them were socially isolated.

[Eudaimonic motivation, intrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20motivation%2C%20intrinsic%20goals): in self-determination theory, the goals with the better well-being consequences: affiliation, growth and community contribution over wealth, fame and image.

[Digital well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Digital%20well-being): the impact digital technologies have on a life that is good for a human being, in the thematic review's definition.

### Flourishing in common

[Common flourishing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Common%20flourishing): the good Aristotle's Politics places as the purpose of the city: the city exists for the sake of living well.

[The polis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20polis): the city-state; man is by nature a political animal, and the polis is prior by nature to the household and the individual.

[Aristotelian friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aristotelian%20friendship): friendship as mutually recognised goodwill; in the complete form, friends love one another for their character.

### The measures and their questions

[Flourishing scale](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20scale): Huppert and So's ten-feature conceptual framework of flourishing, and the measurement distinction the page keeps: their framework, Diener et al.'s eight-item questionnaire.

[PERMA model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PERMA%20model): Seligman's account of well-being as five elements: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment; the 2011 hypothesis, and its critics, are carried on the card.

[The two-factor model of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being): Keyes' answer to the measurement question: mental health is not just the low end of mental illness but its own factor.

[Complete mental health model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complete%20mental%20health%20model): Keyes' operationalization of mental health as a syndrome of symptoms of positive feelings and positive functioning.

[Sustainability of well-being gains](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sustainability%20of%20well-being%20gains): whether boosts from intentional effort outlast the lift, framed by the set point, circumstances and activities model.

[Broaden-and-build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Broaden-and-build): Fredrickson's theory of what positive emotions are for: they broaden thought-action repertoires and build enduring resources.

[Pursue becoming, not arrival](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pursue%20becoming%2C%20not%20arrival): Maslow's own correction to his earlier framing: growth as a process active throughout life, not a far goal.

### The counterfeits, and the counterweight of impermanence

[The paradox of hedonism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20paradox%20of%20hedonism): Sidgwick's finding that pleasure, pursued as a direct target, tends to defeat itself.

[Happiness as by-product](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20by-product): the claim in Frankl and before him Mill: happiness arrives as a side effect of caring about something else, and dissolves when it becomes the target.

[The meaning of life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20meaning%20of%20life): Frankl's reversal: life questions us, and we answer with our conduct.

[Impermanence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Impermanence): the fact that everything that arises will pass: the cross-tradition hub, and the counterweight to any picture of flourishing as a settled possession.

Mill's version of the counterfeit, from the Autobiography:

> "Those only are happy (I thought) who have their minds fixed on some object other than their own happiness; on the happiness of others, on the improvement of mankind, even on some art or pursuit, followed not as a means, but as itself an ideal end." ([Mill, Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10378/10378-h/10378-h.htm)) — Primary text; quoted passage checked.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is virtue sufficient for flourishing?

The shared question: what does a good life require beyond a good character? The Stoic position was stated outright. Diogenes Laertius, in Hicks's translation, reports:

> "Moreover, they hold that it is in itself sufficient to ensure well-being: thus Zeno, and Chrysippus in the first book of his treatise On Virtues, and Hecato in the second book of his treatise On Goods." ([Diogenes Laertius VII.127-128, Hicks translation](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

> "For if magnanimity by itself alone can raise us far above everything, and if magnanimity is but a part of virtue, then too virtue as a whole will be sufficient in itself for well-being — despising all things that seem troublesome." ([Diogenes Laertius VII.128, Hicks translation](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

Aristotle answers the same question with a list:

> "Still it is quite plain that it does require the addition of external goods, as we have said: because without appliances it is impossible, or at all events not easy, to do noble actions: for friends, money, and political influence are in a manner instruments whereby many things are done... the addition of prosperity of this kind does seem necessary to complete the idea of Happiness." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.8, Chase translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm)) — Primary text; quoted passage checked.

The school was not unanimous either: "Panaetius, however, and Posidonius deny that virtue is self-sufficing: on the contrary, health is necessary, and some means of living and strength" ([Diogenes Laertius VII](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html), quoted passage checked). What remains unresolved is whether the psychological findings decide the question or restate it: the social-determinants and set-point findings put circumstances back into the picture the Stoics classed as indifferent, and whether that counts against Stoic sufficiency or only against the claim that circumstances are what flourishing is, is not something the data settles.

### Can flourishing be read off how a life feels?

The shared question: is the measure of a flourishing life how it feels from inside, or does the genuine article have criteria that a satisfied life can fail? Ryan and Deci's review states the two perspectives side by side, in the passage quoted in the map above. The two-factor findings complicate the feeling side: Keyes found mental health and mental illness separate but correlated factors, so the absence of one is not the presence of the other ([The two-factor model of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being)). And the hedonic target has its own instability, in Sidgwick's paradox and Mill's testimony, quoted above.

What remains unresolved is whether the hedonic and eudaimonic measures track one thing seen two ways or two things that come apart; the compendium's boundary between this dossier and Happiness is an editorial drawing of that line, and the cards carry the evidence on both sides.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the shape of the pursuit itself: the target fixed on one's own happiness, the arrival mistaken for the goal, the external goods either worshipped or dismissed, and character treated as a given rather than something built. The adaptive direction is ends chosen for themselves, growth treated as a process rather than a far goal, circumstances worked on as instruments without being mistaken for the good, and the day arranged so that virtuous activity can actually happen.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [Evening examination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K274%20%E2%80%94%20Evening%20examination): each evening, three questions: what did I do well, where did I drift, what will I do differently tomorrow; the Stoic evening review as a working practice.

2. [Habituation by design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K257%20%E2%80%94%20Habituation%20by%20design): one virtue, thirty days of micro-actions so small that skipping feels absurd; character built the way Aristotle said it is, by doing.

3. [Gratitude for sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K280%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20for%20sufficiency): each evening, name one thing you already have that is enough; not a new gratitude, but the sufficiency you normally overlook.

4. [Future-self dialogue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K322%20%E2%80%94%20Future-self%20dialogue): a letter from your self ten years out, about the decision you are facing; the complete-life question asked from the far end.

5. [Don't-break-the-chain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K186%20%E2%80%94%20Don%27t-break-the-chain): a visible calendar and a marker; the growing chain of days becomes its own reason not to stop.

6. [Mortality-to-action translation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K316%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality-to-action%20translation): five minutes on "I will die. The timing is unknown. What does this make urgent?", then one insight translated into one action this week; the impermanence counterweight turned into work.

The evidence limits are uneven across the list, and the kernels say so where it matters: the evening examination is a Stoic practice whose value is in the tradition, not in trials; the future-self dialogue carries the Hershfield line of research; the chain technique spread from a developer's account rather than a study.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them.

# Genuine Flourishing

> What does it actually take for a life to go well, and how is the genuine article told apart from its counterfeits?

This dossier is a map, not an essay. A reader who thinks "I want to understand flourishing" can open this page and see everything the compendium holds on it, grouped by the question each entry answers, with a link to each card. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The compendium's wider aim runs through the page: to help a person tell a life that is going well from one that only looks or feels that way, and to find practices that move a life toward the first.

Boundary with Happiness. This dossier takes the large question: what a good life consists of. The Happiness dossier takes the affective state and its research: how pleasant feelings behave, why they fade, why they slip away when chased. The two overlap, and the overlap is a live scholarly dispute that this page sets out below. Material about hedonic adaptation and the pleasant feeling itself sits mainly with Happiness; material about what a life is for, what it needs and what it must not be confused with sits here.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. Every concept card carries onward to the practice principles that cite it, so the map needs no separate principle index.

- Well-being: the umbrella term the science uses; its eudaimonic side is held here by [Eudaimonic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being), its two-factor structure by [The two-factor model of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being).

These groupings are editorial; the settled distinctions, where the sources draw them, are quoted in the entries below.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on flourishing

### What the good life is: the classical accounts

[Eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonia): Aristotle's term for the highest human good: a life of flourishing, of living well and doing well, rather than a passing state of mind.

[Virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue): excellence of character: a stable disposition to feel, choose and act well, acquired through habituation.

[Arete](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Arete): the Greek concept of excellence: a thing's full realization of its inherent function; in ethics, moral virtue.

[Everyone desires eudaimonia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Everyone%20desires%20eudaimonia): Aristotle's claim in Nicomachean Ethics I that every other good is chosen at least partly for the sake of happiness, while happiness is chosen only for itself.

[The function argument](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20function%20argument): Aristotle's route from human nature to human good: ask what the work (ergon) of a human being is, as one asks it of a flute-player or a sculptor.

[Happiness as activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20activity): for Aristotle, happiness is something we do and not something we have: an activity of the soul in accordance with virtue, over a complete life.

[The sufficient good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20sufficient%20good): Aristotle's account of happiness as final and self-sufficient.

[Virtue ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20ethics): the family of views that put character first, in contrast to approaches that put duties or consequences first.

[The doctrine of the mean](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20doctrine%20of%20the%20mean): Aristotle's account of virtue as the intermediate state between excess and deficiency, found by practical judgment and not by arithmetic.

[Practical wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Practical%20wisdom): Aristotle's Book VI account of the capacity to see what is best for a human being in action, not only in general principles.

[The joy of virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20joy%20of%20virtue): Aristotle's account in Nicomachean Ethics X of the pleasure a good person takes in activity consistent with virtue.

[Ataraxia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ataraxia): freedom from mental disturbance, the central condition of the good life in Epicurean philosophy.

[Katastematic pleasure](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Katastematic%20pleasure): Epicurus's pleasure of a settled state, not of a process.

[Autarky](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autarky): self-sufficiency: the ideal of needing nothing external for the good life.

[Contentment with sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20with%20sufficiency): the Daodejing's teaching that to be content and to know when to stop is to be free of danger and shame.

[Blessedness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Blessedness): Spinoza's beatitudo: the active joy that goes with the third kind of knowledge, a state of being and not a prize handed out afterward.

[Common flourishing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Common%20flourishing): the good Aristotle's Politics sets as the purpose of the city: living, and living well, together.

[The polis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20polis): the city-state; man is by nature a political animal, and the polis is prior by nature to the household and the individual.

[Aristotelian friendship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aristotelian%20friendship): friendship as mutually recognised goodwill; in the complete form, friends love one another for their character.


One passage fixes the Aristotelian definition. It is the line the rest of the page keeps answering:

> "if this is the case, human good turns out to be activity of soul in accordance with virtue, and if there are more than one virtue, in accordance with the best and most complete. But we must add 'in a complete life.' For one swallow does not make a summer, nor does one day; and so too one day, or a short time, does not make a man blessed and happy." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.7, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### What the science took from it

[Positive psychology](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20psychology): the turn in the discipline toward the strengths and conditions that let a life go well, alongside its long work on repairing damage.

[Eudaimonic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being): Ryan and Deci's 2001 line: the eudaimonic approach focuses on meaning, self-realization and full functioning, set against a hedonic approach focused on pleasure attainment and pain avoidance.

[Psychological well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20well-being): Ryff's theory-guided six-dimension model of positive functioning: self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, personal growth.

[Holistic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Holistic%20well-being): Ryff's 1989 opening move: the reigning measures of psychological well-being had little theoretical grounding.

[Eudaimonic motivation, intrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20motivation%2C%20intrinsic%20goals): in self-determination theory, goals sort into intrinsic ones (affiliation, growth, community contribution) and extrinsic ones (wealth, fame, image), with different well-being consequences.

[Psychological needs theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20needs%20theory): the Deci and Ryan account of the needs whose satisfaction predicts well-being: autonomy, competence and relatedness.

[Complete mental health model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Complete%20mental%20health%20model): Keyes's operationalization of mental health as positive feelings plus positive functioning, treated as separate from, but correlated with, mental illness.

[The two-factor model of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20two-factor%20model%20of%20well-being): Keyes's finding that the presence of mental health and the absence of mental illness are correlated but separate factors.

[Flourishing scale](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20scale): Huppert and So's ten-feature framework and operational definition of flourishing; the card separates it from the eight-item Flourishing Scale of Diener and colleagues.

[PERMA model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PERMA%20model): Seligman's account of well-being as five elements: Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Accomplishment, which he calls a hypothesis.

[Self-actualization (Maslow)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-actualization%20%28Maslow%29): the need to become what one has it in one to become, in Maslow's 1943 statement.

[Hierarchy of Needs Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hierarchy%20of%20Needs%20Theory): Maslow's claim that human needs arrange themselves in hierarchies of pre-potency.

[Psychological richness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20richness): Oishi and Westgate's proposed third aspect of a good life: varied, interesting, perspective-changing experiences, distinct from both happiness and meaning.

[Vitality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vitality): Ryan and Frederick's felt sense of being alive and having energy available for what one cares about.

[Vital engagement](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vital%20engagement): Nakamura and Csikszentmihalyi's compound of absorption plus significance.

[Flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow): the state of energized focus, full involvement and enjoyment in the process of an activity.

[Personal projects](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Personal%20projects): Brian Little's proposal that the quality of a life depends on whether its core projects can be pursued sustainably.

[Character strengths (Peterson & Seligman)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Character%20strengths%20%28Peterson%20%26%20Seligman%29): the 24 strengths grouped under virtue categories in Character Strengths and Virtues (2004).

[Positive emotions research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20emotions%20research): Fredrickson's broaden-and-build programme: positive emotions broaden momentary thought-action repertoires and so build lasting personal resources.

[Broaden-and-build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Broaden-and-build): Fredrickson's theory of what positive emotions are for: they broaden thought-action repertoires and build enduring resources.

[Self-determined personality growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-determined%20personality%20growth): the wiki's label for the Deci and Ryan claim that people can be proactive and engaged or passive and alienated, depending largely on social conditions.

Ryff's founding complaint, and what she built from it, opens the scientific side:

> "Reigning measures of psychological well-being have little theoretical grounding, despite an extensive literature on the contours of positive functioning. Aspects of well-being derived from this literature (i.e., self-acceptance, positive relations with others, autonomy, environmental mastery, purpose in life, and personal growth) were operationalized." ([Ryff 1989, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology](https://thefpr.org/wp-content/uploads/Ryff1989_happiness.pdf)): Study; quoted passage checked.

### What gets mistaken for it

[Materialism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Materialism): in psychology, an orientation toward money and possessions as a route to personal achievement and well-being.

[Extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations): wealth, fame and image goals correlate with lower well-being; the card notes the effects are small to moderate and that the "even when achieved" part rests on a later study, not the 1996 paper.

[Intrinsic versus extrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intrinsic%20versus%20extrinsic%20goals): the distinction between goals whose pursuit meets basic psychological needs directly and goals whose value depends on other people's reactions.

[Aspiration index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index): the instrument that assesses people's life goals, used in the intrinsic and extrinsic goals research.

[Hedonic adaptation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hedonic%20adaptation): the hedonic treadmill: people adapt to good and bad changes and drift back toward a stable baseline.

[Valuing happiness paradox](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Valuing%20happiness%20paradox): the finding that making happiness one's goal can reduce it; in two studies the effect ran through disappointment at one's own feelings.

[The hedonic trap of seeking peak experiences](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20hedonic%20trap%20of%20seeking%20peak%20experiences): Mauss and colleagues' 2011 finding that valuing happiness more goes with feeling worse, mostly when circumstances are good.

[The paradox of hedonism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20paradox%20of%20hedonism): Sidgwick's finding that the impulse toward pleasure, if too predominant, defeats its own aim.

[Happiness as by-product](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Happiness%20as%20by-product): Frankl's claim, after Mill, that happiness arrives as a side effect of caring about something else and dissolves once it becomes the target.

[Hypercompetitiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hypercompetitiveness): Horney's construct: an exaggerated need to compete and win, with self-worth riding on the outcome.

[Instrumental reason](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Instrumental%20reason): reason reduced to the choice of means, which has stopped asking whether what it pursues is worth wanting.

### Meaning, purpose and the hard parts

[Meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meaning): the sense that life is about something: purpose, coherence and mattering; Frankl made it the primary human drive.

[The meaning of life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20meaning%20of%20life): Frankl's reversal in Man's Search for Meaning: the question cannot be answered in general, because life questions us and we answer with our conduct.

[Purpose](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose): an aim or reason for carrying on, with Frankl's camp account of what a psychologist could actually do for prisoners.

[Tragic optimism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Tragic%20optimism): Frankl's name for saying yes to life in spite of what he called the tragic triad.

[Post-traumatic growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Post-traumatic%20growth): Bonanno's finding that resilience is common, set beside Tedeschi and Calhoun's growth findings.

[Resilience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Resilience): positive adaptation in the context of adversity; Masten's conclusion is how ordinary it is.

[Antifragility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Antifragility): Taleb's third category beyond fragile and robust: the antifragile gets better under stress.

[Being-toward-death](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Being-toward-death): Heidegger's stance in which a person takes ownership of finitude instead of fleeing it.

[Impermanence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Impermanence): the fact that everything that arises will pass: the cross-tradition hub, and the counterweight to any picture of flourishing as a settled possession.

[Authenticity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Authenticity): the congruent relationship between what a person presents outwardly and what they hold inwardly.

[One thing necessary](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=One%20thing%20necessary): Luke 10:42: a single priority beneath many demands.

[Wabi-sabi](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wabi-sabi): the Japanese aesthetic of the imperfect, impermanent and incomplete.

### The conditions: other people, society and freedom

[Connectedness in well-being research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Connectedness%20in%20well-being%20research): Diener and Seligman's 2002 study of very happy people, in whom ties to others travelled with how well life went.

[The need to belong](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20need%20to%20belong): Baumeister and Leary's hypothesis that the drive for social attachment is a fundamental human motivation.

[Social determinants of well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20determinants%20of%20well-being): the idea that how people rate their lives depends heavily on the social conditions around them, not only on traits or income.

[Flourishing as capability](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flourishing%20as%20capability): Nussbaum's version of the capability approach: judge a life by the real opportunities a person has to do and be what they have reason to value.

[The capabilities approach](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20capabilities%20approach): Sen's and Nussbaum's way of evaluating how well a life is going, and how fair a society is, by what people are able to do and be.

[Positive freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20freedom): freedom as self-mastery: the presence of a self in charge of its own life, not only the absence of interference.

[The common good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20common%20good): what a community owes to all its members, in the Stanford Encyclopedia's working definition.

[Communitarianism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Communitarianism): the view that human identities are largely shaped by constitutive communities.

[Constitutive relationality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Constitutive%20relationality): Sandel's thought that the self is made by its ties and not only accompanied by them.

[Ubuntu](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ubuntu): the Nguni word for humanness: a person is a person through other persons.

[Te Whare Tapa Whā](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Te%20Whare%20Tapa%20Wh%C4%81): Mason Durie's Māori model of wellbeing as a meeting house with four walls, all of which must stand.

[Cross-cultural variations in conceptions of happiness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cross-cultural%20variations%20in%20conceptions%20of%20happiness): Joshanloo's review of Western and Eastern conceptions of the good life, identifying six fundamental differences.

[Cultural fit theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cultural%20fit%20theory): the claim that well-being tracks how well a person's values match the values around them; Hanel and colleagues tested it in 29 countries.

[Time affluence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Time%20affluence): the subjective sense of having enough time, with autonomy over its use, associated with well-being independently of income.

[The social gradient of health](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20social%20gradient%20of%20health): health tracks social position at every rung of the ladder, not only at the bottom.

[Intergenerational responsibility](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intergenerational%20responsibility): the duties owed to people who do not yet exist, in Jonas's account.

[Digital well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Digital%20well-being): the impact of digital technologies on a life that is good for a human being.

### How it is built: growth and practice

[Character development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Character%20development): Aristotle's account of habituation: we become virtuous by doing the acts.

[Virtue development research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20development%20research): the study of how stable virtuous traits are acquired; Sanderse on the neo-Aristotelian mechanism of habituation.

[Potentiality to actuality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Potentiality%20to%20actuality): Aristotle's distinction between a thing's capacity to become and its being in act.

[Growth through practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Growth%20through%20practice): the Buddhist theory of change: qualities are not given but cultivated (bhavana).

[Pursue becoming, not arrival](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pursue%20becoming%2C%20not%20arrival): Maslow's own correction to his earlier framing: growth as a process active throughout life, not a far goal.

[Sustainability of well-being gains](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sustainability%20of%20well-being%20gains): whether boosts in happiness from intentional effort outlast the lift, in the Lyubomirsky, Sheldon and Schkade model.

[Person-activity fit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Person-activity%20fit): the degree to which a well-being practice suits the person doing it.

[Positive activity interventions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20activity%20interventions): simple intentional practices, such as gratitude and kindness, meant to raise well-being.

[Gratitude](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gratitude): the felt recognition of a benefit received, with Seneca and Aristotle as classical anchors.

[Capitalization](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Capitalization): how you respond to others' good news predicts relationship quality (Gable and colleagues, 2004).

[Self-transcendent emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-transcendent%20emotions): awe, gratitude, love and peace carry the link between spirituality and well-being.

[Moral exemplarism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20exemplarism): Zagzebski's theory that morality is built from the people we find supremely admirable.

### The thinkers

[Aristotle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T002%20%E2%80%94%20Aristotle): Greek philosopher, student of Plato and founder of the Lyceum; author of the Nicomachean Ethics, quoted above.

[Ryan and Deci](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T015%20%E2%80%94%20Ryan%2C%20Richard%20M.%20and%20Deci%2C%20Edward%20L.): the self-determination theory authors, whose 2000 American Psychologist article ties motivation, development and well-being together.

[Seligman](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T026%20%E2%80%94%20Seligman%2C%20Martin): director of the Positive Psychology Center at the University of Pennsylvania; in 1998, as president of the American Psychological Association, he turned the field toward what makes life worth living; author of the PERMA account.

[Fredrickson](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T027%20%E2%80%94%20Fredrickson%2C%20Barbara): principal investigator of the Positive Emotions and Psychophysiology Laboratory; builder of the broaden-and-build account.

[Maslow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T029%20%E2%80%94%20Maslow%2C%20Abraham): founded humanistic psychology and gave the study of flourishing its most famous image, the hierarchy of needs.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is virtue enough, or does a good life need good fortune?

The shared question: if a person is fully good, can anything still stop their life going well? Aristotle says yes, something can. His account of happiness needs the support of the world:

> "Yet evidently, as we said, it needs the external goods as well; for it is impossible, or not easy, to do noble acts without the proper equipment. In many actions we use friends and riches and political power as instruments" ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics I.8, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

The Stoics say no. Diogenes Laertius reports their school's position and, within it, a dissent:

> "Moreover, they hold that it is in itself sufficient to ensure well-being: thus Zeno, and Chrysippus in the first book of his treatise On Virtues , and Hecato in the second book of his treatise On Goods" ([Diogenes Laertius, Lives VII.127-128](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

> "For if magnanimity by itself alone can raise us far above everything, and if magnanimity is but a part of virtue, then too virtue as a whole will be sufficient in itself for well-being — despising all things that seem troublesome." ([Diogenes Laertius, Lives VII.128](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html)): Primary text; quoted passage checked.

> "Panaetius, however, and Posidonius deny that virtue is self-sufficing: on the contrary, health is necessary, and some means of living and strength." ([Diogenes Laertius, Lives VII.128](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/7/Zeno*.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

Both sides agree that character is the centre of a good life. They part on whether that centre can stand alone. The reported Stoic split matters for a modern reader: even inside the school that made sufficiency famous, later members pulled back toward Aristotle's position. The capability approach carries the dispute into policy, because it counts the real opportunities a person has and not only the state they end up in. What remains open is how much loss a life can absorb and still be called flourishing. Aristotle's own line about the swallow, quoted above, shows why he will not call a life happy on the strength of one good day.

### Is the good life a pleasant one?

The shared question: what does "going well" feel like, and does feeling good count as the thing itself? Epicurus gives pleasure the leading role, then refuses the crude reading of it:

> "By pleasure we mean the absence of pain in the body and of trouble in the soul." ([Epicurus, Letter to Menoeceus, Hicks translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/menoec.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

Aristotle's definition above makes the good an activity of the soul, and treats the pleasure a good person takes as something that goes with virtuous activity. Modern psychology took the split over as two research traditions. Ryan and Deci's review set it out:

> "Thus, from the eudaimonic perspective, subjective happiness cannot be equated with well-being." ([Ryan and Deci, On Happiness and Human Potentials, Annual Review of Psychology 2001](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked.

> "These two views have given rise to different research foci and a body of knowledge that is in some areas divergent and in others complementary." ([Ryan and Deci 2001, abstract](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2001_RyanDeci_Happiness.pdf)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked.

Whether the line between the two is real has itself been argued in print. Kashdan, Biswas-Diener and King say it does not hold up as science:

> "Unfortunately, this distinction (rooted in philosophy) does not necessarily translate well to science." ([Kashdan, Biswas-Diener and King 2008, abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760802303044)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked.

Waterman replies that the two are "inter-related but reliably distinguishable and qualitatively distinct conceptions of happiness" ([Waterman 2008, abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760802303002)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. Keyes and Annas add data: in a national sample, 48.5% had high hedonic well-being, only 18% were flourishing, and the 30.5% who felt good but functioned only moderately well had nearly twice the rate of mental illness as the flourishing group ([Keyes and Annas 2009, abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17439760902844228)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. Keyes's earlier work found flourishing in 17.2 percent of a sample and languishing in 12.1 percent, with the risk of a major depressive episode nearly six times greater among the languishing than among the flourishing ([Keyes 2002, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12096700/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. The disagreement is partly about words and partly about evidence, and it is not settled. Oishi and Westgate then argue that both camps leave something out, proposing "psychological richness", lives "characterized by a variety of interesting and perspective-changing experiences" ([Oishi and Westgate 2022, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34383524/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked.

### Can flourishing be pursued directly?

The shared question: if the good life is what people want, why not aim straight at it? Sidgwick, writing about pleasure, saw a trap in the aim:

> "Here comes into view what we may call the fundamental paradox of Hedonism, that the impulse towards pleasure, if too predominant, defeats its own aim." ([Sidgwick, The Methods of Ethics, Book I, Ch. IV, section 2](https://www.laits.utexas.edu/poltheory/sidgwick/me/me.b01.c04.s02.html)): Primary text; quoted passage checked.

Mill reached the same trap from his own life:

> "Those only are happy (I thought) who have their minds fixed on some object other than their own happiness; on the happiness of others, on the improvement of mankind, even on some art or pursuit, followed not as a means, but as itself an ideal end." ([Mill, Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10378/10378-h/10378-h.htm)): Primary text; quoted passage checked.

Experimental psychology found a version of the trap. In Mauss and colleagues' two studies, valuing happiness more went with lower happiness under low life stress, and participants induced to value happiness reacted less positively to a happy emotion induction, an effect mediated by disappointment: "valuing happiness may lead people to be less happy just when happiness is within reach" ([Mauss and colleagues 2011, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21517168/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. The studies used female participants and measured happiness, not flourishing as a whole, so the result does not show that aiming at a good life defeats it. It does say that aiming at a feeling can. The eudaimonic answer is to aim at the activities and goals that a good life is made of, which leads to the next question.

### Which goods count, and who decides?

The shared question: is there a content to flourishing that holds for everyone, or does it depend on person and place? Self-determination theory takes the universal side:

> "SDT also postulates that there are basic psychological needs that universally must be satisfied for people to experience ongoing growth, integrity, and wellness, namely needs for competence, autonomy, and relatedness." ([Ryan and Deci, SDT entry, Encyclopedia of Quality of Life and Well-Being Research](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022_RyanDeci_SDT_Encyclopedia.pdf)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked.

Its goal research adds that the content of what a person pursues matters. In a one-year study of post-college adults, attaining intrinsic aspirations related positively to psychological health, while "attainment of extrinsic aspirations related positively to indicators of ill-being" ([Niemiec, Ryan and Deci 2009, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20161160/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. The cross-cultural side complicates the picture. Joshanloo compared Western accounts with Hinduism, Buddhism, Taoism, Confucianism and Sufism and identified "six fundamental differences in western and eastern conceptualizations of the good life" ([Joshanloo review, abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10902-013-9431-1)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. Across 29 countries, Hanel and colleagues found that value type moderated the effect of fit: people who valued self-direction, stimulation and hedonism more and lived where others did too reported lower well-being, while those who valued achievement, power and security more and lived where others did too reported higher well-being ([Hanel and colleagues 2020, abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7554046/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. The capability approach offers a third route, defining well-being by freedoms and not by a list of states: "Capabilities are the doings and beings that people can achieve if they so choose" ([Stanford Encyclopedia, The Capability Approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/)): Secondary source; quoted passage checked. Even Nussbaum's list names ten central capabilities, from life and bodily health to play and control over one's environment, so the approach is neither empty nor culture-free. What remains unresolved is how much of flourishing is universal and how much is local; the evidence above supports some of each.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the aim itself. The counterfeits share a shape: a good life taken to be a feeling to be held, a number to be reached, or an image to be kept up. The adaptive direction is toward the aim the classical and scientific accounts keep returning to: activity that uses a person's capacities, goals that meet real needs, ties to other people, and a sense of what the life is for.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [Values clarification](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K149%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20clarification): write what the life should stand for, as directions and not goals, across work, relationships, body, growth and community.

2. [Eulogy exercise](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K151%20%E2%80%94%20Eulogy%20exercise): write the eulogy you would want, to test the values against mortality.

3. [Desire inventory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K281%20%E2%80%94%20Desire%20inventory): list current desires and ask of each whether it fits what you value, whether it serves your flourishing, and what happens if you let it go.

4. [Sufficiency testing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K279%20%E2%80%94%20Sufficiency%20testing): decide in advance what would be enough in one domain and see whether the goalpost moves.

5. [Enough lines](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K155%20%E2%80%94%20Enough%20lines): define sufficiency for each life domain while the judgment is still cold.

6. [Signature strengths in new ways](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K251%20%E2%80%94%20Signature%20strengths%20in%20new%20ways): find the top five strengths on the VIA inventory and use one in a new way each day.

7. [Meaning audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K223%20%E2%80%94%20Meaning%20audit): a monthly pass through the month's activities with the four reason questions that tell a chosen goal from an imposed one.

8. [The meaning question](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K351%20%E2%80%94%20The%20meaning%20question): keep the question of what this life is for open as an ongoing inquiry.

9. [Life review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K232%20%E2%80%94%20Life%20review): review the life in chapters, noting turning points and what each chapter taught.

10. [Gratitude practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K164%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20practice): write down three specific things you are grateful for, or say it to the person directly.

11. [Savoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K110%20%E2%80%94%20Savoring): when something pleasant arrives, stop and extend it on purpose.

12. [Evening examination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K274%20%E2%80%94%20Evening%20examination): each evening, three questions: what did I do well, where did I drift, what will I do differently tomorrow; the Stoic evening review as a working practice.

13. [Habituation by design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K257%20%E2%80%94%20Habituation%20by%20design): one virtue, thirty days of micro-actions so small that skipping feels absurd; character built the way Aristotle said it is, by doing.

14. [Gratitude for sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K280%20%E2%80%94%20Gratitude%20for%20sufficiency): each evening, name one thing you already have that is enough; not a new gratitude, but the sufficiency you normally overlook.

15. [Future-self dialogue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K322%20%E2%80%94%20Future-self%20dialogue): a letter from your self ten years out, about the decision you are facing; the kernel carries the Ersner-Hershfield future-self-continuity research, with the authors' own caution that the causal direction is unspecified.

16. [Don't-break-the-chain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K186%20%E2%80%94%20Don%27t-break-the-chain): a visible calendar and a marker; the growing chain of days becomes its own reason not to stop.

17. [Mortality-to-action translation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K316%20%E2%80%94%20Mortality-to-action%20translation): five minutes on "I will die. The timing is unknown. What does this make urgent?", then one insight translated into one action this week; the impermanence counterweight turned into work.

The evidence limits are uneven across the list. The evening examination is a Stoic practice whose kernel cites the self-reflection research; the future-self dialogue carries the Ersner-Hershfield future-self-continuity research, with the authors' own caution that the causal direction is unspecified; the chain technique spread from a developer's account and not from a study. The other kernels carry their own evidence notes on their cards, and those notes are worth reading before relying on any of them.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the eulogy, or the enough line, or the monthly audit has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The compendium's stories, retold in a line: the ones that best carry this theme's aspects. Each line gives the story's own turn, compressed from the story card; the full telling lives on the card.

### What the good life is: the classical accounts

[ST143 — Solon and Croesus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST143%20%E2%80%94%20Solon%20and%20Croesus): Croesus, king of Lydia, shows Solon his treasury and asks who is the happiest of men, expecting to hear his own name; Solon names Tellus of Athens, and holds that no one is counted happy until the end.

[ST173 — Aristotle's Answer](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST173%20%E2%80%94%20Aristotle%27s%20Answer): asked what he had gained from philosophy, Aristotle answers that he does without being ordered what others do from fear of the laws.

[ST147 — Hercules at the Crossroads](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST147%20%E2%80%94%20Hercules%20at%20the%20Crossroads): two women offer Hercules the easy road of pleasure without labour or the steep road of toil and lasting deeds; he chooses the steep one.

### What the science took from it

[ST297 — Ryff's Six Dimensions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST297%20%E2%80%94%20Ryff%27s%20Six%20Dimensions): Ryff attacks the equation of well-being with happiness and proposes six dimensions drawn from Aristotle, Mill and humanistic psychology; her scales remain the standard instruments.

[ST136 — Seligman Renames the Target](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST136%20%E2%80%94%20Seligman%20Renames%20the%20Target): Seligman announces that happiness was the wrong target, too thin and too dependent on mood, and replaces it with PERMA, each part measurable on its own.

[ST303 — Broaden and Build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST303%20%E2%80%94%20Broaden%20and%20Build): on Fredrickson's account negative emotions narrow attention to the threat while positive emotions broaden the repertoire and build durable resources; joy prompts play, interest prompts exploration.

### What gets mistaken for it

[ST035 — Ivan Ilyich's Question](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST035%20%E2%80%94%20Ivan%20Ilyich%27s%20Question): a judge has lived the life that everyone around him considers correct — career, propriety, a well-furnished house — and on his deathbed the question arrives: what if the whole of it was wrong?

[ST171 — Soma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST171%20%E2%80%94%20Soma): in the World State the citizens receive soma, a euphoric with no hangover, conditioned from birth to take it at any discomfort — pleasant affect as the whole answer to the question of a life.

[ST298 — The Positivity Ratio Falls](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST298%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Positivity%20Ratio%20Falls): the 3:1 positivity ratio was popular because it was actionable and wrong because the maths did not support it; the correction came from outside the field.

### Meaning, purpose and the hard parts

[ST033 — Mandela's Twenty-Seven Years](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST033%20%E2%80%94%20Mandela%27s%20Twenty-Seven%20Years): twenty-seven years in prison, eighteen of them on Robben Island breaking rocks — the story of what a long confinement is made of, and what it is for.

[ST193 — Stockdale and the Epistle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST193%20%E2%80%94%20Stockdale%20and%20the%20Epistle): a naval aviator spends seven and a half years as a POW, four in solitary, repeatedly tortured, and credits Epictetus — the Enchiridion's teachings on what is and is not in one's power — with his survival: literally a manual for the cell.

[ST279 — Frankl's Meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST279%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Meaning): in the camps, the prisoners who kept a reason to live, a person to return to or a work to finish, endured differently; the freedom to choose one's attitude is the last of the human freedoms.

[ST277 — Sisyphus Is Happy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST277%20%E2%80%94%20Sisyphus%20Is%20Happy): the gods meant the endless boulder as the worst punishment; Camus concludes that the struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart.

[ST322 — Post-Traumatic Growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST322%20%E2%80%94%20Post-Traumatic%20Growth): a significant minority of people who live through major crises report positive change in five domains, from appreciation of life to spiritual change.

### The conditions: other people, society and freedom

[ST144 — Dorothea Brooke and the Unhistoric Acts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST144%20%E2%80%94%20Dorothea%20Brooke%20and%20the%20Unhistoric%20Acts): an idealistic young woman marries a desiccated scholar, and Eliot's finale gives the verdict: the growing good of the world rests on unhistoric acts, and on persons half buried in the privacy of ordinary lives.

[ST316 — How You Respond to Good News](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST316%20%E2%80%94%20How%20You%20Respond%20to%20Good%20News): the response to a partner's good news predicts relationship quality as strongly as the response to bad news; the first response is the relationship's business.

[ST107 — Jane Addams Moves In](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST107%20%E2%80%94%20Jane%20Addams%20Moves%20In): Addams rents a mansion on Halsted Street; she does not arrive with a programme, she arrives with a residence, and the programmes come from what the neighbours ask for.

[ST226 — Mandela Invites the Jailer](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST226%20%E2%80%94%20Mandela%20Invites%20the%20Jailer): on inauguration day Mandela seats his former Robben Island warders as honoured guests; the new country is to be built on recognition rather than revenge.

### How it is built: growth and practice

[ST001 — Cook Ding and the Ox](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST001%20%E2%80%94%20Cook%20Ding%20and%20the%20Ox): Cook Ding has carved oxen for nineteen years and his blade is still as sharp as if it had just left the whetstone; the carving follows the ox's own lines — the skill, not the strength, does the work.

[ST222 — Phil Connors Practises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST222%20%E2%80%94%20Phil%20Connors%20Practises): trapped in a repeating day, Phil Connors runs through indulgence, despair and manipulation before the long middle of daily practice, piano, French, small kindnesses, done with no audience.

[ST184 — Franklin's Little Book](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST184%20%E2%80%94%20Franklin%27s%20Little%20Book): Franklin's little book gives each of thirteen virtues a page and a week, marking faults with black spots; he never became perfect, and the practice, not the perfection, was the gain.

[ST397 — Hokusai Asks for Five More Years](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST397%20%E2%80%94%20Hokusai%20Asks%20for%20Five%20More%20Years): at seventy-five Hokusai prints a plan for the decades ahead, at ninety to see further into the underlying principles of things, at a hundred and ten every dot and every stroke as though alive.

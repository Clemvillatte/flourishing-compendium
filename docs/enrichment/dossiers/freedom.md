# Freedom

> What is a person fleeing when she flees her own freedom, and what does she exercise when she takes it back?

On the accounts gathered here, freedom arrives with a cost built in: to be free is to be the one who has to choose, and much of what people do with their freedom is look for ways to give it back. The accounts in this map divide on exactly that point. The agency-side, the capacity to act from one's own understanding, Berlin's positive freedom and Frankfurt's second-order desires, is held by Agency. The felt side, Kierkegaard's dizziness before possibility, is held by Anxiety. What neither held is the flight-and-will wing: the analyses of why people give their freedom away, and the accounts of freedom as an exercise performed rather than a status possessed. That wing is this dossier's territory, with the two boundaries stated at the edges and held throughout.

## Nearby terms

- Positive freedom: Berlin's wish to be one's own master; mapped by Agency.
- The dizziness of freedom: Kierkegaard's image for anxiety before possibility; mapped by Anxiety, whose dossier holds the Kierkegaardian wing.
- Second-order desires: Frankfurt's endorsement structure, the person as the one who can disown a desire; mapped by Agency.
- Autonomy: the agency-side word for much the same territory; see Agency.
- Bad faith: Sartre's name for the flight; the story group below carries its most famous example.

## The map: the entries on freedom

### The flight: why freedom is given away

1. [Escape from freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Escape%20from%20freedom): Fromm's diagnosis of the modern problem, negative freedom arrived before the capacities to bear it, with the escape mechanisms the work enumerates, especially automaton conformity, the adoption of the offered personality:

> "To put it briefly, the individual ceases to be himself; he adopts entirely the kind of personality offered to him by cultural patterns; and he therefore becomes exactly as all others are and as they expect him to be. ... But the price he pays, however, is high; it is the loss of his self." ([Fromm, Escape from Freedom, panarchy excerpt](https://www.panarchy.org/fromm/escapefreedom.html)): Primary text; both sentences checked against the excerpt this session.

2. [ST269 — The Waiter's Dance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST269%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Waiter%27s%20Dance): Sartre's café waiter whose movements are too exact, too quick, too waiter-like; the performance of the role with excess precision as the flight from the responsibility of having chosen it, and the card's diagnostic, that a role performed with too much precision is usually such a flight, with the remedy of catching oneself mid-gesture.

3. [Psychological reactance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Psychological%20reactance): the measurable face of the flight-and-will line, Brehm's motivational state: the boomerang effect, the anti-drinking message that produces a drink, and the sideways restorations, talking up the liking of what was threatened, derogating the source, denying the threat.

### The exercise: freedom as what a person does

4. [Radical freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Radical%20freedom): Sartre's account, the claim that nothing but our own choices conditions our freedom, argued through the impossibility of giving up freedom, since giving it up is itself a choice; the formula the card carries is being condemned to be free.

5. [Anguish (angoisse)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anguish%20%28angoisse%29): the affective signature of the same account, the vertigo at the cliff edge, fear not of falling but of throwing oneself over, and the disclosure it carries: no past resolution and no external constraint stands between the person and the act, and that gap is what the tradition calls freedom.

6. [Situated freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Situated%20freedom): the critique that keeps the account honest, freedom exercised within limits not chosen by the chooser, with the constraint-provision insight that the bodies, habits and situations we chafe against are the very things that give weight and traction to a free act; nothing is done all by itself, and neither is anything done all alone.

### The political edges

7. [Natural liberty versus the chains](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Natural%20liberty%20versus%20the%20chains): Rousseau's framing of the double question, the chains we are born into and the liberty that obedience to self-prescribed law can restore:

> "Man is born free; and everywhere he is in chains. One thinks himself the master of others, and still remains a greater slave than they." ([Rousseau, The Social Contract, Book I, ch. 1, Cole trans., Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46333/46333-h/46333-h.htm)): Primary text; checked against the Gutenberg edition this session.

> "[T]he mere impulse of appetite is slavery, while obedience to a law which we prescribe to ourselves is liberty." ([Rousseau, The Social Contract, Book I, ch. 8, Cole trans., Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/46333/46333-h/46333-h.htm)): Primary text; checked against the same edition this session, with the sentence's opening word bracketed per the full text.

8. [The Cynic's freedom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20Cynic%27s%20freedom): freedom in three forms, eleutheria, autarkeia and parrhesia, with the poverty that frees one from having to pay court to a ruler, and the exchange that carries the whole stance in two lines, ask of me any boon you like, stand out of my sunlight ([Diogenes, quoted at Philoscopia](https://www.philoscopia.com/en/quotes/diogenes/)): Primary text; both lines checked this session.

9. [The interdependence of freedoms](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20interdependence%20of%20freedoms): Beauvoir's argument that a project has meaning only in a world other people have taken up, so the wish to be free already contains the wish that others be free:

> "To will oneself free is also to will others free. This will is not an abstract formula." ([Beauvoir, The Ethics of Ambiguity, Part II, ch. 1](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/ethics/de-beauvoir/ambiguity/ch02.htm)): Primary text; checked against the online text this session.

## Related questions and parallels

Freedom from or freedom to? The two Rousseau quotes above answer the two sides separately, the chains to be removed and the self-prescribed law to be obeyed; the pairing of the two sides under one comparison is this dossier's editorial move, and Berlin's two concepts of liberty, the distinction's own treatment, belong to Agency.

Why keep the Kierkegaardian material out? Because the dizziness is anxiety's evidence, and Anxiety maps it; this dossier's Kierkegaard-adjacent entries, the anguish and the vertigo, are kept to the Sartrean wing where they carry the will rather than the feeling. The two dossiers share the phenomenology and cite each other's territory.

Is radical freedom livable? The map holds the question open rather than resolving it: the situated-freedom critique is the card's own correction of the account it sits under, and the map keeps them in the same group rather than taking sides.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the lived-as-determined default, the reading of one's own situation as fixed fact end to end; the practices meet it by shrinking the frame to what is genuinely not up to you and enumerating what remains.

::Selection:: The practice I have selected as the source of that variation:

1. [K315 — Freedom in constraint](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K315%20%E2%80%94%20Freedom%20in%20constraint): name one constraint as fixed for the exercise, grieve it briefly and stop arguing with it, then write every choice that remains yours inside the frame, pick three, and make one concrete move in each this week; the payload is the difference the constraint makes while the moves are being made.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. The kernel's warrant is Frankl's, the last of the human freedoms as the choice of one's own way inside circumstances that take everything else, and its measured translation is psychological flexibility, the response repertoire that stays healthy when the situation does not change. The cards state the evidence for the direction, not for the exercise itself; a reader who keeps one enumerated frame has what the theme offered, and the waiter's diagnostic, catching oneself mid-gesture in a role performed too exactly, is the portable remainder.

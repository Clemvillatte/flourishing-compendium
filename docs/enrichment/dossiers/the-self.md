# The Self

The question this dossier serves: what am I, if not the story I keep telling
about myself? The mission behind it is the practical one that runs through the
whole compendium: to loosen the grip of the self-as-object, the self known,
judged and defended, because that grip is where much avoidable suffering
lives.

Two essay angles the material supports. First, the two great examinations of
experience, the Buddhist aggregate analysis and Advaita Vedānta's, both take
up identity and suffering together, and where they part is what the
examination finds. Second, a small lever with measured effects: the pronoun
people think in. Across three experiments with 693 participants, people
reasoned more wisely about another person's problems than about their own,
and instruction to self-distance eliminated the asymmetry
([Grossmann and Kross 2014](https://uwaterloo.ca/wisdom-and-culture-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/grossmann_kross_-_exploring_solomons_paradox_-_article_proof.pdf)).

Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only. A checked
quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. The base address
for all page links is the wiki's card view.

## Theme-Based Compilation

### What do I take myself to be?

[Anatta](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anatta)
is the Buddhist teaching that no permanent, unchanging, independent self is to
be found. The analysis in the Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta runs through the five
aggregates, testing each for the control a true owner would have:

> "Form, monks, is not self. If form were the self, this form would not lend
> itself to dis-ease. It would be possible (to say) with regard to form, 'Let
> my form be thus. Let my form not be thus.'" ([SN 22.59, trans. Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN22_59.html))

and closes each examination with:

> "Every form is to be seen with right discernment as it has come to be:
> 'This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am.'" (SN 22.59)

Primary text; quoted passages checked. Practice principle:
[B003 — Hold the self lightly](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B003%20%E2%80%94%20Hold%20the%20self%20lightly%20(anatta)).

[Atman](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Atman)
is Advaita Vedānta's answer: the self, examined to the bottom, is pure
consciousness. Neil Dalal's Stanford entry states the position:

> "The fundamental thrust of Advaita Vedānta is that the ātman is pure
> non-intentional consciousness. It is one without a second, nondual, infinite
> existence, and numerically identical with brahman." ([Dalal, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/shankara/))

Scholarly interpretation; cited passage checked. This is Advaita's position,
not a statement about Indian philosophy as a whole.

[The observing self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20observing%20self)
is Arthur Deikman's name for the witness that notices thoughts and feelings
without being any of them:

> "Awareness is the ground of conscious life, the background or field in
> which all elements exist, different from thoughts, sensations, or images."
> ([Deikman 1982](https://www.deikman.com/observing.html))

> "We cannot observe the observing self; we must experience it directly. It
> has no defining qualities, no boundaries, no dimensions." (Deikman 1982)

Primary text; quoted passages checked. Practice principle:
[SE002 — Recognise the observing self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=SE002%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20observing%20self%20%E2%80%94%20you%20are%20not%20only%20the%20contents%20of%20awareness).

### What changes when I observe a thought?

[Distanced self-talk](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distanced%20self-talk%20(Kross))
is the research programme that measured what the observing position does. The
authors' own image:

> "It is also possible for people to take a step back when thinking about past
> experiences and reason about them from the perspective of a distanced
> observer, akin to a fly on the wall." ([Kross and Ayduk 2011](https://rascl.studentorg.berkeley.edu/assets/files/kross_ayduk_2011_cd.pdf))

Study text; quoted passage checked. The wise-reasoning result is quoted in the
opening above (Grossmann and Kross 2014, quoted passage checked). Practice
principle:
[CO066 — Take the third-person perspective](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=CO066%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20take%20the%20third-person%20perspective%20%E2%80%94%20distanced%20self-talk%20improves%20reasoning).

[Self-as-context](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-as-context)
is the version from acceptance and commitment therapy, where the observing
position is trained as a skill:

> "ACT seeks to undermine an attachment to a conceptualized self (our fused,
> evaluative stories about who we are) and to promote contact with a sense of
> self based on the "I/here/nowness" of conscious experience. It is usually
> termed "self as context" although other terms ("noticing self," "observer
> perspective," "transcendent sense of self") have been used."
> ([Hayes et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3635495/))

Scholarly review; cited passage checked. Training kernel:
[K139 — Self-as-context](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K139%20%E2%80%94%20Self-as-context).

### What do relationships contribute to identity?

[Independent vs interdependent self-construal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Independent%20vs%20interdependent%20self-construal%20(Markus%20%26%20Kitayama))
is Hazel Markus and Shinobu Kitayama's account of the two ways cultures teach
people to be a self. Their definitions:

> "Achieving the cultural goal of independence requires construing oneself as
> an individual whose behavior is organized and made meaningful primarily by
> reference to one's own internal repertoire of thoughts, feelings, and action,
> rather than by reference to the thoughts, feelings, and actions of others."

> "Experiencing interdependence entails seeing oneself as part of an
> encompassing social relationship and recognizing that one's behavior is
> determined, contingent on, and, to a large extent organized by what the actor
> perceives to be the thoughts, feelings, and actions of others in the
> relationship." ([Markus and Kitayama 1991](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Influence_Compliance/Markus_Kitayama_1991_Culture_and_the_self.pdf))

Primary text; quoted passages checked. Related compendium pages:
[Ubuntu](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ubuntu)
and
[The relational self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20relational%20self).

### What, if anything, remains through change?

The [chariot image](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anatta)
from the Milindapañha gives the aggregate analysis its everyday handle:

> "It is on account of its having all these things—the pole, and the axle, the
> wheels, and the framework, the ropes, the yoke, the spokes, and the
> goad—that it comes under the generally understood term, the designation in
> common use, of 'chariot.'" ([Mil 3.1.1, trans. Rhys Davids](https://suttacentral.net/mil3.1.1/en/tw_rhysdavids))

Primary text; quoted passage checked. Practice kernel:
[K144 — The chariot analysis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K144%20%E2%80%94%20The%20chariot%20analysis).

Further reading; not assessed for this dossier:
[Minimal self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Minimal%20self),
[The narrative self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20narrative%20self),
[Ecological self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ecological%20self),
[Ego dissolution](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ego%20dissolution).

A caution worth carrying into any essay: four claims that sound alike and are
not. That experience has a first-person character, that a person needs a
narrative, that identity is social through and through, and that a permanent
metaphysical essence exists. A tradition can hold any three and deny the
fourth, so a disagreement about "the self" is never about one thing.

## Theme-Based Areas of divergence

### Is there a permanent self?

The shared question, stated narrowly: what can rightly be regarded as self?
The Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta answers by examination. Each aggregate, past or
future, internal or external, is tested for ownership, and the test fails:
"This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am" (SN 22.59,
quoted passage checked). The examination locates no owner in the body or the
mind. Advaita Vedānta, in Dalal's account, runs an examination of its own and
takes it to end elsewhere: in consciousness that is not an object. "Advaitins
understand brahman as transcending individuality and empirical plurality.
They seek to establish that the essential core of one's self (ātman) is
brahman" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/shankara/),
cited passage checked). The entry describes the error as mutual
superimposition: "We mutually superimpose consciousness on to our mind-body,
and the mind-body on to consciousness (i.e., I am the mind-body, and the
mind-body is I)" (SEP, cited passage checked).

The two accounts even share a diagnosis of suffering, in Dalal's phrasing of
the Advaita side: Śaṅkara identifies avidyā as "the root cause of all
existential suffering", producing "a baseline of fear and anxiety due to
assuming one's self as a limited being subject to sorrow, sickness, and death"
(SEP, cited passage checked).

The precise difference is what the examination is taken to find. The sutta's
analysis works through the contents of experience one by one and grants none
of them the status of owner. Advaita holds that consciousness itself, the
field in which all contents appear, is the one referent that is not a content.
What remains unresolved, and what an essay can honestly leave open: whether
the observing position that the practices below train is a final referent or
one more event to be examined. The psychological research measures what the
perspective does; it does not settle what the perspective is.

### Is the self separate or relational?

The shared question: is identity primarily a possession of the individual or
a property of relationships? Markus and Kitayama's two construals, quoted
above, give the cleanest statement of each pole: the self organized by one's
own internal repertoire of thoughts, feelings and action, against the self
organized by what the actor perceives to be the thoughts, feelings and
actions of others in the relationship (1991, quoted passages checked).

The compendium holds both as emphases a person moves between rather than
types of person, and the concept page carries the debate the theory set off
over whether whole cultures fit either pole. The relational pole is carried
by [Ubuntu](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ubuntu)
and
[The relational self](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20relational%20self);
the delicate work of holding both is
[R077 — Practise relational autonomy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=R077%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20relational%20autonomy).
Unresolved: whether the construals describe selves, or the situations selves
move between.

## Theme-Based practice

An editorial sequence of existing practice kernels, ordered from the smallest
move to the widest view. No study tested this sequence; each kernel carries
its own evidence and its own limits, stated with it.

1. [K126 — Cognitive defusion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K126%20%E2%80%94%20Cognitive%20defusion),
for the moment a single sticky thought bites. The technique is defined as
designed "to reduce the functions of thoughts by altering the context in
which they occur, rather than the attempting to alter the form, frequency, or
situational sensitivity of the thoughts themselves"
([Masuda et al. 2004](https://contextualscience.org/sites/default/files/305%20Defusion%20word%20repetition%20BRAT%202004.pdf),
quoted passage checked). The kernel's protocol, an adaptation of the
tradition, reframes the thought as an event: "I notice I'm having the thought
that I will fail." Evidence limits, in the authors' own words: their data "do
not show, however, that Titchener's defusion procedure, or defusion
procedures more generally, are actually clinically useful" (Masuda et al.
2004, quoted passage checked).

2. [K261 — Self-distanced reflection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K261%20%E2%80%94%20Self-distanced%20reflection),
for when a whole situation loops. The kernel's protocol is an editorial
arrangement: write about the situation from the fly-on-the-wall perspective,
in the third person, then keep what the observer knows that the player did
not. The research behind the move is quoted in the opening: self-distancing
eliminated the own-versus-other asymmetry in wise reasoning across three
experiments, N = 693 (Grossmann and Kross 2014, quoted passages checked), and
spontaneous self-distancers report less distress and rumination (Kross and
Ayduk 2011, quoted passage checked). The measured outcome was coded
reasoning about relationship problems, and the studies concern chosen
reflection tasks, so the practice is a tool for a chosen question.

3. [K139 — Self-as-context](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K139%20%E2%80%94%20Self-as-context),
for when a self-judgment has hardened into a verdict. The kernel's protocol,
an adaptation of the ACT tradition, names the story as a story and moves
attention to the one noticing it. The component evidence is thin, and the
review that canvassed it says so in its own words: "there is only provisional
but very limited evidence to suggest that SAC can be effectively taught and
implemented as a stand-alone process to manage emotional well-being"
([Godbee and Kangas 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33051034/),
quoted passage checked).

4. [K144 — The chariot analysis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K144%20%E2%80%94%20The%20chariot%20analysis),
for when a self-image starts speaking as the whole self. The kernel's
protocol is an adaptation of the Milindapañha dialogue, which asks, part by
part, whether the "I" is this feeling, this memory, this role, and then
closes with the dialogue's own questions: "Then is it all these parts of it
that are the chariot?" "No, Sir." "But is there anything outside them that
is the chariot?" And still he answered no. (Mil 3.1.1, trans. Rhys Davids,
[quoted passages checked](https://suttacentral.net/mil3.1.1/en/tw_rhysdavids).)
The dialogue is a philosophical text, and the kernel's page says so plainly.

Further practices held in the corpus, as exploration rather than steps:
[K335 — The witness practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K335%20%E2%80%94%20The%20witness%20practice)
and
[K146 — Ego dissolution practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K146%20%E2%80%94%20Ego%20dissolution%20practice).
Widening the self relationally is a reading direction here, through
[Ubuntu](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ubuntu)
and the further-reading pages above, and the compendium's verified practice
for that work belongs to a future dossier, not this one.

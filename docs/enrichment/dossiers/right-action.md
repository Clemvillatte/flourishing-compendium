# Right Action

> What restraints on the body make a life livable with itself?

Right action is the fourth factor of the Eightfold Path: what the body does when it does no harm. The precepts are short, and the life they point to is not: a floor of non-harm under conduct, with the counterparts practised above that floor. A life whose conduct keeps the floor is livable with itself in a way a life of unexamined habit is not.

## Nearby terms

- Right conduct, ethical action: other renderings of the same Pali factor, sammā kammanta; my card is [Right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action), and nothing is split between the names.
- Non-harm: the floor this factor rests on; the harmlessness resolve as an intention belongs to the Right Intention page, this page holds the bodily restraints that resolve issues in.
- Behaviour: the general science of habit formation stays there; this page holds the path's ethical restraints on bodily conduct.
- Right speech and right livelihood: the neighbouring conduct factors, each with its own page; the map below points to them only where the boundary matters.

## The map: the entries on right action

### What the factor is

[Right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action): the fourth factor, Pali sammā kammanta; abstaining from killing, from taking what is not given, and from sexual misconduct.

[Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path): the umbrella hub; action is grouped with speech and livelihood in the ethics training.

[The three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings): the grouping that places conduct under ethics, with wisdom and concentration as the other two trainings.

[Action over intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20over%20intention): the label I use for this factor, marking where the path moves from resolve to conduct.

[Karma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karma): bodily action as karma; intentional conduct through body, speech or mind is what sets results going, and this factor holds the bodily side of it.

[Spontaneous right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spontaneous%20right%20action): Wang Yangming's doctrine that knowing the good already issues in acting; quoted in the related question below.

The Magga-vibhanga Sutta gives the definition:

> "And what, monks, is right action? Abstaining from taking life, abstaining from stealing, abstaining from unchastity: This, monks, is called right action." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)).

Bhikkhu Bodhi's study of the path supplies the positive counterparts to the three abstentions:

> "The positive counterpart to abstaining from taking life, as the Buddha indicates, is the development of kindness and compassion for other beings. The disciple not only avoids destroying life; he dwells with a heart full of sympathy, desiring the welfare of all beings." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

> "The positive virtue corresponding to the abstinence is, for laypeople, marital fidelity. Husband and wife should each be faithful and devoted to the other, content with the relationship, and should not risk a breakup to the union by seeking outside partners." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

### The three restraints and their counterparts

The first restraint, taking life, and its counterpart, compassion:

[Compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion): being moved by suffering and motivated to act; the counterpart Bodhi names first.

[Nonviolent action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20action): Gene Sharp's theory of power and his documented methods of nonviolent action.

[Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20communication): Rosenberg's process for reducing conflict in everyday interactions.

[Conduct even in just war](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conduct%20even%20in%20just%20war): Aquinas's three requirements for a just war, and the warning that a war can meet two of them and still be rendered unlawful through a wicked intention.

[Courage — action despite fear](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Courage%20%E2%80%94%20action%20despite%20fear): acting for the sake of something despite fear; the courage the counterparts can demand.

[Harm principle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Harm%20principle): Mill's principle for when society may coerce an adult; grouped here by editorial connection, as the boundary question of harm done to others.

[Judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm): the Stoic claim that events do not disturb people, the opinions about the events do; grouped here by editorial connection as a claim about disturbance rather than harm done.

The second restraint, taking what is not given, and its counterparts, honesty, contentment and generosity:

The most eminent opposite virtue is generosity, giving away one's own wealth and possessions in order to benefit others." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

[Generosity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Generosity): Aristotle's liberality, the mean with regard to wealth, concerned especially with giving.

[Prosocial honesty research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20honesty%20research): the research on truth-telling that hurts, grouped here by editorial connection; the card's material belongs with the speech factor's honesty question.

[Truthfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Truthfulness): Aristotle's account in Nicomachean Ethics IV.7, the character of a person true in word and in life where nothing is at stake.

The third restraint, sexual misconduct, and its counterpart, fidelity:

[Fidelity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fidelity): Gabriel Marcel's treatment of fidelity as more than keeping a promise; the problem of constancy.

## Related questions and parallels

### May taking life ever be right action?

The path's definition states the restraint without exception:

> "Abstaining from taking life, abstaining from stealing, abstaining from unchastity: This, monks, is called right action." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)).

The Christian tradition codifies the other answer, that taking up the sword of war can be lawful conduct. Aquinas asks whether it is always sinful to wage war, and the reply opens by giving what makes a war just:

> "In order for a war to be just, three things are necessary. First, the authority of the sovereign by whose command the war is to be waged." ([Aquinas, Summa Theologiae II-II, q.40, a.1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3040.htm)).

The permission is bounded, not an endorsement of harm for its own sake; the same article condemns the passion for inflicting harm and the cruel thirst for vengeance in war. The two answers to the same question are incompatible: the definition of right action names abstaining from taking life, and the just-war tradition names the conditions under which a sovereign may rightly command it. I hold both sides, the non-harm floor on one side and the just-war card on the other, and do not reconcile them.

### Can knowledge coexist with failure to act?

In Aristotle's report, Socrates denies that someone can knowingly act against what they judge best.

> "Socrates in fact contended generally against the theory, maintaining there is no such state as that of Imperfect Self-Control, for that no one acts contrary to what is best conceiving it to be best but by reason of ignorance what is best." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VII.2, Chase translation, Project Gutenberg ebook 8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action holds the same shape, that genuinely knowing already issues in acting:

> "There have never been people who know but do not act. Those who are supposed to know but do not act simply do not yet know." ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Wang Yangming (1472—1529)"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/)).

Aristotle, in the same discussion, calls the plain facts against him:

> "With all due respect to Socrates, his account of the matter is at variance with plain facts, and we must enquire with respect to the affection, if it be caused by ignorance what is the nature of the ignorance: for that the man so failing does not suppose his acts to be right before he is under the influence of passion is quite plain." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VII.2, Chase translation, Project Gutenberg ebook 8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

Aristotle's resolution, later in Book VII, moves part of the way back toward Socrates:

> "the result which Socrates was seeking comes out, that is to say, the affection does not take place in the presence of that which is thought to be specially and properly Knowledge, nor is this dragged about by reason of the affection, but in the presence of that Knowledge which is conveyed by Sense." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VII.3, Chase translation, Project Gutenberg ebook 8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

Wang's knowledge, as the reference article presents it, is knowing how to respond rather than grasping information:

> "For Wang, knowledge means knowing how to respond to a given situation and action is responding to a given situation." ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Wang Yangming (1472—1529)"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/)).

Socrates, as Aristotle reports him, denies the state exists, and Aristotle calls the plain facts against him, then moves part of the way back with the resolution above. What remains open is the fit between the two sides' terms, whether Wang's knowing-how is Aristotle's Knowledge proper, which passion cannot suspend, or the sense-conveyed knowing that it can; I hold materials on both sides, the spontaneous-right-action card with Socrates and Wang, the virtue-rehearsal kernels with Aristotle, and records the pair as a related question rather than a settled disagreement.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the default setting of bodily conduct: harm done by convenience, giving governed by mood, fidelity held only while feeling holds, courage postponed until the moment passes. The adaptive direction is the factor's own shape, the non-harm floor kept under whatever else is undertaken, and the counterparts, compassion, generosity, fidelity, practiced above it.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K250 — Virtue rehearsal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K250%20%E2%80%94%20Virtue%20rehearsal): pick one virtue and practise it in small daily acts that get written down, drawing on Aristotle's habituation teaching and Franklin's chart.

2. [K255 — Virtue journaling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K255%20%E2%80%94%20Virtue%20journaling): each evening, one sentence, the virtue, the situation, the action; a training log rather than a diary.

3. [K263 — Post-action phronesis review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K263%20%E2%80%94%20Post-action%20phronesis%20review): after a significant action, four questions, the intention, the outcome, the gap, the revision.

4. [K266 — Impression-judgement-action log](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K266%20%E2%80%94%20Impression-judgement-action%20log): a three-column log for one week, the impression, the judgement, the action, training the gap Epictetus puts at the root of disturbance.

5. [K184 — Opposite action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K184%20%E2%80%94%20Opposite%20action): when an emotion urges a behaviour that runs counter to your values, do the opposite; the action does not deny the emotion, it refuses to let it drive.

6. [K152 — Committed action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K152%20%E2%80%94%20Committed%20action): one action today consistent with a chosen value, however small; the action matters more than the feeling.

7. [K290 — Prosocial courage drill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K290%20%E2%80%94%20Prosocial%20courage%20drill): a standing weekly slot for speaking up where unfairness would normally be met with silence, converting the act from a decision under pressure into a decision already made.

8. [K216 — Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K216%20%E2%80%94%20Nonviolent%20communication): the four-step structure for conflict, observation, feeling, need, request, giving dispute a shared language instead of a counterattack.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the evening sentence, or the weekly slot, or the three-column log has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### The stopped harm

[ST405 — Angulimala Is Told to Bear the Stones](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST405%20%E2%80%94%20Angulimala%20Is%20Told%20to%20Bear%20the%20Stones): Angulimala, the bandit wearing a garland of fingers, cannot catch the Buddha walking at normal pace, hears the explanation that the Buddha has stopped "once & for all" having cast off violence toward all living beings, and throws his sword over a cliff; the clod, stone and potsherd thrown at him on his alms round are met with the Buddha telling him to bear it as the fruit of karma now experienced in the here-&-now.

### Harm discovered late

[ST087 — Schindler's List](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST087%20%E2%80%94%20Schindler%27s%20List): Schindler arrives in Kraków as a profiteer and ends with his entire fortune spent buying lives; the card's own reading is that the film does not depict him as having become good, it depicts him discovering too late what the money had been for all along.

### Taking for others

[ST295 — Prometheus Steals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST295%20%E2%80%94%20Prometheus%20Steals): Prometheus steals fire from the gods and gives it to humanity, and is chained to a rock with his liver eaten daily; the card reads the theft as the creative act and the punishment as its permanent cost, an editorial juxtaposition with this factor's second restraint rather than the myth's own framing.

### Rules and situations

[ST384 — The Tree Is Spared; the Goose Is Killed](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST384%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Tree%20Is%20Spared%3B%20the%20Goose%20Is%20Killed): a tree is spared because its wood is good for nothing and a goose is killed because it cannot cackle; asked which condition he would prefer, Zhuangzi smiles that he would rest halfway between the two, and that even that position cannot avoid trouble. The card reads this as a warning that a rule which protects in one setting may fail in another.

# Right Action

> What restraints on the body make a life livable with itself?

Right action is the fourth factor of the Eightfold Path: what the body does when it does no harm. The precepts are short, and the life they point to is not: a floor of non-harm under conduct, with the counterparts practised above that floor.

I hold this factor close because conduct is where intentions meet their test. A resolve can live comfortably in the mind for years without costing anything; conduct is where it either shows up or does not. A life whose conduct keeps the floor is livable with itself in a way a life of unexamined habit is not.

### Nearby terms

- Right conduct, ethical action: other renderings of the same Pali factor, sammā kammanta; my card is [Right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action), and nothing is split between the names.
- Non-harm: the floor this factor rests on. The harmlessness resolve as an intention belongs to the Right Intention page; right action holds the bodily restraints that resolve issues in.
- Behaviour: the general science of habit formation stays there; right action holds the path's ethical restraints on bodily conduct.
- Right speech and right livelihood: the neighbouring conduct factors, each with its own page and dossier.
- Spontaneous right action: Wang Yangming's doctrine that knowing the good already issues in acting; it returns in the question of whether knowledge can coexist with failure to act.

## What the factor is

The definition is stated in one breath. The [Magga-vibhanga Sutta](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html) gives it:

> "And what, monks, is right action? Abstaining from taking life, abstaining from stealing, abstaining from unchastity: This, monks, is called right action." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)).

[Right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action) is the fourth factor of the [Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path), and [the three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings) group it under the ethics training, with wisdom and concentration as the other two. The conduct factors sit together on the path, speech first, then action, then livelihood, and each has its own page and dossier.

[Action over intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20over%20intention) is my label for this factor, marking where the path moves from resolve to conduct. Here the resolve has to show up in what the hands do, which is where most intentions meet their test.

The factor is also where conduct touches what [karma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karma) is, because intentional conduct through body, speech or mind is what sets results going, and this factor holds the bodily side of it. The precepts name three abstentions and leave the positive side to be inferred; Bhikkhu Bodhi's study of the path supplies the counterparts. For taking life:

> "The positive counterpart to abstaining from taking life, as the Buddha indicates, is the development of kindness and compassion for other beings. The disciple not only avoids destroying life; he dwells with a heart full of sympathy, desiring the welfare of all beings." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

And for the third abstention:

> "The positive virtue corresponding to the abstinence is, for laypeople, marital fidelity. Husband and wife should each be faithful and devoted to the other, content with the relationship, and should not risk a breakup to the union by seeking outside partners." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

So the factor is a floor with practices above it: abstain from three things, cultivate compassion, honesty, contentment, generosity and fidelity. [Spontaneous right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spontaneous%20right%20action), Wang Yangming's doctrine that knowing the good already issues in acting, waits with the question of whether knowledge can fail to act.

## The first restraint: taking life

The first abstention is taking life. [Ahimsa](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ahimsa) is the Indic principle of non-harm; I read it as the starting floor of this factor: whatever else a practice does, it begins by doing no injury. The counterpart Bodhi names first is [compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion), being moved by suffering and motivated to act, and [courage as action despite fear](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Courage%20%E2%80%94%20action%20despite%20fear) is the courage the counterparts can demand, acting for the sake of something despite fear.

Non-harm also has working methods. [Nonviolent action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20action) is Gene Sharp's theory of power and his documented methods of nonviolent action; [nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20communication) is Rosenberg's process for reducing conflict in everyday interactions.

Two neighbouring claims about harm answer questions of their own rather than this one. [Harm principle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Harm%20principle) is Mill's principle for when society may coerce an adult, the boundary question of harm done to others; [judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm) is the Stoic claim that events do not disturb people, the opinions about the events do, a claim about disturbance rather than harm done. The question this restraint raises is whether the floor is absolute: whether taking life can ever be right action. [Aquinas](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conduct%20even%20in%20just%20war) answers differently: his three requirements for a just war, with the warning that a war can meet two of them and still be rendered unlawful through a wicked intention. That exchange is the first genuine disagreement below.

## The second restraint: taking what is not given

The second abstention is taking what is not given, and its positive side is a cluster rather than one virtue. Bodhi's study names them together:

> "The positive counterpart to abstaining from stealing is honesty, which implies respect for the belongings of others and for their right to use their belongings as they wish. Another related virtue is contentment, being satisfied with what one has without being inclined to increase one's wealth by unscrupulous means. The most eminent opposite virtue is generosity, giving away one's own wealth and possessions in order to benefit others." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

[Generosity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Generosity) is Aristotle's liberality, the mean with regard to wealth, concerned especially with giving; [truthfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Truthfulness) is his account in Nicomachean Ethics IV.7, the character of a person true in word and in life where nothing is at stake. The research on truth-telling that hurts, [prosocial honesty research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20honesty%20research), is about honesty in speech rather than conduct of the hands, and belongs with the speech factor's honesty question.

## The third restraint: sexual misconduct

The third abstention is sexual misconduct, and its positive counterpart for laypeople is fidelity. [Fidelity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fidelity) is Gabriel Marcel's treatment of fidelity as more than keeping a promise: the problem of constancy. The abstention states a boundary; the counterpart names what has to be kept alive inside it.

## How conduct is trained

The rule answer and the situation answer address different questions. A rule tells a person what to do; a situation tells a person where the doing happens, and the two can be trained at once.

### The rule answer

[The categorical imperative](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20categorical%20imperative) is Kant's supreme principle of morality, binding regardless of what a person wants: the restraint stated as a universal law. Its companion on the motivational side is [the courage to act against impulse](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20courage%20to%20act%20against%20impulse), Kant's account of acting from duty against inclination, my own heading for it.

The character answer instead asks what kind of person is doing the acting, and it starts from a puzzle: some people seem good from birth. [Natural virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Natural%20virtue) is Aristotle's name for those dispositions, set against virtue in the strict sense where the difference is practical wisdom. [The catalogue of virtues](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20catalogue%20of%20virtues) is Aristotle's table of the character virtues as means between vices of excess and deficiency; [cardinal virtues](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cardinal%20virtues) are the four hinge virtues, prudence, justice, fortitude and temperance, on which the classical tradition says the whole of moral life turns.

[Virtue ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Virtue%20ethics) is the normative approach that puts character first, contrasted with deontology and consequentialism; [the unity of the virtues](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20unity%20of%20the%20virtues) is the thesis, argued by Socrates in the Protagoras and reworked by Aristotle in Nicomachean Ethics VI, that the virtues come together rather than standing as separate talents.

### How character forms

Aristotle's account of virtue of character holds that character is formed by doing:

> "men become builders by building and lyreplayers by playing the lyre; so too we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1, Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)).

The habituation ordering runs through several traditions. [Action before feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20before%20feeling) is my application of Aristotle's habituation ordering: the Ethics itself teaches habituation, and II.4 distinguishes performing a just action from acting as a just person. [Action precedes feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20precedes%20feeling) is the same act-first claim from Epictetus's counsels to William James's habit essay. [The rehearsal of virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20rehearsal%20of%20virtue) is the Stoic practice of running doctrine through action until the mind takes its shape, Seneca's defense of precepts in Letter 94.

[Moral cultivation (xiushen)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20cultivation%20%28xiushen%29) is the Confucian practice of building moral character; the term literally reads as body-fixing. [Effortless action through long effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20action%20through%20long%20effort) is effortlessness in action as the far end of long effort, skill matured until the act stops feeling like an act.

[Opposite action (Linehan)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Opposite%20action%20%28Linehan%29) is the DBT skill of acting against the action urge that comes with an emotion; [action commitment](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20commitment) is ACT's committed action, one act consistent with a chosen value, however small. Both focus on what to do next. [Self-constitution through action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-constitution%20through%20action) is Kierkegaard's Judge Vilhelm: choosing shapes personality, and a life that keeps choice at arm's length loses definition.

[Svadharma, one's own duty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Svadharma%2C%20one%27s%20own%20duty) addresses whose duty it is: the Bhagavad Gita's teaching that one's own duty, however imperfect, is better than another's done well. [The bodhisattva vow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20bodhisattva%20vow) addresses who the conduct is for: the Mahayana commitment to work for the liberation of all beings, conduct undertaken for everyone's sake. [Skillful means (upaya)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Skillful%20means%20%28upaya%29) addresses how the teaching meets the case: the Lotus Sutra's flexible application of it to the situation.

### The situation answer

The moral situation is Dewey's term for where morality actually happens: the concrete meeting of a person's dispositions with an environment, beyond both the rule and the inner self.

The situationist literature backs Dewey's placement with findings: [moral situation research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20situation%20research) gathers Doris's Lack of Character, the hurrying seminarians and Milgram's obedience findings.

If the setting carries that much of the outcome, the training can be arranged rather than willed. [Designing for virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Designing%20for%20virtue) is the design-research idea that products and environments can support virtuous behaviour, the third ingredient of the Positive Design Framework; [designing the environment for virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Designing%20the%20environment%20for%20virtue) is arranging the settings of action, from laws and institutions down to the room one works in, so that virtuous acts become the ones a person repeatedly performs. [Ethics as foundation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ethics%20as%20foundation) is Johnston's reading of Yoga Sūtra II.29, the Commandments of non-injury, truthfulness and abstention from stealing, placed before the Rules, conduct as the foundation of practice.

### How moral reasoning develops

[Kohlberg's theory of moral development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Kohlberg%27s%20theory%20of%20moral%20development) and [moral development research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20development%20research) are the research tradition on how moral reasoning develops, from stage theory to the intuition approaches that followed.

[Social cognitive theory of morality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20cognitive%20theory%20of%20morality) is Bandura's account of moral conduct through the interaction of personal standards, self-regulation and social circumstances; [moral disengagement research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20disengagement%20research) is his account of how moral self-sanctions are selectively disengaged from inhumane conduct.

### The relational accounts

The relational traditions hold that conduct is answerable to particular others rather than to a rule or a character alone. [Care ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Care%20ethics) and [Gilligan's ethics-of-care theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gilligan%27s%20ethics-of-care%20theory) are moral reasoning with more than one voice, the relationship-oriented ethic of care contrasted with the ethic of justice: the care ethic asks what the relation asks, the justice ethic asks what anyone in that position owes.

[Role ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Role%20ethics) makes the relation constitutive: on its family of views, a person's roles and relationships are central to what is morally required and to who the person is. [Confucian role ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Confucian%20role%20ethics) is the Confucian branch of that family.

[The call of conscience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20call%20of%20conscience) and [conscience (Gewissen)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conscience%20%28Gewissen%29) are Heidegger's account of Dasein summoned out of its lostness in the "they".

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### May taking life ever be right action?

The path's definition states the restraint without exception:

> "Abstaining from taking life, abstaining from stealing, abstaining from unchastity: This, monks, is called right action." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)).

The other side is named: for [Aquinas](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conduct%20even%20in%20just%20war), taking up the sword of war can be lawful conduct. He asks whether it is always sinful to wage war, and the reply opens by giving what makes a war just:

> "In order for a war to be just, three things are necessary. First, the authority of the sovereign by whose command the war is to be waged." ([Aquinas, Summa Theologiae II-II, q.40, a.1](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/3040.htm)).

The permission is bounded, not an endorsement of harm for its own sake; the same article condemns the passion for inflicting harm and the cruel thirst for vengeance in war. The two answers to the same question are incompatible: the definition of right action names abstaining from taking life, and the just-war tradition names the conditions under which a sovereign may rightly command it. I hold both sides, the non-harm floor on one side and the just-war card on the other, and do not reconcile them.

### Can knowledge coexist with failure to act?

In Aristotle's report, Socrates denies that someone can knowingly act against what they judge best.

> "Socrates in fact contended generally against the theory, maintaining there is no such state as that of Imperfect Self-Control, for that no one acts contrary to what is best conceiving it to be best but by reason of ignorance what is best." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VII.2, Chase translation, Project Gutenberg ebook 8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action holds the same shape, that genuinely knowing already issues in acting:

> "There have never been people who know but do not act. Those who are supposed to know but do not act simply do not yet know." ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Wang Yangming (1472—1529)"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/)).

Aristotle, in the same discussion, calls the plain facts against him:

> "With all due respect to Socrates, his account of the matter is at variance with plain facts, and we must enquire with respect to the affection, if it be caused by ignorance what is the nature of the ignorance: for that the man so failing does not suppose his acts to be right before he is under the influence of passion is quite plain." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VII.2, Chase translation, Project Gutenberg ebook 8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

Aristotle's resolution, later in Book VII, moves part of the way back toward Socrates:

> "the result which Socrates was seeking comes out, that is to say, the affection does not take place in the presence of that which is thought to be specially and properly Knowledge, nor is this dragged about by reason of the affection, but in the presence of that Knowledge which is conveyed by Sense." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics VII.3, Chase translation, Project Gutenberg ebook 8438](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8438/pg8438.txt)).

Wang's knowledge, as the reference article presents it, is knowing how to respond rather than grasping information:

> "For Wang, knowledge means knowing how to respond to a given situation and action is responding to a given situation." ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Wang Yangming (1472—1529)"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/)).

Socrates, as Aristotle reports him, denies the state exists, and Aristotle calls the plain facts against him, then moves part of the way back with the resolution above. The fit between the two sides' terms can be argued from both texts. Wang's own account of the failure state supplies his side. His disciple Xu Ai records the exchange in Instructions for Practical Living:

> "The Teacher said, "The knowing and acting you refer to are already separated by selfish desires and are no longer knowing and acting in their original substance. There have never been people who know but do not act. Those who are supposed to know but do not act simply do not yet know. When sages and worthies taught people about knowing and acting, it was precisely because they wanted them to restore this original substance, and not just to have them behave like that and be satisfied."" ([Wang Yangming, Instructions for Practical Living, in Wm. Theodore de Bary and Irene Bloom (comps.), Sources of Chinese Tradition, via Columbia University Asia for Educators](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_china-wang-yangming-knowing-acting.htm)).

The knowing that fails to act is, in Wang's terms, already separated by selfish desires; the original substance of knowing and acting does not fail. Aristotle's resolution in Book VII makes a parallel qualification: the affection does not take place in the presence of Knowledge proper, only in the presence of the sense-conveyed knowing that passion can drag about. Both accounts qualify what counts as knowing when action fails to follow it; the precise relation between Wang's original substance and Aristotle's Knowledge proper remains open.

The two accounts also differ in emphasis. Aristotle's chapter on virtue of character forms the agent through practice, becoming just by doing just acts; Wang's instruction points to restoring the original substance, removing the selfish desires that did the separating. Whether character formed through practice and knowing restored by clearing are one work or two is not settled by the passages.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the default setting of bodily conduct: harm done by convenience, giving governed by mood, fidelity held only while feeling holds, courage postponed until the moment passes. The adaptive direction is the factor's own shape, the non-harm floor kept under whatever else is undertaken, and the counterparts, compassion, generosity, fidelity, practiced above it.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K250 — Virtue rehearsal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K250%20%E2%80%94%20Virtue%20rehearsal): pick one virtue and practise it in small daily acts that get written down, drawing on Aristotle's habituation teaching and Franklin's chart; hold the focus on that one virtue for a week or a month, then move to the next and come round again. On a day of failure, mark the fault plainly, Franklin's black spot, and notice where the spots cluster across the rotation.

2. [K255 — Virtue journaling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K255%20%E2%80%94%20Virtue%20journaling): each evening, one sentence, the virtue, the situation, the action, and on missed days what got in the way, in the flat voice of a missed training session rather than a confession. The loop closes on the weekly reread: notice the situations that recur, because the repetition is where the next deliberate practice gets aimed.

3. [K263 — Post-action phronesis review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K263%20%E2%80%94%20Post-action%20phronesis%20review): after a significant action, write the four questions: the intention, what actually happened, where the gap between them came from, what to do differently. Notice whether the review changes anything; a review that changes nothing is a diary entry. End with one concrete amendment for the next similar action.

4. [K266 — Impression-judgement-action log](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K266%20%E2%80%94%20Impression-judgement-action%20log): keep the three-column log for one week, the impression, the judgement, the action, one row per situation that stirred you. At the week's end, read the judgement column alone and notice the interpretations that recur; for each, ask what in the impression column supports it.

5. [K184 — Opposite action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K184%20%E2%80%94%20Opposite%20action): when an emotion urges a behaviour that runs counter to your values, do the opposite act in the opposite direction, full-sized; sadness says withdraw, so reach out; anger says attack, so step back. Stay in the act, recording afterwards whether the intensity changed. The laboratory test behind the skill is small, sixteen people with borderline personality disorder over six laboratory sessions, and it favoured opposite action for sadness and guilt or shame while showing no advantage for anxiety or anger in that design.

6. [K152 — Committed action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K152%20%E2%80%94%20Committed%20action): take one action today consistent with a chosen value, however small, at a size you can actually do, especially when you do not feel like it. In the evening log, notice which actions happened and which stayed on the list; keep the sizes that get done and drop the ones that do not.

7. [K290 — Prosocial courage drill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K290%20%E2%80%94%20Prosocial%20courage%20drill): choose a weekly slot in advance; during the week, write down one situation where you saw unfairness and stayed silent, then act on it or its next occurrence: the talked-over colleague named in the meeting, the one-line disagreement with the biased joke, the safety concern reported. Afterwards note what you did, what it cost and what it accomplished; acts that misfired get adjusted, not abandoned.

8. [K216 — Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K216%20%E2%80%94%20Nonviolent%20communication): before the hard conversation, write the four steps out for yourself: what happened without evaluation, what you feel, what you need, what you request. Speak them in order and slowly; the request is askable and refusable. When attacked, run the same steps on the attacker's words.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the evening sentence, or the weekly slot, or the three-column log has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

Wealth, theft and harm each meet a person who keeps acting after the choice is made.

### The stopped harm

[ST405 — Angulimala Is Told to Bear the Stones](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST405%20%E2%80%94%20Angulimala%20Is%20Told%20to%20Bear%20the%20Stones): Angulimala, the bandit wearing a garland of fingers, cannot catch the Buddha walking at normal pace, hears the explanation that the Buddha has stopped "once & for all" having cast off violence toward all living beings, and throws his sword over a cliff; the clod, stone and potsherd thrown at him on his alms round are met with the Buddha telling him to bear it as the fruit of karma now experienced in the here-&-now.

### Harm discovered late

[ST087 — Schindler's List](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST087%20%E2%80%94%20Schindler%27s%20List): Oskar Schindler arrives in Kraków hiring Jewish workers because they are cheap, and ends with his entire fortune spent buying lives; surrounded by the people he saved, he breaks down over this watch, this car, this could-have-been-more.

### Taking for others

[ST295 — Prometheus Steals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST295%20%E2%80%94%20Prometheus%20Steals): Prometheus steals fire from the gods and gives it to humanity, and is chained to a rock in the Caucasus with an eagle eating his liver daily, the liver regrowing nightly, forever.

### Rules and situations

[ST384 — The Tree Is Spared; the Goose Is Killed](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST384%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Tree%20Is%20Spared%3B%20the%20Goose%20Is%20Killed): a tree is spared because its wood is good for nothing and a goose is killed because it cannot cackle; asked which condition he would prefer, Zhuangzi smiles that he would rest halfway between the two, and that even that position cannot avoid trouble.

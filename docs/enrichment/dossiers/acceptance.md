# Acceptance

> What changes in a life when what cannot be changed stops being fought?

Acceptance is the point where the alleviation of suffering becomes practical. The word is used loosely everywhere; the traditions that thought about it hardest all converge on one negative definition: acceptance is not resignation. The ACT programme defines it as the active and aware embrace of private events without unnecessary attempts to change their frequency or form, taught as the alternative to experiential avoidance. The Stoic line is Enchiridion 8, wishing that things happen as they do happen. Nietzsche's is the most radical: amor fati, wanting what happens rather than merely bearing it. The Buddhist face of the same question is the second arrow, the suffering added by the war against the facts. The map holds them side by side, with the equanimity boundary stated where it must be.

## Nearby terms

Resignation: what acceptance is not; the distinguishing mark carried in nearly every entry below.

Equanimity: the even-minded stance toward experience, the nearest neighbour; the boundary is stated in the map's classical group — acceptance as willingness toward what is already here, equanimity as evenness of mind.

Tolerance: enduring while the war continues; the DBT material distinguishes the two.

Surrender: giving up the fight in the military sense; not a term the cards use, noted here to keep it out.

## The map: the entries on acceptance

### The clinical programme

[Acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acceptance): the Hayes et al. 2006 definition, the alternative to experiential avoidance, and the card's own statement that it is not resignation. The definition:

> "Acceptance involves the active and aware embrace of those private events occasioned by one's history without unnecessary attempts to change their frequency or form, especially when doing so would cause psychological harm." [Experiential avoidance theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Experiential%20avoidance%20theory): the mechanism acceptance answers; suffering compounds when contact with unwanted inner experience is refused.

[Acceptance alongside values-based action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acceptance%20alongside%20values-based%20action): the pairing; acceptance and committed action as two of the six.

## Related questions and parallels

Is acceptance passive? Every entry above answers no in its own way. The ACT definition says active and aware embrace; the chronic-pain definition is a behavior pattern of engagement; the Stoic line is a discipline of wanting. The question survives because the pop-psychology usage of the word does not.

Does acceptance mean approving? The cards distinguish the two repeatedly; the radical-acceptance card's mechanism is the ending of the second suffering, not the endorsement of the first cause.

Where does acceptance end? The AF040 principle keeps the two questions apart; the map holds the boundary as stated there, not resolved.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

The practices are [Radical acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K123%20%E2%80%94%20Radical%20acceptance)'s skill and [Cognitive acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K385%20%E2%80%94%20Cognitive%20acceptance%3A%20watch%20thoughts%20float%20by)'s watching of thoughts as they float by, with the AF039 willingness as the daily form and the AF040 distinction as the check.

This is an editorial combination, not a validated protocol. The trial is: name one thing being fought this week, apply the willingness, and watch whether the behavior freed by it serves a stated value. Retain what frees; drop what does not.

# Acceptance

> What changes in a life when what cannot be changed stops being fought?

The word is used loosely everywhere, and reading the traditions that thought about it hardest, I hold one negative definition: acceptance is not resignation. The clinical definition says active and aware embrace of private events without unnecessary attempts to change their frequency or form ([Hayes et al. 2006, full text](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf)). The Stoic line says not to demand that things happen as you wish, but to wish that they happen as they do happen ([Enchiridion 8, Long translation, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)). Nietzsche's is the most radical: [amor fati](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Amor%20fati), wanting what happens rather than merely bearing it. The Buddhist face of the same question is the [second arrow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20second%20arrow), the suffering added by the war against the facts.

I keep three distinctions in view while reading all of them. Acceptance is not approval: the radical-acceptance material ends the second suffering, it does not endorse the first cause. Acceptance is not passive: the definitions gathered here describe something a person does. And acceptance has a boundary: [AF040 — Distinguish accepting a feeling from accepting a circumstance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF040%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20accepting%20a%20feeling%20from%20accepting%20a%20circumstance) keeps the two questions apart, because accepting that grief is present is one work and accepting that an injustice should stand is another.

### Nearby terms

- [Equanimity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Equanimity): the nearest neighbour, the even-minded stance toward experience, upekkha as its Buddhist face. The boundary is a matter of aim: acceptance is willingness toward what is already here; equanimity is evenness of mind across what comes.

- Tolerance: enduring while the war continues. The Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) material distinguishes the two, and the distinction is the whole difference.

- [Experiential avoidance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Experiential%20avoidance%20theory): the mechanism acceptance answers, suffering that compounds when contact with unwanted inner experience is refused.

- Surrender: giving up the fight in the military sense. Kept out of the map on purpose, so that it does not quietly become the reader's word for the whole theme.

## What acceptance is, and what it refuses to be

### The clinical programme

In the clinical programme, acceptance is defined against the failure mode it answers. [Experiential avoidance theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Experiential%20avoidance%20theory) holds that suffering compounds when a person refuses contact with unwanted inner experience, because the refusal must be maintained, and the maintaining is itself suffering. [Acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acceptance) is defined against that backdrop:

> "Acceptance involves the active and aware embrace of those private events occasioned by one's history without unnecessary attempts to change their frequency or form, especially when doing so would cause psychological harm." ([Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda & Lillis 2006, full-text PDF](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf))

[Acceptance and Commitment Therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acceptance%20and%20Commitment%20Therapy) (ACT) builds the intervention from six interlinked processes, and [acceptance alongside values-based action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acceptance%20alongside%20values-based%20action) is the pairing that matters most here: willingness without direction can drift into passivity, so the embrace of inner events travels with committed action toward what a person stands for. The definition is behavior, not mood, all the way down: [chronic pain acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Chronic%20pain%20acceptance) means engagement in activity with pain present, without restrictions by pain or attempts to avoid or control it.

Linehan's [radical acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Radical%20acceptance) is the DBT form, taught in the distress-tolerance module: the fully open experience of what is, with the unwillingness to accept pain taught as the route to greater suffering. The card that adapts her handout steps carries her own caution beside the definition: you cannot make yourself accept, and telling someone to accept is invalidating; her word for the instruction is practice. [AF039 — Learn acceptance — the willingness to have a feeling without fighting it](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF039%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20acceptance%20%E2%80%94%20the%20willingness%20to%20have%20a%20feeling%20without%20fighting%20it) is the principle form, carrying the Sallatha Sutta's instructed disciple.

### The classical material

The Stoic version is a discipline of wanting. [Serene acceptance of necessity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Serene%20acceptance%20of%20necessity) is Enchiridion 8: don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish that they happen as they do happen, and you will go on well. The demand is the suffering; the wish that replaces it is trained, not wished for. [ST188 — Epictetus and the Leg](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST188%20%E2%80%94%20Epictetus%20and%20the%20Leg) dramatises it: a slave lamed by his master's violence, reported by later sources, saying as the leg breaks that he told him it would. The anecdote's historicity is uncertain; what the tradition preserved is a body that was the site of the dichotomy, the leg not up to him, the speech up to him.

Nietzsche turns the discipline up a notch: [amor fati](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Amor%20fati), the love of fate, wanting nothing to be different and loving the necessary rather than merely bearing it. The Daoist neighbour is [D098 — Accept what destiny brings (li ming)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D098%20%E2%80%94%20Accept%20what%20destiny%20brings%20%28li%20ming%29), the Liezi's staged debate between Endeavour and Destiny, in which long life, failure, rank and wealth come about of themselves and no one directs them. At the far edge of the theme, [death acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Death%20acceptance) in Wong's research programme splits into three attitudes measured by the Death Attitude Profile-Revised (DAP-R): neutral acceptance of death as a natural process, approach acceptance looking forward to a blessed afterlife, and escape acceptance of death as a better alternative to present sufferings ([Jansen et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6740004/)).

### The junction with suffering

Acceptance is where the alleviation of suffering becomes practical, and the junction is the second arrow. [The second arrow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20second%20arrow) is the suffering added by the war against the facts; [Judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm) is the Stoic statement of the same line, men disturbed not by things but by the opinions about the things. What acceptance trains, on this junction view, is the dropping of the added layer. It does not remove the first arrow; none of the sources gathered here promise that.

Is acceptance passive? Each entry gathered here answers no in its own way: the ACT definition says active and aware, the chronic-pain definition is a behavior pattern of engagement, the Stoic line is a discipline of wanting. The question survives because the loose popular usage of the word does. Does acceptance mean approving? The radical-acceptance mechanism ends the second suffering without endorsing the first cause; the two are kept apart in the material gathered here. Where acceptance ends, [AF040](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF040%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20accepting%20a%20feeling%20from%20accepting%20a%20circumstance) keeps the boundary as a pair of distinct questions rather than a resolved rule.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is adversity providentially assigned?

Two traditions tell a person to accept what happens, and they differ on whether what happens is assigned a purpose.

Marcus Aurelius grounds it in providence. Meditations V.8 compares adversity to a physician's prescription and concludes: "accept everything which happens, even if it seem disagreeable, because it leads to this, to the health of the universe and to the prosperity and felicity of Zeus (the universe). For he would not have brought on any man what he has brought, if it were not useful for the whole" ([Meditations V.8, Long translation, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html)). The Liezi's debate ends on the other footing: when a thing is destined, no one directs it, and long life and short, failure and success, high rank and low, wealth and poverty come about of themselves ([D098](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D098%20%E2%80%94%20Accept%20what%20destiny%20brings%20%28li%20ming%29), the Liezi's own answer to Endeavour; corroborated in the Giles translation, [Liezi ch. 6](https://www.thedaobums.com/topic/54935-liezi-chapter-6-effort-and-destiny/)).

The difference is in the direction of the happening, not in the acceptance of it. Marcus's passage reads each adversity as prescribed, fitted to the good of the whole; the Liezi's passage drops the question of who, if anyone, is doing the prescribing. Whether the Liezi's person can be grateful, or trusts anything, is not settled by the passages; what they state is the presence or absence of a prescriber. My lean is editorial rather than sourced: I prefer beginning without attributing a purpose to the loss, but a reader who needs the loss to mean something is not misreading Marcus.

### Is acceptance a different process from changing what one thinks?

Hayes and colleagues present acceptance as part of a model that differs from standard cognitive-behavioural therapy in how it works: "What evidence is available suggests that ACT works through different processes than active treatment comparisons, including traditional CBT" ([Hayes, Luoma, Bond, Masuda & Lillis 2006, abstract](https://anxietyinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Hayes-et-al.-2006.pdf)). The paper adds: "Component analysis studies have generally failed to find support for the importance of direct cognitive change strategies" (same paper).

Hofmann answers on two fronts. His 2008 abstract says the criticism ACT offers against CBT rests on a misrepresentation of the empirical evidence, that ACT's strategies are not specific to the theory and philosophy underlying ACT, and that they resemble Morita therapy, developed 80 years earlier ([Hofmann 2008, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy: New Wave or Morita Therapy?, Clinical Psychology: Science and Practice, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1468-2850.2008.00138.x)). With Asmundson he places the two on one map: CBT works on antecedent-focused regulation and the acceptance strategies of ACT counteract response-focused strategies such as suppression, and the techniques are fully compatible with CBT ([Hofmann & Asmundson 2008, Clinical Psychology Review, abstract, PMID 17904260](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=17904260&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)).

I have read the abstracts on Hofmann's side, not the full papers, and I have not checked his account of the evidence against Hayes's. What the abstracts do show is that the quarrel is about the theory of why acceptance helps: a separate process, or one stage of emotion regulation among others. Hofmann's point about compatibility matters for a writer, because it means the practice described here does not require siding with either theory. My lean is practical and unsourced: use acceptance where the added layer is the problem, as the junction section describes, and leave the mechanism question to the trials.

### Is bearing necessity enough, or must it be loved?

Epictetus sets the bar at wishing: "Don't demand that things happen as you wish, but wish that they happen as they do happen, and you will go on well" ([Enchiridion 8, MIT Classics](https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html)). Elsewhere in the same text, a little spilt oil is "the price paid for equanimity, for tranquillity" (same text).

Nietzsche sets it higher. "Not only must the necessary be borne, and on no account concealed,--all idealism is falsehood in the face of necessity,--but it must also be loved" ([Ecce Homo, Why I Am So Clever, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/52190/pg52190.txt)). He also attacks the Stoic ground directly: Stoicism, he writes, is self-tyranny, because the Stoics read their own ideals into nature and then claim to follow it ([Beyond Good and Evil, section 9, Zimmern translation, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4363/pg4363.txt)).

The wording is closer than the quarrel suggests, since a wish that things be as they are already goes past bearing. The difference the texts support is in the aim and the charge: tranquillity against a formula for greatness, and the accusation that the Stoic stance is a disguised act of will. Whether loving a loss is something a person can be asked to do is a further question that neither text addresses; the [radical acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Radical%20acceptance) card carries Linehan's caution that telling someone to accept can invalidate them. My lean is editorial: begin with Epictetus's lower bar, and treat Nietzsche's as something a person may arrive at, not something to be handed over.


## Practice: willingness, and what it frees

::Variation:: If a feeling, a fact or a loss is being met with a running war of clenching, rehearsing and bracing, the variation to make is a change of relationship to what is already here: willingness toward the feeling, without needing the feeling to change first. The maladaptive pattern is the war; the adaptive direction is the space the war was occupying.

::Selection:: Choose one exercise and run it on something small first, an irritation, a delay, a minor loss. Willingness trained on the small things is available when a large one arrives.

### Open to what is here

1. [K123 — Radical acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K123%20%E2%80%94%20Radical%20acceptance): accept the reality of a situation you cannot change, as acknowledgment rather than approval. Linehan's steps run from noticing the fight against reality to practicing with the whole self ([Guilford reproducible teaching materials, DBT Skills Training Manual](https://www.guilford.com/add/linehan7_old/lin-p-4teaching.pdf?t=1)); her caution stands: acceptance is practiced, not commanded.

2. [K385 — Cognitive acceptance: watch thoughts float by](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K385%20%E2%80%94%20Cognitive%20acceptance%3A%20watch%20thoughts%20float%20by): for five minutes, watch thoughts pass like clouds, including the recurring one, without judging or trying to change or avoid them. The aim is not to make the thought go away; it is to stop adding the struggle on top of it.

### Check the boundary

3. [AF040 — Distinguish accepting a feeling from accepting a circumstance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF040%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20accepting%20a%20feeling%20from%20accepting%20a%20circumstance): when acceptance feels like betrayal, ask which of the two is being asked for. Accepting that grief or anger is present does not accept the circumstance that caused it, and the second question, whether to work to change the circumstance, is a separate one with its own answer.

::Retention:: This is an editorial combination rather than a validated protocol. Keep the exercise that changes what you do when the war starts; drop what does not. What people keep is the willingness itself, in whatever form worked for them.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

[ST188 — Epictetus and the Leg](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST188%20%E2%80%94%20Epictetus%20and%20the%20Leg): born a slave, Epictetus is reported by later sources to have been lamed by his master, warning as the leg was being broken that he had told him it would break, and saying when it did that he had told him so.

[ST293 — The Three Metamorphoses](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST293%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Three%20Metamorphoses): Zarathustra's spirit becomes camel, lion and child; the child is the sacred yes-saying, a new beginning and a self-propelled wheel, and only the child creates. I read the sequence as the story the amor fati material leans on: bearing, then destroying, then affirming, in that order.

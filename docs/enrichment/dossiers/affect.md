# Affect: the domain of feeling, and what can be done with it

> What is an emotion made of, and what is it telling the person who feels it?

Feelings are where a life registers itself. An emotion can arrive in the chest before an email is opened, in the flatness of a week that looked fine on paper, in the lift a friend's good news gives. When I ask what an emotion is made of, I am asking what is happening in those moments, and whether the feeling deserves the authority it tends to claim.

The question has consequences. How I read a feeling decides whether I act on it, talk it down, name it, or let it pass. Get the reading wrong in either direction and something goes wrong with the action: a signal treated as a verdict, or a verdict ignored because it arrived as a mood.

The question runs through several domains at once. What an emotion is made of, how the feelings are structured, how they behave over time, what they do to thinking, how they are shaped and regulated, and how they move between people are aspects of one material. The specific states (anger, anxiety, fear, sadness, happiness) are each treated on their own; what belongs here are the questions that cut across all of them.

### Nearby terms

- Affect and emotion: [Affects](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affects) is Spinoza's name for the family: a state of the body's power of acting, together with the idea of that state. Psychologists tend to reserve affect for the raw, dimension-level feeling and emotion for the categorised episode; the boundary is drawn differently by [Basic emotion theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Basic%20emotion%20theory), the [Circumplex model of affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Circumplex%20model%20of%20affect) and the [Theory of constructed emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Theory%20of%20constructed%20emotion).

- Feeling and fact: [Distinguishing feeling from fact](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distinguishing%20feeling%20from%20fact) is the Stoic separation of the thing from the judgement about it, applied here to feelings and their interpretations.

- Judgment side: [Affect heuristic](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20heuristic) handles the judgment side of the intrusion, treated in the cognition dossier; here the question is what the feeling does.

- The contribution of feeling to a life going well is treated in the flourishing dossiers; see [Common flourishing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Common%20flourishing) and [Eudaimonic well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20well-being). Feeling well and living well are different questions, kept different here.

## What a feeling is, and what it asks of the person feeling it

### Does the body run ahead of the feeling?

James's 1884 proposal is the [James-Lange theory of emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=James-Lange%20theory%20of%20emotion). Common sense, he says, has the order backwards: we do not cry because we are sorry, or strike because we are angry; the bodily change comes first, and the emotion is the feeling of that change:

> "Common sense says, we lose our fortune, are sorry and weep; we meet a bear, are frightened and run; we are insulted by a rival, are angry and strike. The hypothesis here to be defended says that this order of sequence is incorrect, that the one mental state is not immediately induced by the other, that the bodily manifestations must first be interposed between, and that the more rational statement is that we feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble"
>
> ([James 1884](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm), Mind)

If James is right, the practical target of emotional work is partly the body, which is why [Action precedes feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20precedes%20feeling) and [Action before feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20before%20feeling) pair his proposal with Aristotle's observation that we become just by doing just acts. The Stoic-cognitivist line pushes the other way: on the view the Stanford Encyclopedia follows through Solomon, Neu and Nussbaum, my anger at someone is the judgment that I have been wronged by that person; see [Emotions as judgments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotions%20as%20judgments). Where James locates the emotion in the body's change, the judgment tradition locates it in an evaluation the person can in principle examine and revise.

Between those poles, other accounts spread the material across body, idea and practice. [Affects](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affects) gives Spinoza's version: an affect is a state of the body's power of acting, increased or lessened, together with the idea of that state, the feeling as a fact about capacity, which is why [Joy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Joy) for him is a transition to greater perfection rather than a static state. [Emotion as construction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20as%20construction) reads Nietzsche's art of giving style to character as applying to feeling itself: emotional life as material re-fitted by long practice. [Fellow feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fellow%20feeling) carries Hume's sympathy, the eighteenth-century mechanism by which others' emotions become ours. Each account implies a different job for the person feeling: attend to the body, examine the judgment, or practise the material.

### The families the feelings sort into

Whatever an emotion is made of, a life contains recognisable kinds. The families are there to be told apart. [Fear](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fear) is the defense-mobilising emotion, Öhman and Mineka's evolved module for threat. The [Self-conscious emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-conscious%20emotions) (embarrassment, guilt, pride, shame) are grouped by their entry requirement, the involvement of the self, since they differ less in feel than in what it takes to have one at all.

Within that family the distinctions carry weight. [Shame](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shame) judges the whole self; [Guilt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Guilt) attaches to a specific act, which is why [Guilt motivates reparative behaviour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Guilt%20motivates%20reparative%20behaviour): its adaptive job is to move the person who did the harm toward undoing it.

[Pride](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pride) is the emotion the traditions cannot agree about, the wisdom traditions' fall against Aristotle's crown of the virtuous.

Envy splits by what it wants. [Envy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Envy) is pain at another's good fortune in van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters' definition; [Benign envy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Benign%20envy) moves the person to move up toward the other, [Malicious envy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Malicious%20envy) to pull the other down.

The [Self-transcendent emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-transcendent%20emotions) (awe, gratitude, love, peace) sort by their fit to religious and spiritual life, and [Mudita (sympathetic joy)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mudita%20%28sympathetic%20joy%29), the third brahmavihara, is gladness at another's good fortune as if it were one's own, the emotional answer to envy's question.

### The passions, named and ordered

The older traditions treated this whole territory as the passions, and asked a further question: can the crowd be ordered? Spinoza's Ethics Part III is [The taxonomy of passions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20taxonomy%20of%20passions): the crowd reduced to three primary affects, the rest derived by combination with objects. Part III opens with the complaint, recorded under [Naming the passions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Naming%20the%20passions), that no one has determined the emotions' definitions.

Ordering the passions suggests correcting them. The early discourses give [The counter-affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20counter-affect), pratipakṣā-bhāvanā, cultivating the opposite when an unskillful state takes hold. Modern psychology finds the same structure in a milder key: [Passion theory Vallerand](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Passion%20theory%20Vallerand) distinguishes harmonious from obsessive passion for an activity, according to how the activity has been internalised into identity. The question in each case is what the strong feeling is doing to the life around it.

### How the feelings are structured

How many kinds of feeling there are is a live scientific question, and the answer shapes everything downstream: measurement, regulation, even what counts as an emotional disorder. Three structures compete here. [Basic emotion theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Basic%20emotion%20theory) holds a small number of emotions (anger, fear, disgust, sadness, enjoyment) to be universal, biologically grounded, distinct in expression and function; Ekman's families of related states. The [Circumplex model of affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Circumplex%20model%20of%20affect), Russell's 1980 alternative, drops the list for two axes, valence and arousal, every state a point in the plane; the [Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance Model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance%20Model) of Mehrabian and Russell adds a third.

[Appraisal theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Appraisal%20theory) and the [Component Process Model of Emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Component%20Process%20Model%20of%20Emotion), Scherer's account of emotion as an episode of synchronised change produced by appraisal checks, locate the structure in the evaluation rather than the feeling itself.

Barrett's [Theory of constructed emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Theory%20of%20constructed%20emotion) goes further: emotions are built from affect, concepts and social reality, with no pre-wired circuit for each basic emotion. [Embodied emotion theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Embodied%20emotion%20theory) grounds the construction in re-enactments of affective, sensory and motor states. And a fourth family asks a different question altogether: [Functional accounts of emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Functional%20accounts%20of%20emotion) (Keltner and Gross framing) explain emotions by what they do.

Structure matters practically because it sets what can be trained. If emotions are positions in a two-dimensional space, then precision is a skill of locating oneself on the axes; if they are built categories, then the concepts one brings partly determine what one feels. The precision cards develop exactly that consequence. [Emotional granularity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20granularity) and [Emotion differentiation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20differentiation) concern how finely a person distinguishes among their own feelings.

[Emotion awareness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20awareness), Lane and Schwartz's five levels, makes that capacity a developmental ladder; [Emodiversity theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emodiversity%20theory) asks how many distinct emotions a life contains and how evenly; [Mixed emotions theory Miyamoto](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mixed%20emotions%20theory%20Miyamoto) finds Japan and the United States differ in how readily opposites are felt together.

The positive side of the ledger has its own programme. [Positive emotions research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20emotions%20research) and [Broaden-and-build](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Broaden-and-build) carry Fredrickson's proposal that positive emotion widens the momentary thought-action repertoire and builds lasting resources; [Fredrickson's positivity portfolios](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fredrickson%27s%20positivity%20portfolios) is the applied form, a personal collection of photos, letters, quotes and songs built to bring back one particular positive emotion.

### Feeling as a moving target

A feeling has a trajectory: it rises, lasts and fades. [Affective chronometry](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20chronometry), Davidson's term, studies that temporal profile: how fast an emotion rises, how long it lasts. The [Affective dynamics framework](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20dynamics%20framework) treats emotion as a time series in which the fluctuation matters as much as the average, and [Emotional inertia theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20inertia%20theory) names the resistance to change: how strongly earlier states predict later ones. The felt difference between having a feeling and being stuck in one turns out to be a measurable property.

The trajectory extends into the future, where feelings mislead in documented ways. [Affective forecasting](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20forecasting) is Wilson and Gilbert's account of predictions about one's own future feelings and the errors they reliably contain; [Anticipatory affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anticipatory%20affect), Loewenstein's visceral factors, is the feeling state that arrives before the outcome and bends decisions.

The past is also unreliable: [Mood-congruent memory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mood-congruent%20memory), Bower's 1981 hypotheses, has affect biasing recall toward congruent material, and [Fading affect bias](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fading%20affect%20bias) is the finding that the intensity of emotions tied to memories of pleasant events fades more slowly than that of unpleasant ones. A feeling that is intense, forward-looking and backward-coloring at once gives its owner several distinct chances to be wrong.

### What feeling does to thinking

The simplest statement of the intrusion is Schwarz's [Affect as information](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20as%20information): people consult their current feelings when judging, as in the sunny-day life-satisfaction result. The [Affect heuristic](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20heuristic), Finucane, Alhakami, Slovic and Johnson's term, extends it to risk: positive or negative feelings about an object drive judgments of its risk and benefit, a puzzle for the mind's bookkeeping, since the world's risks and benefits are positively correlated.

Feeling also enters reasoning as its substrate. The [Somatic marker hypothesis (Damasio)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Somatic%20marker%20hypothesis%20%28Damasio%29) has decision-making steered by marker signals arising in the body's bioregulatory processes, emotion as a condition of good reasoning rather than its enemy.

[Moral emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20emotions), Tangney's review, link moral standards to moral behaviour. [Affective disposition theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20disposition%20theory), Zillmann and Cantor's account of media enjoyment (we like seeing liked characters succeed and disliked ones suffer) and the [Reinforcement-Affect Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Reinforcement-Affect%20Theory) of Byrne and Clore (we come to like stimuli and people associated with positive affect) show the same mechanism at work in liking itself.

One entry in this cluster is a practice rather than a finding: [Affect labeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20labeling), putting a feeling into words as it occurs, with a 2007 neuroimaging study behind it. That sits exactly at the hinge the section keeps arriving at: the feeling affects the thought, and the treatment of the feeling affects it right back.

### Regulating and shaping feeling

If feelings inform thought, the next question is whether they can be worked with. Gross's answer is a definition and a map: [Emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20regulation) is the processes by which we influence which emotions we have, when we have them, and how we experience and express them. The [Process model of emotion regulation Gross](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Process%20model%20of%20emotion%20regulation%20Gross) gives five points of intervention: situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change, response modulation.

The [Extended process model of emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extended%20process%20model%20of%20emotion%20regulation) later reframes regulation as something a person decides to do: noticing, choosing, carrying out. [Mid-level emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mid-level%20emotion%20regulation) covers the strategies deployed in the moment, inside that valuation account.

The best-studied contrast inside the map is reappraisal against suppression. [Cognitive reappraisal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20reappraisal) changes an emotional event's meaning, so that the feeling that follows changes; the [Expressive suppression versus reappraisal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Expressive%20suppression%20versus%20reappraisal), Gross and John's 2003 comparison, sets the conscious inhibition of expression against it; and the [Emotional suppression theory Butler Gross](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20suppression%20theory%20Butler%20Gross) adds that instructed suppression raises blood pressure in regulators and partners, suppression as a cost paid socially.

One limit matters: [Reappraisal's effectiveness depends on separating the feeling from the appraisal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Reappraisal%27s%20effectiveness%20depends%20on%20separating%20the%20feeling%20from%20the%20appraisal): it works by changing the appraisal, so the person has to be able to tell the two apart.

The clinical programmes extend the toolkit. [Opposite action (Linehan)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Opposite%20action%20%28Linehan%29) is the DBT skill of acting against the action urge that comes with an emotion; [Expressive writing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Expressive%20writing) is Pennebaker and Beall's 1986 paradigm.

The [DBT Biosocial Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=DBT%20Biosocial%20Theory) and the [Dialectical behaviour therapy, emotion modulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dialectical%20behaviour%20therapy%2C%20emotion%20modulation) module address emotions that fire easily, hit hard and take long to subside, and locate their origin in a biological vulnerability transacting with an invalidating environment.

[Compassion-focused therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion-focused%20therapy), Gilbert's approach, targets high shame and self-criticism.

Two older strategies survive inside the modern material. [Pre-commitment as wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pre-commitment%20as%20wisdom) is Seneca's counsel for dealing with emotion by timing, arranging in advance what the emotion would otherwise undo. And the pursuit itself can misfire: [The paradox of valuing a goal excessively](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20paradox%20of%20valuing%20a%20goal%20excessively) carries Mauss and colleagues' finding on wanting something too much.

Regulation is also not one-size-fits-all, at two levels. [State-matched emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=State-matched%20emotion%20regulation) matches strategy to circumstance; and [Ideal affect theory Tsai](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ideal%20affect%20theory%20Tsai) finds that cultures differ in the emotions they value and want to feel, and that people act to move toward their ideal affect. What counts as successful regulation depends partly on what a person, and their culture, wants to feel.

### Feeling between people

The last movement of the thread moves outward: feelings travel between people. [Emotional contagion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20contagion), Hatfield's moment-to-moment transmission between people in contact, and [Group affective synchronisation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Group%20affective%20synchronisation), Barsade's transfer of moods in groups, give the mechanism; the old warning is [Beware the moods of the many](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beware%20the%20moods%20of%20the%20many), Laozi, Daodejing 20. A feeling caught this way presents itself exactly as one's own, which is the practical problem.

The positive side of the transmission has its own tradition. [Empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathy) is the felt understanding of another's experience: a family of capacities running from Buddhist compassion through Confucian shu to Levinas; [Sympathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sympathy) is Adam Smith's account of the resonance by which others' joy and misery become in some measure ours; [Compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion) is being moved by suffering and motivated to act.

Between people who know each other well, transmission becomes regulation. [Co-regulation of affect in relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Co-regulation%20of%20affect%20in%20relationships), Butler and Randall's dyadic system, and [Co-regulation in relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Co-regulation%20in%20relationships), Butler's 2011 TIES framework, describe partners regulating each other's affect. When that loop turns negative it is [Conflict spirals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conflict%20spirals), Gottman's pattern in which one partner's negative affect is met with the other's. The feeling between people is the same material as the feeling within, with the added fact that each person's response is the other person's stimulus.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Are the emotions discrete categories, or positions on two dimensions?

Ekman's research programme takes the categorical side, and states the alternative it rejects in the process:

> "We each found high agreement across members of diverse Western and Eastern literate cultures in selecting emotion terms that fit facial expressions."
>
> "Distinctive universal expressions have been identified for anger, fear, disgust, sadness, and enjoyment."
>
> "The research on facial expressions has also shown the utility of conceiving of emotions as separate discrete states, such as fear, anger, and disgust, rather than simply as positive versus negative states or even more simply as differing only in respect to arousal." ([Ekman 1993](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~pr10/iui/ekman93.pdf), American Psychologist 48(4), April 1993, "Facial Expression and Emotion", pp. 384-387)

The circumplex model takes the dimensional position Ekman names as the rival hypothesis: that what varies is valence and arousal, not category membership. Its authors state the contrast themselves:

> "This model stands in contrast to theories of basic emotions, which posit that a discrete and independent neural system subserves every emotion."
>
> "We propose that clinicians and researchers move away from a strictly basic emotion model of affective states, where each emotion is thought to emerge from independent neural systems, to more dimensional models of emotions, in which all affective states are understood to arise from common, overlapping neurophysiological systems."
>
> Posner, Russell and Peterson propose instead that "all affective states arise from two independent neurophysiological systems," the valence and arousal systems ([Posner, Russell and Peterson 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2367156/), Development and Psychopathology).

The incompatibility is stated by the circumplex authors themselves: affective states arising from common, overlapping systems against the discrete and independent neural system they attribute to theories of basic emotions. Ekman's own passage argues from cross-cultural agreement in reading expressions; the neural-systems framing of his side is his rivals' characterization, and I do not attribute it to him beyond that. Where the causal structure sits (in separate systems each owning an emotion, or in shared systems whose combinations produce them) is what the disagreement turns on.

### Is an emotion a bodily change, or a judgment?

The two accounts identify the emotion with different things. James's hypothesis identifies it with the feeling of bodily change; the judgmental tradition identifies it with an evaluation. Each side is quotable. From James:

> "the more rational statement is that we feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble" ([James 1884](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm), Mind)

From the cognitivist side, following the Stoics:

> "On a common interpretation of their view, my anger at someone is the judgment that I have been wronged by that person. To generalize, the proposal is that an emotion *E* is a judgment that the formal object of *E* is instantiated (by some particular object *X*)." ([Scarantino and de Sousa, SEP "Emotion", §5](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotion/))

Where one locates the emotion decides where the work is applied. If the emotion is the feeling of bodily change, the work addresses the body: what James's line implies for practice is spelled out by the action-precedes-feeling pairing above. If the emotion is a judgment, the work examines the evaluation: the reappraisal material above takes exactly that opening. The two passages state the identifications; they do not settle which is right, and a reader of the affect material has to choose where to work first.

### Does the gambling task show guidance before reportable knowledge?

[Somatic marker hypothesis (Damasio)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Somatic%20marker%20hypothesis%20%28Damasio%29) proposes that signals arising in the body's regulatory processes influence decisions, sometimes consciously and sometimes outside awareness ([Bechara and Damasio 2005, Games and Economic Behavior](https://people.ict.usc.edu/~gratch/CSCI534/Readings/The%20somatic%20marker%20hypothesis.pdf)). One disputed piece of evidence came from the Iowa gambling task. Bechara and colleagues' own summary of it: "Normals began to choose advantageously before they realized which strategy worked best, whereas prefrontal patients continued to choose disadvantageously even after they knew the correct strategy." They read that sequence as support for guidance by nonconscious somatic markers, proposing that overt reasoning "is preceded by a nonconscious biasing step that uses neural systems other than those that support declarative knowledge" ([Bechara et al. 1997, Science, abstract via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Deciding%20advantageously%20before%20knowing%20the%20advantageous%20strategy%22&format=json&resultType=core)). The disagreement concerns how that gap between performance and knowledge should be explained.

Maia and McClelland challenged the interpretation with more sensitive questions about what participants knew. Participants reported knowledge of the advantageous strategy more reliably than they behaved advantageously. When they behaved advantageously, their verbal reports nearly always provided evidence of quantitative knowledge about the decks' outcomes sufficient to guide those choices ([Maia and McClelland 2004](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22reexamination%20of%20the%20evidence%20for%20the%20somatic%20marker%20hypothesis%22&format=json&resultType=core)). The authors described these findings as compatible with choices and verbal reports drawing on consciously accessible knowledge: "there is no need to appeal to nonconscious somatic markers."

What the dispute turns on is whether advantageous play requires an explanation in terms of nonconscious markers, or whether reportable knowledge accounts for it. I give the methodological objection weight, since the claim that participants lacked knowledge depends on how their knowledge was assessed, and the re-examination supplies an alternative explanation of the task performance. Whether marker signals also contributed is a separate question this comparison does not settle.

## Practice: working with what a feeling is doing

::Variation:: The same emotion behaves differently at different time-scales, moods move through groups, and feelings arrive already colored by memory and expectation. Vary the point of intervention before settling on one technique: some feelings yield to naming, some to writing, some to action, some to waiting.

::Selection::

1. [Affect labeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K159%20%E2%80%94%20Affect%20labeling): name the emotion precisely, not "stressed" but "tight chest, dread, buzzing". Notice whether the intensity changes once it has a name.

2. [Feeling-tone pass](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K160%20%E2%80%94%20Feeling-tone%20pass): three times daily, name the tone of the moment as pleasant, unpleasant or neutral, before any story about it. Notice how often the story and the tone disagree.

3. [Emotional writing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K171%20%E2%80%94%20Emotional%20writing): fifteen to twenty minutes, three or four consecutive days, unedited and uncensored. Notice what the second or third day surfaces that the first did not.

4. [Body-first emotion work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K172%20%E2%80%94%20Body-first%20emotion%20work): when an emotion is hard to locate or name, start with the body: where is the tension, what is the temperature, what is the texture. Notice whether the feeling becomes nameable from the body up.

5. [Low emotional response](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K124%20%E2%80%94%20Low%20emotional%20response): when provoked, respond with low emotional intensity; do not match the other person's escalation. Notice what the other person's next move becomes when yours declines to rise.

Evidence limits: the outcome language above is the kernels' own text, compressed. The labeling kernel rests on the affect-labeling study cited on its card, the writing kernel on the expressive-writing literature cited on its card, the DBT module on the treatment literature for its clinical population, each cited at second hand, with no claim about study sizes or designs. The feeling-tone and low-response practices carry no outcome claim. The categorical-versus-dimensional disagreement is a live theoretical dispute; no practice selection follows from either side alone.

::Retention:: Keep what changes how quickly an emotion is named, or how long it outlasts its trigger; the chronometry and dynamics entries are the natural measurement tools for checking what a practice has changed.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The oldest questions about feeling, made concrete: Medea's monologue, Riley's headquarters, a doctor's electrodes, an Achilles who cannot stop.

### The oldest questions about feeling

[ST160 — Medea Knows](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST160%20%E2%80%94%20Medea%20Knows): Medea, abandoned by Jason for a royal marriage, plans the murder of her children as revenge; Euripides gives her the monologue that remains the definitive portrait of akrasia, knowing what evil she is about to do, and doing it.

### The feeling families

[ST062 — Sadness Gets a Role](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST062%20%E2%80%94%20Sadness%20Gets%20a%20Role): in Riley's mind, Joy runs operations and has been keeping Sadness contained; the move to San Francisco ejects them both, and on the journey back Joy discovers that the moment she was proudest of, Riley winning a hockey game, only happened because Sadness had first made her cry out for help.

[ST187 — Joy Reads the Memory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST187%20%E2%80%94%20Joy%20Reads%20the%20Memory): Joy, examining a sad core memory, rewinds it and watches the part after the loss, when Riley's parents and teammates came onto the ice and comforted her, the sadness and the comfort bound together in one memory.

[ST386 — Duchenne's Two Kinds of Smile](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST386%20%E2%80%94%20Duchenne%27s%20Two%20Kinds%20of%20Smile): a doctor holds electrodes to the face of his old patient and photographs the muscles of expression raised one at a time; the distinction he draws turns on the will, the muscle that pulls the mouth into a smile obeys it, and the muscle around the eyes, which he names the muscle of benevolence, does not.

[ST146 — Achilles' Disproportionate Rage](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST146%20%E2%80%94%20Achilles%27%20Disproportionate%20Rage): Agamemnon takes Briseis, and the anger is not wrong in its origin; the violation of honour calls for response; it is wrong in its measure: withdrawal from the war, every embassy and every offer of recompense refused.

### What feeling does to thinking

[ST170 — The Lotus-Eaters](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST170%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Lotus-Eaters): Odysseus' scouts reach a land where those who taste the lotus forget home and want only to stay, eating more; Odysseus drags them weeping to the ships and ties them under the benches.

### Regulating and shaping feeling

[ST185 — The Gentle Person's Anger](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST185%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Gentle%20Person%27s%20Anger): the gentle-tempered person is praised for being angry in the right way: at the right things, with the right people, for the right duration, in the right manner.

[ST401 — Dipa Ma Watches Anger Arise and Pass](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST401%20%E2%80%94%20Dipa%20Ma%20Watches%20Anger%20Arise%20and%20Pass): a Bengali woman in Rangoon loses a daughter at three months and a son at childbirth, and her heart trouble reaches the stage where her doctors expected her to die at any time; the practice she turns to is in the title: watching anger arise and pass.

[ST391 — Backsliding into Abundance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST391%20%E2%80%94%20Backsliding%20into%20Abundance): the Buddha's retrospective on his pre-awakening efforts: clenching his teeth and pressing his tongue against the roof of his mouth, he beat down, constrained and crushed his mind with his awareness, and the force failed.

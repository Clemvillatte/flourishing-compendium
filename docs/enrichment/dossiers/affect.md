# Affect: the domain of feeling, and what can be done with it

> When the feelings are already here — what are they made of, how are they mapped, and how much of them can be worked with?

This page groups the compendium's entries on feelings, moods and emotion: their structure, their change over time, their regulation, and their transmission between people. It runs from the classic questions — does the body run ahead of the feeling (James-Lange), are emotions universal packages (Ekman) or built from dimensions and interpretation (Russell, Barrett) — to the practical programmes that treat feeling as something regulable, differentiable and shared. The Attention and Cognition dossiers cover what awareness selects and what thinking does; this one covers the material both of them work on.

How to read this page: each entry is a link into the compendium's own explanation of that item, in the voice its author wrote. The one-line summaries here are orientation, not claims of their own; the fuller statements live on the linked pages, each carrying its own sources. Where the page quotes directly, the passage has been checked word-for-word against the linked carrier.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on affect

### The oldest questions about feeling

- [Affects](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affects): Spinoza's Ethics defines an affect as a state of the body's power of acting, increased or lessened, together with the idea of that state — the feeling as a fact about capacity.
- [James-Lange theory of emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=James-Lange%20theory%20of%20emotion): William James's 1884 proposal that the perception comes first, the bodily change second, and the emotion is the feeling of that change — the body runs ahead of the feeling. In James's own statement:

> "Common sense says, we lose our fortune, are sorry and weep; we meet a bear, are frightened and run; we are insulted by a rival, are angry and strike. The hypothesis here to be defended says that this order of sequence is incorrect, that the one mental state is not immediately induced by the other, that the bodily manifestations must first be interposed between, and that the more rational statement is that we feel sorry because we cry, angry because we strike, afraid because we tremble"
>
> ([James 1884](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/emotion.htm), Mind; primary text; quoted passage checked, the source's closing comma omitted)
- [Emotion as construction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20as%20construction): the compendium's label for a reading of The Gay Science §290 — the give-style-to-character art turned on feeling: emotional life as material re-fitted by long practice and daily work rather than merely undergone.
- [Emotions as judgments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotions%20as%20judgments): the Stoic-cognitivist line — anger at someone is the judgment that one has been wronged by them.
- [Fellow feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fellow%20feeling): the eighteenth-century sentimentalist name for the tendency to catch others' emotions; Hume's sympathy as the mechanism of Book III of the Treatise.
- [Distinguishing feeling from fact](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distinguishing%20feeling%20from%20fact): the Stoic distinction between things and judgements about them, applied to feelings and their interpretations.

### How the feelings are structured

- [Basic emotion theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Basic%20emotion%20theory): a small number of emotions — anger, fear, disgust, sadness, enjoyment — as universal, biologically grounded, distinct in expression and function; Ekman's families of related states.
- [Circumplex model of affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Circumplex%20model%20of%20affect): Russell's 1980 alternative to the list — two axes, valence and activation, every emotional state a point in the plane.
- [Theory of constructed emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Theory%20of%20constructed%20emotion): Barrett's programme — emotions built from affect, concepts and social reality; the brain holds no pre-wired circuits for each "basic" emotion.
- [Embodied emotion theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Embodied%20emotion%20theory): emotional processing draws on re-enactments of affective, sensory and motor states — the feeling rebuilt from the body's stored responses.
- [Component Process Model of Emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Component%20Process%20Model%20of%20Emotion): Scherer's account of emotion as an episode of synchronized change produced by a sequence of appraisal checks.
- [Functional accounts of emotion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Functional%20accounts%20of%20emotion): the family of theories — Keltner and Gross the framing — that explain emotions by what they do.
- [Positive emotions research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20emotions%20research): Fredrickson's broaden-and-build programme — positive emotion widens the momentary thought-action repertoire and builds lasting resources.
- [Mixed emotions theory Miyamoto](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mixed%20emotions%20theory%20Miyamoto): Japan and the United States compared on how readily opposites are felt together.
- [Emotional granularity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20granularity) and [Emotion differentiation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20differentiation): how finely a person distinguishes among their own feelings — the fineness that predicts better regulation.
- [Emotion awareness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20awareness): Lane and Schwartz's five structural levels of the ability to process emotion — a cognitive-developmental ladder.

### The feeling families

- [Fear](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fear): the emotion that mobilizes defense against threat — Öhman and Mineka's evolved module for fear elicitation and fear learning.
- [Joy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Joy): Spinoza's laetitia — joy defined by a change rather than a state: the transition from a lesser to a greater perfection.
- [Self-conscious emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-conscious%20emotions): the family that critically involves the self — embarrassment, guilt, pride, shame — grouped by their entry requirement rather than their feel.
- [Shame](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shame): the whole self judged negatively — distinct from guilt, which attaches to a specific act.
- [Guilt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Guilt): the self-conscious emotion about a specific wrong — Tangney's triad of shame, guilt and embarrassment reviewed for their relative advantages.
- [Pride](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pride): the emotion the traditions cannot agree about — the wisdom tradition's fall, Aristotle's crown of the virtuous.
- [Envy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Envy): pain at another's good fortune — van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters' definition.
- [Self-transcendent emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-transcendent%20emotions): the class that sorts by fit to religious and spiritual life — awe, gratitude, love and peace against amusement.
- [Mudita (sympathetic joy)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mudita%20%28sympathetic%20joy%29): the third brahmavihara — gladness at another's good fortune as if it were one's own.

### The passions, named and ordered

- [The taxonomy of passions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20taxonomy%20of%20passions): Spinoza's Ethics Part III project — the crowd of human emotions reduced to three primary affects, the rest derived by combination with objects.
- [Naming the passions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Naming%20the%20passions): the compendium's editorial heading for Spinoza's definitions of the emotions — Part III opens with the complaint that no one has determined them.
- [The counter-affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20counter-affect): pratipakṣā-bhāvanā, cultivating the opposite — the early discourses' method when an unskillful state of mind takes hold.
- [Passion theory Vallerand](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Passion%20theory%20Vallerand): the Dualistic Model of Passion — passion for an activity harmonious or obsessive, according to how the activity has been internalized into identity.

### Feeling as a moving target

- [Affective chronometry](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20chronometry): Davidson's study of the temporal profile of emotional response — how fast an emotion rises, how long it lasts.
- [Affective dynamics framework](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20dynamics%20framework): emotion as a time series rather than a score — the average matters, and so does the fluctuation.
- [Emotional inertia theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20inertia%20theory): how strongly earlier emotional states predict later ones — the resistance to emotional change.
- [Fading affect bias](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fading%20affect%20bias): the intensity of emotions tied to memories of pleasant events fades more slowly than that of unpleasant ones — the finding stated in one line by later research.
- [Affective forecasting](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20forecasting): Wilson and Gilbert's account of predictions about one's own future feelings, and the errors they reliably contain.
- [Anticipatory affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anticipatory%20affect): Loewenstein's visceral factors — the feeling state that arrives before the outcome and bends decisions.
- [Mood-congruent memory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mood-congruent%20memory): Bower's 1981 family of hypotheses — affect biases recall toward affectively congruent material.

### What feeling does to thinking

- [Affect as information](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20as%20information): Schwarz's feelings-as-information account — people consult their current feelings when judging, as in the sunny-day life-satisfaction result.
- [Affect heuristic](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20heuristic): Finucane, Alhakami, Slovic and Johnson's term for the finding that positive or negative feelings about an object drive judgments of its risk and benefit — a puzzle for the mind's bookkeeping, since the world's risks and benefits are positively correlated.
- [Affect labeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affect%20labeling): putting a feeling into words as it occurs — the 2007 neuroimaging result that labeling reduces amygdala reactivity.
- [Moral emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20emotions): Tangney and colleagues' review — the emotions that link moral standards to moral behavior.
- [Affective disposition theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Affective%20disposition%20theory): Zillmann and Cantor's account of media enjoyment — we like seeing liked characters succeed and disliked ones suffer.

### Regulating and shaping feeling

- [Emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotion%20regulation): Gross's definition — the processes by which we influence which emotions we have, when we have them, and how we experience and express them.
- [Process model of emotion regulation Gross](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Process%20model%20of%20emotion%20regulation%20Gross): the five points of regulation — situation selection, situation modification, attentional deployment, cognitive change, response modulation.
- [Extended process model of emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extended%20process%20model%20of%20emotion%20regulation): the later Gross — regulation as something you decide to do: noticing, choosing, carrying out.
- [Mid-level emotion regulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mid-level%20emotion%20regulation): the strategies deployed in the moment — the reappraisal-versus-suppression record the card sets against suppression and venting — inside Gross's valuation-system account of regulation.
- [Emotional suppression theory Butler Gross](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20suppression%20theory%20Butler%20Gross): instructed suppression raising blood pressure in regulators and partners — suppression as a cost paid socially.
- [Dialectical behaviour therapy, emotion modulation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dialectical%20behaviour%20therapy%2C%20emotion%20modulation): Linehan's skills module for emotions that fire easily, hit hard and take long to subside.
- [Action precedes feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20precedes%20feeling) and [Action before feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20before%20feeling): the double tradition — Aristotle's virtues acquired by doing the acts, and James's body moving before the feeling forms.
- [Ideal affect theory Tsai](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ideal%20affect%20theory%20Tsai): affect valuation theory — cultures differ in the emotions they value and want to feel; people act to move toward their ideal affect.

### Feeling between people

- [Emotional contagion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Emotional%20contagion): Hatfield's moment-to-moment transmission between people in contact, and Christakis and Fowler's network patterns of mood and behavior.
- [Empathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Empathy): the felt understanding of another's experience — a family of capacities: Buddhist compassion, Confucian shu, Hume's sympathy, Levinas's responsibility.
- [Sympathy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sympathy): Adam Smith's account in the Theory of Moral Sentiments — the resonance by which others' joy and misery become, in some measure, ours.
- [Compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion): being moved by suffering and motivated to act — Gilbert's definition, a basic kindness with deep awareness.
- [Group affective synchronisation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Group%20affective%20synchronisation): Barsade's group emotional contagion — the transfer of moods among people in a group and its influence on group dynamics.
- [Co-regulation of affect in relationships](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Co-regulation%20of%20affect%20in%20relationships): Butler and Randall's dyadic emotional system — partners regulating each other's affect rather than only their own.

### Sayings

- [Beware the moods of the many](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beware%20the%20moods%20of%20the%20many): Laozi, Daodejing 20; the compendium links it to guarding against affect contagion.

### Kernels

- [Low emotional response](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K124%20%E2%80%94%20Low%20emotional%20response): when provoked, respond with low emotional intensity; do not match the other person's escalation.
- [Affect labeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K159%20%E2%80%94%20Affect%20labeling): name the emotion precisely — not "stressed" but "tight chest, dread, buzzing"; the kernel's outcome claim, stated on its card, is that fine-grained labeling reduces amygdala reactivity.
- [Feeling-tone pass](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K160%20%E2%80%94%20Feeling-tone%20pass): three times daily, name the tone of the moment as pleasant, unpleasant or neutral, before any story about it.
- [Emotional writing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K171%20%E2%80%94%20Emotional%20writing): fifteen to twenty minutes, three or four days in a row, unedited and uncensored; the kernel's outcome claim, stated on its card, is that putting experience into language reduces its intensity.
- [Body-first emotion work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K172%20%E2%80%94%20Body-first%20emotion%20work): when an emotion is hard to locate or name, start with the body — tension, temperature, texture; the body often knows first.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Are the emotions discrete categories, or positions on two dimensions?

Ekman's research programme takes the categorical side, and states the alternative it rejects in the process:

> "We each found high agreement across members of diverse Western and Eastern literate cultures in selecting emotion terms that fit facial expressions."
>
> "Distinctive universal expressions have been identified for anger, fear, disgust, sadness, and enjoyment."
>
> Ekman concludes the research supports conceiving "of emotions as separate discrete states, such as fear, anger, and disgust, rather than simply as positive versus negative states or even more simply as differing only in respect to arousal." ([Ekman 1993](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~pr10/iui/ekman93.pdf), American Psychologist 49(4), "Facial Expression and Emotion"; award address; quoted passages checked, pp. 384-387)

The circumplex model takes the dimensional position Ekman names as the rival hypothesis — that what varies is valence and arousal, not category membership:

> "Investigations of the subjective, or experiential, components of emotion, rather than supporting a one to one correspondence between a discrete emotion and an underlying neural system, have instead suggested that emotions arise from cognitive interpretations of core physiological experiences (Cacioppo et al., 2000; Russell, 2003)."
>
> Posner, Russell and Peterson propose instead that "all affective states arise from two independent neurophysiological systems," the valence and arousal systems ([Posner, Russell and Peterson 2005](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2367156/), Development and Psychopathology; scholarly review; cited passages checked).

Each claim rests on a different evidential base: Ekman's on the cross-cultural recognition of facial expressions, the circumplex on the structure of subjective reports of feeling. Whether the two are competing explanations of the same phenomenon or descriptions of different phenomena is part of what is disputed.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: Work the map's breadth before committing to a single technique — the sayings warn that moods move through groups; the chronometry and dynamics entries warn that the same emotion behaves differently at different time-scales; the contagion and co-regulation entries show the feeling is not contained by the person feeling it.

::Selection::

1. [Affect labeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K159%20%E2%80%94%20Affect%20labeling): name the emotion precisely — not "stressed" but "tight chest, dread, buzzing".
2. [Feeling-tone pass](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K160%20%E2%80%94%20Feeling-tone%20pass): three times daily, name the tone of this moment as pleasant, unpleasant or neutral, before any story about it.
3. [Emotional writing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K171%20%E2%80%94%20Emotional%20writing): fifteen to twenty minutes, three or four consecutive days, uncensored; no editing, no censoring.
4. [Body-first emotion work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K172%20%E2%80%94%20Body-first%20emotion%20work): when an emotion is hard to locate or name, start with the body — where is the tension, what is the temperature, what is the texture.
5. [Low emotional response](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K124%20%E2%80%94%20Low%20emotional%20response): when provoked, respond with low emotional intensity; do not match the other person's escalation. The kernel adds, as its own claim, that calm is contagious in both directions.

Evidence limits: the outcome language in the kernel descriptions above is the kernels' own text, compressed; this dossier verified no trial of these exact exercises. The labeling kernel rests on the affect-labeling study cited on its card, the writing kernel on the expressive-writing literature cited on its card, the DBT module on the treatment literature for its clinical population — none of those carriers was opened here, and no claim about their study sizes or designs is made. The feeling-tone and low-response practices carry no outcome claim verified in this dossier. The categorical-versus-dimensional disagreement is a live theoretical dispute; no practice selection follows from either side alone.

::Retention:: Any of these that changes how quickly an emotion is named, or how long it outlasts its trigger, earns a place; the chronometry and dynamics entries are the natural measurement tools for checking what a practice has changed.

# Attention

> What is attention, what does it cost, and what does it build?

This dossier is a map, not an essay. The aim is that a reader can say "I want to think about attention", open this page, and see the whole spread of what the compendium holds on it: every entry that bears on attention, grouped by the question it answers, each with a card link. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The editorial aim running through the compendium also runs through this page: to treat attention as the place a life is lived, and to loosen the grip of whatever pulls it away.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. Every concept card carries onward to the practice principles that cite it, so the map needs no separate principle index.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on attention

### What attention is taken to be

[Attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention): the selection and sustaining of awareness; William James's classic definition and the experiential weight he gave it.

[Spotlight model of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spotlight%20model%20of%20attention): Posner's 1980 account of covert orienting, attention as a beam that can be moved across the visual field.

[Spotlight vs floodlight attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spotlight%20vs%20floodlight%20attention): the aperture metaphor: attention narrowed to a detail or broadened to the whole, with different consequences for what is seen.

[Attentional breadth global vs local processing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20breadth%20global%20vs%20local%20processing): the spatial extent over which attention is applied, from Goodhew's working definition.

[Focus theory of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Focus%20theory%20of%20attention): the attended stimulus dominates awareness and processing; includes Eriksen and St. James's zoom-lens model.

[Two modes of attentional control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Two%20modes%20of%20attentional%20control): Corbetta and Shulman's two partly separate brain systems, one goal-directed, one a circuit breaker for salient events.

[Goal-directed attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Goal-directed%20attention): the top-down half of selection, preparing and applying selection for stimuli and responses according to what you are trying to do.

[Goal-directed attentional control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Goal-directed%20attentional%20control): the same division of labor stated for practice: attention steered by intention rather than pulled by whatever is loud, new or threatening.

[Selective attention (Broadbent's filter model)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Selective%20attention%20%28Broadbent%27s%20filter%20model%29): the first influential account of how selection happens: a limited-capacity channel with a filter in front of it.

[Load theory of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Load%20theory%20of%20attention): Lavie's resolution of the early-or-late selection debate, tying the filter's place to how loaded perception is.

[Divided attention theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Divided%20attention%20theory): what happens when a person tries to do two things at once; performance usually suffers, and researchers disagree about why.

[Attentional blink](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20blink): the finding that attention briefly goes dark after it lands; probes are poorly detected in a window beginning 180 ms after a target.

[Inattentional blindness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Inattentional%20blindness): the failure to perceive a fully visible but unexpected object when attention is engaged elsewhere; the invisible-gorilla experiment.

[Attention Network Test (ANT)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20Network%20Test%20%28ANT%29): the computer task that measures the efficiency of three attentional networks, alerting, orienting and executive attention, in one sitting.

[Attention Schema Theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20Schema%20Theory): Graziano's account of awareness as the brain's simplified model of its own attention.

William James gave the field its definition and its stakes, both in the Principles of Psychology:

> "Every one knows what attention is. It is the taking possession by the mind, in clear and vivid form, of one out of what seem several simultaneously possible objects or trains of thought. Focalization, concentration, of consciousness are of its essence. It implies withdrawal from some things in order to deal effectively with others" ([James, Principles of Psychology, ch. 11](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin11.htm)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

> "My experience is what I agree to attend to. Only those items which I notice shape my mind — without selective interest, experience is an utter chaos." ([James, Principles of Psychology, ch. 11](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin11.htm)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

Lavie's load theory, in the review that states its central prediction:

> "High perceptual load that engages full capacity in relevant processing would leave no spare capacity for perception of task-irrelevant stimuli. In situations of low perceptual load, however, any capacity not taken up in perception of task-relevant stimuli would involuntarily 'spill over' to the perception of task-irrelevant distractors." ([Lavie 2005, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://labs.wsu.edu/attention-perception-performance/documents/2016/05/attentional-load-review_lavie_05.pdf)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

### What attention costs and how it recovers

[The effort of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20effort%20of%20attention): James's phrase from the chapter on will: where a thought prevails with effort, attention is doing the work.

[Directed attention fatigue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Directed%20attention%20fatigue): Kaplan's name for the fatigue of the effortful, voluntarily controlled kind of attention.

[Attention restoration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20restoration): Kaplan's framework that directed attention fatigues with use and that certain experiences and environments, natural ones above all, allow it to recover.

[Effortless attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20attention): Bruya's term for attention or action experienced as effortless, or exertion with lower-than-normal subjective effort while effectiveness holds.

[Ultradian rhythms in attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ultradian%20rhythms%20in%20attention): the roughly 90-minute basic rest-activity cycle Kleitman proposed, visible as fluctuations in alertness across the day.

[Attention residue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20residue): Leroy's name for what remains of attention on a previous task after switching, degrading the next task while it persists.

[Attention management](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20management): the practice of governing where attention goes: choosing, protecting, and handling what switching costs.

[Sustained attention theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sustained%20attention%20theory): how people hold a detection-ready state over time, and why that state degrades.

[Vigilance decrement](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vigilance%20decrement): the decline in the ability to detect critical signals as time on a monitoring task increases; one of the most consistently replicated findings in attention research.

James gave the effort its place in the will:

> "Effort of attention is thus the essential phenomenon of will." ([James, Principles of Psychology, vol. 2, ch. XXVI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57634/pg57634.txt)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

> "The essential achievement of the will, in short, when it is most 'voluntary,' is to attend to a difficult object and hold it fast before the mind." ([James, Principles of Psychology, vol. 2, ch. XXVI](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57634/pg57634.txt)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

The degradation of attention over time on task, in the meta-analytic review's own terms:

> "Maintaining attention for more than a few seconds is essential for mastering everyday life. Yet, our ability to stay focused on a particular task is limited, resulting in well-known performance decrements with increasing time on task." ([Langner and Eickhoff 2012, Psychological Bulletin](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627747/)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

### Attention and the emotional life

[Attentional bias research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20bias%20research): whether what attention selects toward threat contributes to emotional vulnerability or merely accompanies it; the field's question is causal.

[Attentional bias theory MacLeod](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20bias%20theory%20MacLeod): anxiety maintained partly by where attention goes; the 1986 dot-probe study founded the paradigm.

[Attentional control theory (Eysenck)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20control%20theory%20%28Eysenck%29): what anxiety does to thinking, developing the processing efficiency account.

[Cognitive attentional syndrome Wells](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20attentional%20syndrome%20Wells): Wells's name for the perseverative, self-relevant, negatively loaded style of processing that underlies most psychological disorders.

[Avoidance versus attentional engagement](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Avoidance%20versus%20attentional%20engagement): the behavioural activation model of what keeps depression going: withdrawal brings short-term relief while removing the experiences that could counter the low mood.

[Attentional deployment theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20deployment%20theory): one of the five families in Gross's process model of emotion regulation: moving attention within a situation toward or away from emotional stimuli.

[Positive attention training attentional deployment toward positive stimuli](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Positive%20attention%20training%20attentional%20deployment%20toward%20positive%20stimuli): the laboratory methods that shift attention toward positive information and test whether emotional responses change.

[Regulatory focus theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Regulatory%20focus%20theory): Higgins's promotion and prevention foci; the same goal pursued under the two foci is a different pursuit.

[Compassion-focused therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion-focused%20therapy): Gilbert's approach for high shame and self-criticism, training soothing rhythms of attention among its three emotion-regulation systems.

### The contemplative trainings

[Focused-attention meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Focused-attention%20meditation): directing and sustaining attention on a chosen object, noticing wandering, disengaging, returning.

[Anchors in focused attention meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anchors%20in%20focused%20attention%20meditation): the chosen object as the stable point attention is brought back to.

[Open-monitoring meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Open-monitoring%20meditation): non-reactive awareness of moment-to-moment experience without a single object as its explicit focus.

[Bare attention in mindfulness practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Bare%20attention%20in%20mindfulness%20practice): Nyanaponika Thera's way of observing experience without immediately answering it with action or commentary.

[Mindful attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindful%20attention): attention deliberately trained on present experience; Kabat-Zinn's operational definition is the field's touchstone.

[Anapanasati (mindfulness of breathing)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anapanasati%20%28mindfulness%20of%20breathing%29): mindfulness of in-and-out breathing, the breath as the portable anchor.

[Walking meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Walking%20meditation): deliberate awareness brought to walking itself: contact, movement, breath.

[Right concentration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20concentration): the eighth factor of the Noble Eightfold Path; the four jhānas, each quieter than the last.

[The noticing-wandering-returning cycle in meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20noticing-wandering-returning%20cycle%20in%20meditation): the repeating sequence inside a session of breath-focused meditation, the cycle that trains the return.

[Focusing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Focusing): Gendlin's method for attending to experience before it has words, the felt sense as its object.

[Attentional control training and the role of repeated redirection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20control%20training%20and%20the%20role%20of%20repeated%20redirection): practising the noticing of distraction and the return of attention to a chosen object.

[Progressive overload in attentional training](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Progressive%20overload%20in%20attentional%20training): the strength-training idea applied to attention: raise the demand in steps so the system keeps adapting.

[Vigilance theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vigilance%20theory): Oaten and Cheng's account of self-control as a strength that practice can build.

The FA/OM distinction, in the review that introduced it to the meditation-science literature:

> "A widespread style of Buddhist practice consists in sustaining selective attention moment by moment on a chosen object, such as a subset of localized sensations caused by respiration. To sustain this focus, the meditator must also constantly monitor the quality of attention. At first, the attention wanders away from the chosen object, and the typical instruction is to recognize the wandering and then restore attention to the chosen object." ([Lutz et al. 2008, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2693206/)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

> "One aims to remain only in the monitoring state, attentive moment by moment to anything that occurs in experience without focusing on any explicit object... the 'effortful' selection or 'grasping' of an object as primary focus is gradually replaced by the 'effortless' sustaining of an awareness without explicit selection." ([Lutz et al. 2008, Trends in Cognitive Sciences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2693206/)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

The laboratory study that gave the meditation cycle its four intervals:

> "This model proposes four intervals in a cognitive cycle: mind wandering, awareness of mind wandering, shifting of attention, and sustained attention." ([Hasenkamp et al. 2012, NeuroImage](https://wendyhasenkamp.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/hasenkamp-neuroimage-2012.pdf)) — Study; quoted passage checked.

### Absorption and its varieties

[Flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow): the state of full immersion in an activity, presented by Csikszentmihalyi in 1975; the positive limit of absorption.

[Hyperfocus theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hyperfocus%20theory): complete absorption in a task to the point of tuning out everything else; poorly defined within the literature.

[Effortless attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20attention): the Bruya volume's subject: the paradox that the highest engagement can feel like the least effort.

[ADHD — interest-based attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ADHD%20%E2%80%94%20interest-based%20attention): attention driven by strong personal interest or imminent unpleasantness rather than by the importance of the task, in Brown's account.

[Autistic cognition — detail-focused processing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autistic%20cognition%20%E2%80%94%20detail-focused%20processing): the weak coherence account: strength in detail and pattern where the typical style pulls for gist and global form.

### Attention in the social field

[Social attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20attention): the way one person's attention is guided by what others attend to, most clearly through where they look.

[Attentional synchronisation in groups](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attentional%20synchronisation%20in%20groups): group members converge in attentional focus; the gaze of conversational pairs is coupled across several seconds.

### Attention in action: process, craft and the economy of the gaze

[Process focus research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Process%20focus%20research): whether attending to the means of a goal works better than attending to its desired result.

[Process versus outcome focus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Process%20versus%20outcome%20focus): where attention rests while pursuing something: on the controllable actions of the moment, or on the desired result.

[The attention economy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20attention%20economy): Herbert Simon's scarcity, commercialised; the page also carries the contested finding that mere smartphone presence reduces cognitive capacity.

[Notification and feed research on attention capture](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Notification%20and%20feed%20research%20on%20attention%20capture): whether smartphone notifications and infinite feeds pull attention away from what a person is doing; the notification study's distraction effects were comparable to actively using the device.

### The moral face of attention

[The craft of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20craft%20of%20attention): Simone Weil's account: the trained capacity to suspend one's own thought so that the thing or person in front of one can appear.

[The discipline of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20discipline%20of%20attention): Weil's essay on school studies: prayer as attention, and attention to a sufferer as the rarest form of it.

The compendium holds the theme in narrative form too:

[ST023 — Simone Weil on Attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST023%20%E2%80%94%20Simone%20Weil%20on%20Attention): the philosopher's own working life as the illustration of what attention costs and gives.

Weil's own lines, from the essay collected in Waiting for God:

> "Attention consists of suspending our thought, leaving it detached, empty, and ready to be penetrated by the object; it means holding in our minds, within reach of this thought, but on a lower level and not in contact with it, the diverse knowledge we have acquired which we are forced to make use of." ([Weil, Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

> "The key to a Christian conception of studies is the realization that prayer consists of attention. It is the orientation of all the attention of which the soul is capable toward God." ([Weil, Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is a wandering mind an unhappy mind?

The shared question: what is mind-wandering for, and what does it cost? Killingsworth and Gilbert's smartphone study, sampling 2250 adults at random moments, found wandering the mind's default and its tax:

> "Mind wandering occurred in 46.9% of the samples and in at least 30% of the samples taken during every activity except making love." ([Killingsworth and Gilbert 2010, Science](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/A_Wandering_Mind_Is_an_Unhappy_Mind.pdf)) — Study; quoted passage checked.

> "In conclusion, a human mind is a wandering mind, and a wandering mind is an unhappy mind. The ability to think about what is not happening is a cognitive achievement that comes at an emotional cost." ([Killingsworth and Gilbert 2010, Science](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/images/uploads/A_Wandering_Mind_Is_an_Unhappy_Mind.pdf)) — Study; quoted passage checked.

The review that keeps both halves in view reads the evidence differently:

> "Substantial evidence suggests that mind-wandering typically occurs at a significant cost to performance. Mind-wandering-related deficits in performance have been observed in many contexts, most notably reading, tests of sustained attention, and tests of aptitude." ([Mooneyham and Schooler 2013](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2013/2013-The-costs-and-benefits-of-mind-wandering-a-review.pdf)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

> "Contemplating this question leads us to propose the following potential functions of mind-wandering: attentional cycling, dishabituation, and relief from boredom... mind-wandering appeared to partially insulate people against the mood costs of engaging in a particularly tedious task." ([Mooneyham and Schooler 2013](https://metalab.psych.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/2013/2013-The-costs-and-benefits-of-mind-wandering-a-review.pdf)) — Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

Both positions answer the same question, whether wandering is a design flaw or a function. The mood cost is measured; the candidate functions are proposals. What remains unresolved is whether the unhappiness finding settles the value of wandering or only its average mood correlate, and whether the contemplative trainings, which treat the noticing of wandering as the trainable moment, are suppressing the default mode or harvesting it.

### Is good attention effortful or effortless?

The shared question: what does well-directed attention feel like from inside? James's account puts effort at the center, in the chapter on will quoted above: "Effort of attention is thus the essential phenomenon of will." The meditation-science literature describes a developmental arc away from that center, in Lutz et al.'s account of open monitoring: "the 'effortful' selection or 'grasping' of an object as primary focus is gradually replaced by the 'effortless' sustaining of an awareness without explicit selection" (quoted above, passages checked). Bruya's volume treats the same paradox as its subject: attention or action experienced as effortless, with effectiveness normal or elevated.

The positions part on whether effortlessness is a late fruit of effortful training or a different mode of attention altogether. The FA instruction, as Lutz et al. state it, never drops the monitoring; the OM account claims the monitoring itself becomes the effortless state. What remains unresolved is whether these are two stages of one skill or two skills, and the wiki holds both without adjudicating.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is where attention goes by default: captured by whatever is loud, new or threatening; left behind on the previous task after a switch; spent on feeds engineered to hold it; and running on a directed-attention budget that is fatigued and never restored. The adaptive direction is selection guided by intention, the return trained rather than the absence of wandering, restoration built into the day rather than borrowed from it, and absorption available when the task deserves it.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [Breath anchoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K100%20%E2%80%94%20Breath%20anchoring): resting attention on the physical sensation of breathing, noting where it went, returning; the return is the practice, not a failure.

2. [Open monitoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K101%20%E2%80%94%20Open%20monitoring): settling first, then widening attention to whatever arises in the field of awareness, letting it come and go without following or pushing.

3. [Body scan](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K102%20%E2%80%94%20Body%20scan): moving attention slowly through the body, noticing sensations in each region without trying to change them.

4. [Noting and labeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K103%20%E2%80%94%20Noting%20and%20labeling): silently naming what is present — "breathing," "hearing," "thinking" — the label a pointer, not a judgment.

5. [Mindful walking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K104%20%E2%80%94%20Mindful%20walking): attending to the sensations of movement, lifting, moving, placing the foot; useful when the mind is too restless for seated practice.

6. [Single-tasking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K105%20%E2%80%94%20Single-tasking): one thing at a time with full attention, the current task protected from interruption for a defined period.

7. [Five-senses grounding](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K106%20%E2%80%94%20Five-senses%20grounding): five things seen, four heard, three touched, two smelled, one tasted; pulling attention out of rumination and into the present sensory field.

8. [Soft fascination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K107%20%E2%80%94%20Soft%20fascination): time in an environment that gently engages attention without demanding it, letting attention rest and restore.

9. [Meta-awareness check](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K108%20%E2%80%94%20Meta-awareness%20check): the periodic question "where is my attention right now?", cued to transitions, meals or hourly alarms.

10. [Quiet eye](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K109%20%E2%80%94%20Quiet%20eye): before a skilled action, holding a steady final gaze on the target and beginning the movement from inside that stillness.

11. [Savoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K110%20%E2%80%94%20Savoring): when a pleasant moment occurs, deliberately extending it; building the capacity to receive good experience.

12. [Scheduled empty time](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K112%20%E2%80%94%20Scheduled%20empty%20time): ten to thirty minutes of unfilled time daily, no agenda, no phone; the mind allowed to wander, settle or do nothing.

13. [Transition pause](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K113%20%E2%80%94%20Transition%20pause): between activities, three breaths, noticing the ending of one thing and the beginning of the next.

14. [Compassion-focused breathing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K163%20%E2%80%94%20Compassion-focused%20breathing): breathing at a soothing rhythm of roughly five to six breaths per minute while evoking warmth and safeness.

15. [Aesthetic attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K328%20%E2%80%94%20Aesthetic%20attention): ten minutes with a work of art, music, scene or crafted object, attending to how it appears rather than what it means.

16. [Shared attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K357%20%E2%80%94%20Shared%20attention): with another person, attending to the same thing — a view, music, food tasted together — with the talking left out.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the breath as a portable anchor, the question that catches a wandering hour, or the walk that restores what work spent has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

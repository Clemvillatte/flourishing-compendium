# Procrastination

> Why do we delay what we intend to value, and what does the delay do to the self that inherits it?

The gap between intending and doing is ordinary. What makes procrastination a problem is its shape: on the mood-repair account, the delay buys a present mood at the price of a future self's time, and the buyer and the payer are the same person. Socrates and Aristotle ask how knowledge relates to action; the modern reviews call procrastination a failure of self-regulation; the practice traditions work on the design of the moment. The question I would put to both reviews is which end deserves the intervention first: what makes the outcome worth pursuing, or what makes starting unpleasant.

## Nearby terms

Akrasia is the oldest name on this territory: the lived problem of acting against one's own better judgment, and the positions taken on whether that is possible at all. Socrates denied the possibility; Aristotle's realism about it grounds his ethics of practice; the modern debate refines what exactly is betrayed, a judgment or a plan.

Habit and behaviour change hold the neighbouring mechanics; the Behaviour dossier carries them. This page takes only what is specific to delay: what the delay protects, and what it costs.

Self-forgiveness concerns how a person responds after an offense. Its application to procrastination appears in the two-exam study described below, where forgiveness for earlier delay was associated with less subsequent procrastination.

## The map: the entries on procrastination

### The classical question: acting against what you know

[Akrasia (weakness of will)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Akrasia%20%28weakness%20of%20will%29): the theory problem, on the encyclopedia's telling. Socrates denies even the possibility of akrasia in the Protagoras; Aristotle's partial agreement holds that the person acting against reason does not have what is thought to be unqualified knowledge, and that an akratic person goes against reason as a result of some pathos; the modern debate runs from Hare's defence of Socrates' conclusion through Davidson's characterization of incontinent action to Holton's reading of weakness of will as a certain kind of failure to stick to one's plans. Aristotle's own distinction between impetuosity and weakness sits at the end.

[Akrasia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Akrasia): acting against one's better judgment, knowing the better and doing the worse. Aristotle treats it as curable, a failure of habituation rather than of reason as such, against Socrates' denial of its possibility; the related web runs through boulesis, Frankfurt's second-order desires and Spinoza's drunkard.

[A056 — Recognise akrasia — weakness of will](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A056%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20akrasia%20%E2%80%94%20weakness%20of%20will): Aristotle's objection to Socrates in NE VII.2, that the view plainly contradicts the observed facts, with his distinction between possessing knowledge and using it, and his closing qualification in Socrates' favour.

[A057 — Know that akrasia is not invincible](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A057%20%E2%80%94%20Know%20that%20akrasia%20is%20not%20invincible): the other half of Book VII. Aristotle separates the unrestrained person from the profligate and holds the former reformable, quoting Rackham's dropsy-and-consumption against epilepsy comparison, and the claim that habit-bred unrestraint is easier to cure than the natural kind.

[Pre-commitment as wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pre-commitment%20as%20wisdom): Seneca's timing counsel. An emotion is weak at the threshold and strong once entered, so the wise act is to refuse the encounter: Ulysses tied to the mast before the voices, not willpower against them at full strength.

[K269 — Assent delay](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K269%20%E2%80%94%20Assent%20delay): the Stoic pause in kernel form. The impression prints itself, the assent remains in our power, and the practice reaches for the pause when the response is already forming.

[MO033 — Learn to use commitment devices — pre-commit to future behaviour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO033%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20commitment%20devices%20%E2%80%94%20pre-commit%20to%20future%20behaviour): the binding lever in its modern form. Bryan, Karlan and Nelson's definition, the arrangement aimed at helping fulfil a plan that would otherwise be difficult owing to intrapersonal conflict, with the hard commitments that carry real economic penalties distinguished from the soft ones that work psychologically.

### What the reviews say the delay is

[MO045 — Recognise procrastination as emotion regulation, not laziness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO045%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20procrastination%20as%20emotion%20regulation%2C%20not%20laziness): the review argument. Sirois and Pychyl contend that procrastination is, in significant part, short-term mood repair: the avoidance manages a feeling, and a schedule alone leaves the feeling unaddressed. The review is explicit that this is not offered as the whole story, and the card carries that limit.

[MO005 — Recognise that motivation is temporal — delay reduces motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO005%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20that%20motivation%20is%20temporal%20%E2%80%94%20delay%20reduces%20motivation): the timing side. Steel and König build delay into a formal model of motivation, and the 2018 test with N = 7,400 found pacing on a hyperbolic curve, susceptibility to proximity of temptation, and procrastination related to intention-action gaps but not to intentions. Delay weakens motivation; the delay is a timing problem more than a missing intention.

[Temporal motivation theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Temporal%20motivation%20theory): the model the Steel review found its effects consistent with, the hybrid of expectancy theory and hyperbolic discounting that [Temporal discounting](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Temporal%20discounting) supplies the mechanism for: the devaluing of rewards as receipt moves away, on a curve steep near the present and flatter further out, which is what lets the present self bind the future one.

[MO050 — Learn to practise self-compassion when procrastinating — guilt backfires](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO050%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20practise%20self-compassion%20when%20procrastinating%20%E2%80%94%20guilt%20backfires): the repair side. Among the 119 first-year students in the two-exam study, self-forgiveness for procrastinating before the first exam went with less procrastination before the second, mediated by negative affect; the self-compassion line is correlational, and neither line shows harshness helping.

[Self-forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-forgiveness): Hall and Fincham describe a shift away from avoiding reminders of an offense and retaliating against oneself, toward self-directed benevolence. The card also points to Marcus's counsel to return after failure without disgust or discouragement.

[The intention-behavior gap](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20intention-behavior%20gap): the measured gap itself. Sheeran's review of ten meta-analyses put the intention-behaviour correlation at 0.53, large, and leaving most of the variance in behaviour unexplained by intention; the best-validated bridge is the if-then plan.

[Temptation bundling (Milkman)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Temptation%20bundling%20%28Milkman%29): the binding lever from the other direction. Pair the want with the should, gym-only audiobooks, and the field experiment found 51% and 29% more gym visits for full and intermediate treatment at first, effects that declined, and 61% willing to pay for the restriction afterwards.

### The shape of the task and its pull

[MO048 — Learn to break tasks down — overwhelm causes avoidance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO048%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20break%20tasks%20down%20%E2%80%94%20overwhelm%20causes%20avoidance): the review's own premise, that procrastination often occurs when people are faced with tasks that are seen as aversive, with the conclusion sentence proposing the primacy of short-term mood repair. The card's task-breakdown exercise is an application, and its effect on procrastination is not established by the quoted passages.

[K189 — Challenge-skill calibration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K189%20%E2%80%94%20Challenge-skill%20calibration): Csikszentmihalyi's rule from Flow. Too easy tips into boredom, too hard into anxiety, and the way back to absorption is an adjustment on one of the two axes; the calibration is the book's structural account, not a tested intervention, and the kernel's daily-use form is editorial.

[MO026 — Learn to use mental contrasting — visualise obstacle, not just outcome](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO026%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20mental%20contrasting%20%E2%80%94%20visualise%20obstacle%2C%20not%20just%20outcome): Oettingen's separation of expectations from fantasies, with the contrasting remedy and the MCII meta-analysis behind it, 21 studies and 15,907 participants, a small-to-medium effect with the publication-bias caveat stated on the card.

[MO027 — Learn to make progress visible — feedback sustains motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO027%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20make%20progress%20visible%20%E2%80%94%20feedback%20sustains%20motivation): the progress side. Amabile and Kramer's small wins, and the 138-study meta-analysis in which monitoring prompts raised goal attainment, d+ = 0.40, larger when progress was physically recorded or made public, strongest for health goals.

[MO021 — Learn to connect goals to identity — I am a person who...](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO021%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20connect%20goals%20to%20identity%20%E2%80%94%20I%20am%20a%20person%20who...): the identity framing. The voter experiments found the being-a-voter wording significantly increased interest in registering to vote, and voter turnout, assessed by official state records, in two statewide elections; the effect is direct support in one domain, not a general result about I-am-a-person-who statements, and the card says so.

[MO022 — Recognise the role of curiosity and interest — intrinsic motivation's engine](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO022%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20role%20of%20curiosity%20and%20interest%20%E2%80%94%20intrinsic%20motivation%27s%20engine): the pull side. In the trivia-question studies, higher curiosity tracked caudate activity implicated in anticipated reward and better recall of surprising answers one to two weeks later; the application is to let the unanswered question do the work.

### The levers the cards carry

[MO028 — Learn to use the fresh start effect — temporal landmarks boost motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO028%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20the%20fresh%20start%20effect%20%E2%80%94%20temporal%20landmarks%20boost%20motivation): temporal landmarks as prompts. Google searches for diet, gym visits and goal commitments all rose after new weeks, months, years and birthdays in the three field studies; the effect is on starting, not on lasting.

[MO049 — Learn to remove distractions — environment drives procrastination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO049%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20remove%20distractions%20%E2%80%94%20environment%20drives%20procrastination): the environment lever, with its honest replication record: the mere-presence studies, the small pooled effect, and the suggested cheap experiment of the same task with the phone in reach and in another room.

[MO051 — Learn to manage energy — procrastination often signals fatigue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO051%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20manage%20energy%20%E2%80%94%20procrastination%20often%20signals%20fatigue): the energy question, held open. The mood-repair account points at feelings about the task; the sleep and depletion findings are mixed, and the card's practice is the two-line diagnosis: how rested, and how the task feels.

[K185 — The two-minute rule](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K185%20%E2%80%94%20The%20two-minute%20rule): the smallest start. Two minutes, meant, with the honest stop kept cheap to obey tomorrow.

[MO047 — Learn to use the 5-minute rule — lower the barrier to starting](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO047%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20the%205-minute%20rule%20%E2%80%94%20lower%20the%20barrier%20to%20starting): the other small start, Pychyl's just-get-started with the card's honest note that five minutes is a convention no study tests against other durations.

[K188 — Friction audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K188%20%E2%80%94%20Friction%20audit): the chain walk. Time each step from intention to action, remove the costliest, add friction to the unwanted chain, and re-run.

[K153 — Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions): the if-then plan, written once for one foreseeable moment.

[MO025 — Learn to use implementation intentions — if-then plans](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO025%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20implementation%20intentions%20%E2%80%94%20if-then%20plans): the principle form of the same lever, carrying the meta-analysis of 94 independent tests, medium-to-large, d = 0.65, on goal attainment, with the component processes the review reports support for.

## Where the two reviews stand

Both opened reviews call procrastination a failure of self-regulation, and both say so in their abstracts:

> "Procrastination is a prevalent and pernicious form of self-regulatory failure that is not entirely understood." (Steel, "The Nature of Procrastination", Psychological Bulletin 133(1), 2007, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201571/))

> "In this paper, we argue that as a form of self-regulation failure, procrastination has a great deal to do with short-term mood repair and emotion regulation." (Sirois and Pychyl 2013, author manuscript, [White Rose eprints PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/1/Compass%20Paper%20revision%20FINAL.pdf))

Steel's meta-analysis of 691 correlations found the strong and consistent predictors to be task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy and impulsiveness, with the conscientiousness facets behind them, and the effects, the abstract says, prove consistent with temporal motivation theory. Sirois and Pychyl emphasize short-term mood repair within that same self-regulation failure.

The two accounts need not compete. A devalued future and an aversive present can be one mechanism seen from two ends, and the mood-repair review itself does not claim to be the whole story. I would ask which intervention to try first: changing what makes starting unpleasant, or changing the timing and attractiveness of the outcome.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Can someone know the better course and still delay?

The classical question is whether a person can know the better course and act against it. Socrates, as reported in the Protagoras, denies it outright:

> "No one, he declared, who either knows or believes that there is another possible course of action, better than the one he is following, will ever continue on his present course" ([Protagoras 358b-c, in SEP "Weakness of Will"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/weakness-will/))

Aristotle objects that the denial cannot be squared with conduct anyone can watch, and then locates the failure in the difference between having knowledge and using it:

> "Now this view plainly contradicts the observed facts, and we must inquire about what happens to such a man; if he acts by reason of ignorance, what is the manner of his ignorance?" ([Nicomachean Ethics VII.2, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html))

> "since we use the word 'know' in two senses (for both the man who has knowledge but is not using it and he who is using it are said to know), it will make a difference whether, when a man does what he should not, he has the knowledge but is not exercising it, or is exercising it" ([Nicomachean Ethics VII.3, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html))

The disagreement, carried on these passages, is not the classical stance versus the research stance: the two modern reviews examine delay without settling the philosophical question. The boundary belongs in the page, not in a footnote. Akrasia is broader than procrastination, and reading the debate onto delay is the page's own connection, not a finding of either review. Where the disagreement does bear, and where the corpus's own cards already carry it, is on what counts as the missing piece: the classical argument turns on what knowing amounts to at the moment of action, while the modern argument turns on predictors of delay that are properties of the valuation and the initiation, not of the knowledge. The [A057](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A057%20%E2%80%94%20Know%20that%20akrasia%20is%20not%20invincible) card carries the reformability claim, and the [Pre-commitment as wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pre-commitment%20as%20wisdom) card the Seneca counsel, both on the classical side of the gap.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the response to the lapse. The moral framing, laziness, guilt, self-blame, is a candidate target on the mood-repair account: the proposed chain is that self-blame feeds the mood being escaped, and the studies behind the self-compassion line are correlational, so the chain is a proposed mechanism, not a demonstrated one. The schedule-first framing rearranges time and, on the mood-repair account, leaves the feeling unaddressed. The intention-first framing assumes the missing piece is deciding, when the intention-action gap is the measured problem. The adaptive direction is to treat the delay as information: how the task feels, how rested the person is, and how far away the reward sits.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K185 — The two-minute rule](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K185%20%E2%80%94%20The%20two-minute%20rule): the two-minute start, meant, with the honest stop. The rule is a summary practice built on Allen's two-minute principle and Clear's stop-if-you-want variant; it is editorial, and its evidence is the plain words of its two sources, not a trial.

2. [K188 — Friction audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K188%20%E2%80%94%20Friction%20audit): walk the chain from intention to action, time each step, remove the costliest, add friction to the unwanted chain at the same time. The protocol steps are editorial; the principle beneath them is the friction literature on present bias.

3. [K153 — Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions): the if-then plan, written once for one foreseeable moment, linking the critical situation to the response in advance. The kernel's evidence section carries the screening-attendance field test: 92 percent attendance among the women who wrote down when, where and how they would make the appointment, against 69 percent among equally motivated controls. The trial tested one if-then plan for one appointment, not the kernel's anxious-moment example, and the abstract reports nothing about the plan's proposed mechanism.

::Retention:: Retention is what the person keeps using because it works for them. The two minutes that keep getting taken, the friction change that survives the re-audit, the if-then plan that fires without being re-read: what stays is a matter of fit between the lever and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

### The wrong kind of effort

[ST391 — Backsliding into Abundance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST391%20%E2%80%94%20Backsliding%20into%20Abundance): before his awakening the Buddha tried to master his mind by force, clenching his teeth and crushing his mind with his awareness, eating a handful of soup at a time until, thinking to touch his belly, he could grab hold of his spine; the answer came as a memory of ease under a rose-apple tree, and when he took solid food to regain strength for it, the five monks said he had abandoned his exertion and was backsliding into abundance.

### Reviewing the day's faults

[ST220 — The Evening Review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST220%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Evening%20Review): Seneca, with the lights out, examines the day's cases, what he did badly and what well, whether he gave up a fault and restrained passion; he sometimes finds himself pleased with his own progress and treats that pleasure as the next thing to watch.

### Marking the faults and continuing

[ST184 — Franklin's Little Book](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST184%20%E2%80%94%20Franklin%27s%20Little%20Book): Franklin at twenty charts thirteen virtues, one focused on each week, every fault marked with a black spot; he never reached perfection and concluded that the practice, not the perfection, was the gain.

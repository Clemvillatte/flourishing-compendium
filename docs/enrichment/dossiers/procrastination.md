# Procrastination

> Why do we delay what we intend to value, and what does the delay do to the self that inherits it?

The gap between intending and doing is ordinary. What makes procrastination a problem is its shape: on the mood-repair account, the delay buys a present mood at the price of a future self's time, and the buyer and the payer are the same person. Socrates and Aristotle ask how knowledge relates to action; the modern reviews call procrastination a failure of self-regulation; the practice traditions work on the design of the moment. The two reviews leave open which end deserves the intervention first: what makes the outcome worth pursuing, or what makes starting unpleasant.

### Nearby terms

- Akrasia: the oldest name on this territory, the lived problem of acting against one's own better judgment. Socrates denied the possibility; Aristotle's realism about it grounds his ethics of practice; the modern debate refines what exactly is betrayed, a judgment or a plan.
- Habit and behaviour change: the neighbouring mechanics of what the delay sits inside. What is specific to delay is what the delay protects, and what it costs.
- Self-forgiveness: how a person responds after an offense. Its application to procrastination appears in the two-exam study described below, where forgiveness for earlier delay was associated with less subsequent procrastination.

## Where the delay comes from

### Can we act against what we know?

[Akrasia](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Akrasia) names it plainly: knowing the better and doing the worse. Socrates denies even the possibility in the Protagoras; Aristotle's partial agreement holds that the person acting against reason does not have what is thought to be unqualified knowledge, and that an akratic person goes against reason as a result of some pathos; the modern debate runs from Hare's defence of Socrates' conclusion through Davidson's characterization of incontinent action to Holton's reading of weakness of will as a certain kind of failure to stick to one's plans.

[A056 — Recognise akrasia — weakness of will](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A056%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20akrasia%20%E2%80%94%20weakness%20of%20will) carries Aristotle's objection to Socrates in NE VII.2, that the view plainly contradicts the observed facts, with his distinction between possessing knowledge and using it. [A057 — Know that akrasia is not invincible](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A057%20%E2%80%94%20Know%20that%20akrasia%20is%20not%20invincible) holds the other half of Book VII: Aristotle separates the unrestrained person from the profligate and holds the former reformable, with habit-bred unrestraint easier to cure than the natural kind.

The classical counsel for the moment is Seneca's, carried on the compendium as [pre-commitment as wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pre-commitment%20as%20wisdom): an emotion is weak at the threshold and strong once entered, so the wise act is to refuse the encounter. Ulysses tied to the mast before the voices, not willpower against them at full strength. [K269 — Assent delay](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K269%20%E2%80%94%20Assent%20delay) is the Stoic pause in kernel form: the impression prints itself, the assent remains in our power.

The modern binding lever is the commitment device. [MO033 — Learn to use commitment devices — pre-commit to future behaviour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO033%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20commitment%20devices%20%E2%80%94%20pre-commit%20to%20future%20behaviour) carries Bryan, Karlan and Nelson's definition, the arrangement aimed at helping fulfil a plan that would otherwise be difficult owing to intrapersonal conflict, with the hard commitments that carry real economic penalties distinguished from the soft ones that work psychologically.

### What the reviews say the delay is

The review argument belongs to Sirois and Pychyl. [MO045 — Recognise procrastination as emotion regulation, not laziness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO045%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20procrastination%20as%20emotion%20regulation%2C%20not%20laziness) carries their contention that procrastination is, in significant part, short-term mood repair: the avoidance manages a feeling, and a schedule alone leaves the feeling unaddressed. The review is explicit that this is not offered as the whole story, and the card carries that limit.

The other end of the mechanism is time. [MO005 — Recognise that motivation is temporal — delay reduces motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO005%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20that%20motivation%20is%20temporal%20%E2%80%94%20delay%20reduces%20motivation) carries Steel and König's formal model of motivation, and the 2018 test with N = 7,400 found pacing on a hyperbolic curve, susceptibility to proximity of temptation, and procrastination related to intention-action gaps but not to intentions. On the model, a distant reward motivates weakly, so the problem sits in the timing between deciding and doing more than in the deciding itself.

The model the Steel review found its effects consistent with is [temporal motivation theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Temporal%20motivation%20theory), the hybrid of expectancy theory and hyperbolic discounting. [Temporal discounting](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Temporal%20discounting) supplies its mechanism: the devaluing of rewards as receipt moves away, on a curve steep near the present and flatter further out. A task that is unpleasant to start now and a reward that is too far away to pull are one predicament seen from two ends, which is why the two reviews can agree on the facts while pulling the lever from different sides.

What follows the lapse has a measured shape. In the two-exam study of 119 first-year students, Wohl, Pychyl and Bennett found that self-forgiveness for procrastinating before the first exam went with less procrastination before the second, mediated by negative affect; the finding is carried on [MO050 — Learn to practise self-compassion when procrastinating — guilt backfires](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO050%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20practise%20self-compassion%20when%20procrastinating%20%E2%80%94%20guilt%20backfires). The self-compassion line is correlational, and neither line shows harshness helping.

[Self-forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-forgiveness) is the capacity behind that finding. Hall and Fincham describe a shift away from avoiding reminders of an offense and retaliating against oneself, toward self-directed benevolence. The card also points to Marcus's counsel to return after failure without disgust or discouragement.

The measured gap itself is [the intention-behavior gap](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20intention-behavior%20gap). Sheeran's review of ten meta-analyses put the intention-behaviour correlation at 0.53, large, and leaving most of the variance in behaviour unexplained by intention; the review's bridge from intention to action is the if-then plan. The binding lever from the other direction is [temptation bundling (Milkman)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Temptation%20bundling%20%28Milkman%29): pair the want with the should, gym-only audiobooks, and the field experiment found 51% and 29% more gym visits for full and intermediate treatment at first, effects that declined, and 61% willing to pay for the restriction afterwards.

### What makes starting unpleasant

The review's own premise is that procrastination often occurs when people are faced with tasks that are seen as aversive, with the conclusion sentence proposing the primacy of short-term mood repair. [MO048 — Learn to break tasks down — overwhelm causes avoidance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO048%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20break%20tasks%20down%20%E2%80%94%20overwhelm%20causes%20avoidance) carries that premise, and the card's task-breakdown exercise is an application, its effect on procrastination not established by the quoted passages.

Csikszentmihalyi's rule from Flow names the fit between the person and the task. [K189 — Challenge-skill calibration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K189%20%E2%80%94%20Challenge-skill%20calibration) holds that too easy tips into boredom, too hard into anxiety, and the way back to absorption is an adjustment on one of the two axes; the calibration is the book's structural account, not a tested intervention.

Oettingen separates expectations from fantasies. [MO026 — Learn to use mental contrasting — visualise obstacle, not just outcome](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO026%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20mental%20contrasting%20%E2%80%94%20visualise%20obstacle%2C%20not%20just%20outcome) carries the contrasting remedy and the MCII meta-analysis behind it, 21 studies and 15,907 participants, a small-to-medium effect with the publication-bias caveat stated on the card.

Making progress visible sustains the work itself. Amabile and Kramer's small wins are carried on [MO027 — Learn to make progress visible — feedback sustains motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO027%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20make%20progress%20visible%20%E2%80%94%20feedback%20sustains%20motivation), and the 138-study meta-analysis in which monitoring prompts raised goal attainment, d+ = 0.40, larger when progress was physically recorded or made public, strongest for health goals.

Identity can carry the intention across the gap. In the voter experiments carried on [MO021 — Learn to connect goals to identity — I am a person who...](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO021%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20connect%20goals%20to%20identity%20%E2%80%94%20I%20am%20a%20person%20who...) the being-a-voter wording significantly increased interest in registering to vote, and voter turnout, assessed by official state records, in two statewide elections; the effect is direct support in one domain, not a general result about I-am-a-person-who statements, and the card says so.

Curiosity pulls from inside the task. In the trivia-question studies carried on [MO022 — Recognise the role of curiosity and interest — intrinsic motivation's engine](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO022%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20role%20of%20curiosity%20and%20interest%20%E2%80%94%20intrinsic%20motivation%27s%20engine), higher curiosity tracked caudate activity implicated in anticipated reward and better recall of surprising answers one to two weeks later; the application is to let the unanswered question do the work.

### The levers that start the starting

Temporal landmarks work as prompts. [MO028 — Learn to use the fresh start effect — temporal landmarks boost motivation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO028%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20the%20fresh%20start%20effect%20%E2%80%94%20temporal%20landmarks%20boost%20motivation) found Google searches for diet, gym visits and goal commitments all rose after new weeks, months, years and birthdays in the three field studies; the effect is on starting, not on lasting.

The environment lever carries an honest replication record. [MO049 — Learn to remove distractions — environment drives procrastination](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO049%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20remove%20distractions%20%E2%80%94%20environment%20drives%20procrastination) holds the mere-presence studies, the small pooled effect, and the suggested cheap experiment of the same task with the phone in reach and in another room. The energy question is held open on [MO051 — Learn to manage energy — procrastination often signals fatigue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO051%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20manage%20energy%20%E2%80%94%20procrastination%20often%20signals%20fatigue): the mood-repair account points at feelings about the task, the sleep and depletion findings are mixed, and the card's practice is the two-line diagnosis, how rested, and how the task feels.

The smallest start is [K185 — The two-minute rule](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K185%20%E2%80%94%20The%20two-minute%20rule): two minutes, meant, with the honest stop kept cheap to obey tomorrow. The other small start is [MO047 — Learn to use the 5-minute rule — lower the barrier to starting](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO047%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20the%205-minute%20rule%20%E2%80%94%20lower%20the%20barrier%20to%20starting), Pychyl's just-get-started with the note that five minutes is a convention, and the cited evidence does not establish an optimal duration.

[K188 — Friction audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K188%20%E2%80%94%20Friction%20audit) is the chain walk: time each step from intention to action, remove the costliest, add friction to the unwanted chain, and re-run. The if-then plan is [K153 — Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions), written once for one foreseeable moment, and its principle form [MO025 — Learn to use implementation intentions — if-then plans](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO025%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20implementation%20intentions%20%E2%80%94%20if-then%20plans) carries the meta-analysis of 94 independent tests, medium-to-large, d = 0.65, on goal attainment, with the component processes the review reports support for.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Can we act against our better judgment?

Plato's Socrates denies that someone who judges another available course better will continue on the worse one:

> "Then, I said, if the pleasant is the good, nobody does anything under the idea or conviction that some other thing would be better and is also attainable, when he might do the better. And this inferiority of a man to himself is merely ignorance, as the superiority of a man to himself is wisdom." ([Protagoras, Jowett translation](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/plato/dialogues/benjamin-jowett/text/protagoras))

> "Then, I said, no man voluntarily pursues evil, or that which he thinks to be evil. To prefer evil to good is not in human nature; and when a man is compelled to choose one of two evils, no one will choose the greater when he may have the less." ([Protagoras, Jowett translation](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/plato/dialogues/benjamin-jowett/text/protagoras))

Aristotle objects that the denial cannot be squared with conduct anyone can watch, and then locates the failure in the difference between having knowledge and using it:

> "Now this view plainly contradicts the observed facts, and we must inquire about what happens to such a man; if he acts by reason of ignorance, what is the manner of his ignorance?" ([Nicomachean Ethics VII.2, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html))

> "since we use the word 'know' in two senses (for both the man who has knowledge but is not using it and he who is using it are said to know), it will make a difference whether, when a man does what he should not, he has the knowledge but is not exercising it, or is exercising it" ([Nicomachean Ethics VII.3, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html))

His conclusion, however, grants Socrates a qualified victory: the position Socrates sought to establish actually seems to result, because incontinence does not arise in the presence of what is thought to be knowledge proper, but of perceptual knowledge ([Nicomachean Ethics VII.3, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.7.vii.html)). The disagreement concerns what kind of knowing is present when judgment and action part.

The dispute bears directly on postponing an intended action: akrasia is broader than procrastination, and neither modern review settles this philosophical question.

### Is delay by its nature self-defeating?

The shared question: does the word procrastination name a delay that costs the delayer, or can chosen delay work in the person's favour? The definition the field works with puts the cost inside the concept. Sirois and Pychyl open their account with it:

> "The voluntary delay of an intended action despite knowing that one will probably be worse off for the delay" (Sirois and Pychyl 2013, author manuscript, quoting the research definition after Steel, [White Rose eprints PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/1/Compass%20Paper%20revision%20FINAL.pdf))

Tice and Baumeister's semester data affirm the cost side:

> "Procrastinators reported lower stress and less illness than nonprocrastinators early in the semester, but they reported higher stress and more illness late in the term, and overall they were sicker. Procrastinators also received lower grades on all assignments." ([Tice and Baumeister, Psychological Science 1997, abstract](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.1997.tb00460.x/))

Their abstract concludes that procrastination "appears to be a self-defeating behavior pattern marked by short-term benefits and long-term costs." Chu and Choi deny the boundary:

> "Active procrastinators are a 'positive' type of procrastinator. They prefer to work under pressure, and they make deliberate decisions to procrastinate." ([Chu and Choi, Journal of Social Psychology 2005, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15959999/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=15959999&rettype=abstract&retmode=text))

> "although active procrastinators procrastinate to the same degree as passive procrastinators, they are more similar to nonprocrastinators than to passive procrastinators in terms of purposive use of time, control of time, self-efficacy belief, coping styles, and outcomes including academic performance."

The disagreement is definitional, and the data leave it live. On the definition that expects the delayer to be worse off, a person who profits by the delay is not procrastinating, and Chu and Choi's positive type falls outside the word; on Chu and Choi's construct, the word covers both harmful and chosen delay, and only the passive kind is the problem. Tice and Baumeister measured a trait scale against set assignments, and Chu and Choi sorted people by whether the delay was deliberate, so the two findings can both stand while the boundary question stays open. Whether "active procrastination" names a separate behaviour or planning that happens to look like delay is not settled by either abstract; the later work that tests the active-procrastination scale is not cited here, and both studies use student samples.

### What explains the delay: dispositions or the moment's mood?

The shared question: the correlations are agreed on both sides, so what kind of explanation does the delay need? Steel's meta-analysis of 691 correlations located the strong predictors in stable dispositions and task properties, and offered the timing account as the integrative explanation:

> "Strong and consistent predictors of procrastination were task aversiveness, task delay, self-efficacy, and impulsiveness, as well as conscientiousness and its facets of self-control, distractibility, organization, and achievement motivation. These effects prove consistent with temporal motivation theory, an integrative hybrid of expectancy theory and hyperbolic discounting." (Steel, "The Nature of Procrastination", Psychological Bulletin 133(1), 2007, [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201571/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=17201571&rettype=abstract&retmode=text))

Sirois and Pychyl accept the correlations and deny that the predictor list explains them:

> "While certainly an oversimplification, this summary statement highlights the correlational nature of this research. What is lacking is an explanation of the processes underlying these relations." (Sirois and Pychyl 2013, author manuscript, [White Rose eprints PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91793/1/Compass%20Paper%20revision%20FINAL.pdf))

Their process is the mood: "aversive tasks lead to anxiety and worry, and that task avoidance is a strategy to avoid this negative mood" (same manuscript).

The disagreement concerns what would count as understanding the delay: a temporal-devaluation mechanism that needs no reference to feelings, or a mood-repair process that gives the temporal pattern its motive force. The two mechanisms are compatible at the data level, an impulsive person may devalue distant rewards, feel the present mood more, or both, and neither abstract tests the difference, which is what keeps the question open rather than settled.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the response to the lapse. The moral framing, laziness, guilt, self-blame, is a candidate target on the mood-repair account: the proposed chain is that self-blame feeds the mood being escaped, and the studies behind the self-compassion line are correlational, so the chain is a proposed mechanism, not a demonstrated one. The schedule-first framing rearranges time and, on the mood-repair account, leaves the feeling unaddressed. The intention-first framing assumes the missing piece is deciding, when the intention-action gap is the measured problem. The adaptive direction is to treat the delay as information: how the task feels, how rested the person is, and how far away the reward sits.

::Selection:: The sources of that variation:

1. [K185 — The two-minute rule](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K185%20%E2%80%94%20The%20two-minute%20rule): the two-minute start, meant, with the honest stop. The rule is a summary practice built on Allen's two-minute principle and Clear's stop-if-you-want variant; it is editorial, and its evidence is the plain words of its two sources, not a trial.

2. [K188 — Friction audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K188%20%E2%80%94%20Friction%20audit): walk the chain from intention to action, time each step, remove the costliest, add friction to the unwanted chain at the same time. The protocol steps are editorial; the principle beneath them is the friction literature on present bias.

3. [K153 — Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions): the if-then plan, written once for one foreseeable moment, linking the critical situation to the response in advance. The kernel's evidence section carries the screening-attendance field test: 92 percent attendance among the women who wrote down when, where and how they would make the appointment, against 69 percent among equally motivated controls. The trial tested one if-then plan for one appointment, not the kernel's anxious-moment example, and the abstract reports nothing about the plan's proposed mechanism.

4. [Pre-commitment as wisdom](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Pre-commitment%20as%20wisdom): Seneca's counsel for dealing with emotion is timing. An emotion is weak at the threshold and strong once it has entered, so the wise act happens early: refuse the encounter rather than win the fight. The emblem he reaches for is Ulysses and the mast.

5. [K269 — Assent delay](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K269%20%E2%80%94%20Assent%20delay): when a strong impression hits, pause before giving it assent. The impression prints itself on the mind, but the assent remains in our power. The three-breath count is the kernel's editorial timing; the withholding it buys is the Stoic act.

6. [Self-forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-forgiveness): the forgiveness a person extends to the self after committing an offense, defined by Hall and Fincham as the motivational changes whereby one becomes decreasingly motivated to avoid the offense stimuli and to retaliate against the self, and increasingly motivated to act benevolently toward the self.

::Retention:: Retention is what the person keeps using because it works for them. The two minutes that keep getting taken, the friction change that survives the re-audit, the if-then plan that fires without being re-read: what stays is a matter of fit between the lever and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

### The wrong kind of effort

[ST391 — Backsliding into Abundance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST391%20%E2%80%94%20Backsliding%20into%20Abundance): before his awakening the Buddha tried to master his mind by force, clenching his teeth and crushing his mind with his awareness, eating a handful of soup at a time until, thinking to touch his belly, he could grab hold of his spine; the answer came as a memory of ease under a rose-apple tree, and when he took solid food to regain strength for it, the five monks said he had abandoned his exertion and was backsliding into abundance.

### Reviewing the day's faults

[ST220 — The Evening Review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST220%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Evening%20Review): Seneca, with the lights out, examines the day's cases, what he did badly and what well, whether he gave up a fault and restrained passion; he sometimes finds himself pleased with his own progress and treats that pleasure as the next thing to watch.

### Marking the faults and continuing

[ST184 — Franklin's Little Book](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST184%20%E2%80%94%20Franklin%27s%20Little%20Book): Franklin at twenty charts thirteen virtues, one focused on each week, every fault marked with a black spot; he never reached perfection and concluded that the practice, not the perfection, was the gain.

### Binding yourself in advance

[ST161 — Odysseus and the Mast](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST161%20%E2%80%94%20Odysseus%20and%20the%20Mast): Odysseus orders his crew to bind him to the mast and add ropes if he demands release; when the Sirens sing, he begs to be freed, and they keep him bound.

[ST160 — Medea Knows](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST160%20%E2%80%94%20Medea%20Knows): Abandoned by Jason for a royal marriage, Medea plans to murder their children, debates with herself, sees the wrong clearly, and carries out her revenge.

### The loop that feeds what it fears

[ST331 — The Hungry Tiger](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST331%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Hungry%20Tiger): A person feeds a hungry tiger to quiet its growling; every feeding calms it and makes it bigger, until feeding it sooner feels like the safest choice.

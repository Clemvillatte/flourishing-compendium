# Suffering

> What is the difference between pain and suffering, and what does suffering ask of the person carrying it?

Pain is the first arrow; suffering is the second. The distinction is the oldest and clearest thing this wiki holds on the subject: the Sallatha Sutta's untaught person, struck once, feels the arrow and then the story about the arrow, and the story is the part that can be dropped. Around that core the map assembles three families: the Buddhist diagnosis, which names craving as the engine; the existential diagnosis, which names death, guilt and struggle as limit situations that cannot be solved, only clarified; and the clinical tradition, which works on avoidance, the reflex that keeps suffering going by feeding it. What holds them together is the practical bias: the tradition's own charter, the poisoned arrow, refuses the questions whose answering would not remove it.

## Nearby terms

Pain: the first arrow, the physical side; held in the map's last group with its sciences.

Dukkha: the Buddhist umbrella; wider than suffering, a background unsatisfactoriness built into conditioned experience.

Distress and anguish: near synonyms held inside the entries below.

Misfortune: what happens to a person; suffering, in this map, is what the person adds or what the situation draws out, and the entries below mark the difference.

## The map: the entries on suffering

### The Buddhist diagnosis

[Dukkha](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dukkha): the first Noble Truth; the reading that separates pain from the suffering added by resisting, proliferating or clinging. The sutta's enumeration:

> "Birth is stressful, aging is stressful, death is stressful; sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair are stressful... In short, the five clinging-aggregates are stressful." (SN 56.11, [Thanissaro translation, dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN56_11.html))

[Four Noble Truths](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Four%20Noble%20Truths): the framing teaching; the card holds the sutta's own verbs, comprehended, abandoned, realized, developed, and the reading of the four as tasks rather than propositions.

[Stuckness as suffering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Stuckness%20as%20suffering): the second truth read through the origination formula; the mind that cannot stop reaching for what feeds the sticking.

[Tanha](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Tanha): the craving the second truth names; for sensual pleasure, for becoming, for non-becoming.

[The avoidance of suffering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20avoidance%20of%20suffering): the reflex of fleeing unsatisfactory experience; aversion as one of the unwholesome roots, and the second truth's own wording that names craving, not avoidance, as the origin.

[The second arrow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20second%20arrow): the additional mental distress that accompanies pain; SN 36.6's two arrows.

[B069 — Don't shoot the second arrow (pain vs. suffering)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B069%20%E2%80%94%20Don%27t%20shoot%20the%20second%20arrow%20%28pain%20vs.%20suffering%29): the principle form of the same sutta.

[B001 — Face suffering directly](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B001%20%E2%80%94%20Face%20suffering%20directly): SN 56.11's list and its task, in the Bodhi translation the principle carries.

[B006 — Craving is the engine of suffering (tanha)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B006%20%E2%80%94%20Craving%20is%20the%20engine%20of%20suffering%20%28tanha%29): the engine stated as principle.

[B182 — Separate pain from suffering — daily](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B182%20%E2%80%94%20Separate%20pain%20from%20suffering%20%E2%80%94%20daily): the practice of the distinction.

[ST078 — The Two Arrows](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST078%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two%20Arrows): the well-taught person struck once, the untaught twice; the first arrow not optional.

[ST059 — The Poisoned Arrow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST059%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Poisoned%20Arrow): the monk who wants the universe answered before he will practise; the tradition's charter for its practical bias.

### The existential diagnosis

[Limit situations (Grenzsituationen)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Limit%20situations%20%28Grenzsituationen%29): Jaspers's term from the Psychology of World Views; moments of dread, guilt or acute anxiety in which the mind confronts the restrictions of its forms, per the SEP entry the card quotes.

[ST289 — Jaspers's Shipwreck](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST289%20%E2%80%94%20Jaspers%27s%20Shipwreck): the structures that cannot be avoided or changed; the response not a solution but an illumination.

[ST290 — Job and the Whirlwind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST290%20%E2%80%94%20Job%20and%20the%20Whirlwind): the oldest of the limit-situation stories.

[The meaning of suffering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20meaning%20of%20suffering): Frankl's account of the camps; the last of the human freedoms, the choosing of attitude, and the consequence that life's meaning includes the potential meaning of unavoidable suffering.

[Tragic optimism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Tragic%20optimism): the yes to life in spite of the tragic triad, pain, guilt and death; the 1983 lecture the card quotes.

[E064 — Practise meaning through the attitude toward suffering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=E064%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20meaning%20through%20the%20attitude%20toward%20suffering): the practice form of the Frankl material.

[ST311 — Frankl's Why](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST311%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Why): the will to meaning and the Nietzschean line, with the card's own caution that meaning does not remove suffering, it changes the relation to it.

[T019 — Frankl, Viktor](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T019%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%2C%20Viktor): the thinker card.

### The Stoic junction

[Judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm): Enchiridion 5; men are disturbed not by the things which happen but by the opinions about the things, with the Sallatha Sutta's two arrows held side by side on the card.

### The clinical tradition and the response

[AF035 — Recognise that avoidance is negatively reinforcing — it works short-term and maintains suffering long-term](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF035%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20that%20avoidance%20is%20negatively%20reinforcing%20%E2%80%94%20it%20works%20short-term%20and%20maintains%20suffering%20long-term): the maintenance mechanism; relief that reinforces the avoidance and removes the chance to learn.

[Chronic pain acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Chronic%20pain%20acceptance): engagement in activity with pain present, without restrictions by pain or attempts to avoid or control it; the McCracken and Vowles definition the card quotes.

[Meaning-making](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meaning-making): Park's model of the response to events that violate belief; revising the event's meaning or the global meaning it violated.

[Post-traumatic growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Post-traumatic%20growth): growth after what should not have happened; the map's boundary against romanticizing suffering is the card's own.

[Benefit finding](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Benefit%20finding): finding good in adversity, distinguished on its card from benefit received from a giver.

[Karuna](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karuna): the wish to relieve suffering when it is seen; the response.

[B099 — Respond to suffering with the wish to relieve it (karuna in action)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B099%20%E2%80%94%20Respond%20to%20suffering%20with%20the%20wish%20to%20relieve%20it%20%28karuna%20in%20action%29): the principle form.

[SE026 — Recognise common humanity — your suffering is not unique or isolating](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=SE026%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20common%20humanity%20%E2%80%94%20your%20suffering%20is%20not%20unique%20or%20isolating): the isolation broken.

[M020 — Accept suffering as a teacher, without seeking it](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=M020%20%E2%80%94%20Accept%20suffering%20as%20a%20teacher%2C%20without%20seeking%20it): the caution carried in the title.

[SP095 — Convert suffering into understanding at the moment of loss](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=SP095%20%E2%80%94%20Convert%20suffering%20into%20understanding%20at%20the%20moment%20of%20loss): the conversion, held as a moment's work, not a life plan.

[Y011 — Prevent suffering before it arrives](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y011%20%E2%80%94%20Prevent%20suffering%20before%20it%20arrives): the preventive side.

### Pain and its sciences

[Gate control theory of pain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gate%20control%20theory%20of%20pain): Melzack and Wall; spinal gates regulated by descending signals and touch determine how much pain reaches the brain.

[Neuromatrix theory of pain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Neuromatrix%20theory%20of%20pain): the later Melzack; pain as a multidimensional experience produced by neurosignature patterns of a widely distributed neural network, generated sometimes independently of sensory input.

[The pain curve](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20pain%20curve): the meditation tradition's instrument; watching a painful sensation long enough to see it arrive, change and pass, instead of treating it as a fixed wall.

[The body-mind unity in pain](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20body-mind%20unity%20in%20pain): Montaigne; the suffering of pain bound up with the mind's attitude, apprehension, expectation and attachment shaping how illness is borne.

## Related questions and parallels

Does suffering have a purpose? The traditions differ. Frankl's material holds that meaning is available in unavoidable suffering; the Buddhist material holds that the suffering is unnecessary in a different sense, manufactured by clinging; Job's story holds that the answer is not given. The wiki carries all three; the question of which is true is not settled by the cards.

Is acceptance the same as resignation? The chronic-pain acceptance definition, engagement without restriction, is a behavior pattern, not a mood; the Stoic material separates assent to a judgment from approval of an event. Both literatures hold the distinction, and the map holds it with them.

Can suffering be prevented? Y011 says yes, before it arrives; the limit-situation material says some of it cannot be. The boundary is the map's honest open point.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

The practices cluster around the two arrows. [B182 — Separate pain from suffering — daily](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B182%20%E2%80%94%20Separate%20pain%20from%20suffering%20%E2%80%94%20daily) is the daily form: name the first arrow, then look for the second. The avoidance side: [AF035](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AF035%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20that%20avoidance%20is%20negatively%20reinforcing%20%E2%80%94%20it%20works%20short-term%20and%20maintains%20suffering%20long-term)'s pattern, watched for where relief is being bought. The meaning side: [E064 — Practise meaning through the attitude toward suffering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=E064%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20meaning%20through%20the%20attitude%20toward%20suffering), the attitude chosen where the situation cannot be. The relational side: [SE026 — Recognise common humanity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=SE026%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20common%20humanity%20%E2%80%94%20your%20suffering%20is%20not%20unique%20or%20isolating), the isolation broken by the recognition that the suffering is not unique.

This is an editorial combination, not a validated protocol. The trial is: for one week, at the moment of pain, name which arrow is which, and see whether the second one loosens. Retain the naming if it does; drop it if it does not.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

[ST059 — The Poisoned Arrow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST059%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Poisoned%20Arrow): a man shot with a poisoned arrow wants to know who shot it and of what wood the bow was made before he will let anyone treat him; he will be dead before the interrogation ends.

[ST078 — The Two Arrows](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST078%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two%20Arrows): the well-taught person struck once, the untaught twice.

[ST289 — Jaspers's Shipwreck](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST289%20%E2%80%94%20Jaspers%27s%20Shipwreck): death, chance, guilt and struggle; the response not a solution but an illumination.

[ST290 — Job and the Whirlwind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST290%20%E2%80%94%20Job%20and%20the%20Whirlwind): Job loses children, property and health; the friends explain his suffering as punishment; God answers from the whirlwind with questions, not explanations.

[ST311 — Frankl's Why](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST311%20%E2%80%94%20Frankl%27s%20Why): he who has a why to live can bear almost any how; Frankl's own use of the line is careful.

# Anger

> What is anger doing when it rises, and what does it cost the one who holds it?

This dossier is a map, not an essay. The aim is that a reader can say "I want to think about anger", open this page, and see the whole spread of what the compendium holds on it: every entry that bears on anger, grouped by the question it answers, each with a card link. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The editorial aim running through the compendium also runs through this page: to loosen anger's grip on the person who holds it, without pretending the provocation never happened.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. Every concept card carries onward to the practice principles that cite it, so the map needs no separate principle index.

## The map — every entry the wiki holds on anger

### What anger is taken to be

[Anger](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger) — Seneca's definition in De Ira: not the first stirring at an apparent injury, but the subsequent mad rush that treats the appearance as true and moves to revenge.

[Righteous anger vs wrath](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Righteous%20anger%20vs%20wrath) — the contrast between anger that fits its object and anger that is excessive, prolonged or vengeful.

[The fury of the virtuous](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20fury%20of%20the%20virtuous) — Nietzsche's diagnosis in the Genealogy: the moral vocabulary of the weak can be a weapon, and the anger can wear the clothes of goodness.

[The irritation of doubt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20irritation%20of%20doubt) — Peirce's name for the discomfort that inquiry exists to settle.

Seneca draws the line in the second book of De Ira:

> "Anger is that which goes beyond reason and carries her away with it: wherefore the first confusion of a man's mind when struck by what seems an injury is no more anger than the apparent injury itself: it is the subsequent mad rush, which not only receives the impression of the apparent injury, but acts upon it as true, that is anger, being an exciting of the mind to revenge, which proceeds from choice and deliberate resolve." ([Seneca, De Ira II.III, trans. Aubrey Stewart](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64576/64576-0.txt)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

The treatise opens with the stakes as Seneca counts them:

> "no plague has cost the human race more dear: you will see slaughterings and poisonings, accusations and counter-accusations, sacking of cities, ruin of whole peoples, the persons of princes sold into slavery by auction, torches applied to roofs, and fires not merely confined within city-walls but making whole tracts of country glow with hostile flame." (Seneca, De Ira I.II) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### How anger begins

[Anger examined early](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger%20examined%20early) — Seneca's advice in On Anger: examine the belief that one is injured before it settles, because anger cannot begin without the mind's agreement.

[Hostile attribution bias](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hostile%20attribution%20bias) — the reading error Dodge's group traced: when a provocation is ambiguous, some children fill the gap with hostile intent, and the fill predicts retaliation.

[Anger's attributional analysis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger%27s%20attributional%20analysis) — the research line treating anger as what follows a judgment about who is to blame; Novaco's stress-inoculation work is the clinical anchor.

[Frustration–aggression hypothesis](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Frustration%E2%80%93aggression%20hypothesis) — the 1939 formulation that aggression follows frustration, one of the most seminal theories in the aggression literature.

[Hostility research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Hostility%20research) — the studies asking whether chronic hostility and anger predict coronary heart disease; Chida and Steptoe's 2009 synthesis found the association.

### What anger costs

[Revenge](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Revenge) — the paradox Carlsmith, Wilson and Gilbert found: people expect hedonic rewards from punishing an offender, but revenge can have consequences precisely the opposite of what was expected.

[Aggression research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aggression%20research) — the modern study of harm-doing; Anderson and Bushman's 2002 definition: behaviour directed at another with the immediate intent to cause harm.

Shantideva treats hatred in the sixth chapter of the Bodhicharyavatara as what destroys accumulated merit:

> "Whatever wholesome deeds. Such as venerating the Buddhas, and generosity, That have been amassed over a thousand aeons Will all be destroyed in one moment of anger." ([Shantideva, Bodhicharyavatara VI.1, Batchelor translation](https://www.tibethouse.jp/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Bodhisattvas_way_English.pdf)) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### What can be done with anger

[Anger management](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger%20management) — the clinical field that treats problematic anger as its own target rather than as a symptom of something else.

[Anger reappraisal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger%20reappraisal) — applying cognitive reappraisal to situations that elicit anger; Gross describes reappraisal as cognitive change directed at a situation's meaning.

[Anger regulation research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anger%20regulation%20research) — what people can do, before and during an episode, to change anger's intensity and expression; Gross's process model organizes the field.

The delay remedy Seneca prescribes is narrow:

> "The greatest remedy for anger is delay: beg anger to grant you this at the first, not in order that it may pardon the offence, but that it may form a right judgment about it: if it delays, it will come to an end." (Seneca, De Ira II.XXIX) — Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### Forgiveness and its work

[Forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Forgiveness) — the release of a debt: the decision and the slow work of ending resentment and the claim to repayment, without erasing the wrong or excusing it.

[REACH model of forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=REACH%20model%20of%20forgiveness) — Worthington's five steps: Recall the hurt, Empathize with the offender, give an Altruistic gift, Commit, Hold on to the forgiveness.

[Self-forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-forgiveness) — Hall and Fincham's construct: the motivational changes whereby one becomes decreasingly motivated to avoid, retaliate against, or withdraw from the self after an offense.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is anger ever fitting?

The shared question: does anger ever fit its object, or is it always a judgment error to be dissolved? Seneca's treatise treats anger as the rush that treats an appearance as true, and so as always resting on an unexamined judgment; the remedy is delay, not better aim. Aristotle's account (carried on [Righteous anger vs wrath](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Righteous%20anger%20vs%20wrath)) allows anger with the right measure at the right object. The two positions answer the same question — what does a person with a grievance owe their anger — and part on whether the fitting case exists. What remains unresolved is whether the psychological findings on hostile attribution and anger's costs settle the question or only describe the typical case.

### Does forgiveness help the one who forgives?

The shared question: does releasing a resentment benefit the holder, or only the offender? Worthington's REACH model and Carlsmith's revenge findings support the holder's side: revenge predicts worse mood and more rumination than forgiveness. Seneca's delay remedy is agnostic on the debt; it asks only for a right judgment. What remains unresolved is whether the benefits generalize, since the evidence base is largely correlational and the model is a protocol, not a mechanism.

## Practice — variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the rush from provocation to verdict: the impression taken as true before it is checked, the hostile attribution that fills ambiguity with intent, and the revenge that promises relief and delivers rumination. The adaptive direction is the gap between the stir and the judgment: the belief examined before it settles, the attribution held as a hypothesis, and the debt released rather than collected.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [Anger dissection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K169%20%E2%80%94%20Anger%20dissection) — take anger apart as it rises: the bodily sensation, the trigger, the story, the value violated, the urge to act.

2. [Forgiveness practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K170%20%E2%80%94%20Forgiveness%20practice) — the REACH steps: name the harm, attempt empathy, give the gift, commit, hold on.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the delay, or the dissection, or the release has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

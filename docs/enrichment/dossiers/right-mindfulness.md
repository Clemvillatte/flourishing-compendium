# Right Mindfulness

> What does it take to know what is happening, while it is happening?

Knowing what is happening while it happens sounds like the simplest of skills and is among the hardest to keep. The seventh factor trains awareness of body, feeling, mind and mental objects as they arise, attention steadied on the actual rather than the assumed. The clinical adaptations carried it into the laboratory, which is both a success story and a boundary dispute: the path factor and the intervention are not the same thing, and the two must not be flattened into one.

## Nearby terms

Mindfulness: the modern practice family, MBSR and its relatives; this page holds the path factor itself and its root text, and points to the family.

Attention: the general science of how experience is selected; this page holds the training of it toward clear seeing.

Right concentration: the neighbouring factor that steadies the mind this one keeps honest; each has its own page.

Meta-awareness: knowing that one is aware; I pair the capacity with sati, and the map below holds both.

## The map: the entries on right mindfulness

### What the factor is

[Right mindfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20mindfulness): the seventh factor of the Eightfold Path; its root text, the Satipatthana Sutta, calls the four establishments the direct path.

[Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path): the fourth Noble Truth, the Buddha's summary of the training; the factor's umbrella hub.

[The three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings): the path in its most compressed form, virtue, concentration and wisdom; every practice placed under one of the three.

[Sati](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sati): the Pali word for mindfulness, keeping something in mind vividly, without commentary.

[Anapanasati (mindfulness of breathing)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anapanasati%20%28mindfulness%20of%20breathing%29): the flagship practice, mindfulness established on the breath.

[The returning practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20returning%20practice): my name for the central move of meditation, noticing that the mind has wandered and bringing it back, until the returning rather than unbroken attention is understood as the practice.

[Vipassana (insight)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vipassana%20%28insight%29): clear-seeing, treated by Thanissaro as a quality of mind rather than a technique, the ability to see events clearly.

The Satipatthana Sutta opens by naming what the practice is for:

> "This is the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of Unbinding — in other words, the four frames of reference." ([MN 10, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)).

### The refrain that does the work

What makes the practice right is less the objects than the posture toward them; for each of the four frames the instruction returns to the same refrain:

> "There is the case where a monk remains focused on the body in & of itself — ardent, alert, & mindful — putting aside greed & distress with reference to the world." ([MN 10, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)).

The observation is effortful and free of grasping. The map holds the capacities the refrain trains:

[Bare attention in mindfulness practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Bare%20attention%20in%20mindfulness%20practice): the attending to what arises before commentary begins.

[Meta-awareness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meta-awareness): knowing that one is aware, the capacity I pair with sati.

[Metacognitive awareness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metacognitive%20awareness): the part of metacognition that can be reported and measured, what a person knows about their own thinking and how they manage it.

[Mindful attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindful%20attention): attention deliberately trained on present experience, Kabat-Zinn's operational definition.

[Mindful presence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindful%20presence): the label I use for the attended presence itself; Kabat-Zinn, 1990.

[Present-moment awareness theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Present-moment%20awareness%20theory): the claim that contact with the current moment, rather than the remembered past or imagined future, underpins wellbeing.

[Detached mindfulness theory Wells](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Detached%20mindfulness%20theory%20Wells): the clinical cousin, watching thoughts without engaging them.

[Decentering](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Decentering): the capacity to observe thoughts and feelings as mental events rather than as the world or the self.

[Cognitive defusion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cognitive%20defusion): the ACT practice of seeing thoughts as thoughts, constructions passing through awareness, rather than as commands or facts.

[Acceptance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Acceptance): the willing embrace of unwanted inner experience, without trying to change its frequency or form.

[Beginner's mind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Beginner%27s%20mind): the Zen practice of meeting experience as if for the first time, many possibilities where the expert's mind holds few.

[Clarity of mind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Clarity%20of%20mind): the label I use for the unclouded mental state the contemplative traditions describe, Stoic assent, Daoist purity-and-stillness, Buddhist clear seeing.

[The lived body](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20lived%20body): the body as experienced from within, the body one is rather than only the body one has; the first frame's home.

### The modern family

[Mindfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindfulness): my entry for the modern practice family.

[Mindfulness-based stress reduction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindfulness-based%20stress%20reduction): Kabat-Zinn's clinical programme.

[Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindfulness-based%20cognitive%20therapy): the programme developed for relapse prevention in depression.

[Dispositional mindfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Dispositional%20mindfulness): the trait version of the capacity, as it varies between people.

[Mindfulness's clarity mechanism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindfulness%27s%20clarity%20mechanism): seeing mental events accurately rather than dimly, the Bishop et al. operational definition.

[Mindful self-compassion programme](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Mindful%20self-compassion%20programme): the practice family's kindness branch.

[Body scan in MBSR (Kabat-Zinn)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Body%20scan%20in%20MBSR%20%28Kabat-Zinn%29): the first formal practice taught in MBSR, attention moved systematically through the body.

[Walking meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Walking%20meditation): deliberate awareness brought to walking itself, the contact of feet with ground, the movement of the body, the flow of breath.

A 2014 review of 47 randomized trials with 3,320 participants found moderate evidence of improvement in anxiety, depression and pain with mindfulness meditation programs. It found no evidence that meditation programs outperformed active treatments such as drugs, exercise or other behavioral therapies ([Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4142584/)). The reviewers' own conclusion was measured:

> "Clinicians should be aware that meditation programs can result in small to moderate reductions in multiple negative dimensions of psychological stress." ([Goyal et al. 2014, JAMA Internal Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4142584/)).

### The attention traditions

[The craft of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20craft%20of%20attention) and [The discipline of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20discipline%20of%20attention): Weil's account of attention as the trained capacity to suspend one's own thought so that what is in front of one can appear.

[The effort of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20effort%20of%20attention): James's phrase from the Principles of Psychology, attention doing the work where a thought prevails with effort.

[Effortless attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20attention): attention or action experienced as effortless, in Bruya's working definition.

[Focused-attention meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Focused-attention%20meditation): directing and sustaining attention on a chosen object, noticing when it wanders, returning it; Lutz et al.'s account.

[Attention management](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20management): the practice of governing where attention goes, choosing what receives it and protecting it from capture.

[Epoché](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Epoch%C3%A9): suspension, the deliberate withholding of an automatic mental act, in Husserl's tradition the trained posture of attention.

[Delight in the ordinary](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Delight%20in%20the%20ordinary): the capacity to find sufficiency and freshness in plain, repeated things, the food, clothes, work and people already at hand.

## Related questions and parallels

### How do these definitions differ?

The clinical definition that the modern practice family works from, Kabat-Zinn's, reads:

> "the awareness that emerges through paying attention, on purpose, in the present moment, and non-judgmentally to the unfolding of experience moment by moment." ([Wikipedia, "Mindfulness", quoting Jon Kabat-Zinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindfulness)).

Thanissaro, reading the Pali sati, holds that the functions the clinical definition names are not automatically part of sati:

> "Directions such as “bring bare attention to the breath,” or “accept the breath,” or whatever else modern teachers tell us that mindfulness is supposed to do, are actually functions for other qualities in the mind. They're not automatically a part of sati, but you should bring them along wherever they're appropriate." ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu, "Mindfulness Defined"](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/mindfulnessdefined.html)).

From the same essay:

> "meditation isn't just a passive process of being nonjudgmentally present with whatever's there and not changing it at all." ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu, "Mindfulness Defined"](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/mindfulnessdefined.html)).

These excerpts emphasize different things: purposeful, nonjudgmental awareness in the clinical definition, and keeping the practice and its purpose in mind in Thanissaro's account. Whether the two definitions name one capacity or two is a question the sources themselves leave open.

### How does bare attention support practice?

Nyanaponika Thera makes bare attention the core of the practice:

> "Bare attention then becomes the key to the meditative practice of satipatthana, opening the door to mind's mastery and final liberation." ([Nyanaponika Thera, The Power of Mindfulness](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/nyanaponika/wheel121.html)).

Thanissaro, as the passage quoted above says, assigns those functions to other mental qualities and allows them alongside sati: functions like bringing bare attention to the breath "are actually functions for other qualities in the mind. They're not automatically a part of sati, but you should bring them along wherever they're appropriate." Nyanaponika makes bare attention central to satipatthana practice; Thanissaro distinguishes its functions from sati itself while allowing them alongside it. The two readings hold together; neither excludes the other.

### Is mindfulness bare, non-conceptual awareness?

The question, stated precisely: is sati essentially nonconceptual awareness, or can its practice deliberately involve concepts? Gunaratana, in Mindfulness in Plain English, identifies sati with the fleeting moment of awareness before thought arrives:

> "Mindfulness is nonconceptual awareness. Another English term for sati is ‘bare attention.’ It is not thinking. It does not get involved with thought or concepts. . . . It is, rather, the direct and immediate experiencing of whatever is happening, without the medium of thought. It comes before thought in the perceptual process." ([Bhante Henepola Gunaratana, Mindfulness in Plain English, quoted in Bhikkhu Bodhi, "What Does Mindfulness Really Mean?"](https://ibc-elibrary.thanhsiang.org/files/public/01_mindfulness_canonical_bhikku_bodhi.pdf)).

Bodhi, reading the canon and its commentaries against that claim, denies it:

> "In certain types of mindfulness practice, conceptualization and discursive thought may be suspended in favour of non-conceptual observation, but there is little evidence in the Pāli Canon and its commentaries that mindfulness by its very nature is devoid of conceptualization." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, "What Does Mindfulness Really Mean? A Canonical Perspective"](https://ibc-elibrary.thanhsiang.org/files/public/01_mindfulness_canonical_bhikku_bodhi.pdf)).

Bodhi allows nonconceptual observation as a practice. His objection is to making it sati's nature: the phrase bare attention, he argues, conflates sati with manasikāra, the mind's automatic turning to an object, and no act of cognition is ever entirely devoid of factors imparting orientation and meaning. What remains unresolved is where sati's home is: the preconceptual moment of awareness, or the wider field of trained attention, evaluation and recollection the canon gives it.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the default setting of the unattended life: experience passing unexamined, the body noticed only when it complains, attention captured by whatever arrives loudest. The adaptive direction is the factor's own shape, awareness established on some frame of the present, breath, body, feeling or thought, and returned to without drama when it wanders.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K102 — Body scan](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K102%20%E2%80%94%20Body%20scan): attention moved deliberately through the body, frame by frame.

2. [K104 — Mindful walking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K104%20%E2%80%94%20Mindful%20walking): the walk itself taken as the frame of reference.

3. [K415 — Mindful eating](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K415%20%E2%80%94%20Mindful%20eating): one meal attended; the look, smell and changing taste of the food and the body's hunger and fullness signals, noticed.

4. [K398 — Mindfulness bells](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K398%20%E2%80%94%20Mindfulness%20bells): two or three ordinary activities from the day turned into bells, pre-set reminders to stop and attend.

5. [K108 — Meta-awareness check](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K108%20%E2%80%94%20Meta-awareness%20check): a standing question through the day, what is the mind doing right now.

6. [K111 — Interoceptive awareness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K111%20%E2%80%94%20Interoceptive%20awareness): subtle body signals, heartbeat, breathing, hunger, tension, temperature, tuned into.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the bell, or the evening scan, or the question at the red light has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### What the factor is

[ST069 — The Four Foundations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST069%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Four%20Foundations): the sutta opens by calling its practice the direct path for the purification of beings, then lays out the four frames of reference, body, feelings, mind and mental qualities.

[ST068 — The Sixteen Steps of the Breath](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST068%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Sixteen%20Steps%20of%20the%20Breath): the Buddha trains mindfulness of breathing step by step, breathing in long or short and discerning it, then breathing sensitive to the entire body, calming bodily fabrication, sensitive to rapture and pleasure, on through the mind to relinquishment; the card reads the progression as the point, nothing skipped, each step verifiable in the body.

### The refrain that does the work

[ST061 — Bāhiya's Single Instruction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST061%20%E2%80%94%20B%C4%81hiya%27s%20Single%20Instruction): the Buddha's brief instruction to Bahiya, in reference to the seen, there will be only the seen; in reference to the heard, only the heard; the senses received without addition, and Bahiya awakens on the spot.

[ST074 — The Five Hindrances as Thieves](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST074%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Five%20Hindrances%20as%20Thieves): the Buddha catalogues five hindrances that obscure the mind, and the instruction is to know them rather than fight them, noticing when one is present, when absent, and how it arose and passed away.

### The attention traditions

[ST071 — The Wind and the Flag](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST071%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Wind%20and%20the%20Flag): two monks argue whether the flag or the wind is moving; Huineng says it is not the wind or the flag, their minds are moving, the Chan tradition's sharpest statement on where events happen.

[ST023 — Simone Weil on Attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST023%20%E2%80%94%20Simone%20Weil%20on%20Attention): Weil's reading of attention as the rarest and purest form of generosity, school studies as its training ground.

[ST122 — Augustine's Threefold Present](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST122%20%E2%80%94%20Augustine%27s%20Threefold%20Present): Augustine's account of the present as a triple structure, a present of things past, of things present and of things future, with attention the one of the three that is lived in.

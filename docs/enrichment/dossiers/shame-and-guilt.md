# Shame & Guilt

> Which of the two moral emotions repairs a life, and which corrodes it?

Shame and guilt answer occasions of exposure and of wrong: a harm done, a standard broken, a self seen and found wanting. The classical traditions treated that whole territory under names like aidos, repentance and teshuva, and asked how the pain over a wrong should be used. The modern research tradition split the territory in two and staked much on the split: one emotion evaluates an act and reaches for repair, the other evaluates the self and reaches for cover.

I came to this dossier with the question of a reader who has done wrong and wants to know what to do next, and both the classical treatments and the split have a claim on me. I keep two questions separate below: what the feelings are and what they move a person to do, and then what repair of a wrong actually consists of, from confession and apology to the harder case where the feeling has turned on the self and become the problem.

### Nearby terms

- Embarrassment: the family's research treats embarrassment as the briefer social-exposure member of the set; the umbrella sits on [Self-conscious emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-conscious%20emotions), and a reader whose question is the flush of exposure should start there.
- Remorse: I hold it inside [Repentance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Repentance), where Spinoza's definition makes the belief clause, pain over an act believed freely done, the operative part.
- Humiliation: the unchosen exposures appear in Aristotle's material on shame ([Shame](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shame)); the social marking of people is [Stigma research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Stigma%20research).
- Self-consciousness: the family umbrella. The set of emotions that take the self as object, shame, guilt, embarrassment, pride, is mapped on [Self-conscious emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-conscious%20emotions).

## What the two feelings are and what they do

### What is the difference between them?

The family umbrella is [Self-conscious emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-conscious%20emotions): embarrassment, guilt, pride and shame, the set whose elicitation requires stable self-representations and a self-evaluation, on the Tracy and Robins model the card carries ([Tracy & Robins, 2007](http://rpforschools.net/articles/ASP/Tracy%20&%20Robbins%202007%20Self-Conscious%20Emotions%20-%20Where%20Self%20and%20Emotion%20Meet.pdf)). [Moral emotions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20emotions) is the wider set that links moral standards to behaviour, and shame and guilt are its self-directed members. The distinction the research runs on comes from Helen Block Lewis, restated in Tangney and colleagues' review:

> "According to Lewis (1971), shame involves a negative evaluation of the global self; guilt involves a negative evaluation of a specific behavior." ([Tangney, Stuewig & Mashek, 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3083636/))

[Guilt](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Guilt) is the negative evaluation of a specific behaviour, its adaptive form moving toward confession, apology and undoing, its chronic form toward rumination and self-blame. [Shame](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shame) is the negative evaluation of the global self, the feeling that the whole person is defective. The difference is not fine-grained psychology for its own sake: one evaluation points at an act that confession, apology and undoing can reach; the other at the whole self rather than the particular action.

### What does each move a person to do?

The action tendencies, from the same review:

> "On the one hand, shame corresponds with attempts to deny, hide, or escape the shame-inducing situation."
>
> "Guilt, on the other hand, corresponds with reparative actions including confessions, apologies, and undoing the consequences of the behavior." ([Tangney, Stuewig & Mashek, 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3083636/))

The empirical claim behind [Guilt motivates reparative behaviour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Guilt%20motivates%20reparative%20behaviour) is that guilt, aimed at a specific harm, corresponds with confession, apology and undoing rather than avoidance. The same review records the cost side: proneness to shame is related to a wide variety of psychological symptoms, from low self-esteem and depression to suicidal ideation. These are correlations with proneness, and the review does not describe a cure.

Guilt has its own failure mode. The advantages of guilt are lost when the guilt experience is magnified and generalized to the self; the review's example is the slide from "Oh, look at what a horrible thing I have done" to "… and aren't I a horrible person" ([Tangney et al., 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3083636/)). The clinical literature adds obsessional guilt: "In both populations, trait guilt significantly predicted obsessional compulsive complaints, independent of anxiety and depression" ([Shafran, Watkins & Charman, 1996](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/guilt-in-obsessive-compulsive-disorder/)).

### What is shame for, in a developing character?

The classical brake runs under the name aidos. [Shamefastness (aidos)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shamefastness%20%28aidos%29) is the brake that restrains before the principled agent has finished forming, backed by the dread of others' sight, and it trains honour kept even when no one watches. [Shamefastness as quasi-virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shamefastness%20as%20quasi-virtue) holds Aristotle's NE IV.9 account of shame, short of virtue and aimed at the developing character, and Aristotle draws the line himself:

> "Shame should not be described as a virtue; for it is more like a feeling than a state of character." ([Aristotle, NE IV.9, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html))

> "For we think young people should be prone to the feeling of shame because they live by feeling and therefore commit many errors, but are restrained by shame; and we praise young people who are prone to this feeling, but an older person no one would praise for being prone to the sense of disgrace, since we think he should not do anything that need cause this sense." ([Aristotle, NE IV.9, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html))

> "But shame may be said to be conditionally a good thing; if a good man does such actions, he will feel disgraced; but the virtues are not subject to such a qualification." ([Aristotle, NE IV.9, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html))

The Rhetoric adds the audience structure: shame is "pain or disturbance in regard to bad things, whether present, past, or future, which seem likely to involve us in discredit", and "the people before whom we feel shame are those whose opinion of us matters to us" ([Aristotle, Rhetoric II.6, Roberts translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/rhetoric.2.ii.html)). Spinoza defines shame by that same audience and judges it double-sided:

> "Shame is pain accompanied by the idea of some action of our own, which we believe to be blamed by others." ([Spinoza, Ethics III, Definitions of the Affects XXXI, Elwes translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm))

> "I will only add that shame, like compassion, though not a virtue, is yet good, in so far as it shows, that the feeler of shame is really imbued with the desire to live honourably; in the same way as suffering is good, as showing that the injured part is not mortified." ([Spinoza, Ethics IV, LVIII note, Elwes translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm))

Nietzsche values the same capacity differently, and the pair is worth holding together. Spinoza judges shame good as a sign of the desire to live honourably ([Spinoza, Ethics IV, LVIII note](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm)); Nietzsche marks its absence as the achievement: "What is the Seal of Liberty Attained? To be no longer ashamed of oneself." ([Nietzsche, The Joyful Wisdom §275, Common translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52881/52881-0.txt))

Whether those two judgments conflict turns on whether the feeling is a possession or a stage. Aristotle's aidos material supplies a stage reading, and it is my own lens here, distinct from either author's position: shamefastness is praised in the young, who live by feeling and are restrained by it, and not praised in the older person, who should not do anything that need cause it ([Aristotle, NE IV.9](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html)). Read that way, Spinoza's sign is right about the learner and Nietzsche's seal about the finished character; but that is my reconciliation, not one either author endorses.

## Being seen

### What is it to be seen?

Sartre gives the experience its structure. [The look (le regard)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20look%20%28le%20regard%29) is his name for being-seen: shame as the discovery that one exists as an object for another consciousness. [Being-for-others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Being-for-others) is the dimension of selfhood the look opens, with the keyhole example as its founding scene, and [The body-for-others](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20body-for-others) is the body as it exists for the other's gaze in Being and Nothingness. The Stanford Encyclopedia's account of the keyhole scene gives the structure:

> "Suddenly, though, we hear footsteps, and we have an involuntary apprehension of ourselves as an object in the eyes of another; a 'pre-moral' experience of shame; a shudder of recognition that we are the object that the other sees, without room for any sort of inferential theorising or cognising (at least that is manifest to our own consciousness)." ([SEP, "Jean-Paul Sartre"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/))

I leave open how this involuntary experience relates to Tangney's account of global self-evaluation; these passages do not establish that the accounts are incompatible ([Tangney et al., 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3083636/); [SEP, "Jean-Paul Sartre"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/)).

### When does a difference become a disqualifying identity?

The sociology of the question is [Stigma research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Stigma%20research): a marked difference turned into a disqualifying social identity, an attribute "deeply discrediting", with status loss and social rejection as its consequences. That is a social process, distinct from the emotion felt from inside; the shame a person feels and the stigma a society applies are different objects, and repair work on one is not repair work on the other.

## The repair of a wrong

### What does repair consist of?

The Jewish practice of return is [Teshuva (return/repentance)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Teshuva%20%28returnrepentance%29), and Maimonides gives it its classic form:

> "What constitutes Teshuvah? That a sinner should abandon his sins and remove them from his thoughts, resolving in his heart, never to commit them again... Similarly, he must regret the past... He must verbally confess and state these matters which he resolved in his heart." ([Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Repentance 2:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Repentance.2.2?lang=bi))

The New Testament's word is on [Metanoia (transformation)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metanoia%20%28transformation%29): Thayer's lexicon gives the sense as a change of mind that embraces recognition of sin, sorrow for it and hearty amendment, with good deeds as its tokens. The experimental literature measures the pieces: [Apology research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Apology%20research) holds that acknowledgment of responsibility carries the most weight in what makes apologies work, an offer of repair the second most. [Self-forgiveness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-forgiveness) is Hall and Fincham's construct for the far side of it: the motivational changes whereby one becomes decreasingly motivated to avoid stimuli associated with the offense, decreasingly motivated to retaliate against the self, and increasingly motivated to act benevolently toward the self.

The mirror the Buddha holds up to his son gives the review its shape:

> "What do you think, Rahula: What is a mirror for?" ... "For reflection, sir." ... "In the same way, Rahula, bodily actions, verbal actions, & mental actions are to be done with repeated reflection." ([MN 61, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html))

[Repentance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Repentance) holds Spinoza's dissent from the whole category, and it is worth quoting for what it attacks:

> "Repentance is pain accompanied by the idea of some action, which we believe we have performed by the free decision of our mind." ([Spinoza, Ethics III, Definitions of the Affects XXVII, Elwes translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3800/3800-h/3800-h.htm))

On his account the pain is real, the free decision it presupposes is not, and that makes the repenter "doubly wretched", though still useful to those ruled by emotion.

### What is conscience?

The German tradition gives conscience a different shape from rule-storage. [Conscience (Gewissen)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conscience%20%28Gewissen%29) is Heidegger's silent call by which the self is summoned to own its situation, and [The call of conscience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20call%20of%20conscience) is the same account's treatment of guilt. The guilt the call summons to is ontological rather than moral: Dasein "need not first load a 'guilt' upon itself through its failures or omissions", on Heidegger's own formulation of the point ([Heidegger, Being and Time §58, Macquarrie & Robinson text](https://dhspriory.org/kenny/PhilTexts/Heidegger/BeingTime08.doc)). A reader who wants the ledger version of conscience will not find it in Heidegger, and a reader who wants the summons version will not find it in the moral-emotions research; the two pictures are after different things.

### How should a wrongdoer be corrected?

Nietzsche's Gay Science pair gives the rule for correcting others:

> "Whom dost thou call Bad? Him who always wants to put others to shame." ([Nietzsche, The Joyful Wisdom §273, Common translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52881/52881-0.txt))

> "What dost thou think most humane? To spare a person shame." ([Nietzsche, The Joyful Wisdom §274, Common translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/52881/52881-0.txt))

The instruction runs against a familiar correction habit, and I take it as a practice choice: deliver the correction, and keep the contempt out of it.

## When the feelings turn on the self

### What is the failure mode?

[Harsh self-criticism](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Harsh%20self-criticism) is the self-attack tangled with shame, associated with psychopathology, with its roots often in histories of abuse and neglect. [Compassion-focused therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion-focused%20therapy) is Gilbert's approach built for exactly this clientele:

> "In compassion-focused therapy it is hypothesised that this affect regulation system is poorly accessible in people with high shame and self-criticism, in whom the 'threat' affect regulation system dominates orientation to their inner and outer worlds." ([Gilbert, 2009](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/introducing-compassionfocused-therapy/ECBC8B7B87E90ABB58C4530CDEE04088))

[Self-compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-compassion) is Neff's construct for the alternative stance, self-kindness, common humanity and mindfulness: a stance toward suffering that does not need a global self-evaluation to defend. [Grace](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Grace) is, as I read the two together, the theological counterpart of that unearned position: what is received before it is earned, Augustine's formulation against the ledger of merit. The classical practice instruction is on [Be gentle with yourself in the review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S066%20%E2%80%94%20Be%20gentle%20with%20yourself%20in%20the%20review): Marcus Aurelius's "Be not disgusted, nor discouraged, nor dissatisfied, if thou dost not succeed in doing everything according to right principles, but when thou hast failed, return back again" (Meditations V.9), and Seneca's nightly audit hands down a pardon with a correction attached.

### Show up, do not hide

The clinical skill for the hiding is [Opposite action (Linehan)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Opposite%20action%20%28Linehan%29): the DBT practice of acting against the emotion's urge, applied to shame, with a five-person pilot finding shame about a specific event could be reduced over a short period. [The truth sets you free](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20truth%20sets%20you%20free) is the Christian tradition's saying (John 8:32); the practice card pairs it with confession under the show-up rule, and I read it as the one-line case for bringing a wrong into the open. The hiding claim needs one qualification, and the qualification is a live finding rather than a settled one:

> "Whereas psychology literature typically portrays shame as being bad for social relations, motivating social avoidance and withdrawal, there are recent indications that shame can be reinterpreted as having clear social tendencies in the form of motivating approach and social affiliation." ([de Hooge et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29303420/))

> "Two studies (N = 148 and N = 133) using different shame inductions both showed people experiencing shame to prefer to be together with others (social approach) over being alone (social withdrawal)." ([de Hooge et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29303420/))

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Does shame move a person toward hiding or toward others?

The two research findings give opposing answers to the same question, and the de Hooge paper frames them as competing interpretations being tested. The Tangney review's action tendencies hold that "shame corresponds with attempts to deny, hide, or escape the shame-inducing situation" ([Tangney et al., 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3083636/)). De Hooge and colleagues' induced-state experiments found the opposite preference: people experiencing shame preferred "to be together with others (social approach) over being alone (social withdrawal)" ([de Hooge et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29303420/)).

These passages do not settle when shame is associated with withdrawal and when with a preference for company. I take these experiments as a reason not to treat withdrawal as inevitable. They do not establish that every episode of shame produces a preference for company, nor do they explain away the withdrawal findings. When I have caused harm and repair remains possible, I would choose the next step by what repair requires, rather than by the feeling alone; shame does not always attach to a reparable wrong.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the slide from act to self: the miss that becomes "I am a fraud", the apology that never gets made because the person stops showing up, the correction that arrives wrapped in contempt. The adaptive direction runs the other way: name the particular act, ask what repair is still available, and deliver correction (to others and to oneself) without the global verdict.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [Moral injury audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K352%20%E2%80%94%20Moral%20injury%20audit): name one decision that violated your code (what was done, what was violated, who was affected), then distinguish the injury from the guilt and take one step of repair. The paper behind it defines moral injury as the lasting impact of transgressing deeply held moral beliefs; it contains no trial of the audit itself.

2. [Amends and restitution](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K289%20%E2%80%94%20Amends%20and%20restitution): name what you did without softening clauses, acknowledge the impact as the other person describes it, offer concrete repair and do it. The supporting study measured victim-side responses to imagined apology and restitution; it does not test wrongdoers making real amends.

3. [Opposite action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K184%20%E2%80%94%20Opposite%20action): when shame urges hiding and the urge runs against your values, do the reverse: keep the appointment, tell one safe person. The laboratory finding holds for guilt/shame conditions specifically, in a small sample.

4. [The Two Teachers: change the tone, keep the lesson](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K380%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Two%20Teachers%3A%20change%20the%20tone%2C%20keep%20the%20lesson): write the inner critic's sentence down, rewrite it in a warm teacher's voice, keep the information and drop the contempt. From Kolts's compassion-focused therapy workbook.

5. [Self-compassion practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K162%20%E2%80%94%20Self-compassion%20practice): hand on chest: "This is a moment of suffering. Suffering is part of life. May I be kind to myself in this moment." The meta-analytic association with lower psychopathology is correlational.

::Retention:: What I would retain from this theme is a question rather than a technique: after a miss, "what exactly did I do, and what can I put right?" A reader who keeps asking it, and who keeps the amends they promised, has the theme's core; whether they add the self-compassion break or the opposite-action experiment is a matter of what their own shame answers to.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

### The act named and repaired

[ST380 — A Mirror for Rahula](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST380%20%E2%80%94%20A%20Mirror%20for%20Rahula): the Buddha asks his son what a mirror is for, then runs the same reflection through every action before, during and after: harm confessed to the Teacher or a knowledgeable companion, restraint ahead, and refreshment and joy for conduct that was skillful.

### Being seen

[ST283 — Sartre's Keyhole](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST283%20%E2%80%94%20Sartre%27s%20Keyhole): a man crouched at a door, absorbed, hears footsteps and is instantly an object caught in the act; the shame is the discovery that he exists for another's eyes.

### The verdict dissolved

[ST363 — Angelina Uses the Front Door](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST363%20%E2%80%94%20Angelina%20Uses%20the%20Front%20Door): the daughter who left her kerchiefed mother at the front door and slipped in by the side door learns the visitors call her mother the best stick-spindle spinner in America, and comes in by the same door as her mother.

### Release from guilt

[ST390 — The Tinker's Chains Fall Off in a Field](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST390%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Tinker%27s%20Chains%20Fall%20Off%20in%20a%20Field): Bunyan's heart "as full of fear and guilt as ever heart could hold" until, crossing a field with dashes on his conscience, the sentence "Thy righteousness is in heaven" falls upon his soul and the chains come off his legs.

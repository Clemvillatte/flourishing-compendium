# Creation

> Why does making things (objects, connections, children, a life) feel like it means something, and what kind of meaning is that?

A life spends itself making things: meals, sentences, friendships, children, institutions, a character. Why does an afternoon spent making something feel like it counts, when the thing itself is often small and temporary?

Frankl lists creating a work or doing a deed alongside experiencing something or encountering someone, and the attitude taken toward unavoidable suffering, as the three ways a life finds meaning.

The other accounts spread the emphasis wider. Bergson reads joy itself as the sign that creation has happened. Arendt gives the maker a world to erect. This page follows each emphasis where it leads, and underneath each runs the same question: whether the meaning is found or conferred, and where it lands when the thing is made.

### Nearby terms

The family around creation overlaps easily, so I keep apart what can be kept apart:

- Creativity: the capacity to produce work that is both novel and appropriate, with its measurement debates and its psychology. The capacity is a different thing from what making means for a life, and the capacity has its own dossier.
- Generativity: Erikson's name for the adult concern for guiding and providing for the next generation, set against stagnation. The nearest research neighbour to creating children and legacies.
- Procreation: the nearest things to it in this material are generativity's concern for future generations and Bergson's mother beholding her child in the joy passage below.
- Meaning-making: Park's process tradition on how people reinterpret events that violate their wants and beliefs, and Ricoeur's narrative work on composing a life.

## Making as a route to meaning

Frankl's logotherapy puts creating a work or doing a deed first among the three routes to meaning:

> "According to logotherapy, we can discover this meaning in life in three different ways: (1) by creating a work or doing a deed; (2) by experiencing something or encountering someone; and (3) by the attitude we take toward unavoidable suffering." ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf))

Frankl's own name for that first route is [Creative values](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20values): what a person gives to the world through a work or a deed. The [will to meaning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20will%20to%20meaning) behind the route is his primary motivational force, built against Freud's pleasure principle and Adler's striving for superiority: the striving to find meaning, not to restore equilibrium.

The clinical descendant is [Meaning-centred therapy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meaning-centred%20therapy), Breitbart's short-term treatment for people with advanced cancer, and [E062 — Practise meaning through creative work](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=E062%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20meaning%20through%20creative%20work) carries the route into work as an ongoing contribution to others, with what calling research says about how people get drawn into it.

Is there evidence that making does anything for the maker? The diary study asks the question empirically. [Everyday creative activities](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Everyday%20creative%20activities) and [Creative activities](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20activities) report Conner, DeYoung and Silvia's 13-day diary study of 658 young adults, which asked how reported creative activity related to the following day's positive affect and flourishing, and words the association carefully:

> "Lagged multilevel models revealed that people felt higher activated PA and flourishing following days when they reported more creative activity than usual. The other direction – PA predicting next-day creative activity – was not supported, suggesting that the cross-day effect was specific to creative activity predicting well-being." ([Conner, DeYoung and Silvia 2018, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2016.1257049))

[BE059 — Engage in creative behaviour — making is well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE059%20%E2%80%94%20Engage%20in%20creative%20behaviour%20%E2%80%94%20making%20is%20well-being) carries the finding into practice, with the study's own limits stated on it. The boundary of the route matters too: [Meaning-making](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Meaning-making) and [Narrative as meaning-making](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Narrative%20as%20meaning-making) hold Park's process tradition revising the meaning of events and Ricoeur's narrative work composing a life out of them, where Frankl's route is about giving something to the world.

## The making that life is

The metaphysical accounts read the making outward, onto life itself. [Creative advance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20advance) is Whitehead's name for the universe's ongoing production of novelty, the becoming by which the many become one and are thereby increased by one. His [P006 — Creativity is the ultimate principle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P006%20%E2%80%94%20Creativity%20is%20the%20ultimate%20principle) states the category at full strength:

> "'Creativity' is the universal of universals characterizing ultimate matter of fact. It is that ultimate principle by which the many, which are the universe disjunctively, become the one actual occasion, which is the universe conjunctively." ([Whitehead, Process and Reality, quoted in the Garland Encyclopedia of Whiteheadian Thought](https://encyclopedia.whiteheadresearch.org/entries/thematic/metaphysics/the-mystery-of-creativity/))

Bergson's [Creative evolution](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20evolution) makes life itself genuinely new, carried by the élan vital, a shared thrust. Mechanism and finalism both make real creation impossible — mechanism reads an impulse of thought backward into inherited suppositions, finalism assumes a conceptual arrangement of means to ends — and both, he says, fail for the same reason.

And the making runs both ways: life makes, and the making remakes the maker. Bergson's [P102 — Trust the creative thrust of life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P102%20%E2%80%94%20Trust%20the%20creative%20thrust%20of%20life) says it in a sentence:

> "It is then right to say that what we do depends on what we are; but it is necessary to add also that we are, to a certain extent, what we do, and that we are creating ourselves continually." ([Bergson, Creative Evolution, ch. 1, Mitchell trans., Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26163/26163-h/26163-h.htm))

[Creative selection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20selection) compresses the same point: for a conscious being, to exist is to change, to change is to mature, to mature is to go on creating oneself endlessly. Dewey's [Creative impulse](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20impulse) follows the idea rather than the self: a raw germ, given shape through emotion, then needing arduous formative work before it becomes art.

Bergson gives the making its felt signature. Joy, on his account, announces that life has succeeded, and [P191 — Joy is the felt signature of creation; distinct from pleasure](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P191%20%E2%80%94%20Joy%20is%20the%20felt%20signature%20of%20creation%3B%20distinct%20from%20pleasure) keeps the signature distinct from pleasure. His [P195 — Creative emotion (émotion créatrice) is generative of thought, not a byproduct of it](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P195%20%E2%80%94%20Creative%20emotion%20%28%C3%A9motion%20cr%C3%A9atrice%29%20is%20generative%20of%20thought%2C%20not%20a%20byproduct%20of%20it) goes further: an emotion that generates representations rather than only following them, the musician's emotion giving rise to the symphony and its score.

The Nahua [Flower and song](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flower%20and%20song) — the difrasismo in xochitl in cuicatl — puts art and metaphor as the world's own activity, on a metaphysics in which the cosmos is the ongoing self-expression of teotl, nature and art the same activity. One entry marks the boundary of the person-level claim: [Creative destruction](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20destruction) is Schumpeter's term for capitalism renewing itself by displacing the old from within — renewal that destroys, and the creation there is the system's, not the person's.

## The maker and the made world

Arendt gives the maker a world. Where the laborer tends the cycle of life, [Homo faber — man the maker](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Homo%20faber%20%E2%80%94%20man%20the%20maker) invents tools and implements in order to erect one:

> "homo faber, the toolmaker, invented tools and implements in order to erect a world, not—at least, not primarily—to help the human life process. The question therefore is not so much whether we are the masters or the slaves of our machines, but whether machines still serve the world and its things, or if, on the contrary, they and the automatic motion of their processes have begun to rule and even destroy world and things." ([Arendt, The Human Condition, excerpt](https://kortina.nyc/notes/the-human-condition/))

Vico's [Verum factum](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Verum%20factum) makes the knowledge claim: the true and the made are one, verum et factum convertuntur — we know the human world fully because we made it, and the mind does not make itself as it gets to know itself. The [pontifex, bridge-maker](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20pontifex%2C%20bridge-maker) holds the sociology of mediation: the Roman high priest's title, pons plus facere, the one who makes the bridge between the community and the gods, connection as something built with a social status to go with it — though ancient scholars contested the etymology from the start.

Connection between people can be made too: [Micro-moments of connection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Micro-moments%20of%20connection) is Fredrickson's account of positivity resonance, shared positive emotions, biobehavioral synchrony and mutual care, swelling and subsiding in moments.

Making connections between ideas is a different use of the term, and the sources for it are different. On [Conceptual metaphor theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conceptual%20metaphor%20theory%20Lakoff%20Johnson), metaphor structures one domain through another, love spoken of in terms of travel, time in terms of space, importance in terms of weight, with systematic correspondences rather than stray figures of speech. And [Insight research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Insight%20research) adds the sudden-comprehension mode: Kounios and Beeman define insight as a sudden reinterpretation that produces a nonobvious, nondominant reading of a stimulus, situation or event, counting comprehension of a joke or metaphor among its forms.

The Stoic [interconnected good](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20interconnected%20good) is the boundary to this cluster: Marcus Aurelius's sympatheia holds that all things are implicated with one another and the bond is holy — a connection the world already has rather than one a person makes.

Nietzsche's maker makes values: [Value creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Value%20creation) is his account of valuing as creating, in the Common translation:

> "Values did man only assign to things in order to maintain himself—he created only the significance of things, a human significance!" ([Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, "The Thousand and One Goals"](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm))

[E107 — Practise the creation of values](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=E107%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20the%20creation%20of%20values) carries it into practice: a value was once given and bestowed, and it was we who gave and bestowed it, with the demand to become what we are, making laws for ourselves and creating ourselves.

Aristotle gives the maker's competence its shape: [A035 — Cultivate art and craft (techne)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A035%20%E2%80%94%20Cultivate%20art%20and%20craft%20%28techne%29) is techne, a rational quality concerned with making that reasons truly; the maker knows why the method works, so the method can be varied, repaired and taught. Sennett's line of work, on [Craftsmanship research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Craftsmanship%20research) and [PP022 — Pursue craftsmanship](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PP022%20%E2%80%94%20Pursue%20craftsmanship), gives the impulse its charter definition:

> "Craftsmanship names an enduring, basic human impulse, the desire to do a job well for its own sake." ([Sennett 2008, quoted in Jones et al. 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13549839.2021.1939289))

Sennett extends the impulse well past skilled manual labour, to programming, medicine and parenting, and holds that in all these domains craftsmanship focuses on objective standards, on the thing itself. I group Zhuangzi's [quiet craftsman's life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20quiet%20craftsman%27s%20life) — Qing's bell-stands look like the work of spirits because his fasting loses thought of reward by the third day, praise by the fifth, and his own body by the seventh — with Weil's [craft of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20craft%20of%20attention), attention as the trained suspension of one's own thought so the thing or person in front of one can appear, as an editorial comparison: kindred focus, contemplative registers.

Marx's account of making under the wage relation runs against that charter definition, and the pair belongs together as a comparison rather than a disagreement, because the two describe different conditions: on Sennett's account the desire to do a job well belongs to the maker; on Marx's, labour under another's command does not belong to the worker's intrinsic nature, and the thing made stands over against the one who made it:

> "The worker becomes all the poorer the more wealth he produces, the more his production increases in power and size."
>
> "This fact expresses merely that the object which labor produces – labor's product – confronts it as something alien, as a power independent of the producer." ([Marx, Estranged Labour, 1844](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1844/manuscripts/labour.htm))

The two can both be true: the impulse can be enduring and the conditions estranging, and the passages do not say how much making under command estranges its maker, or only some.

## What outlasts the maker

Erikson's generativity — the adult concern for guiding and providing for the next generation, set against stagnation — became a measurable research programme. [Generativity theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Generativity%20theory) records the arrangement:

> "McAdams and de St. Aubin's approach described generativity as an arrangement of seven empirically grounded facets including desire, cultural demand, concern, belief, commitment, action, and narration ( McAdams & de St. Aubin, 1992 ; McAdams, Hart, & Maruna, 2004). Accordingly, cultural demand in combination with inner desire (for agentic immortality and communal nurturance) fosters a conscious concern for future generations. Concern for future generations is strengthened by a belief in the goodness of humankind." ([Rubinstein et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4542587/))

[Legacy motivation research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Legacy%20motivation%20research) picks the concern up as a motive measurable in the lab: Fox, Tost and Wade-Benzoni on the legacy motive as a link between life-meaning and prosocial motivation, developed from intergenerational decision making, generativity and terror management theory, and measured by what participants endorse across a list of ends beyond themselves.

The practices are concrete. [K224 — Legacy letter](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K224%20%E2%80%94%20Legacy%20letter) is the letter to be read after your death, to one named person, covering what you learned, what you love and what you left unfinished; the value is in the writing. [K225 — Service and creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K225%20%E2%80%94%20Service%20and%20creation) is the weekly kernel: one act of service or creation that outlasts the week, with the test of whether a trace of it will still exist next month.

## Making as identity

Whether a person sees themselves as a maker is itself studied. [Creative identity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20identity) is Beghetto's line of work on seeing oneself as someone who makes things, with what the 2006 study of two middle schools actually measured: creative self-efficacy, self-judgments of creative ability. [Creative self-efficacy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Creative%20self-efficacy) is Tierney and Farmer's construct, extended from Bandura's self-efficacy to creative work: the belief that one can produce creative outcomes.

Nietzsche's [creative life](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20creative%20life) is a mode of existence rather than a career: one must still have chaos in oneself to give birth to a dancing star, and the spirit's three metamorphoses end in the child, "a new beginning, a game, a self-rolling wheel, a first movement, a holy Yea." ([Thus Spake Zarathustra, Common trans.](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm)) Kant's [dignity of self-expression](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20dignity%20of%20self-expression) gives the identity its moral floor: saying what one needs and where one stands treats the speaker as an end, not a mere means; whatever has value can be replaced, and what is above value has dignity.

Maslow's [P114 — Create as growth — creation is the evidence of growth](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P114%20%E2%80%94%20Create%20as%20growth%20%E2%80%94%20creation%20is%20the%20evidence%20of%20growth) reads creativeness as an open, spontaneous way of approaching ordinary activities, with his own caution that producing something does not by itself prove psychological health. And [P149 — Use instability creatively](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P149%20%E2%80%94%20Use%20instability%20creatively) records Prigogine's non-equilibrium systems, where order can arise away from equilibrium; the card is careful that the physical account does not establish that a personal crisis produces growth, and I borrow its question, not a law of recovery: when an arrangement has broken down, what could be reorganized, and what support would that require?

## Where the meaning of making lands

When a person makes something, the sources put the meaning in three different places, and they do not rank them.

On the work and the ones who receive it: Frankl's creative values route puts the meaning in what the work gives, and the generativity researchers put the adult's concern in guiding and contributing to the welfare of the next generation. The legacy researchers ask people to weigh ends that reach beyond their own lifetime.

On the maker: Bergson's joy passages:

> "Pleasure is only a contrivance devised by nature to obtain for the creature the preservation of its life, it does not indicate the direction in which life is thrusting. But joy always announces that life has succeeded, gained ground, conquered." ([Bergson, "Life and Consciousness"](https://www.anthologialitt.com/post/henri-bergson-life-and-consciousness))

> "wherever there is joy, there is creation; the richer the creation, the deeper the joy. The mother beholding her child is joyous, because she is conscious of having created it, physically and morally." ([Bergson, "Life and Consciousness"](https://www.anthologialitt.com/post/henri-bergson-life-and-consciousness))

The diary study found a lagged association: days with more reported creative activity than usual were followed by higher activated positive affect and flourishing, the reverse was not supported, and the observational design does not establish causation. And on Bergson's account the making changes the maker: "we are, to a certain extent, what we do, and that we are creating ourselves continually." ([Bergson, Creative Evolution, ch. 1, Mitchell trans., Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/26163/26163-h/26163-h.htm))

Maslow and Frankl give the maker's fulfilment different directions. Maslow, naming the need he called self-actualization in 1943, gives the maker's own completion as the stake — "A musician must make music, an artist must paint, a poet must write, if he is to be ultimately happy. What a man can be, he must be." ([Maslow, "A Theory of Human Motivation", 1943](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Maslow/motivation.htm))

Frankl reads the attainment differently — "In other words, self-actualization is possible only as a side-effect of self-transcendence." ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)) An artist could need to paint, as Maslow says, while finding the fulfilment Frankl describes, by attending to the work rather than the self; Maslow's own satisfaction condition, doing what one is fitted for, already puts the work in the foreground. I read the two as different angles on one restlessness, and the sources do not settle it.

Freud and Frankl read the maker's motive differently. Freud, in his 1908 essay on creative writers and day-dreaming, explains how a writer's wishes can take literary form — "The motive forces of phantasies are unsatisfied wishes, and every single phantasy is the fulfilment of a wish, a correction of unsatisfying reality." — and the work itself, he says, runs from "a wish which finds its fulfilment in the creative work" ([Freud, "Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming", 1908](https://archive.org/download/the-adolescent-fyodor-dostoevsky-novel/Creative%20Writers%20and%20Daydreaming%20%28Sigmund%20Freud%20Essay%29.pdf)). The account reaches the reader's side too: the pleasure the work affords is, on his account, a fore-pleasure, and "our actual enjoyment of an imaginative work proceeds from a liberation of tensions in our minds." ([Freud, "Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming", 1908](https://archive.org/download/the-adolescent-fyodor-dostoevsky-novel/Creative%20Writers%20and%20Daydreaming%20%28Sigmund%20Freud%20Essay%29.pdf))

Frankl rejects reducing the search for meaning to instinctual drives: "Man's search for meaning is the primary motivation in his life and not a 'secondary rationalization' of instinctual drives." ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf))

These claims need not conflict: a work could fulfil a wish and still answer a meaning beyond its author. Freud offers the wish formula as a first approach — the essay says so itself — and gives the account for the day-dream-like work. I find Frankl's emphasis the more useful one when deciding what to make, and that preference is mine; it does not show Freud's account of wish-fulfilment false. The sources do not settle how much of any maker's work is one or the other.

On the work itself: Sennett's craftsman wants to do a job well for its own sake, with objective standards, on the thing itself. Zhuangzi's bell-stand maker loses thought of reward and praise before he touches the tree. Arendt's homo faber erects a world and asks whether the machines still serve it.

Arendt and Bergson differ in what they watch making for: Arendt's toolmaker "invented tools and implements in order to erect a world, not—at least, not primarily—to help the human life process" ([Arendt, The Human Condition, excerpt](https://kortina.nyc/notes/the-human-condition/)), while Bergson's creation runs on the vital side of that line — joy "always announces that life has succeeded, gained ground, conquered", and his example of a creator is the mother beholding her child ([Bergson, Life and Consciousness](https://www.anthologialitt.com/post/henri-bergson-life-and-consciousness)).

The two lives named here are not the same thing — Arendt's life process is the biological cycle of labour, Bergson's life is the creative thrust — and neither passage denies what the other affirms. I read it as a difference of emphasis about the same making: the thing may serve a durable world and still be, for the maker, one more form that life takes.

Camus considers the work that may not last. The absurd creator, on his account, is asked "to sculpture in clay, to know that one's creation has no future, to see one's work destroyed in a day while being aware that fundamentally this has no more importance than building for centuries—this is the difficult wisdom that absurd thought sanctions." ([Camus, The Myth of Sisyphus, 1942](https://archive.org/download/CamusMythOfSisyphusAndOtherReadings/Camus%20Myth%20of%20Sisyphus%20and%20other%20readings_djvu.txt)) The work's worth sits less in the thing than in what it demands: "the great work of art has less importance in itself than in the ordeal it demands of a man and the opportunity it provides him of overcoming his phantoms and approaching a little closer to his naked reality."

These passages do not establish a disagreement with Frankl's creative-values route. The disappearance of a work does not, by itself, settle what making it meant. For me, this raises a practical question: would I still choose the work knowing it might disappear tomorrow?

Can these aims conflict, and what should take priority if they do? The passages quoted here do not settle it.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is the meaning of a life discovered or conferred?

A tension runs through the sources, and it bears on making: when a maker finds meaning in the making, is the meaning something a person discovers, or something they confer?

Frankl locates meaning beyond the self, as something to discover and fulfil. The sentence that precedes the three routes in the book: "the true meaning of life is to be discovered in the world rather than within man or his own psyche, as though it were a closed system" ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf)).

Sartre gives the conferred answer, in two sentences of the same lecture:

> "Man is nothing else but that which he makes of himself."
>
> "to say that we invent values means neither more nor less than this; that there is no sense in life a priori. Life is nothing until it is lived; but it is yours to make sense of, and the value of it is nothing else but the sense that you choose." ([Sartre, Existentialism is a Humanism, 1946](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm))

On the question whether the meaning of a life is discovered or conferred, the two answers are incompatible. Frankl presents the meaning of a life as something to discover in the world. Sartre explicitly denies sense in life a priori and says its value is the sense one chooses. The disagreement concerns whether life's meaning is discovered or conferred; these passages do not decide between the accounts.

Nietzsche had said created, about things rather than about the self. The significance of things is human, assigned, and nothing carries it until a valuer assigns it:

> "Values did man only assign to things in order to maintain himself—he created only the significance of things, a human significance!" ([Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra, "The Thousand and One Goals"](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm))

> "Valuing is creating: hear it, ye creating ones!" ([Nietzsche, Thus Spake Zarathustra](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm))

Nietzsche's claim is narrower than Sartre's: he speaks of the significance of things, not of the sense of a life, so Frankl's discovered meaning and Nietzsche's conferred significance can both hold. That reading is mine; the sources do not make it. On the question itself I lean toward Frankl, because his account asks what the work can answer in the world, a useful question when deciding what to make. That lean is mine; the passages leave both accounts standing.

### Can a maker choose beyond his conditioning?

The maker's freedom is itself disputed. Skinner's behavioural science puts the case against the originator: in the scientific view a person's behaviour is determined by a genetic endowment traceable to the evolutionary history of the species and by the environmental circumstances to which the individual has been exposed, and "as we learn more about the effects of the environment, we have less reason to attribute any part of human behaviour to an autonomous controlling agent." ([Skinner, Beyond Freedom and Dignity, 1971](https://archive.org/download/Beyond_Freedom_and_Dignity/Beyond%20Freedom%20%26%20Dignity%20-%20Skinner.pdf)) The autonomous man of the traditional account is, in his own sentence, "a device used to explain what we cannot explain in any other way."

Frankl names the position he is answering, "pan-determinism": "the view of man which disregards his capacity to take a stand toward any conditions whatsoever. Man is not fully conditioned and determined but rather determines himself whether he gives in to conditions or stands up to them. In other words, man is ultimately self-determining." The basis of prediction, he concedes, "would be represented by biological, psychological or sociological conditions. Yet one of the main features of human existence is the capacity to rise above such conditions, to grow beyond them." ([Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning](https://antilogicalism.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/mans-search-for-meaning.pdf))

The disagreement turns on one exact question: whether genetic and environmental conditions exhaust what determines a person's response. Skinner affirms that they do; Frankl affirms that they do not. Both passages are about human conduct in general; neither mentions making. Applied to making, Skinner's account explains the maker's decisions through genetic and environmental histories, and Frankl maintains that a person can take a stand toward those conditions; the disagreement concerns whether those conditions exhaust the explanation of the response. Skinner does not deny that people intend things or pursue purposes; his claim is that the inner originator explains less than the consequences that selected the conduct.

I lean toward Frankl because his account offers a language for responsibility while acknowledging conditions; Skinner challenges whether that response adds anything to a causal explanation. The sources do not settle it, and Skinner says so himself: "Neither view can be proved, but it is in the nature of scientific inquiry that the evidence should shift in favour of the second." ([Skinner, Beyond Freedom and Dignity, 1971](https://archive.org/download/Beyond_Freedom_and_Dignity/Beyond%20Freedom%20%26%20Dignity%20-%20Skinner.pdf))

### Is there a human nature we do not make?

The third disagreement is about what the making has to work with. Sartre denies a pre-given human essence: "there is no human nature, because there is no God to have a conception of it", and every man is "condemned at every instant to invent man" — "without any support or help whatever". He does not deny shared circumstances, or responsibility toward others, or the weight of conditions on a choice; what he denies is a pre-given essence, a definition of the person that precedes what the person makes of themselves. ([Sartre, Existentialism is a Humanism, 1946](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/sartre/works/exist/sartre.htm))

Camus's analysis of rebellion points toward a shared human nature, and he marks his opposition himself; it leads him "to the suspicion that, contrary to the postulates of contemporary thought, a human nature does exist, as the Greeks believed." The evidence he offers is the act of refusing, and the appeal it makes: "If men cannot refer to common values, which they all separately recognize, then man is incomprehensible to man." ([Camus, The Rebel, 1951](https://archive.org/download/in.ernet.dli.2015.125230/2015.125230.The-Rebel_text.pdf))

The disagreement turns on whether a shared human nature exists independently of each person's self-definition. Sartre affirms that it does not; Camus's analysis leads him to affirm that it does. The bearing on making is direct. Making changes who I am, and the question is whether those changes answer to something common that my choices did not create. Camus's given nature does not forbid self-formation, and Sartre's invented man does not deny shared conditions; the disagreement is about whether self-formation answers to a shared human nature that individual choices did not create. I am drawn to Camus's suggestion that self-formation encounters something human we share before we choose. The passages do not decide between the accounts.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the direction of the energy: consuming without contributing, waiting to feel like making before making, making only for display, or treating the finished thing as the only point, so that the maker is left out of the account. The adaptive direction is making that is small, regular, connected to someone beyond the maker, and allowed to be imperfect.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K324 — Constraint-based creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K324%20%E2%80%94%20Constraint-based%20creation): choose a creative task and impose a constraint, 100 words, one colour, three ingredients, one hour. What to notice: whether the constrained version produces more ideas, or better ones, than the free version. In the rhyme experiments, constrained versions were rated more creative, and the Dr. Seuss wager shows a constraint doing generative work. The findings are task-specific; the kernel does not claim every constraint helps every task.

2. [K225 — Service and creation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K225%20%E2%80%94%20Service%20and%20creation): one act of service or creation a week that outlasts the week, with the trace-next-month test. What to notice: whether the trace test changes what you choose to make. The weekly cadence and the test are the practice's own prompts, not a tested intervention schedule; the research tradition behind it is generativity.

3. [K224 — Legacy letter](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K224%20%E2%80%94%20Legacy%20letter): write the letter to be read after your death, to one named person, covering what you learned, what you love and what you left unfinished. What to notice: which unfinished thing the letter asks to be finished. The value is in the writing; the life-review literature behind it studied reminiscence, ego integrity and life satisfaction in older adults, and the letter format itself is editorial.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. The constraint someone keeps using, the weekly act that survives its month, the letter that gets reread: what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

### Making as forgetting oneself

[ST026 — Woodcarver Qing Makes a Bell-Stand](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST026%20%E2%80%94%20Woodcarver%20Qing%20Makes%20a%20Bell-Stand): asked how his bell-stands look like the work of spirits, Qing describes a fasting that loses thought of reward by the third day, praise by the fifth, and his own body by the seventh; only then does he enter the mountain forest, where the right tree already holds the finished stand.

### Making under constraint, for a village

[ST247 — William Kamkwamba Builds the Windmill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST247%20%E2%80%94%20William%20Kamkwamba%20Builds%20the%20Windmill): forced out of school by the 2001 Malawi famine, a 14-year-old finds a library book about energy and builds a windmill from village junkyard parts, a bicycle dynamo, a tractor fan and blue-gum poles, that first powers lights and later a water pump.

[ST309 — Jiro Makes Sushi](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST309%20%E2%80%94%20Jiro%20Makes%20Sushi): Jiro Ono, 85, makes sushi in a ten-seat Tokyo subway restaurant with three Michelin stars, doing the same thing over and over, improving bit by bit; his son Yoshikazu, 50, has spent a lifetime preparing to take it over and still has not.

### Making a teacher out of a connection

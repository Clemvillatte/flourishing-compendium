# Right Intention

> What is worth resolving on, and what does a resolved life look like?

Intention is where a life's autopilot gets interrupted: renunciation instead of craving, goodwill instead of ill will, harmlessness instead of cruelty. The resolve is not a mood but a standing direction, and the traditions are honest that it fights the current of wanting, disliking and harming. A life cannot be lived without aims; the question worth resolving on is which ones are worth the resolving.

## Nearby terms

Right resolve, right thought: other renderings of the same Pali factor, sammā saṅkappa; my card is [Right intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20intention), and nothing is split between the names.

Intention: the general material on intending, and on the gap between intending and doing, lives in the map below ([The intention-behavior gap](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20intention-behavior%20gap), [Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Implementation%20intentions)); the factor is the path's own discipline of aim, not intending in general.

Aspiration: the measured family of life goals ([Aspiration index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index), [Extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations)) answers what people aim at; it sits in the map's aim side.

Renunciation: the first resolve's material; the boundary against the Motivation and Goals dossiers is that those hold the general science of wanting and pursuing, while this page holds the path's discipline of aim.

## The map: the entries on right intention

### What the factor is

[Right intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20intention): the second factor, Pali sammā saṅkappa; the resolve toward renunciation, freedom from ill will and harmlessness. Translations differ (right resolve, right intention, right thought), but the three components are the same across them.

[Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path): the umbrella hub; intention is the second of eight factors, grouped with view in the wisdom training.

[The three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings): the grouping that places view and intention together under wisdom, alongside ethics and concentration.

[Karma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karma): the Buddha's definition of karma as intention makes this factor the place where practice touches what karma is; what is done deliberately through body, speech or mind sets results going.

[Right view](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20view): the factor listed just before intention; SN 45.8 names right view, then right resolve, then right speech and right action.

The Magga-vibhanga Sutta gives the definition:

> "And what is right resolve? Being resolved on renunciation, on freedom from ill will, on harmlessness: This is called right resolve." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)).

And the Nibbedhika Sutta makes intention the hinge of action:

> "Intention, I tell you, is kamma. Intending, one does kamma by way of body, speech, & intellect." ([AN 6.63, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.063.than.html)).

The Mahācattārīsaka Sutta grades the factor in two forms, a resolve with effluents and a transcendent one that is a factor of the path:

> "And what is the right resolve with effluents, siding with merit, resulting in acquisitions? Resolve for renunciation, resolve for freedom from ill will, resolve for harmlessness. This is the right resolve with effluents... And what is the right resolve that is noble, without effluents, transcendent, a factor of the path? The thinking, directed thinking, resolve, mental fixity, mental transfixion, focused awareness, & verbal fabrications in one developing the noble path whose mind is noble, whose mind is without effluents, who is fully possessed of the noble path." ([MN 117, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.117.than.html)).

### The first resolve: renunciation

My positive rendering of the first resolve is simplicity.

[The insatiability of craving](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20insatiability%20of%20craving): Schopenhauer's structural claim about desire: wanting does not end with getting, because wanting keeps regenerating itself.

[The craving-to-addiction model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20craving-to-addiction%20model): craving as a learned trigger-response loop that mindfulness can unwind (Brewer).

[Contentment with sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20with%20sufficiency): the Daodejing's teaching on contentment and sufficiency; chapter 44 prices the question against life itself.

[Contentment in station](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20in%20station): Epictetus's role-playing counsels from the Discourses.

[Vairagya](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vairagya): dispassion in Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra, the release of the grip on outcomes once they are seen as impermanent.

[Detachment from outcomes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Detachment%20from%20outcomes): the Bhagavad Gita's instruction to act from duty while releasing ownership of the results (2.47).

[Nonattachment](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonattachment): the psychological account of release from mental fixations, a flexible relationship with experience.

[Non-attachment](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Non-attachment): the construct Sahdra, Shaver and Brown built to measure the Buddhist concept, distinguished from interpersonal attachment styles.

[Gelassenheit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gelassenheit): Eckhart's releasement, something a person actively cultivates.

The Two Sorts of Thinking discourse sets the standard the renunciation-thought is held to:

> "And as I remained thus heedful, ardent, & resolute, thinking imbued with renunciation arose in me. I discerned that 'Thinking imbued with renunciation has arisen in me; and that leads neither to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the affliction of both. It fosters discernment, promotes lack of vexation, & leads to Unbinding." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)).

### The second resolve: freedom from ill will

My positive rendering is goodwill.

[Metta (loving-kindness)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metta%20%28loving-kindness%29): the Buddhist practice of goodwill, first of the four brahmaviharas.

[Four immeasurables](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Four%20immeasurables): the set metta belongs to: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy and equanimity.

[Compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion): being moved by suffering and motivated to act; the second of the immeasurables.

[Benevolence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Benevolence): Hume's treatment of benevolence in the second Enquiry.

[Agape](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Agape): the Pauline love given without regard to worth or return.

[Enemy-love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enemy-love): the Gospel's instruction to love the enemy (Matthew 5).

[Enemy as teacher](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enemy%20as%20teacher): Śāntideva's argument that the adversary provides the occasion for forbearance.

[The free person loves all](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20free%20person%20loves%20all): Spinoza's free person, living by reason, desiring the good for everyone.

[The counter-affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20counter-affect): the early discourses' method of cultivating the opposite when an unskillful state takes hold.

[The expanding circle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20expanding%20circle): Singer's name for the history and task of widening moral concern.

[Realistic conflict theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Realistic%20conflict%20theory) and [Intergroup contact](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intergroup%20contact): the study of where ill will between groups comes from, and the finding that structured contact reduces it.

The training the discourse prescribes for the ill-will-thought is stated plainly, it is abandoned:

> "Whenever thinking imbued with ill will had arisen, I simply abandoned it, dispelled it, wiped it out of existence." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)).

### The third resolve: harmlessness

My positive rendering is refusing to add suffering. The Stoic and modern entries below are grouped here by editorial connection: the path factor names a resolve, the neighbors answer kindred questions about harm, and I link them without claiming equivalence.

[Ahimsa](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ahimsa): the Indic principle of non-harm; I read it as a starting floor: whatever else a practice does, it begins by doing no injury.

[Nonviolent action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20action): Gene Sharp's theory of power and his documented methods of nonviolent action.

[Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20communication): Rosenberg's process for reducing conflict in everyday interactions.

[Judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm): the Stoic claim that events do not disturb people, the opinions about the events do (Enchiridion 5); a claim about disturbance, which I connect editorially to the harmlessness resolve.

[The inner friend](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20inner%20friend): Hecato's line via Seneca on making peace with oneself.

### How the resolve is trained

The factor is a resolve, and the discourses train it as a discipline of thought, which is what the translation "right thought" is reaching for.

The Buddha's own method, before his awakening, was to sort his thinking:

> "So I made thinking imbued with sensuality, thinking imbued with ill will, & thinking imbued with harmfulness one sort, and thinking imbued with renunciation, thinking imbued with non-ill will, & thinking imbued with harmlessness another sort." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)).

And the mechanism the discourse names is practice bending the mind:

> "Whatever a monk keeps pursuing with his thinking & pondering, that becomes the inclination of his awareness." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)).

[The counter-affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20counter-affect) (again): pratipakṣā-bhāvanā, the deliberate turning of attention toward what counters an unskillful state.

[Akrasia (weakness of will)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Akrasia%20%28weakness%20of%20will%29): the standing problem that knowing the aim does not secure acting on it.

[The intention-behavior gap](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20intention-behavior%20gap): Sheeran's measurement of how little intentions predict what people then do.

[Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Implementation%20intentions): Gollwitzer's if-then plans, advance decisions that link a situation to a response.

[Morning intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Morning%20intention): the daily setting of one sentence of aim.

[Intentionality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intentionality): Brentano's revival of the scholastic term for the mind's directedness at its objects.

### What a resolved life aims at

[Purpose](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose): Frankl's account of purpose as the thing that lets a person bear almost any how.

[Purpose-linked practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose-linked%20practice): the self-transcendent purpose research on why people keep working at tedious tasks that matter (Yeager and colleagues).

[The bodhisattva vow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20bodhisattva%20vow): the Mahayana commitment to work for the liberation of all beings rather than one's own alone.

[Aspiration index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index): the instrument measuring what people aim at, intrinsic versus extrinsic.

[Extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations): the finding that wealth, fame and image goals correlate with lower well-being even when achieved.

[Self-concordance theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-concordance%20theory): goals that fit developing interests and core values sustain effort and make attaining more rewarding.

[Distinguishing needs from wants (SDT)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Distinguishing%20needs%20from%20wants%20%28SDT%29): the theory's line between the few innate needs whose satisfaction grows a person and the wants that can crowd them out.

[Eudaimonic motivation, intrinsic goals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eudaimonic%20motivation%2C%20intrinsic%20goals): my label for the pull toward intrinsic aspirations, affiliation, growth and contribution; the source paper frames the distinction as intrinsic versus extrinsic aspirations.

[Action over intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20over%20intention): the label for the right action factor, which comes fourth on the path, after right speech; the map of this factor ends where the path moves from resolve to conduct.

## Related questions and parallels

The passages below are held side by side; whether the traditions disagree about them is an open research question. MN 19 commends renunciation-thinking; the Bhagavad Gita says abstention and renunciation alone do not attain perfection, and directs action without entitlement to its fruits:

> "Not by merely abstaining from work can one achieve freedom from reaction, nor by renunciation alone can one attain perfection." ([Bhagavad Gita 3.4](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-3-4/)).

> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action." ([Bhagavad Gita 2.47](https://www.bhagavad-gita.us/bhagavad-gita-2-47/)).

These excerpts do not establish whether the traditions disagree about leaving household life: the MN 19 passage affirms renunciation-thinking without prescribing the abandonment of duties, and the Gita passages deny that renunciation is sufficient, not that it is beneficial. The two questions below gather further passages held side by side. Each is framed around one proposition with quotations from opened carriers; in both cases the carriers can be read as compatible, and the open question is recorded rather than a manufactured disagreement.

### Is the resolve an abstention or a cultivation?

The proposition: freedom from ill will is the whole of the second resolve. The Magga-vibhanga Sutta states the resolve as an absence, being resolved on freedom from ill will. The Karaṇīya Metta Sutta describes goodwill as something cultivated without limit:

> "As a mother would risk her life to protect her child, her only child, even so should one cultivate a limitless heart with regard to all beings. With good will for the entire cosmos, cultivate a limitless heart: Above, below, & all around, unobstructed, without enmity or hate." ([Sutta Nipāta 1.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.1.08.than.html)).

Whether the two suttas disagree is the open question. Each can be read as answering the other's need: the resolve names the absence, the metta instruction names the cultivation, and nothing in either text rejects the other. The Two Sorts of Thinking discourse sits between them, treating non-ill will as a thought that is cultivated and kept; I do not settle which reading the factor itself commits to.

### Less wanting or better wanting?

The proposition: wanting is best cured by aiming it better. The path's instruction contrasts the sensuality-thought with the renunciation-thought, and warns that pursuing the former bends the mind:

> "If a monk keeps pursuing thinking imbued with sensuality, abandoning thinking imbued with renunciation, his mind is bent by that thinking imbued with sensuality." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)).

The motivation science studies what people aim at and what aiming does to them. Ryan and Deci, summarising Kasser and Ryan's aspiration studies:

> "They found, first, that placing strong relative importance on intrinsic aspirations was positively associated with well-being indicators such as self-esteem, self-actualization, and the inverse of depression and anxiety, whereas placing strong relative importance on extrinsic aspirations was negatively related to these well-being indicators." ([Ryan & Deci 2000, Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

The two findings run alongside each other: the aspiration studies report associations between what people emphasise and their well-being, and the path grades the aims it names. The question whether the resolve calls for less wanting or better wanting has a direct comparison inside the tradition's own commentary. Bodhi, writing on the renunciation resolve, marks desire for abandonment:

> "The Buddha's message of renunciation states exactly the opposite: the pull of desire is to be resisted and eventually abandoned. Desire is to be abandoned not because it is morally evil but because it is a root of suffering." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)).

Thanissaro, writing on the effort the path requires, marks desire as its working part:

> "It's worth noting here the centrality of desire in right effort. As AN 10:58 observes, all phenomena are rooted in desire. This observation applies to skillful as well as to unskillful phenomena. Without skillful desire, it would be impossible to develop the path (SN 51:15)." ([Thanissaro Bhikkhu, Right Mindfulness](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/rightmindfulness.pdf)).

The word desire is doing different work in the two passages. Bodhi's target is the desire that runs toward gratification, which he names a root of suffering; his own chapter on right effort distinguishes the energy behind practice from that pull, writing that the same factor "fuels desire, aggression, violence, and ambition on the one hand, and generosity, self-discipline, kindness, concentration, and understanding on the other" ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)). Thanissaro's skillful desire is the wish that moves practice. Read together, the two passages mark the same divide from two sides, craving to be abandoned and skillful wanting to be trained. What neither passage settles is whether the skillful wanting is itself relinquished when the work is done; that question stays open.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the aim's default setting: wanting inherited rather than chosen, ill will rehearsed until it feels like seeing, harm done by habit rather than decision. The adaptive direction is the resolve itself, aims examined and chosen, ill will noticed as a thought that can be abandoned, harmlessness kept as a floor under whatever else is undertaken.

::Selection:: The kernels I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K156 — Morning intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K156%20%E2%80%94%20Morning%20intention): write or speak one sentence of intention for the day: what you want to stand for, what you want to practice, what matters today.

2. [K149 — Values clarification](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K149%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20clarification): what you want your life to stand for, written in three to five domains, values as directions rather than goals.

3. [K150 — Values card-sort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K150%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20card-sort): a deck of values sorted by importance until the top ten stand out.

4. [K153 — Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions): the if-then plan that specifies situation and response in advance, reducing reliance on in-the-moment decision-making.

5. [K154 — Mental contrasting (WOOP)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K154%20%E2%80%94%20Mental%20contrasting%20%28WOOP%29): wish, best outcome, main obstacle, plan; the contrast that links aspiration to resistance.

6. [K155 — Enough lines](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K155%20%E2%80%94%20Enough%20lines): sufficiency defined in advance for each domain, while the judgment is still cold.

7. [K161 — Loving-kindness meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K161%20%E2%80%94%20Loving-kindness%20meditation): phrases of goodwill extended from yourself to a difficult person and all beings.

8. [K247 — Motivational interviewing (self-directed)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K247%20%E2%80%94%20Motivational%20interviewing%20%28self-directed%29): the ruler question asked of a change you are ambivalent about.

9. [K227 — Time-affluence audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K227%20%E2%80%94%20Time-affluence%20audit): a periodic review of whether the trades of time for money can be reversed.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the morning sentence, or the enough line, or the goodwill phrase has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the resolve and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### The three resolves in narrative

[ST376 — Meghiya Finds the Right Grove and the Wrong Thoughts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST376%20%E2%80%94%20Meghiya%20Finds%20the%20Right%20Grove%20and%20the%20Wrong%20Thoughts): Meghiya gets the grove he wanted and, alone under the tree, meets thoughts of sensuality, ill will and harm; wanting the place to practise was not the same as knowing what to cultivate.

[ST352 — The Cowherd After the Harvest](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST352%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Cowherd%20After%20the%20Harvest): the Buddha's own two pictures of watching the mind, the cowherd guarding ripening crops with a stick, then resting in the shade simply mindful of the cows.

### Choosing what to aim at

[ST301 — The Aspiration Index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST301%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Aspiration%20Index): Kasser and Ryan measure what people value and find the relative priority of money, image and fame, controlling for actual income, predicts worse outcomes.

### Testing the aim

[ST066 — The Kālāmas Test It](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST066%20%E2%80%94%20The%20K%C4%81l%C4%81mas%20Test%20It): teachers contradict each other in the Kālāmas' town, and the Buddha tells them to know for themselves which qualities, adopted and carried out, lead to harm and suffering and which to welfare and happiness.

### Ill will and its costs

[ST085 — The Quarrel at Kosambī](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST085%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Quarrel%20at%20Kosamb%C4%AB): monks at Kosambī quarrel over a point of monastic rule and harden into two factions; the Buddha's attempts to reconcile them fail and he leaves, and the episode's teaching is the founder's limits, reconciliation could not be imposed.

### Harmlessness at the far end

[ST086 — The Bodhisattva and the Tigress](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST086%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Bodhisattva%20and%20the%20Tigress): the bodhisattva finds a tigress too weak from hunger to feed her cubs and lies down where she can reach him; compassion as a willingness to be spent, the tradition's extreme case placed at the edge of the map on purpose, to orient the ordinary ones.

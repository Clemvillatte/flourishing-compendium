# Right Effort

> Where should effort go, and how much is the right amount?

Effort has two dimensions the traditions are careful to separate: where it goes, and how much of it. Straining at practice is a recognised failure, and so is the laziness that calls itself ease. The question of how hard to try has real answers, and they are not one-size-fits-all: energy aroused and applied at the right objects, in the right dose, is what the factor actually asks for.

## Where the effort goes

### What the factor asks for

[Right effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20effort) is the sixth factor of the [Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path), the fourth Noble Truth's summary of the training. It sits inside [the three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings) in the concentration division alongside mindfulness and concentration, and it is energy aimed at the mind itself; the four directions give it its shape, and how much is a separate question.

The sibling factors [right speech](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20speech), [right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action) and [right livelihood](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20livelihood) each carry their own dossier in this programme.

The calibration the factor belongs to is [the middle path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20middle%20path): neither the indulgence nor the mortification the Buddha tried first.

The four efforts, in the sutta's own words:

> "There is the case where a monk generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his intent for the sake of the non-arising of evil, unskillful qualities that have not yet arisen... He generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his intent for the sake of the abandonment of evil, unskillful qualities that have arisen... He generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his intent for the sake of the arising of skillful qualities that have not yet arisen... He generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his intent for the maintenance, non-confusion, increase, plenitude, development, & culmination of skillful qualities that have arisen: This, monks, is called right effort." ([Thanissaro, SN 45.8](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)). The four directions: preventing, abandoning, arousing, maintaining.

### What the four directions train

The factor is effort aimed at particular movements of the mind, in a particular direction, at a particular moment, and the first verb is generates desire, so effort begins as wanting the result, before any gritting of teeth. The preventing effort is [B065 — Guard the mind at the source (mind precedes all)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B065%20%E2%80%94%20Guard%20the%20mind%20at%20the%20source%20%28mind%20precedes%20all%29): the guarding done before the quality arises, the Dhammapada's claim that mind precedes all states. The abandoning effort at its smallest is [B116 — Catch irritation early — sparks before fires](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B116%20%E2%80%94%20Catch%20irritation%20early%20%E2%80%94%20sparks%20before%20fires): the spark caught before it is a fire.

The general-capacity hope has a caution attached. Miles and colleagues' six-week program of small self-control tasks found no effect on any measure of self-control, and the authors' conclusion is stated without hedging, carried on [self-control training](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-control%20training).

Duckworth's [grit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Grit) is defined by what it sustains: perseverance and passion for long-term goals, effort and interest kept up over years, through failure, adversity and plateaus; the published abstract puts grit at an average of 4% of the variance in success outcomes, a small share. The other side of a stalled pursuit is [goal adjustment capacity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Goal%20adjustment%20capacity): Wrosch, Scheier, Miller, Schulz and Carver's construct for the withdrawal of behavioral efforts and psychological commitment from a goal that has become unattainable, and the taking up of another, with the findings crediting disengagement with ameliorating distress and protecting health.

### How much is enough

Aristotle's mean is [the right amount of effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20right%20amount%20of%20effort): to feel and to act at the right times, with reference to the right objects, towards the right people, with the right motive and in the right way. The Buddha's tuning image for the monk Soṇa is [ST064 — The Lute String](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST064%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Lute%20String), the vina strung neither too taut nor too loose.

The sutta's own statement of the calibration:

> "In the same way, Sona, over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness, overly slack persistence leads to laziness. Thus you should determine the right pitch for your persistence, attune [2] the pitch of the [five] faculties [to that], and there pick up your theme." ([Thanissaro, AN 6.55](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)). The bracketed glosses are the translator's.

Effort's other half is recovery. [Effort-recovery cycles](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effort-recovery%20cycles) carries Sonnentag and Fritz's four recovery experiences, psychological detachment from work, relaxation, mastery and control. [The seasons of energy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20seasons%20of%20energy) accepts that capacities are not constant: Ecclesiastes' time to every purpose under the heaven, the low times not defects in a steady machine.

The paradox of effortlessness has its own resolutions. [Effortless action through long effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20action%20through%20long%20effort) is the Zhuangzi's: wu wei can look like doing nothing, but the stories that define it are stories of training, Cook Ding's three years of oxen. [Effortless attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20attention) is Bruya's volume on attention and action experienced as effortless: meeting an increase in demand with sustained efficacy without an increase in felt effort.

The Daoist refusal of forcing is [non-striving](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Non-striving), the attitude the Daodejing places in the Dao itself: the Tao in its regular course does nothing for the sake of doing it, and so there is nothing which it does not do. Csikszentmihalyi's absorbed engagement is [flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow), the modern comparison the Right effort card itself offers: challenge matched to skill, effort felt as effortless.

### What the disciplines aim effort at

Epictetus' curriculum, stated in Discourses 3.2, is [the three disciplines](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20disciplines): three areas of study in which a person must train; Hadot named them the disciplines of desire, action and assent, the lived versions of Stoic physics, ethics and logic. [The discipline of assent](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20discipline%20of%20assent) is the Stoic training in treating every impression as a candidate for judgment rather than a judgment already made. [The discipline of non-response](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20discipline%20of%20non-response) is the practice of not adding a reflexive answer to an impression: between the impression and the response there is a judgment, and the judgment is the only movable part.

Simone Weil's account of attention in her essay on the right use of school studies, collected in Waiting for God, is [the discipline of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20discipline%20of%20attention): studies as the exercise of attention. Hadot's name for the practice side of ancient philosophy is [spiritual exercises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spiritual%20exercises): the daily, repeated work by which a student worked toward the complete reversal of our usual ways of looking at things. William James's claim, carried on [the effort of attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20effort%20of%20attention), is that the faculty of voluntarily bringing back a wandering attention, over and over, is the very root of judgment, character and will.

Foucault's term for what modern power makes of flesh is [the disciplined body](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20disciplined%20body): bodies trained, timed and arranged until the body itself carries the discipline; the caution that training and domination can look alike.

The theme comes in narrative form too. [ST112 — Edison Burns the Filaments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST112%20%E2%80%94%20Edison%20Burns%20the%20Filaments) is the filament search testing thousands of materials, platinum, carbonised cotton thread, bamboo from a Japanese fan, with the notebooks recording the failures as data. [ST376 — Meghiya Finds the Right Grove and the Wrong Thoughts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST376%20%E2%80%94%20Meghiya%20Finds%20the%20Right%20Grove%20and%20the%20Wrong%20Thoughts) is Meghiya asking three times to practise in a mango grove that looks ideal, and alone beneath the tree being troubled by thoughts of sensuality, ill will and harm; wanting to exert oneself is not yet knowing what to cultivate.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Does self-control practice strengthen a general capacity?

The shared question: the Self-control training card holds both sides of it. Does the repeated practice of self-control exercises build a general capacity, or does it only rehearse the exercises themselves? The exercise-programme study was read by its authors as the capacity building:

> "Relative to the control phase, participants who exercised showed significant improvement in self-regulatory capacity... The uptake and maintenance of an exercise programme over a 2-month period produced significant improvements in a wide range of regulatory behaviours." ([Oaten & Cheng 2006, British Journal of Health Psychology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17032494/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=17032494&rettype=abstract&retmode=text))

The six-week training study, built with active control conditions and real-world measures to answer the same question, did not detect improvements on its self-control measures:

> "Despite high levels of adherence to the training tasks, there was no effect of training on any measure of self-control... The implication is that training self-control through repeated practice does not result in generalized improvements in self-control." ([Miles et al. 2016, Journal of Experimental Psychology: General](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/107549/))

The disagreement turns on method: the earlier gains came against passive control phases, and the later study was built to rule exactly that out; its own text names the suspicion that the wide-ranging benefits observed after self-control training could be attributable to confounds such as Hawthorne effects, placebo effects, and demand characteristics. The meta-analysis that followed finds a small effect whose mechanism remains poorly understood, with the critical role of repeatedly overriding responses still unestablished ([Friese et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28846503/)). What remains unresolved is whether some forms of practice, a maintained exercise programme against small laboratory tasks, build something the others do not.

For the four directions of the path factor, nothing here changes the aiming. What the disagreement questions is the hope of a general capacity built by rehearsal.

### Is willpower a reservoir that effort drains?

The strength model that the [Self-control](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-control%20training) card carries has two halves, and the training study above tested only one of them. The other half says that using self-control spends it. The model's founding paper stated the budget plainly:

> "Choice, active response, self-regulation, and other volition may all draw on a common inner resource." ([Baumeister et al. 1998, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9599441/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=9599441&rettype=abstract&retmode=text))

Its four experiments — radishes resisted against chocolates, choices made, emotion suppressed, each followed by a further test of persistence or performance — ended in the paper's own summary:

> "These results suggest that the self's capacity for active volition is limited and that a range of seemingly different, unrelated acts share a common resource." ([Baumeister et al. 1998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9599441/))

Two decades later, the evidence base itself had already been questioned, and the replication report's abstract says so in one sentence:

> "Although a meta-analysis of ego-depletion experiments found a medium-sized effect, subsequent meta-analyses have questioned the size and existence of the effect and identified instances of possible bias." ([Hagger et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/))

Twenty-three laboratories with 2,141 participants, preregistered and running a standardized version of the same sequential-task test, could not find the drain:

> "Meta-analysis of the studies revealed that the size of the ego-depletion effect was small with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) that encompassed zero (d = 0.04, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.15]." ([Hagger et al. 2016, Perspectives on Psychological Science](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=27474142&rettype=abstract&retmode=text))

The model's authors did not accept that verdict. Their published reply charged that the standardized protocol never engaged the thing it claimed to test: "Signs indicate the RRR was plagued by manipulation failure and therefore did not test ego depletion", and they stood on their own record: "For two decades, we have conducted studies of ego depletion carefully and honestly, following the field's best practices, and we find the effect over and over" ([Baumeister & Vohs 2016, reply to the Hagger RRR](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/sites/carlsonschool.umn.edu/files/2019-04/baumeister_vohs_2016_perspectives_comment_on_hagger_rrr_misguided_effort_with_elusive_implications_2_0.pdf)). They announced a preregistered multisite test of their own, and it has since run: thirty-six laboratories and 3,531 participants, with the confirmatory result again nonsignificant, d = 0.06 ([Vohs et al. 2021](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/sites/carlsonschool.umn.edu/files/2026-01/vohs-et-al-2021-psci-ego-depletion.pdf)). A third multilab, using a different depleting task, found a small effect that cleared significance: twelve laboratories, 1,775 participants, d = 0.10 ([Dang et al. 2021](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/71213306/junhua_dang_et_al_a_multilab_replication_of_the_ego_depletion_effect_publishersversion.pdf)).

The disagreement is now explicit on both sides, and it is about the same proposition: whether exerting self-control leaves less of it for what follows. One camp reads the multilab record as the model failing its best tests; the other reads the tests as failing the model. I have read the founding abstracts, the authors' reply and the two follow-up reports; the statistics I cannot judge myself.

For the factor's own text, nothing here changes the four directions either. What this dispute questions is the evidence for depletion. The lute string sits oddly between the two camps, since the sutta treats persistence as something tuned rather than spent, and I would not claim the sutta settles what the laboratories have not.

### Is grit anything more than conscientiousness?

[Grit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Grit) is the research field's best-known measure of long effort, and its own record is disputed. Duckworth and colleagues found it accounted for "an average of 4% of the variance in success outcomes", and reported it "highly correlated with Big Five Conscientiousness" and still incrementally predictive "over and beyond IQ and conscientiousness" ([Duckworth et al. 2007, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17547490/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=17547490&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)).

A meta-analysis ten years later, 584 effect sizes from 88 samples and 66,807 people, found that the higher-order structure of grit "is not confirmed", that grit is "only moderately correlated with performance and retention", and that it is "very strongly correlated with conscientiousness". It left one part standing: the perseverance-of-effort facet "explains variance in academic performance even after controlling for conscientiousness" ([Credé, Tynan & Harms 2017, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27845531/), [plain text](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=27845531&rettype=abstract&retmode=text)). A third paper argues that the mixed record comes from measurement, because the grit scale captures perseverance and "has not adequately captured passion", so "perseverance without passion isn't grit, but merely a grind" ([Jachimowicz et al. 2018, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.1803561115), [full text in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6176608/)).

So one side holds that grit adds something beyond a known trait, one that it is mostly the known trait, and one that the scale measures half of what it names. I have only the abstracts and cannot judge the statistics. What the three share, and the factor's own sutta supplies, is the question of what the effort is for: the sutta defines right effort by four directions, and grit by how long a person keeps going. I would use the research as a caution against calling persistence a virtue before asking what is being persisted in.

### Is trying the road or the obstacle?

The Dhammapada puts the work on the practitioner: "You yourselves must strive; the Buddhas only point the way" ([Dhammapada 276, Buddharakkhita translation, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.20.budd.html)). The sutta quoted above begins its four efforts with a person who "generates desire, endeavors, activates persistence, upholds & exerts his intent" ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)). The Tao Te Ching reads in the other direction: the one who devotes himself to the Tao "(seeks) from day to day to diminish (his doing)" until he arrives at "doing nothing (on purpose)", and "if one take trouble (with that end), he is not equal to getting as his own all under heaven" ([Tao Te Ching 48, Legge translation, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt)).

The two do not meet head-on. The Daoist text describes a direction of travel, from more doing to less, and says the aim should not be pursued with trouble, while the Buddhist texts describe the work along the way, and the lute image already tells the monk to tune his persistence so that it is neither over-aroused nor slack. Where they do differ is the starting instruction: the sutta begins by generating wanting for the result, and the Tao Te Ching names that wanting as what defeats the result.

I read the lute string as the mediating text, since it makes the amount of effort something to be tuned and not a creed, and I would not claim the sources settle whether the Daoist "diminishing" and the Buddhist tuning describe the same skill. The [Federer story](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST030%20%E2%80%94%20Federer%27s%20Effortlessness) in this dossier is an observer's report of effortlessness, so it does not bear on the question either way.


## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the undifferentiated push: effort aimed at everything at once and therefore at nothing, the over-aroused persistence that arrives as restlessness, the slack persistence that arrives as its own diagnosis, and the effort held at full pitch through seasons that call for a lower one. The adaptive direction is the aiming and the tuning: the four directions picked one at a time, the pitch checked against restlessness and laziness rather than against the calendar, and the effort sized to the season the body is actually in.

::Selection:: The practices I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K199 — Energy matching](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K199%20%E2%80%94%20Energy%20matching): match the intervention to the state: dull or lethargic, straighten the posture and take sharp breaths; restless or agitated, lengthen the exhale and slow the movements.

2. [K242 — Daily architecture](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K242%20%E2%80%94%20Daily%20architecture): a morning intention, cue-based micro-practices through the day, an evening review; the architecture is the retention system for everything else.

3. [K193 — Weekly habit review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K193%20%E2%80%94%20Weekly%20habit%20review): a weekly look at which cued behaviors fired and which did not, run as a design review rather than a willpower audit.

4. [B058 — Sit daily — consistency over intensity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B058%20%E2%80%94%20Sit%20daily%20%E2%80%94%20consistency%20over%20intensity): the small, repeatable start: five minutes on a low-energy morning counts, and the repetition is what trains the skill.

5. [MO046 — Learn to start — the hardest part is beginning](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO046%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20start%20%E2%80%94%20the%20hardest%20part%20is%20beginning): Laozi's arithmetic, whatever is large was assembled from something small, with Masicampo and Baumeister's finding that a specific plan quiets the interference from unfulfilled goals: only the first piece has to be faced at once.

6. [B036 — Tune, don't force — the lute string](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B036%20%E2%80%94%20Tune%2C%20don%27t%20force%20%E2%80%94%20the%20lute%20string): the Sona image as the check: when the effort produces restlessness or laziness, retune rather than push.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the energy matching, or the weekly review, or the tuning check has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### The pitch found

[ST064 — The Lute String](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST064%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Lute%20String): Soṇa strains so hard in meditation that he considers giving up; the Buddha asks the former lute player whether strings sound best too tight or too slack, and says the energy is the same.

### Wanting to exert, and knowing what to cultivate

[ST376 — Meghiya Finds the Right Grove and the Wrong Thoughts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST376%20%E2%80%94%20Meghiya%20Finds%20the%20Right%20Grove%20and%20the%20Wrong%20Thoughts): Meghiya finds a mango grove that looks ideal for meditation and asks three times to practise there; alone beneath a tree he is troubled by thoughts of sensuality, ill will and harm, and returns to ask the Buddha about them; the episode distinguishes wanting to exert oneself from knowing what to cultivate.

### Effortlessness on the far side of training

[ST030 — Federer's Effortlessness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST030%20%E2%80%94%20Federer%27s%20Effortlessness): Wallace, watching Federer at Wimbledon, reaches for a metaphysical explanation: one of those rare, preternatural athletes who appear to be exempt, at least in part, from certain physical laws; the approaching ball hangs, for him, a split-second longer than it ought to.

### The failures kept as data

[ST112 — Edison Burns the Filaments](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST112%20%E2%80%94%20Edison%20Burns%20the%20Filaments): the filament search tests thousands of materials, platinum, carbonised cotton thread, bamboo from a Japanese fan, and the notebooks record the failures as data; the insight was not in any single test, it was the refusal to stop generating variants until one survived.

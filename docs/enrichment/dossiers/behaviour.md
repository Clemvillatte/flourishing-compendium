# Behaviour

> How do repeated actions become a life, and how does a stuck pattern change?

A habit is what the doing becomes once the deciding drops out of it. The route home, the first reach for the phone and the tone a person takes with certain people can all happen without being chosen, and a life is largely made of such settled patterns. The question worth taking seriously is where those patterns come from, what they build, and what it actually takes to change one that hurts.

The answers differ more than I first expected. Consequence learning, context cuing and deliberate repetition each explain a different part of the doing. The classical traditions add a stronger claim on top: what is repeated hardens into character as well as routine, and the hardening can be aimed. Where the aim belongs, in the will or in the world around it, is a live dispute, and the traditions hold it honestly.

### Nearby terms

- Habit: the umbrella card [Habit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habit) holds the family; formation, maintenance, discontinuity and the body that carries it are all parts of this theme rather than neighbours of it.

- Action and conduct: the same territory seen from the choosing side. The Agency dossier holds the say a person has over their doing; [Autonomy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Autonomy) is the need that pairs them.

- Practice: the deliberate, designed side of repetition; [Habituation by design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K257%20%E2%80%94%20Habituation%20by%20design) is its kernel form.

- Behaviour change: the applied science of moving stuck patterns, from if-then plans to whole taxonomies of techniques.

- Lapse and routine: the moments and grooves a pattern runs through. The return after a lapse is where most change succeeds or fails.

## What repetition builds, and what it asks for

### What a habit is, once the deciding drops out

The science of habit formation starts from a plain observation: repetition gets faster and easier, until the act no longer waits for a decision. [Habit formation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habit%20formation) holds the acquisition question. Lally's study had 96 volunteers carry out a daily behaviour in the same context for 12 weeks, and the time it took participants to reach 95 percent of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days, model estimates that for some participants extended beyond the study's 84 days ([European Journal of Social Psychology 40(6), 2010, abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674)). That range is itself the finding: there is no single timeline a person can promise themselves.

Wendy Wood and David Neal gave the mechanism its sharpest form. On their model, [context-dependent behaviour](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Context-dependent%20behaviour%20%28Wood%20%26%20Neal%29) is not weak will but learned association:

> "Habits emerge from the gradual learning of associations between responses and the features of performance contexts that have historically covaried with them (e.g., physical settings, preceding actions). Once a habit is formed, perception of contexts triggers the associated response without a mediating goal." ([Wood and Neal, "A New Look at Habits and the Habit-Goal Interface", Psychological Review 114(4), 2007, abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ776723))

The goal-directed tradition draws the line on the other side of the same behaviour. Dickinson and Balleine's tests, restated by Ostlund and Balleine, are that, in order to be classified as goal-directed, "the performance of an action must be shown to depend on two things: (i) the causal relationship between that action and its outcome (i.e., its action-outcome contingency) and (ii) the current motivational value of that outcome" ([Ostlund and Balleine, "On habits and addiction", Drug Discovery Today: Disease Models](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2891067/)). [Goal-directed behavior theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Goal-directed%20behavior%20theory) carries the two tests, and the accounts turn out to meet edge to edge: both agree that a habit, once formed, no longer tracks the value of its outcome, which is why devaluing the reward often leaves the behaviour running. The tests tell a person when to stop trusting the pattern; the context model tells them what to change.

The classical side of the same question is older and less mechanistic. [Habituation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habituation) keeps Aristotle's Book II line that we become just by doing just acts, and [Second nature](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Second%20nature) names the settled result: a disposition that works like a natural one. The Daoist [Effortless action through long effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20action%20through%20long%20effort) puts wu wei at the far end of the same road, Cook Ding's years of oxen behind a blade that never dulls. [Excellence as habit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Excellence%20as%20habit) carries Durant's paraphrase of Aristotle, and the card is honest that it is paraphrase, not text.

### Repetition becomes character

Aristotle states the classical claim at the opening of Book II of the Ethics, in the plainest words the tradition has:

> "men come to be builders, for instance, by building; harp-players, by playing on the harp: exactly so, by doing just actions we come to be just; by doing the actions of self-mastery we come to be perfected in self-mastery; and by doing brave actions brave." (Nicomachean Ethics II.1, trans. D. P. Chase, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8438/8438-h/8438-h.htm))

[A004 — Become virtuous by doing virtuous acts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A004%20%E2%80%94%20Become%20virtuous%20by%20doing%20virtuous%20acts) carries the principle with Book II's conditions for an act counting as virtuous, and [A108 — Practise habituation through repeated action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=A108%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20habituation%20through%20repeated%20action) repeats the mechanism as practice: the same repetition forms dispositions in both directions. The Buddhist rendering, [B088 — Every repetition is a vote (habit as karma)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B088%20%E2%80%94%20Every%20repetition%20is%20a%20vote%20%28habit%20as%20karma%29), strengthens the track with each pass, for better or worse.

Two daily practices keep the classical shape. [B058 — Sit daily — consistency over intensity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B058%20%E2%80%94%20Sit%20daily%20%E2%80%94%20consistency%20over%20intensity) takes the Dhammapada's smith removing dross from silver little by little, and [S134 — Build a daily structure](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S134%20%E2%80%94%20Build%20a%20daily%20structure) takes Marcus naming the day's difficult people before meeting them. Small, boring, repeated: the size of the act is not the point.

William James gave the modern tradition its own version. [P038 — Make habit your flywheel](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P038%20%E2%80%94%20Make%20habit%20your%20flywheel) takes his image from the Principles of Psychology, where he writes that "Habit is thus the enormous fly-wheel of society, its most precious conservative agent" ([James, The Principles of Psychology, ch. 4](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). The flywheel keeps a life on its lines, which is a comfort when the lines are good and a problem when they are not.

Thich Nhat Hanh reads the same settledness from inside. [B184 — Recognize habit energy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B184%20%E2%80%94%20Recognize%20habit%20energy) names the restless habit of running toward a future happiness, and his practice is to recognize the tendency and meet it with mindfulness rather than fight it; he concedes that some habit energies are very difficult to transform. The Buddha's own retrospective gives the same warning a classical form: after years of, in his words, beating down, constraining and crushing his mind with his awareness, his verdict was that the racking practice of austerities had attained no superior human state, and the path appeared instead as a memory of ease ([Mahasaccaka Sutta, MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)). The two methods, effort and recognition, are answers to different failures, and a person facing a stuck pattern does well to know which failure is theirs.

### The body that carries it

Habits are not confined to the head. Merleau-Ponty's [habit body](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20habit%20body) treats habit as the body's own way of having a world: knowledge in the hands, a mediator between thought and setting, distinct from both. [E014 — Practise the habit body](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=E014%20%E2%80%94%20Practise%20the%20habit%20body) carries the principle form, the body as our general medium for having a world.

John Dewey makes habit the material of growth. [Plasticity of human nature](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Plasticity%20of%20human%20nature) carries his claim that habits are what education works on, and that their real existence differs in old and young. A habit that runs smoothly gives no occasion for thought, so the blockage is where inquiry begins: that is Dewey's [Obstruction as stimulus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Obstruction%20as%20stimulus). Deliberation, on his account, is itself a [rehearsal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Deliberation%20as%20rehearsal): a trial balancing of habits in imagination.

### What consequences teach

The learning sciences explain behaviour from its results. [Operant conditioning (Skinner)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Operant%20conditioning%20%28Skinner%29) defines the operant as a class of responses that operate on the environment to generate consequences, and [Reinforcement schedules (Ferster & Skinner)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Reinforcement%20schedules%20%28Ferster%20%26%20Skinner%29) adds that when reinforcement arrives matters as much as whether it does.

Complex behaviour is built, not summoned. [Shaping (Skinner)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shaping%20%28Skinner%29) reinforces successive approximations; in Science and Human Behavior, Skinner writes that operant conditioning shapes behaviour as a sculptor shapes a lump of clay, and that complicated operants can be built that would otherwise never appear. [Behavioural chaining (applied behaviour analysis)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Behavioural%20chaining%20%28applied%20behaviour%20analysis%29) teaches routines as chains of component steps, in sequence.

The willpower side of this science has not held up well. [Ego depletion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ego%20depletion) holds the willpower-as-resource account together with its contested history, and [Self-control training](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-control%20training) carries the strongest caution: Miles and colleagues' six-week programme with active controls found no effect of training on any measure of self-control. What those nulls mean is disputed.

### Why maintenance fails

If forming a pattern is hard, keeping it is harder. [Habit maintenance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habit%20maintenance) opens with a systematic review of 100 behaviour-change theories and its opening line: interventions are effective at achieving temporary change while maintenance is rarely attained. That is the review's verdict on its hundred theories, not a proof about every change.

Life-course change offers one opening. [Habit discontinuity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habit%20discontinuity) carries two studies behind one argument: Wood, Tam and Witt tracked people through an actual move and found the habits that persisted were the ones whose performance context had stayed the same, and Verplanken and Roy's 800-participant experiment found that a major change in life circumstances loosened old habits most for people whose old contexts did not recur and whose new settings supported the change. For clinical patterns, [Relapse prevention protocols](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Relapse%20prevention%20protocols) identify Marlatt's high-risk situations and answer them with coping skills, and [Habit reversal theory Azrin](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habit%20reversal%20theory%20Azrin) pairs awareness training with a competing response for nervous habits and tics.

The environment can be arranged directly. [Stimulus control theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Stimulus%20control%20theory) is Bootzin's arrangement of the setting so the target response occurs on cue and competing responses do not; a later review found benefit for insomnia while judging the studies generally poor and the conditioning mechanism unproven. And the return matters as much as the streak: [S133 — Accept that you will fail and return](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=S133%20%E2%80%94%20Accept%20that%20you%20will%20fail%20and%20return) carries Marcus's line on returning to the harmony quickly.

### Changing a pattern by design

The design tools start from the moment of performance. [Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Implementation%20intentions) are Gollwitzer's if-then plans, advance decisions linking a critical situation to a goal-directed response; the meta-analytic verdict is that effects are typically of medium-to-large size. [Tiny habits (Fogg)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Tiny%20habits%20%28Fogg%29) shrinks the behaviour to something motivation can always carry and anchors it to a routine, and [Habit stacking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Habit%20stacking) names the recipe cards: the Anchor Moment, the Tiny Behavior and the celebration.

The clinical side has its own design logics. [Behavioral activation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Behavioral%20activation) interrupts the depression loop from the behaviour side, acting according to the schedule rather than the feeling. [Behavioral self-regulation theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Behavioral%20self-regulation%20theory) is Carver and Scheier's cybernetic account: goals as reference values, perception as input, action closing the gap.

Readiness models and mechanism models answer different questions. [Transtheoretical model (Prochaska & DiClemente)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Transtheoretical%20model%20%28Prochaska%20%26%20DiClemente%29) maps whether a person has moved from precontemplation through to maintenance, and [Behavior change wheel theory Michie](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Behavior%20change%20wheel%20theory%20Michie) asks instead whether the change has the capability, opportunity and motivation it needs.

The design can also be somebody else's. [Nudge theory Thaler](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nudge%20theory%20Thaler) is choice architecture, fruit at eye level rather than the ban.

A person can do it to themselves: [Situation selection](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Situation%20selection) chooses the situations that select the behaviour, and [Situational strategies](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Situational%20strategies) carries Duckworth, Gendler and Gross's account of situational self-control, which nips a tempting impulse in the bud before it grows stronger over time.

[PP072 — Design the environment to support the practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PP072%20%E2%80%94%20Design%20the%20environment%20to%20support%20the%20practice) makes it concrete: phone in another room, guitar left visible. [Keystone habits](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Keystone%20habits) adds the spillover claim, with its tendency-not-guarantee caveat.

### Watching what you do

Measurement is its own lever. [Self-monitoring (Michie et al.)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-monitoring%20%28Michie%20et%20al.%29) is technique 16 of the CALO-RE taxonomy, keeping a record as a method of change, and [BE011 — Recognise that behaviour is measurable — tracking improves outcomes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE011%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20that%20behaviour%20is%20measurable%20%E2%80%94%20tracking%20improves%20outcomes) carries Harkin's 138-study meta-analysis: monitoring prompts raised goal attainment by a moderate average, mainly in personal health goals, and the effect is no guarantee for every person or goal. [MO044 — Learn to track habits — monitoring sustains](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO044%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20track%20habits%20%E2%80%94%20monitoring%20sustains) separates cue-monitoring from vigilant monitoring for unwanted habits, from the Gardner 2021 review.

A response can also be rehearsed before it is needed. [Behavioural rehearsal role-play (DBT, social skills training)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Behavioural%20rehearsal%20role-play%20%28DBT%2C%20social%20skills%20training%29) practises a target response before performing it in real life, and [BE021 — Learn to use behavioural rehearsal — practise before performing](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=BE021%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20use%20behavioural%20rehearsal%20%E2%80%94%20practise%20before%20performing) offers role-play, dialogue rehearsal and, for those who decline both, covert rehearsal in imagination.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Does a habit need an active goal to fire?

Consider the cyclist who rides to campus every morning. When the lectures move online, does the bicycle still get ridden? The two research traditions answer differently, one from experiments on travel choices, the other from a review and a reanalysis of reaction-time data.

Aarts and Dijksterhuis tested habits as goal-action links. In their travel studies, "the automaticity in habits is conditional on the presence of an active goal (cf., goal-dependent automaticity; J. A. Bargh, 1989), supporting the idea that habits are mentally represented as goal—action links" ([Aarts and Dijksterhuis, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology 78(1), 2000, abstract](https://research.tue.nl/en/publications/habits-as-knowledge-structures-automaticity-in-goal-directed-beha/)). In their account the goal is the trigger: habits are mentally represented as goal-action links, and the automaticity is conditional on that goal being active. Their abstract adds a practical note: forming implementation intentions, a strong mental link between a goal and an action, may simulate goal-directed automaticity in habits.

Wood, Mazar and Neal state the opposing view directly: "We found that habitual responses are activated regardless of goals, but they can be performed in concert with goal pursuit" ([Wood, Mazar and Neal, "Habits and Goals in Human Behavior: Separate but Interacting Systems", 2021](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Wood.Mazar_.Neal_.2021.pdf)). On their account, "habits are not goal dependent; they are activated directly by context cues without supporting goals", and "strong habits persist in memory regardless of whether they are consistent with current goals". The cyclist's morning might fire from the hallway and the helmet hook alone.

The disagreement concerns what the trigger is, and it stays a disagreement about activation: whether an active goal is necessary for a habitual response to fire, not whether a person must perform every response that comes to mind. Wood, Mazar and Neal's own sentence keeps the two apart, reporting responses activated regardless of goals but performed in concert with goal pursuit. I lean toward the context-direct account, because it predicts the failure that matters most here: the pattern that survives its own rejection. In practice I examine cues alongside intentions, because changing the aim may leave the old response readily activated, and neither account alone establishes which intervention will work for a particular person. That leaves the operational question where it belongs: not only what should I do instead, but what am I already primed to do next?

### Can self-control be strengthened by practice?

The strength tradition says yes. Oaten and Cheng put participants through a two-month programme of regular physical exercise, chosen because keeping it up requires regulation, and found "significant improvement in self-regulatory capacity" together with reported decreases in perceived stress, emotional distress, smoking, alcohol and caffeine consumption, and improvements in healthy eating, emotional control, study habits and spending control ([Oaten and Cheng, British Journal of Health Psychology 11(4), 2006, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17032494/)). Their conclusion was direct: "The idea that the capacity for self-regulation can be improved is therefore of vast practical importance."

Miles and colleagues ran the same hypothesis with active and no-contact control conditions. They had 174 participants complete a six-week programme of cognitive or behavioural self-control tasks, compared training with active and no-contact control conditions, and found "no effect of training on any measure of self-control"; trained participants did not show reduced ego depletion, become better at overcoming their habits, or report exerting more self-control in everyday life ([Miles et al., Journal of Experimental Psychology: General 145, 2016, abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:10.1037/xge0000185&resultType=core&format=json)). Their stated implication is that "training self-control through repeated practice does not result in generalized improvements in self-control."

The disagreement turns on what the earlier studies actually tested. Oaten and Cheng's design compared each person against their own earlier control phase, and Miles and colleagues note that in many earlier studies improvements "could be attributable to methodological factors (e.g., passive control conditions)". The two papers also used different interventions, exercise programmes against cognitive and behavioural tasks, so the null is not a failed replication of the earlier result. Where I lean: I would not spend six weeks on self-control drills, and I would treat any single-study claim on either side with the caution the designs invite.

### Does one self-control task impair performance on the next?

One proposed mechanism is a fuel: the strength model holds that self-control is a limited resource depleted after a period of exertion. The model's operational prediction was the sequential-task effect, a measurable drop in performance on a second self-control task, and a meta-analysis of the experiments initially found a medium-sized effect.

The prediction then met a registered replication. Twenty-three laboratories ran the standardized sequential-task protocol on 2,141 participants, and "the size of the ego-depletion effect was small with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) that encompassed zero (d = 0.04, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.15]" ([Hagger et al., Perspectives on Psychological Science 11(4), 2016, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/)). The result concerns one standardized protocol, not every depletion finding ever published.

The disagreement turns on what the near-zero effect settles. It bears on the protocol's operational prediction, and not directly on the resource mechanism: even a positive result would not by itself have established that mechanism, and a failed manipulation is a further possible reading of a null. What I take from the exchange is practical: a plan that only works if my willpower tank is full is a fragile plan, and the situational tools above do not depend on the tank.



## Practice: designing what repeats

::Variation:: A stuck pattern usually needs the cue, the environment or the review rhythm to vary, and rarely needs the person to become someone else first. Begin by naming the pattern's cue and the moment it fires, before choosing any exercise from below.

::Selection:: The kernels cluster into design, review and loosening. Choose one behaviour, one anchor and one review, and let the trial decide what stays.

### Make the behaviour the easy choice

1. [Environment design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K177%20%E2%80%94%20Environment%20design): arrange the setting so the desired behaviour is the easy choice and the undesired one is hard. Notice how often the cue, not the intention, decides the moment.

2. [Habit stacking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K176%20%E2%80%94%20Habit%20stacking): anchor the new behaviour to a cue that already fires, and let the existing routine do the reminding. Notice whether the anchor holds through a change of place or schedule.

3. [Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions): pre-decide the moment as an if-then plan, so the decision is made before fatigue arrives. Notice how much deciding the plan removes at the critical moment.

4. [Habituation by design](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K257%20%E2%80%94%20Habituation%20by%20design): choose one virtue and a micro-action that expresses it, repeated over thirty days. Notice the act becoming easier before it becomes interesting.

### Review what repeats

5. [Weekly habit review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K193%20%E2%80%94%20Weekly%20habit%20review): read the week as a design review, one page, asking which cued behaviours fired and which did not. Notice which cue did the work and which the review missed.

6. [Deimplementation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K181%20%E2%80%94%20Deimplementation): regularly retire a practice, strategy or rule that no longer earns its keep. Notice what the removal makes room for.

### Loosen what is stuck

7. [The Habit Releaser](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K393%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Habit%20Releaser): once this week, deliberately break a small habit: a different chair, a different route. Notice how much of the day runs on autopilot, and how perception sharpens when the pattern is broken.

::Retention:: Keep what survives the trial only if the pattern still serves the aim it was chosen for: a behaviour that fires reliably but works against the aim is a habit doing its job in the wrong cause, and the weekly review is where that gets caught. What people keep, in the end, is what works for them.

## Story: lives shaped by repetition

The stories hold the pattern at full length: years of it, the daily offices of it, and the return after it fails.

### Years of practice

[Cook Ding and the Ox](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST001%20%E2%80%94%20Cook%20Ding%20and%20the%20Ox): nineteen years of carving and a blade still sharp; he never cuts through bone, reads the joints, and slows down at the difficult ones.

[Woodcarver Qing Makes a Bell-Stand](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST026%20%E2%80%94%20Woodcarver%20Qing%20Makes%20a%20Bell-Stand): he fasts to still his mind, forgets reward by the third day, praise by the fifth, his body by the seventh; the carving is the last step.

[Deliberate Practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST308%20%E2%80%94%20Deliberate%20Practice): Ericsson's team followed violinists at a Berlin conservatory and tallied how they practised; by the same age, the best had logged thousands more hours of work focused on weaknesses, with immediate feedback, at the edge of ability.

[The Apprentice Sweeps](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST180%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Apprentice%20Sweeps): a medieval apprentice sweeps the shop and holds the tools for years before being trusted with a whole one; the residency and the clerkship keep the logic.

[Franklin's Little Book](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST184%20%E2%80%94%20Franklin%27s%20Little%20Book): Franklin's thirteen virtues, one focused on each week, faults marked with black spots; he never reached perfection and concluded the practice was the gain.

[Phil Connors Practises](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST222%20%E2%80%94%20Phil%20Connors%20Practises): a weatherman trapped in a repeating day practises piano, French and ice sculpture with no audience and no reward, until the practice becomes what he is.

### The day's offices

[The Morning Preparation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST217%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Morning%20Preparation): at dawn Marcus names the day's difficult people and their ignorance of good and evil before he meets them.

[The Evening Review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST220%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Evening%20Review): Seneca, with the lights out, reviews the day's cases, what he did badly and what well.

### Returning after a fall

[The Monk and the Seasons](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST094%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Monk%20and%20the%20Seasons): a child monk on a floating temple torments animals, learns love, commits murder, does penance dragging a millstone up a mountain; spring returns with a new child.

[Backsliding into Abundance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST391%20%E2%80%94%20Backsliding%20into%20Abundance): the Buddha's own account of force failing, remembered ease becoming the path, and the return to solid food judged as part of the practice.

# Right Concentration

> What is collectedness, and how is it tuned rather than forced?

Collectedness is the gathering of the mind, the way an instrument is tuned rather than forced. The traditions treat it as trainable, protectable and available inside ordinary activity, not a rare condition reserved for hermits. The counter-image is the scattered, forced focus of everyday distraction, and the counterweight the traditions propose is practice: a capacity tuned like an instrument and reachable inside ordinary life.

I began with the tuning question: whether the gathered mind is something I force or something I tune.

### Nearby terms

- Samadhi: the umbrella term both traditions use, in the Yoga group below.
- One-pointedness and collectedness: the renderings of the same Pali term, in their own group below.
- Absorption: the English word under which the jhanas and flow meet; the research construct is held at the Attention boundary above.
- Flow: the psychological cousin, in its own group below.
- Right effort: the lute-string tuning is the factor shared with [right effort](right-effort.md); the two dossiers quote the same sutta for different reasons.
- The sibling factors: [right view](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20view), [right intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20intention) and [right mindfulness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20mindfulness) each have their own card, and each factor has a dossier of its own. Right mindfulness carries the sati side of the collected mind, and I keep the samadhi side.

## The path's own account

### What does the eighth factor train the mind into?

The path's own account of what the eighth factor trains is DN 2's four jhanas, each quieter than the last, from rapture-and-pleasure born of withdrawal to "purity of equanimity and mindfulness, neither-pleasure nor stress", held on [Right concentration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20concentration). [Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path) places it in the SN 56.11 listing, where "right" (sammā) reads as complete or whole: a skill to develop, not a rule to obey.

The training instruction is [B028 — Develop right concentration (samadhi)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B028%20%E2%80%94%20Develop%20right%20concentration%20%28samadhi%29): MN 118's breath steps, steadying the mind on the in-breath and the out-breath, with the Dhammapada's pairing of concentration and insight. [B020 — Train ethics, concentration, and wisdom together (the threefold training)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B020%20%E2%80%94%20Train%20ethics%2C%20concentration%2C%20and%20wisdom%20together%20%28the%20threefold%20training%29) reads it inside the Dhammapada's one-verse curriculum, avoid evil, do the skillful, cleanse the mind, in which concentration is one third of a single training.

[B095 — Collect the mind (samadhi as gathering)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B095%20%E2%80%94%20Collect%20the%20mind%20%28samadhi%20as%20gathering%29) renders samadhi as gathering rather than blankness, one step in MN 118's sequence that also senses, gladdens and releases the mind. The fourth jhana's tone is [Equanimity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Equanimity): even-mindedness regardless of valence, with the research definition and the near-enemy warning against indifference.

## The tuning teaching, and its two failure modes

### How is effort tuned rather than forced?

[B036 — Tune, don't force: the lute string](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B036%20%E2%80%94%20Tune%2C%20don%27t%20force%20%E2%80%94%20the%20lute%20string) carries the Sona Sutta's craftsman's question, neither taut nor slack, with over-aroused persistence leading to restlessness and overly slack persistence to laziness. That is the tuning teaching the opening question names, and it comes with a diagnosis step: [Y021 — Diagnose the mind's state before choosing a practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y021%20%E2%80%94%20Diagnose%20the%20mind%27s%20state%20before%20choosing%20a%20practice) holds Vyasa's five states of mind, from restless to arrested, with the fitness rule that samadhi belongs to the one-pointed mind.

[Restlessness as weather](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Restlessness%20as%20weather) gives the Gita's wind-simile for the restless mind, the Pali hindrance of restlessness-and-worry, and calm (samatha) as the tradition's own remedy. I take the simile as a practice reminder of my own, not the tradition's instruction: weather is tuned around, not shouted down.

## The Yoga account: three steps to absorption

### How does the Yoga tradition stage absorption?

[Samadhi](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Samadhi) is the Yoga Sutras' culminating step, where the object fills awareness and the sense of a separate onlooker falls away (III.3 in Johnston's translation), and [Yoga — union of body, breath, and mind](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Yoga%20%E2%80%94%20union%20of%20body%2C%20breath%2C%20and%20mind) is the stilling of the citta's fluctuations (YS I.2) that the inner steps serve. [T041 — Patanjali](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=T041%20%E2%80%94%20Patanjali) compiled the 196 sutras, with the system's heart in two lines, union through control of the versatile psychic nature.

The staging itself is three moves. [Y073 — Bind attention to one place](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y073%20%E2%80%94%20Bind%20attention%20to%20one%20place) is dhāraṇā, the binding of consciousness to a region, with the commentary's named supports and its rule that fixing needs something to fix on. [Y074 — Let absorption come from continuity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y074%20%E2%80%94%20Let%20absorption%20come%20from%20continuity) is dhyāna as the unbroken stream of one kind of idea on the same object; holding, not forcing. [Y075 — Let the object fill the field](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y075%20%E2%80%94%20Let%20the%20object%20fill%20the%20field) defines samadhi by what happens to the object: shining forth in consciousness as the intended object and nothing more.

The three stages then receive a single name and a final distinction. [Y076 — Combine the three into one skill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y076%20%E2%80%94%20Combine%20the%20three%20into%20one%20skill) gives them samyama, the combined tool Book III then uses for everything that follows, and [Y078 — Distinguish settling with and without seed](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Y078%20%E2%80%94%20Distinguish%20settling%20with%20and%20without%20seed) separates the seeded settling that has an object from the seedless state that follows when the impressions cease.

## The concentration practices, and their mechanics

### What are the two styles of practice?

The research typology names two. [Focused-attention meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Focused-attention%20meditation) is the Lutz-group definition, notice the wandering, release the distraction, return to the object, with the research limits the review itself names; its principle form is [AT047 — Learn focused attention meditation — concentration on a single object](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT047%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20focused%20attention%20meditation%20%E2%80%94%20concentration%20on%20a%20single%20object), attending to a chosen object in a sustained fashion, with the training in the return. [AT048 — Learn open monitoring meditation — awareness without narrow focus](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT048%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20open%20monitoring%20meditation%20%E2%80%94%20awareness%20without%20narrow%20focus) is the other style, non-reactive monitoring of the content of experience, moment to moment.

### What do the anchors and the calm train?

[Anchors in focused attention meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anchors%20in%20focused%20attention%20meditation) holds that the return is the practice, not the object, and that the definition leaves the object open. [Anapanasati (mindfulness of breathing)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Anapanasati%20%28mindfulness%20of%20breathing%29) is MN 118's sixteen steps on the breath as the portable anchor, from plain observation to relinquishment. [Shamatha (calm abiding)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Shamatha%20%28calm%20abiding%29) is the settling of the mind comfortably in the present, with Thanissaro's pairing of calm and insight as complementary, not rival techniques.

[B035 — Balance calming and insight (samatha-vipassana; zhiguan)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B035%20%E2%80%94%20Balance%20calming%20and%20insight%20%28samatha-vipassana%3B%20zhiguan%29) gives the Mohe Zhiguan's account of the pair as one thing, and [B042 — Develop calm (samatha)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=B042%20%E2%80%94%20Develop%20calm%20%28samatha%29) is MN 118's repeated breath instruction, in which calming is trained with each in-breath and out-breath, and serenity becomes an awakening factor once body and mind have grown calm.

### What does the Daoist counterpart gather around?

[D048 — Guard the One (shouyi)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D048%20%E2%80%94%20Guard%20the%20One%20%28shouyi%29) is the Neiye's instruction, 'Hold fast to the One; do not lose it, and you will be able to master the myriad things,' with a recipe that aligns the body before it unifies the awareness, a stillness-based counterpart to the binding of attention.

### What limits the capacity, and what builds it?

[Sustained attention theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Sustained%20attention%20theory) explains why the detection-ready state degrades with time on task, and the two accounts, fatigue and boredom, between which the meta-analysis declines to choose. [AT025 — Build concentration capacity through practice — attention is like a muscle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT025%20%E2%80%94%20Build%20concentration%20capacity%20through%20practice%20%E2%80%94%20attention%20is%20like%20a%20muscle) is the capacity claim, with the randomized-trial framing.

[AT031 — Cultivate attentional endurance — build capacity gradually](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT031%20%E2%80%94%20Cultivate%20attentional%20endurance%20%E2%80%94%20build%20capacity%20gradually) holds the deliberate-practice finding that full-attention practice is limited in daily duration, starts at an hour or less, and separates skill levels by years of accumulation rather than heroic sessions. And the bodily base the tuning teaching cannot tune around is [AT042 — Recognise the role of sleep in attention — sleep deprivation destroys concentration](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT042%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20role%20of%20sleep%20in%20attention%20%E2%80%94%20sleep%20deprivation%20destroys%20concentration).

## One-pointedness, and its double sense

### Is the gathered mind fixed or unified?

[One-pointedness](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=One-pointedness) renders citt'ekaggata two ways at once: one-pointedness, fixated on a single object, and unification of mind, still but not merged, able to observe the changing flow.

## Absorption in activity: flow and its cousins

### What does absorption in activity look like?

[Flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Flow) is Csikszentmihalyi's absorption in activity, energized focus, full involvement, autotelic, with the three conditions and the founder's own caution about the addictive side. Set beside the jhana's entry, entered "quite withdrawn from sensuality" ([DN 2, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.02.0.than.html)), the two accounts part on the door: withdrawal versus challenge-skill engagement. I keep these terms distinct because the passages compared here do not establish that they describe the same state; the two are cousins, not a demonstrated disagreement.

The engineering side of flow is carried by its principles. [P118 — Engineer flow — match challenge to skill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P118%20%E2%80%94%20Engineer%20flow%20%E2%80%94%20match%20challenge%20to%20skill) is the challenge-skill match, checked against the meta-analysis that followed; [P119 — Give flow clear goals and immediate feedback](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P119%20%E2%80%94%20Give%20flow%20clear%20goals%20and%20immediate%20feedback) is the other two conditions, engineered rather than awaited.

[P173 — Engineer absorption](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=P173%20%E2%80%94%20Engineer%20absorption) is Nakamura and Csikszentmihalyi's conditions, perceived challenges that stretch existing skills, clear nearby goals, and immediate feedback about progress. [AT026 — Learn to enter flow — the state of full attentional absorption](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT026%20%E2%80%94%20Learn%20to%20enter%20flow%20%E2%80%94%20the%20state%20of%20full%20attentional%20absorption) reads attention as the finite medium of consciousness, and what that bound places on experience.

What happens to a state once found has its own principles. [PP019 — Protect absorption](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=PP019%20%E2%80%94%20Protect%20absorption) holds that the state found is not the state kept, and protection is its own principle; [MO060 — Recognise the role of flow in motivated well-being](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=MO060%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20role%20of%20flow%20in%20motivated%20well-being) reads flow as one of the most studied optimal motivational states, associated with intrinsic reward and high well-being.

### What is the Daoist version of effortless absorption?

[D057 — Enter flow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=D057%20%E2%80%94%20Enter%20flow) is the Daoist entry point, Cook Ding's blade that needs no sharpening because it moves through the joints; [Wu wei](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Wu%20wei) is non-forcing as the Daoist cousin of tuned effort, from the Daodejing's doing-nothing line; [Effortless action through long effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20action%20through%20long%20effort) is the nineteen-year blade: the effortlessness that long practice, not abstention from effort, produces.

## Single-tasking, and the modern conditions

### What are the modern conditions the practice meets?

[AT016 — Cultivate single-tasking — do one thing at a time](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT016%20%E2%80%94%20Cultivate%20single-tasking%20%E2%80%94%20do%20one%20thing%20at%20a%20time) is the Monsell switching costs, principle form: the gathered mind's everyday answer. [Attention residue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Attention%20residue) is Leroy's name for the cost itself, what switching leaves behind: part of the thinking stays with the task left, and the next task suffers while it does.

[AT027 — Recognise the conditions for deep work — environment, time, and energy](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT027%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20the%20conditions%20for%20deep%20work%20%E2%80%94%20environment%2C%20time%2C%20and%20energy) is the environment side of the protected block, and [AT063 — Recognise attentional costs of media multitasking — heavy multitaskers have worse attention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=AT063%20%E2%80%94%20Recognise%20attentional%20costs%20of%20media%20multitasking%20%E2%80%94%20heavy%20multitaskers%20have%20worse%20attention) is the scatter the single-tasking principle answers.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is the collected mind fixed on one point or gathered into a whole field?

The shared question: what does collecting the mind do to the field of awareness? The Theravada commentarial tradition's definition, in Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga, fixes the mind on a single object:

> "the state in virtue of which consciousness and its concomitants remain evenly and rightly on a single object, undistracted and unscattered" ([Wikipedia, Samadhi, quoting Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi)): Secondary; the passage was checked against the raw HTML of the article.

Shankman's reading of the same family of terms holds a wider option inside the same word. Samadhi "basically means 'undistractedness'"; it may be viewed as "an exclusive focus on a single object," but also as "a broader state of awareness in which the mind remains steady and unmoving, yet aware of a wide range of phenomena around the meditation object." The same source renders citt'ekaggata both as one-pointedness, a mind fixated on a single object, and as unification of mind, in which mind becomes very still but does not merge with the object of attention, and is thus able to observe and gain insight into the changing flow of experience. ([Wikipedia, Samadhi, quoting Richard Shankman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi)): Secondary; the passages were checked against the raw HTML of the article.

The disagreement is real and live: the article's Criticism section reports that the one-pointed interpretation "has become a matter of debate." What remains unresolved is whether ekaggata is exclusive fixation or unified stillness; the practice instructions differ, a single fixed object on one reading, a steady awareness of the wide field on the other, and my own pages hold both.

### Does the mind fade with use, or drift from boredom?

The shared question is the one the capacity section names from the research side: why does a detection-ready state degrade with time on task? A 2013 meta-analytic review of vigilant attention describes competing explanations of that decrement:

> "There is ample empirical evidence for both the fatigue/resource-depletion and the boredom/absentmindedness accounts, but neither is consistent with all the available data." ([Langner and Eickhoff, Psychological Bulletin, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627747/))

The two accounts locate the failure in different places. The resource-depletion account, which the review reports as strongly supported, holds that the decline results from a depletion of attentional resources under continuous allocation: sustaining attention to a simple task is effortful and demanding, and the available attentional resources decline. The boredom account, which the review reports others arguing, holds that cognitively more challenging and interesting tasks can pose similar or even higher demands and still fail to elicit strain or deterioration, and reads the decline in monotonous tasks as boredom, with the mind-wandering it brings, redirecting processing resources away from the task at hand.

Langner and Eickhoff propose a synthesis: maintaining attention on an unrewarding task requires continuing self-regulation, whose decline could contribute both to reduced attention and to mind-wandering. They treat sustained attentional demand as necessary but insufficient for the typical decrement. In 2013, they described direct evidence for this proposal as sparse. I find the synthesis worth considering, while keeping that evidential limit in view.

### Is concentration for stilling the mind or for seeing what it does?

The shared question: what the gathered mind is for once it is gathered. Patanjali's opening definition, as rendered on the Sacred Texts transcription of the Yoga Sutras, puts the weight on restraint:

> "Union is restraining the thought-streams natural to the mind." (Yoga Sutras I.2) "Then the seer dwells in his own nature." (I.3) ([Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, Sacred Texts](https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/yogasutr.htm))

The same page gives I.4 as "Otherwise he is of the same form as the thought-streams." On this reading the mind's activity is the obstacle and its quieting is the result. The text also allows a stage with content: "Cognitive meditation is accompanied by reasoning, discrimination, bliss and the sense of 'I am.'" (I.17), followed by "another meditation which is attained by the practice of alert mental suspension until only subtle impressions remain" (I.18).

A discourse in the Pali collection uses immersion for a different job:

> "Mendicants, develop immersion. A mendicant who has immersion truly understands. What do they truly understand? The origin and ending of form, feeling, perception, choices, and consciousness." ([SN 22.5, Sujato trans.](https://suttacentral.net/sn22.5/en/sujato))

Here the gathered mind is the condition for seeing how the five aggregates arise and cease. Sujato's note to the passage adds that mendicants are similarly enjoined to develop immersion to understand the six senses and the four noble truths (same page).

Both treat collectedness as trainable and as a means, so the two do not oppose each other on whether to practise it. They differ on where the practice ends. In the Yoga line, as I read these aphorisms, discriminating meditation is a stage and the endpoint is the seer abiding in its own nature once the thought-streams are restrained. In the Pali line the endpoint is knowledge of arising and ending, which requires that the mind stay active enough to observe. The dossier's earlier question about one-pointedness versus a wide field bears on this, since a wide field gives the observer more to watch.

My lean, which is mine: for an essay on alleviating suffering, the Pali framing is the more usable, because it ties concentration to a change in how experience is understood. I hold the Yoga framing as the cleaner account of what deep stillness is like.

Limits: I read Book I to aphorism 1.18 of the Yoga Sutras and one Pali discourse. The Yoga Sutras name further stages of absorption that I have not examined, and discernment appears in I.17 as a stage, so the contrast above may be sharper than the full text supports. The Sacred Texts page does not name its translator.

### Can liberation come without jhana?

The shared question: whether awakening requires the absorptions the canon defines as right concentration. Gunaratana, writing on the jhanas in Theravada practice, says the tradition itself admits that it does not:

> "The possibility of attaining the supramundane path without possession of a mundane jhana has been questioned by some Theravada scholars, but the Visuddhimagga clearly admits this possibility when it distinguishes between the path arisen in a dry-insight meditator and the path arisen in one who possesses a jhana but does not use it as a basis for insight (Vism.666-67; PP.779)." ([Gunaratana, The Jhanas in Theravada Buddhist Meditation](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/gunaratana/wheel351))

He adds that "Textual evidence that there can be arahats lacking mundane jhana is provided by the Susima Sutta" together with its commentaries (same book). Thanissaro reads the same discourse the other way:

> "This discourse is sometimes cited as proof that a meditator can attain Awakening (final gnosis) without having practiced the jhanas, but a close reading shows that it does not support this assertion at all." ([Thanissaro, translator's note to SN 12.70](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn12/sn12.070.than))

In a longer treatment he sorts the positions into three, the first being the commentarial one that jhana is unnecessary, and concludes: "Evidence from the Canon supports the third position, but not the other two", the third being that at least the first jhana is essential at every level of awakening ([Thanissaro, Wings to Awakening, Part III](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/wings/part3.html)).

The two answer one question, whether a person can awaken with no jhana, and give opposite answers. Part of the gap is a matter of what the word covers. Thanissaro says the commentaries are right to call their kind of jhana unnecessary, while awakening cannot occur without jhana in the canonical sense (same Wings page). Gunaratana distinguishes mundane jhana from the supramundane jhana that comes with the path itself. If the two use the word jhana for different things, the surviving disagreement is smaller than the quotes suggest: whether the strong absorptions the canon describes can be skipped.

My lean, which is mine: for an essay about developing wisdom, I would present this as a live disagreement within the tradition and avoid asserting either side. The practical question for a reader is how much stillness insight needs, and the sources I opened do not answer that in numbers.

Limits: I read excerpts of two books and one translator's note, not the Pali texts or the commentary lines Gunaratana cites. I have not opened Mahasi-line teachers who hold the dry-insight view in their own words, so that side comes through Gunaratana and the commentaries as he reports them.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the scatter and the strain: attention divided across tabs and tasks, the switching costs paid as residue, and the white-knuckle forcing that the lute-string teaching names as the first failure mode. The adaptive direction is the tuned middle: one object, one task, one protected block, with the return counted as the practice rather than as failure, and the effort checked against the two failure modes the sutta names, restless over-effort and slack neglect.

::Selection:: The practices I have selected as sources of that variation:

1. [K100 — Breath anchoring](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K100%20%E2%80%94%20Breath%20anchoring): rest attention on the breath's physical sensation; note where it went; return. The return is the practice, not a failure.

2. [K105 — Single-tasking](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K105%20%E2%80%94%20Single-tasking): one task with its next concrete step chosen in advance, protected for a defined block of forty-five to ninety minutes.

3. [K190 — Flow block](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K190%20%E2%80%94%20Flow%20block): the protected block assembled from flow's own conditions: clear goal, feedback signal, notifications cleared.

4. [K392 — The Three-minute Breathing Space](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K392%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Three-minute%20Breathing%20Space): the three-step mini-practice that narrows to the breath and widens back to the whole body: the double sense of collectedness in one exercise.

5. [K417 — Shouyi (guarding the One)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K417%20%E2%80%94%20Shouyi%20%28guarding%20the%20One%29): the Daoist guarding of the One, a stillness-based counterpart to the binding of attention, one of the oldest named Daoist meditations on the card's account.

6. [K391 — Bring raisin mind to one routine activity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K391%20%E2%80%94%20Bring%20raisin%20mind%20to%20one%20routine%20activity): one daily activity done for a week with full attention: the single-tasking taught in the small.

::Retention:: What I would retain from this theme is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters: one breath anchor, one protected block, or the tuning feel itself. Any one of the three, kept, carries what the theme offered.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The stories that best carry the theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### The rose-apple tree

[ST391 — Backsliding into Abundance](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST391%20%E2%80%94%20Backsliding%20into%20Abundance): MN 36's account of the forcing that failed, the clenched-teeth austerities that attained nothing, and the first jhana returning as a memory of sitting in the cool shade of a rose-apple tree, rapture and pleasure born from seclusion; the path followed the memory, not the strain.

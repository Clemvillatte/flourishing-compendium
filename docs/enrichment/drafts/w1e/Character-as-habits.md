## Page body (for content/concepts/Character as habits.md)

Character as habits is William James's thesis from The Principles of Psychology (1890): "When we look at living creatures from an outward point of view, one of the first things that strike us is that they are bundles of habits" ([James 1890, ch. IV](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). The habit chapter gives the mechanism and the moral together: habits are laid down by a plastic nervous system, and character is what the laying-down accumulates to.

### The mechanism

Habit rests on plasticity, "the possession of a structure weak enough to yield to an influence, but strong enough not to yield all at once," and James's first proposition is that "the phenomena of habit in living beings are due to the plasticity of the organic materials of which their bodies are composed" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). He borrows Carpenter's summary: "our nervous system grows to the modes in which it has been exercised" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). Habit simplifies the movements required to achieve a given result, makes them more accurate and diminishes fatigue ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). The same fact at social scale: "Habit is thus the enormous fly-wheel of society, its most precious conservative agent" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)).

### The moral

The ethical upshot is that small acts are not small. "We are spinning our own fates, good or evil, and never to be undone. Every smallest stroke of virtue or of vice leaves its never so little scar" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). Nothing is wiped out: "As we become permanent drunkards by so many separate drinks, so we become saints in the moral, and authorities and experts in the practical and scientific spheres, by so many separate acts and hours of work" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). The famous limit: "in most of us, by the age of thirty, the character has set like plaster, and will never soften again" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)).

### The maxims

Education's great thing is "to make our nervous system our ally instead of our enemy," which means to "make automatic and habitual, as early as possible, as many useful actions as we can" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). The maxims follow: "Never suffer an exception to occur till the new habit is securely rooted in your life"; "Seize the very first possible opportunity to act on every resolution you make"; and, to keep the will itself in training, "Keep the faculty of effort alive in you by a little gratuitous exercise every day" ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)). The misery of the alternative is to have nothing habitual but indecision ([James 1890](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/James/Principles/prin4.htm)).

### In the compendium

P056 "Your character is your habits" is the compendium's direct statement of the thesis, citing this chapter: you are what you repeatedly do, so choose your repetitions. P075 "Treat habits as will" extends it through Dewey: will is the organized system of habits, so building the habit is building the will.

"Identified versus introjected motivation" is self-determination theory's distinction between two ways of carrying a rule inside. On the [[Self-determination|self-determination]] continuum, extrinsically motivated behaviors "cover the continuum between amotivation and intrinsic motivation, varying in the extent to which their regulation is autonomous" ([Ryan & Deci 2000, American Psychologist](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

Introjected regulation "involves taking in a regulation but not fully accepting it as one's own. It is a relatively controlled form of regulation in which behaviors are performed to avoid guilt or anxiety or to attain ego enhancements such as pride. Put differently, introjection represents regulation by contingent self-esteem" ([Ryan & Deci 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

### Identification

One step more autonomous: "Identification reflects a conscious valuing of a behavioral goal or regulation, such that the action is accepted or owned as personally important" ([Ryan & Deci 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)). Beyond it stands integration, in which identified regulations "have been evaluated and brought into congruence with one's other values and needs" — though integrated actions "are still considered extrinsic because they are done to attain separable outcomes rather than for their inherent enjoyment" ([Ryan & Deci 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)). The contrast in a sentence: an introjected motive behaves like an inner policeman; an identified motive behaves like a chosen project.

### What internalization needs

The continuum is not a ladder people must climb stage by stage; internalization depends on the supports around it. Relatedness matters ("internalization is more likely to be in evidence when there are ambient supports for feelings of relatedness"), competence matters, and autonomy is the one the paper singles out as critical for integration: "To integrate a regulation, people must grasp its meaning and synthesize that meaning with respect to their other goals and values" ([Ryan & Deci 2000](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)).

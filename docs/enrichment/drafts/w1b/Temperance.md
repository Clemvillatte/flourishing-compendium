"Temperance" is one of the four cardinal virtues: the right ordering of pleasure and appetite. Plato's Republic defines it as "a kind of beautiful order and a continence of certain pleasures and appetites," and says it bears "more likeness to a kind of concord and harmony than the other virtues did" ([Republic IV.430e, Shorey translation](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0168%3Abook%3D4%3Asection%3D430e)). Aristotle locates it on the map: "temperance is a mean with regard to pleasures" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.11, Ross translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### The strange phrase

Plato flags the ordinary phrase for it as a riddle: "the phrase 'master of himself' is an absurdity... for he who is master of himself would also be subject to himself." The absurdity is the clue. In the temperate soul the better part rules the worse, and rules with consent — a concord rather than a garrison. Temperance is not appetite's defeat but its harmony.

### What it is about

Aristotle narrows the terrain: temperance "must be concerned with bodily pleasures, but not all even of these," specifically "touch and taste," the pleasures "that the other animals share in." The temperate person desires moderately "the things that, being pleasant, make for health or for good condition"; the self-indulgent person "craves for all pleasant things...at the cost of everything else," and is pained by the craving itself — "it seems absurd to be pained for the sake of pleasure" ([Nicomachean Ethics III.11](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.3.iii.html)).

### In the compendium

The body principles give the modern instances: BI040 on caffeine, BI015 on sleep hygiene, BI007, BE042 and BE044 on moderating consumption. The Stoic line turns temperance toward the dichotomy of control: appetite is the chief thing not in one's direct command, so the discipline of desire becomes the curriculum. Habituation, not willpower, is the vehicle — a trained disposition rather than a repeated decision.

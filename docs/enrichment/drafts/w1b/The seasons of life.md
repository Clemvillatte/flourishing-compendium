"The seasons of life" is the ancient image that human life has a temporal shape: stages that ask different things. Ecclesiastes 3 puts it as poetry: "To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven: a time to be born, and a time to die; a time to plant, and a time to pluck up that which is planted... a time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance" ([Ecclesiastes 3:1-4, KJV](https://www.biblehub.com/kjv/ecclesiastes/3.htm)).

### Erikson's ground plan

The developmental-science treatment of the same temporal question is Erikson's. His epigenetic principle, borrowed from embryology, states "that anything that grows has a ground plan, and that out of this ground plan the parts arise, each part having its time of special ascendancy, until all parts have risen to form a functional whole" (Erikson 1968: 92, quoted in the archived text "Erik Erikson's Theory of Identity Development," Internet Archive). Each of the eight stages has its polarity — trust versus mistrust in infancy, autonomy versus shame and doubt, initiative versus guilt, industry versus inferiority, identity versus identity confusion in adolescence, intimacy versus isolation, generativity versus stagnation in adulthood, and integrity versus despair at the end ([same archived text](https://ia601802.us.archive.org/16/items/theory-of-identity-erikson/theory%20of%20identity%20erikson.pdf), drawing on Erikson 1950, 1968).

### What the seasons claim

The claim is not that development is fixed. On the account opened here, "each crisis or conflict is never completely solved but appears to be most pronounced at the age at which it emerges," and "both syntonic and dystonic opportunities are essential for development" — the negative pole is part of the design, not simply failure ([archived text](https://ia601802.us.archive.org/16/items/theory-of-identity-erikson/theory%20of%20identity%20erikson.pdf)). And each item "exists in some form before 'its' decisive and critical time normatively arrives" (Erikson 1968: 93, 95, quoted in the same text).

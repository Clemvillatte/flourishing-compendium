"Express by shaping, not venting" is John Dewey's distinction between expression and discharge. A scream or a slammed door discharges emotion; expression is different in kind. On the encyclopedia's account of Dewey's Art as Experience, "All expression involves discharge, but only a particular kind of discharge counts as expression. Dewey insists that someone who simply acts angrily is not expressing anger" ([SEP, "Dewey's Aesthetics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)). The goal of expression "is not to get rid of the initial emotion... but to achieve a clearer sense of its content and significance" ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)).

### The shaping

Expression requires a medium and work on it. Dewey's own line: "where there is no administration of objective conditions, no shaping of materials in the interest of embodying the excitement, there is no expression" ([Art as Experience, quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)). The emotion is not finished before the shaping begins; the emotive impulsion works like a magnet that "evokes, assembles, accepts, and rejects memories, images, observations, and works them into a whole" ([Dewey, quoted in SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey-aesthetics/)). What is vented is left as it was; what is shaped becomes intelligible.

### What the lab found

The experimental literature sides with Dewey against venting. In Bushman's study, 600 angered participants hit a punching bag while either ruminating on the person who angered them or thinking about becoming physically fit, while a control group did nothing. The rumination group was the angriest and most aggressive afterward, and the control group the least: "Rumination increased rather than decreased anger and aggression. Doing nothing at all was more effective than venting anger. These results directly contradict catharsis theory" ([Bushman 2002, PSPB](https://faculty.washington.edu/jdb/345/345%20Articles/bushman(2002).pdf)).

### In the compendium

The practices that shape rather than vent: K210's active constructive responding shapes joy into connection, K338's restorative conversation shapes grievance into repair, S122's journal is a medium rather than a drain, and AF076 warns that suppression in close relationships costs intimacy.

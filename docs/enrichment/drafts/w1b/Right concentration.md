Right concentration (sammā samādhi) is the eighth factor of the Buddhist Noble Eightfold Path: the collecting of the mind onto one object, steady and wholesome. In the Pali discourses it is defined by the four jhānas, the graded absorptions. "Quite withdrawn from sensuality, withdrawn from unskillful mental qualities, he enters and remains in the first jhana: rapture and pleasure born from withdrawal, accompanied by directed thought and evaluation," each jhāna quieter than the last, ending in "purity of equanimity and mindfulness, neither-pleasure nor stress" ([Samaññaphala Sutta, DN 2](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.02.0.than.html)).

### Tuning, not forcing

The practice is tuned rather than forced. In the Sona Sutta the Buddha teaches a former musician with his own instrument: "when the strings of your vina were neither too taut nor too loose, but tuned to be right on pitch, was your vina in tune & playable?" Over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness, overly slack persistence to laziness, so the practitioner determines the right pitch and picks up the theme ([Sona Sutta, AN 6.55](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.055.than.html)). Concentration is an achievement of calibration, the opposite of white-knuckled effort.

### In the compendium

B028 "Develop right concentration (samadhi)" carries the concept directly. The attention principles translate it for a modern life: AT047's focused-attention meditation is the laboratory version, AT026's flow is absorption in doing rather than sitting, AT016's single-tasking and AT027's deep-work conditions are its everyday training, and AT063 warns that heavy media multitasking works against it. AT031 builds the capacity gradually, and ST067's Fletcher classroom shows the tuning in a teacher's craft. ST066, the Kālāmas story, keeps the whole path honest: test the fruits in experience.

# S007 — Accept what happens as the will of providence (candidate, pending advisor QC)

Body, Plain words and For example only; front-matter, blockquote, all link sections and
the reflections comment untouched. Seed locator preserved.

---

Chapter V.8 of the Meditations compares what befalls you to a physician's prescription: as Aesculapius prescribes exercise or cold baths for health, "the nature of the universe prescribed to this man disease or mutilation or loss or anything else of the kind" (Med. V.8, Long). The chapter's instruction follows from the analogy:

> "And so accept everything which happens, even if it seem disagreeable, because it leads to this, to the health of the universe and to the prosperity and felicity of Zeus (the universe). For he would not have brought on any man what he has brought, if it were not useful for the whole."
> (Med. V.8, [George Long's translation](https://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.5.five.html))

Marcus states the same as a habit of attention: "Does anything happen to me? I receive it and refer it to the gods, and the source of all things, from which all that happens is derived" (Med. VIII.25, Long). The compendium's practical reading: after a setback, treat what happened as material the situation is offering. (Med. IV.10, V.8, VIII.50; Disc. I.12, I.17; Seneca, Letters 107, On Providence.)

**Plain words** The prescription analogy is the whole idea. When the doctor orders something unpleasant, you take it because you believe it serves your health; Marcus asks you to read events the same way, as serving a whole whose ledger you cannot see. The practice is not deciding that the loss was good. It is refusing to treat your first reaction as the last word, and asking what the situation now makes possible.

*For example:* A promotion falls through. After the sting, the exercise is to ask what the situation now makes possible, rather than re-arguing the decision.

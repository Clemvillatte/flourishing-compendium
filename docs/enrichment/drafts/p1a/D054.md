The Secret of the Golden Flower sets the term of the practice:

> "Only after a hundred days of consistent work, only then is the Light real; only then can one begin work with the spirit-fire."
> (Secret of the Golden Flower, [Wilhelm/Baynes translation](https://archive.org/stream/TheSecretOfTheGoldenFlowerByRichardWilhelmAndCarlJung/The+Secret+of+the+Golden+Flower+by+Richard+Wilhelm+and+Carl+Jung_djvu.txt))

The text adds that the hundred days depends on "the degree of industry in work" and "the degree of strength in the physical constitution", and warns that "whoever is weak and lazy will not produce it even after the hundred days". The Daodejing gives the arithmetic of small work: "The tree which fills the arms grew from the tiniest sprout; the tower of nine storeys rose from a (small) heap of earth; the journey of a thousand li commenced with a single step" (DDJ 64, Legge). The compendium's practical reading: schedule brief, regular practice and let the hundred days accumulate; a single heroic session cannot replace the accumulation. (Secret of the Golden Flower; DDJ 64.)

**Plain words** A practice you can do on your worst day is worth more than one that needs your best. The hundred days is not a magic number; it is the observation that the effect of practice lives in the repetition, and repetition is what the calendar gives you, not the mood. Ten minutes at the same time each day compounds; the three-hour rescue session is a payment against a debt, and the debt is still there next week.

*For example:* Suppose you want to start meditating. Compare a ten-minute slot you can keep every morning with an hour-long Sunday session; the question is which one you would still keep in a bad week.

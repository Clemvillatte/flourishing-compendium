# Right Intention

> What is worth resolving on, and what does a resolved life look like?

This dossier is a map, not an essay. The aim is that a reader can say "I want to think about right intention", open this page, and see the whole spread of what the compendium holds on the second factor of the Eightfold Path: every entry that bears on the resolve, grouped by the question it answers, each with a card link. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The editorial aim running through the compendium also runs through this page: to loosen the autopilot of wanting, disliking and harming, without pretending a life can be lived without aims.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. Every concept card carries onward to the practice principles that cite it, so the map needs no separate principle index.

## Nearby terms

- Right resolve, right thought: other renderings of the same Pali factor, sammā saṅkappa; the wiki's card is [Right intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20intention), and nothing is split between the names.
- Intention: the general material on intending, and on the gap between intending and doing, lives in the map below ([The intention-behavior gap](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20intention-behavior%20gap), [Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Implementation%20intentions)); the factor is the path's own discipline of aim, not intending in general.
- Aspiration: the measured family of life goals ([Aspiration index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index), [Extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations)) answers what people aim at; it sits in the map's aim side.
- Renunciation: the first resolve's material; the boundary against the Motivation and Goals dossiers is that those hold the general science of wanting and pursuing, while this page holds the path's discipline of aim.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on right intention

### What the factor is

[Right intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20intention): the second factor, Pali sammā saṅkappa; the resolve toward renunciation, freedom from ill will and harmlessness. Translations differ (right resolve, right intention, right thought), but the three components are the same across them.

[Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path): the umbrella hub; intention is the second of eight factors, grouped with view in the wisdom training.

[The three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings): the grouping that places view and intention together under wisdom, alongside ethics and concentration.

[Karma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karma): the Buddha's definition of karma as intention makes right intention the path's karma-work; what is done deliberately through body, speech or mind sets results going.

[Right view](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20view): the factor that precedes intention; the resolve follows the seeing, and the first sermon's listing puts resolve straight after view and before any speech or action.

The Magga-vibhanga Sutta gives the definition:

> "And what is right resolve? Being resolved on renunciation, on freedom from ill will, on harmlessness: This is called right resolve." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

And the Nibbedhika Sutta makes intention the hinge of action:

> "Intention, I tell you, is kamma. Intending, one does kamma by way of body, speech, & intellect." ([AN 6.63, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an06/an06.063.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### The first resolve: renunciation

The path's word for the first resolve is nekkhamma, going out; the wiki's positive rendering is simplicity.

[The insatiability of craving](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20insatiability%20of%20craving): Schopenhauer's structural claim about desire: wanting does not end with getting, because wanting keeps regenerating itself.

[The craving-to-addiction model](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20craving-to-addiction%20model): craving as a learned trigger-response loop that mindfulness can unwind (Brewer).

[Contentment with sufficiency](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20with%20sufficiency): the Daodejing's teaching on contentment and sufficiency; chapter 44 prices the question against life itself.

[Contentment in station](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Contentment%20in%20station): Epictetus's role-playing counsels from the Discourses.

[Vairagya](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Vairagya): dispassion in Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra, the release of the grip on outcomes once they are seen as impermanent.

[Detachment from outcomes](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Detachment%20from%20outcomes): the Bhagavad Gita's instruction to act from duty while releasing ownership of the results (2.47).

[Nonattachment](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonattachment): the psychological account of release from mental fixations, a flexible relationship with experience.

[Gelassenheit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gelassenheit): Eckhart's releasement, something a person actively cultivates.

The Two Sorts of Thinking discourse sets the standard the renunciation-thought is held to:

> "And as I remained thus heedful, ardent, & resolute, thinking imbued with renunciation arose in me. I discerned that 'Thinking imbued with renunciation has arisen in me; and that leads neither to my own affliction, nor to the affliction of others, nor to the affliction of both. It fosters discernment, promotes lack of vexation, & leads to Unbinding.'" ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### The second resolve: freedom from ill will

The wiki's positive rendering is goodwill.

[Metta (loving-kindness)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Metta%20%28loving-kindness%29): the Buddhist practice of goodwill, first of the four brahmaviharas.

[Four immeasurables](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Four%20immeasurables): the set metta belongs to: loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy and equanimity.

[Benevolence](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Benevolence): Hume's treatment of benevolence in the second Enquiry.

[Agape](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Agape): the Pauline love given without regard to worth or return.

[Enemy-love](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enemy-love): the Gospel's instruction to love the enemy (Matthew 5).

[Enemy as teacher](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Enemy%20as%20teacher): Śāntideva's argument that the adversary provides the occasion for forbearance.

[The free person loves all](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20free%20person%20loves%20all): Spinoza's free person, living by reason, desiring the good for everyone.

[The counter-affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20counter-affect): the early discourses' method of cultivating the opposite when an unskillful state takes hold.

[The expanding circle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20expanding%20circle): Singer's name for the history and task of widening moral concern.

[Realistic conflict theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Realistic%20conflict%20theory) and [Intergroup contact](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intergroup%20contact): the study of where ill will between groups comes from, and the finding that structured contact reduces it.

The training the discourse prescribes for the ill-will-thought is abandonment without negotiation:

> "Whenever thinking imbued with ill will had arisen, I simply abandoned it, dispelled it, wiped it out of existence." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

### The third resolve: harmlessness

The wiki's positive rendering is refusing to add suffering.

[Ahimsa](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ahimsa): the Indic principle of non-harm; the compendium reads it as a starting floor: whatever else a practice does, it begins by doing no injury.

[Nonviolent action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20action): Gene Sharp's theory of power and his documented methods of nonviolent action.

[Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20communication): Rosenberg's process for reducing conflict in everyday interactions.

[Judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm): the Stoic claim that events do not disturb people, the opinions about the events do (Enchiridion 5).

[The inner friend](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20inner%20friend): Hecato's line via Seneca on making peace with oneself.

### How the resolve is trained

The factor is a resolve, and the discourses train it as a discipline of thought, which is what the translation "right thought" is reaching for.

The Buddha's own method, before his awakening, was to sort his thinking:

> "So I made thinking imbued with sensuality, thinking imbued with ill will, & thinking imbued with harmfulness one sort, and thinking imbued with renunciation, thinking imbued with non-ill will, & thinking imbued with harmlessness another sort." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

And the mechanism the discourse names is practice bending the mind:

> "Whatever a monk keeps pursuing with his thinking & pondering, that becomes the inclination of his awareness." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

[The counter-affect](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20counter-affect) (again): pratipakṣā-bhāvanā, the deliberate turning of attention toward what counters an unskillful state.

[Akrasia (weakness of will)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Akrasia%20%28weakness%20of%20will%29): the standing problem that knowing the aim does not secure acting on it.

[The intention-behavior gap](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20intention-behavior%20gap): Sheeran's measurement of how little intentions predict what people then do.

[Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Implementation%20intentions): Gollwitzer's if-then plans, advance decisions that link a situation to a response.

[Morning intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Morning%20intention): the daily setting of one sentence of aim.

[Intentionality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Intentionality): Brentano's revival of the scholastic term for the mind's directedness at its objects.

### What a resolved life aims at

[Purpose](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose): Frankl's account of purpose as the thing that lets a person bear almost any how.

[Purpose-linked practice](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Purpose-linked%20practice): the self-transcendent purpose research on why people keep working at tedious tasks that matter (Yeager and colleagues).

[The bodhisattva vow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20bodhisattva%20vow): the Mahayana commitment to work for the liberation of all beings rather than one's own alone.

[Aspiration index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Aspiration%20index): the instrument measuring what people aim at, intrinsic versus extrinsic.

[Extrinsic aspirations](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Extrinsic%20aspirations): the finding that wealth, fame and image goals correlate with lower well-being even when achieved.

[Self-concordance theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-concordance%20theory): goals that fit developing interests and core values sustain effort and make attaining more rewarding.

[Action over intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20over%20intention): the label for the next factor, right action; the map of this factor ends where the path moves from resolve to act.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Is the resolve an abstention or a cultivation?

The shared question: what does freedom from ill will ask of a mind? The Magga-vibhanga Sutta states the second resolve as an absence, being resolved on freedom from ill will, a turning away. The Karaṇīya Metta Sutta asks for more than absence:

> "As a mother would risk her life to protect her child, her only child, even so should one cultivate a limitless heart with regard to all beings. With good will for the entire cosmos, cultivate a limitless heart: Above, below, & all around, unobstructed, without enmity or hate." ([Sutta Nipāta 1.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.1.08.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

The two positions answer the same question and part on whether the absence suffices or a boundless warmth must be actively grown. What remains unresolved is whether the abstention reading understates the factor or the cultivation reading adds a later ideal to a narrower instruction; the Two Sorts of Thinking discourse sits between them, treating non-ill will as a thought that is cultivated and, when harmfulness has been abandoned, kept.

### Less wanting or better wanting?

The shared question: what should a person's wanting be like? The path's instruction turns thinking away from the objects of wanting:

> "If a monk keeps pursuing thinking imbued with sensuality, abandoning thinking imbued with renunciation, his mind is bent by that thinking imbued with sensuality." ([MN 19, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.019.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

The motivation science keeps the wanting and re-aims it. Ryan and Deci, summarising Kasser and Ryan's aspiration studies:

> "They found, first, that placing strong relative importance on intrinsic aspirations was positively associated with well-being indicators such as self-esteem, self-actualization, and the inverse of depression and anxiety, whereas placing strong relative importance on extrinsic aspirations was negatively related to these well-being indicators." ([Ryan & Deci 2000, Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf)): Scholarly review; cited passage checked.

Both sides agree that some aims impoverish a life. They part on the remedy: a resolve away from wanting's objects, or a re-aiming of wanting at what satisfies needs. What remains unresolved is whether renunciation and re-aiming are two descriptions of one work or two different works; the compendium's own principle on the factor keeps the parallel deliberately loose, noting that the fit-between-goals-and-values finding is about fit, not about renunciation or harmlessness.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the aim's default setting: wanting inherited rather than chosen, ill will rehearsed until it feels like seeing, harm done by habit rather than decision. The adaptive direction is the resolve itself, aims examined and chosen, ill will noticed as a thought that can be abandoned, harmlessness kept as a floor under whatever else is undertaken.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [K156 — Morning intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K156%20%E2%80%94%20Morning%20intention): write or speak one sentence of intention for the day: what you want to stand for, what you want to practice, what matters today.

2. [K149 — Values clarification](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K149%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20clarification): what you want your life to stand for, written in three to five domains, values as directions rather than goals.

3. [K150 — Values card-sort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K150%20%E2%80%94%20Values%20card-sort): a deck of values sorted by importance until the top ten stand out.

4. [K153 — Implementation intentions](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K153%20%E2%80%94%20Implementation%20intentions): the if-then plan that carries an aim into situations and past decision fatigue.

5. [K154 — Mental contrasting (WOOP)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K154%20%E2%80%94%20Mental%20contrasting%20%28WOOP%29): wish, best outcome, main obstacle, plan; the contrast that links aspiration to resistance.

6. [K155 — Enough lines](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K155%20%E2%80%94%20Enough%20lines): sufficiency defined in advance for each domain, while the judgment is still cold.

7. [K161 — Loving-kindness meditation](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K161%20%E2%80%94%20Loving-kindness%20meditation): phrases of goodwill extended from yourself to a difficult person and all beings.

8. [K247 — Motivational interviewing (self-directed)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K247%20%E2%80%94%20Motivational%20interviewing%20%28self-directed%29): the ruler question asked of a change you are ambivalent about.

9. [K227 — Time-affluence audit](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K227%20%E2%80%94%20Time-affluence%20audit): a periodic review of whether you are trading time for money in ways that diminish well-being.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the morning sentence, or the enough line, or the goodwill phrase has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the resolve and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The compendium's stories that best carry this theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### The three resolves in narrative

[ST376 — Meghiya Finds the Right Grove and the Wrong Thoughts](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST376%20%E2%80%94%20Meghiya%20Finds%20the%20Right%20Grove%20and%20the%20Wrong%20Thoughts): Meghiya gets the grove he wanted and, alone under the tree, meets thoughts of sensuality, ill will and harm; wanting the place to practise was not the same as knowing what to cultivate.

[ST352 — The Cowherd After the Harvest](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST352%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Cowherd%20After%20the%20Harvest): the Buddha's own two pictures of watching the mind, the cowherd guarding ripening crops with a stick, then resting in the shade simply mindful of the cows.

### Choosing what to aim at

[ST301 — The Aspiration Index](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST301%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Aspiration%20Index): Kasser and Ryan measure what people value and find the relative priority of money, image and fame, controlling for actual income, predicts worse outcomes.

### Testing the aim

[ST066 — The Kālāmas Test It](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST066%20%E2%80%94%20The%20K%C4%81l%C4%81mas%20Test%20It): teachers contradict each other in the Kālāmas' town, and the Buddha tells them to know for themselves which undertakings lead to harm and which to welfare.

### Ill will and its costs

[ST085 — The Quarrel at Kosambī](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST085%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Quarrel%20at%20Kosamb%C4%AB): monks at Kosambī harden into factions, the Buddha's two reconciliation attempts fail, and the supporters withdraw alms until the factions, starved and chastened, come to him.

### Harmlessness at the far end

[ST086 — The Bodhisattva and the Tigress](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST086%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Bodhisattva%20and%20the%20Tigress): the bodhisattva finds a tigress too weak from hunger to feed her cubs and lies down where she can reach him; the tradition marks the far end of the spectrum on purpose, to orient the ordinary cases.

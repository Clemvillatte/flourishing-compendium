India, Pali Canon · Saṃyutta Nikāya 35.28, Thanissaro Bhikkhu translation; the audience a thousand monks the carrier introduces as former fire-worshippers

The setting is Gaya Head: "the Blessed One was staying near Gayā at Gayā Head with 1,000 monks. There he addressed the monks" ([SN 35:28, Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html)). The page's introduction identifies that audience as monks "who formerly worshiped fire" ([dhammatalks.org](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html)). To them the Buddha gives a one-line diagnosis instead of a rebuke: "Monks, the All is aflame" ([SN 35:28, Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html)).

The catalogue that follows moves inward through their own act of sensing. The eye is aflame, forms are aflame, eye-consciousness is aflame, eye-contact is aflame, and whatever arises dependent on that contact, "experienced as pleasure, pain or neither-pleasure-nor-pain," is aflame too. Then comes the sentence that names the fuel:

> Aflame with what? Aflame with the fire of passion, the fire of aversion, the fire of delusion. Aflame, I tell you, with birth, aging & death, with sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs. ([SN 35:28, Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html))

The move lands on this audience because it takes their own object and turns it into the symptom. Nothing is said against fire-worship; the fire turns out to be real, and to be running through the whole field of experience rather than sitting on an altar. The three fuels the sutta names are the three roots — passion, aversion, delusion — delivered not as a list of vices but as the thing already burning in seeing, hearing and feeling. The compendium reads this as B016's diagnostic framing in narrative form: the roots are located in experience itself, which is why identifying them is a step in treatment rather than a moral verdict.

The outcome is told in one line. "Gratified, the monks delighted in the Blessed One's words. And while this explanation was being given, the minds of the 1,000 monks, through lack of clinging/sustenance, were released from effluents" ([SN 35:28, Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html)). The release arrives inside the sermon, and its mechanism is the fire running out of fuel: minds released through lack of clinging, not through the fires being fought.

What the page does not give is the principle's second half — the practice of catching which root is active in a particular moment. The sermon names the three together, once, and moves on. That naming step is the reader's to add.

**Principles:**
- [[B016 — Know the three root poisons|B016 Know the three root poisons]] — the sermon names passion, aversion and delusion as the fires burning all experience and locates them in the senses rather than in bad character, carrying the principle's three-root diagnosis and its non-moral use. ([SN 35:28, Thanissaro](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html))

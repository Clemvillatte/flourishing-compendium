Historical/biographical narrative, cross-cultural · Greece, 4th century BCE · [Diogenes Laertius, Lives of the Eminent Philosophers, Book VI (Loeb 1925), via LacusCurtius](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/6/Diogenes*.html)

Diogenes the Cynic lives in a storage jar in Athens, owning almost nothing. Seeing a child drink from cupped hands, he throws away his own cup, saying a child has beaten him in the economy of living. The anecdote is recorded by Diogenes Laertius, Book VI, chapter 2, among the stock stories of his life.

In the Loeb translation: "One day, observing a child drinking out of his hands, he cast away the cup from his wallet with the words, 'A child has beaten me in plainness of living.' He also threw away his bowl when in like manner he saw a child who had broken his plate taking up his lentils with the hollow part of a morsel of bread" ([Diogenes Laertius VI.2, Loeb 1925](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Diogenes_Laertius/Lives_of_the_Eminent_Philosophers/6/Diogenes*.html)).

My reading: the gesture is deliberately comic rather than ascetic. He is not renouncing comfort for its own sake; he is testing each possession against actual use — the bowl goes the same way as the cup once the bread does the bowl's work.

Parable · Warring States China · Zhuangzi, Enjoyment in Untroubled Ease, section 6 · quotations from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

Huizi's complaint: "The king of Wei sent me some seeds of a large calabash, which I sowed. The fruit, when fully grown, could contain five piculs (of anything)" ([Zhuangzi I §6, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). As a water vessel it was too heavy for one man to lift, and as drinking vessels it failed: "I cut it in two to make the parts into drinking vessels; but the dried shells were too wide and unstable and would not hold (the liquor); nothing but large useless things! Because of their uselessness I knocked them to pieces" ([Zhuangzi I §6, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Zhuangzi's reply opens with the verdict — "You were indeed stupid, my master, in the use of what was large" ([Zhuangzi I §6, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)) — and then the salve story: a family's chapped-hand remedy sold to a stranger who became a Wu fleet commander with it, while the family went on bleaching silk. Of it he says: "The keeping the hands from getting chapped was the same in both cases; but in the one case it led to the investiture (of the possessor of the salve), and in the other it had only enabled its owners to continue their bleaching. The difference of result was owing to the different use made of the art" ([Zhuangzi I §6, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The retort returns to the gourd: "Now you, Sir, had calabashes large enough to hold five piculs; why did you not think of making large bottle-gourds of them, by means of which you could have floated over rivers and lakes, instead of giving yourself the sorrow of finding that they were useless for holding anything. Your mind, my master, would seem to have been closed against all intelligence!" ([Zhuangzi I §6, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

My reading: same object, two framings. The story is about the poverty of fixed use — the way wanting something to be one thing blinds you to everything it could be. The smashed gourd is the cost of that blindness, and the raft is what the same gourd becomes when the question changes.

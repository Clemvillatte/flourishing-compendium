Canonical verse narrative · from the Padhana Sutta, Sn 3.2, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation (Access to Insight).

The discourse opens with the striving itself: "To me — resolute in exertion near the river Nerañjara, making a great effort, doing jhana to attain rest from the yoke" ([Sn 3.2, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.3.02.than.html)). Mara comes to petition the striver to live, and the reply the sutta gives the Blessed One is a roll call: "Sensual passions are your first army. Your second is called Discontent. Your third is Hunger & Thirst. Your fourth is called Craving. Fifth is Sloth & Drowsiness. Sixth is called Terror. Your seventh is Uncertainty. Hypocrisy & Stubbornness, your eighth" ([Sn 3.2, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.3.02.than.html)). The passage closes with the armies as a single force: "That, Namuci, is your army, the Dark One's commando force. A coward can't defeat it, but one having defeated it gains bliss" ([Sn 3.2, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.3.02.than.html)).

The connection with B034 is the naming done in the heat of the meeting. The hindrances the principle page teaches a sitter to know one by one arrive here as an army drawn up in order, and the reply does not argue with any of them; it lists them, names the whole force, and states the resolve to "smash with discernment — as an unfired pot with a stone" ([Sn 3.2, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.3.02.than.html)). I read the list as the practice in its oldest register: each force is named as it arrives, and the naming is itself the standing ground.

Not carried: the page's seated-practice instructions for working with a hindrance as it arises; the sutta keeps to the battlefield image and the list.

**Principles:**
- [[B034 — Know the five hindrances]]: the Padhana Sutta's armies named in sequence — desire, aversion's kin, sloth, terror, uncertainty — with the hindrances met as a listed force rather than a fog.

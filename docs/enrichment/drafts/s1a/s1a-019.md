Buddhist commentarial narrative · Dhammapada Atthakatha, Ciñcamanavikavatthu, trans. Burlingame, Buddhist Legends XIII.9

A woman pretending to be heavily pregnant walks into the Buddha's teaching hall at Savatthi and accuses him before the crowd: he made her pregnant and provides nothing for her confinement. He stops the discourse and, roaring like a lion, gives her one sentence: whether her words are true or false is known only to her and to him. The deception then unmasks itself before everyone. ([Buddhist Legends XIII.9](https://ancient-buddhist-texts.net/English-Texts/Buddhist-Legends/13-09.htm))

The frame is reputation weather: as the Buddha's gain and honor grow, the heretics lose theirs, "even as fireflies lose their brilliance before the coming of the sun", and they plot in secret. A wandering nun named Ciñcā Māṇavikā takes the job. For weeks she is seen walking toward Jetavana at dusk and back at dawn; asked where she has spent the night, she answers, "at Jetavana alone with the monk Gotama in the Perfumed Chamber". After three or four months she binds her belly and goes about saying, "I have conceived a child by the monk Gotama." ([Buddhist Legends XIII.9](https://ancient-buddhist-texts.net/English-Texts/Buddhist-Legends/13-09.htm))

When eight or nine months have passed she fastens a disk of wood beneath her cloak, pounds her hands and feet with an ox's jawbone to swell them, and stands before the Buddha as he preaches: "Mighty monk, mighty is the throng to which you preach the Law; sweet is your voice, soft are your lips. Nevertheless you are the one by whom I have conceived a child, and the time of my delivery is near at hand." The Tathāgata stops his discourse and, roaring like a lion, answers, "Sister, as to whether what you have said be true or false, that is something which only you and I know." ([Buddhist Legends XIII.9](https://ancient-buddhist-texts.net/English-Texts/Buddhist-Legends/13-09.htm))

The machinery then turns. Sakka's seat grows warm; he sets out with four deities, the four become little mice, and "With one bite of their teeth these little mice severed the cords with which the disk of wood was fastened to the belly of the woman"; the disk falls and cuts off her toes, the crowd spits on her and drives her from Jetavana, and the earth opens and swallows her into Avīci hell. The next day the monks discuss her ruin; the Buddha's recorded teaching is about lying, not composure: "If a man break one commandment, if he speak lies,
If he abandon the next world, there is no evil deed he will not commit." ([Buddhist Legends XIII.9](https://ancient-buddhist-texts.net/English-Texts/Buddhist-Legends/13-09.htm))

This page compares the Buddha's reply with B054's response to blame. The narrator describes him as "roaring like a lion"; the comparison does not depend on a quiet tone. His brief reply offers one illustration of answering an accusation without an extended quarrel. The story does not describe his inner state or his response to praise. Its explicit concluding lesson concerns falsehood. ([Buddhist Legends XIII.9](https://ancient-buddhist-texts.net/English-Texts/Buddhist-Legends/13-09.htm))

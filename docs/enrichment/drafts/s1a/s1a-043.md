Warring States China · Zhuangzi, Book VI, section 9 · a canonical parable, James Legge translation

In a three-visit exchange inside the Zhuangzi, the student Yan Hui reports progress to Confucius as forgetting: first the moral program, then the ritual program, then everything. Twice the teacher says good, but not enough; on the third report he changes countenance and ends by asking to follow the student. The dialogue is the source of the practice name zuowang, sitting and forgetting, and it carries the whole arc of D047 in one page. ([Zhuangzi VI.9, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The exchange, as Legge has it ([Zhuangzi VI.9](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)):

> Yan Hui said, 'I am making progress.' Zhongni replied, 'What do you mean?' 'I have ceased to think of benevolence and righteousness,' was the reply. 'Very well; but that is not enough.' Another day, Hui again saw Zhongni, and said, 'I am making progress.' 'What do you mean?' 'I have lost all thought of ceremonies and music.' 'Very well, but that is not enough.' A third day, Hui again saw (the Master), and said, 'I am making progress.' 'What do you mean?' 'I sit and forget everything.' Zhongni changed countenance, and said, 'What do you mean by saying that you sit and forget (everything)?' Yan Hui replied, 'My connexion with the body and its parts is dissolved; my perceptive organs are discarded. Thus leaving my material form, and bidding farewell to my knowledge, I am become one with the Great Pervader. This I call sitting and forgetting all things.' Zhongni said, 'One (with that Pervader), you are free from all likings; so transformed, you are become impermanent. You have, indeed, become superior to me! I must ask leave to follow in your steps.'

My reading: the parable stages zuowang as graded subtraction rather than as an added state. The first two reports remove what a student could be praised for: benevolence and righteousness, then the ceremonies and music that order a life. The teacher certifies each step and keeps the bar up. The third report removes what no program can run without: the sense of the body and its parts, and knowledge itself. The teacher's reply names what the state is worth, freedom from likings and impermanence, and then does the thing a Confucian master is least expected to do: he ranks his student above himself and asks to follow. The Zhuangzi often casts Confucius as the certifying authority for Daoist advances, and the reversal is part of the design: the one who forgot the program outranks the one who masters it. Read alongside D047, the story carries the set-up, the graded operation, the turn and the consequence; it gives no instruction on how to sit, and names no safeguards.

Biographical account · Samuel Pepys (1633-1703), diarist, via his diary entry for Sunday 2 September 1666 (Project Gutenberg) and the Wikipedia article on the fire.

Jane called the household up about three in the morning to tell of a great fire in the City; Pepys "rose and slipped on my nightgowne, and went to her window, and thought it to be on the backside of Marke-lane at the farthest; but, being unused to such fires as followed, I thought it far enough off; and so went to bed again and to sleep" ([Pepys, 2 Sept. 1666, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)). Rising at seven, he "saw the fire not so much as it was and further off" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)); by Jane's next report, "above 300 houses have been burned down to-night by the fire we saw" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)). The fire had begun after midnight at Thomas Farriner's bakery in Pudding Lane ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London)). From the Tower he learned where it began, then went down to the water-side and got a boat through the bridge; it was there, from the river, that he stayed an hour and "seen the fire: rage every way, and nobody, to my sight, endeavouring to quench it, but to remove their goods, and leave all to the fire" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)). He went on to White Hall, was called for, and "did tell the King and Duke of Yorke what I saw, and that unless his Majesty did command houses to be pulled down nothing could stop the fire" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)).

The connection with D060 is my retrospective reading, and the draft keeps it separate from the diary's own words. What the entry records is a man judging a fire far enough off and going back to bed, unused himself, as the entry says, to such fires as followed, and the same man telling the King that only pulling down houses would stop it ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)). The computed destruction ran to "13,200-13,500 houses, 86 or 87 parish churches", St Paul's Cathedral and the western city gates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London)), and what stopped it in the end was the east wind dropping and the Tower garrison's gunpowder firebreaks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London)). The draft makes no claim about what earlier action would have achieved; what it carries is the principle's timing seen from behind, the great that a sleepy observer judged far enough off. Whether this inversion earns its place on D060 is routed to the supervisor; the page's positive instruction, doing the great while it is small, is not carried here.

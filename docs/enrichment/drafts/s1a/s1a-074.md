Biographical account · Samuel Pepys (1633-1703), diarist, via his diary entry for Sunday 2 September 1666 (Project Gutenberg) and the Wikipedia article on the fire.

The great was small first, and a man looked at it and went back to bed. Jane called the household up about three in the morning to tell of a great fire in the City; Pepys "rose and slipped on my nightgowne, and went to her window, and thought it to be on the backside of Marke-lane at the farthest; but, being unused to such fires as followed, I thought it far enough off; and so went to bed again and to sleep" ([Pepys, 2 Sept. 1666, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)). Rising at seven, he "saw the fire not so much as it was and further off" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)); by Jane's next report, "above 300 houses have been burned down to-night by the fire we saw" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)). The fire had begun after midnight at Thomas Farriner's bakery in Pudding Lane ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London)). From the Tower that day Pepys watched it "rage every way, and nobody, to my sight, endeavouring to quench it, but to remove their goods, and leave all to the fire" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)), and by evening he was telling the King "that unless his Majesty did command houses to be pulled down nothing could stop the fire" ([Pepys, Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/4200/pg4200.txt)).

The connection with D060 is the cautionary inversion, and the draft says so rather than pretending otherwise. The principle's instruction is to do the great while it is small, because the work is cheapest then; the story shows the same arithmetic from the other side. What stopped the fire in the end was the strong east wind dropping and the Tower garrison blowing up houses on a large scale for firebreaks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London)); the computed destruction ran to "13,200-13,500 houses, 86 or 87 parish churches", St Paul's Cathedral and the western city gates ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_London)). The demolished houses of the second day were the same houses that pulling down one or two of, early, might have saved.

- [[D060 — Do the great while it is small]]: the fire that was judged far enough off at three in the morning, carried as the cautionary inversion of the principle's timing, with the inversion named on the page.

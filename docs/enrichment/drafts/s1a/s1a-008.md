*India, 5th century BCE · the Titthayatana Sutta, Udana 6.4, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation*

The Buddha tells of a king in Sāvatthī who orders a man to "gather together all the people in Sāvatthī who have been blind from birth." The man rounds them up, and the king gives an instruction that sets the story in motion: "Very well then, I say, show the blind people an elephant." To some of them the man shows the elephant's head, to some the ear, the tusk, the trunk, the body, the foot, the hindquarters, the tail, and to some the tuft at the end of the tail, telling each group, "This, blind people, is what an elephant is like."

Then the king asks them what an elephant is, and each group answers from what its hands actually held:

> The blind people who had been shown the elephant's head said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like a jar."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's ear said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like a winnowing basket."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's tusk said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like plowshare."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's trunk said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like the pole of a plow."
> Those who had been shown the elephant's body said, "The elephant, your majesty, is just like a granary."

And the rest answer from the foot, the hindquarters, the tail, the tuft: a post, a mortar, a pestle, a broom. Every report is true. Every report is of a part. And then comes the sentence that makes the parable a story:

> Saying, "The elephant is like this, it's not like that. The elephant's not like that, it's like this," they struck one another with their fists. That gratified the king.

The Buddha's coda names the quarrel as the point: the wanderers of other sects are "blind & eyeless," and "they keep on arguing, quarreling, & disputing, wounding one another with weapons of the mouth, saying, 'The Dhamma is like this, it's not like that.'" His verse ends with the diagnosis in five words: "people seeing one side."

The compendium reads the parable as D080 told as a street scene. The principle dissolves rigid right and wrong: the qiwu insight is not that the blind men are wrong, each felt something real, but that a partial truth gripped as the whole turns into a fist. Right and wrong here are functions of where the hands landed; the elephant is what it is regardless of the quarrel, and the king, who knows what he has shown each group, is the only one in the story with nothing to prove. What the story does not carry is the deeper mystical facet of the principle, the clarity beyond views entirely; the parable stays at the level where views are compared, and that needs its own stories.

**Principles:**
- [[D080 — Dissolve rigid right and wrong (qiwu)|D80 Dissolve rigid right and wrong (qiwu)]] — each report is true, the fight comes from gripping a part as the whole.

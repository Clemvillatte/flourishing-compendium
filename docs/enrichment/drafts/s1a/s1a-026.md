Biographical account with primary text · Thich Nhat Hanh and Jim Forest, The Miracle of Mindfulness (the dishwashing and tangerine passages); Forest's later retelling preserved by others

Jim Forest, an American Catholic peace activist, helped bring the book to English readers ([Jim Forest memorial page](https://jfi.ssu.ca/jim-forest)). The book's teaching scene is a kitchen: one evening Forest offered to wash the dishes, and Nhat Hanh answered: "There are two ways to wash the dishes. The first is to wash the dishes in order to have clean dishes and the second is to wash the dishes in order to wash the dishes" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality,%20Meditation,%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)).

The warning inside the second way is about the first: washing while "hurrying to get the dishes out of the way as if they were a nuisance" means "we are not alive during the time we are washing the dishes. In fact we are completely incapable of realizing the miracle of life while standing at the sink" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality,%20Meditation,%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)). The failure travels to the tea afterward: "we are incapable of actually living one minute of life"; Nhat Hanh's own report reads the other way: "The fact that I am standing there and washing these bowls is a wondrous reality" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality,%20Meditation,%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)).

The tangerine gave the lesson a second form. Traveling together in the United States, they shared a tangerine under a tree; Forest, absorbed in plans, kept a section ready before chewing the one in his mouth, and "He was hardly aware he was eating a tangerine". Nhat Hanh's correction was one sentence: "You ought to eat the tangerine section you've already taken" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality,%20Meditation,%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)). Forest lowered his hand, attended to the section already in his mouth, and chewed it before taking another; if he had been eating anything, Nhat Hanh says, "he was \"eating\" his future plans" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality,%20Meditation,%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)).

Forest later served a prison year, 1969-70, for antiwar actions ([Jim Forest, "When America Met Thich Nhat Hanh"](https://jimandnancyforest.com/2021/12/when-america-met-thich-nhat-hanh/)), and Nhat Hanh sent a short letter: "Do you remember the tangerine we shared when we were together? Your being there is like the tangerine. Eat it and be one with it. Tomorrow it will be no more" ([The Miracle of Mindfulness](https://truthbrary.mpaq.org/BOOKS/Spirituality,%20Meditation,%20Self-finding%20%28Books%29/Zen%20Buddhism%20-%20Thich%20Nhat%20Hanh/The_Miracle_of_Mindfulness__An_Introductio_-_Thich_Nhat_Hanh.pdf)). A memorial contributor recalls Forest retelling the scene and correcting the book: the scene "isn't what happened", he said, and what helped was an instruction the book omits, "Wash each dish like it is the infant Christ", after which "I volunteered to do the dishes the rest of the week" ([Jim Forest memorial page](https://jfi.ssu.ca/jim-forest)).

Read beside B176, the story is the principle's smallest units made flesh: a sink, one section of tangerine, a prison year. The miracle is not elsewhere; it is what is in your mouth and hands, and the test is whether you are there for it. ([B176](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/B176%20%E2%80%94%20Treat%20the%20present%20moment%20as%20the%20miracle.md))

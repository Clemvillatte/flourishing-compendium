Traditional Buddhist commentarial narrative · the Rohini water dispute, from the Dhammapada Commentary in E. W. Burlingame's translation (Access to Insight), attributed there to Dhammapada verses 197-199.

The Sakiyas and the Koliyas confined the waters of the river Rohini "by a single dam between the city of Kapilavatthu and the city of Koliya and cultivated the fields on both sides of the river" ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)). In the month Jetthamula the crops drooped, and the quarrel over a single watering grew until "one arose and struck another a blow", a general fight ensuing with aspersions on the origins of the two royal families ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)). Surveying the world at dawn, the Teacher thought, "If I refrain from going to them, these men will destroy each other. It is clearly my duty to go to them", and flew alone to the spot, seating himself cross-legged in the air over the middle of the river; his kinsmen threw away their weapons and did reverence ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)).

The connection with D083 is the question chain that re-prices the quarrel. The Buddha works down the ranks asking what the quarrel is about, and the slave-laborers answer, "The quarrel is about water, reverend sir" ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)). Then the questions turn on the price of things: "How much is water worth, great king?" - "Very little, reverend sir." - "How much are Khattiyas worth, great king?" - "Khattiyas are beyond price, reverend sir." - "It is not fitting that because of a little water you should destroy khattiyas who are beyond price" ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)). The blow and the quarrel had been priced in water; I read the question chain as changing the comparison from the water allocation to the lives at risk, setting the same facts in the currency of lives, and the kings "were silent", after which the Teacher addressed them: "Were I not here present today, you would set flowing a river of blood" ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)).

Not carried: the page's Zhuangzi analysis, asking which terms can change while the total stays fixed; the commentary's reframe works by re-pricing, and the analysis stays on the principle page. The story's own sequence is also kept exact: the weapons were set aside when the Teacher seated himself over the river, before the questions, and the consequence the story gives after the questions is the kings' silence; the draft claims neither that the re-pricing caused the disarmament nor that the waters came to be shared ([Burlingame](https://accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/burlingame/wheel324.html)).

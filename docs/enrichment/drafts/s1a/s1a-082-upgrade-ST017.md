Parable · Warring States China · Zhuangzi, The Seal of Virtue Complete and The Normal Course for Rulers and Kings · quotations from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

The still-water image comes in the Wang Tai dialogue: "Men do not look into running water as a mirror, but into still water - it is only the still water that can arrest them all, and keep them (in the contemplation of their real selves)" ([Zhuangzi, Seal of Virtue Complete, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). And the mirror itself is named in the closing chapters: "A pure vacancy (of all purpose) is what characterises him. When the perfect man employs his mind, it is a mirror. It conducts nothing and anticipates nothing; it responds to (what is before it), but does not retain it. Thus he is able to deal successfully with all things, and injures none" ([Zhuangzi, Normal Course for Rulers and Kings §6, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: I take the two images together as a description of attention. Most minds are not mirrors; they are projectors, running loops of commentary over whatever appears. The passage's mirror "responds to (what is before it)" — so stillness is not blankness; it is responding to what is actually here rather than to the playback, and not retaining the playback after it passes.

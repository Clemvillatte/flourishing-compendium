*Historical/biographical narrative · United States, 1730s · [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin); quotations from [Franklin, Autobiography, ed. Pine (Project Gutenberg #20203)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)*

In his autobiography, Franklin describes the project plainly: "It was about this time I conceived the bold and arduous project of arriving at moral perfection" ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)). He identified thirteen virtues, gave each a short precept, and made a little book: "I made a little book, in which I allotted a page for each of the virtues", ruled so that he "might mark, by a little black spot, every fault I found upon examination to have been committed respecting that virtue upon that day" ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)). He focused on one virtue at a time, "a week's strict attention to each of the virtues successively", and the book cycled so that "I could go thro' a course compleat in thirteen weeks, and four courses in a year" ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)).

The first result corrected his self-image: "I was surpris'd to find myself so much fuller of faults than I had imagined; but I had the satisfaction of seeing them diminish" ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)). He never became perfect, and the book's punchline is his own conclusion that the practice, not the perfection, was the gain: "tho' I never arrived at the perfection I had been so ambitious of obtaining, but fell far short of it, yet I was, by the endeavour, a better and a happier man than I otherwise should have been if I had not attempted it" ([Autobiography](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/20203/pg20203.txt)).

Franklin's chart is the first self-tracking protocol on record, three centuries before apps. Its interest is structural: he discovered in practice what Aristotle argued in theory, that a disposition is built by attending to its instances one at a time, and that the tracking itself is the habituation.

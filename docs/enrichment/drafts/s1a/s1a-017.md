Historical biography · Sima Qian, Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian), ch. 92, completed c. 94 BCE

A poor young man who carries a sword is stopped in the market of Huaiyin and given a public choice: stab me, or crawl beneath me. He looks at his challenger, bends down, and crawls while the whole market laughs. Years later, as King of Chu, he summons that same challenger and gives him an officer's post. The biography of Han Xin, the general who decided the war between Liu Bang and Xiang Yu, opens with this humiliation and closes it without a blow. ([Shiji ch. 92, Chinese Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

The biography opens with what he was before he was anything: "始爲布衣時，貧無行，不得推擇爲吏，又不能治生商賈，常從人寄食飲，人多厭之者" — as a commoner he was poor and without standing, could not be recommended for office, and ate at other people's tables until most were tired of him. A woman washing silk by the city wall fed him for weeks and scolded him for promising repayment. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

Then comes the challenge, from a youth of the butcher's quarter: "信能死，刺我；不能死，出我袴下。於是信孰視之，俛出袴下，蒲伏。一市人皆笑信，以爲怯。" In translation: if you dare to die, stab me; if not, crawl out beneath my crotch. Han Xin studied him, bowed down, crawled, and lay prone; the whole market laughed at him for a coward. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

The biography carries him onward through the fall of Qin: unknown under Xiang Liang, a guard under Xiang Yu whose advice went unused, nearly executed in Liu Bang's camp, then rising to the generalship that broke Zhao at Jingxing and trapped Xiang Yu at Gaixia. In the fifth year of Han, "漢五年正月，徙齊王信爲楚王，都下邳" — Han Xin was moved from King of Qi to King of Chu. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

Back in his own country he settles the old accounts in order. The washerwoman receives a thousand gold; the pavilion head who had wearied of feeding him receives a hundred coins and a rebuke; and then this: "召辱己之少年令出胯下者以爲楚中尉。告諸將相曰：『此壯士也。方辱我時，我寧不能殺之邪？殺之無名，故忍而就於此。』" — he summoned the youth who had made him crawl out beneath his legs and made him a commandant in Chu, telling his generals and ministers: this is a brave man; when he humiliated me, could I not have killed him? To kill him would have gained no name; so I endured, and came to this. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

For D068 the story carries the whole arc in one life. The set-up is a crowd demanding that strength prove itself on the spot; the operation is a man measuring the street fight against the years it would cost; the consequence is a future kept intact, closed by appointment rather than revenge. The market's verdict and Sima Qian's verdict sit side by side, and the later passage shows which held. The English renderings are this page's own translations of the quoted classical Chinese; the Chinese text is the primary source. ([Shiji ch. 92](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E5%8F%B2%E8%A8%98/%E6%B7%AE%E9%99%B0%E4%BE%AF%E5%88%97%E4%BC%A0))

**Principles:**
- [[D068 — Yield in conflict to remain whole|D068 Yield in conflict to remain whole]]: Han Xin gives up the street fight and keeps the life that carries his purpose; the biography's closing appointment shows the wholeness was real, not rhetorical.

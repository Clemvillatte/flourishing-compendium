Warring States China · Heguanzi, Shixian chapter · retold in Joseph Needham's Science and Civilization in China

A Warring States dialogue explains why the best physician can be the one nobody has heard of. In the story, relayed by Phang Hsüan to a king, Duke Wên of Wei asks the great physician Pien Chhüeh (Bian Que) which of his three brothers is the best doctor. The answer inverts the question: "The eldest is the best, then the second, and I am the least worthy of the three" ([Heguanzi, Shixian, trans. Needham](https://www.dynamicpoints.com/p/bian-ques-older-brothers-and-treating-illness-before-it-occurs)). The duke asks to hear how that can be, since Bian Que is the famous one. The explanation, in Needham's translation:

> "My eldest brother, in dealing with diseases, is attentive to the spirit (shên). Before [any symptoms] have formed, he has already got rid of it. Thus his fame has never reached beyond our own clan. My next brother treats disease when its signs are most subtle, so his name is unknown beyond our own village. As for myself, I use stone needles on the blood vessels, prescribe strong drugs, and fortify the skin and the flesh. Thus my name has become known among all the feudal lords." ([Heguanzi, Shixian, trans. Needham](https://www.dynamicpoints.com/p/bian-ques-older-brothers-and-treating-illness-before-it-occurs))

The three brothers mark three points on one timeline. The eldest treats the illness before it has any form at all; the second treats it at its first faint signs; Bian Que treats it after it has arrived in the flesh, with needles and strong drugs. The dramatic rescue at the end of that timeline is what earns the name that travels to the feudal lords. The work that prevents the illness never becomes a story, because there is nothing to tell: the ailment was removed before it existed, and the family who never fell ill has no tale of rescue.

The compendium's reading: the eldest brother is D061 in practice, acting before the thing arises. The Daodejing's own reasoning is that a thing is easiest to handle before it shows any sign of itself, and the awkward part is that the early action looks like effort spent on nothing. The story adds the social cost: prevention earns no reputation, because the prevented event leaves no evidence. A person practising early action should expect the fame to run the wrong way, and keep acting early anyway.

Zhuangzi parable · Warring States China · [Zhuangzi, The Full Understanding of Life (ch. 19)](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/the-full-understanding-of-life) · quotation from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

Woodcarver Qing's bell-stands are so beautiful people assume they are the work of spirits. His preparation is mostly forgetting: he fasts to still his mind, and by the third day he has forgotten reward; by the fifth, praise; by the seventh, that he has four limbs and a body. Only then does he enter the forest; when he sees the right tree, the stand is already there.

In Legge's translation: "Qing, the Worker in Rottlera wood, carved a bell-stand, and when it was completed, all who saw it were astonished as if it were the work of spirits. The marquis of Lu went to see it, and asked by what art he had succeeded in producing it. 'Your subject is but a mechanic,' was the reply; 'what art should I be possessed of? Nevertheless, there is one thing (which I will mention). When your servant had undertaken to make the bell-stand, I did not venture to waste any of my power, and felt it necessary to fast in order to compose my mind. After fasting for three days, I did not presume to think of any congratulation, reward, rank, or emolument (which I might obtain by the execution of my task); after fasting five days, I did not presume to think of the condemnation or commendation (which it would produce), or of the skill or want of skill (which it might display). At the end of the seven days, I had forgotten all about myself - my four limbs and my whole person. By this time the thought of your Grace's court (for which I was to make the thing) had passed away; everything that could divert my mind from exclusive devotion to the exercise of my skill had disappeared. Then I went into the forest, and looked at the natural forms of the trees. When I saw one of a perfect form, then the figure of the bell-stand rose up to my view, and I applied my hand to the work. Had I not met with such a tree, I must have abandoned the object; but my Heavengiven faculty and the Heaven-given qualities of the wood were concentrated on it. So it was that my spirit was thus engaged in the production of the bell-stand.'" ([Zhuangzi, The Full Understanding of Life, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the skill is not the carving. The carving is the last step — "I applied my hand to the work" comes only after the fast and the forest. The work is the long subtraction: reward by the third day, praise by the fifth, himself by the seventh, gone before the first cut.

In October 1802, writing from Heiligenstadt, Beethoven described his hearing loss in a letter to his brothers ([Beethoven's Letters, trans. Lady Wallace](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13065/13065-h/13065-h.htm)). The letter was written to be read after his death, with instructions that his physician describe his malady and add these pages to it ([Beethoven's Letters](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13065/13065-h/13065-h.htm)).

He described his illness, in the Wallace translation: "six years ago I was attacked by an incurable malady, aggravated by unskilful physicians, deluded from year to year, too, by the hope of relief, and at length forced to the conviction of a lasting affliction (the cure of which may go on for years, and perhaps after all prove impracticable)" ([Beethoven's Letters](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13065/13065-h/13065-h.htm)). The difficulty was experienced; the future course remained uncertain. He described the humiliation that stung the most: "what humiliation when any one beside me heard a flute in the far distance, while I heard nothing, or when others heard a shepherd singing, and I still heard nothing!" ([Beethoven's Letters](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13065/13065-h/13065-h.htm)). And he described the thing the fear had pushed him toward, plainly: "Such things brought me to the verge of desperation, and wellnigh caused me to put an end to my life. Art! art alone, deterred me. Ah! how could I possibly quit the world before bringing forth all that I felt it was my vocation to produce?" ([Beethoven's Letters](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13065/13065-h/13065-h.htm))

A year earlier he had put the defiance in writing to his friend Wegeler: "i want to reach into the throat of fate, it shall certainly not bend me down completely -" ([Beethoven-Haus Bonn](https://www.beethoven.de/en/g/schicksal)). Later in the Heiligenstadt letter, he names a course he can still choose: "It is decreed that I must now choose Patience for my guide! This I have done." ([Beethoven's Letters](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/13065/13065-h/13065-h.htm))

### Action

Write down what has happened, what you predict, and what you still need to find out. Note the evidence for each prediction, then identify any practical preparation you can make.

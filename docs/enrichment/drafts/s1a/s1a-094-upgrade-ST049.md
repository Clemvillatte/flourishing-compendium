Zhuangzi parable · Warring States China · quotations from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

Ziqi of the Southern Gate sits lost in himself, his body like dead wood. His student asks what happened. He replies that he just lost himself. Then he unfolds the image: the pipes of earth are the hollows and cavities of the mountains; the pipes of man are the bamboo; the pipes of heaven are the wind blowing through them all, and each hollow sounding its own note. Who is it, he asks, that stirs them all?

In Legge's translation, the opening: "Nan-Guo Zi-Qi was seated, leaning forward on his stool. He was looking up to heaven and breathed gently, seeming to be in a trance, and to have lost all consciousness of any companion" ([Zhuangzi II.1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). And Zi-Qi's answer: "I had just now lost myself; but how should you understand it? You may have heard the notes of Man, but have not heard those of Earth; you may have heard the notes of Earth, but have not heard those of Heaven" ([Zhuangzi II.1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The wind itself: "When the breath of the Great Mass (of nature) comes strongly, it is called Wind. Sometimes it does not come so; but when it does, then from a myriad apertures there issues its excited noise; have you not heard it in a prolonged gale?" And the close of the exchange: "The notes of Earth then are simply those which come from its myriad apertures; and the notes of Man may just be compared to those which (are brought from the tubes of) bamboo- allow me to ask about the notes of Heaven.' Zi-Qi replied, 'Blowing the myriad differences, making them stop [proceed] of themselves, sealing their self-selecting - who is it that stirs it all up?'" ([Zhuangzi II.1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the passage states what perception is: not the ten thousand opinions (the notes of Man) and not the physical holes (the notes of Earth), but the sounding itself, which no one makes and everyone borrows. Argument, in this view, is just the wind arguing about its echo.

China · recorded in The Gateless Gate (1228), case 38 in this edition · a Zen koan, Senzaki and Reps translation

A monk asks a doctrinal question, and the reply he gets is a tree. The case is short enough to hold entire, and I offer it as a companion to B124's invitation to pause before supplying a verdict, told as a scene ([The Gateless Gate, case 38](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Gateless_Gate/An_Oak_Tree_In_The_Garden)):

> A monk asked Joshu why Bodhidharma came to China.

> Joshu said: "An oak tree in the garden."

The compiler Mumon's comment on it ([The Gateless Gate, case 38](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Gateless_Gate/An_Oak_Tree_In_The_Garden)):

> If one sees Joshu's answer clearly, there is no Shakyamuni Buddha before him and no future Buddha after him.

And his verse ([The Gateless Gate, case 38](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Gateless_Gate/An_Oak_Tree_In_The_Garden)):

> Words cannot describe everything.
>
> The heart's message cannot be delivered in words.
>
> If one receives words literally, he will be lost,
>
> If he tries to explain with words, he will not attain enlightenment in this life.

My reading: I use Joshu's unexpected answer as a companion to B124's invitation to pause before supplying a verdict. The monk asks about Bodhidharma's coming; the reply names an oak tree. For this practical application, the shift can interrupt the rush toward another doctrinal explanation. The case does not establish that perception is unmediated, that interpretation can be eliminated, or that Joshu intended this particular exercise; it does not say that he pointed, that a particular tree stood nearby, or that the answer was meant as a sensory-attention drill. Mumon's comment and verse leave the exchange larger than that application.

**Principles:**
- [[B124 — Meet suchness — things as they are|124. Meet suchness: things as they are]] — asked about the meaning of Bodhidharma's coming, Joshu answers with the oak tree in the garden, a reply offered as a companion to pausing over what is there before supplying a verdict

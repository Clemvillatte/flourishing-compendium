New York City, 1972-2020 · the Orpheus Chamber Orchestra, told by founder Julian Fifer (1994) and artistic director James Wilson (2020) · an organizational narrative, opened web sources

Since 1972 the Orpheus Chamber Orchestra has performed symphonic repertoire with no one on the podium, and the machinery it built to replace the conductor is a working model of D115's claim that leading from behind is a structure, not an absence. The ensemble's members lead and follow in rotation, and the work is credited to the group ([The Violin Channel](https://mobile.theviolinchannel.com/blog-orpheus-chamber-orchestra-james-wilson-process-inner-workings/), [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-02-02-ca-18216-story.html)).

The founder, cellist Julian Fifer, described the arrangement in 1994 ([Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1994-02-02-ca-18216-story.html)):

> "The 26-member ensemble has played without a conductor since Fifer founded it in 1972."

> "We rotate all seating positions in the orchestra," explained Julian Fifer, executive director of the ensemble. "The sections are absolutely interchangeable, and we rearrange seating for every piece. There is no such thing as being first violin player, or principal * anything, * in Orpheus."

> "In virtually all respects it is self-governing; impermanent seating plans reflect the fact that artistic decisions are made by revolving committees, or core groups."

> "As far as expressing ideas, and providing interpretive leadership, I suppose it could be anyone," he said.

The artistic director James Wilson described the same machinery from inside ([The Violin Channel](https://mobile.theviolinchannel.com/blog-orpheus-chamber-orchestra-james-wilson-process-inner-workings/)):

> "Many ensembles depend on the expertise of the conductor to provide the direction needed to lead, but for Orpheus Chamber Orchestra, the ensemble depends on each and every member to initiate, interpret, and perform."

> "One of the first things you will notice if you see us perform is that we employ a rotating leadership model. There will be different people sitting in the leadership chairs for each work on the program. The leaders of each section for any given work are collectively referred to as 'The Core' and they will have primary responsibility for overseeing the preparation and creating an artistic vision for the piece."

> "In Orpheus' model the concertmaster does not fully craft the artistic vision of a piece of music but is the important first point of contact for visual cues and for verbal communication as the rehearsal goes on. In cases of stalemate, where the rehearsal might get stuck, the concertmaster makes final decisions or decides to move on or table the discussion."

My reading: DDJ 17 ranks rulers by how the people respond, and puts at the top the ruler whose people barely notice a ruler at all, because the work reads as their own doing. An Orpheus concert is that ranking staged: the audience watches an orchestra with an empty podium, and the performance proceeds as if it happened of itself. The mechanism matches the principle's other face, that the leader serves from below and behind. Leadership exists, but as a rotating chair: the Core prepares a concept and then, in Wilson's words, its "responsibility to communicate its concepts to the rest of the group" makes the leaders answerable to the ensemble rather than the reverse, and the concertmaster is explicitly not the vision-craftsman but the contact point who steps in only when the process is stuck. Fifer's remark that interpretive leadership "could be anyone" is the principle's claim in its plainest form: authority attaches to the work, not to a person. What the story does not carry is the political frame of the chapters, a ruler's relation to a state and the faith-deficiency chain of DDJ 17; it carries the pattern of leading from behind where the led never feel the leader's weight.

**Principles:**
- [[D115 — Lead from behind|115. Lead from behind]] — the conductorless orchestra replaces the podium with rotating leadership chairs and core groups answerable to the whole ensemble, the work proceeding as if of itself

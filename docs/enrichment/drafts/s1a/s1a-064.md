Biographical account · Alfred Nobel (1833-1896), inventor and businessman, via NobelPrize.org's biography and will pages, a 2017 retrospective in the Rambam Maimonides Medical Journal, and a 2026 investigation of the obituary story.

Nobel built his fortune on controlled explosion. He held 355 patents, invented dynamite, and within ten years sixteen explosives factories had been founded in fourteen countries; he also bought the Bofors-Gullspång machine works at Karlskoga ([NobelPrize.org biography](https://www.nobelprize.org/alfred-nobel/)). The media coverage of his career highlighted "the manufacturing of explosive chemicals and devices and the development of armaments", and he "was portrayed as an arms merchant" ([Lichtman 2017](https://www.rmmj.org.il/issues/34/748/manuscript)). In 1888 his brother Ludvig died in Cannes, and a Paris paper mixed up the brothers. The notice that survives read: "A man who can hardly be called a benefactor of humanity died yesterday in Cannes. It is Mr. Nobel, inventor of dynamite" ([bgodinspired 2026](https://www.bgodinspired.com/index.php/bgodinspired-news/alfred-nobel-merchant-of-death-obituary/)). The famous headline "Le marchand de la mort est mort" has never been found in any newspaper from that time; a researcher traced the dramatic version to a biography published in 1959, and Nobel's biographer Ingrid Carlberg has said the mix-up is not mentioned in his letters ([bgodinspired 2026](https://www.bgodinspired.com/index.php/bgodinspired-news/alfred-nobel-merchant-of-death-obituary/)). A retrospective puts it with a hedge: his philanthropic interests "may have crystallized after reading his own obituary" ([Lichtman 2017](https://www.rmmj.org.il/issues/34/748/manuscript)).

The record's hard fact is the will. On 27 November 1895 Nobel signed his third and last will, directing that the fortune's interest be distributed as prizes "to those who, during the preceding year, have conferred the greatest benefit to humankind", with nationality expressly disregarded ([NobelPrize.org will](https://www.nobelprize.org/alfred-nobel/alfred-nobels-will/)). His family opposed it, the awarders he named refused, and five years passed before the first prize in 1901 ([NobelPrize.org will](https://www.nobelprize.org/alfred-nobel/alfred-nobels-will/)).

The connection with B025 is the accounting itself: what the work helped happen was counted, arms and all, and the fortune's final instruction bound it to benefit. The page's questions run forward from there; whether Nobel asked them because of a newspaper notice, no document shows.

**Principles:**
- [[B025 — Practice right livelihood]]: the explosives fortune's consequences were named by the world as armaments, and the will redirected the fortune toward the greatest benefit to humankind; the causal story behind the redirection is honestly marked as undocumented.

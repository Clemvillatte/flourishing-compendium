Biographical account · Vedran Smailović (b. 1956), principal cellist of the Sarajevo Opera Orchestra, via a Sarajevo city account of the 1992 episode.

On 27 May 1992 a mortar shell fell on the queue outside a bakery on Vase Miskina Street in Sarajevo and killed twenty-two people ([Explore Sarajevo](https://exploresarajevo.com/stories/2026/09/sarajevo-cellist-smailovic-albinoni/)). The next afternoon Smailović, thirty-six, "in full evening dress carried his instrument and a stool to the crater, sat down, and began to play. He came back at the same time the following day. And the day after. Twenty-two consecutive days, one for each of the dead" ([Explore Sarajevo](https://exploresarajevo.com/stories/2026/09/sarajevo-cellist-smailovic-albinoni/)). The piece was the one the world knows as Albinoni's Adagio in G minor ([Explore Sarajevo](https://exploresarajevo.com/stories/2026/09/sarajevo-cellist-smailovic-albinoni/)).

His own account of the reason has survived in his interviews with the BBC and The Irish Times: "he did not play in the crater to make a statement about war. He played to keep himself from despairing, and because, as he put it in a 2002 interview, 'a cellist plays. That is what a cellist does'" ([Explore Sarajevo](https://exploresarajevo.com/stories/2026/09/sarajevo-cellist-smailovic-albinoni/)).

The connection with B094 is offered with its bounds named. The principle's sutta treats calm as the right response to a restless mind, the wet grass for the large fire ([SN 46.53, Thanissaro, cited on the B094 page](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn46/sn46.053.than.html)), and this story is that logic at the scale of a city. What the cellist did was small, repeated, and placed exactly in the crater: an hour's music a day, at the same hour, in the same dress he wore for the concert hall. I read the daily return as the practice keeping its shape while everything around it burned; the draft does not claim the city became calm, and the man's own words give a humbler reason than any reading would.

Not carried: the sutta's graded training of the seven factors and the right-time logic of matching calm to a restless mind; the page keeps that teaching, and the story keeps the crater.

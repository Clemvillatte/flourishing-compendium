Biographical account · Aron Ralston (b. 1975), outdoorsman, via the Wikipedia article on his 2003 accident.

On April 26, 2003, in a solo descent of Bluejohn Canyon in Utah, Ralston dislodged a boulder that pinned his right wrist to the canyon wall. He spent five days rationing roughly 350 ml of water and two burritos, "repeatedly trying to extricate his arm", and after three days of trying to lift and chip away at the boulder he prepared to amputate the trapped arm to escape ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Ralston)). The chronology the account gives is specific: on the fourth day, after experimenting with tourniquets and making exploratory cuts, he realized he would have to cut through bone and his tools were insufficient; the epiphany that followed was about the means, that "he could break his radius and ulna using torque against his trapped arm"; he did so, then amputated his forearm with the dull two-inch knife and pliers of his multi-tool, an hour-long process with a CamelBak tube for a tourniquet and the major arteries left until last ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Ralston)).

Ralston later said that if he had amputated earlier he would have bled to death before being found, and that if he had not done it he would have been found dead days later ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Ralston)); the incident is documented in his autobiography Between a Rock and a Hard Place and the film made from it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Ralston)).

Parable · Warring States China · Zhuangzi III.2 · quotations from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

A cook is cutting up an ox for the ruler Wen Hui, and the sounds of the knife fall into cadence — "as in the dance of 'the Mulberry Forest' and the blended notes of the King Shou.'" ([Zhuangzi III.2, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Asked how his art became so perfect, the cook corrects the premise: "What your servant loves is the method of the Dao, something in advance of any art" ([Zhuangzi III.2, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). He describes how the practice changed what he sees: "Now I deal with it in a spirit-like manner, and do not look at it with my eyes. The use of my senses is discarded, and my spirit acts as it wills. Observing the natural lines, (my knife) slips through the great crevices and slides through the great cavities, taking advantage of the facilities thus presented. My art avoids the membranous ligatures, and much more the great bones" ([Zhuangzi III.2, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The blade's condition is the evidence: "Now my knife has been in use for nineteen years; it has cut up several thousand oxen, and yet its edge is as sharp as if it had newly come from the whetstone. There are the interstices of the joints, and the edge of the knife has no (appreciable) thickness; when that which is so thin enters where the interstice is, how easily it moves along! The blade has more than room enough" ([Zhuangzi III.2, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

And the parable keeps its most practical teaching for the hard part: "whenever I come to a complicated joint, and see that there will be some difficulty, I proceed anxiously and with caution, not allowing my eyes to wander from the place, and moving my hand slowly. Then by a very slight movement of the knife, the part is quickly separated, and drops like (a clod of) earth to the ground" ([Zhuangzi III.2, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The ruler's closing line is the frame Zhuangzi himself puts on it: "Excellent! I have heard the words of my cook, and learned from them the nourishment of (our) life" ([Zhuangzi III.2, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the parable is less about skill than about where resistance comes from. The blade stays sharp because the cook has stopped fighting the ox — the difficulty at the complicated joint is met with slower movement, not more force, and that is the transferable part for a life.

**Principles:**
- [[D001 — Align your life with the Dao|D001 Align your life with the Dao]]: the cook's own thesis line — the method of the Dao is "something in advance of any art".
- [[D002 — Practice ziran — naturalness|D002 Practice ziran: naturalness]]: the knife follows the natural lines and crevices rather than an imposed plan.
- [[D004 — Cultivate de — your embodied character-power|D004 Cultivate de: your embodied character-power]]: nineteen years of practice carried in the body — the senses discarded, the spirit acting as it wills.
- [[D013 — Keep the valley-mind — low, open, receptive|D013 Keep the valley-mind: low, open, receptive]]: at the difficult joint the cook yields attention to the place — caution and slow movement instead of force.

**Resonates:** [[D055 — Don't force|D055 Don't force]] — the blade goes where the openings are and avoids the bones; [[D070 — Let stillness precede mastery|D070 Let stillness precede mastery]] — the complicated joint is met with the slow hand; [[P073 — See conduct as interaction|P073 See conduct as interaction]] — the work is read from the ox's structure, not imposed on it.

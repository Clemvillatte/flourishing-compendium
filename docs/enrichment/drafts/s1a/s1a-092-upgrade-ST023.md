Biographical/philosophical narrative, cross-cultural · France, 1942 · [Simone Weil, "Reflections on the Right Use of School Studies with a View to the Love of God"](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf)

Weil, working through war and illness, argues that attention is the substance of prayer — and that school studies, of all things, train it. Her essay opens: "The key to a Christian conception of studies is the realization that prayer consists of attention. It is the orientation of all the attention of which the soul is capable toward God" ([Weil, school-studies essay](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf)).

School work, she says, develops a lower kind of attention that is nevertheless real training: "School children and students who love God should never say: 'For my part I like mathematics'; 'I like French'; 'I like Greek.' They should learn to like all these subjects, because all of them develop that faculty of attention which, directed toward God, is the very substance of prayer" ([Weil, school-studies essay](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf)).

And she defines the faculty itself: "Attention consists of suspending our thought, leaving it detached, empty, and ready to be penetrated by the object; it means holding in our minds, within reach of this thought, but on a lower level and not in contact with it, the diverse knowledge we have acquired which we are forced to make use of. Our thought should be in relation to all particular and already formulated thoughts, as a man on a mountain who, as he looks forward, sees also below him, without actually looking at them, a great many forests and plains. Above all our thought should be empty, waiting, not seeking anything, but ready to receive in its naked truth the object that is to penetrate it" ([Weil, school-studies essay](https://www.themathesontrust.org/papers/christianity/Weil-Reflections.pdf)).

My reading: the claim sounds mystical until you test it against ordinary experience: the people you consider most loving are rarely the most effusive. They are the ones who listen as if the matter were difficult and important, because to them it is. Attention, on Weil's definition, is exactly that — thought suspended, empty, not seeking anything, ready to receive the object in its naked truth.

Zhuangzi metaphor · Warring States China · [Zhuangzi, ch. 6, "The Great and Most Honoured Master"](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/the-great-and-venerable-teacher) · quotation from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

The true man of old, says the text, breathed from his heels. Everyone else breathes from the throat. Those crushed and defeated in argument gasp out their words as if retching.

In Legge's translation: "The True men of old did not dream when they slept, had no anxiety when they awoke, and did not care that their food should be pleasant. Their breathing came deep and silently. The breathing of the true man comes (even) from his heels, while men generally breathe (only) from their throats. When men are defeated in argument, their words come from their gullets as if they were vomiting. Where lusts and desires are deep, the springs of the Heavenly are shallow" ([Zhuangzi VI, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the contrast between heels and throat makes breathing part of the portrait of composure; I would not treat it as a literal breathing instruction. The passage sets the deep, silent breath against the words that come from the gullets of the defeated, and ends with the diagnostic: "Where lusts and desires are deep, the springs of the Heavenly are shallow."

Traditional Chan teaching narrative · Pang Yun, the Layman Pang (traditionally 740-808), Tang-dynasty householder, via the Terebess archive page and a World Congress of Philosophy paper quoting the standard translation.

The story turns on a question and its answer. Staying on to practice with Master Shitou, Pang was asked: "Since seeing me what have your daily activities been?" Pang said, "When you ask about my daily activities I can't open my mouth." Shitou said, "Because I know you're like that, I'm asking you." Pang's reply, in the exchange as the Terebess page gives it, was "How wondrous, how miraculous – chopping wood and carrying water" ([Terebess](https://terebess.hu/zen/pang.html)). The same moment survives as a verse in the standard translation: "My daily activities are not unusual, / I'm just naturally in harmony with them. / Grasping nothing, discarding nothing, / In every place there is no hindrance, nor conflict... / [My] supernatural power and marvelous activity — / Drawing water and carrying firewood" ([Sasaki tr. 1971, p. 46, via WCP paper](https://www.bu.edu/wcp/Papers/Asia/AsiaMits.htm)).

The verse sets up the categories and then fills them with chores: the supernatural power and the marvelous activity are the water-drawing and the firewood-carrying. Shitou later asked whether Pang would shave his head and take robes; he answered "I want to do what I like," and remained a layman ([Terebess](https://terebess.hu/zen/pang.html)).

I read the answer as locating the realization in the ordinary work it describes: the daily activities are not a preparation for something elsewhere, and the verse gives them the language the tradition reserved for its highest claims. That is my reading of the recorded exchange, not a claim about Pang's inner state.

The fit with B179 is direct on its central facet, the goal answered by the path itself; the page's practice instructions for sustaining awareness in such work are not carried here and remain the principle page's own.

**Principles:**
- [[B179 — The path is the goal]]: asked what his daily activities have been, Pang answers with the chores named as the supernatural power and marvelous activity; the ordinary work is the answer rather than the road to one.

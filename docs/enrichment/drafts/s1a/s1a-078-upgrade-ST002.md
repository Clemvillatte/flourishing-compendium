Parable · Warring States China · Zhuangzi, The Adjustment of Controversies, section 14 · quotations from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

"Formerly, I, Zhuang Zhou, dreamt that I was a butterfly, a butterfly flying about, feeling that it was enjoying itself. I did not know that it was Zhou. Suddenly I awoke, and was myself again, the veritable Zhou" ([Zhuangzi II §14, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). He then states the puzzle precisely, without solving it: "I did not know whether it had formerly been Zhou dreaming that he was a butterfly, or it was now a butterfly dreaming that it was Zhou" ([Zhuangzi II §14, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). And the closing line: "But between Zhou and a butterfly there must be a difference. This is a case of what is called the Transformation of Things" ([Zhuangzi II §14, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the story invites an essayist to examine how confidently they identify with a current point of view. Zhou reports not knowing which experience was the dream, while the closing sentence still insists on a difference between Zhou and the butterfly. I take that combination as a prompt to question certainty about the self, not as a proof that all perspectives are equally true.

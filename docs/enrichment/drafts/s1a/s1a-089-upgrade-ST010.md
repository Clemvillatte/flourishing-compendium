Zhuangzi parable · Warring States China · [Zhuangzi, Autumn Floods (ch. 17)](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/the-floods-of-autumn) · quotation from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

The frog in the shallow well is the master of all he surveys. He leaps among the rails, rests in the cracks, and knows nothing beyond the rim. The turtle of the Eastern Sea tries to describe the ocean: a thousand li without deepening, a thousand li without rising. The frog's mind boggles; the well is all the world it has.

In Legge's translation, the frog boasts to the turtle: "How I enjoy myself? I leap upon the parapet of this well. I enter, and having by means of the projections formed by the fragments of the broken tiles of the lining proceeded to the water, I draw my legs together, keep my chin up, (and strike out). When I have got to the mud, I dive till my feet are lost in it. Then turning round, I see that of the shrimps, crabs, and tadpoles there is not one that can do like me" ([Zhuangzi, The Floods of Autumn, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The turtle answers with the sea: "A distance of a thousand li is not sufficient to express its extent, nor would (a line of) eight thousand cubits be equal to sound its depth... no change is produced in its waters by any cause operating for a short time or a long, and that they do not advance nor recede for any addition or subtraction, whether great or small; and this is the great pleasure afforded by the Eastern Sea." And the close: "When the frog of the dilapidated well heard this, he was amazed and terror-struck, and lost himself in surprise" ([Zhuangzi, The Floods of Autumn, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the parable is not mockery of the frog so much as a mirror. Every mind is a well; the question is whether it knows the sea exists and keeps the opening wide. The frog's real problem is that he mistakes his well for the world — "the greatest pleasure to enjoy one's self here in this dilapidated well."

Traditional Daoist parable · from Zhuangzi, chapter 2, "The Adjustment of Controversies", in Legge's translation as printed on the Chinese Text Project.

A keeper of monkeys tells them: "In the morning I will give you three (measures) and in the evening four." This makes them all angry, and he says: "Very well. In the morning I will give you four and in the evening three." The monkeys were all pleased. The two proposals were substantially the same, "but the result of the one was to make the creatures angry, and of the other to make them pleased - an illustration of the point I am insisting on" ([Zhuangzi 2, Legge, ctext](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/adjustment-of-controversies)).

The D124 mapping is withdrawn, and the draft points where the passage actually fits. What the passage shows is a keeper accommodating another response to the same allocation: the total does not change, the arrangement does, and the monkeys' anger changes completely. I read the chapter's frame as naming what the monkeys' anger and pleasure both miss: "When we toil our spirits and intelligence, obstinately determined (to establish our own view), and do not know the agreement (which underlies it and the views of others), we have what is called 'In the morning three'" ([Zhuangzi 2, Legge, ctext](https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/adjustment-of-controversies)). The passage does not show the keeper identifying what is true or right in an opposing argument, which is D124's central instruction, so that mapping is withdrawn rather than held through metaphor. The D083 page, opened directly, uses this exact passage as its own example: it reads the daily total of acorns as the same in both proposals, what differs as the distribution, and the monkeys' anger and pleasure as tracking the distribution, and asks which terms can change while the total remains fixed. The keeper's switch is that operation in the parable's own words. Both this draft and s1a-075 now point at D083, and which story serves the page is routed to the supervisor.

Not carried: D124's central instruction, stating the opposing position's strongest point in your own words before answering; the parable shows accommodation of a preference, not that operation, and that mapping is withdrawn; the page's exercise keeps to the principle page.

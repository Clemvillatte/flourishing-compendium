Buddhist sutta · Majjhima Nikaya 20, Vitakkasanthana Sutta, trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu

A monk sits down intent on the heightened mind, and the thoughts keep arriving: thoughts imbued with desire, aversion and delusion. The Buddha's discourse on relaxing such thoughts gives him a ladder of five moves, each one an operation on the thought rather than a debate with it, and each rung ends the same way: the thoughts are abandoned and subside, and the monk steadies his mind right within. The question the sutta puts to a thought is never whether the thought is true. ([MN 20, Access to Insight](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

The opening names the situation plainly: "There is the case where evil, unskillful thoughts — imbued with desire, aversion, or delusion — arise in a monk while he is referring to and attending to a particular theme." The first move is to attend to another theme, apart from that one, connected with what is skillful. ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

If the thoughts still arise, the monk climbs. He scrutinizes their drawbacks, and the sutta puts the reflection in his own mouth: "Truly, these thoughts of mine are unskillful, these thoughts of mine are blameworthy, these thoughts of mine result in stress." Then he pays them no mind and no attention. Then he attends to the relaxing of thought-fabrication with regard to those thoughts. And as a last resort, "with his teeth clenched and his tongue pressed against the roof of his mouth — he should beat down, constrain, and crush his mind with his awareness." ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

The discourse gives its own picture of what the ladder is: "Just as the thought would occur to a man walking quickly, 'Why am I walking quickly? Why don't I walk slowly?' So he walks slowly. The thought occurs to him, 'Why am I walking slowly? Why don't I stand?' So he stands": through sitting and lying down. "In this way, giving up the grosser posture, he takes up the more refined one." ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

The outcome is mastery, and the wording is the point: "He is then called a monk with mastery over the ways of thought sequences. He thinks whatever thought he wants to, and doesn't think whatever thought he doesn't." For B074 the story carries the whole move: the thought arrives, it is examined for skillfulness rather than received as fact, it is worked until it subsides, and what remains is a mind that treats thoughts as things it uses rather than things that rule it. ([MN 20](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.020.than.html))

**Principles:**
- [[B074 — Thoughts are not facts|B074 Thoughts are not facts]]: MN 20's ladder treats an arriving thought as an event to be scrutinized, ignored, relaxed or crushed before it is obeyed; its test of a thought is skillfulness, and its outcome is mastery over thought-sequences rather than belief in them.

Constantin Brancusi carved a man and a woman embracing, and called it The Kiss. He kept it a block of stone. The two figures are carved from a single block with no attempt to disguise the block; their arms are locked around each other, and their profiles are symmetrical, the eyes, lips and hair carved the same ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/documents/198/brancusi_tkit.pdf)). Tate explains that carving the couple from one piece also expresses their union ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/documents/198/brancusi_tkit.pdf)).

His explanation of *Fish* makes the distinction concrete:

> "When you see a fish you don't think about its scales, you think about the speed of its movement, its gleaming body seen through the water. That's what I wanted to express… I was searching for the spark that animates its spirit."

([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/documents/198/brancusi_tkit.pdf))

He knew the materials pushed back, too. As he told Dorothy Dudley in 1927: "you cannot make what you want to make, but what the material permits you to make... Each material has its own life, and one cannot without punishment destroy a living material to make a dumb senseless thing. That is, we must not try to make materials speak our language, we must go with them to the point where others will understand their language" ([ICR](https://www.icr.ro/pagini/quotes-on-and-from-brancusi/de)). He said as much himself, twice. In the catalog of his 1926 New York exhibition: "Simplicity is not an end in art, but one arrives at simplicity in spite of oneself, in approaching the real sense of things" ([ICR](https://www.icr.ro/pagini/quotes-on-and-from-brancusi/de)). And to Dorothy Dudley in 1927, plainer still: "I never seek to make what they call a pure or abstract form. Pureness, simplicity is never in my mind; to arrive at the real sense of things is the one aim" ([ICR](https://www.icr.ro/pagini/quotes-on-and-from-brancusi/de)). ### Action

Pick something you are making. List its parts, then ask the fish question: which part is the speed, and which is only scales? Remove one scale before you add anything new.

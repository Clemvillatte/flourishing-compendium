Constantin Brancusi carved a man and a woman embracing, and called it The Kiss. He kept it a block of stone. The two figures are carved from a single block with no attempt to disguise the block; their arms are locked around each other, and their profiles are symmetrical, the eyes, lips and hair carved the same ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/documents/198/brancusi_tkit.pdf)). What he took away is the whole tradition of pretending that stone is skin. What he kept is the one thing the subject was about.

Brancusi spent decades doing this to birds, fish and columns. His own account of the fish is the clearest statement of the method I know:

> "When you see a fish you don't think about its scales, you think about the speed of its movement, its gleaming body seen through the water. That's what I wanted to express… I was searching for the spark that animates its spirit."

([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/documents/198/brancusi_tkit.pdf))

Note what he does not say. He does not say he simplified the fish because simple forms are fashionable, or easier, or modern. He says the scales were never the fish. The speed was the fish. The subtraction is not a style; it is an accuracy.

He said so himself, twice, in words that could headline a book on Daodejing 48. In the catalog of his 1926 New York exhibition: "Simplicity is not an end in art, but one arrives at simplicity in spite of oneself, in approaching the real sense of things" ([ICR](https://www.icr.ro/pagini/quotes-on-and-from-brancusi/de)). And to Dorothy Dudley in 1927, plainer still: "I never seek to make what they call a pure or abstract form. Pureness, simplicity is never in my mind; to arrive at the real sense of things is the one aim" ([ICR](https://www.icr.ro/pagini/quotes-on-and-from-brancusi/de)). Diminish, and diminish, and what is left is not less. It is the thing itself, standing there without its disguise.

### Action

Pick something you are making. List its parts, then ask the fish question: which part is the speed, and which is only scales? Remove one scale before you add anything new.

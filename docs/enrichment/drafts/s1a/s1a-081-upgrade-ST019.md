Dialogue · Warring States China · Zhuangzi, Man in the World, Associated with Other Men, section 1 · quotations from the James Legge translation (1891), opened at [METIS ETH Zurich](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)

Yan Hui wants to go reform the young tyrant of Wei, and Zhongni (Confucius) dismantles each plan he offers. Yan Hui asks about fasting — he has in mind the dietary kind, months without wine or strong-smelling vegetables — and is corrected: "It is the fasting appropriate to sacrificing, but it is not the fasting of the mind" ([Zhuangzi IV §1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The instruction is graduated listening: "You will not wait for the hearing of your ears about it, but for the hearing of your mind. You will not wait even for the hearing of your mind, but for the hearing of the spirit" ([Zhuangzi IV §1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The definition follows: "Let the hearing (of the ears) rest with the ears. Let the mind rest in the verification (of the rightness of what is in the will). But the spirit is free from all pre-occupation and so waits for (the appearance of) things" ([Zhuangzi IV §1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). And the summary line: "Where the (proper) course is, there is freedom from all pre-occupation; such freedom is the fasting of the mind" ([Zhuangzi IV §1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Yan Hui's response records what the practice does to the practitioner: "Before it was possible for me to employ (this method), there I was, the Hui that I am; now, that I can employ it, the Hui that I was has passed away" ([Zhuangzi IV §1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The dialogue closes with the practical rule for the hard conversation itself: "If he listen to your counsels, let him hear your notes; if he will not listen, be silent. Open no (other) door; employ no other medicine; dwell with him (as with a friend) in the same apartment, and as if you had no other option, and you will not be far from success in your object" ([Zhuangzi IV §1, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

My reading: the dialogue is the oldest systematic account of what preparation for a hard conversation actually involves. It is not the gathering of arguments; it is the quieting of the agitated strategist who wants to win — the ears rest with hearing, the mind rests with verifying, and only what is free of pre-occupation can wait for the other person to appear.

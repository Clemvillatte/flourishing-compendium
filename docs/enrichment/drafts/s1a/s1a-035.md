In late 1967, Agnes Martin burned all of the art left in her New York studio, gave away her art supplies, and left. She had finally established the career she had pursued for two decades ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)). Later, she offered different explanations for going, including: "I thought I would experiment in solitude, you know. Simple living." She also wrote, "I left New York in 1967 because every day I suddenly felt I wanted to die and it was connected with painting. It took me several years to find out that the cause was an overdeveloped sense of responsibility."

She spent the proceeds of a $5,000 arts grant on a Dodge pickup and an Airstream trailer, then spent a year and a half camping across Canada and the American West. "I drove all in the West and up in Canada and couldn't make up my mind where to stop," she recalled. What stopped her was odder than any plan: "I had a vision of an adobe brick. And I thought, that means I should go to New Mexico." She leased fifty acres on Mesa Portales near Cuba, New Mexico, lived in her trailer and began building an adobe house ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)).

In "The Current of the River of Life Moves Us," Martin described stillness as part of her working practice ([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf)):

> "To live and work by inspiration you have to stop thinking. You have to hold your mind still in order to hear inspiration dearly."

The printed text says "dearly", not "clearly" — the edition preserves her typing, and the slip somehow suits the sentence.

In her "Lecture at Cornell University" ([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf)), she wrote:

> "Inspiration is there all the time. For everyone whose mind is not clouded over with thoughts whether they realize it or not."
>
> "Of course we know that an untroubled state of mind cannot last. So we say that inspiration comes and goes but really it is there all the time waiting for us to be untroubled again."

([Writings, ed. Dieter Schwarz](https://ia600605.us.archive.org/9/items/AgnesMartinWritings/Agnes%20Martin%20-%20Writings%20-%20Agnes%20Martin.pdf))

In 1974 Martin built a thirty-five-foot-square log-cabin-style studio on the property and began painting again. Her first exhibition at Pace Gallery opened on March 1, 1975 ([Art Canada Institute](https://artbooks.artcanada.com/agnes-martin/biography)).

### Action

When your work goes dark, ask first whether the water has been stirred. Before adding effort, try two minutes of doing nothing at all, and see what becomes audible.

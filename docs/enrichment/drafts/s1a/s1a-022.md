Canonical sutta, the Buddha's own retrospective · Mahāsaccaka Sutta (MN 36), trans. Thanissaro

Before his awakening, he says, he tried to master his mind by force: "clenching my teeth and pressing my tongue against the roof of my mouth, I beat down, constrained, & crushed my mind with my awareness". ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) He reports persistence and mindfulness alongside bodily agitation: "although tireless persistence was aroused in me, and unmuddled mindfulness established, my body was aroused & uncalm because of the painful exertion". ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) He ate "only a little food at a time, only a handful at a time of bean soup, lentil soup, vetch soup, or pea soup", until "when I thought of touching my belly, I grabbed hold of my spine as well". ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

The verdict on that striving is his own: "But with this racking practice of austerities I haven't attained any superior human state, any distinction in knowledge or vision worthy of the noble ones. Could there be another path to Awakening?" ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) The answer came as a memory, not a harder effort: "I recall once, when my father the Sakyan was working, and I was sitting in the cool shade of a rose-apple tree", he had entered the first jhana, "rapture & pleasure born from seclusion". ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) Following that memory came the realization: "That is the path to Awakening." ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) His next thought is the hinge of the story: "So why am I afraid of that pleasure that has nothing to do with sensuality, nothing to do with unskillful mental qualities?" ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) The body, he judged, needed strength for it: "Suppose I were to take some solid food: some rice & porridge." ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

The five monks interpreted renewed eating as abandoning exertion: "Gotama the contemplative is living luxuriously. He has abandoned his exertion and is backsliding into abundance." ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) The account continues: "So when I had taken solid food and regained strength, then — quite secluded from sensuality, secluded from unskillful mental qualities, I entered & remained in the first jhana: rapture & pleasure born from seclusion". ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

Read beside B026's instruction to choose the target of effort first, this episode offers a comparison with developing a helpful mental quality. ([B026](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/B026%20%E2%80%94%20Apply%20right%20effort%20%E2%80%94%20the%20four%20efforts.md)) MN 36 reports tireless persistence and unmuddled mindfulness during painful exertion, yet no attainment of the goal. ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) After recalling first jhana, he took food, regained strength and entered it again. ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html)) The comparison concerns the development arm; it neither demonstrates all four efforts nor establishes that target rather than intensity was the sole variable. ([B026](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/B026%20%E2%80%94%20Apply%20right%20effort%20%E2%80%94%20the%20four%20efforts.md))

**Principles:**
- [[B026 — Apply right effort — the four efforts|B026 Apply right effort — the four efforts]]: A comparison with the development arm: recalling first jhana, taking food and regaining strength precede renewed attainment; the other three efforts are not narrated here. ([MN 36](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

Canonical sutta, the Buddha's own retrospective · Mahāsaccaka Sutta (MN 36), trans. Thanissaro

Before his awakening, he says, he tried to master his mind by force: "clenching my teeth and pressing my tongue against the roof of my mouth, I beat down, constrained, & crushed my mind with my awareness". The striving was maximal, and he says so exactly: "although tireless persistence was aroused in me, and unmuddled mindfulness established, my body was aroused & uncalm because of the painful exertion". He ate "only a little food at a time, only a handful at a time of bean soup, lentil soup, vetch soup, or pea soup", until "when I thought of touching my belly, I grabbed hold of my spine as well". ([MN 36, trans. Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

The verdict on all that striving is his own: "But with this racking practice of austerities I haven't attained any superior human state, any distinction in knowledge or vision worthy of the noble ones. Could there be another path to Awakening?" The answer came as a memory, not a harder effort: "I recall once, when my father the Sakyan was working, and I was sitting in the cool shade of a rose-apple tree", he had entered the first jhana, "rapture & pleasure born from seclusion". Following that memory came the realization: "That is the path to Awakening." His next thought is the hinge of the story: "So why am I afraid of that pleasure that has nothing to do with sensuality, nothing to do with unskillful mental qualities?" The body, he judged, needed strength for it: "Suppose I were to take some solid food: some rice & porridge." ([MN 36, trans. Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

The five monks attending him read the bowl as betrayal: "they were disgusted and left me, thinking, 'Gotama the contemplative is living luxuriously. He has abandoned his exertion and is backsliding into abundance.'" The sutta reports the sequel without polemic: "So when I had taken solid food and regained strength, then — quite secluded from sensuality, secluded from unskillful mental qualities, I entered & remained in the first jhana: rapture & pleasure born from seclusion". ([MN 36, trans. Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.036.than.html))

This page compares the account with B026's plain-words instruction: choose the target of your effort first. The story's teaching is the same point from the other side. Desire, persistence and mindfulness are reported as already maximal during the austerities; what failed was the target, crushing the mind and starving the body, which kept the skillful quality he already knew as a boy from arising. The recalibration is not less effort but a different job, the development of a skillful quality, served by food and strength. The five monks' misreading shows the outside view of effort, difficulty mistaken for direction. The page illustrates the four efforts' development arm; prevention, abandonment and maintenance are not narrated here.

**Principles:**
- [[B026 — Apply right effort — the four efforts|B026 Apply right effort — the four efforts]]: the founder's own account of maximal striving mis-aimed at mind and body, recalibrated toward the arising of a skillful quality; effort's target, not its intensity, is the variable.

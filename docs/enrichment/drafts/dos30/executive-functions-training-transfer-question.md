### Does training working memory carry over to other abilities?

The shared question: if a person practises a working-memory task until they improve, does the gain show up anywhere else, in reasoning, in attention or in schoolwork? Melby-Lervåg and Hulme meta-analysed 23 studies with 30 comparisons and concluded:

> "The authors conclude that memory training programs appear to produce short-term, specific training effects that do not generalize." ([Melby-Lervåg and Hulme, Developmental Psychology 2013, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22612437/))

Karbach and Verhaeghen examined 49 studies of executive-function and working-memory training in older adults and reached a different verdict:

> "Summarized, we found that process-based training of EF and WM in old age is highly effective, and leads to reliable small to medium-sized transfer effects to both the latent construct trained and the wider cognitive system." ([Karbach and Verhaeghen, Psychological Science 2014, open-access manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4381540/))

Their abstract adds that "far-transfer effects were significant in 3 out of 4 comparisons" (same source), and its introduction describes the transfer findings as ones that "have inspired heated recent debates" (same source).

The two reviews sample different people. Melby-Lervåg and Hulme combined children and adults, clinical and typically developing, and Karbach and Verhaeghen studied adults over sixty and compared them with younger adults in the same studies. A claim that holds in one group need not hold in the other, so the page does not say that one review overturns the other. What they dispute directly is the weight of the far-transfer evidence: whether a small average gain on distant measures counts as generalization.

My lean, which is mine, is that the careful claim for the essays is the narrow one, that practice reliably improves the practised kind of task and that the case for wider benefit is contested and may depend on age. Limits: I read an abstract for the first review and the abstract, introduction and discussion excerpts of the second, I did not read either paper in full, and I did not open the later meta-analyses that revisit the question.

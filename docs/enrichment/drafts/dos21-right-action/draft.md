# Right Action

> What restraints on the body make a life livable with itself?

This dossier is a map, not an essay. The aim is that a reader can say "I want to think about right action", open this page, and see the whole spread of what the compendium holds on the fourth factor of the Eightfold Path: every entry that bears on bodily conduct, grouped by the question it answers, each with a card link. The lines are short on purpose; the dense arguments live on the cards. The editorial aim running through the compendium also runs through this page: a life whose conduct keeps a floor of non-harm under it, and practices the counterparts above that floor, is livable with itself in a way a life of unexamined habit is not.

How to read this page. Each line gives the entry's own opening claim, compressed from the entry itself. Evidence notes in this dossier describe the quoted passages only; a checked quotation certifies the passage, not the whole linked page. Every concept card carries onward to the practice principles that cite it, so the map needs no separate principle index.

## Nearby terms

- Right conduct, ethical action: other renderings of the same Pali factor, sammā kammanta; the wiki's card is [Right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action), and nothing is split between the names.
- Non-harm: the floor this factor rests on; the harmlessness resolve as an intention belongs to the Right Intention page, this page holds the bodily restraints that resolve issues in.
- Behaviour: the general science of habit formation stays there; this page holds the path's ethical restraints on bodily conduct.
- Right speech and right livelihood: the neighbouring conduct factors, each with its own page; the map below points to them only where the boundary matters.

## The map: every entry the wiki holds on right action

### What the factor is

[Right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Right%20action): the fourth factor, Pali sammā kammanta; abstaining from killing, from taking what is not given, and from sexual misconduct.

[Eightfold Path](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Eightfold%20Path): the umbrella hub; action is grouped with speech and livelihood in the ethics training.

[The three trainings](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20three%20trainings): the grouping that places conduct under ethics, after wisdom and alongside concentration.

[Action over intention](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20over%20intention): the compendium's label for this factor, marking where the path moves from resolve to conduct.

[Karma](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Karma): bodily action as karma; what is done through the body counts as deliberate action and sets results going.

[Spontaneous right action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Spontaneous%20right%20action): Wang Yangming's doctrine that knowing the good already issues in acting; held by the wiki as the far end of this factor's question (see the divergence below).

The Magga-vibhanga Sutta gives the definition:

> "And what, monks, is right action? Abstaining from taking life, abstaining from stealing, abstaining from unchastity: This, monks, is called right action." ([SN 45.8, Thanissaro translation](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/sn/sn45/sn45.008.than.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked.

Bhikkhu Bodhi's study of the path supplies the positive counterparts to the three abstentions:

> "The positive counterpart to abstaining from taking life, as the Buddha indicates, is the development of kindness and compassion for other beings. The disciple not only avoids destroying life; he dwells with a heart full of sympathy, desiring the welfare of all beings." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)): Scholarly study; quoted passages checked.

> "The positive virtue corresponding to the abstinence is, for laypeople, marital fidelity. Husband and wife should each be faithful and devoted to the other, content with the relationship, and should not risk a breakup to the union by seeking outside partners." ([Bhikkhu Bodhi, The Way to the End of Suffering](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/waytoend.html)): Scholarly study; quoted passages checked.

### The three restraints and their counterparts

The first restraint, taking life, and its counterpart, compassion:

[Ahimsa](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Ahimsa): the Indic principle of non-harm; the compendium reads it as the starting floor of this factor.

[Compassion](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Compassion): being moved by suffering and motivated to act; the counterpart Bodhi names first.

[Nonviolent action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20action): Gene Sharp's theory of power and his documented methods of nonviolent action.

[Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Nonviolent%20communication): Rosenberg's process for reducing conflict in everyday interactions.

[Conduct even in just war](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conduct%20even%20in%20just%20war): Aquinas's three requirements for a just war, and the warning that a war can meet two of them and still be rendered unlawful through a wicked intention.

[Courage — action despite fear](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Courage%20%E2%80%94%20action%20despite%20fear): acting for the sake of something despite fear; the courage the counterparts can demand.

[Harm principle](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Harm%20principle): Mill's principle for when society may coerce an adult; grouped here by editorial connection, as the boundary question of harm done to others.

[Judgment adds the harm](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Judgment%20adds%20the%20harm): the Stoic claim that events do not disturb people, the opinions about the events do; grouped here by editorial connection as a claim about disturbance rather than harm done.

The second restraint, taking what is not given, and its counterparts, honesty and generosity:

[Generosity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Generosity): Aristotle's liberality, the mean with regard to wealth, concerned especially with giving.

[Prosocial honesty research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Prosocial%20honesty%20research): the research finding that brutal honesty often serves the speaker's discharge, not the listener's benefit.

The third restraint, sexual misconduct, and its counterpart, fidelity:

[Fidelity](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Fidelity): Gabriel Marcel's treatment of fidelity as more than keeping a promise; the problem of constancy.

### How conduct is trained

[The categorical imperative](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20categorical%20imperative): Kant's supreme principle of morality, binding regardless of what a person wants; the restraint stated as a universal law.

[Natural virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Natural%20virtue): Aristotle's name for the dispositions people seem to have from birth, set against virtue in the strict sense where the difference is practical wisdom.

[The catalogue of virtues](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20catalogue%20of%20virtues): Aristotle's table of the character virtues as means between vices of excess and deficiency.

[Cardinal virtues](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Cardinal%20virtues): the four hinge virtues, prudence, justice, fortitude and temperance, on which the classical tradition says the whole of moral life turns.

[Kohlberg's theory of moral development](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Kohlberg%27s%20theory%20of%20moral%20development) and [Moral development research](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Moral%20development%20research): the research tradition on how moral reasoning develops, from stage theory to the intuition approaches that followed.

[Social cognitive theory of morality](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Social%20cognitive%20theory%20of%20morality): Bandura's account of moral conduct as learned through observation and self-regulation.

[Opposite action (Linehan)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Opposite%20action%20%28Linehan%29): Linehan's DBT skill of acting against the action urge that comes with an emotion.

[Self-constitution through action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Self-constitution%20through%20action): Kierkegaard's Judge Vilhelm, choosing shapes personality; a life that keeps choice at arm's length loses definition.

[Svadharma, one's own duty](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Svadharma%2C%20one%27s%20own%20duty): the Bhagavad Gita's teaching that one's own duty, however imperfect, is better than another's done well.

[The bodhisattva vow](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20bodhisattva%20vow): the Mahayana commitment to work for the liberation of all beings; conduct undertaken for everyone's sake.

[Care ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Care%20ethics) and [Gilligan's ethics-of-care theory](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Gilligan%27s%20ethics-of-care%20theory): the ethics that starts from the caring relation rather than the universal rule.

[Role ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Role%20ethics) and [Confucian role ethics](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Confucian%20role%20ethics): the Confucian account of conduct learned inside roles and relations rather than from principles.

[The call of conscience](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=The%20call%20of%20conscience) and [Conscience (Gewissen)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Conscience%20%28Gewissen%29): Heidegger's account of Dasein summoned out of its lostness in the "they"; conscience as the call.

[Skillful means (upaya)](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Skillful%20means%20%28upaya%29): the Lotus Sutra's skillful means, the flexible application of the teaching to the situation.

[Action precedes feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20precedes%20feeling) and [Action before feeling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20before%20feeling): the teaching that action precedes feeling, from Aristotle to James; the act comes first and the feeling follows.

[Action commitment](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Action%20commitment): ACT's committed action, one act consistent with a chosen value, however small.

[Effortless action through long effort](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Effortless%20action%20through%20long%20effort): effortlessness in action as the far end of long effort, skill matured until the act stops feeling like an act.

[Designing for virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Designing%20for%20virtue) and [Designing the environment for virtue](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=Designing%20the%20environment%20for%20virtue): the modern work on designing situations for virtue rather than relying on willpower.

## Where the traditions genuinely disagree

### Does knowing the good suffice for doing it?

The shared question: where does right conduct come from? Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action holds that genuinely knowing already issues in acting:

> "There have never been people who know but do not act. Those who are supposed to know but do not act simply do not yet know." ([Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Wang Yangming (1472—1529)"](https://iep.utm.edu/wangyang/)): Scholarly reference; quoted passages checked.

Aristotle's account of virtue of character holds that conduct is formed by doing, and that the one who knows and does not act needs practice rather than more knowledge:

> "men become builders by building and lyreplayers by playing the lyre; so too we become just by doing just acts, temperate by doing temperate acts, brave by doing brave acts." ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.1, Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html)): Primary text; quoted passages checked. The quotation begins mid-sentence at "men", as the raw text reads "e.g. men become builders".

The two positions answer the same question and part cleanly: for Wang, the person who fails to act fails to know, and the remedy is removing the delusion that separates them; for Aristotle, we are adapted by nature to receive the virtues and made perfect by habit, and the remedy is repeated doing. What remains unresolved is whether these are two descriptions of one training or two different accounts of the person; the compendium holds materials on both sides, the spontaneous-right-action card on Wang's and the virtue-rehearsal kernels below on Aristotle's, and does not settle the matter.

## Practice: variation, selection, retention

::Variation:: What tends to need varying on this theme is the default setting of bodily conduct: harm done by convenience, giving governed by mood, fidelity held only while feeling holds, courage postponed until the moment passes. The adaptive direction is the factor's own shape, the non-harm floor kept under whatever else is undertaken, and the counterparts, compassion, generosity, fidelity, practiced above it.

::Selection:: The kernels the compendium holds on this theme, each a selected source of that variation:

1. [K250 — Virtue rehearsal](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K250%20%E2%80%94%20Virtue%20rehearsal): pick one virtue and practise it in small daily acts that get written down, drawing on Aristotle's habituation teaching and Franklin's chart.

2. [K255 — Virtue journaling](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K255%20%E2%80%94%20Virtue%20journaling): each evening, one sentence, the virtue, the situation, the action; a training log rather than a diary.

3. [K263 — Post-action phronesis review](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K263%20%E2%80%94%20Post-action%20phronesis%20review): after a significant action, four questions, the intention, the outcome, the gap, the revision.

4. [K266 — Impression-judgement-action log](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K266%20%E2%80%94%20Impression-judgement-action%20log): a three-column log for one week, the impression, the judgement, the action, training the gap Epictetus puts at the root of disturbance.

5. [K184 — Opposite action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K184%20%E2%80%94%20Opposite%20action): when an emotion urges a behaviour that runs counter to your values, do the opposite; the action does not deny the emotion, it refuses to let it drive.

6. [K152 — Committed action](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K152%20%E2%80%94%20Committed%20action): one action today consistent with a chosen value, however small; the action matters more than the feeling.

7. [K290 — Prosocial courage drill](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K290%20%E2%80%94%20Prosocial%20courage%20drill): a standing weekly slot for speaking up where unfairness would normally be met with silence, converting the act from a decision under pressure into a decision already made.

8. [K216 — Nonviolent communication](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=K216%20%E2%80%94%20Nonviolent%20communication): the four-step structure for conflict, observation, feeling, need, request, giving dispute a shared language instead of a counterattack.

::Retention:: Retention is what people keep because it works for them. A reader who keeps the evening sentence, or the weekly slot, or the three-column log has what the theme offered; what stays is a matter of fit between the practice and the life it enters.

## Story: the stories that carry the theme

The compendium's stories that best carry this theme's aspects, each retold in a line; the full telling lives on the card.

### The stopped harm

[ST405 — Angulimala Is Told to Bear the Stones](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST405%20%E2%80%94%20Angulimala%20Is%20Told%20to%20Bear%20the%20Stones): Angulimala, the bandit wearing a garland of fingers, cannot catch the Buddha walking at normal pace, hears the explanation that the Buddha has stopped "once & for all" having cast off violence toward all living beings, and throws his sword over a cliff; the harm stops, and the stones he later takes on his alms round are the price the town exacts from a changed man.

### Harm discovered late

[ST087 — Schindler's List](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST087%20%E2%80%94%20Schindler%27s%20List): Schindler arrives in Kraków as a profiteer and ends with his entire fortune spent buying lives; the film does not depict him as having become good, it depicts him discovering too late what the money had been for all along.

### Taking for others

[ST295 — Prometheus Steals](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST295%20%E2%80%94%20Prometheus%20Steals): Prometheus steals fire from the gods and gives it to humanity, and is chained to a rock with his liver eaten daily; the myth holds the theft as the creative act and the punishment as the permanent cost of it, a deliberate tension with this factor's second restraint.

### Rules and situations

[ST384 — The Tree Is Spared; the Goose Is Killed](https://clemvillatte.github.io/flourishing-compendium/#card=ST384%20%E2%80%94%20The%20Tree%20Is%20Spared%3B%20the%20Goose%20Is%20Killed): a tree is spared because its wood is useless and a goose is killed because it cannot cackle; Zhuangzi's answer to his disciples challenges both a fixed strategy and a supposedly safe midpoint, a rule that protects in one setting may fail in the next.

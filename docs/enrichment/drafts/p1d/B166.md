> **Principle 166** — Chop wood, carry water

The source of the phrase is Layman Pang (Pang Yun, 740-808), a Tang-dynasty Chan layman. Asked by the master Shitou what his daily activities had been since arriving, Pang said he could not open his mouth about them; pressed, he answered:

> "How wondrous, how miraculous - chopping wood and carrying water." (Layman Pang, [Encounter Dialogues and Verses of Layman Pangyun, tr. Satyavayu](https://terebess.hu/zen/pang.html))

His verse on the same subject describes his daily activity as nothing unusual, just spontaneous harmony, neither grasping nor rejecting, and names fetching water and carrying firewood as his supernatural powers and miraculous activity ([Layman Pang, Encounter Dialogues and Verses of Layman Pangyun](https://terebess.hu/zen/pang.html)). Read as practical advice: the practice is not in addition to the chores; it is the attention brought to them.

(Layman Pang; Zen tradition.)

**Plain words**
The point is not that chores are holy in some disguised way. It is that the attention you bring to a task is a variable the task does not fix. The same bucket of water can be hauled while rehearsing an argument or while carrying water. Pang's claim is that the second way of hauling is the marvelous activity, and nothing extra is required.

*For example:*
Suppose you are washing dishes. The task takes the same time whether you rehearse yesterday's argument or attend to the water and the pan; the pan does not care either way. The principle is about where the attention is during the washing.

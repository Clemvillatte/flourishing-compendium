> **Principle 192** — Begin the day with intention; end it with review

The root text is the Buddha's instruction to Rahula in MN 61: reflect on an intended action before doing it, again while doing it, and once more after it is done, asking whether it leads to self-affliction, the affliction of others, or both ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)).

> "Thus, Rahula, you should train yourself: 'I will purify my bodily actions through repeated reflection. I will purify my verbal actions through repeated reflection. I will purify my mental actions through repeated reflection.'" (MN 61, [Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html))

The Dhammapada adds that the wise remove their own impurities one by one, little by little, moment by moment, as a smith removes dross from silver ([Dhp 239, Buddharakkhita](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/dhp.18.budd.html)).

The morning-intention, evening-review shape is the compendium's distillation of this: the before-during-after reflection applied at the day's scale. MN 61's own after-action instruction has two branches: a faulty action is confessed and restraint exercised for the future, a clean one leaves you mentally refreshed and joyful, training day and night in skillful qualities ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). For a faulty mental action, the instruction is to feel distressed, ashamed and disgusted with it, then exercise restraint ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)).

(MN 61; Dhammapada 239.)

**Plain words**
The sutta asks for three looks at anything you do: before, during and after. The third look is not a verdict on you; it is information for the next round. The morning line and the evening line are the two ends of that: one sets what you will watch for, the other collects what happened. The Dhammapada's smith removes dross one bit at a time; the review works the same way.

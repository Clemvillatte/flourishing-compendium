> **Principle 81** — Cultivate psychological flexibility

ACT names its general goal as increasing psychological flexibility, which its authors define as:

> "the ability to contact the present moment more fully as a conscious human being, and to change or persist in behavior when doing so serves valued ends" (Hayes et al., 2006, "Six Core Processes of ACT")

The model locates suffering in two habits. One is experiential avoidance, "the attempt to alter the form, frequency, or situational sensitivity of private events even when doing so causes behavioral harm" (Hayes et al., 2006, p. 7). The authors argue that avoidance backfires: "attempts to avoid uncomfortable private events tend to increase their functional importance" and to "narrow the range of behaviors that are possible" (p. 7). Values, the "long term desired qualities of life," take a backseat to "being right, looking good, feeling good, defending a conceptualized self" (p. 7). Six trained processes build the alternative: acceptance, [[Cognitive defusion|cognitive defusion]], contact with the present moment, self-as-context, [[Values clarification|values clarification]], and committed action.

Evidence for the construct is largely correlational: a meta-analysis of 151 studies across at least 35 countries found that inflexibility, measured with Acceptance and Action Questionnaire variants, is associated with worse well-being, r = -.47 (Ong et al., 2024). The questionnaire itself is contested; a review of ACT process research concludes the AAQ-II "performs more as a measure of distress/neuroticism than acceptance" (Arch et al., 2023). (Hayes et al., 2006; Ruiz, 2010; Gloster et al., 2020.)

**Plain words**
Suppose a friend is grieving and the phone call keeps getting postponed because it feels awkward. The awkwardness is real, but so is the wish to help. The rigid pattern settles the matter by avoidance: no call, no discomfort, and no help either. Flexibility holds both at once: the discomfort can stay, and the decision about what to do goes to what the friend needs. A call tonight might be right; a message now and a visit later might fit better. The right action can change as the situation changes, with the awkward feeling still there.

*For example:* Feeling anxious before the meeting and going anyway, with the anxiety along for the ride.

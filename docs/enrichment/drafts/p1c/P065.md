James's radical empiricism starts from a rule about what counts as real:

> "To be radical, an empiricism must neither admit into its constructions any element that is not directly experienced, nor exclude from them any element that is directly experienced. For such a philosophy, the relations that connect experiences must themselves be experienced relations, and any kind of relation experienced must be accounted as 'real' as any thing else in the system." (James, A World of Pure Experience, 1904)

The rule aims at a habit James thought philosophy had absorbed from ordinary empiricism, which "has always shown a tendency to do away with the connections of things, and to insist most on the disjunctions." His counter is that the prepositions and conjunctions are data, not glue added later by thought: "Philosophy has always turned on grammatical particles. With, near, next, like, from, towards, against, because, for, through, my -- these words designate types of conjunctive relation arranged in a roughly ascending order of intimacy and inclusiveness." The "with" you walk into is one of those particles. (James, Essays in Radical Empiricism, 1912.)

**Plain words**
Two people sit in a kitchen and say nothing. You walk in and know, before anyone speaks, that something has happened between them. Where did that knowledge come from? The usual answer is inference: you noticed clues and worked it out. James's answer is that the between itself showed up in experience, the same way the furniture and the light did. The principle's instruction follows: when the connection is what you are experiencing, report it as data, on the same level as anything else in the room.

*For example:* Entering the kitchen after the argument — nobody says anything, and you experience the between directly. The relation is as real as the furniture.

Literary figure used as a parable · William James, "The Laws of Habit," in Talks to Teachers (1899).

James, speaking to teachers, closes his talk on habit with a character from the stage: "The drunken Rip Van Winkle, in Jefferson's play, excuses himself for every fresh dereliction by saying, 'I won't count this time!'" ([James, Talks to Teachers, ch. VIII](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16287/pg16287.txt)).

James does not mock Rip. He answers him. "Well, he may not count it, and a kind Heaven may not count it; but it is being counted none the less." The counting is done, James says, "Down among his nerve-cells and fibres," where "the molecules are counting it, registering and storing it up to be used against him when the next temptation comes." And: "Nothing we ever do is, in strict scientific literalness, wiped out" ([James](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16287/pg16287.txt)).

Then James turns the argument over. "As we become permanent drunkards by so many separate drinks, so we become saints in the moral, and authorities and experts in the practical and scientific spheres, by so many separate acts and hours of work." The young person who keeps "faithfully busy each hour of the working day" may "safely leave the final result to itself." The competence builds itself up: "Silently, between all the details of his business, the power of judging in all that class of matter will have built itself up within him" ([James](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/16287/pg16287.txt)).

The compendium reads this beside P056 and P038. Rip's excuse, that this one does not count, is a familiar one. James's reply is that nothing is exempt, and that this is good news as well as a warning.

Limits: this is a passing illustration. James is using a stage character, and the play was not opened here, so nothing is claimed about Rip's lines beyond what James quotes. The "molecules" are 1899 language for what James took to be the physiology of habit, and the page does not treat them as a modern account. James himself allows that habits can be broken by "critical and revolutionary experiences" earlier in the same passage.

**Principles:**
- [[P056 — Your character is your habits]]: the small act that "does not count" is counted.
- [[P038 — Make habit your flywheel]]: the same law builds competence from separate acts and hours.

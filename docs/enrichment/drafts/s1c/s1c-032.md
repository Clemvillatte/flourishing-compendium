Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 5, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Shentu Jia, a man whose foot was cut off as a punishment, studies with Zichan of Zheng under the same master. Zichan tells him: "If I leave first, you wait awhile before going out; when you leave first, I will wait." The next day, side by side on one mat, he says it again, and adds that Shentu Jia should show deference to a chief minister ([Eno, §5.2](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). Legge has Zichan ask whether he counts himself equal to "official rank" ([Legge, ch. 5 §2](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Shentu Jia refuses the premise. At their master's gate, he asks, could there be a chief minister? He then says: "A polished mirror is bright because no dust has settled on it." Those who dwell long among worthy men, he says, are left without a flaw, so how can Zichan talk like this at their master's gate? Zichan answers with an insult: in that state, does Shentu Jia still try to compete with Emperor Yao in goodness? Shentu Jia does not defend his offence. He says plenty of people laugh at him because his leg is gone and theirs are whole, "and it does make my blood boil, but when I come here to the Master’s place I’m able to cast my rage aside and return without it" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Then the turn: "I have been the Master’s pupil for nineteen years and never has he looked at me as an amputee. Now you and I are here to wander in the realms that lie deep within our bodily forms, yet you judge me according to the outer appearance of my body." Zichan's "brow furrowed" and he "altered his expression and stance." In Legge: "You need not, Sir, say anything more about it" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content); [Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The compendium reads this beside D096 and D098. Shentu Jia's acceptance includes the admission that anger still arises, and a place where it can be put down.

Limits: the translations differ on the nineteen years. Eno has the master never looking at him "as an amputee"; Legge has Shentu Jia not knowing "that I am without my feet." Eno notes that Zichan was a real person and that the master's name is not a real one. The text does not show a punishment as fair, and Shentu Jia's calm about fate should not be taken as a general instruction for people who have suffered injury or injustice.

**Principles:**
- [[D096 — Don't let the outer displace the inner]]: "you judge me according to the outer appearance of my body."
- [[D098 — Accept what destiny brings (li ming)]]: the archer image and the admission that anger still comes.

Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 17, in Giles's and Legge's translations.

Zhuangzi and Huizi are walking on a bridge over the river Hao. Zhuangzi says: "See how the minnows are darting about! That is the pleasure of fishes" ([Giles, ch. 17](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt)). Legge has Zhuangzi say that the fish "play about at their ease - that is the enjoyment of fishes." Huizi answers: "You are not a fish; how do you know what constitutes the enjoyment of fishes?" ([Legge, ch. 17 §13](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Zhuangzi hands it back: "You are not I. How do you know that I do not know what constitutes the enjoyment of fishes?" Huizi follows his own logic: if he, not being Zhuangzi, cannot know what Zhuangzi knows, then Zhuangzi, not being a fish, cannot know what the fish enjoys. In Legge's version Huizi concedes that he does not fully know Zhuangzi, but says the argument stands against Zhuangzi knowing the fish ([Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf); [Giles](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt)).

Zhuangzi's final move is to go back to the beginning. "Let us keep to your original question." In Giles: "Your very question shows that you knew I knew." And then: "I knew it from my own feelings on this bridge." The question was asked from the bridge, and so is the answer. Neither man concedes the other's point in the text ([Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf); [Giles](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/59709/pg59709.txt)).

The compendium reads this beside P034 and P008. Zhuangzi does not claim to see the fish from nowhere. He claims a view from a place and a mood, and he says so.

Limits: the last reply turns on the word "how" in the question; Giles marks this with a note on "how" and "knew". Legge adds a parenthesis, "(from our enjoying ourselves together)," that is his own reading, and Giles's closing note, "From my own feelings above the bridge I infer those of the fishes," is Giles's commentary, not the text. The story does not settle whether we can know another creature's inner state, and the page does not claim it does.

**Principles:**
- [[P034 — Know that every view is from somewhere]]: "I knew it from my own feelings on this bridge."
- [[P008 — Take perspectives seriously — the human measure is real]]: both positions stay standing.

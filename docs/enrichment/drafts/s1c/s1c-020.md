Early Buddhist discourse with a narrative frame · Digha Nikaya 16, in Vajira and Story's translation, cross-read with Sujato's.

At Beluva, near Vesali, the Buddha sends the monks off to wait out the rains retreat among their friends and stays behind with Ananda. A severe illness comes on him, "sharp and deadly pains," and he endures it mindfully. Then he decides it would not be fitting to die without taking leave of the community, so he suppresses the illness "by strength of will" and lives on ([Vajira and Story, DN 16 §§27-30](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.16.1-6.vaji.html)).

When he recovers and sits in the shade, Ananda tells him how it was to watch. His body became "weak as a creeper," and what comforted him was the thought that the Buddha would not die "until he had given some last instructions respecting the community." The Buddha asks: "What more does the community of bhikkhus expect from me, Ananda?" He has taught openly and has no teacher's "closed fist"; Sujato puts it as "The Realized One doesn’t have the closed fist of a tutor when it comes to the teachings" ([Sujato, DN 16 §12](https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato)). Only someone who thinks he should lead the community would have instructions to give, and the Buddha says he has no such thought.

Then the cart. "Even as an old cart, Ananda, is held together with much difficulty, so the body of the Tathagata is kept going only with supports." Sujato's version is a decrepit cart kept going by a rope. Therefore, he says, "be islands unto yourselves, refuges unto yourselves, seeking no external refuge", with the Dhamma as island and refuge ([Vajira and Story](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/dn/dn.16.1-6.vaji.html)). Both versions then spell out how: by contemplating body, feelings, mind and phenomena, earnest and mindful, rid of desire and sorrow for the world. Sujato translates the instruction as "live as your own island, your own refuge, with no other refuge" ([Sujato](https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato)).

Limits: this is also a leader's farewell, delivered by someone who has just made a conscious choice to stay alive. In the very next section, Sujato's heading calls it "An Obvious Hint": the Buddha says three times that he could live out the full lifespan if asked, and Ananda "didn’t get it" ([Sujato §13](https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato)). The text gives its own reading of that failure, that Ananda's mind was possessed by Mara. The two translations render the Pali dipa as "island" (the choice of the lamp image in B195's title is discussed on that page), and the Pali was not read here.

- [[B194 — Be your own island (attadipa)]]: the instruction, in its scene.

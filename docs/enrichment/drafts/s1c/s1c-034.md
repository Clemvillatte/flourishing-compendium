Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 5, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Shushan the Toeless, a man of Lu whose foot was cut off, comes hobbling to visit Confucius. Confucius greets him with: "You failed to be careful, and having run afoul of trouble this is the result! Isn’t it too late to be coming to see me?" ([Eno, §5.3](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Shushan does not argue about the past. He says he lost the foot by carelessness, "I have come to you now because I still possess something more important than a foot," and adds that he had taken Confucius for heaven and earth, which cover and carry everything: "how could I have known you would turn out to be like this?" Confucius answers at once, "I have behaved crassly," and asks him to come in. Legge's Confucius says: "I am but a poor creature." But Shushan leaves ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content); [Legge, ch. 5 §3](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Confucius turns to his disciples and holds Shushan up as an example. Shushan, meanwhile, tells Lao Dan that Confucius is far from perfect, and "He’s just hoping to become famous through some bizarre and illusory process." In Legge: "He is seeking to have the reputation of being an extraordinary and marvellous man, and does not know that the Perfect man considers this to be as handcuffs and fetters to him." Lao Dan asks whether Shushan could not simply have led Confucius to see life and death as one thread. Shushan: "Heaven is punishing him" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content); [Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The compendium reads this beside D185 and D184. Confucius repairs his manner in a sentence. The criticism that follows is about something quieter: the pull of being known as a good teacher.

Limits: Confucius is a foil here, and the text is not a fair report of the historical Confucius. Eno notes that legend has the young Confucius visiting Laozi. The story ends without release, and Shushan's judgment of Confucius is delivered behind his back, to a third party. Shushan, a man with a punished body, is the one the text lets say what matters.

**Principles:**
- [[D185 — Prefer being to seeming]]: Shushan's charge that Confucius wants the reputation of a marvel.
- [[D184 — Don't over-cultivate the appearance of virtue]]: the "handcuffs and fetters" of reputation.

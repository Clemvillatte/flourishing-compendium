Philosophical anecdote · Zhuangzi, chapter 6, in Robert Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Yi Erzi visits Xu You. Xu You asks what Yao has taught him. Yi Erzi says Yao told him to submit his whole person to humanity and righteousness, and to state clearly what is right and what is wrong. "Why have you come, then?" says Xu You. "Yao has already branded your face with humanity and righteousness and sliced off your nose with right and wrong." How will he then roam along the free path of change? ([Eno, §6.7](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). Eno notes that branding the face and cutting off the nose were mutilating punishments in the early Chinese legal code, so the image is of a lasting mark, put on from outside.

Yi Erzi does not give up. "I’m willing to roam along its borders." Xu You refuses: a blind man cannot enjoy a lovely face. Yi Erzi replies that Wuzhuang, Ju Liang and the Yellow Emperor each lost something, and were reshaped "in the smelter’s cauldron and by smith’s hammer." Who is to say the Creator will not "fade my brand and patch my nose and restore me to wholeness?" Legge's Xu You puts the same charge as the brand of "benevolence and righteousness" and the cut-off nose of "his right and wrong" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content); [Legge, ch. 6](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

Xu You answers: "I cannot be sure." And he describes his own teacher, who "mixes together the myriad things of the world like a dish of pickles, but does not present his recipe as right (yi)." In Legge, "He gives to all things their blended qualities, and does not count it any righteousness." That teacher provides sustenance to everything and does not see it as ren, humaneness ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content); [Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

The compendium reads this beside P044 and D067. Yi Erzi's request is to be unmarked, and Xu You's honest answer is that nobody can promise that. What is described instead is a giver who keeps no account of the giving.

Limits: the text gives no outcome, and does not say whether Yi Erzi stayed. Yao's teaching is presented from a Zhuangzi-ist point of view and is a caricature of what a Confucian would say, so the story should not be used against moral commitment as such. Eno also notes that the stories of Wuzhuang and Ju Liang are lost, so the reply about being reshaped cannot be fully read.

**Principles:**
- [[P044 — Unlearn on purpose]]: the wish to have the brand faded, and Xu You's refusal to promise it.
- [[D067 — Don't claim credit]]: a teacher who "does not present his recipe as right."

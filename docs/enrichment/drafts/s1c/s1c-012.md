Philosophical anecdote with humour · Zhuangzi, chapter 6, in Eno's translation, cross-read with Legge's.

Ziyu, Zisi, Zili and Zilai are friends who laugh together at the question of who can see nothingness as the head, life as the spine and death as the rump. Soon Ziyu falls ill ([Eno, §6.4](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). When Zisi visits, Ziyu says, "How remarkable!" and then, "The Creator of things is making me into this hooked shape." A hump has thrust up from his back, his shoulders rise above his head, and his pigtail points at the sky. "Yet he was calm at heart and unconcerned."

"Crawling to the well, he looked in at his reflection." What he says is: "Oh, my! The Creator’s made me even more crooked!" Zisi asks whether he resents it. "Why, no! What is there to resent?" Perhaps his left arm will become a rooster and keep watch at night, or his buttocks wheels, and "I’ll ascend into the sky with my spirit as my horse!" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

Then Zilai lies gasping, near death, with his family wailing around him. Zili arrives and tells them to stand back, "Don’t disturb the process of change!" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)). Legge's version of the same line is "Hush! Get out of the way! Do not disturb him as he is passing through his change" ([Legge, ch. 6 §5](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Zilai answers that yin and yang are as much a person's parents as his own, and he follows them as a child would. Then the image: if a smith were casting metal and the metal leapt up and said "I insist on becoming a Moye-type sword!", the smith would find it inauspicious metal. A person who says "I won’t be anything but a man!" is the same. Heaven and earth are a great furnace, he says, and the Creator a smith. "My life complete, I will fall asleep, and then suddenly, I will wake up" ([Eno](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/5dc89a23-c6bf-4190-a1e4-6762dc5359cc/content)).

The compendium reads the scenes together under D098 and D108. Ziyu looks at the crooked body in the water and finds it funny and strange, and neither resents nor denies it. Zilai gives the fullest argument for accepting whatever the furnace does.

Limits: Eno notes the Creator is probably "a literary rather than a religious object." The story does not say what Zilai's wife and children felt about being sent out of the room. It also does not say that every illness should be met with a joke. The text gives no later scene for either man.

**Principles:**
- [[D098 — Accept what destiny brings (li ming)]]: Zilai's furnace-and-smith argument.
- [[D108 — Accept imperfection]]: Ziyu looks at his reflection and carries on.

A fixed view of the self is the belief that who you are is a set quantity: abilities, worth or personality traits that cannot be grown. The seed names the two research lines behind the label, Dweck's implicit theories (2006) and Crocker's contingencies of self-worth (2002), and the shared prediction, the seed's own claim, is fragility: a self that must be proved rather than developed breaks when it is tested.

### Two routes to fragility

Dweck's route is attribution. Children who read failure as evidence of fixed ability show the "helpless" pattern, "more negative affect, falling expectancies, less effective strategies and lower persistence," and this pattern "did not in any way stem from lower ability" ([Dweck, implicit theories chapter, 2011](http://www.rpforschools.net/Faith/Mindset/Dweck%20Implicit%20Theories%202011.pdf)). Her later summary of the fixed view: "intelligence was always at stake," so that "challenges, setbacks, and even high effort were risky" ([Dweck 2017, Child Development Perspectives](https://eclass.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP387/Dweck-2017-Child_Development_Perspectives.pdf)).

Crocker's route is contingency. Contingencies of self-worth are "the domains in which success or failure leads to increases or decreases in self-esteem" ([Crocker 2002, The Costs of Seeking Self-Esteem](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Costs-of-Seeking-Self%E2%80%93Esteem-Crocker/9d41d23c3f140cb520a222c3cca775fc2ac8fcb4)). Her account of the cost: external contingencies "require validation from others and are unreliable as a basis of self-esteem." The evidence follows the prediction: students who stake worth on academic competence show larger self-esteem swings in response to grades and to graduate-school acceptances and rejections ([Crocker lab, CSW research page](https://u.osu.edu/crockerlab/contingencies-of-self-worth-csw-scale/)).

### The limits of the evidence

The fragility prediction has been tested and partially failed. Burgoyne, Hambrick and Macnamara (2020) tested six premises of mindset theory in a large university sample and "no support was found for most premises"; in their test of performance after failure, "students with more of a fixed mind-set outperformed students with more of a growth mind-set" in one sample, and the mindset effect was not significant after controlling for cognitive ability ([Burgoyne et al. 2020](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2020/Burgoyne_Hambrick_Macnamara_2020_Mindset.pdf)). Their conclusion is scoped, and worth quoting: "the results of our investigation and others suggest that the theoretical basis for these programs may not be sound."

### In the compendium

The compendium treats the fixed view as a diagnosis with an antidote. P001 builds life on becoming rather than being, SE062 cultivates a growth orientation, and CO065 treats wisdom itself as a trainable skill rather than a fixed trait. The Buddhist line holds the whole framework lightly: B003 on anatta, B126 on holding views lightly, B138 on emptying the relations too. R105 practises narrative identity, the self as an authored story rather than a verdict, SE073 handles the bicultural and marginalised selves that fixed views flatten, and K147's "I am becoming" reframes and P184's twice-born conversion mark the compendium's two routes out.

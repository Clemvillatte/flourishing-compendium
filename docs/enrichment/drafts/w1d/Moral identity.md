Moral identity is the degree to which morality is part of who one takes oneself to be. Blasi's 1984 paper, with Damon's parallel 1984 work, established the construct in post-Kohlberg moral psychology to close the gap between moral judgment and moral action: people can reason well and still not act, and the difference, on his self model of moral functioning, is whether moral notions are "central, essential, and important to one's self-understanding" ([Lapsley, Moral Self-Identity as the Aim of Education](https://maplab.nd.edu/assets/224376/lapsley_stey_moral_self_as_aim_of_education_956_06_hb_moral_06.pdf); [Blasi 1984, cited in UBC thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/download/pdf/831/1.0089559/1)).

### The self model

Blasi's model has four components: after a moral judgment, the person judges whether the behavior is personally theirs to do; the judgment of responsibility binds when the behavior is related to the self; the motivation to act comes from the drive for consistency between self and behavior; and follow-through depends on inhibiting the defensive strategies that would loosen the consistency ([Blasi 1984, four components, UBC thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/download/pdf/831/1.0089559/1)). The engine is self-consistency: "self-consistency is the basic motivational spring of moral action" ([Blasi 1993, p. 99, quoted in the UBC exposition of the four components](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/download/pdf/831/1.0089559/1)). Failing to act on what is central to identity is "to risk self-betrayal" ([Lapsley](https://maplab.nd.edu/assets/224376/lapsley_stey_moral_self_as_aim_of_education_956_06_hb_moral_06.pdf)), and the morally identified can find some acts "simply ‘unthinkable’" ([UBC exposition, citing Blasi 1993](https://open.library.ubc.ca/media/download/pdf/831/1.0089559/1)).

The later "standard model" operationalizes the construct: "Moral identity is the degree to which being a moral person is important to an individual's identity" ([Hardy and Carlo 2011, quoted in Karger commentary](https://karger.com/hde/article/58/3/164/158043/Moral-Identity-and-Developmental-TheoryCommentary)), with Aquino and Reed's centrality research showing the self as "a mélange of different identities of which morality is but one" ([Karger commentary](https://karger.com/hde/article/58/3/164/158043/Moral-Identity-and-Developmental-TheoryCommentary)).

### In the compendium

The principles treat moral identity as the bridge between virtue and action. A004's becoming virtuous by doing virtuous acts is the Aristotelian formation of it; A047 lets phronesis unify the virtues so the identity has a judge. R034's tika carries the Māori form, the question of what is right asked of every decision; R052's yi is the Confucian virtue; R024 grounds moral selfhood in respect for the Dreaming; R103 practises the communitarian critique; and SP009 translates moral judgment into Spinoza's power question, perfection as degree of power of acting rather than blame. B142 warns that attachment to views, even moral views, breeds conflict. The kernel layers V2K and V2K-S01 carry the evidence.

"The eternal contribution" is the compendium's own label, not a term from the tradition: it names an ethical reading of Nietzsche's eternal recurrence, the sense in which BE068, the one principle that carries it, uses it. If this life returns in every detail, then what a person gives in it returns with it, and the part of a life most worth willing back is the part that outlives it ([GS §341](https://americanliterature.com/author/friedrich-nietzsche/book/the-gay-science/book-fourth-sanctus-januarius); [SEP, Nietzsche](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nietzsche/)).

### What the recurrence adds

The demon of The Gay Science section 341 asks whether one would will this life, "once more, and also innumerable times," in the same series and sequence ([§341](https://americanliterature.com/author/friedrich-nietzsche/book/the-gay-science/book-fourth-sanctus-januarius)). The practical reading of that test, after Nehamas, is that the emotional reaction to the prospect of return reveals the value of the life ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nietzsche/)). The contribution reading, as the compendium uses it, turns the same test outward: the question becomes which of a life's works are worth their return, and the candidates are the ones that reach past it, mentoring, institutions, trees planted for people not yet born.

### The psychology next door

Erikson places generativity against stagnation in middle adulthood: "Generativity is the concern of guiding the next generation," the stage's existential question is "Can I Make My Life Count?", people in it "consider what they are leaving behind for their posterity and community," and its virtue is care ([Wikipedia, Erikson's stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erikson%27s_stages_of_psychosocial_development)). The compendium's Generativity theory concept tracks the research programme McAdams and de St. Aubin built on this ground.

### In the compendium

BE068 "Engage in legacy behaviour — contribute beyond yourself" is the concept's single carrier: legacy behaviour, creating things that outlast you, mentoring, contributing to future generations, building institutions, is associated with meaning, generativity and purpose, and its parallels pair the eternal contribution with Erikson's generativity vs stagnation and the proverb about planting trees under whose shade you will never sit.

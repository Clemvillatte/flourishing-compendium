Mind in life is Evan Thompson's thesis that life and mind are deeply continuous. The book Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind (2007) opens with the claim in Thompson's own words: "The theme of this book is the deep continuity of life and mind. Where there is life there is mind, and mind in its most articulated forms belongs to life. Life and mind share a core set of formal or organizational properties, and the formal or organizational properties distinctive of mind are an enriched version of those fundamental to life" ([Thompson, Précis of Mind in Life](https://evanthompson.me/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/thompson_precis_jcs_author_proof.pdf)).

### The argument

The claim's base is autopoiesis, Maturana and Varela's term for a system's continuously producing and maintaining itself, combined with Hans Jonas's phenomenological analysis of life. Living being, Thompson argues, "instantiates a kind of interiority that escapes the objectivist picture of nature," while subjectivity "instantiates a kind of exteriority that escapes the internalist picture of consciousness" ([Précis](https://evanthompson.me/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/thompson_precis_jcs_author_proof.pdf)). Cognition, on this view, is sense-making enacted by a living body coupled with its environment: "Mind is life-like and life is mind-like" ([Thompson 2007, p. 128, quoted in the life-mind continuity literature](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/23468/1/LMC%20ver.%202.5.pdf)). The human mind is embodied in the whole organism and in the world, its three intertwined modes of bodily activity being "self-regulation, sensorimotor coupling, and intersubjective interaction" ([Précis](https://evanthompson.me/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/thompson_precis_jcs_author_proof.pdf)).

The thesis has serious critics. Michael Wheeler argues the notion needs disambiguation, reading Thompson's position as making life an enrichment of cognition rather than the reverse ([Wheeler, Mind in Life or Life in Mind?](https://storre.stir.ac.uk/bitstream/1893/11393/1/wheeler_mind_life_life_mind_storre.pdf)); others resist ascribing phenomenological sense-making to all living things, reserving it for sentient creatures ([Phenomenology and Naturalism in Autopoietic and Radical Enactivism, PhilArchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/KEEPAN)).

### In the compendium

The principles take the thesis as an ontology of practice. P139 states cognition as embodied sense-making and P143 thinks with the whole body; P140 treats mind and life as continuous, the direct application. B020's threefold training, S123's daily reading and reflection, D191's lifelong beginner's pace, CO070's epistemic curiosity and P129's sympathetic understanding are the mind-in-life practices; ST082's Huineng hearing one line is the thesis told as a story; and AT075 marks the clinical boundary, where attention problems need evaluation, not interpretation.

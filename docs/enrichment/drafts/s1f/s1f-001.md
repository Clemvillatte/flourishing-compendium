Fiction · Nikolai Gogol, "The Mantle" ("The Overcoat"), 1842; the Claud Field translation ([Project Gutenberg ebook 36238](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt))

In Gogol's tale a St Petersburg copy clerk lives for decades as pure mechanism, comes alive for one year through the chosen acts a new cloak demands, and sinks back when the cloak is stolen. Beside P193 the story dramatizes the principle's two poles: consciousness as choice, and the dimness of a life with no options in it ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

Akaky Akakievich copies. However many directors come and go, "he was always seen in the same spot, in the same attitude, busy with the same work" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). Superiors hand him papers without a word, colleagues mock him, and "outside this copying nothing appeared to exist for him" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). Offered work that required judgement, changing headings and pronouns, it confused and exhausted him until he begged: "No! Please give me again something to copy" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

The turn arrives through the cloak. Petrovitch the tailor refuses to repair it; a new one costs eighty roubles, a fifth of Akaki's yearly income. He plans: no evening tea, copying in the landlady's room to save fuel, a kopeck into a money-box for every rouble. The narrator marks the change: "From that time he became more lively, and his character grew stronger, like that of a man who has set a goal before himself which he will reach at all costs" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). "All that was indecisive and vague in his gait and gestures had disappeared. A new fire began to gleam in his eyes" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). He visits the tailor monthly to settle the cloth, the price and the colour, and the day the finished cloak arrives is "the most solemn in Akaki's life" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

The loss is as quick. After a celebration evening, men rob him on a dark square. The sentinel saw it and thought they were friends; the authorities route his petition through secretaries; a personage scolds him nearly unconscious, calling it "disgraceful to see young people so rebellious towards their superiors" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). Fever follows, with "his thoughts... continually revolving round a cloak" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). He dies leaving quills, socks, buttons and the old coat, and "the great city of St Petersburg continued its life as though he had never existed" ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)).

Gogol adds a coda: a ghost strips cloaks from passers-by "without any regard for rank or title" until it takes the personage's own cloak and vanishes ([Field translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/36238/36238.txt)). The compendium's reading for P193: the copying life is the mechanism pole, days with no fork in them; the cloak project is consciousness as choice, a goal that fills the fringe of possible action with small decisions; the ghost is the parody, taking without choosing. What the page does not carry is the principle's everyday phenomenology of attention; it makes no factual claim about clerks or poverty.

**Principles:**
- [[P193 — Consciousness is choice; where there is no choice, there is mechanism|P193 Consciousness is choice]]: Akaky's year of the cloak is the chosen-goal pole of the principle; the copying life before it and the bereft run of days after it are the mechanism pole.

Canonical narrative, translated · Anathapindika, a dying householder in pain, and Sariputta's instruction on non-clinging through the whole range of experience

The Satipatthana Sutta opens with the claim that the four frames of reference are the direct path for the purification of beings ([MN 10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)). MN 143 gives a different register of the same non-clinging work: a dying householder, an instruction, and a request.

Anathapindika is gravely ill, and the sutta records his own description of the pain: his painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding, and he describes the pain as a strong man splitting his head open with a sharp sword, and as violent winds cutting through his head ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)).

Sariputta's instruction, which the page presents as a table of rows, applies one move to the whole range of experience: do not cling to the eye, the ear, the nose, the tongue, the body, the mind; do not cling to forms, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and mind-objects; do not cling to the consciousnesses, the contacts, and the feelings born of contact; do not cling to the four elements, the five aggregates, the formless spheres, this world and the world beyond; and do not cling to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, encountered, sought after, and examined by the mind. In each case, the instruction adds that consciousness is not to be dependent on what is not clung to ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)).

Then the exchange. "When this was said, Anathapindika wept and shed tears." Ananda asks him whether he is foundering. Anathapindika answers: "I am not foundering, Ananda, I am not sinking. But although I have long waited upon the Teacher and bhikkhus worthy of esteem, never before have I heard such a talk on the Dhamma." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)) The sutta records his tears and his own account of them, and this page gives no further cause for the weeping.

Anathapindika's last recorded request runs to the people outside the monasteries: "Well, then, Sariputta, let such talk on the Dhamma be given to lay people clothed in white. There are people with little dust in their eyes who are wasting away through not hearing such talk on the Dhamma. There will be those who will understand." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)) The sutta's own closing line records what follows: "Soon after they had left, the householder Anathapindika died and reappeared in the Tushita heaven." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)) The page records the rebirth as the sutta's account and does not treat it as evidence of what the practice produced.

**Principles:**
- [[B073 — Hold experience loosely (non-attachment)|B073 Hold experience loosely (non-attachment)]]: Sariputta's instruction illustrates non-clinging applied to the whole range of experience in a deathbed case — a dying householder in pain, the non-clinging instruction, and the recorded tears and request; the sutta records his own account of the weeping, and the rebirth in the closing line is the sutta's account, not treated here as evidence of the practice's outcomes. ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

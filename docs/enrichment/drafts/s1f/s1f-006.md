Canonical narrative, translated · Anathapindika, a dying householder in pain, and Sariputta's instruction on non-clinging through the whole range of experience

The householder Anathapindika is afflicted, suffering, and gravely ill, and Sariputta and Ananda come to see him. Sariputta asks him how he is doing. Anathapindika answers with his own description of the pain: "I am not getting well, I am not comfortable. My painful feelings are increasing, not subsiding. Just as if a strong man were splitting my head open with a sharp sword, so too violent winds cut through my head." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

Sariputta tells him not to cling to any part of experience: do not cling to the eye, the ear, the nose, the tongue, the body, the mind; do not cling to forms, sounds, smells, tastes, touches, and mind-objects; do not cling to the consciousnesses, the contacts, and the feelings born of contact; do not cling to the four elements, the five aggregates, the formless spheres, this world and the world beyond; and do not cling to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, encountered, sought after, and examined by the mind. In each case, the instruction adds that consciousness is not to be dependent on what is not clung to ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)).

"When this was said, Anathapindika wept and shed tears." Ananda asks him whether he is foundering. Anathapindika answers: "I am not foundering, Ananda, I am not sinking. But although I have long waited upon the Teacher and bhikkhus worthy of esteem, never before have I heard such a talk on the Dhamma." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

His last recorded request runs to the people outside the monasteries: "Well, then, Sariputta, let such talk on the Dhamma be given to lay people clothed in white. There are people with little dust in their eyes who are wasting away through not hearing such talk on the Dhamma. There will be those who will understand." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

The sutta concludes: "Soon after they had left, the householder Anathapindika died and reappeared in the Tushita heaven." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

**Principles:**
- [[B073 — Hold experience loosely (non-attachment)|B073 Hold experience loosely (non-attachment)]]: Sariputta's instruction illustrates non-clinging applied to the whole range of experience in a deathbed case — a dying householder in pain, the non-clinging instruction, and the recorded tears and request; the rebirth in the closing line is the sutta's account, not treated here as evidence of the practice's outcomes. ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

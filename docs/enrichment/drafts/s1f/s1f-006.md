Canonical narrative, translated · Anathapindika, a dying householder, and Sariputta's instruction through the whole range of experience; with Ananda present and a rebirth recorded

The Satipatthana Sutta gives the four domains as the direct path for the purification of beings ([MN 10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.010.than.html)). MN 143 gives the same coverage in a different register: a dying householder, an instruction, and a weeping.

Sariputta's instruction runs through the field, domain by domain. "I will not cling to the eye; the ear; the nose; the tongue; the body; the mind and my consciousness will not be dependent on the eye; the ear; the nose; the tongue; the body; the mind" ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)). The same move follows for the sense objects, for the sense consciousnesses, for the contacts, for the feelings born of contact, for the four elements, and then for the aggregates: "I will not cling to material form; feeling; perception; formations; consciousness and my consciousness will not be dependent on material form; feeling; perception; formations; consciousness" ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)). The instruction closes with the two worlds and with what the mind can take hold of: "I will not cling to this world; the world beyond and my consciousness will not be dependent on this world; the world beyond" ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)); and "I will not cling to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, encountered, sought after, and examined by the mind and my consciousness will not be dependent on what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, encountered, sought after, and examined by the mind" ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)).

The formula is the instruction, and it is the same move every time: the domain changes and the move does not.

Then the scene turns. "When this was said, Anathapindika wept and shed tears." Ananda asks him whether he is foundering. Anathapindika answers: "I am not foundering, Ananda, I am not sinking. But although I have long waited upon the Teacher and bhikkhus worthy of esteem, never before have I heard such a talk on the Dhamma." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)) The tears carry the practice through grief rather than around it. Anathapindika's last recorded request runs to the people outside the monasteries: "Well, then, Sariputta, let such talk on the Dhamma be given to lay people clothed in white. There are people with little dust in their eyes who are wasting away through not hearing such talk on the Dhamma. There will be those who will understand." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

The sutta's own closing line records what follows: "Soon after they had left, the householder Anathapindika died and reappeared in the Tushita heaven." ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html)) The page records the rebirth as the sutta's account and does not treat it as evidence of what the practice produced.

**Principles:**
- [[B039 — Practice the four foundations of mindfulness (satipatthana)|B039 Practice the four foundations of mindfulness (satipatthana)]]: Sariputta's instruction illustrates the four foundations in a deathbed case — the same non-clinging move through each domain of experience, in a scene that carries the practice through weeping and severe pain; the instruction covers the whole field in the sutta's own terms, and the rebirth recorded in the closing line is the sutta's own account, not treated here as evidence of the practice's outcomes. ([MN 143, Olendzki](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.143x.olen.html))

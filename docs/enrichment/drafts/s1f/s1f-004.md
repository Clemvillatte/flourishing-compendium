Biographical narrative, with the practice manual quoted · 20th century; a Sri Lankan monk in America, and the book that made the satipatthana instruction teachable

The biography page records the start: "Venerable Henepola Gunaratana was ordained at the age of 12 as a Buddhist monk at a small temple in Malandeniya Village in Kurunegala District in Sri Lanka," with higher ordination at 20 in 1947. ([Vipassana Fellowship biography](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_1.html)) He came to the United States in 1968, and the biography closes on the work of the later decades at the Bhavana Society in West Virginia. ([Vipassana Fellowship biography](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_1.html))

The Satipatthana Sutta's claim is that the four frames of reference are the direct path for the purification of beings. ([B011](https://github.com/Clemvillatte/flourishing-compendium/blob/main/content/principles/B011%20%E2%80%94%20Make%20mindfulness%20the%20master%20tool%20(sati).md)) What the sutta gives in framing formula, the manual gives in working instruction. The book's first rule runs against any impulse to fix the problem before seeing it: "if you want to change something, the first thing you have to do is see it the way it is." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The book names the function and gives it a job description. "Mindfulness is a function that disarms distractions, in the same way that a munitions expert might defuse a bomb." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html)) The sorting is what matters, not the object: "It actually makes rather little difference what the object of mindfulness is." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The book's larger claim is stated plainly, in the same register as the sutta: "The purpose of meditation is to achieve uninterrupted mindfulness. Mindfulness, and only mindfulness, produces Enlightenment." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html)) And the claim that makes the faculty trainable: "Mindfulness grows by the exercise of mindfulness. It is like exercising a muscle. Every time you work it, you pump it up just a little. You make it a little stronger." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The book's own summary of the stakes closes the chapter quoted: "Vipassana meditation is not a game. Clear awareness is more than a pleasurable pastime. It is a road up and out of the quagmire in which we are all stuck, the swamp of our own desires and aversions." ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

The compendium reads his account as a carrying case for B011. A monk who spent his teaching life in America, with a manual written for people who would meditate in a spare room and then go to work, took the sutta's framing formula and gave it working instructions. The story does not supply evidence that sati produces awakening; it quotes the book's claim. The chapter quoted is meditation instruction, and the everyday-life application is carried by the biography, not by the chapter's own text.

**Principles:**
- [[B011 — Make mindfulness the master tool (sati)|B011 Make mindfulness the master tool (sati)]]: the book's own passages carry sati as the primary faculty, trainable by use and indifferent to the object; the sutta's four-frames instruction is shown in working form rather than asserted; the chapter quoted does not itself give an everyday-life application, and the claim that mindfulness produces Enlightenment is quoted as the book's assertion, not demonstrated. ([Mindfulness in Plain English](https://www.vipassana.com/meditation/mindfulness_in_plain_english_14.html))

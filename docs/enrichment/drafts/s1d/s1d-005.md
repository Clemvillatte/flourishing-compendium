France, 1800-1828 · biographical account from Jean Itard's Mémoire (1801) and Rapport (1806), with the UQAM/psychaanalyse French text, CYC-Net's English account and Le Monde diplomatique

In 1800 a boy was found in the mountains of the Aveyron, mute and wild, and brought to Paris for examination. The psychiatrist Philippe Pinel pronounced him "an incurable idiot, a hopeless case" ([CYC-Net, after Shattuck 1980](https://cyc-net.org/cyc-online/cycol-0103-mcdermott.html)). A young physician named Itard saw the causes differently: "Loin de les imputer à une quelconque arriération biologique, Itard y voit le résultat de la complète solitude dans laquelle a vécu l'enfant depuis sa naissance", instead of biological retardation, the complete solitude the child had lived in since birth ([Le Monde diplomatique](https://www.monde-diplomatique.fr/mav/131/POPELARD/54353); translations of the French are mine). He took the boy into the Institute for Deaf Mutes, named him Victor, and set out to build a self.

Itard's program, in his own five-point statement, opened with the social: "L'attacher à la vie sociale", attach him to social life, and "réveiller la sensibilité nerveuse... par les vives affections de l'âme", awaken his sensibility by the liveliest affections of the soul ([Itard, Mémoire 1801](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/memoire_et_rapport_sur_victor_aveyron.pdf)). The material the self was built from was named person by person. Victor's affection "is to be noticed above all in his relations with Madame Guerin", the housekeeper who was "always there, physically and emotionally" ([CYC-Net, after Shattuck 1980](https://cyc-net.org/cyc-online/cycol-0103-mcdermott.html)). His first exclamation of joy was borrowed from her: "C'est l'exclamation Oh Dieu ! qu'il a prise de Mme Guérin, et qu'il laisse fréquemment échapper dans ses grandes joies" ([Itard, Mémoire 1801, IV](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/memoire_et_rapport_sur_victor_aveyron.pdf)).

The 1806 report is the honest ledger. The affective faculties had begun to give rise to "quelques-uns de ces sentiments généreux", some of the generous sentiments that make the happiness of the human heart ([Rapport 1806, XLII](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/memoire_et_rapport_sur_victor_aveyron.pdf)). But the speech never came: "Mon espérance fut entièrement déçue", my hope was entirely deceived, "et j'abandonnai mon élève à un mutisme incurable", and I abandoned my pupil to an incurable mutism ([Rapport 1806, XLI](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/memoire_et_rapport_sur_victor_aveyron.pdf)). The education "doit être à jamais incomplète", he concluded, must remain forever incomplete, and the generous passions are "l'heureux fruit de son éducation", the happy fruit of education ([Rapport 1806, conclusion](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/memoire_et_rapport_sur_victor_aveyron.pdf)). Victor lived on with Mme Guérin until his death in 1828 ([Le Monde diplomatique](https://www.monde-diplomatique.fr/mav/131/POPELARD/54353)).

The compendium's reading for P057: Itard's credo is the principle stated in advance of Mead: "l'homme n'est que ce qu'on le fait être", man is only what others make of him, "ses idées ne sont plus à lui", his ideas are no longer his own ([Mémoire 1801](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/memoire_et_rapport_sur_victor_aveyron.pdf)). The case shows both halves of the principle's plain words: the self is a social achievement, and the company you keep is the material it is made from. What Victor had, he had from two people; the record shows almost no peers, and the missing speech marks the material that never arrived in time.

**Principles:**
- [[P057 — Your self is social through and through|P057 Your self is social through and through]]: Victor's case runs Mead's claim as an experiment - the self he gained was assembled from the company available, and the incomplete result shows what a self costs when built from too little company.

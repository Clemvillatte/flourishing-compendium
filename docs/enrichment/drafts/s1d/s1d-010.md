Sutta narrative from the Kalama Sutta (AN 3.65), Pali Canon, in the translations of Thanissaro Bhikkhu and Soma Thera, with the principle file in the compendium working tree

Confused by visiting teachers who praised their own doctrines and attacked others', the Kalamas of Kesaputta asked the Buddha who was telling the truth. He guided them to examine qualities through their consequences, blamelessness and the judgment of the wise. Their question as they put it: "They leave us absolutely uncertain & in doubt: Which of these venerable brahmans & contemplatives are speaking the truth, and which ones are lying?" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)).

Their doubt, he said, fitted the case: "Of course you are uncertain, Kalamas. Of course you are in doubt. When there are reasons for doubt, uncertainty is born" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)). The grounds to set aside: "don't go by reports, by legends, by traditions, by scripture, by logical conjecture, by inference, by analogies, by agreement through pondering views, by probability, or by the thought, 'This contemplative is our teacher'" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)).

He gave them an examination to run. "When greed arises in a person, does it arise for welfare or for harm?" They answered, "For harm, lord." A person overcome by greed "kills living beings, takes what is not given, goes after another person's wife, tells lies, and induces others to do likewise, all of which is for long-term harm & suffering" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)). The same questions covered aversion and delusion. A quality is judged by what it makes a person do and whether the wise praise it.

The positive criterion follows: "When you know for yourselves that, 'These qualities are skillful; these qualities are blameless; these qualities are praised by the wise; these qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to welfare & to happiness' — then you should enter & remain in them" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)). The discourse next describes a disciple pervading every direction with good will, compassion, appreciation and equanimity; with a mind free from hostility, free from ill will, undefiled and pure, that disciple gains four assurances, among them that if there is no world after death and no fruit of actions rightly & wrongly done, "here in the present life I look after myself with ease" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)). The Kalamas took refuge, calling the teaching one that could "carry a lamp into the dark so that those with eyes could see forms" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)).

The compendium's reading for B018: the grounds are set aside as insufficient by themselves, scripture, logic and a teacher's standing included; a belief is then tested by the results it yields in practice and checked against the experience of the wise. Thanissaro's note warns against reading the discourse as a license for private preference: results "must further be checked against the experience of people who are wise" ([AN 3.65, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an03/an03.065.than.html)).

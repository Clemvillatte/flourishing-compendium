> **Principle 16** — Distrust names and labels

The Daodejing opens with the principle as its first lines:

> "The Tao that can be trodden is not the enduring and unchanging Tao. The name that can be named is not the enduring and unchanging name." (Daodejing 1, trans. Legge, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm))

Chapter 32 states the same from the other side, and says when names arrive: "The Tao, considered as unchanging, has no name"; "As soon as it proceeds to action, it has a name. When it once has that name, (men) can know to rest in it" (Daodejing 32, trans. Legge, [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm)). Names are what a thing gets when it is acted on; the chapter also treats a name as something men can rest in.

The Zhuangzi's second chapter makes the parallel claim in its own voice: "The Dao that is displayed is not the Dao" (Zhuangzi II, §10, trans. Legge, [ETH Zurich PDF](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

(DDJ 1, 32; Zhuangzi 2.)

### Plain words

Words are handles, and handles are useful. The trouble is closing your hand on the handle alone. "Procrastinator" freezes one morning's pattern into a self; "failure" freezes one result into a story. The test is whether the word still shows you anything new about the thing. When it stops doing that, put it down and look at what is actually in front of you.

### For example

Someone who starts fine in the mornings but calls herself a procrastinator is being governed by a label her own mornings contradict.

## Interpretation limits (for the ledger)

- DDJ 32's "when it once has that name, (men) can know to rest in it" is presented as the
  chapter's own statement; the page does not claim the DDJ endorses or condemns names
  overall. The "distrust" is the compendium's framing, anchored in DDJ 1's opening lines.
- Zhuangzi II §10's "The Dao that is displayed is not the Dao" is the parallel claim; the
  page does not use the "map is not the territory" wording (Korzybski parallel is inherited
  in the link sections, untouched).
- The procrastinator example is framed as an observation about a contradiction between
  label and mornings, not a claimed psychological outcome.
- The old body's label examples ("success," "failure," "loser," "depression") and the
  "maps, not territory" phrasing (Korzybski's, via the philosophy-parallel link) were
  replaced by source-anchored text.
- Linked concepts read: Cognitive defusion, Language games, Two truths, Pu (the uncarved
  block); their inherited text was not used as evidence.

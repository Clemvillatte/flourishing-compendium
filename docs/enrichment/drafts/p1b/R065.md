> **Principle 327** — Practise the extension of knowledge (zhizhi)

The Great Learning opens with a chain of eight steps, each serving the next. The ancients who
wished to illustrate illustrious virtue throughout the kingdom first ordered well their own
states; ordering the state required regulating the family; regulating the family, cultivating
the person; cultivating the person, rectifying the heart; rectifying the heart, sincerity of
thought; and for that, they "first extended to the utmost their knowledge" ([The Great
Learning, trans. Legge](https://ctext.org/liji/da-xue)). The text then states what that
extension rests on:

> "Such extension of knowledge lay in the investigation of things. Things being investigated,
> knowledge became complete." (The Great Learning, opening passage ("The ancients who
> wished..."), trans. [Legge](https://ctext.org/liji/da-xue))

The classical text ties the completion of knowledge to the investigation of things, and puts
sincere thought downstream of completed knowledge. Zhizhi is not a separate study: it is the
step that lets the rest of the chain, from rectified heart to ordered state, begin.

The Analects passage cited with this principle describes Confucius's own learning, not the
Great Learning's method. Confucius asked Zigong whether he took him for one "who learns many
things and keeps them in memory", and rejected the description: "I seek a unity all pervading"
(Analects 15.3, [Legge](https://ctext.org/analects/wei-ling-gong)).

(The Great Learning; Confucius, The Analects 15.3.)

### Plain words

The text puts examination before judgment. Before your thinking about something can be
sincere, it says, your knowledge of that thing has to be complete, and knowledge becomes
complete through investigating the thing itself. We usually run this backwards: we settle what
we think, then look at the thing only if it goes badly. Acting on the principle means noticing
when your conviction has outrun what you have actually examined, and going to look.

### For example

A modern application, not the classical text's procedure: two colleagues disagree about credit
for a shared project. Before arguing the division, each lists what the other actually
contributed. The claim each makes after that examination is better grounded than the one made
from memory.

## Interpretation limits (for the ledger)

- The Great Learning passage connects investigation of things, completed knowledge, sincere
  thoughts and self-cultivation. It does not rank experience above books, and does not
  prescribe experimental testing. No such claim appears in this draft.
- Analects 15.3 concerns unity in Confucius's learning; it is presented without any bridge to
  zhizhi.
- "Zhizhi is not a separate study: it is the step that lets the rest of the chain begin" rests
  on the text's own sequence ("Their knowledge being complete, their thoughts were sincere").
- The For example section is explicitly marked as a modern application.

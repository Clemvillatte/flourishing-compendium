# E012 review draft

## Proposed body

Husserl's reduction redirects inquiry from the world taken for granted to how that world is experienced. In Ideas I §31, bracketing leaves a conviction intact while withholding its use as a premise; a phenomenologist can examine a perception without first deciding whether its object exists ([Husserl, Kersten translation, UCL extracts](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~uctysar/Husserl%20I-C%20lects%20-%20Ideas%20extracts.htm); [Beyer, §§5–6](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/Win2023/entries/husserl/)).

> "We do not give up the positing we effected" (Ideas I §31, trans. F. Kersten, p. 59, [UCL extracts](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~uctysar/Husserl%20I-C%20lects%20-%20Ideas%20extracts.htm)).

The ongoing task is to sustain this changed direction of inquiry. Robert D. Walsh documents Husserl's warnings about slipping back into the natural attitude, even through apparently innocent spatial metaphors, and reads the reduction as a practice requiring renewed effort ([Walsh, pp. 212–214, 222](https://journals.ku.edu/auslegung/article/download/12981/12277/25612)).

(Husserl, 1954/1970, The Crisis, §§35-38, 71; Husserl, 1913/1982, Ideas I, §§31-33.)

### Plain words

Normally a description says what is there: a tree beside the road. This inquiry asks how it appears: one side is visible, while the rest is expected. Holding that question open requires attention each time a description slips back into simply asserting what exists. Learning the vocabulary once cannot do that work ([Beyer, §§5–6](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/Win2023/entries/husserl/); [Walsh, pp. 212–214](https://journals.ku.edu/auslegung/article/download/12981/12277/25612)).

### For example

As an illustration, describe a cup from one seat, then another. Record the visible handle, the hidden side anticipated, and how the appearances belong to one cup; repeat the inquiry instead of treating a first description as complete.

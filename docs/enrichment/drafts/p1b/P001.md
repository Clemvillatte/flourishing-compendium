> **Principle 1** — Build your life on becoming, not being

The principle's oldest witness is the river fragment. In the numbering scholars use, DK B12, it reads:

> "On those stepping into rivers staying the same other and other waters flow." (Heraclitus, fragment DK B12, trans. [Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/heraclitus/))

The fragment's message, as the Stanford Encyclopedia reads it, "is not that all things are changing so that we cannot encounter them twice, but something much more subtle and profound. It is that some things stay the same only by changing" ([Graham, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/heraclitus/)). The SEP also notes that B12 is the one and only river fragment, the only actual quotation from Heraclitus' book; the famous "cannot step into the same river twice" is a later reading.

The James quote below carries the same point for the mind: consciousness "is nothing jointed; it flows".

(Heraclitus, fragments; Whitehead, Process and Reality, 1929.)

### Plain words

The question "what is it?" asks for a snapshot, and the river fragment's point is that the snapshot is not the thing: the river stays a river because other waters keep arriving. So the question that fits a marriage, a career, a friendship is "how is it going?". The label freezes; the going question tracks the waters. Take the thing you are most tempted to define once and for all, and ask instead what is currently flowing through it.

### For example

Imagine two careers hit by the same layoff: one was the job title, the other a moving set of skills. The title is what the layoff took; the skills are already partly somewhere else.

## Primary source (verbatim)

> "Consciousness, then, does not appear to itself chopped up in bits. Such words as 'chain' or 'train' do not describe it fitly as it presents itself in the first instance. It is nothing jointed; it flows. A 'river' or a 'stream' are the metaphors by which it is most naturally described."
> — William James, The Principles of Psychology, vol. I (Henry Holt, 1890).
> Locator: Ch. IX "The Stream of Thought", p. 239.

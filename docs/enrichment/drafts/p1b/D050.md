> **Principle 50** — Keep a mirror-mind

The image comes at the end of the seventh book, after a run of instructions to hold nothing. The perfect man "fulfils all that he has received from Heaven, but he does not see that he was the recipient of anything. A pure vacancy (of all purpose) is what characterises him" ([Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Then the mirror:

> "When the perfect man employs his mind, it is a mirror. It conducts nothing and anticipates nothing; it responds to (what is before it), but does not retain it. Thus he is able to deal successfully with all things, and injures none." (Zhuangzi VII, "The Normal Course for Rulers and Kings", trans. Legge, [ETH Zurich PDF](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The book says the same of stillness elsewhere: "The still mind of the sage is the mirror of heaven and earth, the glass of all things" (Zhuangzi XIII, "The Way of Heaven", trans. Legge, [ETH Zurich PDF](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). The mirror is not detachment from things; it is exact response without storage. The text's own "thus" makes the no-storage the reason he deals with things successfully.

(Zhuangzi 7.)

### Plain words

Take the mirror literally for a second. It shows whatever is in front of it, fully, and keeps none of it. When the scene changes, the new scene is what shows; there is no residue of the last one. The instruction is about your mind's hold on things: the argument you won yesterday, the slight you are still rehearsing, the outcome you are already managing before it arrives. A mirror does none of this. Zhuangzi's claim is that dropping the storage is what lets you respond well, not what dulls the response.

### For example

Imagine hosting a long party: every guest met fully, and on the drive home none of the conversations is still running.

## Interpretation limits (for the ledger)

- Legge's "It conducts nothing and anticipates nothing; it responds to (what is before it),
  but does not retain it" is the citable rendering of 不將不迎，應而不藏. The old body's
  "greets and sees off... without holding or hoarding" tracked other translations; replaced.
- The causal reading ("dropping the storage is what lets you respond well") rests on the
  text's "Thus he is able to deal successfully with all things".
- The "still mind of the sage" mirror passage is from Book XIII (The Way of Heaven), not
  Book VII; cited accordingly.
- The party example is explicitly hypothetical, not a claim about hosts.

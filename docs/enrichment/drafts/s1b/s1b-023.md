2011 · clinical teaching vignette · Mark Webster, adapting Hayes, Strosahl and Wilson

In Webster's vignette, someone stands beside a ravine, pulling a rope against an unseen monster. Greater effort brings the person closer to the edge, and the monster pulls harder. The box ends with this worsening predicament ([Box 6](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/introduction-to-acceptance-and-commitment-therapy/D67B44FDED4147CA35C1AA8FA9D3DDA5)).

Later, the article's example treatment protocol introduces willingness through the idea of releasing the struggle. This is where Webster explicitly supplies the phrase:

> Letting go of the rope
>
> Webster, example protocol, Session 2 ([article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/advances-in-psychiatric-treatment/article/introduction-to-acceptance-and-commitment-therapy/D67B44FDED4147CA35C1AA8FA9D3DDA5)).

### An editorial application

Suppose irritation accompanies preparing a shared meal. Choose one manageable contribution and ask whether eliminating the irritation is necessary before doing it. Try allowing the feeling while carrying out that contribution, then examine what happened: did the meal benefit, and was the action within your capacity? What worked can inform the next attempt. Affect and overt behaviour are separate observations here.

The analogy addresses a costly response to an unwanted experience. It does not establish that all control attempts fail or that pulling harder is always a mistake. Some problems require sustained effort, outside help or a change in conditions.

Nor does the story report a monster's disappearance or a successful treatment outcome. Releasing this rope is an image for reconsidering a particular struggle, leaving practical decisions about the world still to be made.

**Principles:**
- [[P155 — Make workability your everyday criterion]]: examine the consequence of pulling before making greater effort the default.
- [[P157 — Accept what cannot be changed while committing to what can]]: the editorial application distinguishes allowing irritation from abandoning the chosen contribution.

Historical account, from the Records of the Grand Historian · Fan Li served King Goujian of Yue for twenty-two years and helped destroy the state of Wu; at the moment of victory he left, and the colleague who stayed did not survive.

Fan Li had seen the work through: twenty-two years of service to Goujian, the defeat of Wu. Then, at the point where most careers are collected, he took the judgment in the other direction. From the state of Qi he sent a letter to Wen Zhong, the minister who had shared the work: "when the soaring birds are gone the good bow is put away; when the cunning hare is dead the hunting dog is boiled." His verdict on the king was a physiognomy and a warning: "a man you can share hardship with but not ease" ([Shiji 41, as translated by the Fan Li page](https://suntzu-artofwar.org/en/world/fan-li/)).

The page reports Wen Zhong's outcome in one line of narration: "Zhong read the letter, pleaded illness, stayed away from court, and was in the end given death" ([Fan Li page, narrating Shiji 41](https://suntzu-artofwar.org/en/world/fan-li/)).

Fan Li's own move is recorded in the money-makers' chapter of the same history: "乃乘扁舟浮於江湖，變名易姓，適齊為鴟夷子皮，之陶為朱公" — he took a small boat onto the rivers and lakes, changed his name, was Chiyi Zipi in Qi and Zhu of Tao at Tao ([Shiji 129, Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/shiji/huo-zhi-lie-zhuan/zh)). What followed the withdrawal is kept here as context for what leaving opened rather than as the point of the story: the chapter's arithmetic runs "十九年之中三致千金，再分散與貧交疏昆弟" — within nineteen years he made a thousand gold three times, and twice distributed it to poor friends and distant kin, which the chapter calls the way of the rich who love practicing virtue ([Shiji 129, Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/shiji/huo-zhi-lie-zhuan/zh)).

Read beside D066, the story carries the moment the principle names: the task complete, the name distinguished, and the withdrawal that follows as its own act. The warning letter, the departure by boat, and Wen Zhong's death order are what the record gives; it reports Fan Li's actions and his letter, not his state of mind.

**Principles:**
- [[D066 — Withdraw when the work is done]]: Fan Li leaves at the moment of victory and warns the colleague who stays; the withdrawal is the illustrated act, the Tao years are context, and Wen Zhong's death order is the record's other edge.

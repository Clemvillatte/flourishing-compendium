Buddhist canonical account · MN 61, the Ambalatthika-rahulovada Sutta, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation at Access to Insight.

On one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Rajagaha, at the Bamboo Grove; his son, the young monk Rahula, was staying at the Mango Stone, and in the late afternoon the Buddha arose from his seclusion and went to where Rahula was staying ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). Rahula saw him coming, set out a seat and water for washing the feet, and the Buddha, having washed his feet, taught first with the little bit of water he had left in the dipper: as little of a contemplative as that in anyone who feels no shame at telling a deliberate lie ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). The instruction continues with a mirror comparison:

> "What do you think, Rahula: What is a mirror for?"
> "For reflection, sir."
> "In the same way, Rahula, bodily actions, verbal actions, & mental actions are to be done with repeated reflection." ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html))

The examination runs through three moments of the same action ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). Before acting: "Whenever you want to do a bodily action, you should reflect on it: 'This bodily action I want to do — would it lead to self-affliction, to the affliction of others, or to both? Would it be an unskillful bodily action, with painful consequences, painful results?'" ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). The same question is asked while doing the action, with an instruction to give it up if it is leading to affliction, and again after doing it ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). A harmful bodily or verbal action is to be confessed to the Teacher or a knowledgeable companion, followed by restraint in the future; a harmful mental action is to be met with distress, shame and disgust ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). A skillful action gets a different close: "you should stay mentally refreshed & joyful, training day & night in skillful mental qualities" ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)).

For B192, the compendium reads the exchange as the sutta's own picture of a review practice: the Buddha instructs his son to examine actions by body, speech and mind before doing them, while doing them, and after doing them ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)). The sutta attaches the examination to each action as it arises rather than prescribing a morning intention and an evening review; the principle page's daily format adapts that examination. The instruction also includes what the principle's plain words keep as the review's emotional half: acknowledgment of harm without collapse, and an allowance of refreshment and joy when conduct was skillful.

**Principles:**
- [[B192 — Begin the day with intention; end it with review]]: the Buddha's instruction to his son examines each action by body, speech and mind before, during and after doing it, with confession and restraint for harmful bodily or verbal conduct, distress for harmful mental conduct, and refreshment and joy for skillful conduct ([MN 61, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/mn/mn.061.than.html)); the principle's morning-and-evening format adapts this action-by-action examination.

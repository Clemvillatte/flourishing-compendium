Buddhist parable · the Lotus Sutra, chapter 8, in H. Kern's 1884 translation.

The chapter's parable, voiced in the disciples' declaration, tells it briefly: "as if some man having come to a friend's house got drunk or fell asleep, and that friend bound a priceless gem within his garment, with the thought: Let this gem be his." ([Lotus Sutra ch. 8, Kern](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot08.htm)) The man wakes and leaves without knowing:

> "The other man, unaware of it, goes forth and from that place travels to another town. There he is befallen with misfortune and, as a miserable beggar, seeks his food in affliction." ([Lotus Sutra ch. 8, Kern, stanza 38](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot08.htm))

He is content with what begging brings, and "as to that jewel, he has forgotten it; he has not the slightest remembrance of its having been tied in his upper robe." ([Lotus Sutra ch. 8, Kern, stanza 39](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot08.htm)) Years later the old friend finds him and asks how he can have such difficulty seeking food and clothing, when he has "bound within thy garment a priceless gem, quite sufficient to fulfil all thy wishes" ([Lotus Sutra ch. 8, Kern](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot08.htm)). In the stanzas: "This friend properly reprimands him and shows him the jewel within his robe." ([Lotus Sutra ch. 8, Kern, stanza 40](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot08.htm)) The chapter applies the parable to the disciples' own former aspiration, laid in them by the Buddha "from time immemorial" without their perceiving it ([Lotus Sutra ch. 8, Kern](https://www.sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot08.htm)).

For D065, the compendium reads the parable as a figure of returning to what was already given. The man's industry is real; what is missing is the check that would have sent him back to his own garment. The principle's plain words put the return as a question asked before the next demand is answered: does this still need my attention, or have I carried on out of habit? The parable's answer is the friend's reprimand, which shows him the source he has been wearing all along.

**Principles:**
- [[D065 — Use the light, return to the insight]]: the man searches distant towns for scraps while a priceless gem hangs bound in his own robe, and the return happens when the friend's reprimand sends his attention back to what he carries; the parable supplies a figure for reverting to the source rather than a new demand.

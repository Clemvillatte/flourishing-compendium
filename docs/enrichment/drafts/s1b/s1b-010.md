Buddhist canonical account · Udāna 1.10, the Bāhiya Sutta, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation at Access to Insight ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)).

Bāhiya of the Bark-cloth lives by the sea at Suppāraka, worshipped and supported as a holy man, when a deity who had once been his blood relative tells him he is not an arahant and has not entered the path, and that the Buddha is teaching in Sāvatthī ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). Bāhiya goes to the Buddha, who is on alms round, and asks for the teaching; the Buddha answers, "This is not the time, Bāhiya. We have entered the town for alms." ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). Bāhiya asks again, saying it is hard to know what dangers may face the Buddha's life or his own ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). The Buddha then teaches in a few lines ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)):

> "In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen. In reference to the heard, only the heard. In reference to the sensed, only the sensed. In reference to the cognized, only the cognized. That is how you should train yourself. When for you there will be only the seen in reference to the seen, only the heard in reference to the heard, only the sensed in reference to the sensed, only the cognized in reference to the cognized, then, Bāhiya, there is no you in connection with that. When there is no you in connection with that, there is no you there. When there is no you there, you are neither here nor yonder nor between the two. This, just this, is the end of stress." ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html))

The sutta reports the result without ceremony: "Through hearing this brief explanation of the Dhamma from the Blessed One, the mind of Bāhiya of the Bark-cloth right then and there was released from effluents through lack of clinging/sustenance." ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)) The story does not end there. Not long after the Buddha's departure, "Bāhiya was attacked & killed by a cow with a young calf" ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)); the Buddha has the monks cremate the body and build a memorial, and the sutta closes with verses about a place where water, earth, fire and wind have no footing ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)).

For B043, the compendium reads this as an illustration of ownerlessness: the instruction moves through seeing, hearing, sensing and cognition to "there is no you in connection with that." The account reports Bāhiya's release on hearing the teaching, through lack of clinging/sustenance ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). As a literary reading, the account also refuses a triumph shape: the release and the cow's arrival sit side by side, and the one does not cancel the other.

**Principles:**
- [[B043 — Develop insight (vipassana)]]: the instruction to keep the seen only the seen and the cognized only the cognized turns on "there is no you in connection with that", and the account reports release through lack of clinging/sustenance on hearing it; the body's death by a cow with a young calf not long after does not undo it ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)).

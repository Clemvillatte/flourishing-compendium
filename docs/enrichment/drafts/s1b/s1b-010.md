Buddhist canonical account · Udāna 1.10, the Bāhiya Sutta, in Thanissaro Bhikkhu's translation at Access to Insight.

Bāhiya of the Bark-cloth lives by the sea at Suppāraka, worshipped and supported as a holy man, when a deity who had once been his blood relative tells him he is not an arahant and has not entered the path, and that the Buddha is teaching in Sāvatthī ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). Bāhiya goes to the Buddha, who is on alms round, and asks for the teaching; the Buddha answers, "This is not the time, Bāhiya. We have entered the town for alms." ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). Bāhiya asks again, saying it is hard to know what dangers may face the Buddha's life or his own ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)). The Buddha then teaches in a few lines:

> "In reference to the seen, there will be only the seen. In reference to the heard, only the heard. In reference to the sensed, only the sensed. In reference to the cognized, only the cognized. That is how you should train yourself. When for you there will be only the seen in reference to the seen, only the heard in reference to the heard, only the sensed in reference to the sensed, only the cognized in reference to the cognized, then, Bāhiya, there is no you in connection with that. When there is no you in connection with that, there is no you there. When there is no you there, you are neither here nor yonder nor between the two. This, just this, is the end of stress." ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html))

The sutta reports the result without ceremony: "Through hearing this brief explanation of the Dhamma from the Blessed One, the mind of Bāhiya of the Bark-cloth right then and there was released from effluents through lack of clinging/sustenance." ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)) The story does not end there. Not long after the Buddha's departure, "Bāhiya was attacked & killed by a cow with a young calf" ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)); the Buddha has the monks cremate the body and build a memorial, and the closing verses place where Bāhiya was freed beyond water, earth, fire and wind, beyond stars, sun and moon ([Ud 1.10, Thanissaro](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/ud/ud.1.10.than.html)).

For B043, the compendium reads the instruction as insight in its most compressed form: nothing is asked of Bāhiya but to let each sense field show only what it shows, and the seeing itself is what releases. The principle's plain words say the verses describe a seeing rather than a believing; here the seeing is the whole of the practice, and its outcome is immediate. The cow's arrival keeps the account from becoming a triumph narrative: the release is not the body's rescue.

**Principles:**
- [[B043 — Develop insight (vipassana)]]: Bāhiya is taught to let the seen be only the seen and the cognized only the cognized, and his mind is released through lack of clinging on hearing it; the seeing, not any belief, is the practice, and the body's death by a cow with a calf days after does not undo it.

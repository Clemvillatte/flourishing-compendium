Recorded dialogue, translated · the biographical record at the end of the Record of Linji, Part 6, in the Ru-Yi Meditation Center's bilingual translation.

As a young monk, Linji asks Huangbo "what the precise great intent of the Buddhadharma is." "Before he had even finished speaking, Huangbo struck him" ([Record of Linji, Part 6](https://www.ruyimeditation.org/sutras/linji-lu/part-06/)). He asks again, and is struck again; the text counts it out: three questions asked, three beatings received. Linji tells the head monk he will leave: "I can only blame my own karmic obstruction for failing to grasp the profound purport — I think I should leave now." Huangbo, instead of letting him go anywhere else, sends him to Dayu at the Gao'an sandbar, "he will surely explain it to you" ([Record of Linji, Part 6](https://www.ruyimeditation.org/sutras/linji-lu/part-06/)).

Dayu asks where he has come from, hears the story of the three beatings, and turns it around: "Huangbo showed you such grandmotherly kindness, wearing himself out completely for your sake — and you come here asking whether you were at fault or not?" At those words the record says Linji was greatly awakened, and his awakening's first words are a judgment on the teacher who beat him: "So, after all, there wasn't much to Huangbo's Buddhadharma." Dayu grabs him: "You bed-wetting little devil! A moment ago you were asking whether you were at fault or not, and now you say there wasn't much to Huangbo's Buddhadharma. What exactly have you seen? Speak quickly, speak quickly!" Linji answers by striking Dayu three times in the ribs; Dayu shoves him away: "Your teacher is Huangbo — this is none of my affair" ([Record of Linji, Part 6](https://www.ruyimeditation.org/sutras/linji-lu/part-06/)).

Back at Huangbo's, the exchange completes itself. Asked where he has been, Linji reports the whole of it; Huangbo says he would like to get his hands on Dayu and give him a good beating; Linji says, "Why wait for him to come? Take it right now," and slaps Huangbo. Huangbo's words are: "This crazy fellow, coming here to pull the tiger's whiskers!" Linji shouts, and Huangbo tells the attendant: "take this crazy fellow to the practice hall" ([Record of Linji, Part 6](https://www.ruyimeditation.org/sutras/linji-lu/part-06/)).

Read beside B161, the two passages make an editorial comparison; the record itself does not connect them. The awakening in this episode can be read as the challenge to unquestioning deference that the sermon states as instruction: the first words out of it demote the teacher's dharma, and the return to the teacher ends with the teacher still standing there, still teaching, giving the attendant an order. What the record does not do is explain the awakening as dismantling Huangbo's authority, and this page does not claim it does. The blows belong to the recorded narrative; the application here is the questioning of authority, not the imitation of physical violence.

**Principles:**
- [[B161 — If you meet the Buddha, kill him]]: The training episode can be read beside the sermon as its narrative counterpart, a challenge to unquestioning deference in which the teacher's dharma is demoted at the moment of awakening while the teacher keeps his place; the comparison is this page's, the connection between the passages is not asserted by the record.

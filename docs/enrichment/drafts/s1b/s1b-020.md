2013 contribution · ACT metaphor · Richard Whitney, Fly Fishing

Whitney pictures a mind that offers personally convincing lures. A fish bites and becomes hooked; struggling drives the hook deeper. Noticing the fly creates an opportunity to continue swimming in a chosen direction ([Fly Fishing, p. 79](https://mimhtraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/The-Big-Book-of-Act-Metaphors.pdf)).

> we don’t have to bite
>
> Whitney, Fly Fishing, p. 79 ([book text](https://mimhtraining.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/The-Big-Book-of-Act-Metaphors.pdf)).

An editorial application begins with a familiar invitation: replay an awkward conversation one more time. Before following it, name the action you intended to take, perhaps checking on a friend. Try sending a short, considerate message while the replay remains available. Afterwards, examine whether you acted with the care you intended; retain or adjust the experiment accordingly. Cognition and overt behaviour are the dimensions to observe.

The practical question is what following the thought does in that situation. Whether its account of the conversation is accurate remains a separate matter. In ACT, defusion concerns a thought's influence on behaviour; it need not settle the thought's truth ([Hayes and colleagues, Cognitive Fusion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3635495/)).

The fishing image also has a limit. A real injury or a credible warning calls for practical attention. The metaphor supplies no reason to dismiss either, and noticing an invitation to ruminate is no guarantee that disengaging will be easy.

**Principles:**
- [[P159 — Defuse — see thoughts as thoughts]]: notice the invitation to follow a thought before responding.
- [[P162 — Take committed action — behavior is where values become real]]: the editorial experiment checks whether an act of care was carried out.

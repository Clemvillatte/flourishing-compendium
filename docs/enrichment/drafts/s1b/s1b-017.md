2011 · ACT metaphor · David Gillanders, The Sailing Boat Metaphor

Water washes into a small sailing boat. The sailor spends more and more time bailing while the boat's direction goes unattended. Then the sailor notices that the bailer has holes: it is a sieve. Gillanders asks whether work together could include taking the tiller, setting the sails and choosing a course while some water remains ([author's 2011 account](https://actsig.wordpress.com/2011/05/22/the-sailing-boat-metaphor/)).

The listener is asked to choose between a drier boat drifting without a chosen course and a wetter boat moving in a wanted direction. No decision is reported. The author also allows better bailing methods where they help: he explains that some pain-control strategies can be effective in his clinical setting ([author's account and qualification](https://actsig.wordpress.com/2011/05/22/the-sailing-boat-metaphor/)).

> there is no speedometer in this work.
>
> Gillanders, paragraph beginning What if our work could be about helping you ([2011 publication](https://actsig.wordpress.com/2011/05/22/the-sailing-boat-metaphor/)).

An editorial application is to choose an activity that expresses care while discomfort is present. Someone missing a friend might try a short message instead of making complete relief from sadness a condition for contact. Notice whether the message served the friendship and whether its scale was manageable; repeat or revise accordingly. Care for the friendship is an ongoing direction; sending the message is a goal that can be completed.

The water is a therapeutic image for unwanted experience. Real flooding requires practical safety measures. Similarly, the metaphor gives no reason to forgo effective treatment or to interpret preventable harm as something a person should endure.

**Principles:**
- [[P155 — Make workability your everyday criterion]]: judge a coping method partly by whether it helps the chosen journey.
- [[P157 — Accept what cannot be changed while committing to what can]]: the sailor can steer while water remains.
- [[P158 — Choose values as directions, not destinations]]: choosing and maintaining a course gives the values comparison its content.

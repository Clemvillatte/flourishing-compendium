Daoist literary narrative · Zhuangzi, Book XIX (Da Sheng), in James Legge's 1891 translation.

Yan Yuan tells Confucius that when he crossed the gulf of Shang-shen, "the ferryman handled the boat like a spirit", and that when he asked whether such handling could be learned, the ferryman answered that good swimmers learn it quickly, while divers can manage a boat without ever having seen one ([Zhuangzi 19.4, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Confucius explains the answer: "Good swimmers acquire the ability quickly - they forget the water (and its dangers)", and divers look on the watery gulf as a hill-side, with the upsetting of a boat no more disturbing than a carriage turning back ([same passage](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)). Then he turns to what ruins such ability:

> "He who is contending for a piece of earthenware puts forth all his skill. If the prize be a buckle of brass, he shoots timorously; if it be for an article of gold, he shoots as if he were blind. The skill of the archer is the same in all the cases; but (in the two latter cases) he is under the influence of solicitude, and looks on the external prize as most important." ([Confucius, in Zhuangzi 19.4, Legge](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf))

The passage closes with his judgment: "All who attach importance to what is external show stupidity in themselves" ([same passage](https://metis.ethz.ch/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Zhuangzi_EnglishLegge.pdf)).

For D073, the compendium reads the archer's ladder as a portrait of planning displacing presence. Nothing in the archer's hands changes between the earthenware and the gold; what changes is the weight the shooter puts on the result, and the degradation follows the weight, not the skill. The ferryman completes the picture from the other side: his competence is not a plan held against the water but familiarity so complete that the danger has been forgotten. The passage names the failure mode the principle's plain words call defending the guess, and it locates the remedy in the same place: attention left with the task rather than lent to the prize.

**Principles:**
- [[D073 — Don't over-plan — act from presence]]: the ferryman's skill is internalized familiarity that has forgotten the water, and the archer's skill is constant while solicitude for the prize makes the same hands shoot timorously and then blind; presence is displaced by attachment to the outcome, not by lack of preparation.

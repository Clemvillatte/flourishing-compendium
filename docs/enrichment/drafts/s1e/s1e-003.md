Biographical account · Brother Lawrence (born Nicholas Herman of Lorraine, c. 1610-1691), a lay brother among the barefooted Carmelites in Paris, from the conversations and letters recorded by Joseph de Beaufort and published after his death.

The record introduces him flatly: "a lowly and unlearned man, who, after having been a footman and soldier, was admitted a Lay Brother among the barefooted Carmelites at Paris in 1666" ([Gutenberg 13871, Preface](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)). The monastery put him in the kitchen, "to which he had naturally a great aversion" ([Gutenberg 13871, Second Conversation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)). That is where the story happens: not in a vision, but at the stove, among pans.

His method was to bring the same attention to the pots that others saved for prayer. He began the work with a short request: "O my God, since Thou art with me, and I must now, in obedience to Thy commands, apply my mind to these outward things, I beseech Thee to grant me the grace to continue in Thy Presence" ([Gutenberg 5657, Fourth Conversation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5657/pg5657.txt)). He accustomed himself "to do everything there for the love of GOD, and with prayer, upon all occasions, for His grace to do his work well" ([Gutenberg 13871, Second Conversation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)). The result he stated himself: "The time of business... does not with me differ from the time of prayer; and in the noise and clatter of my kitchen, while several persons are at the same time calling for different things, I possess GOD in as great tranquility as if I were upon my knees at the blessed sacrament" ([Gutenberg 13871, Fourth Conversation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)).

The consequence was not an escape from the kitchen but a change in its weight. He "had found everything easy, during fifteen years that he had been employed there" ([Gutenberg 13871, Second Conversation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)), and he "was pleased when he could take up a straw from the ground for the love of GOD, seeking Him only, and nothing else, not even His gifts" ([Gutenberg 13871, Second Conversation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)). The same man's turn toward God had begun with an ordinary sight: a dry and leafless tree in midwinter, and the thought of the spring that would come for it ([Gutenberg 13871, Preface](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/13871/pg13871.txt)). The compendium reads him for B176 exactly there: the miracle was never staged elsewhere. A straw, a skillet, a bare tree — the ordinary held the whole thing, and the only question was whether he was in it.

Not carried: the principle's Buddhist frame of walking meditation and the TNH tea exercise; Lawrence's presence is devotional, aimed at God rather than at bare attention, though the two train the same muscle.

**Principles:**
- [[B176 — Treat the present moment as the miracle]]: Brother Lawrence kept the presence in the clatter of a kitchen he disliked, until business and prayer became one time and a straw picked up from the ground was enough — the ordinary, fully inhabited, as the miracle the principle names.

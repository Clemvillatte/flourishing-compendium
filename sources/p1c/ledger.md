## D022 — Subtract, don't add (DDJ 48)

- Opened: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt (The Tao Teh King, trans.
  James Legge; ebook #216) and cross-checked the identical text at
  https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm (HTML edition).
- Verbatim passage (ch. 48, parts 1-3): "He who devotes himself to learning (seeks) from
  day to day to increase (his knowledge); he who devotes himself to the Tao (seeks) from
  day to day to diminish (his doing). He diminishes it and again diminishes it, till he
  arrives at doing nothing (on purpose). Having arrived at this point of non-action, there
  is nothing which he does not do. He who gets as his own all under heaven does so by
  giving himself no trouble (with that end). If one take trouble (with that end), he is
  not equal to getting as his own all under heaven."
- Used in page: blockquote of part 2 (core), inline fragments of parts 1 and 3.
- Limitations: owner's Google Drive Daoism folder not fetched for this item (web edition
  of the same Legge translation used; drive link untested this session). No commentary
  paragraph attached to ch. 48 in the Gutenberg text.
- QC: advisor round 1 flagged interpretation-as-source ("forced doing"), long duplicate
  blockquote, metaphor pile-up in Plain words, and unsupported empirical framing of the
  For example; all addressed in revision (2026-10-19, genuineflourishing29). Round 2:
  inherited seed line "In learning and living, loss beats gain..." replaced (the chapter
  opposes learning-increase to Tao-diminution; it does not say loss beats gain in
  learning), and the For example reframed as an explicitly modern application instead of
  "the chapter's fix". Round 3: the remaining opening directive ("Remove a bad habit...")
  and its (DDJ 48.) locator deleted at advisor direction; the body now opens on the
  chapter explanation, matching the D087 exemplar pattern. Protected blockquote statement,
  wikilinks and reflections unchanged (validator ok).

## D006 — Move by return (fu) (DDJ 16, 40)

- Opened: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/216/pg216.txt (Legge, ebook #216),
  chapters 16 and 40.
- Verbatim passage (ch. 16, part 1): "All things alike go through their processes of
  activity, and (then) we see them return (to their original state). When things (in the
  vegetable world) have displayed their luxuriant growth, we see each of them return to
  its root. This returning to their root is what we call the state of stillness; and that
  stillness may be called a reporting that they have fulfilled their appointed end."
- Ch. 16 part 2 quote used inline: "To know that unchanging rule is to be intelligent;
  not to know it leads to wild movements and evil issues."
- Ch. 40 (Legge verse) paraphrased, not quoted, after advisor round 1: "The movement of
  the Tao / By contraries proceeds; / And weakness marks the course / Of Tao's mighty
  deeds."
- QC: advisor round 1 flagged unsupported psychological generalisations (mood/luck
  waves, farmer claim), capitalisation in the ch. 40 quote, and ambiguity of "the
  chapter goes on"; all fixed in revision. Round 2: para 2 replaced entirely with the
  advisor's scoped sentence ("The compendium applies the image of return by asking where
  a plan allows for stopping and reconsideration."). Validator ok against pre-item
  baseline 6a2bc86.

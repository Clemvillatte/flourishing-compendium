# A014 — Practice magnificence (megaloprepeia) (p1d, genuineflourishing30)

## Sources opened

- Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.2, W. D. Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive: https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html (same edition the owner update referenced for CO072)

## Supported passages (verbatim, with locators)

- Blockquote: "The magnificent man is like an artist; for he can see what is fitting and spend large sums tastefully."
- Fittingness (paraphrased): "Therefore the result should be worthy of the expense, and the expense should be worthy of the result, or should even exceed it... the expenditure should be worthy of his means, and suit not only the result but also the producer."
- Honourable expenditures (paraphrased): "Magnificence is an attribute of expenditures of the kind which we call honourable, e.g. those connected with the gods-votive offerings, buildings, and sacrifices... or equip a chorus or a trireme, or entertain the city."
- Public objects / lasting works (paraphrased): "the magnificent man spends not on himself but on public objects" / "will spend by preference on those works that are lasting (for these are the most beautiful)" / "for even a house is a sort of public ornament".
- Vulgar man (paraphrased): "he gives a club dinner on the scale of a wedding banquet... not for honour's sake but to show off his wealth".

## Limitations / decisions

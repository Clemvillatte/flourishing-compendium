# D005 — Expect reversal: extremes turn into their opposites (p1d, genuineflourishing30)

## Sources opened

- Laozi, Tao Teh King (Daodejing), James Legge translation (1891), Project Gutenberg:
  https://www.gutenberg.org/files/216/216-h/216-h.htm

## Supported passages (verbatim, with locators)

- Ch. 40: "The movement of the Tao / By contraries proceeds; / And weakness marks the course / Of Tao's mighty deeds." (used as the standalone blockquote)
- Ch. 58: "Misery!—happiness is to be found by its side! Happiness!—misery lurks beneath it! Who knows what either will come to in the end?" and "The (method of) correction shall by a turn become distortion, and the good in it shall by a turn become evil."
- Ch. 9: "It is better to leave a vessel unfilled, than to attempt to carry it when it is full. If you keep feeling a point that has been sharpened, the point cannot long preserve its sharpness." / "When gold and jade fill the hall, their possessor cannot keep them safe. When wealth and honours lead to arrogancy, this brings its evil on itself." / "When the work is done, and one's name is becoming distinguished, to withdraw into obscurity is the way of Heaven."

## Limitations / decisions

- The old body's images (blade dulls fastest under hard use, guarded with total control becomes most likely lost) were loose paraphrases; replaced with Legge's own images (vessel, sharpened point, gold and jade) so every image is now verbatim or tight paraphrase from an opened source.
- The old Plain words was a replicate of the body (same three clauses); re-derived as the mechanism reading (each extreme builds the pressure that undoes it) with fresh instances (certainty, indulgence).
- The old For example's second half ("the person who builds modest habits keeps the weight off") was an unsupported empirical claim; the new example keeps only the cycle logic, framed as the principle's own application.
- "Heraclitus, fragments" and the psychology parallels (hedonic adaptation, affective forecasting) were inherited link-section content, untouched.

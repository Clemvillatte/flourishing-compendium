# A077 — Honour friendship in prosperity as well as adversity (p1d, genuineflourishing30)

## Sources opened

- Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, W. D. Ross translation (Oxford 2009 edition), Internet Archive: https://ia600704.us.archive.org/26/items/aristotle-entire-collected-writings/Aristotle/Nicomachean%20Ethics%20%5Btrans.%20Ross%20%26%20Brown%5D/Aristotle%20-%20Nicomachean%20Ethics%20(Oxford%2C%202009).pdf (Book IX ch. 11 and Book VIII ch. 1 extracted; same edition opened for A043 and A049)

## Supported passages (verbatim, with locators)

- Blockquote (IX.11, 1171a-b, contiguous sentence): "Friendship, then, is more necessary in bad fortune, and so it is useful friends that one wants in this case; but it is more noble in good fortune, and so we also seek for good men as our friends, since it is more desirable to confer benefits on these and to live with these."
- Go unasked (IX.11, paraphrased): "Conversely, it is fitting to go unasked and readily to the aid of those in adversity (for it is characteristic of a friend to render services, and especially to those who are in need and have not demanded them; such action is nobler and pleasanter for both persons)."
- Tardy to receive (IX.11, paraphrased): "but when our friends are prosperous we should join readily in their activities... but be tardy in coming forward to be the objects of their kindness; for it is not noble to be keen to receive benefits."
- Summon readily (IX.11, paraphrased): "For this cause it would seem that we ought to summon our friends readily to share our good fortunes... but summon them to our bad fortunes with hesitation."
- Only refuge (VIII.1, paraphrased): "And in poverty and in other misfortunes men think friends are the only refuge."

## Limitations / decisions

- The page's locator line (NE VIII.5, VIII.9, IX.11; EE VII.3) kept as the page's citation; only VIII.1 and IX.11 were opened and used, and no claims are sourced to the unopened chapters or to the Eudemian Ethics.
- The old body's framing ("It is easy to be a friend in adversity. It is harder... not to be envious, not to be too busy, not to take the friend for granted") is the page's own gloss, preserved in the statement (protected); the new body grounds the prosperity duty in IX.11's specific instructions (join readily, be tardy to receive, summon readily to good fortunes).
- The old Plain words was a near-verbatim replicate of the body; replaced with the necessity/nobility reading. A077 is not on plain-words-replicates.md, but the pattern was the flagged one.
- The old For example (launch party) mirrors the summon-readily passage; kept unchanged.
- The inherited psychology-parallel claims (Condon and Makransky 2020; Gable et al. 2004) are not restated in the body.

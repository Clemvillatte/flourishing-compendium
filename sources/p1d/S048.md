# S048 — Withhold assent when the impression is uncertain (p1d, genuineflourishing30)

## Sources opened

- Epictetus, Discourses I.28, George Long translation, StoicSource: https://www.stoicsource.com/epictetus/discourses/1.28/
- Epictetus, Enchiridion, Internet Classics Archive: https://classics.mit.edu/Epictetus/epicench.html (chapter 45 extracted; same page opened earlier for BI012)

## Supported passages (verbatim, with locators)

- Blockquote (Disc. I.28, contiguous): "What is the cause of assenting to anything? The fact that it appears to be true. It is not possible then to assent to that which appears not to be true. Why? Because this is the nature of the understanding: to incline to the true, to be dissatisfied with the false, and in matters uncertain to withhold assent."
- Madman (I.28): "And what is the name of those who follow every appearance? They are called madmen."
- Scales (I.28): "There is then nothing like scales (balance), nothing like a rule: but some appearance is presented, and straightway I act according to it."
- Ench. 45: "Thus you will not run the hazard of assenting to any appearances but such as you fully comprehend."

# A017 — Practice gentleness (praotes) (p1d, genuineflourishing30)

## Sources opened

- Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics IV.5, W. D. Ross translation, Internet Classics Archive: https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.4.iv.html (the Book IV page, opened for A014 and re-extracted for the good-temper chapter)

## Supported passages (verbatim, with locators)

- Blockquote (contiguous): "The man who is angry at the right things and with the right people, and, further, as he ought, when he ought, and as long as he ought, is praised."
- Good-tempered man (paraphrased): "For the good-tempered man tends to be unperturbed and not to be led by passion... the good-tempered man is not revengeful, but rather tends to make allowances."
- Deficiency blamed (paraphrased): "those who are not angry at the things they should be angry at are thought to be fools... to endure being insulted and put up with insult to one's friends is slavish."
- Two speeds (paraphrased): hot-tempered people's "anger ceases quickly-which is the best point about them" (that passage) vs choleric people "ready to be angry with everything and on every occasion" (the next passage); sulky people "hard to appease, and retain their anger long... most troublesome to themselves and to their dearest friends."
